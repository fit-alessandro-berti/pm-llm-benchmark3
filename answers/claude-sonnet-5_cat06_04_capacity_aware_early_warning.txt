# Portfolio Early-Warning and Swarm Allocation Policy

## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility gate (hard filter, not a probability threshold):**
A case is swarm-eligible only if it is strictly before `Diagnosis Complete`. `I-03` is therefore excluded regardless of `p_now` — its stated ARR (0.00–0.03) confirms the intervention has essentially no causal leverage once diagnosis has finished. High `p_now` alone never overrides stage ineligibility.

**Calibration adjustment (segment-specific, applied before ranking, not after):**
Raw `p_now` is not used directly. Apply a directional correction band before any risk-based reasoning:
- SME: `p_adj = p_now + 0.04 to 0.09` (underprediction)
- Strategic Enterprise: `p_adj = p_now  0.05 to 0.12` (overprediction)
- Public Sector: `p_adj  p_now` (small widening of uncertainty only)
- Non-Native-Language: `p_adj = p_now ± wide band`, treated as low-confidence in both directions
- Data-quality-flagged cases (I-06): treat `p_now` as *unverified*; use lower half of plausible range until verified.

**Minimum evidence change to count as a "new alert":**
A repeat alert is only actionable if at least one of:
(a) stage transition, (b) new artifact/data (log, escalation, customer contact), (c) a probability delta exceeding a noise band (e.g. |p| > 0.05) that is *not* attributable purely to a 30-min refresh with an unchanged case file, or (d) explicit human case-owner flag.
Absent these, repeated refreshes are **suppressed** for prioritization purposes (see Section 4).

**Prioritization score (used to rank eligible, non-suppressed cases):**

```
Priority = [ARR_mid × Value  SwarmCost(€450)  FA_burden]
           × ConfidenceWeight(segment, data-quality)
           × UrgencyWeight(time-to-deadline vs. remaining process steps)
            SpilloverExternality(queue congestion at time of decision)
```

- `ConfidenceWeight` shrinks toward 0.5–0.7 for sparse/uncertain segments (NNL) or unverified data (I-06), and stays near 1.0 for complete, well-calibrated segments.
- `UrgencyWeight` up-weights cases nearing `Diagnosis Complete` (their eligibility window is closing) more than cases still in early Triage with long runway, even if their raw `p_now` is lower.
- Value is *not* used unadjusted at extreme magnitudes; for prioritization only (not for reporting), value is capped (e.g., at €50k) so one very high-value case cannot mechanically dominate the entire slot budget every cycle (fairness safeguard, elaborated in §5).

This is deliberately **not** a single p-threshold rule: eligibility, evidence freshness, calibration, and externalities all gate action independently of where a case sits on raw `p_now`.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **Cool-down:** once a case has been scored (alerted) without triggering a swarm, a subsequent alert triggered purely by the routine 30-minute refresh does **not** reset priority or count as escalation unless the minimum-evidence-change condition (Section 1) is met.
- **Suppression:** cases meeting none of the new-evidence criteria are suppressed from the active decision queue but remain visible on a monitoring dashboard; suppression is time-bounded (re-checked every cycle) so a case cannot be silently ignored indefinitely.
- **Escalation (non-swarm):** a case suppressed for 3+ consecutive alerts with sustained elevated risk is escalated to a human case owner for manual review (e.g., customer check-in, workload reassignment) — a track that does **not** consume swarm capacity, because the model's stable estimate is already known and repeating it adds no information.
- **Re-entry:** a suppressed case re-enters the active decision queue upon (i) new evidence, (ii) stage transition, or (iii) a probability move outside the noise band.

**I-05 vs. I-07, explicitly contrasted:**
- **I-05**: third alert, `p_prev=0.55  p_now=0.57` — essentially flat, consistent with refresh noise, no new artifact. Despite a large ARR range (0.25–0.40) and SME underprediction implying true risk may be materially higher (~0.61–0.66), the *repetition itself* carries no new information. Decision: suppress from active swarm competition this cycle; escalate to human monitoring given the underprediction concern; revisit immediately if genuine new evidence appears.
- **I-07**: third alert, but accompanied by a **new customer diagnostic log** — a concrete evidentiary change — even though `p_now` nominally *fell* slightly (0.750.72). The new information, not the raw probability trajectory, is what resets the cooldown. Decision: treat as a fresh, legitimate alert; competes for the reserved slot on time-criticality grounds (1.5h to deadline), tempered by its low value and the operational question of whether any action can complete in time.

This contrast is the core test of the policy: identical "alert count" (both third alerts) produces opposite treatment because the *evidentiary basis* differs, not the probability number.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based starvation:** Ranking on `ARR × Value` systematically deprioritizes lower-contract-value cases (SME: I-05, I-07, I-10) even when their proportional risk and ARR are high. *Safeguard:* cap the value term used in the priority score (e.g., at €50k) for ranking purposes only, and require that at least a minimum share of slots over a rolling period (e.g., 1 in 4 over a week) go to below-median-value cases with high standalone risk×ARR, tracked and reported separately from the raw €-optimizing allocation.

- **Non-Native-Language uncertainty (I-09):** small validation sample means both `p` and ARR are poorly characterized — this can cut either way (over- or under-servicing). *Safeguard:* apply an explicit confidence discount in the priority score, but require periodic *mandatory* sampling/audit of NNL cases (whether or not currently alerting) to build a labeled dataset; require human sign-off before committing scarce slots to this segment until sample size improves.

- **Differential calibration across segments:** Strategic Enterprise overprediction and SME underprediction, if left uncorrected, would make Strategic Enterprise cases look artificially urgent and SME cases artificially safe. *Safeguard:* segment-specific correction bands (as applied above), refreshed monthly, with allocation-rate-by-segment reporting to detect residual bias even after correction.

- **Selective intervention contaminating future labels:** Treated cases that avoid breach because of the swarm will appear in future training data as "low risk, no breach," teaching the model that similar profiles are safe — a survivorship-style feedback loop that erodes future detection power. *Safeguard:* flag every intervened case with a treatment indicator, exclude or specially model treated outcomes in retraining (uplift/counterfactual modeling), and maintain a small deliberate holdout — e.g., 1 in 10 otherwise-eligible high-priority cases left untreated for causal comparison — to preserve an unbiased estimate of natural breach probability and true ARR.

- **Spillover harm falling on untreated groups:** The 6 non-intervened diagnosis-stage cases absorbing congestion delay are, by construction, the ones *not* selected — often the lower-value/SME cases that were already deprioritized. This compounds inequity: they get no benefit and bear a cost. *Safeguard:* track spillover incidence by segment/value tier; if it concentrates on lower-value or SME cases, either cap consecutive same-queue slot usage or give spillover-affected cases a compensating priority bump in the next cycle.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **Operational monitoring:** daily tracking of slots used, specialist time diverted from ordinary diagnosis, realized queue delay vs. the 8–20 min (or 15–30 min adverse) planning range, and reserve-slot utilization (how often it's used for regulatory events vs. reallocated).

- **Predictive/calibration monitoring:** rolling segment-level backtests (monthly for well-sampled segments, more frequent/pooled for NNL as data accumulates) to update the correction bands in Section 1; monitor for calibration drift specifically *after* policy deployment, since selective treatment changes the observed outcome distribution.

- **Causal-effect monitoring:** since assignment is priority-score-driven rather than random, rely on (a) the small deliberate untreated holdout described in §5, and/or (b) matched comparison (same stage, similar p, similar time-to-deadline, same segment) between treated and untreated cases to re-estimate ARR ranges quarterly; treat currently supplied ARR ranges as provisional priors, not fixed truths.

- **Capacity monitoring:** track daily demand-vs-supply of slots, specialist fatigue/interruption counts, and whether the reserve policy is chronically unused (suggesting over-conservatism) or chronically insufficient (suggesting under-resourcing); adjust the reserve rule accordingly.

- **Fairness monitoring:** quarterly report of slot allocation by segment and value tier relative to underlying alert volume; track suppression rates by segment (to catch, e.g., systematic suppression of SME alerts due to flat-but-elevated probabilities); track where spillover harm lands.

- **Triggers for policy revision:** formally revisit thresholds/weights if (i) realized ARR falls below the stated lower bound in a large share of audited cases, (ii) realized spillover harm exceeds direct benefit for two or more consecutive reporting periods (as flagged as plausible in the adverse scenario above), (iii) segment calibration bias shifts materially at the next backtest, or (iv) fairness metrics show sustained disproportionate allocation to the highest-value tier. Any such trigger routes to a joint ops/data-science/fairness review rather than an automatic threshold change, since the policy is a multi-factor rule, not a single number to tune.
