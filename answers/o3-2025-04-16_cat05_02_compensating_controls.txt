## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition  

FEP-1 breaks down into at least five concrete objectives / threat scenarios.  
For each one the table shows whether a given compensating control can (P)revent, (D)etect, (C)orrect, or is (–) Not able to address the risk.  

| # | Control-objective / Threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 (whole package) |
|---|-------------------------------------|------|------|------|----------------------|
| 1 | A single insider pays a fake vendor or diverts funds to own account | P (bank-change & ownership test) | P/D (independent content review) | D/C (audit & recall if <24 h) | P/D/C (token + AV-2 + L3 mgr + 24 h audit) |
| 2 | A single insider accidentally enters wrong amount or duplicate invoice | P (duplicate & limit check) | P/D (reasonableness review) | D/C (audit & possible recall) | P/D/C |
| 3 | Colluding preparer & releaser fabricate receipt or overbill (two-person fraud) | – (cannot see collusion) | P/D (independent mgr may spot) | D (audit may spot) | D (mgr+audit independent) |
| 4 | Emergency: real need but timing pressure tempts bypass of controls | – (still runs) | P (mgr before settlement) | D/C (audit+recall) | P/D/C (explicit emergency conditions) |
| 5 | External account compromise after legitimate vendor bank change | P (bank-change <7 d) | D (mgr may notice) | D/C (audit & recall) | P/D/C |
| 6 | Override abuse of automated warning | – (override possible) | D (override appears in mgr review) | D/C (audit) | P/D/C (override forbidden in ED-1) |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test  

We treat a compensating package as:

• Fully Equivalent – All FEP-1 objectives are met with **at least comparable** assurance on six axes:  
1. Timing (independent review occurs before irrevocable settlement);  
2. Independence (reviewer not the preparer/releaser, no conflict);  
3. Coverage (invoice authenticity, bank ownership, amount reasonableness, duplicate & sanctions);  
4. Overrideability (no single actor can suppress the control un-logged);  
5. Recoverability (defect likely detected while recall window is open);  
6. Evidence Quality (review leaves audit trail suitable for accountability).  

• Partially Equivalent – Same six axes satisfied except one dimension weakened but residual risk still **low to moderate** and well understood (e.g., audit just at 25 h, or AV-2 pass relied on but goods receipt not yet verified).

• Merely Mitigating – Multiple axes weakened so residual risk remains material but lower than with no control.

• Ineffective – Any axis so weak that major threat scenarios are left essentially uncontrolled.

Same expected-loss is NOT the only standard because internal control also pursues:  
a) deterrence through segregation,  
b) accountability,  
c) ability to perform forensic reconstruction, and  
d) compliance with policy and external audit expectations—elements that cannot be captured by pure loss expectancy.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison  

| Control | Acting point | Nature | Why |
|---------|--------------|--------|-----|
| AV-2 automatic “full pass” | Before Release_Payment button enabled | Preventive | Blocks or warns prior to authorization |
| AV-2 warning override | Same moment | Preventive IF independent, else risk-increasing | Because same user can suppress – becomes negative control |
| MG-4 before Release_Payment | Preventive | Second human stops bad payment before authorization |
| MG-4 after Release but before bank settlement | Hybrid: detective (because release already happened) and corrective (can still stop or recall) | Timing allows stop/recall but not ideal prevention |
| MG-4 after settlement | Detective only | Cannot prevent; may support recovery or discipline |
| RA-7 within 24 h | Detective + partially corrective | Bank recall still feasible |
| RA-7 >24 h | Detective | Recovery unlikely |
| ED-1 token + mgr + audit (whole package) | Preventive (token & mgr), Detective (audit), Corrective (recall window) | Designed to emulate segregation under emergency |

## 5. Counterfactual and Residual-Risk Analysis  

1. AP-201  
   • Plausible loss: preparer could have altered payee account.  
   • Broken by: independent mgr pre-settlement review of bank details.  
   • Residual: mgr might rubber-stamp; no recall after 24 h.  
   • With true second releaser: single-user convenience gone, deterrence stronger; risk further reduced.

2. AP-202  
   • Loss: collusive fraud using new vendor and bank-change warning.  
   • Control that failed: override credential not independent; mgr late.  
   • Residual: funds gone.  
   • With independent releaser, same user could not override; fraud probability sharply lower; expected loss materiality drops from very-high to low-medium.

3. AP-203  
   • Loss: none observed, but emergency could hide fake repair.  
   • Broken by: mgr review of service report before release; audit same day.  
   • Residual: low (collusion between u31 & m02 would be required).  
   • Extra independent releaser would add little incremental risk reduction; ED-1 seems adequate.

4. AP-204  
   • Loss: colluding two-person fraud, fabricated consulting services.  
   • Control that failed: MG-4 absent before settlement; AV-2 can’t see collusion.  
   • Residual: full amount likely unrecoverable.  
   • Independent second releaser from different team could have noticed missing deliverables; probability of success of fraud much lower.

5. AP-206  
   • Loss: possibility that token was back-dated to justify convenience.  
   • Control: mgr independent approval and next-day audit.  
   • Residual: moderate; if token illegitimate, objective not met.  
   • With separated releaser, emergency token would be unnecessary; residual risk low.

6. AP-208  
   • Loss: potential misdirection to wrong bank account while AV-2 offline.  
   • Nothing stopped it before settlement.  
   • Residual: high but luckily no actual loss observed yet.  
   • True four-eyes or functioning AV-2 would probably have demanded proof of bank ownership, lowering risk materially.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations  

Alerts (not proofs):  

• Same-user Prepare + Release rate >0.5 % of payments outside ED-1 scope.  
• AV-2 warning override rate by same user that prepared.  
• Manager approvals timestamped after bank settlement.  
• RA-7 audits started >24 h after same-user payment.  
• Repeated preparer–releaser pairs 25 times/month on same workstation.  
• Payments with bank-account change 7 d but no AV-2 block.  
• Payments executed while AV-2 “Service_Unavailable”.  
• Emergency token used on payments >EUR 100k or to vendors <180 d.  

Evidence collection:  
1. Cryptographic link between emergency token and Prepare_Payment event to prove ordering.  
2. Secure credential management preventing supervisors from sharing override passwords.  
3. Workstation logs to confirm two distinct human beings in four-eyes cases.  
4. Automatic flag to audit when MG-4 approval occurs after settlement.  
5. Persistent store of vendor-bank-ownership documents accessible to MG-4 review.
