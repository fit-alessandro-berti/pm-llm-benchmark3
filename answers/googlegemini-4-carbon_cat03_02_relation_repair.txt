## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

Repairing an incomplete OCEL 2.0 log requires separating structural schema constraints from defeasible operational patterns. Treating soft business expectations as axioms risks erasing genuine exceptions—such as unapplied cash, scanner outages, or partial portal confirmations—that process mining is supposed to surface. We organize our abductive repair logic into four tiers:

### Hard invariants
1. **Closed-world entity reference:** Repairs may only link existing event IDs (`ev01`–`ev14`) and existing object IDs; no synthetic events or objects are fabricated.
2. **Directional O2O typing (Rule 8):** O2O qualifiers carry strict domain-to-range types (`Order contains Item`, `Package packs Item`, `Delivery carries Package`, `Invoice bills Order`, `Payment settles Invoice`). Reversed edges such as `PK-8 carries DEL-Y` are syntactically invalid.
3. **Referential uniqueness:** No duplicate `(ocel:eid, ocel:oid, ocel:qualifier)` or `(ocel:oid, ocel:oid_2, ocel:qualifier)` tuples may exist.
4. **Causal monotonicity:** An event cannot attach to an object in a manner that contradicts established creation order (e.g., packing an item prior to `Create Order`).

### Strong defaults
1. **Activity–object participation templates (Rules 1–4, 7):** `Create Order` binds one `Order`; `Add Item` binds one `Order` and one `Item`; `Pack Item` binds one `Item` and one `Package` unless disrupted by a scanner outage; `Dispatch Package` binds at least one `Package` and one `Delivery`; `Record Payment` binds one `Payment` and typically one `Invoice`.
2. **E2O–O2O structural coherence:** When an operational event establishes a persistent relationship (e.g., `Pack Item` placing an item into a package, or `Issue Invoice` billing an order), the corresponding E2O and static O2O edges should normally agree unless the log represents an explicitly un-reconciled ledger state.
3. **Physical routing and containment consistency:** Packages assigned to a delivery should match its `route` (`NORTH`), and package `gross_weight` should remain plausible for the contained SKUs.

### Weak heuristics
1. **Identifier and sequence similarity (Rule 9):** Sequential scanner tags (`PK8-1` at `09:04` followed by `PK8-2` at `09:05`), sequential bank references (`B-771`, `B-772`), uncertain strings (`N-421?`), and masked prefixes (`N-42*`) provide directional clues but cannot establish identity alone.
2. **Arithmetic sum matching (Rule 6):** `ORD-A` (`130`) + `ORD-B` (`100`) = `INV-44` (`230`), and `PAY-1` (`100`) + `PAY-2` (`130`) = `230`. Arithmetic equality raises posterior plausibility for consolidated billing and split settlement, but taxes, fragile-glass handling surcharges, or coincidental balances mean arithmetic is corroborative evidence rather than proof.
3. **Shared customer context:** `ORD-A`, `ORD-B`, `INV-44`, and `ev11` all reference `C17`, increasing the prior probability of warehouse co-packing and consolidated invoicing.

### Evidence against a repair
1. **Explicit uncertainty markers:** The trailing question mark in `external_ref=N-421?` (`ev10`) and wildcard in `N-42*` (`ev11`) actively warn against treating string similarity as clean foreign keys.
2. **Known exception pathways (Rules 3, 5, 7):** Because portal carrier loss (`ev11`), unapplied cash (`ev14`), and fee-adjusted single-order invoices (`ev12`) are recognized domain behaviors, forcing standard edges suppresses real exception variants.
3. **Lifecycle stage mismatch:** Attaching `ORD-B` to `Issue Invoice` (`ev12` at `15:00`) is weakened by the absence of any delivery confirmation for `ORD-B` at `14:00`.
4. **Type-Jumping (transitive flattening):** Attaching `Order` directly to `Pack Item` (`ev08`) or `Package` directly to `Confirm Delivery` (`ev11`) violates observed event schemas (`ev06`, `ev07`).

### Evidence synthesis model
We score each candidate edge on $[0, 1]$ by combining independent feature channels: schema expectation + temporal adjacency + attribute/ID alignment + physical/customer compatibility, penalized by ambiguity markers and legitimate exception explanations. Critically, **E2O and O2O hypotheses derived from the same missing event are scored together rather than treated as independent confirmations**. Candidates scoring $\ge 0.80$ with low structural distortion receive `accept`; plausible multi-signal candidates ($0.50\text{–}0.79$) with viable non-link explanations receive `provisional`; genuinely underdetermined candidates ($0.30\text{–}0.49$) remain `unresolved`; schema violations or weakly supported temptations ($< 0.30$) receive `reject`.

- **`PAY-2` and `INV-44`:** Links `PAY-2` (`130`) to `INV-44`, settling `ORD-A`'s `230` invoice across two tranches (`100` surcharge/deposit + `130` principal).
- **Holistic coherence:** Bundle C demonstrates why arithmetic matching is non-decisive: it achieves coherent logistics and ledger lifecycles without forcing premature billing on an unconfirmed order (`ORD-B`).

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

Auditing **Bundle A** across all six object types highlights what is resolved, what remains intentionally open, and where circular reasoning must be avoided:

- **Order (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`):** `ORD-A` exhibits a complete 5-event commercial lifecycle (`Create Order -> Add Item -> Add Item -> Confirm Delivery -> Issue Invoice`) backed by independent E2O (`ev03`, `ev04`, `ev12`) and O2O (`contains`, `bills`) records. `ORD-B` stops after `Create Order -> Add Item` (`ev02`, `ev05`). Under Bundle A, this truncated trace is plausible and non-anomalous: its physical item (`IT-B1`) enters warehouse packing, while commercial confirmation and billing have either not occurred by `16:00` or failed to bind. One-to-many containment (`ORD-A` to two items) is standard.
- **Item (`IT-A1`, `IT-A2`, `IT-B1`):** All three items follow a clean two-step lifecycle (`Add Item -> Pack Item`, cardinality `1:1` per activity). For `IT-A1` and `IT-A2`, E2O and observed O2O (`contains`, `packs`) both exist in the raw data. For `IT-B1`, `ORD-B contains IT-B1` is observed, whereas `PK-8 packs IT-B1` is added alongside `ev08 -> PK-8`.
- **Package (`PK-7`, `PK-8`):** Both packages progress cleanly from `Pack Item` to `Dispatch Package`. Repairing `ev08 -> PK-8` shifts `PK-8` from one `Pack Item` event to two (`ev07`, `ev08`), creating a **many-to-many indirect relationship between `Order` and `Package`**: `ORD-A` spans `PK-7` and `PK-8` (split shipment), while `PK-8` consolidates items from `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` (warehouse co-packing). This many-to-many structure is operationally natural for same-customer (`C17`), same-route (`NORTH`) orders created two minutes apart.
- **Delivery (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`):** `DEL-X` participates only in `ev09` (`Dispatch Package`) and carries `PK-7` in both E2O and O2O. **`DEL-Y` remains a complete orphan object** (zero E2O events, zero O2O links), while `ev10` remains a partial-binding event (`PK-8` without a `Delivery`). Preserving `DEL-Y` as an explicit orphan accurately reflects the unresolved `?` in `ev10` rather than disguising data uncertainty.
- **Invoice (`INV-44`):** Follows `Issue Invoice (ev12) -> Record Payment (ev13)`, linked to `ORD-A` and `PAY-1` in both E2O and O2O (`1:1` observed, partial settlement of `100` against `230` open balance).
- **Payment (`PAY-1`, `PAY-2`):** Both have valid single-event lifecycles (`Record Payment`). `PAY-1` is supported by both `ev13 -> INV-44` and `PAY-1 settles INV-44`. `PAY-2` participates in `ev14` without an `Invoice` link (not an orphan event since `PAY-2` is bound, representing unapplied cash).

### Explicit check for circular reasoning
A major methodological trap here is inferring `PK-8 packs IT-B1` (`P02`) from `ev08 -> PK-8` (`P01`) and then claiming that `PK-8 packs IT-B1` provides independent O2O confirmation for `ev08 -> PK-8` (or doing the same with `INV-44 bills ORD-B` and `ev12 -> ORD-B`). **In Bundle A, `P01` and `P02` are explicitly recognized as non-independent projections of a single abductive hypothesis.** Even for pre-existing observed O2O rows (like `INV-44 bills ORD-A`), O2O tables in ERP exports are frequently materialized from the same foreign-key column that populates E2O relations, meaning E2O/O2O agreement verifies extraction consistency rather than independent physical ground truth.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

Choosing between the unrepaired log, Bundle A, and Bundle B substantially alters discovered models and metrics:

1. **OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph):**
   - *Unrepaired:* `PK-8` follows `Pack Item (ev07) -> Dispatch Package (ev10)`; `DEL-X` is a single-node trace (`ev09`); `DEL-Y` is invisible; `ORD-B` terminates at `Add Item (ev05)`.
   - *Bundle A:* Attaching `ev08 -> PK-8` changes `PK-8`'s trace to `ev07 -> ev08 -> ev10`, introducing a **`Pack Item -> Pack Item` self-loop** on the `Package` lifecycle while leaving `Order`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice` control-flow untouched.
   - *Bundle B:* Adds three major control-flow structures: (i) **`Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery`** appears for `Delivery` (weight 2, via `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`); (ii) **`Add Item -> Issue Invoice`** appears on `Order` via `ORD-B` (`ev05 -> ev12`), creating a false bypass around `Confirm Delivery`; and (iii) **`Record Payment -> Record Payment`** appears as a self-loop on `Invoice` (`ev13 -> ev14`).

2. **OTG (Object Type Graph):**
   - *Unrepaired & Bundle A:* Identical type-level topology: `Order–Item`, `Item–Package`, `Package–Delivery`, `Order–Invoice`, and `Invoice–Payment`. `P01`/`P02` only increase the weight of `Item–Package` without adding new type edges. Notably, `Order` and `Delivery` have no direct edge.
   - *Bundle B:* Linking `ev11` (`ORD-A`) to `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y` introduces a **new `Order–Delivery` edge** in the OTG.

3. **ET–OT graph (Event-Type to Object-Type cardinalities):**
   - *Unrepaired:* Optional (`0..1`) cardinalities appear on `Pack Item -> Package` (`ev08`), `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` (`ev10`), and `Record Payment -> Invoice` (`ev14`); `Confirm Delivery` links solely to `Order` (`1..1`).
   - *Bundle A:* Tightens `Pack Item -> Package` to strict `1..1`, leaving other optionalities intact as true data/process anomalies.
   - *Bundle B:* Tightens `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` and `Record Payment -> Invoice` to `1..1`, adds a **new `Confirm Delivery -> Delivery` (`1..*`) arc**, and widens `Issue Invoice -> Order` from `1..1` to **`1..*`**, shifting both activities into batch/convergence operators.

4. **Flattenings across object types:**
   - *Order:* Unrepaired and Bundle A produce `[ev01, ev03, ev04, ev11, ev12]` (`ORD-A`) and `[ev02, ev05]` (`ORD-B`). Bundle B appends `ev12` to `ORD-B`, causing **convergence duplication** of `Issue Invoice` across both flattened traces.
   - *Package:* Unrepaired drops `ev08` entirely (deficiency). Both Bundle A and Bundle B recover `ev08` inside `PK-8` (`[ev07, ev08, ev10]`).
   - *Delivery:* Unrepaired and Bundle A yield one 1-event trace (`DEL-X`: `[ev09]`), dropping `DEL-Y`, `ev10`, and `ev11`. Bundle B yields two 2-event traces (`[ev09, ev11]`, `[ev10, ev11]`), recovering `DEL-Y` and `ev10` but **duplicating `ev11` (`Confirm Delivery`)**.
   - *Invoice:* Unrepaired and Bundle A yield `[ev12, ev13]`; Bundle B yields `[ev12, ev13, ev14]`.
   - *Payment:* Identical across all three (`[ev13]` and `[ev14]`), illustrating that flattening on `Payment` is completely blind to whether `PAY-2` settles `INV-44`.

5. **Activity frequencies (events vs. unique objects vs. total object-event bindings):**
   - For `Pack Item`: event count is `3` everywhere; unique `Package` count is `2` everywhere (`PK-7`, `PK-8`); **total `Package` participations rise from `2` (Unrepaired) to `3` (Bundles A and B)** because `PK-8` binds twice (`ev07`, `ev08`).
   - For `Record Payment`: event count is `2` everywhere; unique `Invoice` count stays `1` (`INV-44`) in both Bundle A and Bundle B, whereas **total `Invoice` participations double from `1` (Unrepaired/Bundle A) to `2` (Bundle B)**.
   - For `Issue Invoice` (`Order` bindings) and `Confirm Delivery` (`Delivery` bindings), event count stays `1`, while unique and total object counts jump from `1 -> 2` and `0 -> 2` respectively under Bundle B.

6. **Equivalent-execution clustering around `Order`:**
   - *Unrepaired:* `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` form **two disconnected object components** (`ORD-A` with 8 objects; `ORD-B` with 2 objects), plus isolated `DEL-Y` and `PAY-2`, yielding two distinct order-execution clusters.
   - *Bundle A:* Adding `PK-8` to `ev08`/`IT-B1` **bridges `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` into a single connected component** (`ORD-A — IT-A2 — PK-8 — IT-B1 — ORD-B`). Connected-component execution extraction now merges both orders into one coupled multi-order execution (leaving `DEL-Y` and `PAY-2` outside).
   - *Bundle B:* Doubly fuses `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` (physically via `PK-8` and financially via `INV-44`) and absorbs `DEL-Y` and `PAY-2` into a single 12-object super-execution with no equivalent peer cluster.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

Aggressive "completion-driven" repair can manufacture five concrete forms of false process behavior in this dataset:

1. **Fabricating skipped-delivery billing shortcuts (`ev12 -> ORD-B` without `ev11 -> ORD-B`):** Linking `ORD-B` to `INV-44` solely because `130 + 100 = 230` creates the `Order` path `Add Item (ev05) -> Issue Invoice (ev12)`. Conformance checking will then flag a grave "invoice-before-delivery" compliance violation that may be purely an artifact of misreading a single-order `230` invoice (`ORD-A` plus fragile-glass/express fees).
2. **Erasing unapplied-cash bottlenecks (`ev14 -> INV-44`):** In accounts receivable, cash arriving without remittance advice (`ev14`, `PAY-2`) sits in an unapplied suspense queue until manual reconciliation. Repairing `ev14 -> INV-44` converts an open exception into an instant 30-minute split settlement, hiding real working-capital friction and inflating invoice straight-through-processing metrics.
3. **Inventing carrier SLA compliance from degraded portal pings (`ev11 -> DEL-X, DEL-Y`):** Attaching both deliveries to `ev11` creates two exact 4.0-hour `Dispatch Package -> Confirm Delivery` lead times (`10:00` to `14:00`). If `DEL-Y` was actually delayed (`N-421?`) and the portal merely logged `ORD-A` with a masked prefix, over-repairing hides a late shipment and halves apparent carrier variance.
4. **Polluting event schemas via transitive O2O propagation (`ev08 -> ORD-B` or `ev09 -> ORD-A`):** Mechanically enriching E2O relations from O2O links pushes `Order` onto `Pack Item` and `Dispatch Package`. Flattening on `Order` will then show `ORD-A` executing two parallel `Pack Item` steps while others do not, breaking schema symmetry with observed events (`ev06`, `ev07`).
5. **Collapsing independent cases into false batch executions (`ev11 -> ORD-B` + `ev12 -> ORD-B`):** Forcing `ORD-B` onto both `ev11` and `ev12` to mirror `ORD-A` turns two distinct customer orders into a synchronized batch process, causing flattened `Order` logs to double-count both `Confirm Delivery` and `Issue Invoice` (convergence inflation).
