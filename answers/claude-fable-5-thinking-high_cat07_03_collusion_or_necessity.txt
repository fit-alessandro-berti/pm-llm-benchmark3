# 2. Benign alternative explanations

**Pattern: Buyer_Carla ↔ Approver_Gita (and Buyer_Dan ↔ Manager_Hugo) fast emergency approvals and four-eyes hits**

1. *Designed emergency fast lane / informal specialization.* Carla is documented as specialized in emergency medical supplies and Gita as the delegated approver for urgent cases; 88% of their joint cases carry emergency_flag=True with low vendor concentration. Supporting evidence: a written delegation-of-authority policy naming Gita for urgent approvals; emergency requests originating from clinical/facilities systems with independent timestamps; diverse requesters and vendors. Refuting evidence: emergency flags set by Carla herself without an upstream trigger; the same vendors recurring across "emergencies"; approvals outside the documented emergency scope.
2. *Shared-account attribution error.* 23 emergency approvals are recorded under the shared account "Approver_Gita" although entered_by shows assistants; some apparent same-person Create+Approve violations may actually involve two different humans. Supporting evidence: entered_by / approved_by fields diverging from org:resource; access logs showing different workstation or user sessions. Refuting evidence: entered_by matching the same individual as the creator; session logs confirming a single person performed both steps.

**Pattern: same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification (17 cases)**

1. *Batch data migration artifact.* 12 of 17 justifications have identical to-the-minute timestamps and came from a quarter-close spreadsheet upload; the justification may have been written contemporaneously on paper/spreadsheet by a different person and merely uploaded under one account. Supporting evidence: the original spreadsheet with author metadata and real dates; email trails showing timely justification. Refuting evidence: no underlying contemporaneous records; justification text written after the fact by the overriding manager.
2. *Understaffed exception handling.* Weekend and quarter-end periods may leave only one authorized person available, so the same manager both overrides and (late) documents. Supporting evidence: staffing rosters showing no second approver on those dates; the pattern clustering on weekends/quarter-end. Refuting evidence: other qualified approvers demonstrably active in the system at the same time.

**Pattern: VendorMaster_Lena ↔ AP_Kim, vendor family V-77x, fast bank-change-to-payment, manual uploads**

1. *Legitimate mass bank-maintenance after the vendor's platform migration.* V-77x changed banking platforms; several bank-detail changes were valid mass-maintenance operations whose ticket linkage is missing from the log. The 17-minute gap could reflect a coordinated cleanup session (fix details, immediately release the blocked payments). Supporting evidence: the maintenance ticket(s) covering the affected vendor records with matching dates; identical new bank details across the V-77x family matching the vendor's official notification; vendor confirmation letters. Refuting evidence: bank accounts differing across the family or not matching the vendor's official notice; changes outside the maintenance window; accounts traceable to unrelated beneficiaries.
2. *Role design and workload routing.* AP_Kim handles higher-risk suppliers by design and Lena owns bank changes, so their co-occurrence is expected; manual_upload may be the standard channel for this vendor family's legacy interface. Supporting evidence: documented role assignments and routing rules; manual_upload used for V-77x before the anomalous period as well. Refuting evidence: manual uploads used only for these 16 cases; other AP staff handling comparable V-77x payments through the normal system.

**Pattern: subcontracting ping-pong loops (Gita↔Carla, Hugo↔Dan, Lena↔Kim)**

1. *Clarification/correction loops.* The routine pairs Aline/Elena (33 loops) and Boris/Felix (30 loops) show almost the same loop frequency on unambiguously routine work, indicating loops are a normal feature of this process (approval comments, corrections). Supporting evidence: change comments or rejection reasons attached to the looped events; content differences between the first and second PO version. Refuting evidence: loops with no recorded change to the case, i.e., pure re-submission and instant re-approval.
2. *Emergency clarification under time pressure.* For the emergency pairs, a quick question-and-confirm cycle by phone with system entries following would produce exactly this loop shape. Supporting evidence: call/chat records; loop durations of minutes during emergencies. Refuting evidence: loops on non-emergency cases with high amounts and no documentation.

**Pattern: fast approvals generally**

1. *Automation adjacency.* Bot_Match→AP_Kim (6 min median) shows that queue-driven work produces short human response times; short latency after a system trigger is normal in exception queues. Supporting evidence: work-queue logs showing the item arrived at the top of the queue. Refuting evidence: approvals faster than the minimum plausible review time for documents of that size, or approvals issued outside working hours from unusual locations.
2. *Pre-negotiated framework contracts.* Where contract_type indicates a framework/call-off agreement, approval is a formality and speed is expected. Supporting evidence: contract_type values; pre-approved catalog items. Refuting evidence: fast approvals on free-text, non-catalog, high-amount POs.

# 3. Case prioritization

**High priority**

- *Cluster H1: the 9 Change Vendor Bank Details + Payment Approval four-eyes violations (VendorMaster_Lena / AP_Kim, vendor family V-77x), plus the 16 fast (17-min) bank-change-to-payment cases with source_system="manual_upload".* Reason: this combines the highest-impact control breach (payment diversion vector), high amounts (68% high-band), vendor concentration (72% V-77x), extreme timing, and a low emergency share that removes the urgency explanation. The missing maintenance-ticket linkage means the benign explanation is currently unverifiable — which is precisely why these cases must be checked first, not why they should be presumed fraudulent. Inspect first: the vendor master change documents and bank-account numbers before/after each change; the vendor's official bank-change notification and the banking-platform migration ticket; payment beneficiary account details vs. vendor legal entity; approval workflow logs and user session/access logs for Lena and Kim on those dates; the manual_upload source files and their authors.
- *Cluster H2: the 5 same-person Emergency Override + Retrospective Justification cases that were NOT part of the quarter-close batch upload.* Reason: for these, the data-quality explanation does not apply, and the compensating control for the emergency exception was genuinely absent. Inspect first: the justification text and its true authorship (entered_by, document metadata), the underlying emergency evidence (repair tickets, medical-supply requests), and whether a second approver was available at the time (staffing rosters, system activity logs).

**Medium priority**

- *Cluster M1: the 42 Create PO + Approve PO four-eyes violations, corrected for the shared-account and legacy_erp bot-mapping errors.* Reason: many will likely dissolve once org:resource is reconciled with entered_by/approved_by; the residual genuine same-person cases, especially any that are high-amount and not emergency-flagged, warrant document review. Inspect first: entered_by vs approved_by vs org:resource for each case; legacy_erp mapping table corrections; emergency_flag provenance (who set it and when); PO documents for the non-emergency residual.
- *Cluster M2: the 12 batch-uploaded retrospective justifications (quarter_close_upload).* Reason: probably a documentation-timeliness and data-migration problem rather than misconduct, but the 3-working-day rule was formally breached and the original records must be located. Inspect first: the source spreadsheet with author and date metadata; email or ticket trails proving contemporaneous justification.
- *Cluster M3: high-amount, non-emergency cases within the Carla→Gita fast lane (the ~12% of their 126 cases without emergency_flag, intersected with the 42% high-amount share).* Reason: the fast lane is legitimate for emergencies; its use for non-emergency high-value purchases would be scope creep of the exception channel. Inspect first: business justification on the PO, requester identity, and vendor diversity.

**Low priority / monitoring only**

- *Cluster L1: Buyer_Aline→Approver_Elena and Buyer_Boris→Approver_Felix routine pairs (510/495 cases) including their correction loops.* Reason: high co-working frequency reflects team structure; low emergency, low amounts, low manual share, ~1% late justification. Monitor via periodic four-eyes and timing dashboards only.
- *Cluster L2: Bot_Match→AP_Kim exception queue (210 cases) and Bot_PO volumes.* Reason: automation adjacency explains the timing; monitor for drift after the legacy_erp mapping fix.
- *Cluster L3: Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by same person (31 cases, Lena/Marc).* Reason: flagged as possibly reflecting role design rather than a four-eyes rule; resolve as a control-design question (should these duties be segregated?) rather than a case-by-case investigation.
- *Cluster L4: Temp_Nina→Buyer_Carla intake pattern.* Reason: consistent with end-of-quarter data-entry support; verify Nina's access rights are limited to data entry, then monitor.

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Evidence that would increase concern**

- Bank-account numbers introduced by the V-77x Change Vendor Bank Details events that do not match the vendor's official bank-migration notification, differ across the vendor family, or resolve to accounts linked to an employee or a related party.
- Absence of any banking-platform maintenance ticket covering the changed vendor records, or a ticket whose dates/scope do not match the changes in the log.
- Payments in the Lena/Kim cluster released to the new bank details and then the details reverted shortly afterwards (change–pay–revert pattern).
- Access logs showing VendorMaster_Lena and AP_Kim sessions from the same workstation/IP, credential sharing, or activity outside their normal working patterns coinciding with the 16 fast manual_upload cases.
- entered_by evidence confirming that the same physical person truly performed both Create PO and Approve PO in the residual (non-artifact) four-eyes cases, especially on high-amount, non-emergency POs.
- emergency_flag values set by the buyer themselves with no upstream requisition, ticket, or clinical/facilities system record substantiating the urgency.
- Retrospective Justification texts that are templated/identical, authored by the overriding manager, or created only after the audit was announced (file metadata, document versioning).
- Goods Receipt or Invoice Match anomalies in the prioritized clusters: missing receipts, quantity/price mismatches waived, or invoices dated before the PO.
- Personal-relationship or conflict-of-interest indicators between the involved employees and V-77x principals (declarations register, vendor master contact data).

**Evidence that would reduce concern**

- The V-77x maintenance ticket, vendor bank-change letters on vendor letterhead, and bank confirmation matching every changed account — this would largely close cluster H1.
- The original quarter-close spreadsheet showing that the 12 batch justifications were written within 3 working days by a person different from the overrider, with author metadata and dates intact.
- A corrected legacy_erp mapping table and shared-account reconciliation showing that most of the 42 Create+Approve violations involve two distinct humans (entered_by ≠ approved_by).
- A written delegation-of-authority and emergency-procedure document naming Approver_Gita and Manager_Hugo for urgent approvals, plus staffing rosters showing no alternative approver was available on the weekend/quarter-end dates.
- Upstream, independently timestamped emergency triggers (medical-supply requests, facilities incident tickets) for the Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo fast-lane cases.
- Demonstration that manual_upload is the long-standing standard interface for the V-77x vendor family (historical logs before the review period showing the same channel with unremarkable timing).
- Loop events (Gita→Carla→Gita etc.) carrying substantive change comments or document deltas, matching the pattern seen in the routine Aline/Elena loops.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Our review of the procurement-to-payment event log identified several control exceptions and statistical anomalies. None of the evidence establishes misconduct; the findings are control exceptions and data patterns that require targeted verification.

The most concerning subgroup is the small cluster of cases involving vendor family V-77x, in which the same resource pair changed vendor bank details and approved the related payment, with intervals as short as 17 minutes and predominantly via manual upload outside the standard system. These cases combine a segregation-of-duties exception, high payment amounts, vendor concentration, and reduced system-enforced traceability. A plausible benign explanation exists — the vendor family underwent a banking-platform migration involving legitimate mass maintenance — but the linkage between the maintenance ticket and the affected cases is missing from the log and must be re-established. We recommend verifying the changed bank accounts against the vendor's official notification, reviewing the manual-upload source files, and examining user access logs for the affected dates.

Separately, seventeen emergency overrides were retrospectively justified by the same person who performed the override; twelve of these appear to be artifacts of a quarter-close spreadsheet migration, and the remaining five should be substantiated with underlying documentation. Fast approvals in the emergency channels for medical supplies and facilities repairs are largely consistent with the designed rush process and are recommended for monitoring rather than investigation.

Data-quality limitations materially affect attribution: a shared approver account, a bot-to-human mapping error in the legacy ERP, and batch-uploaded timestamps each inflate apparent same-person violations. These should be corrected before any conclusions about individual conduct are drawn.
