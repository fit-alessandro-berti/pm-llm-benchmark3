# 1. Assumptions and notation

- **A1 (Event-Order Convention):** For simultaneous rounded events (e.g., a case completing S1 at the exact minute another case arrives at S2 or starts S1), departures from a queue and arrivals to a station are processed in chronological order of internal case identifiers (P01 to P22) when timestamps are identical.
- **A2 (Rounding Treatment):** All timestamps are rounded to the nearest 5 minutes. The true underlying continuous timestamp $t_{\text{true}}$ for any reported timestamp $t$ lies in the closed interval $[t - 2.5\text{ min}, t + 2.5\text{ min}]$. 
- **A3 (Capacity & Workstation Continuity):** S1 operates with two single-task resources ($I1, I2$); S2 operates with two single-task resources ($C1, C2$). No resource can execute more than one case simultaneously. Preemption is not permitted. Cases flow strictly from S1 to S2.
- **A4 (C2 Maintenance Outage):** Resource $C2$ is unavailable from 10:55 to 11:10. No case can start service on $C2$ during this 15-minute window.
- **A5 (Imputation Bounds for MISSING Starts):** When an activity start timestamp is marked `MISSING`, the start time is bounded below by the maximum of: (a) the completion of the previous case on the same resource, (b) the arrival of the case at that station (for S1: `arrival_s1`; for S2: completion of S1), and (c) lower rounding limit ($t - 2.5$). It is bounded above by the recorded end timestamp minus the minimum allowable service time (1 minute) or upper rounding limit ($t + 2.5$). Central estimates use the midpoint of the feasible start window, adjusted for rounding compatibility.
- **A6 (Censoring for OPEN Activities):** For activities marked `OPEN` at 14:00 (e.g., P21 in S1; P15, P17 in S2), service is right-censored at 14:00. Elapsed service is measured from start to 14:00; final service time is unobserved and bounded below by elapsed service.
- **A7 (Waiting vs. Service Semantics):** S1 waiting time begins at external arrival (`arrival_s1`) and ends at `s1_start`. S2 waiting time begins at `s1_end` and ends at `s2_start`.

---

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

- **S1 Waiting Time:** $W_{S1} = t_{\text{s1\_start}} - t_{\text{arrival\_s1}}$.
- **S1 Active Service Time:** $S_{S1} = t_{\text{s1\_end}} - t_{\text{s1\_start}}$.
- **S2 Waiting Time:** $W_{S2} = t_{\text{s2\_start}} - t_{\text{s1\_end}}$.
- **S2 Active Service Time:** $S_{S2} = t_{\text{s2\_end}} - t_{\text{s2\_start}}$.
- **Case Total Sojourn Time:** $T_{\text{sojourn}} = t_{\text{s2\_end}} - t_{\text{arrival\_s1}}$ (for fully completed cases).
- **WIP & Queue Length:** Evaluated via time-interval expansion. A case contributes 1 to station queue length during $W$, and 1 to station active service WIP during $S$. Total WIP is the sum across S1 and S2 queues and active services. Simultaneous events at boundaries use a right-open interval convention $[t_{\text{start}}, t_{\text{end}})$.
- **Resource Utilization:** Total busy time of a resource divided by the observation span (08:00 to 14:00 = 360 minutes, or active operational window minus maintenance). Lower, central, and upper utilization bounds are derived from the bounds on start times and service durations.
- **Little's Law Consistency:** Checked over steady-state subperiods (e.g., 09:00–12:00) where arrival rate ($\lambda$), mean sojourn time ($W$), and mean WIP ($L$) satisfy $L = \lambda W$.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

- **Early Morning (08:00 – 10:00):** Balanced flow. S1 and S2 operate with minor queues. Utilization is moderate (~60-70%).
- **Late Morning / Maintenance Window (10:00 – 11:30):** S2 experiences a severe capacity constraint due to the scheduled maintenance of resource C2 (10:55–11:10), coinciding with a rising arrival surge. Cases completing S1 back up into the S2 queue. 
- **Afternoon (11:30 – 14:00):** S2 becomes the definitive bottleneck. While S1 maintains a steady processing rate, cases accumulate rapidly in the S2 waiting queue (P16, P18, P19, P20 waiting at 14:00; P15, P17 open in S2). The bottleneck shifts decisively from a balanced state to S2-bound starvation of downstream capacity.

---

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

1. **Optimistic Imputation:** Assumes missing starts occurred at the earliest possible bound (immediate resource availability upon prior task completion) and open cases required minimal additional service (0 minutes past observation). Results in lower bound waiting times and higher throughput estimates.
2. **Central Imputation (Base Case):** Uses midpoint intervals adjusted for rounding and resource contention constraints. Results are reported in Section 3 and 4 tables.
3. **Pessimistic Imputation:** Assumes missing starts occurred at the latest possible bound (just before recorded end minus minimum service) and open cases experience long tails. Results in upper bound waiting times and heightened WIP estimates.
- **Stable Conclusions:** S2 is indisputably overloaded by 12:00 onwards; C2 maintenance acts as a tipping point; S1 queue length grows moderately but remains manageable compared to the S2 queue explosion.
- **Unstable Conclusions:** Exact mean waiting times for P07, P11, and P15 vary by up to 15 minutes across scenarios, directly affecting precise hourly utilization figures during the 11:00–12:00 transition.
