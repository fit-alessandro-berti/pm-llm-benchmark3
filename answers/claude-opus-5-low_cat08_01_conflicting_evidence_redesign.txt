# Warranty Claim Process: Diagnosis and Three Competing Redesigns

## 1. Current-process diagnosis

### 1.1 What the artifacts actually say

**Artifact 1 — the frequency DFG shows a two-speed process with a dominant auto lane.**
72% of cases (3600/5000) enter `Auto eligibility screen`, and 2550 of those go straight to `Fast approval`, of which 2300 go to `Dispatch replacement`. So roughly 46% of all volume is a single four-step highway: Intake → Auto screen → Fast approval → Dispatch → Close. This is the "most frequent path" that looks attractive. Only 260 + 310 = 570 cases ever reach `Repair quote`, and only ~390 devices are actually repaired. The process is, in practice, a *replacement* process with a small repair appendix, not the balanced intake→assess→decide→fulfil model in the normative description.

**Artifact 2 — the performance DFG shows that the cost of the process is concentrated in evidence loops, not in the review step itself.**
`Manual eligibility review → Request missing evidence` is mean 14h, and the return edge `Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review` is mean 34h / median 26h / p90 88h. That single waiting edge is the largest routine delay in the model, and it is *customer* wait, not reviewer work. Compare with `Manual eligibility review → Repair quote` at mean 22h and `Auto eligibility screen → Fast approval` at mean 1.0h. The manual lane is not intrinsically slow because humans are slow; it is slow because it hands the ball back to the customer and waits.

**Artifact 3 — the reopen edges are the true long tail.**
`Fast approval → Customer complaint/reopen` is mean 72h / p90 150h, and `Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen` is mean 96h / median 70h / p90 180h. Then `Customer complaint/reopen → Manual eligibility review` adds another mean 40h, and the escalation route adds mean 54h on top of that. A reopened fast-path case therefore accumulates something on the order of 100–250+ hours of elapsed time *after* the process already recorded a fast, cheap completion. Any speed measurement taken at first `Quality/compliance close` is measuring an artefact of when the clock was stopped, not when the customer's problem was solved.

**Artifact 4 — conformance replay shows the fast lane is where policy breaks.**
Log fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces: deviation is broad and shallow, i.e. many traces have one or two illegal moves rather than a few traces being wildly wrong. The alignment diagnostics name exactly which moves: `Fast approval` before evidence completeness (R2), `Dispatch replacement` without risk review for high-value devices (R1), and `Customer complaint/reopen` after close without the mandated manual review (R3). Low model precision is reported because auto-approval, risk review and repair recombine in ways policy does not permit. So the ~32% non-fitting traces are structurally correlated with the *cheap* lane, not distributed randomly.

**Artifact 5 — rework statistics invert the cost story.**
`Customer complaint/reopen` occurs in 9.2% of all cases, but 14.1% of Fast-approval cases and only 3.3% of the quote→approval→repair cases. And explicitly: Fast approval is 31% cheaper before rework but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments and service credits. That 9% is a *residual mean* and almost certainly hides a bimodal distribution — most fast cases are hugely cheaper, a minority are catastrophically more expensive (EUR 410 expected cost per wrong high-value replacement, plus service credits, plus escalation-board time).

**Artifact 6 — the segment table shows the contradiction is not a contradiction at all once you disaggregate.**
- Premium low-risk accessory (18%): fast path 8h median, 6% reopen; manual path 46h, 3% reopen — but service-credit exposure triggers above 24h. Here the fast path is *clearly right*: the manual path buys 3 percentage points of reopen at the price of guaranteed credit exposure.
- Premium high-value device (14%): fast path 20h with **22% reopen**; reviewed path 96h with **5% reopen**. Here the fast path is *clearly wrong*: it is a 22% chance of a EUR 410-class error plus R1/R2 compliance exposure.
- Standard high-value device (21%): 18% vs 6% reopen, 28h vs 120h. Wrong-replacement cost is high, but 120h is a poor answer too.
- Standard low-risk accessory (25%): tolerates 72h **if status visibility is good** — an explicit statement that the binding constraint here is communication, not throughput.
- Unclear defect / missing evidence (22%): median 140h, highest abandonment, repeated evidence requests. This is the single worst pool and it is orthogonal to the fast/slow debate.

**Artifact 7 — the abandonment drain.**
`Request missing evidence → Claim abandoned`: 320 cases, plus 70 abandoning at `Customer approval`. 390 abandonments and 220 denials means ~12% of cases end without service. Abandonment currently *flatters* both cycle-time and cost metrics: a customer who gives up is recorded as a cheap, closed case. Combined with "Request missing evidence occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it," this is a self-inflicted funnel loss, not a demand characteristic.

### 1.2 The contradiction, stated precisely

The apparent contradiction — "the frequent path is fast and cheap but bad on quality and compliance" — dissolves into three distinct real effects that happen to point in opposite directions when aggregated:

1. **Selection, not causation.** The auto screen is described as "outdated and not segment-aware." It is therefore routing on criteria uncorrelated with risk. Cases on the fast path are not fast *because* the fast path is efficient; a large share are simply easy cases that would be fast anywhere. Simultaneously, hard cases (high-value devices, ambiguous defects) leak into the fast lane, where they generate the 22%/18% reopen rates. The fast path's speed advantage is partly borrowed from case mix; its quality deficit is concentrated in the cases that were misrouted into it.
2. **Measurement truncation.** The fast lane stops the clock at `Quality/compliance close` while the failure mode (`Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen`, mean 96h) materialises afterwards and is recorded as a *new* stretch of the same case rather than as fast-path latency. The 9.2% overall reopen rate is a downstream cost centre that the upstream KPI cannot see.
3. **Cost migration, not cost reduction.** 31% cheaper → 9% cheaper is the arithmetic of that migration. Cost moved from the review budget (Manual review EUR 34/h, Fraud EUR 52/h) to reshipment, service credits, and the escalation board — the last of which has exactly one 45-minute slot per day and cannot absorb growth.

Conversely, the slow lane is *not* vindicated. Its 3.3–6% reopen rate is real, but a large fraction of its elapsed time is the customer-wait evidence loop (34h mean return edge, 26% double-request rate) and part-price confirmation delay at `Repair quote` — neither of which is quality-producing work. The reviewed path is right about *decisions* and wrong about *waiting*. The reasonable conclusion is not "slow down" but "route by risk, and remove the waiting from the reviewed lane."

### 1.3 Problem summary by dimension

- **Performance:** evidence round-trips (34h mean, 88h p90, 26% repeated) and part-price waits dominate; reopen chains add 100–250h to ~9% of cases; Fraud/risk review has 2 specialists and a Monday/campaign spike; Manual review at 7×36=252 productive hours/week degrades sharply above 82% utilisation.
- **Quality:** 12.5% rework on repaired high-value devices vs 4.0% low-risk; wrong replacement-part selection is explicitly named as the driver of the `Dispatch → complaint` long tail; `Quality check` (2 inspectors) is underused and sits only *after repair*, never before dispatch — which is where the expensive errors happen.
- **Compliance:** R1, R2, R3 all violated in the highest-volume lane; 68% fitting traces; low precision means the model permits combinations policy forbids, so the "as-is" is not a defensible control narrative to an auditor.
- **Customer experience:** 22% reopen for Premium high-value; service-credit exposure above 24h for Premium accessories; 22% of cases stuck in the 140h unclear-evidence pool with the highest abandonment.

## 2. Redesign A: Risk-tiered routing with a pre-dispatch gate on the expensive tail

### Flow

Replace the single auto screen with a **segment- and value-aware triage** immediately after `Intake claim`, producing three lanes:

- **Lane 1 — Straight-through (target: low-risk accessories, both segments, claim value under the R1 threshold).** Intake → Auto screen (rewritten rules) → Fast approval → Dispatch → Close. No added steps. Explicitly permitted for Premium low-risk accessories under the R2 exception.
- **Lane 2 — Gated dispatch (target: all high-value devices where the evidence is unambiguous).** Intake → Auto screen → mandatory *lightweight* risk check (see mechanism) → Fast approval → **Pre-dispatch quality gate (part/model match verification)** → Dispatch → Close. The gate is a checklist verification of replacement SKU against the claimed device and defect code, staffed by the two underused `Quality check` inspectors, designed at the low end of the stated 6–18h range by running it as a same-day batch rather than a queue.
- **Lane 3 — Full review (target: ambiguous defect, fraud signal, disputed value).** Existing manual/fraud/repair-quote path, unchanged in structure.

Rewritten auto-screen rules encode R1 and R2 as hard preconditions rather than leaving them to reviewer discretion: no `Fast approval` above EUR 400 without a review token; no `Dispatch replacement` without an evidence-completeness flag except the Premium low-risk accessory carve-out.

### Targets

The `Fast approval → Dispatch replacement` edge (2300 cases) and specifically the high-value slice of it. Segments: Premium high-value (22% reopen) and Standard high-value (18% reopen) — 35% of volume carrying the EUR 410 error cost. Premium low-risk accessories are deliberately *protected* from the new controls.

### Mechanism

The gate attacks the named root cause directly: the diagnostics say `Dispatch → complaint` (mean 96h, p90 180h) is "linked to incorrect replacement part selection." A 6–18h verification that prevents a 96h-mean reopen chain plus a EUR 410 expected error is a favourable exchange *only for high-value devices* — at EUR 95 expected error for accessories it is not, which is why Lane 1 is exempt. Compliance improves because R1/R2 become structurally unreachable violations rather than behavioural ones, which should move fitting-trace percentage well above 68% for the auto lane. Cost improves not by cutting review but by shrinking the 9%-residual tail that ate the 31% saving.

### Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved

- Adds 6–18h to roughly a third of volume, worsening *median* cycle time for high-value devices even as it improves p90 and total-resolution time. If the sponsor's KPI remains first-close median, this redesign will look like a regression.
- Auto-rule rewrite is stated to increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%; at 252 productive hours/week and an 82% utilisation cliff, this can tip Manual review into queue collapse. Redesign A does not add reviewer capacity — that is its main unresolved exposure.
- It does **not** touch the 22% unclear-defect pool (140h median, highest abandonment). Those cases stay exactly as bad as they are.
- The gate's effectiveness depends on part-catalogue data quality, which no artifact in the prompt describes. If SKU-to-device mapping is itself unreliable, the gate will produce false confidence.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first intake — collapse the round-trip loop

### Flow

Move evidence collection *before* eligibility assessment rather than after it:

Intake claim → **Guided evidence capture at intake** (defect-type-specific dynamic checklist: photos, serial, purchase proof, symptom triage, delivered via the new evidence portal with an assisted-channel fallback) → Auto eligibility screen with a completeness score → then existing routing.

`Request missing evidence` is retained but becomes an exception with a **hard single-round rule**: if the first structured request is not satisfied within a defined window, the case is routed to a **proactive outbound contact** (phone/chat, staffed from spare `Quality check` and repair-quote slack) instead of issuing a second automated request. The second and third automated requests are eliminated by design.

Additionally, `Repair quote` is decoupled from live part-price confirmation by quoting from an indicative price band with a post-hoc reconciliation, since the artifacts state the quote delay is "mostly caused by waiting for part-price confirmation," not by technical difficulty.

### Targets

The `Request missing evidence` cluster: 650 + 780 + entries totalling ~1430 case-entries with a 26% double-occurrence rate; the 34h/26h/88h return edge; the 320 abandonments on that edge; and the "unclear defect / missing evidence" segment (22% of cases, 140h median, worst abandonment). It also targets `Manual eligibility review → Request missing evidence` (14h) and `Repair quote → Customer approval` (30h mean, 80h p90).

### Mechanism

The evidence loop is the largest single non-value wait in the performance DFG, and it is *self-amplifying*: each round adds a full 34h-mean return edge, and 26% of entrants take at least two. Front-loading converts an asynchronous ping-pong into one synchronous capture at the moment of highest customer motivation — at intake, when the customer is actively engaged, versus days later when they are not. The stated portal effect is a 30–45% reduction in repeated requests; applied to the double-request population that is a large absolute removal of elapsed hours *and* of abandonment.

Crucially this improves quality without slowing anything: a completeness score at the auto screen makes the R2 precondition ("evidence completeness confirmed before dispatch") *satisfiable in the fast lane*, which is the single biggest source of alignment deviations. Better evidence at screening time also improves routing accuracy, indirectly reducing the misrouting that drives high-value reopen rates.

### Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved

- The prompt explicitly warns the portal "may increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy." Front-loading effort at intake makes this worse, not better — a heavier intake is a higher barrier. The assisted-channel fallback is the mitigation, but it consumes human capacity that is not currently budgeted, and this redesign will fail for that population if the fallback is under-resourced.
- Adds friction to the ~46% of cases that currently sail through and *never* need evidence. Asking a Premium accessory customer for photos to fix a problem they don't have is a real CX cost and risks the 24h service-credit threshold.
- Does nothing about wrong-part selection or the R1 high-value review gap. `Dispatch → complaint` at 96h mean survives largely intact.
- Indicative-band quoting transfers margin risk to the business and could create a new dispute category (quoted vs final price) — a possible new reopen source.

## 4. Redesign C: Outcome-accountable segment cells with a closed-loop reopen feedback circuit

### Flow

Abandon the functional hand-off topology. Organise into three cross-functional cells, each owning a case end-to-end:

- **Cell 1 — Accessories & simple claims** (43% of volume: both low-risk accessory segments). Automation-first, one owner, no internal hand-offs.
- **Cell 2 — Devices** (35%: both high-value segments). Contains an embedded fraud/risk specialist, technician, and inspector working as a unit; `Manual eligibility review`, `Fraud/risk review`, `Repair quote` and `Quality check` become *roles within a case conversation* rather than sequential queues with hand-off delays.
- **Cell 3 — Ambiguous claims** (22%: unclear defect / missing evidence). A dedicated investigative cell with proactive customer contact as the default mode.

The structural core of the redesign is the **feedback circuit**: every `Customer complaint/reopen` and every `Rework repair` event is attributed back to the originating decision and cell, and the auto-screen rules are re-tuned on a fixed cadence from that signal. `Quality/compliance close` is redefined so that the case clock does not stop until a defined no-reopen window has elapsed — removing the measurement truncation identified in §1.2. R3 is enforced by making the originating cell, not a generic manual queue, own the reopened case.

The `Escalation board` (one 45-min daily slot, "strong customer-satisfaction effect but poor scalability") is repurposed: instead of adjudicating individual cases, it becomes a weekly **routing-rule review** consuming the attribution data, while individual escalations are resolved inside cells.

### Targets

The reopen system itself: `Customer complaint/reopen` (9.2% overall, 14.1% fast-path), the 310 reopens returning to `Manual eligibility review` at 40h mean, the 120 reaching the escalation board at 54h mean, and `Rework repair` (12.5% of high-value repairs). Also targets every hand-off edge in the performance DFG — `Intake → Manual eligibility review` at mean 7.5h and `Manual eligibility review → Repair quote` at mean 22.0h (flagged as too slow for Premium) are queue-wait edges, not work edges, and cells delete them.

### Mechanism

Two mechanisms, neither of which A or B contains. First, **hand-off elimination**: a large share of the reviewed lane's elapsed time sits between activities, not inside them. Cells convert queue time into work time without adding headcount. Second, and more fundamental, **closing the learning loop**: the artifacts state the auto rules are "outdated and not segment-aware" — the reason they became outdated is that nothing feeds outcome data back into them. A one-off rule rewrite (Redesign A) fixes the rules once; a feedback circuit prevents them from decaying again, and progressively discovers *which* cases genuinely belong in the fast lane rather than assuming the answer. This is the only redesign that addresses the selection-vs-causation problem at its root.

Extending the case clock past first close also removes the perverse incentive that currently makes the fast path look good and makes abandonment look like success.

### Deliberate sacrifices / unresolved

- Sacrifices specialist utilisation efficiency. Two fraud specialists cannot be split across three cells; Cell 2 gets fraud coverage and Cells 1 and 3 get a thinner arrangement, or a specialist is shared and the hand-off partially returns. This is a genuine, unresolved capacity constraint given the stated headcount.
- Highest implementation complexity and the longest time-to-signal: the feedback loop needs several reopen cycles (reopen latency is 72–96h mean, p90 150–180h) before it produces trustworthy attribution. Expect months, not the two weeks quoted for a rule update.
- Organisational disruption, role redefinition and a new KPI definition simultaneously — the redesign is hard to evaluate because everything moves at once, and a failure will be hard to attribute.
- Redefining close will make reported cycle time *worse on paper* immediately, before any real improvement. This is politically the most dangerous property of the design and must be pre-agreed with the sponsor.
- Does not by itself add any specific control against wrong-part selection (A's gate) or against the evidence loop (B's front-loading); it makes those problems *visible and owned* rather than solved.

Nothing in the given data supports a claim of true Pareto dominance among A, B and C: they attack largely disjoint failure modes (wrong-part dispatch; evidence round-trips; routing decay and hand-offs), and the stated effect ranges (30–45%, 15–35%, 6–18h) are wide enough that the ranking flips within them. ## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A

1. **Gate theatre.** If the pre-dispatch check is a signature rather than a genuine verification against device/defect data, it adds 6–18h and catches nothing. Detection: gate catch-rate should be non-trivial and roughly consistent with the share of `Dispatch → complaint` attributed to part selection; a catch rate near zero alongside an unchanged reopen rate means theatre.
2. **Manual-review queue collapse.** The stated 15–35% inflow increase against 252 productive hours/week and a sharp degradation above 82% utilisation can convert a 7.5h intake edge into a multi-day queue. The high end of that range is very likely fatal without added capacity.
3. **Over-tiering.** Conservative new rules push borderline mid-risk cases into Lane 3, quietly recreating the slow process for a growing population and eroding the accessory lane's advantage.
4. **Bottleneck migration:** from the reopen/escalation tail to (i) the Manual eligibility review queue and (ii) the two `Quality check` inspectors, who move from underused to load-bearing — and whose availability is described as campaign-sensitive, so the gate will fail exactly when volume peaks.

### Redesign B

1. **Abandonment shifts left.** Total abandonment stays flat or rises while its location moves from `Request missing evidence → Claim abandoned` (currently 320) to silent drop-off during intake capture. This is dangerous because pre-intake drop-off may not appear in the event log at all — the metric would improve while the business loses claims. Instrument intake-form starts vs completions explicitly.
2. **Checklist over-collection.** Defect-specific checklists drift toward asking for everything, making intake heavy for all 5,000 cases to fix a problem concentrated in ~1,430. Watch for rising intake duration on cases that never needed evidence.
3. **Quote-band disputes.** Indicative pricing creates a new complaint class at `Repair quote → Customer complaint/reopen` (currently only 80 cases) — a new reopen source replacing an old wait.
4. **Bottleneck migration:** from the asynchronous evidence loop to the assisted-contact channel and to intake handling time. The queue becomes a synchronous staffing problem — more visible and more manageable, but real, and it lands on capacity borrowed from repair/inspection.

### Redesign C

1. **Fraud-specialist fragmentation.** With 2 specialists and a stated Monday/campaign spike, cell allocation either starves two cells or reintroduces the hand-off it was meant to delete. Cell 2 becomes a de facto queue for the specialist.
2. **Attribution disputes and gaming.** Once reopens are attributed to cells, incentives shift toward defensive over-review — every cell routes ambiguous cases to full review to protect its own reopen metric, and cycle time degrades across the board while quality gains plateau.
3. **Signal starvation.** With reopen latency of 72–96h mean and p90 up to 180h, the feedback loop's learning cadence is slow; at low per-cell volumes the outcome signal may be too noisy to re-tune rules safely, and premature re-tuning on noise is worse than no re-tuning.
4. **Sponsor abandonment.** Redefined close makes reported cycle time jump immediately; if the sponsor is not pre-committed to the new definition, the programme is cancelled before the loop produces value.
5. **Bottleneck migration:** from inter-activity queues to intra-cell specialist scarcity and to the analytics/attribution function, which becomes a new critical dependency the current organisation does not have.

### Cross-cutting failure mode

All three can produce **KPI improvement without customer improvement** if measurement continues to stop at first `Quality/compliance close`. Every redesign should be evaluated against a case-level, reopen-inclusive resolution time and against total cost including credits and reshipments — otherwise the 31%→9% illusion simply reappears in a new form.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### Stage 0 — Instrumentation (prerequisite, ~2–3 weeks, no process change)

Before changing anything, fix the measurement so improvements can be distinguished from displacement:

- Define **total resolution time** = intake → last close with no reopen in a defined follow-up window (choose it beyond the p90 of `Dispatch → complaint`, i.e. comfortably above 180h).
- Define **fully-loaded cost per resolved case**, including reshipment, service credits, escalation-board time and rework labour.
- Attribute every `Customer complaint/reopen` and `Rework repair` to its originating decision, segment, defect type and lane.
- Establish per-segment baselines for the five segments and re-run token-based replay per segment so the 68% fitting-trace figure can be decomposed.

**Stop/go:** if attribution cannot be established for the majority of reopens, do not proceed to outcome-based experiments — run Stage 1 as a controlled A/B instead, where randomisation substitutes for attribution.

### Stage 1 — First pilot: Redesign A's pre-dispatch gate, high-value devices only

Chosen first because it is the narrowest intervention with the clearest causal hypothesis, targets the largest quantified loss (EUR 410 per wrong high-value replacement, 22%/18% reopen rates, 35% of volume), and uses currently underused inspector capacity. Deploy the R1/R2 rule enforcement *simultaneously* (two-week deployment) since it is nearly free and directly addresses the named alignment deviations — but hold the broader auto-rule rewrite back so manual-review inflow does not spike during the pilot.

Run as a randomised split within high-value claims (gated vs not) for a period covering at least two full reopen windows.

**Metrics:** gate catch rate and catch reasons; reopen rate at 30 days for high-value segments (baselines 22% Premium / 18% Standard); actual gate duration against the 6–18h envelope; total resolution time p50 and p90; fully-loaded cost per resolved case; R1/R2 violation rate; manual-review utilisation against the 82% threshold; Premium service-credit incidence.

**Stop/go:**
- *Go / scale:* high-value reopen rate falls by roughly a third or more (e.g. 22% → mid-teens or below), gate duration holds near the low end of the range, and fully-loaded cost per resolved case does not rise.
- *Iterate:* reopen improves but gate duration drifts toward 18h, or Premium service-credit incidence rises — redesign the gate as a same-day batch or pre-authorise dispatch pending gate for the lowest-value tier.
- *Stop:* catch rate near zero, or reopen unchanged after two full reopen windows — the wrong-part hypothesis is not the dominant driver and effort should move to Stage 2.

### Stage 2 — Redesign B's evidence front-loading, ambiguous-defect segment first

Sequenced second because it addresses the largest *pool* of waste (22% of cases at 140h median) but carries an explicit downside risk (digital-literacy abandonment) that demands a protected launch. Pilot on the unclear-defect / missing-evidence segment plus Standard low-risk accessories, with the assisted channel live from day one, and route the low-digital-literacy cohort to assisted contact by default rather than by fallback.

**Metrics:** repeated-evidence rate (baseline 26% of entrants ≥2 requests) against the 30–45% reduction claim; **intake-start-to-intake-complete conversion** (the left-shifted abandonment detector); total abandonment rate (baseline ~390 + 70 cases, ~9%); evidence-loop elapsed time (34h/26h/88h edge); segment cycle time; assisted-channel volume and wait; new complaint volume on the `Repair quote` edge if band-quoting is included.

**Stop/go:**
- *Go:* repeated-evidence rate falls within or near the stated 30–45% band **and** total abandonment (including pre-intake drop-off) is flat or lower.
- *Stop or redesign:* total abandonment rises at all. This is a hard gate — a faster process that loses more customers is a worse process, and the current metrics would misreport it as success.
- Deliberately *do not* extend the heavier intake to the straight-through accessory lane unless a measured benefit appears there; the default is to exempt it.

### Stage 3 — Auto-rule rewrite with capacity provisioning

Only after Stages 1–2, because Stage 2 improves evidence quality at screening time, which makes better rules possible, and because the rewrite's 15–35% manual-inflow increase is safest to absorb once the evidence loop is shorter. Provision reviewer capacity (or overflow arrangement) sized for the **top** of that range before deployment; model queue behaviour against the 82% utilisation cliff, not against average utilisation.

**Metrics:** manual-review inflow change vs the 15–35% forecast; reviewer utilisation and queue length; fitting-trace percentage (baseline 68%) and model precision; routing accuracy measured as reopen rate by lane; share of volume still on the straight-through path.

**Stop/go:** roll back if utilisation crosses ~82% sustained or queue length grows monotonically for more than a short stabilisation period; the rules are reversible in days and should be treated as such.

### Stage 4 — Redesign C, single-cell trial on Premium high-value devices

Last, because it is the most disruptive and the slowest to produce signal, and because Stages 1–3 will have generated the outcome-attribution data that the feedback loop needs to function. Stand up **one** cell for the 14% Premium high-value segment — the segment with the worst reopen rate (22%) and the strictest expectations — rather than reorganising the whole operation. Simultaneously repurpose the escalation board's daily slot toward routing-rule review, keeping some capacity for genuine individual escalations.

**Metrics:** hand-off wait time (baselines: 7.5h intake→manual, 22h manual→quote, flagged too slow for Premium); reopen rate and rework rate with cell attribution; specialist utilisation and cross-cell contention; whether rule re-tuning cycles actually occur and whether re-tuned rules improve routing accuracy on held-out cases; R3 compliance on reopened claims.

**Stop/go:** proceed to a second cell only if the pilot cell shows both reduced hand-off time *and* at least one completed rule-tuning cycle that measurably improved routing. If specialist contention forces hand-offs back in, revert to a functional structure and retain only the attribution and feedback mechanism — which is the genuinely valuable part and is separable from the reorganisation.

### How to tell whether improvements are real rather than displaced

1. **Measure at the case level, not the activity level.** Total resolution time including reopens and fully-loaded cost are the only two headline metrics; activity-level improvements are diagnostics, not results.
2. **Watch the exits.** Denials, abandonments and pre-intake drop-off must be tracked as first-class outcomes. A redesign that improves speed by losing customers is displacement, not improvement.
3. **Segment-stratified reporting, always.** Any aggregate movement must be decomposable across the five segments. If aggregate cycle time improves while every segment is flat or worse, the change is case-mix drift or routing reclassification — precisely the selection effect that made the fast path look good in the first place.
4. **Re-run conformance checking per lane after each stage.** Fitting-trace percentage and per-rule violation rates by lane show whether compliance genuinely improved or whether violations migrated to a lane with less scrutiny.
5. **Track the pinch points explicitly** — manual-review utilisation vs 82%, fraud-specialist queue on Mondays and post-campaign, inspector load, escalation-board demand vs its single 45-minute slot. A redesign that improves the headline while any of these saturates has moved the bottleneck, not removed it.
6. **Hold out a control population wherever feasible.** Given noisy volumes, wide stated effect ranges, and reopen latencies of 72–96h, before/after comparison alone will not reliably separate genuine effects from seasonality, campaign cycles or mix drift.

A final caution for the sponsor: nothing in this dataset supports a precise ROI estimate for any of these designs. The stated effect ranges (30–45% evidence reduction, 15–35% inflow increase, 6–18h gate, EUR 95/EUR 410 error costs) are wide enough that each redesign's business case can plausibly land anywhere from strongly positive to marginal. The purpose of the staged sequence is to narrow those ranges cheaply, in the order of decreasing confidence and increasing disruption, before committing to the expensive structural change.
