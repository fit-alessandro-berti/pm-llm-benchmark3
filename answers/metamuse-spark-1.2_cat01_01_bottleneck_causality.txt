## 1. Executive causal assessment
**Provisional verdict: `mainly confounded` — confidence 68/100.**

The crude comparison is strongly confounded by case mix and routing policy, not a reliable estimate of a total causal effect of Orion. Orion's overall mean cycle time is 21.96h vs Nova 15.24h (+6.7h). However, within every observed complexity stratum Orion is *faster*: Low 7.50 vs 9.64h (-2.14h), Medium 17.17 vs 19.38h (-2.21h), High 31.57 vs 35.00h (-3.43h). This reversal is a classic Simpson's paradox driven by selection: Orion handles 42.9% high-complexity vs Nova 12.5%, 64.3% compliance-required vs 25%, and 92.9% night shift vs 25% with high arrival load. Standardizing to a common complexity distribution (overall: 40% Low, 33% Medium, 27% High) yields 17.1h Orion vs 19.7h Nova (-2.5h favoring Orion). Mean queue wait and mean review service show the same pattern: crude Orion longer, stratified Orion shorter.

Strongest evidence *for* mainly confounded: consistent stratum-specific advantage for Orion on cycle time, wait, and service, plus documented routing rule that Orion is selected for difficult cases. Strongest evidence *against* definitive conclusion: tiny cells (e.g., n=2 for Low-Orion, High-Nova; only 1 Orion-Day case), near-violation of positivity on shift, unobserved case risk/reviewer skill, and conditioning on `case_completed` only. Queue wait includes compliance waiting, so wait differences partly reflect compliance mediation, not group efficiency.

Proposed 50% blind reassignment may fail or backfire: it assumes Nova can absorb high-complexity/compliance/night work with same speed and quality, ignores that Nova's advantage disappears when given Orion-like mix, and risks overloading Nova days, increasing queueing, rework, and appeals. It may succeed only if the small within-stratum gap reflects true Orion inefficiency, which the data contradict. A safer estimand is the within-stratum or standardized effect under controlled routing, not the crude mean difference.

**Final recommendation (156 words):** Do not execute the 50% blind reassignment. The crude 6.7h gap is a textbook confounding artifact; within every complexity level Orion is 2–3h faster with lower wait and equal service, so naïve shifting will move Orion's difficult, night, compliance-heavy mix to Nova, likely *raising* Nova's mean, degrading queueing, and providing no cycle-time gain while risking quality. The resource group is a bundle of routing policy, shift, load, and compliance capacity, not an isolated causal label. The evidence is consistent with mainly confounded with possible modest Orion benefit, but cells are tiny and unobserved risk plus selection on completed cases leave substantial uncertainty — confidence 68 not 95. Adopt the phased learning plan: first run a small medium-complexity randomized pilot (Phase 1) to obtain an unconfounded effect, simultaneously pilot a queueing/compliance capacity fix (Phase 2) which the wait decomposition suggests is the real leverage point. Only scale reassignment if pilot shows standardized benefit and overlap is adequate.

        for (a,b), cnt in sorted(dfg.items(), key=lambda x: -x[1]):

        print(f"  start: {start_act}  end: {end_act}")
except Exception as e:
    print(f"PM4Py DFG failed: {e}")
    # fallback: manual directly-follows counting from trace strings
    from collections import Counter
    for grp in ["Orion","Nova"]:
        counter = Counter()
        for t in df[df["group"]==grp]["trace"]:
            acts = t.split(">")
            for a,b in zip(acts, acts[1:]):
                counter[(a,b)] += 1
        print(f"\n{grp} manual DFG:")
        for (a,b),cnt in counter.most_common():

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. **Within-level unobserved case risk** — Most valuable: structured case-risk score (or reviewer difficulty rating at `Classify`) plus text-derived complexity features to enable finer risk adjustment and sensitivity bounds.
2. **Selection on completed cases only** — Most valuable: case-level `case_completed` flag with open/abandoned cases and their elapsed times/reasons, to estimate censoring-corrected effects (Lee bounds/IPCW).
3. **Queueing vs service confounding** — Most valuable: event timestamps for `Classify`, `Compliance Check` start/end, and `Review` start/end to reconstruct queue state, backlog at arrival, and true service vs wait.
4. **Shift and resource as bundle / positivity** — Most valuable: randomized or stepped-wedge routing pilot where `resource_group` is assigned independent of `shift`/`arrival_load` for a small eligible cohort to directly estimate transportable group effect.
5. **Reviewer skill heterogeneity and compliance capacity** — Most valuable: reviewer-level identifiers, certification/skill level, and compliance lane capacity/utilization by hour to separate `unobserved_reviewer_skill` from group label and test capacity intervention.
