## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

**Chemistry.** The first analyst frees up at about Fri 15:54–16:24. Under risk priority, that analyst takes the urgent sample, and B-417 typically starts at about 16:40–17:30. Roughly 0.5–1.3 h of its 1.5–4.5 h of primary service fits before closing. The remaining service resumes after the 62 h shutdown at Mon 08:00, giving a typical primary finish around Mon 09:00–11:00. Under FIFO, four samples have to start ahead of B-417. With the proxy service distribution, B-417 usually starts Mon 11:00–13:00 after roughly 68–70 elapsed hours of waiting, and finishes around Mon 13:00–16:30. A slow FIFO combination can carry the primary test into Tuesday.

**Microbiology.** Incubation reaches technical readiness Sun 20 Sep, 12:00–20:00. On-call readout gives a primary record Sun 13:00–23:00. With routine staffing, the usable primary result is around Mon 08:30–09:30, including the 0.5–1.5 h readout. The routine staffing wait alone is about 12–20 h after technical readiness. QA is closed throughout Sunday, so a Sunday result helps Monday release only when microbiology would otherwise hold up the join.

**Join and QA.** The join is the **later of the two usable-result timestamps**, including any rerun or required confirmation. The two branch durations therefore do not add together. From the forecast origin to Mon 08:00, QA has 4.5 open hours on Friday and 4 on Saturday, both with one reviewer. Roughly three new batches arrive in those 8.5 hours on average. Service demand and arrivals vary enough that a Monday backlog of several cases is plausible, as is a free reviewer at B-417’s join. With two Monday reviewers, the queue approximation gives waits of zero to about five elapsed hours for much of the distribution, with some cases spilling to Tuesday. Add review service separately in open hours, with a 1.5 h median and about 3.5 h at the 80th percentile. Reviews in progress pause at closing. Following acceptance, add 0.25–0.75 h for continuously available release.

- **Smooth path.** The primary chemistry service and the primary microbiology readout place the join on Monday, usually morning under risk priority and early to midafternoon under FIFO. Incubation and staffing explain the weekend floor. Given the observed chemistry queue and immediate routine readout assumption, chemistry often supplies the later Monday timestamp. If QA receives both results Mon 14:30, a two-hour wait and 1.5 h review give acceptance around 18:00 and release shortly after.
- **Isolated chemistry rerun.** The first chemistry result triggers another 1–3 h of priority service. The microbiology primary result is already usable by the time most reruns finish. If FIFO chemistry finishes at Mon 17:00 and the rerun needs two hours, one hour runs Monday and the rest resumes Tue 08:00–09:00. The join then occurs Tue 09:00, followed by the queue observed at that time, initial review, and electronic release.
- **Microbiology confirmation.** The chemistry clock follows the ordinary or rerun schedule. Microbiology needs another 20–30 h of continuous incubation from the recorded indication and 0.5–1.5 h of staffed readout. With on-call primary readout, confirmation can finish as early as Mon 09:30 or slip to Tuesday morning. With routine primary readout, confirmation typically finishes Tue 08:30–17:00. For example, Mon 09:00 primary readout plus 24 h incubation and one hour of readout gives a Tue 10:00 usable result. This usually sets the join, after which QA waits and reviews as above if release remains possible. If confirmation establishes a critical defect and recording can start immediately, the example ends in rejection around Tue 10:30–11:00.
- **Documentation query without investigation.** Chemistry and microbiology first join on the later usable result, often Monday and sometimes Tuesday with branch rework. After the initial QA wait and review, count 4–12 working hours for the response. An eight-hour hold from Mon 17:00 runs three hours Monday and five hours Tuesday, making the case ready to re-enter at Tue 13:00. Follow-up queueing and review precede release.
- **Documentation-only investigation.** The same branch clocks feed the join, followed by initial QA wait and review. A 14-working-hour investigation that starts Mon 17:00 reaches Tue 19:00. Follow-up review can cross the Tuesday cutoff. An accompanying query adds a further working-hours hold and another queue entry in this estimate. Acceptance leads to electronic release, while a confirmed critical defect leads to rejection recording.
- **Laboratory investigation.** The primary or reworked branch results still govern entry to QA, and the initial queue and review still matter. The laboratory investigation then adds 3–7 elapsed days. For example, six days from Mon 17:00 ends Sun 27 at 17:00. A case that can still be released then waits for QA on Mon 28 and for follow-up review. If the investigation confirms a critical defect, add 0.5–1.0 h from confirmation for rejection recording.

Estimating a numerical rejection share or its effect on the pooled percentiles requires the fraction and timing of indications and deviations that confirm a critical defect.

## 5. Main uncertainty drivers

1. **Whether QA requires a laboratory investigation — path uncertainty.** The additional 3–7 calendar days move the upper percentiles most, especially when findings arrive near a weekend.
2. **QA workload at the actual join — queue uncertainty.** Initial work, new arrivals, and long reviews can fill both Monday reviewers and shift B-417 into the next opening period.
3. **Chemistry dispatch order and work ahead — queue uncertainty.** Priority can buy Friday processing time. FIFO can push the join to Monday afternoon, and a late rerun can cross into Tuesday.
4. **Microbiology staffing and the confirmation clock — mainly calendar uncertainty.** Weekend authorization determines when the primary result can be read. After an indication, that staffing decision also changes the start of the 20–30 h confirmation clock.
5. **The unresolved temperature excursion — dependence uncertainty.** A confirmed excursion raises rerun and indication risks together and increases the chance of a QA investigation.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The **first microbiology readout** is the most informative upcoming operational event. A normal result fixes the microbiology branch clock and removes the confirmation delay. Attention shifts to chemistry completion and the QA queue, with a Monday join likely. A contamination indication starts the extra 20–30 h incubation and makes a Tuesday join more likely, especially if the first readout waits until Monday. It also increases the probability of an investigation and makes rejection a possible near-term outcome once a critical defect is confirmed.

The secondary trigger is **resolution of the temperature-excursion flag**. Confirmation shifts weight toward joint rework and investigation. Clearing the flag shifts weight toward the smooth path while leaving some baseline risk of each complication.
