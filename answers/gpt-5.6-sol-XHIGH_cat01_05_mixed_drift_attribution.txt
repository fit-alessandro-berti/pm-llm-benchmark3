## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

March’s raw fitness collapse is primarily evidence of semantic and provenance drift. Raw fitness falls from 0.93 in February to 0.55 in March, while mapped fitness is 0.90. Thus, mapping `Eligibility Screen` back to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicated `Open Case` events recovers most of the apparent loss. It does not establish that every migrated event has the same business meaning, granularity, timestamp semantics, or lifecycle interpretation as its predecessor.

A label mapping can make traces fit the old model because conformance checking treats the two labels as equivalent. The underlying eligibility decision could nevertheless have changed in automation, routing, duration, exceptions, or decision criteria. Those possibilities require attribute, temporal, and outcome comparisons rather than label-level alignment alone.

Duplicated technical events directly create false paths, variants, and alignment costs. They can also distort event counts and inferred starts. Missing resource identifiers have less effect on activity-only DFGs, but undermine handover, workload, segregation-of-duties, team-performance, and queue attribution analyses. Because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities, complete-case resource analyses would be selected rather than representative.

The post-May evidence cannot be dismissed as logging drift. Normalized `Assess -> Request Info` rises from 12 to 24 per 100 cases in May and 29 in July; rework variants and cycle-time tails move similarly. Mapped fitness also weakens, and operational-table measures show the same direction. These changes align temporally with supplier disruption, capacity loss, priority policy, and seasonality, although their separate causal contributions remain unidentified.

### Normalized DFG distance

For the seven supplied relations, define the restricted distance from January as:

\[
D_{L1}(m,\mathrm{Jan})=\sum_e |r_{m,e}-r_{\mathrm{Jan},e}|
\]

where rates are occurrences per 100 cases. The mean absolute edge deviation is \(D_{L1}/7\). Because these are occurrence rates rather than a probability distribution, the distance is not divided by two.

| month | restricted L1 distance | mean absolute edge-rate deviation | principal deviations from January |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-01 | 0 | 0.00 | Reference |
| 2026-03 | 3 | 0.43 | Only one-point changes in three relations |
| 2026-05 | 40 | 5.71 | Request-info loop +12/+12; decision relation -7 |
| 2026-07 | 59 | 8.43 | Request-info loop +17/+17; decision relation -11 |
| 2026-09 | 28 | 4.00 | Partial reversion of rework and decision relations |
| 2026-10 | 64 | 9.14 | Senior assessment +18; request-info loop +14/+14 |
| 2026-12 | 36 | 5.14 | Residual rework +9/+9 and senior assessment +10 |

March is structurally close to January after normalization, unlike May onward. Omitted edges are treated as unobserved, not zero, and all supplied edges receive equal weight. The result is therefore a fragment distance, not a full-process DFG distance.

### Cycle-time decomposition

The January–February baseline averages approximately 31.2 hours at the median and 68 hours at p90. November reaches 49 and 121 hours, increases of about 17.9 and 53 hours.

| component | evidence | plausible contribution to the roughly 18-hour median increase | interpretation |
|---|---|---:|---|

| Control-flow/rework | Rework variants rise from 11–12% to 29%; request-info loop rates more than double | 3–5 h | Extra document collection and reassessment add both work and delay |
| Service time | Recorded `Assess` median rises only about 0.6 h by July–August | 0.5–1.5 h | Direct processing slowdown is too small to explain the total |
| Queueing/resource delay | Reconstructed waiting before `Assess` rises substantially; four assessors leave; p90 grows much more than service time | 8–12 h | Most plausible dominant proximate component, especially in the tail |

An illustrative central accounting is 3 hours mix, 4 hours rework, 1 hour service, and 10 hours queueing. It is not a causal estimate: case mix and rework create workload that is mediated through queueing, so their causal shares overlap.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0: Intake cohort | Registration month or week; all registered cases | Primary system-level monitoring without selecting on completion or outcome | Retain all cohorts; use censoring-aware metrics until mature | Month/week trends, seasonal recurrence, global DFG and performance | Low, provided incomplete cases are retained |
| 1: Stable case attributes | Supplier, declared complexity, region, customer type | Separate exposure and population composition | At least 100 cases per monthly cell or 300 over a rolling quarter; otherwise pool levels | Z versus non-Z, complexity and regional standardization | Complexity and supplier may be correlated; historical complexity measurement is inconsistent |
| 2: Policy strata | Priority class and queue-entry date | Diagnose redistribution caused by July policy | At least 75 cases per class-window and at least 20 observed queue exits | High versus standard waiting and throughput | Priority is treatment-related and should not be used to estimate total population effects |
| 3: Organizational strata | Team, assessor grade, roster period | Diagnose capacity, routing, and handover drift | At least 50 mature cases and 20 activity completions per team-window; pool smaller teams | Pre/post departures, workload, service, handovers | Team assignment is post-intake and affected by priority and workload |
| 4: Technical provenance | Source system, schema version, migrated activity, resource completeness | Isolate logging and semantic drift | Report all schema violations; require 100 cases for rate comparison | Raw versus canonical labels, missingness, duplicate-event analysis | Technical version is nearly collinear with calendar time |
| 5: Controlled intersections | Supplier × complexity; priority × complexity; team × source version | Test mechanism-specific interactions | At least 75 cases per cell over a rolling quarter; collapse rare categories into “other” | Matched and standardized process comparisons | Sparse cells and collider bias increase rapidly |
| 6: Diagnostic path groups | Fast, rework, senior-assessment, rejection path | Explain alerts after they occur, not define the primary denominator | At least 30 traces and 20 occurrences of the focal edge | Variant and alignment root-cause analysis | Strong post-treatment conditioning; unsuitable for prevalence or causal attribution |

The primary monitoring population should be **all cases cohorted by registration time, using canonical activity labels while retaining raw labels and provenance in parallel**. This denominator is stable, avoids selecting only closed or successfully logged cases, and captures both customer-facing performance and system risk. Supplier, complexity, and policy strata should be diagnostic views rather than replacements for the intake population. Cycle-time reporting should use mature cohorts or censoring-aware survival estimates.

Seasonality should be modeled explicitly through renewal-season reference windows, weekday and holiday controls, and—once multiple years exist—seasonal forecasts or hierarchical time-series models. With only one observed year, claims of recurrence should remain provisional. Multiple testing should use a hierarchy: first test system-level families such as schema, control flow, timing, and outcomes; only after a family alert should edge or segment diagnostics be opened. Within each family, use false-discovery-rate control, minimum effect sizes, support rules, and persistence requirements rather than one universal threshold.

for metric, threshold in practical_thresholds.items():
    diffs = monthly[metric].diff()
    flagged_positions = np.flatnonzero((diffs.abs() + 1e-12 >= threshold).to_numpy())

change_flags = pd.DataFrame(change_flags).sort_values(
    ["to_month", "metric"]

try:
    import pm4py
except ImportError:
    pm4py = None

def _require_pm4py():
    if pm4py is None:
        raise ImportError("Install a current PM4Py release to run Part B.")

def format_event_dataframe(
    df,
    case_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
):
    """Return a PM4Py-formatted copy without modifying the caller's dataframe."""
    _require_pm4py()
    required = {case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col}
    missing = required.difference(df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing event columns: {sorted(missing)}")

    work = df.copy(deep=True)
    if work[[case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col]].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Case, activity, and timestamp keys must be non-null.")
    work[timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(
        work[timestamp_col], errors="raise", utc=True
    )

    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work,
        case_id=case_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )

def slice_time_window(
    formatted_log,
    start,
    end,
    mode="traces_intersecting",
):
    """
    Slice using PM4Py. 'traces_intersecting' preserves complete intersecting
    traces; 'events' avoids repeating traces across windows but can truncate paths.
    """
    _require_pm4py()
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted_log,
        pd.Timestamp(start).to_pydatetime(),
        pd.Timestamp(end).to_pydatetime(),
        mode=mode,
    )

def discover_artifact_bundle(formatted_log):
    """Discover DFG, variants, and temporal profile for one formatted slice."""
    _require_pm4py()
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(formatted_log)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(formatted_log)
    temporal_profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(formatted_log)
    return {
        "dfg": dfg,
        "start_activities": start_activities,
        "end_activities": end_activities,
        "variants": variants,
        "temporal_profile": temporal_profile,
    }

def compute_monthly_artifacts(
    formatted_log,
    start,
    end,
    slice_mode="traces_intersecting",
):
    """
    Compute PM4Py artifacts by calendar window.

    For production cohort monitoring, a separate case-level registration-month
    attribute is preferable: event slicing can truncate traces, while intersecting
    trace slicing can place the same long-running trace in multiple months.
    """
    _require_pm4py()
    periods = pd.period_range(
        pd.Timestamp(start).to_period("M"),
        pd.Timestamp(end).to_period("M"),
        freq="M",
    )

    results = {}
    for period in periods:
        window = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            formatted_log,
            period.start_time.to_pydatetime(),
            period.end_time.to_pydatetime(),
            mode=slice_mode,
        )
        if len(window) == 0:
            results[str(period)] = None
            continue

        dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(window)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(window)
        temporal_profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(window)
        results[str(period)] = {
            "dfg": dfg,
            "start_activities": starts,
            "end_activities": ends,
            "variants": variants,
            "temporal_profile": temporal_profile,
            "event_rows": len(window),
        }
    return results

def discover_optional_declarative_models(
    formatted_log,
    include_log_skeleton=True,
    include_declare=False,
):
    """Optionally discover declarative artifacts supported by PM4Py."""
    _require_pm4py()
    output = {}
    if include_log_skeleton:
        output["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(formatted_log)
    if include_declare:
        output["declare"] = pm4py.discover_declare(formatted_log)
    return output

def normalized_event_copy(
    df,
    case_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    label_mapping=None,
    duplicate_flag_col=None,
):
    """
    Create a canonical copy. If no explicit duplicate flag exists, remove only
    exact duplicate Open Case records; near-duplicates require audited event IDs
    or a source-specific rule and should not be inferred silently.
    """
    norm = df.copy(deep=True)
    mapping = label_mapping or {
        "Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"
    }
    norm[activity_col] = norm[activity_col].replace(mapping)

    open_case = norm[activity_col].eq("Open Case")
    if duplicate_flag_col is not None:
        if duplicate_flag_col not in norm.columns:
            raise ValueError(f"Missing duplicate flag: {duplicate_flag_col}")
        flag = norm[duplicate_flag_col]
        if flag.dtype != bool:
            flag = flag.astype(str).str.lower().isin(
                {"1", "true", "yes", "y"}
            )
        remove = open_case & flag
    else:
        exact_duplicate = norm.duplicated(
            subset=[case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col],
            keep="first",
        )
        remove = open_case & exact_duplicate

    return norm.loc[~remove].copy()

def compare_raw_and_normalized(
    df,
    case_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    label_mapping=None,
    duplicate_flag_col=None,
):
    """Compare artifacts while preserving the original event dataframe."""
    _require_pm4py()
    original_snapshot = df.copy(deep=True)

    raw_formatted = format_event_dataframe(
        df, case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col
    )
    normalized_df = normalized_event_copy(
        df,
        case_col=case_col,
        activity_col=activity_col,
        timestamp_col=timestamp_col,
        label_mapping=label_mapping,
        duplicate_flag_col=duplicate_flag_col,
    )
    normalized_formatted = format_event_dataframe(
        normalized_df, case_col, activity_col, timestamp_col
    )

    raw_artifacts = discover_artifact_bundle(raw_formatted)
    normalized_artifacts = discover_artifact_bundle(normalized_formatted)

    if not df.equals(original_snapshot):
        raise RuntimeError("Original dataframe was unexpectedly modified.")

    return {
        "raw": raw_artifacts,
        "normalized": normalized_artifacts,
        "raw_event_rows": len(df),
        "normalized_event_rows": len(normalized_df),
        "removed_event_rows": len(df) - len(normalized_df),
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the **March fitness collapse is predominantly logging-semantic drift** (about 95% confidence). The migration changed the eligibility label, introduced technical duplicates, and worsened resource provenance. Mapping and deduplication restore fitness from 0.55 to 0.90 in March, while the normalized DFG remains very close to January. This does not prove the underlying eligibility operation was identical, but it makes a large March business-process break unlikely.

The **May increase in rework is most plausibly supplier-driven** (80–90% confidence). Supplier Z’s share rises sharply as request-information loops double and fast variants decline. Attribution is not clean because Z already handled more complex, non-random cases. The supplier mechanism would weaken if matched non-Z cases showed the same May discontinuity or if Z’s within-stratum rework remained stable.

The **June–August cycle-time deterioration is most plausibly dominated by queueing after staffing loss** (about 80% confidence). Assessment service time moves only modestly, whereas reconstructed waiting and p90 cycle time rise substantially. Supplier rework likely mediated this effect by increasing offered workload before four assessors left. The July priority rule is a plausible contributor to redistribution of delay (about 60% confidence), but the aggregate cannot show whether high-complexity cases benefited at the expense of standard cases.

The **October–November intake change is strongly seasonal/population-driven** (about 90% confidence): both volume and high-complexity share jump at the known renewal-season boundary. The resulting performance peak is probably an interaction among seasonality, reduced capacity, residual rework, and priority policy (about 80% confidence), rather than a standalone seasonal effect. The senior-assessment increase is only moderately attributable to real behavior because historical recording was inconsistent.

December improvement is jointly attributable to lower load and active interventions, with only moderate confidence in either separate effect. Persistent adaptation remains plausible but unproven because rework stays above baseline after Supplier Z’s reported recovery. I would revise this account using supplier-by-complexity event-level DFGs, queue-entry timestamps, complete rosters, source-system provenance, intervention exposure, risk-adjusted outcomes, and observations from another renewal season.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops sharply while mapped fitness, normalized DFG distance, and outcomes remain stable and new labels or duplicates rise, THEN open a schema/provenance incident before escalating to process redesign, BECAUSE semantic migration can create false conformance drift.

2. IF normalized request-information loops rise within Supplier Z after adjustment for complexity, region, and intake week, THEN trigger supplier remediation and document-defect sampling, BECAUSE a within-supplier change is stronger evidence of bundle-quality failure than an aggregate share change.

3. IF queue waiting and backlog age rise while activity service times remain stable and workload per staffed assessor-hour increases, THEN add or rebalance capacity, BECAUSE the proximate performance mechanism is congestion rather than slower execution.

4. IF high-priority waiting improves after July while standard-case waiting worsens without a corresponding outcome benefit, THEN audit and retune the priority rule, BECAUSE aggregate stability can conceal an inequitable redistribution of delay.

5. IF aggregate DFG or cycle-time drift disappears after standardizing supplier, complexity, region, and season, THEN classify it primarily as population drift and update planning baselines, BECAUSE stable within-stratum behavior indicates composition rather than changed handling.

6. IF resource missingness rises for particular migrated activities or sources, THEN suspend resource-level rankings and repair provenance before organizational attribution, BECAUSE complete-case team analysis would be selectively biased.

7. IF mapped conformance, rework, queue waiting, or risk-adjusted outcomes remain materially abnormal across two mature cohorts after mix and season adjustment, THEN launch an event-level multi-mechanism review, BECAUSE persistent normalized drift is unlikely to be explained by logging or transient composition alone.
