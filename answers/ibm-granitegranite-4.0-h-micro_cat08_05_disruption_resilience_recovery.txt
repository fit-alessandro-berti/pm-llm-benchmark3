## 1. Disruption Timeline

**T0 (Day -30 to Day -1): Baseline Period**

- Normal operations with high throughput and low risk.
- System met all compliance and traceability requirements.
- Cycle times met targets: Hospital-critical (8h), Pharmacy-standard (23h), Wholesale-bulk (36h).

**T1 (Day 0 morning): Incident Begins**
- Warehouse Management System (WMS) goes offline due to cyber incident.
- Immediate loss of real-time inventory, order validation, and automated workflows.
- System availability dropped to 0% immediately, impacting all process stages.

**T2 (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2): Manual Workaround Phase**

- Teams improvised using manual processes like printed inventory snapshots, phone confirmations, and spreadsheets.
- **Patterns observed:** A, B, C, D, E, F.
- **Risk Posture:** 
  - **Resilience:** Some adaptations (A, B) preserved critical hospital orders and compliance checks in critical segments.
  - **Hidden Risk:** Patterns C, D, E, F introduced significant compliance and traceability risks, especially for cold-chain and controlled substances.

**T3 (Day 3 to Day 6): Partial Recovery**

- Order intake system partially restored; inventory synchronization unreliable.
- **Patterns observed:** G, H, I, K.
- **Risk Posture:** 
  - **Resilience:** Focused on critical items (hospital) with manual checks (F).
  - **Hidden Risk:** G (dispatch without scan) and I (defer noncritical orders) created gaps in audit trails and increased cancellation rates.

**T4 (Day 7 to Day 21): Stabilization**

- Systems mostly recovered; backlog and reconciliation ongoing.
- **Patterns observed:** J, L.
- **Risk Posture:** 
  - **Resilience:** J (backlog prioritization) and L (audit remediation) helped in restoring service levels.
  - **Hidden Risk:** Ongoing audit remediation created a backlog of compliance issues, potentially leading to future vulnerabilities.

## 3. Resilience versus Concealed Noncompliance

**Resilience** in this context refers to adaptations that maintain operational integrity, protect customer safety, and ensure traceability under disruption. These include adaptations A, B (modified), and J (prioritized backlog).

**Concealed Noncompliance** involves shortcuts that compromise system integrity but may appear effective in the short term. These include C, D (modified), E, F (retired), G, H (modified), I, and L (modified).

- **True Resilience:** Adaptations that maintain necessary controls while allowing for rapid response (A, B, J).
- **Risky Shortcuts:** Adaptations that bypass critical controls, increasing compliance and traceability risks (C, D, E, F, G, H, I, L).
- **Ambiguous Adaptations:** Patterns that could be beneficial if properly implemented but require strict oversight (J).

## 4. Recovery Process Model

### Trigger Conditions
- **System Unavailability:** Immediate halt of all automated processes.
- **Critical Order Queue:** Orders flagged as hospital-critical or controlled substances.
- **Compliance Gaps Detected:** Missing temperature documentation, authorization, or proof of delivery.

### Triage Rules
1. **Immediate Action:** 
   - Activate manual fast lane for hospital-critical orders.
   - Route controlled substances through double manual verification.
2. **System Restoration Check:** 
   - Verify system health before resuming automated workflows.
3. **Audit Trail Integration:** 
   - Ensure every manual entry is logged and traceable.

### Minimum Controls by Risk Level
- **Critical (Hospital):** Real-time authorization, dual-control for inventory, mandatory temperature checks.
- **Standard (Pharmacy):** Automated authorization with manual override for exceptions.
- **Bulk (Wholesale):** Automated processes with periodic manual audits.

### Manual Evidence Capture
- **Pick Lists:** Digitally captured and timestamped.
- **Proof of Delivery:** Phone entries entered into system within 30 minutes.
- **Temperature Documentation:** Manual logs scanned and uploaded electronically.

### Backlog Prioritization
- **Rule-Based Prioritization:** Orders prioritized by criticality and product risk.
- **Dynamic Reassessment:** Continuous evaluation and adjustment based on system recovery status.

### Reconciliation and Audit Remediation
- **Automated Reconciliation:** Compare manual and system records daily.
- **Audit Remediation Queue:** Dedicated team to address missing compliance items, with weekly reviews.

## 5. Resilience Indicators

- **Throughput:** Ability to process orders without system downtime.
- **Service Protection:** Percentage of critical orders delivered on time.
- **Traceability:** Accuracy and completeness of temperature and authorization records.
- **Control Completeness:** Presence of all required manual and automated controls.
- **Backlog Age:** Time orders spend in backlog before processing.
- **Rework Rate:** Frequency of corrections needed after system recovery.
- **Fairness Across Customer Groups:** Consistency in service delivery across hospital, pharmacy, and wholesale segments.

## 6. Recommendations for Future Disruptions

1. **Implement Automated Triggers:** Develop automated alerts for system failures to initiate recovery protocols instantly.
2. **Enhanced Manual Protocols:** Formalize manual processes with standardized checklists and training for compliance staff.
3. **Real-Time Audit Trail:** Ensure every manual entry is logged and integrated with the system for traceability.
4. **Dynamic Backlog Management:** Use risk-based prioritization to manage backlog effectively.
5. **Continuous Monitoring:** Implement continuous system health monitoring to detect and respond to issues before they impact operations.

**Implementation Effort:** High (requires system integration, training, and process redesign).
**Expected Benefit:** Significant reduction in recovery time and risk of compliance breaches.
**Residual Risk:** Low, assuming proper implementation and adherence to protocols.

## 7. Management Trade-off Statement

Management must balance the need to **preserve critical throughput and service levels** with the imperative to **maintain rigorous compliance and traceability**. While rapid response adaptations (A, B, J) are essential for protecting patient safety and meeting regulatory requirements, shortcuts (C, D, E, F, G, H, I, L) introduce unacceptable risks. Evidence indicating a shift towards resilient practices—such as formalized manual protocols, real-time audit trails, and dynamic backlog management—should drive decision-making, while any continuation of risky shortcuts should be promptly addressed.
