# 1. Decision Rule

### Eligibility  
- A case is eligible for intervention if it satisfies **all** of the following criteria:  
  1. Its current stage is `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started` (intervention is effective only before `Diagnosis Complete`).  
  2. Its predicted breach probability (`p_now`) is above **0.50**, unless the segment has specific calibration concerns.  
  3. There is a **credible evidence change** (e.g., materially updated information or new events), unless it’s the first alert.  
  4. It does not already have a repeated alert unless (a) the change in circumstances is clear or (b) manual review justifies reconsideration.

### Alert Persistence and Minimum Evidence Change  
- **Repeated Alerts**: A repeated alert is actionable only if accompanied by a material change in input data or events (e.g., a new customer log or diagnostic information). Suppress unchanged alerts that arise solely due to model updating.  
- For calibration issues:
  - **SME Segment**: Adjust `p_now` downward by 0.04 (due to consistent underprediction).  
  - **Strategic Enterprise Segment**: Adjust `p_now` upward by 0.08 (due to overprediction).  
  - **Non-Native-Language Support**: Treat model predictions with skepticism, and prioritize only when other indicators (e.g., evidence change or expert input) are aligned.

### Priority Determination  
1. Calculate **expected net value per slot**, accounting for:  
    - Estimated absolute breach risk reduction × breach value  
    - Subtract labor cost (€450), false-alarm burden, and spillover effects. Communicate as a score or tier to simplify comparison.  
2. Rank cases within a competitive queue based on the above, but penalize for:  
    - High uncertainty in calibration or effect estimates.  
    - Alert persistence without evidence changes (score decay for repeated cases without updates).

### Robustness to Uncertainty  
- Use the **lower bound** of the intervention's absolute risk reduction range in adversarial operating conditions (e.g., high congestion or miscalibrated segments).   
- Ensure fairness by reserving capacity for segments with higher calibration uncertainty (e.g., Non-Native-Language Support).

# 4. Repeated Alerts and Temporal Policy  

### I-05 vs. I-07 Comparison  
- **I-05**: Third alert with no material evidence change; action is **suppressed** because the system would waste capacity without clear justification for a difference in outcome.  
- **I-07**: Third alert with new customer logs, implying new diagnostic opportunities; action is **escalated** due to actionable new information.

### Temporal Rules  
1. **Cool-down Period**: Cases with unchanged breach probabilities should not be eligible for re-alerting for at least **2 full cycles** unless a new event arises.  
2. **Escalation Threshold**: Increase priority for repeating cases only when the breach risk changes by at least **10 percentage points** or other material information arises.  
3. **Re-entry Logic**: Suppressed cases re-enter evaluation if (a) there is a new event, or (b) elapsed time necessitates a procedural refresh (e.g., two SLA thresholds pass).

# 5. Fairness and Feedback-Loop Risks  

### Identified Risks and Mitigations  

1. **Value-Based Starvation**: High-value cases dominate intervention allocation, leaving lower-value cases untreated.  
   - **Safeguard**: Design a minimum guaranteed slot allocation (e.g., 20% per week) for underrepresented segments like Public Sector or SME.  

2. **Non-Native-Language Support Uncertainty**: Sparse data for both breach probabilities and intervention effects risks biased or inequitable decisions.  
   - **Safeguard**: Require manual review for major decisions or develop targeted recalibration models after data is consolidated.

3. **Differential Calibration**: Model errors across segments create unequal likelihood of intervention.  
   - **Safeguard**: Regular backtesting and segment-specific re-weighting thresholds.

4. **Selective Intervention Changing Labels**: Prioritized interventions distort historical training data.  
   - **Safeguard**: Retain treated and untreated data under separate model feeds, and use independent estimation for counterfactual outcomes.  

5. **Spillover Distribution**: Queued cases facing spillover harm may fall systematically among lower-priority incidents.  
   - **Safeguard**: Require spillover impact logging and diagnostic queue diversity audits to detect systematic harm spread.

# 6. Monitoring and Recalibration Plan  

### Operational Monitoring  
- Track intervention slots used, spillover delays, breach rates, and alerts suppressed versus escalated.

### Predictive Monitoring  
- Conduct bi-weekly recalibration tests for each segment to detect systematic deviations in predicted vs. actual breach rates.  
- Develop an interim model to prioritize Non-Native-Support cases once sample size grows.

### Causal-Effect Monitoring  
- Use a random holdout group (non-intervened cases) to measure baseline breach rates and infer intervention efficacy.

### Capacity Monitoring  
- Assess whether the current allocation rules leave adequate flexibility for future weeks. Aim for 80–90% slot utilization with safety margins.

### Fairness Monitoring  
- Publish weekly reports breaking down slot allocation, spillover harms, and calibration error by segment and business priority.

---

By integrating these principles, the policy ensures decision-theoretic coherence, operational realism, and a robust approach to mitigating uncertainty.
