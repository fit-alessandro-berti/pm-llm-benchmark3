## 1. Duplicate-handling decision tree

Preserve an immutable raw snapshot first. Generate candidates using source keys, business identifiers, time, source activity meaning and object overlap; none of those alone establishes duplication.

1. Same source and source event key, identical business attributes and identical E2O relations? **Drop** the redundant technical copy after archiving it. A repeated key with conflicting content is **quarantined for review**.
2. Reliable reconciliation shows that different records describe the same physical event? **Merge attributes and relations**, retaining both source versions and using a documented per-field authority policy. If meanings, identities or relations conflict without an explanation, **quarantine for review**.
3. One record summarizes, reports or is derived from another? **Retain both with refined labels or qualifiers** at their respective abstraction levels. A transition-level view may merge them only after confirming the derivation and explicitly choosing that grain.
4. Equal harmonized labels hide different source meanings? **Retain both with refined labels or qualifiers**, and preserve the original source meanings.
5. Concurrent same-label occurrences concern different business objects or transaction IDs? **Retain both unchanged** when their labels are already accurate.
6. Evidence does not resolve the relationship? **Quarantine the candidate for review**, while retaining both records unchanged in the analytical log with an uncertainty flag.

The tests are not sequential deletion operations. Reconciliation and specific business identity override superficial label/time matches; an absent relation is not evidence that an event did not concern an object.

## 3. Timestamp-tie ordering rules

Keep the original timestamp as the time coordinate. Within each equal-time bucket construct a directed graph, then topologically sort it. The graph contains semantic constraints supported by evidence:

- Explicit domain facts: e09 precedes e10 and e12 precedes e13.
- Within-source sequence: compare sequence numbers only within the same source, and only when they encode business precedence. Preserve sequence evidence from every alias of a merged event; never compare numbers across sources.
- Lifecycle constraints: apply a validated rule only to the appropriate shared object, such as authorization before capture for M1. Do not infer one transaction's lifecycle from another's.
- Source-specific semantics and identified message causality: add edges only when the source contract or evidence establishes them. For example, release before carrier acceptance is plausible, but is not imposed here without a validated handoff rule. A generic summary does not identify all of its inputs.

When several nodes are eligible, select the smallest stable canonical ID. This fallback is deterministic, not semantic evidence. Ingestion time is technical and is not the primary business order or a precedence constraint; if used as an optional fallback it must be labeled arbitrary. Detect cycles, report the contributing constraints and resolve them rather than silently ignoring an edge. Preserve the graph, so a total-order export does not erase knowledge of incomparable events.

Under the conservative decisions, one admissible order for each tie is:

- 10:00: e03, e05, e06, e07. The scanner alias e04 establishes e03 before e05, and WMS sequence establishes e03 before e06. Without an additional validated rule, e05 versus e06 and e06 versus e07 are not forced; e03, e06, e05, e07 is also admissible.
- 10:15: e08, e09, e10. e09 precedes e10; without a validated release-to-acceptance rule, e09, e08, e10 is also admissible.
- 12:00: e11, e12, e13, e14, e15. PSP sequence orders e12, e13, e14; ERP sequence orders e11 before e15. The exact payment messages underlying e15 are not identified, so no capture-to-summary edge is assumed. Other topological orders are admissible.

Adding microseconds before choosing semantic precedence makes arbitrary row order appear to be observed business time. Even after sorting, increments are only a compatibility representation: a deterministic total order does not prove physical order or elapsed time.

## 4. Conservative cleaning pipeline

Archive raw events, objects, relations, source dictionaries and pre-harmonization meanings. Profile exact technical duplicates and candidate groups, then adjudicate them using source evidence. Merge only e03/e04 and e11/e16, with canonical IDs e03 and e11. Keep every source record and attribute, resolve fields using an authority matrix where one exists, and flag unresolved conflicts. Deduplicate only identical resulting relation rows; preserve qualifiers and source-specific relation evidence.

Keep e06/e07 and e15 at their respective abstraction levels, qualifying them as WMS packing transaction, ERP packing status, and ERP payment balance update. Refine e08 to “Release from Warehouse” and e09 to “Accept by Carrier.” Keep e05 and e14. Store original identifiers, labels, timestamps and sources as well as the precedence graph and rank. An optional adjusted-timestamp export is separate from the original-time analytical view.

The listed input has **17 events and 44 E2O relations**. The conservative view has **15 events and 39 relations**, assuming one unqualified row for each listed event/object pair: e04 contributes three redundant links and e16 two. Distinct qualifiers or additional technical duplicates change the relation count. The 15 retained events are observations at different grains, not necessarily 15 physical transitions.

## 5. Aggressive alternatives

**Evidence-based transition merge.** Starting from the conservative view, merge e07 into e06 only after confirming their particular derivation and choosing transition-level analysis. Preserve the ERP observation, attributes and links in the audit record. The expected result is **14 events and 37 E2O relations**, since e07's O1/P1 links already occur on e06. The separate ERP reporting step and its latency disappear from the event stream, and the merged row can misleadingly suggest simultaneous observations. Keep e15 separate until its payment coverage is established.

**Mechanical PM4Py merge.** On an independent copy of the raw harmonized log, run `ocel_merge_duplicates(..., have_common_object=True)` before refining labels. For the installed implementation and exactly the listed unqualified relations, expect **10 events and 28 E2O relations**: it combines e03/e04/e05, e06/e07, e08/e09, e11/e16, and e13/e14/e15. Verify actual groups and counts for the installed version. Distinct picks, release and acceptance, and partial captures collapse; the union of their objects attaches to a single representative event. Attributes and generated IDs may also be lost or replaced.

`have_common_object=True` requires overlap, not matching transaction identity. e13 and e14 share O1 and V1 but concern distinct Payment objects M1 and M2. Shared objects do not protect those captures, and do not prove that e15 describes either capture.

## 6. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation

This example targets the listed data and the installed PM4Py API. It requires unique event IDs, matching event and relation timestamps and activities, and the metadata columns shown in the question. It refuses nonempty E2E relations: a production implementation must remap and audit them rather than leave dangling references. The audit and archive should be persisted durably. The conditional transition merge remains disabled until an analyst confirms its evidence.

```python
import copy
import heapq
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

archive = copy.deepcopy(ocel_raw)
base = copy.deepcopy(ocel_raw)
eid, oid = base.event_id_column, base.object_id_column
act, ts, ot = base.event_activity, base.event_timestamp, base.object_type_column
assert base.events[eid].is_unique and base.objects[oid].is_unique
assert getattr(base, "e2e", None) is None or base.e2e.empty, \
    "Remap and audit E2E links before merging events"

base.events["original_timestamp"] = base.events[ts].copy()
base.events["original_activity"] = base.events[act].copy()
base.events["source_event_ids"] = base.events[eid].map(lambda x: (x,))
# All source fields, including source and source_event_key, remain accessible.
base.events["source_records"] = base.events.apply(lambda r: (r.to_dict(),), axis=1)

candidates = (base.events.groupby([act, ts], dropna=False)[eid].agg(list)
              .loc[lambda s: s.map(len) > 1])
print("Activity/time candidates for review:", candidates.to_dict())
print("Repeated source keys for review:",
      base.events.loc[base.events.duplicated(["source", "source_event_key"],
                                             keep=False),
                      [eid, "source", "source_event_key"]])

def validate(log):
    ev, obj = log.events.set_index(eid), log.objects.set_index(oid)
    assert ev.index.is_unique and obj.index.is_unique
    assert log.relations[eid].notna().all() and log.relations[oid].notna().all()
    assert log.relations[eid].isin(ev.index).all()
    assert log.relations[oid].isin(obj.index).all()
    assert (log.relations[act].to_numpy() ==
            log.relations[eid].map(ev[act]).to_numpy()).all()
    assert (log.relations[ts].to_numpy() ==
            log.relations[eid].map(ev[ts]).to_numpy()).all()
    assert (log.relations[ot].to_numpy() ==
            log.relations[oid].map(obj[ot]).to_numpy()).all()

validate(base)
audit = []

def verified_merge(log, canonical, others, rule, confidence):
    """Only adjudicated pairs; retain all source rows and relation evidence."""
    ids = [canonical] + list(others)
    rows = log.events.set_index(eid).loc[ids]
    assert rows.index.is_unique and rows[ts].nunique(dropna=False) == 1
    assert rows[act].nunique(dropna=False) == 1
    rels = log.relations[log.relations[eid].isin(ids)].copy(deep=True)
    records = tuple(rec for group in rows["source_records"] for rec in group)
    aliases = tuple(alias for group in rows["source_event_ids"] for alias in group)
    conflicts = {}
    for col in rows.columns:
        if col in ("source_records", "source_event_ids"):
            continue
        vals = [(i, rows.at[i, col]) for i in ids if pd.notna(rows.at[i, col])]
        if len({repr(v) for _, v in vals}) > 1:
            conflicts[col] = vals
    audit.append({"canonical_eid": canonical, "source_eids": aliases,
                  "rule": rule, "reviewer_or_rule_version": "example-v1",
                  "confidence": confidence, "source_rows": records,
                  "original_relations": rels.to_dict("records"),
                  "conflicts_not_in_canonical_view": conflicts})
    ci = log.events.index[log.events[eid].eq(canonical)][0]
    log.events.at[ci, "source_event_ids"] = aliases
    log.events.at[ci, "source_records"] = records
    # Canonical row is a projection; noncanonical values remain in records.
    # Apply field-specific authority after it is supplied by the data owner.
    log.events = log.events[~log.events[eid].isin(others)].copy()
    log.relations.loc[log.relations[eid].isin(others), eid] = canonical
    # Distinct qualifiers and other relation attributes remain separate.
    log.relations = log.relations.drop_duplicates().copy()
    validate(log)

conservative = copy.deepcopy(base)
verified_merge(conservative, "e03", ["e04"], "reconciled physical scan", "high")
verified_merge(conservative, "e11", ["e16"], "invoice V1 issuance", "high")

refined = {"e06": "Pack Package [WMS transaction]",
           "e07": "Pack Package [ERP status]",
           "e08": "Release from Warehouse", "e09": "Accept by Carrier",
           "e15": "Payment Balance Update [ERP]"}
conservative.events[act] = conservative.events[eid].map(refined).fillna(
    conservative.events[act])
conservative.relations[act] = conservative.relations[eid].map(
    conservative.events.set_index(eid)[act])
validate(conservative)

# Installed ocel_drop_duplicates removes RELATIONS using activity/time/object.
# Restrict the demonstration to e17, whose group contains no competing event.
# This is a no-op on the stated data; it could remove only exact repeated links.
scope = copy.deepcopy(conservative)
scope.events = scope.events[scope.events[eid].eq("e17")].copy()
scope.relations = scope.relations[scope.relations[eid].eq("e17")].copy()
scope.objects = scope.objects[scope.objects[oid].isin(scope.relations[oid])].copy()
k = [act, ts, oid]
assert scope.relations.groupby(k, dropna=False)[eid].nunique().le(1).all()
for _, group in scope.relations.groupby(k, dropna=False):
    assert len(group.drop_duplicates()) == 1, "Different relation attributes"
assert set(scope.events[eid]) == set(scope.relations[eid])
assert set(scope.objects[oid]) == set(scope.relations[oid])
scoped_deduplicated = pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates(scope)
validate(scoped_deduplicated)
# Do not apply this function to the full log: e03 and e05 share O1/P1.

evidence = copy.deepcopy(conservative)
confirmed_e06_e07_transition = False  # set only after specific confirmation
if confirmed_e06_e07_transition:
    evidence.events.loc[evidence.events[eid].isin(["e06", "e07"]), act] = "Pack Package"
    evidence.relations[act] = evidence.relations[eid].map(evidence.events.set_index(eid)[act])
    verified_merge(evidence, "e06", ["e07"], "confirmed same transition", "conditional")
    evidence.events.loc[evidence.events[eid].eq("e06"), act] = "Pack Package [transition]"
    evidence.relations[act] = evidence.relations[eid].map(evidence.events.set_index(eid)[act])
    validate(evidence)

# WARNING: deliberately over-merges; never substitute for adjudication.
aggressive = copy.deepcopy(base)
members = (base.events.groupby([act, ts], dropna=False)[eid]
           .agg(lambda s: tuple(sorted(s))).to_dict())
aggressive = pm4py.ocel_merge_duplicates(aggressive, have_common_object=True)
validate(aggressive)
print("Mechanical candidate memberships:", members)
print("Mechanical event/relation counts:", len(aggressive.events), len(aggressive.relations))
# This dataset's equal-activity/time groups all share objects. The library can
# generate UUIDs; verify the one-group-per-key result before restoring stable IDs.
keys = list(zip(aggressive.events[act], aggressive.events[ts]))
assert len(keys) == len(set(keys)) == len(members) and all(k in members for k in keys)
id_map = {row[eid]: min(members[(row[act], row[ts])])
          for _, row in aggressive.events.iterrows()}
aggressive.events[eid] = aggressive.events[eid].map(id_map)
aggressive.relations[eid] = aggressive.relations[eid].map(id_map)
validate(aggressive)

def ordered_copy(log, adjust=False):
    """Order a verified-merge view; archive constraints, not just ranks."""
    out = copy.deepcopy(log)
    alias_to_id = {alias: row[eid] for _, row in out.events.iterrows()
                   for alias in row["source_event_ids"]}
    assert set(alias_to_id) == set(base.events[eid])
    src = base.events.copy()
    src["canonical"] = src[eid].map(alias_to_id)
    ranks, graph_audit = {}, []
    for time, bucket in out.events.groupby(ts, sort=True):
        nodes, edges = set(bucket[eid]), set()
        sr = src[src["original_timestamp"].eq(time)]
        for _, group in sr.groupby("source"):
            group = group.dropna(subset=["source_seq"]).sort_values(["source_seq", eid])
            seqs = list(group[["source_seq", "canonical"]].itertuples(index=False, name=None))
            for i, (seq_a, a) in enumerate(seqs):
                for seq_b, b in seqs[i + 1:]:
                    if seq_a < seq_b and a != b:
                        edges.add((a, b))
        for a, b in [("e09", "e10"), ("e12", "e13")]:
            a, b = alias_to_id[a], alias_to_id[b]
            if a in nodes and b in nodes and a != b:
                edges.add((a, b))
        following = {n: set() for n in nodes}
        degree = {n: 0 for n in nodes}
        for a, b in edges:
            following[a].add(b)
            degree[b] += 1
        ready = [n for n in nodes if degree[n] == 0]
        heapq.heapify(ready)
        order = []
        while ready:
            n = heapq.heappop(ready)
            order.append(n)
            for nxt in sorted(following[n]):
                degree[nxt] -= 1
                if degree[nxt] == 0:
                    heapq.heappush(ready, nxt)
        if len(order) != len(nodes):
            raise ValueError(f"Conflicting precedence at {time}: {sorted(edges)}")
        ranks.update({n: i for i, n in enumerate(order)})
        graph_audit.append((time, sorted(edges), order))
    out.events["tie_rank"] = out.events[eid].map(ranks)
    out = pm4py.ocel_sort_by_additional_column(out, "tie_rank", primary_column=ts)
    position = {x: i for i, x in enumerate(out.events[eid])}
    out.relations["_order"] = out.relations[eid].map(position)
    out.relations = (out.relations.sort_values(["_order", oid], kind="stable")
                     .drop(columns="_order").reset_index(drop=True))
    if adjust:
        # Installed PM4Py adds GLOBAL index milliseconds, not microseconds.
        adjusted = pm4py.ocel_add_index_based_timedelta(copy.deepcopy(out))
        original = adjusted.events["original_timestamp"].tolist()
        shifted = adjusted.events[ts].tolist()
        for i, value in enumerate(original):
            next_time = next((t for t in original[i + 1:] if t > value), None)
            if next_time is not None and shifted[i] >= next_time:
                raise ValueError("Synthetic offset crosses original time bucket")
        assert shifted == sorted(shifted)
        out = adjusted
    validate(out)
    return out, graph_audit

conservative_ordered, conservative_graph = ordered_copy(conservative)
evidence_ordered, evidence_graph = ordered_copy(evidence)
# Optional compatibility export; do not use adjusted times for real durations.
conservative_adjusted, _ = ordered_copy(conservative, adjust=True)
print("Conservative counts:", len(conservative.events), len(conservative.relations))
```

## 8. Audit trail and reversibility

Keep immutable raw event, object, E2O and E2E tables, source dictionaries and pre-harmonization labels. Store a versioned decision for every candidate, including retained and unresolved pairs:

- canonical ID, source event IDs, source systems and source event keys;
- merge or retention rule, reviewer or rule version, confidence, reason and reconciliation evidence reference;
- original source activity, harmonized activity and refined activity; original and adjusted timestamps, timestamp precision, rank, precedence edges and tie-break policy;
- attributes retained from each source, field-level authority, conflicting values and values discarded from the canonical projection;
- original and resulting relations, object IDs and types, qualifiers and source, plus any remapped E2E links.

Record scenario, input checksums, software version, parameters and validation counts. “Discarded” applies only to the canonical projection; the source value remains archived. Reconstruct the raw view from the immutable source records and original relations, independently of adjusted timestamps. Reconstruct a scenario by replaying its versioned decisions and order graph. This lets an analyst undo one merge without reversing unrelated cleaning.

## 9. Failure modes and safeguards

1. **Collapse distinct partial payments:** compare payment and transaction identities and preserve e13 and e14 even though O1/V1 overlap.
2. **Convert abstraction-level updates into false rework:** identify e06/e07 and e15 by source and grain; distinguish physical-transition metrics from observation metrics.
3. **Order by ingestion time:** use supported semantic edges; retain ingestion time as technical metadata and label any fallback as arbitrary.
4. **Treat shared objects as proof of duplication:** require business identity and source meaning, not common O1/V1 alone.
5. **Union incompatible E2O relations:** validate each relation and qualifier, preserve its source, and quarantine unexplained conflicts; an indiscriminate union can fabricate a joint event.
6. **Change durations through synthetic microseconds:** retain original timestamps and use them for performance. The installed PM4Py helper actually adds global index-based milliseconds, so check for crossing original time buckets.
7. **Lose source-authoritative attributes:** archive complete source rows, apply a field-level authority matrix and flag unresolved conflicts.
8. **Apply duplicate functions to the whole OCEL without inspecting groups:** profile first and use isolated copies; the PM4Py dropper removes relations and can also remove events through propagation.
9. **Create contradictory precedence:** detect cycles, retain the supporting evidence and review source sequence and lifecycle assumptions before exporting an order.
10. **Lose IDs, qualifiers or integrity:** maintain aliases and relation provenance, validate references and synchronized activity/timestamp fields, and explicitly remap E2E links. Library-generated IDs alone are not reproducible.
11. **Overinterpret one total order:** retain incomparable pairs and test alternative topological orders; deterministic adjacency does not prove physical sequence.
12. **Confuse cleaning with improved performance:** compare counts, variants and durations with raw and alternative scenarios, including effects of label and grain changes.
