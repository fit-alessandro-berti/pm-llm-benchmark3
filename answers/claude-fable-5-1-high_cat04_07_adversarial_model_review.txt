# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

**Use cases I distinguish.** The two submissions are being compared as if there were one "best model". There is not. I review them against four distinct uses, because the acceptable fitness/precision trade-off is different for each:

| Use case | What the model must do well | Fitness posture | Precision posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exploration / discovery | Replay almost everything so analysts see where reality deviates; never silently hide a deviation | High fitness required, but *deviations must remain visible as deviations* | Low precision tolerable, but the model must not encode policy violations as normal behavior, otherwise exploration hides exactly what it should reveal |
| Conformance enforcement / compliance | Reject policy violations, accept every policy-valid trace including combinations not yet observed | Fitness only matters on *policy-valid* traces; unfitting noncompliant traces are a feature | High precision required, measured against negative cases, not only against the positive log |
| Simulation / what-if | Produce realistic traces with realistic branching frequencies | Must be able to generate legal combinations that did not occur yet | Must not generate illegal traces (skipping a mandatory check would corrupt any KPI simulation) |
| Stakeholder communication / training | Be readable and correctly convey the rules | Secondary | Secondary, but the picture must not *misrepresent* the rule set (a picture that says "Credit Check is optional" is wrong, however readable) |

**Assumptions I make explicit.**

- A1. The process-tree strings are the models. I reason from their formal semantics (pm4py process-tree semantics: `->` sequence, `X` exclusive choice, `+` interleaving parallelism, `*` loop, `tau` silent). Both submitted trees are loop-free, so their languages are finite and acceptance is decidable by inspection; I flag any parsing uncertainty where relevant.
- A2. "Accepts" means a perfect alignment (zero-cost apart from silent moves, i.e., alignment fitness 1.0). Partial fitness values are not acceptance.
- A3. The policy P1–P7 is the reference, with two acknowledged gaps: no sourcing attribute (P3) and no affected-check attribute (P6). Where policy is silent (for example, whether Supplier Review may precede the mandatory checks, or whether a correction may follow Expedite Review), I state the design decision rather than pretending the policy answered it.
- A4. The reported metrics were produced by two different teams with undisclosed seeds, preprocessing, cost functions, and holdout cases. I treat every number as a rough indication of order of magnitude and never as a comparable measurement.
- A5. The 80/20 split is a case split, presumably random, not temporal; I cannot verify this.

**How use changes the trade-off.** For exploration, a model like S with fitness ~0.98 is acceptable *only if* its permissiveness is understood as "not enforcing" rather than "everything here is allowed"; the moment the picture is shown to a business user as "the process", it becomes a misrepresentation. For enforcement, holdout fitness of 0.744 (Model C) is not by itself disqualifying — if the 25% missing fitness were truly noncompliant behavior, that would be the model doing its job. What disqualifies it is that F4 says the unfitting holdout cases are legitimate new combinations, and the probe matrix confirms C rejects at least one trace (B2) that policy explicitly declares valid. So the fitness gap is a false-negative gap, not a compliance signal. That distinction — *why* a trace fails — is the whole review.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for S.** It is sound and block-structured, has 9 leaves and zero duplicate labels, is trivially maintainable (adding an activity = one new leaf), aligns in milliseconds, and replays ~98% of unseen cases. For a discovery workshop it lets analysts overlay frequencies on a stable picture. Its permissiveness is at least partly deliberate: "robust to missing events" means that a case whose Supplier Review event was lost by a system interface (F4 confirms this happens) does not show up as a deviation. Its advocate's position F7 — exploration, not enforcement — is coherent if and only if the model is labelled that way everywhere it appears.

**Strongest case against S.** It is not a model of the policy; it is a model of "some subset of four activities in some order, then an approval". Quantitatively: the parallel block of four `X(a, tau)` choices admits every subset of {Credit Check, Technical Review, Supplier Review, Expedite Review} in every order: 1 + 4 + 12 + 24 + 24 = 65 orderings, times 2 approvals = 130 traces in its language. Of these, only orderings that contain both mandatory checks and place Expedite Review (when present) after both of them satisfy P2 and P4: {CC,TR}: 2; {CC,TR,SR}: 6; {CC,TR,ER}: 2; {CC,TR,SR,ER}: 8 → 18 orderings × 2 approvals = 36 traces. So 94 of 130 traces (72%) in S's language violate P2 or P4 even before considering the sourcing attribute. Meanwhile its language contains zero traces with Correct Dossier, so P6 and P7 lie entirely outside it. The "robust to missing events" claim is also only half true: S tolerates *absent* events but cannot replay *repeated* events or corrections at all.

**Concrete undesirable or policy-ambiguous behaviors admitted by S (all fit with alignment fitness 1.0, since only silent moves are needed):**

1. Skip both mandatory checks (violates P2): `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` (= B3).
2. Skip one mandatory check and auto-approve (violates P2, and the combination of "no human check" with "no human approval" is the worst possible compliance case): `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order`.
3. Expedite Review before the mandatory checks (violates P4): `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (= B4).
4. Expedite Review as the *only* review, with auto-approval: `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`.
5. Supplier Review as the only activity between registration and approval: `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order`.
6. Expedite Review between the two mandatory checks (P4 requires both checks before it): `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`.
7. Supplier Review on what may be an internal order (policy-ambiguous, undecidable without the attribute; S cannot even record the ambiguity): `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Supplier Review, Auto Approval, Release Order`.

**Behaviors S *cannot* express, which is a different kind of flaw:**

8. Any correction: `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` (= B6) → rejected with two log moves (Correct Dossier and the second Technical Review), approximate fitness 0.8.
9. Suspicious repetition without correction: `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` (= B8) → rejected with one log move, approximate fitness ~0.89.

Note the perverse ordering: S gives the suspicious trace B8 a *higher* fitness than the possibly legitimate correction trace B6, and gives the forbidden trace B3 a perfect score. Fitness under S is anti-correlated with policy on exactly the cases that matter.

**Intentional noise tolerance vs accidental semantic flaws.**

- Intentional: making Supplier Review skippable (the attribute is missing, and events are known to be lost) is a defensible modelling choice for exploration. Making Expedite Review skippable is correct (P4 says optional).
- Intentional but misplaced: making Credit Check and Technical Review skippable "for robustness to missing events" moves a *data-quality* assumption into *control-flow semantics*. The right place for missing-event tolerance is the alignment cost function or a preprocessing/repair step, not the model. This is the single largest flaw in S and it is the one the team advertises as a feature.
- Accidental: placing Expedite Review in the same parallel block as the checks (P4 orders it after them; nothing in the log or policy suggested otherwise), and omitting Correct Dossier entirely while claiming robustness. The absence of any loop means S rejects the very "duplicate" events a noise-tolerant model would be expected to absorb. These are modelling errors, not design choices.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case for C.** Every branch is a concrete, readable standard operating procedure. It rejects B3, B4 and B8 correctly; it never lets a mandatory check be skipped; it orders Expedite Review after the checks; it forces both rechecks after a correction; it never auto-approves an externally sourced order (in its language, Auto Approval only occurs with no Supplier Review). Its advocate's default-deny stance (F8) is a legitimate compliance philosophy: unseen behavior is quarantined until someone approves it, and adding a branch is an explicit, auditable governance event. For an auditor who wants a whitelist, C is a whitelist.

**Strongest case against C.**

- *Branch enumeration encodes accidental correlations as rules.* C says Auto Approval is only legal when Credit Check preceded Technical Review and no Supplier/Expedite Review happened (branch 8 only). It says Supplier Review + Expedite Review is only legal with Credit Check first (branch 6). It says a correction always requires both rechecks, always in the order Credit Check then Technical Review, always ends in Manager Approval, and happens at most once. None of these are in P1–P7; they are artifacts of which variants happened to be frequent in the training window. Of the 36 correction-free orderings that P2/P4/P5 allow, C contains 7 (≈19%). Every other legal combination is a false negative.
- *It rejects a trace the policy explicitly calls valid.* B2 (`Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order`) satisfies P1, P2 (either order), P5. C rejects it because branch 2 ends in Manager Approval and branch 8 has Credit Check first. Best alignment costs two moves (fitness ≈ 0.8). A compliance model that flags valid cases teaches operations to ignore its alarms.
- *Unseen combinations are the normal case, not the exception.* Two check orders × optional Supplier Review × optional Expedite Review (with 2 relative orders when both present) × 2 approvals already gives 20 correction-free combinations under the conservative reading, before corrections multiply them. A model that must be re-enumerated each time a new legal combination is observed will never be complete, and every gap is a false alarm.
- *Correction handling is wrong in both directions.* It rejects B6 (single recheck, policy says valid if the correction affected only Technical Review) and rejects any second correction, while offering no way to express the affected-check decision F6 asks for. It is also silent on the P7 distinction: B8 is rejected simply because it is not one of the 8 strings, not because a rule about corrections was violated. The model has no rule; it has a list.
- *Holdout degradation.* 0.973 → 0.744 is not "unseen behavior is noncompliant" (F8) unless someone has audited the unfitting holdout cases and found them noncompliant. F4 says the opposite. Until that audit exists, the degradation is evidence of overfitting, and the burden of proof is on the C team.
- *High precision as an artifact.* A model whose language is (a subset of) the training variants has escaping-edge precision near 1 by construction. It would score 0.94 no matter whether the 8 variants were legal, illegal, or random. Precision was also computed against positive traces only (F5); it cannot reward C for rejecting bad traces or punish it for rejecting good ones. The metric is not measuring what the C team says it measures.
- *Duplicate labels.* 48 labelled leaves for 9 activities. Practical consequences: (i) every change to an activity (owner, name, system) touches 4–9 nodes (F3); (ii) alignments face a choice among identical labels — the prefix `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review` is consistent with 6 of 8 branches, so online/prefix conformance cannot commit to a branch and offline alignment cost grows with the branch count (part of the 0.44 s); (iii) TBR generalization is mechanically depressed; (iv) the root-level XOR of 8 sequences converts to a net with an 8-way choice at the very first place, which is where the arc-degree simplicity of 0.281 comes from.
- *Maintainability.* Adding one attribute (say, a new optional review) doubles the branch count. Fixing B2 requires adding branches, which raises fitness and lowers precision without any change in the underlying rule. The model has no abstraction level at which "Credit Check and Technical Review may be in either order" can be stated once.

Verdict on the memorization question: yes, C's precision is predominantly a memorization artifact. The combination "training fitness < 1, holdout fitness collapses, precision ≈ 1, and the model is literally a disjunction of sequences" is the signature of a variant whitelist.

# 5. Behavior-Probe Matrix

Semantics used: both trees are loop-free, so languages are finite and acceptance is exact. For S, any trace is accepted iff it is `Register Dossier`, then some subset of the four review activities each at most once in any order, then exactly one of the approvals, then `Release Order`. For C, a trace is accepted iff it equals one of the 8 enumerated sequences. There is no loop or parsing ambiguity that affects these verdicts; the only parsing caveat is that `tau` must be recognized as the silent leaf by the parser, which pm4py's `parse_process_tree` does.

| Probe | Model S accepts? | Model C accepts? | Policy assessment | Consequence for review |

| B2 `RD, TR, CC, AA, RO` | Yes | **No** (branch 2 has MA; branch 8 has CC first; best alignment cost 2, fitness ≈ 0.8) | valid | C produces a false negative on a policy-valid trace. Disqualifying for C as a compliance model unless the board changes the policy to forbid this combination. |
| B3 `RD, MA, RO` | **Yes** (all four checks skipped via tau, fitness 1.0) | No | forbidden | S produces a perfect-fitness score for the most serious violation. Disqualifying for S as a compliance model, and dangerous for simulation. |

Summary: S is wrong on B3 and B4 (accepts forbidden) and blind on B6/B8 (cannot express corrections). C is wrong on B2 (rejects valid), over-strict on B5, and inflexible on B6. Only B1 and B7 are handled identically and correctly by both.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by likely impact on the board's decision (1 = most consequential).

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior.** S's 0.992/0.981 is achieved by wrapping every check in `X(a, tau)`. Silent skips are free in the standard alignment cost, so B3 scores 1.0. Any model that makes everything optional will win on fitness; the metric cannot penalize it. This is the pathology that makes the fitness row of the table meaningless for S.

2. **Positive-log-only precision and memorization.** C's 0.941 comes from a model that is a disjunction of observed sequences; escaping-edge precision against the same log is then near 1 regardless of policy correctness (F5). No negative traces were used, so the metric cannot detect that C rejects B2. Conversely, it cannot detect that S accepts B3 — precision just says "S allows more than seen", not "S allows forbidden things".

3. **Train/holdout leakage and split sensitivity, and non-reproducible pipeline.** No seeds, no case IDs, no preprocessing. A random case split leaves the same variants on both sides, so C's holdout fitness is likely *optimistic* relative to a temporal split, and S's holdout number is uninformative either way. Two teams claiming "the same split" without evidence means the comparison is not reproducible; the one number that discriminates the models most (0.981 vs 0.744) is exactly the one we cannot trust. Two rows that need no log at all already fail reproduction: the duplicate-label count (39 by direct count vs 33 reported) and the arc-degree simplicity, which comes out S ≈ 0.64 / C ≈ 0.89 with the standard pm4py conversion — the reverse of the reported 0.918 / 0.281. If the log-free numbers are wrong, the log-dependent numbers deserve no more trust.

4. **Missing-event bias.** F4 says Supplier Review events are sometimes lost. S absorbs the loss (fitness up, and the loss becomes invisible); C penalizes it (fitness down) even when the case was compliant. Both react to a data-quality issue as if it were a process issue. Without the sourcing attribute, neither can tell "Supplier Review not required" from "Supplier Review not logged". The bias inflates the fitness gap between S and C.

5. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness / size confounding.** TBR generalization measures how often each transition fires. S: few transitions, fired constantly → 0.907. C: ~48 transitions, most rarely fired → 0.412. The metric would also score a flower model near 1. It has no relation to out-of-sample behavior and should not have been presented as "generalization" without that caveat.

6. **Duplicated-label effects.** 39 (by my count) extra labelled transitions in C affect (i) alignment search — A* must keep several branch hypotheses alive, which inflates runtime and, depending on the implementation, can affect which optimal alignment is reported; (ii) TBR generalization (each duplicate splits the visit counts); (iii) escaping-edge precision (state-based computation on a net where one label maps to several transitions); (iv) the duplicate-label count itself, whose undocumented definition (33) I could not reproduce.

7. **Silent-transition effects.** S's net has a tau skip per optional activity plus parallel split/join taus. Silent model moves cost 0 (or a negligible epsilon in pm4py), so skipping four checks costs nothing — this is the mechanism behind pathology 1. Precision is also affected: silent transitions enlarge the set of reachable markings, which changes the escaping-edge denominator in a way that depends on the tool's handling of tau closures.

8. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Standard unit costs treat "Supplier Review missing" (model move) and "Supplier Review extra" (log move) identically, and treat a skipped Credit Check the same as a skipped Expedite Review. With policy-weighted costs (high cost for model moves on mandatory checks, low cost for Supplier Review moves), C's holdout fitness would move and S's would not (S never needs a visible model move). Neither team reported their cost function, so neither fitness number is interpretable to more than one decimal place.

9. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** Informal, unknown n, unknown raters, unknown question. S's 4.4 coexists with F1, which shows that at least one careful reader understood S and rejected it. "Comprehension" likely measured visual appeal. A proper protocol asks raters to classify probe traces using the diagram and scores accuracy.

10. **Runtime confounding by model size and by fit.** 0.44 s vs 0.018 s conflates branch count, duplicate labels, and the fact that a quarter of C's holdout traces do not fit and therefore need longer A* searches. Median also hides the tail. Practically irrelevant for batch audit; reported as if it were a model quality.

# 7. Third Alternative

**Strategy.** A block-structured procedural backbone (process tree, with an equivalent POWL construction) that encodes exactly the control-flow rules P1–P7 that do not need attributes, plus a small declarative/post-hoc policy layer for the attribute-dependent rules (P3 sourcing, P6 affected checks) that returns three-valued verdicts: conformant, violation, or unverifiable-without-attribute. When the attributes become available, the backbone is instantiated as a two-member attribute-conditioned family (external/internal) generated from one template, so there is still a single point of maintenance.

## 7.1 Compact formal description

```text
model_third =
->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review'),                       -- first pass: both mandatory, either order
  *( tau,                                                       -- zero or more correction cycles
     ->( 'Correct Dossier',
         X( 'Credit Check',                                     -- recheck only CC
            'Technical Review',                                 -- recheck only TR
            +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review') ) ) ),        -- recheck both, either order
  +( X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau) ),   -- post-check reviews, any relative order
  X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval'),                      -- exactly one approval
  'Release Order'
)
```

Size: 13 labelled leaves over 9 labels (Credit Check ×3, Technical Review ×3, all others ×1) → 4 extra labelled leaves versus 39 in Model C and 0 in Model S. No duplicate for Register Dossier, Release Order, approvals, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, or Correct Dossier, so impact analysis on those is a single node. Language: 20 correction-free traces (2 check orders × {none, SR, ER, SR·ER, ER·SR} × 2 approvals), all of which satisfy P1, P2, P4, P5, times an unbounded number of correction cycles, each of which satisfies P6/P7 structurally. All 8 branches of Model C are in this language. Model S's language, restricted to traces without corrections, is a strict superset (130 traces) of which the third model keeps exactly the 20 that satisfy P2/P4 under the ordering assumption below.

## 7.2 Self-contained PM4Py code

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog, Trace, Event

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- backbone --
# pm4py >= 2.7 parses loops with two children *(do, redo).  If your installation
# requires the three-child form, use *(tau, ->(...), tau).
MODEL_THIRD_STRING = """
->(
  'Register Dossier',
  +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review'),
  *( tau,
     ->( 'Correct Dossier',
         X( 'Credit Check',
            'Technical Review',
            +('Credit Check', 'Technical Review') ) ) ),
  +( X('Supplier Review', tau), X('Expedite Review', tau) ),
  X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval'),
  'Release Order'
)
"""

model_third = pm4py.parse_process_tree(MODEL_THIRD_STRING)
net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)

is_sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(net_third, im_third, fm_third, print_diagnostics=False)
assert is_sound, diagnostics


# ------------------------------------- equivalent POWL construction (optional) --
def build_third_powl():
    from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (Transition, SilentTransition,
                                        OperatorPOWL, StrictPartialOrder)
    from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

    reg = Transition("Register Dossier")
    cc1, tr1 = Transition("Credit Check"), Transition("Technical Review")
    first_pass = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cc1, tr1])          # no edge -> concurrent

    cd = Transition("Correct Dossier")
    cc2, tr2 = Transition("Credit Check"), Transition("Technical Review")
    cc3, tr3 = Transition("Credit Check"), Transition("Technical Review")
    both = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cc3, tr3])
    recheck = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[cc2, tr2, both])
    correction = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[cd, recheck])
    correction.order.add_edge(cd, recheck)
    corr_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP,
                             children=[SilentTransition(), correction])

    sr = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                      children=[Transition("Supplier Review"), SilentTransition()])
    er = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                      children=[Transition("Expedite Review"), SilentTransition()])
    reviews = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sr, er])              # any relative order

    approval = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                            children=[Transition("Manager Approval"),
                                      Transition("Auto Approval")])
    rel = Transition("Release Order")

    root = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reg, first_pass, corr_loop, reviews, approval, rel])
    for a, b in [(reg, first_pass), (first_pass, corr_loop), (corr_loop, reviews),
                 (reviews, approval), (approval, rel)]:
        root.order.add_edge(a, b)
    return root
```

## 7.3 Declarative / post-hoc policy layer

The backbone cannot see attributes, and two policy rules depend on them. Rather than silently accepting or rejecting, the layer returns a third verdict.

```python
CC, TR, SR, ER, CD = ("Credit Check", "Technical Review", "Supplier Review",
                      "Expedite Review", "Correct Dossier")
MA, AA, RD, RO = ("Manager Approval", "Auto Approval", "Register Dossier", "Release Order")

# Declarative statement of the policy, for documentation and for pm4py's
# DECLARE checker where a template exists.  Custom rules are checked below.
POLICY_DECLARE = {
    "init":            {RD},                                  # P1
    "end":             {RO},                                  # P1
    "existence":       {CC: 1, TR: 1},                        # P2
    "precedence":      {ER: {CC, TR},                         # P4: every ER after both checks
                        MA: {CC, TR}, AA: {CC, TR}},          # P2: approval after both checks
    "exactly_one_of":  [{MA, AA}],                            # P5
    "response":        {CD: {CC, TR}},                        # P6: every correction followed by >=1 recheck
    "repeat_requires": {CC: CD, TR: CD},                      # P7: a repeat needs an intervening correction
    "attribute_rules": {
        "P3": "Supplier Review present  <=>  case:sourcing == 'external'",
        "P6": "set of rechecks after each Correct Dossier == event:affected_checks",
    },
}


def check_policy(trace, sourcing=None, affected_by_correction=None):
    """Return (verdict, violations, unverifiable).
    verdict in {'conformant', 'violation', 'conformant-unverified'}.
    sourcing: 'external' | 'internal' | None
    affected_by_correction: list of sets, one per Correct Dossier event, or None
    """
    acts = [e["concept:name"] for e in trace]
    v, u = [], []

    if not acts or acts[0] != RD or acts.count(RD) != 1:
        v.append("P1: Register Dossier must be first and unique")
    if not acts or acts[-1] != RO or acts.count(RO) != 1:
        v.append("P1: Release Order must be last and unique")

    approvals = [i for i, a in enumerate(acts) if a in (MA, AA)]
    if len(approvals) != 1:
        v.append("P5: exactly one approval required")
    appr_idx = approvals[0] if approvals else len(acts)

    for a in (CC, TR):
        if a not in acts[:appr_idx]:
            v.append(f"P2: {a} missing before approval")

    for i, a in enumerate(acts):
        if a == ER:
            if CC not in acts[:i] or TR not in acts[:i]:
                v.append("P4: Expedite Review before a mandatory check")
            if i > appr_idx:
                v.append("P4: Expedite Review after approval")

    for a in (CC, TR):                                       # P7
        idx = [i for i, x in enumerate(acts) if x == a]
        for p, q in zip(idx, idx[1:]):
            if CD not in acts[p + 1:q]:
                v.append(f"P7: repeated {a} without intervening Correct Dossier")

    cd_idx = [i for i, a in enumerate(acts) if a == CD]      # P6
    for k, i in enumerate(cd_idx):
        nxt = cd_idx[k + 1] if k + 1 < len(cd_idx) else appr_idx
        rechecked = {x for x in acts[i + 1:nxt] if x in (CC, TR)}
        if not rechecked:
            v.append("P6: Correct Dossier not followed by any recheck")
        if affected_by_correction is None:
            u.append("P6: affected-check attribute missing; cannot verify recheck scope")
        elif rechecked != set(affected_by_correction[k]):
            v.append(f"P6: rechecked {sorted(rechecked)} but correction affected "
                     f"{sorted(affected_by_correction[k])}")

    has_sr = SR in acts                                      # P3
    if sourcing is None:
        u.append("P3: sourcing attribute missing; Supplier Review requirement unverifiable")
    elif sourcing == "external" and not has_sr:
        v.append("P3: external order without Supplier Review")
    elif sourcing == "internal" and has_sr:
        v.append("P3: internal order with Supplier Review (policy-ambiguous, flag)")

    if v:
        return "violation", v, u
    return ("conformant-unverified" if u else "conformant"), v, u


def accepted_by_model(trace, net, im, fm):
    """Structural acceptance: perfect alignment (fitness 1.0)."""
    log = EventLog([trace])
    res = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, im, fm)[0]
    return res["fitness"] == 1.0


def combined_verdict(trace, net, im, fm, **attrs):
    structural = accepted_by_model(trace, net, im, fm)
    policy, violations, unverifiable = check_policy(trace, **attrs)
    return {"structural_fit": structural, "policy": policy,
            "violations": violations, "unverifiable": unverifiable}
```

The two layers are deliberately redundant on P1, P2, P4, P5, P7: the backbone enforces them structurally, the checker names the violated rule so that the diagnostic says "P4: Expedite Review before a mandatory check" instead of "log move at position 2". The checker is the only layer that can express P3 and the scope half of P6.

## 7.4 Explicit semantics

- **Mandatory checks (P2).** `+('Credit Check', 'Technical Review')` is not skippable: no tau alternative. Both must occur before the loop can be entered or exited, hence before any review or approval. Order is free.
- **Optional Supplier Review (P3).** `X('Supplier Review', tau)` after the check/correction phase. This is the only place the third model deliberately uses optionality to stand in for a missing attribute, and it is documented as such: the checker reports "unverifiable" on every case until sourcing is logged; once logged, `model_third_external` / `model_third_internal` remove the optionality entirely.
- **Expedite Review ordering (P4).** Placed after the check/correction phase and before approval; concurrent with Supplier Review, so `SR, ER` and `ER, SR` are both accepted, satisfying "may occur before or after Supplier Review". B4 and the ER-between-checks trace are rejected.
- **Exactly one approval (P5).** `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` with no tau: exactly one, no zero, no two.
- **Correction / recheck (P6, P7).** `*(tau, ->('Correct Dossier', X(CC, TR, +(CC, TR))))`: zero or more cycles; every cycle starts with Correct Dossier and is followed by at least one recheck; the recheck may be CC only, TR only, or both in either order. Consequences: a repeated check is only reachable through Correct Dossier (P7 enforced structurally; B8 rejected), a correction without recheck is rejected, and *which* subset is rechecked is left to the checker because only the attribute can validate it. B6 is structurally accepted and reported as `conformant-unverified` until the affected-check attribute exists.

Design decisions where the policy is silent (documented, reversible):

- D-a. Supplier Review and Expedite Review are placed *after* the mandatory checks and corrections. P3 does not state this for Supplier Review; all observed variants and probes have it there, and it is the safer reading (a supplier is not reviewed on a dossier that may still be corrected). If the process owner confirms Supplier Review may precede the checks, move `X('Supplier Review', tau)` into a parallel block with the check/correction phase; the checker is unaffected.
- D-b. Corrections happen before Supplier/Expedite Review. A correction *after* Expedite Review (see adversarial trace T5 in section 8) is rejected and surfaced as a policy gap for the board, not silently accepted.
- D-c. Auto Approval is allowed after Supplier or Expedite Review (B5 accepted) because the policy does not forbid it. If the board decides that external or expedited orders require a human approver, that is a one-line change in the checker (`precedence: AA requires not SR`) and, if desired, in the external family member.

## 7.5 Limitations from the missing attributes

- Without sourcing, the third model cannot distinguish "Supplier Review not required" from "Supplier Review not logged" (F4). It accepts both and marks them unverifiable. It therefore still overestimates fitness on lost-event cases in the same way S does, but it says so per case instead of hiding it.
- Without affected-check, B6-type traces are accepted structurally. A malicious or careless operator could record Correct Dossier, repeat only the cheaper check, and pass. The checker flags every such case as unverifiable so that a sample can be manually audited; the loop bound (number of cycles) and the identity of the rechecked activity are reported as diagnostics.
- The model deliberately does **not** make Credit Check, Technical Review, the approvals, Register Dossier, or Release Order optional to "absorb" missing data. Missing mandatory events remain visible as model moves; if the data-quality team can prove an interface loses them, the fix belongs in the alignment cost function or a repair step (section 9), not in the model.
- Because the loop is unbounded, the language is infinite and precision will be lower than C's on the observed log. That is intended: the extra language consists of legal correction sequences, not of policy violations. The negative suite (section 9) is what verifies that claim.

```python
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net.variants import state_equation_a_star as sea

    # Model moves on mandatory checks are expensive; on Supplier Review cheap
    # (known lost events); silent moves free.  Sync moves free.

            model_cost[t] = 0
        elif t.label in (CC, TR, MA, AA):

        else:
            model_cost[t] = ali.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR.value.STD_MODEL_LOG_MOVE_COST
        sync_cost[t] = 0
```

## 9.9 Stakeholder comprehension protocol

- Raters: at least 3 per stakeholder group (compliance, operations, IT, process owner), none from either modelling team.
- Task: given the diagram (not the tree string) and 10 probe traces (B1–B8 plus two decoys), classify each as accepted/rejected by the model. Score accuracy against the formal verdict, and separately ask "is this behavior *supposed* to be allowed?" to measure whether the diagram *conveys* policy or *misrepresents* it.
- Report accuracy, time to answer, and inter-rater agreement (Fleiss' kappa). A high subjective score with low classification accuracy (the likely outcome for Model S) is reported as "readable but misleading".

## 9.10 Rejection rule despite good aggregate metrics

A model is rejected for the compliance role if any of the following holds, regardless of fitness/precision/generalization/simplicity:

- R1. It accepts any trace in the NEGATIVE suite (currently S fails on B3, B4, T7).
- R2. It rejects any trace in the POSITIVE suite that the process owner has confirmed valid (currently C fails on B2, T3).
- R3. Holdout fitness on any stratum falls below the agreed floor *and* a sample audit of ≥30 unfitting cases in that stratum finds more than 20% to be legitimate (this is the F4 vs F8 test; it decides whether low fitness is compliance or overfitting).
- R4. Fitness is flat (slope < 0.1 per 10% drop rate) under random deletion of mandatory-check events — the model is absorbing missing mandatory events.
- R5. It is unsound, or has a dead labelled transition.
- R6. Diagram-based classification accuracy in the comprehension protocol is below 70% for the stakeholder group that will use it.

A model is rejected for the exploration role only under R5 or if it cannot replay activities that exist in the policy (S cannot replay Correct Dossier).

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Model S.** Not approved for compliance, simulation, or as "the process" in communication: it scores the most serious violation (B3) as perfect, orders Expedite Review freely, and cannot replay corrections. It may continue to be used *temporarily* as an exploration overlay for frequency and duration annotations on the correction-free part of the process, on two conditions: every rendering carries the label "descriptive sketch: does not encode mandatory checks or corrections", and it is retired from all uses once `model_third` passes 9.7 and 9.3. Its advocate's argument F7 is accepted for the interim only.

**Model C.** Retire as a model. Its 8 branches should be kept, verbatim, as the initial POSITIVE suite: they are valuable as confirmed legal variants and as regression tests for whatever model replaces them. Its default-deny philosophy (F8) is not rejected — it is relocated: the *negative suite* and the *policy checker* become the whitelist/blacklist mechanism, and new combinations are approved by adding a probe trace, not a branch. C must not be used for compliance because it rejects a policy-valid trace (B2) and would generate false alarms on every unseen legal combination; it must not be used for exploration because its 25% holdout gap hides the actual variety.

**Third alternative.** Provisionally approved for exploration and for *shadow-mode* compliance (verdicts logged, not enforced). Conditions before enforcement deployment:

1. Passes soundness and reachability (9.7).
2. Accepts 100% of POSITIVE and rejects 100% of NEGATIVE (9.3); every POLICY_DEPENDENT row has a written ruling from the process owner, and D-a/D-b/D-c are confirmed or amended accordingly.
3. Reproducible random and temporal splits with published seeds and fingerprints (9.1); stratified fitness (9.2) reported per stratum, with a sample audit of unfitting cases in any stratum below the floor (rule R3).
4. Perturbation slopes (9.4) show it does not absorb missing mandatory-check events.
5. Alignment cost function chosen, documented, and frozen (9.6); Supplier Review moves may be down-weighted only with a written data-quality justification.
6. Logging changes committed with a date: `case:sourcing` at registration and `affected_checks` on every Correct Dossier event. Until then, `conformant-unverified` verdicts are sampled (≥5% or ≥30 per month) for manual audit, and the family members `model_third_external/internal` are not deployed.
7. Comprehension protocol (9.9) passes R6 for compliance and operations.
8. A change procedure: any new legal combination is added as a POSITIVE probe first; the backbone changes only if the probe fails, and the change is a rule change (one node), never a branch addition.

Shadow mode should run for at least one full business cycle (long enough to observe corrections and external orders) before enforcement.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal.** The reviewer replaces two measured models with an unmeasured one. Model S and Model C have numbers, however flawed; `model_third` has a table of hand-checked probes and the reviewer's own assurances. Its correction loop makes the language infinite, so its log precision will be lower than C's, and the reviewer preempts that criticism by declaring the extra behavior "legal by construction" — the very argument they refused to accept from the S team. Two of its ordering rules (Supplier Review after checks, no correction after Expedite Review) are inventions labelled "design decisions"; a compliance model that rejects T6 on the modeller's intuition is doing exactly what C is condemned for. The recommendation also relocates C's whitelist into a Python checker, which is less transparent to auditors than eight readable sequences and is not covered by any soundness or replay tooling. Model C's advocate has a coherent position: in a regulated custom-order process, default-deny is standard, and the cost of a false alarm (a human looks at a case) is far lower than the cost of a false pass. B2 is one branch away from being fixed. A pragmatic board would fund a branch generator for C — enumerate the 20 legal combinations mechanically, keep the whitelist semantics, keep the 0.94 precision — rather than adopt a new model whose only evidence is that it agrees with its author's reading of seven policy sentences.

**Evidence that would change the recommendation.**

- A sample audit of C's unfitting holdout cases finding that most are genuinely noncompliant would convert the 0.744 from an overfitting signal into a compliance signal and would substantially rehabilitate the default-deny reading (rule R3).
- Once `affected_checks` is logged, finding that corrections virtually always trigger both rechecks would justify C's rigid correction branch and remove the main functional advantage of the third model's loop.
- If the process owner rules that Supplier Review may legitimately precede the checks (T6) and that corrections after Expedite Review are legitimate (T5), the third model as specified has two false negatives and must be revised before the comparison is fair.
- If `model_third` accepts any trace in a negative suite curated by compliance rather than by me, or if its shadow-mode false-positive rate on audited cases is not materially below C's, the "safer than S, more general than C" claim fails.
- If a mechanically generated Model C (all 20 legal combinations plus bounded corrections) reaches full positive-suite recall while keeping negative-suite rejection at 100% and stakeholders demonstrably classify probes better with the enumerated view, the maintainability objection becomes a tooling problem rather than a modelling one, and C's descendant would be the better compliance artifact.
