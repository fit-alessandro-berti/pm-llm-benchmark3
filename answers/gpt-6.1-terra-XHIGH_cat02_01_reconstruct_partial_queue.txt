# 1. Assumptions and notation

All durations below are minutes unless stated otherwise. The records are observations, not evidence that the process started empty or that no other cases existed.

- **A1 — Population and window.** Reconstruct the 22 supplied cases from 08:00 to 14:00, treating the window boundaries as exact. For reported-time calculations, assume no supplied case was already in a station at 08:00 and no unlisted case occupied a resource. These are closed-population assumptions, not observations. P01's rounded 08:00 arrival could actually precede the boundary.
- **A2 — Event order.** Use half-open activity intervals `[start, end)`. At each reported time apply all completions, then arrivals or transfers, then starts, and report the state *after the entire batch*. Thus a completion and a start at the same time do not overlap, and no artificial instantaneous queue peak is counted. Station 2 entry coincides with station 1 completion. Hourly flow intervals are `[h, h+1 hour)`; the 14:00 state is the snapshot at the window end.
- **A3 — Rounding.** Main tables use reported times as exact for transparent bookkeeping. An observed timestamp represents approximately ±2.5 minutes; an observed difference can therefore differ by up to 5 minutes, subject to chronology and resource constraints. An elapsed duration from a timestamp to the exact 14:00 cutoff has approximately ±2.5 minutes uncertainty. Joint perturbations are needed: errors sharing a timestamp are correlated, and maxima for separate cases need not be simultaneously attainable. The maintenance endpoints and 14:00 snapshot are treated as exact. Rounding can shift events across hourly boundaries and can conceal short apparent gaps or overlaps.
- **A4 — Capacity and assignment.** I1, I2, C1 and C2 are single-task resources; assume listed assignments are accurate and no unlisted work or assistance. C2 is unavailable during `[10:55, 11:10)`. These constraints supply *conditional* missing-start bounds. Relaxing them to allow delayed records, other work or assistance changes those bounds and utilization interpretations. There are no observed reported-time overlaps on the same resource. This does not prove actual absence of short overlaps after rounding.
- **A5 — Missing starts.** A missing start is no earlier than arrival at its station and the previous listed completion on its resource, and no later than its own completion and the next listed start. Nonnegative service is permitted; zero is a mathematical boundary, not a claim that real work took zero time. At reported times the intervals are P05 S2 `[09:45, 10:15]`, P07 S1 `[09:25, 10:00]`, P11 S2 `[12:00, 12:25]`, P15 S1 `[11:40, 12:15]`. P11's bound uses P08's C2 completion at 12:00; hence it does not overlap maintenance. Use the midpoint for central calculations, and both endpoints for sensitivity. No FIFO rule or typical service duration is imposed.
- **A6 — Censoring.** OPEN starts are observed and their ends are later than 14:00; elapsed service is a lower bound, not a completed duration. Waiting cases have waited at least until 14:00, with eventual waits unknown. Case and station sojourns for unfinished cases are likewise right-censored. No unobserved completions are imputed. Reported-time estimates at the cutoff use the recorded state even where rounding could place an event near the cutoff.
- **A7 — Scope of utilization.** Busy time means recorded or imputed case work inside the window; it is not proof of all productive work. Scheduled capacity is 720 resource-minutes per station; available S2 capacity is 705 after subtracting C2's 15-minute outage. Rounding and unlisted work add uncertainty beyond the scenario ranges.

# 2. Reconstruction method and formulas

For case `i`, let `a` be external arrival, `b` S1 start, `c` S1 end/S2 entry, `d` S2 start, and `e` S2 end. Then `W1=b-a`, `S1=c-b`, `T1=c-a`, `W2=d-c`, `S2=e-d`, and `T2=e-c`. For a completed case, total `T=e-a=W1+S1+W2+S2`. At cutoff `H`, substitute `H` only to calculate elapsed time for a case still waiting or in service; do not treat it as its actual start or completion. Completed-case averages are conditional on completion and can be selection-biased.

At each time, queue length is the number entered but not started; station WIP is queue plus in-service count. System WIP is external arrivals less S2 completions, and equals the sum of station WIP for this population. Time-weighted means integrate these counts over the window and divide by 360 minutes; a peak is the maximum after a batch of simultaneous events. Station arrival and departure rates are counts per hour, not reciprocal case interarrival times. Resource busy time is the sum of clipped service intervals, including elapsed open activities. Utilization is busy time divided by scheduled or available resource-minutes, as labeled.

For each missing start, the earliest feasible start gives lower waiting and upper service; the latest gives upper waiting and lower service. The midpoint is a descriptive scenario, not a statistical multiple imputation. Station sojourn and total WIP do not depend on the missing starts at reported times; the split between waiting and service does. Since waits and services move in opposite directions, add their bounds only with their shared-start dependence respected. The same applies to rounding: a comprehensive exact uncertainty envelope would require a joint constrained event-time model, rather than independently adding ±5 minutes to every aggregate.

Little's Law `L=λW` ordinarily describes a stable system over a sufficiently long interval with consistent population definitions. This trace builds backlog and has a material boundary population, so no stationary Little's-Law estimate is warranted. The finite-window sample-path identity is exact: area under WIP equals the sum of each case's time inside the window. It is a consistency check, not proof of stationarity.

# 5. Bottleneck and temporal interpretation

**Evidence:** Early external arrivals exceed S1 completions: 13 versus 6 by 10:00, and the S1 queue reaches 6 then and peaks at 7 later. The two inspectors are busy much of the window, and S1 completed-case sojourns grow to around two hours. This supports S1 as the initial accumulation point.

S2 receives 20 cases but completes 14 by 14:00, leaving four waiting and two in service. Its queue is 4 at both noon and 13:00, and S2 waits for later cases reach 45–75 minutes before starts. The backlog shifts downstream as S1 catches up with a declining external arrival stream; at 14:00 S2 has six cases versus S1's two. This supports a time-varying combination, with S1 initially constraining flow and S2 increasingly constraining final completion. It does not establish a unique intrinsic bottleneck: utilization averages, arrivals, and short-window throughput alone do not identify capacity under sustained saturation.

**Maintenance interpretation:** C2 is unavailable 10:55–11:10, reducing available S2 capacity by 15 resource-minutes. P06 finishes on C2 at 10:50 and P08 starts at 11:15, so the observed schedule respects the outage even with the stated rounding. P11 starts after P08 finishes under A4, so its missing start does not explain work during maintenance. The outage plausibly adds delay, but this log cannot identify its causal contribution or claim all 15 minutes were productive capacity that would otherwise have been used. S2's queue also grows outside that interval; workload, service durations and varying arrivals are competing explanations. P17 starts while earlier S2 entrant P16 is still waiting; this is a visible non-FIFO pattern, not evidence of bypassing S2. Priorities or case-specific readiness are hypotheses only.

# 6. Missingness, censoring, and sensitivity analysis

| Scenario | Missing starts P05 S2 / P07 S1 / P11 S2 / P15 S1 | S1 mean queue / busy minutes | S2 mean queue / busy minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early starts: lower waits, upper service | 09:45 / 09:25 / 12:00 / 11:40 | 4.11 / 605 | 2.14 / 570 |
| Midpoints | 10:00 / 09:42:30 / 12:12:30 / 11:57:30 | 4.21 / 570 | 2.22 / 542.5 |
| Late starts: upper waits, lower service | 10:15 / 10:00 / 12:25 / 12:15 | 4.31 / 535 | 2.29 / 515 |

Endpoint scenarios are algebraic extremes, potentially implausible because they include zero service; they are not confidence intervals. WIP areas and completed total sojourns remain fixed in these scenarios, while queue/service splits and utilization move. Peak queue counts can also change with the imputed starts, even though station WIP peaks do not. The table of hourly snapshots uses midpoints; for example at 12:00 P15 is in S1 service in the midpoint scenario but still waiting in the late-start scenario. Reported queue peaks above are central estimates.

For sensitivity to rounding, perturb each recorded timestamp within its rounding interval, preserve case chronology, single-resource feasibility, outage and observed status, and recompute the sweep. Small shifts can change boundary snapshots and hourly counts, especially simultaneous transfers; they do not reasonably erase the multi-case backlogs or the six unfinished S2 cases at 14:00, given the stated status semantics. Exact best/worst busy-time or peak bounds require joint optimization. The four waiting S2 cases and one waiting S1 case have unknown final waits; the three open services have unknown final service durations. Completed-case averages therefore do not describe all 22 entrants, and there is no finite upper bound on their final waiting or sojourn times from this data alone.

# 7. Reproducible Python

The script uses `pandas` and an installed `pm4py`. It reconstructs reported-time scenarios, including censored observations, and prints compact tables. The PM4Py projection contains only the 14 completed cases, with a zero-duration external arrival marker; the midpoint is used for missing starts. Its PM4Py outputs describe these selected **cases**, not station queues or actual rates per hour.

```python
from io import StringIO
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = '''case|arrival_s1|s1_start|s1_end|s1_resource|s2_start|s2_end|s2_resource|state_at_14_00
P01|08:00|08:05|08:25|I1|08:30|09:00|C1|COMPLETE
P02|08:05|08:10|08:35|I2|08:40|09:20|C2|COMPLETE
P03|08:10|08:30|08:55|I1|09:05|09:45|C1|COMPLETE
P04|08:20|08:40|09:10|I2|09:25|10:05|C2|COMPLETE
P05|08:25|09:00|09:25|I1|MISSING|10:15|C1|COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P06|08:35|09:15|09:45|I2|10:10|10:50|C2|COMPLETE
P07|08:45|MISSING|10:00|I1|10:20|11:00|C1|COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P08|09:00|09:50|10:20|I2|11:15|12:00|C2|COMPLETE
P09|09:05|10:05|10:35|I1|11:00|11:40|C1|COMPLETE
P10|09:15|10:25|10:55|I2|11:45|12:10|C1|COMPLETE
P11|09:25|10:40|11:05|I1|MISSING|12:25|C2|COMPLETE_WITH_MISSING_START
P12|09:35|11:00|11:30|I2|12:15|13:00|C1|COMPLETE
P13|09:50|11:10|11:40|I1|12:30|13:20|C2|COMPLETE
P14|10:00|11:35|12:00|I2|13:05|13:50|C1|COMPLETE
P15|10:10|MISSING|12:15|I1|13:30|OPEN|C2|OPEN_IN_S2
P16|10:25|12:05|12:35|I2||||WAITING_FOR_S2
P17|10:45|12:20|12:50|I1|13:55|OPEN|C1|OPEN_IN_S2
P18|11:00|12:40|13:10|I2||||WAITING_FOR_S2
P19|11:15|12:55|13:25|I1||||WAITING_FOR_S2
P20|11:30|13:15|13:45|I2||||WAITING_FOR_S2
P21|11:50|13:30|OPEN|I1||||OPEN_IN_S1
P22|12:10|||||||WAITING_FOR_S1'''

df = pd.read_csv(StringIO(raw), sep='|', dtype=str, keep_default_na=False)
day = '2026-05-12 '
lo, hi = pd.Timestamp(day + '08:00'), pd.Timestamp(day + '14:00')
timestamp_cols = ['arrival_s1', 's1_start', 's1_end', 's2_start', 's2_end']
for col in timestamp_cols:
    # Preserve status separately; NaT by itself must not collapse these meanings.
    df[col + '_kind'] = df[col].map(lambda x: x if x in ('MISSING', 'OPEN', '') else 'TIME')
    df[col] = pd.to_datetime(day + df[col].where(df[col + '_kind'] == 'TIME'), errors='coerce')

bounds = {
    ('P05', 's2_start'): ('09:45', '10:15'),
    ('P07', 's1_start'): ('09:25', '10:00'),
    ('P11', 's2_start'): ('12:00', '12:25'),
    ('P15', 's1_start'): ('11:40', '12:15'),
}

def minutes(delta):
    return delta.total_seconds() / 60

def scenario(fraction):
    x = df.copy()
    for (case, col), (left, right) in bounds.items():
        a, b = pd.Timestamp(day + left), pd.Timestamp(day + right)
        x.loc[x.case == case, col] = a + fraction * (b - a)
    return x

def station_rows(x, n):
    rows = []
    for _, r in x.iterrows():
        entry = r.arrival_s1 if n == 1 else r.s1_end
        if pd.isna(entry):  # S1 still open or not begun: no S2 arrival
            continue
        start, end = r[f's{n}_start'], r[f's{n}_end']
        kind = r[f's{n}_end_kind']
        rows.append(dict(case=r.case, entry=entry, start=start, end=end,
                         end_kind=kind, resource=r[f's{n}_resource']))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def sweep(st, window_start=lo, window_end=hi):
    # Direct pandas station calculations are safer than case-level PM4Py
    # interval conversion: unfinished queues, open services and uncertain
    # starts must remain explicit, and S2 entry is an S1 completion.
    events = []
    for r in st.itertuples():
        events.append((r.entry, 1, 0))
        if pd.notna(r.start):
            events.append((r.start, -1, 1))
        if pd.notna(r.end):
            events.append((r.end, 0, -1))
    ev = pd.DataFrame(events, columns=['time', 'q', 'service']).groupby('time').sum().sort_index()
    q = s = 0
    prev = window_start
    qarea = sarea = peakq = peakw = 0
    for t, row in ev.iterrows():
        if t > window_end:
            break
        dt = minutes(t - prev)
        qarea += q * dt
        sarea += s * dt
        q += int(row.q)
        s += int(row.service)
        peakq, peakw = max(peakq, q), max(peakw, q + s)
        prev = t
    qarea += q * minutes(window_end - prev)
    sarea += s * minutes(window_end - prev)
    return dict(mean_queue=qarea/360, mean_wip=(qarea+sarea)/360,
                peak_queue=peakq, peak_wip=peakw, busy_minutes=sarea,
                end_queue=q, end_service=s)

summaries = []
for name, fraction in [('early', 0.0), ('midpoint', 0.5), ('late', 1.0)]:
    x = scenario(fraction)
    for n in (1, 2):
        st = station_rows(x, n)
        completed = st[st.end.notna()].copy()
        waited = (completed.start - completed.entry).dt.total_seconds() / 60
        served = (completed.end - completed.start).dt.total_seconds() / 60
        rec = dict(scenario=name, station=f'S{n}', arrivals=len(st),
                   completed=len(completed), complete_mean_wait=waited.mean(),
                   complete_mean_service=served.mean())
        rec.update(sweep(st))
        summaries.append(rec)
print('\nStation scenarios (minutes and time-weighted counts):')
print(pd.DataFrame(summaries).round(2).to_string(index=False))

x = scenario(0.5)
print('\nHourly flows [start, end):')
flow = []
for h in pd.date_range(lo, hi, freq='h')[:-1]:
    nxt = h + pd.Timedelta(hours=1)
    count = lambda col: int(((x[col] >= h) & (x[col] < nxt)).sum())
    flow.append([h.strftime('%H:%M'), count('arrival_s1'), count('s1_end'), count('s2_end')])
print(pd.DataFrame(flow, columns=['hour', 'S1 arrivals', 'S1 ends / S2 arrivals', 'S2 ends']).to_string(index=False))

print('\nMidpoint resource busy minutes, including elapsed open work:')
for n in (1, 2):
    st = station_rows(x, n)
    st = st[st.start.notna()].copy()
    st['busy_min'] = (st.end.fillna(hi) - st.start).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    print(f'S{n}:', st.groupby('resource').busy_min.sum().round(1).to_dict())

# Interval events for observed or midpoint-imputed complete activities only.
# Open activities are retained in the pandas calculations above, not given
# artificial PM4Py completion events at the cutoff.
events = []
for _, r in x.iterrows():
    for n in (1, 2):
        start, end = r[f's{n}_start'], r[f's{n}_end']
        if pd.notna(start) and pd.notna(end):
            events.append({'case:concept:name': r.case, 'concept:name': f'S{n}',
                           'start_timestamp': start, 'time:timestamp': end,
                           'org:resource': r[f's{n}_resource']})
intervals = pd.DataFrame(events)
intervals = pm4py.format_dataframe(intervals, case_id='case:concept:name',
                                   activity_key='concept:name',
                                   timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
                                   start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')
print('\nPM4Py-compatible activity intervals:', len(intervals))

# Complete cases have two disjoint station intervals. Add arrival as a point
# event so case-level waiting includes the initial wait from external arrival.
# PM4Py's arrival/finish "rates" are elapsed seconds between case boundaries,
# not counts per hour; projection excludes the 8 unfinished cases.
completed_cases = x.loc[x.s2_end.notna(), 'case']
projection = intervals[intervals['case:concept:name'].isin(completed_cases)].copy()
markers = x[x.case.isin(completed_cases)].apply(
    lambda r: pd.Series({'case:concept:name': r.case, 'concept:name': 'External arrival',
                         'start_timestamp': r.arrival_s1, 'time:timestamp': r.arrival_s1,
                         'org:resource': 'external'}), axis=1)
projection = pd.concat([projection, markers], ignore_index=True)
projection = pm4py.format_dataframe(projection, case_id='case:concept:name',
                                    activity_key='concept:name',
                                    timestamp_key='time:timestamp',
                                    start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')
projection = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(
    projection, start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')
projection = pm4py.insert_case_arrival_finish_rate(
    projection, start_timestamp_key='start_timestamp')
print('\nPM4Py CASE-level durations and boundary gaps (seconds), selected completed cases:')
cols = ['case:concept:name', '@@service_time', '@@waiting_time', '@@sojourn_time',
        '@@arrival_rate', '@@finish_rate']
print(projection[cols].drop_duplicates('case:concept:name').head(8).to_string(index=False))
```
