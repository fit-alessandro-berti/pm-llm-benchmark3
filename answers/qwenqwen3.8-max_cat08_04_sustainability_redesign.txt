# 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Environmental scope used for this advice**

The boundary is the **order-to-close spare-parts fulfillment process** visible in the enriched event log, including:

- **Order handling and availability check**
- **Stock picking, partial shipment, and backorder waiting**
- **Production planning, batching, CNC processing, heat treatment, inspection, and rework**
- **Emergency production**
- **Packaging, packaging rework, package material use**
- **Transport mode selection and delivery confirmation**
- **Cancellation due to delay**
- **Obsolescence risk from batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days**

Environmental impacts considered inside the boundary:

- **Energy use**: `energy_kwh`, mainly production, inspection, packaging, and warehouse activity visible in the log.
- **Transport emissions**: `transport_kgco2e` by `route_mode`.
- **Material waste**: `material_scrap_kg`.
- **Packaging material use**: `package_material_kg`.
- **Rework-related waste and energy**: `rework_flag`, rework loops, packaging rework, and repeated CNC/inspection cycles.
- **Obsolescence waste risk**: batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong.

Outside the boundary, or only partially represented:

- Upstream raw-material extraction and supplier-side manufacturing.
- Use-phase impacts of the spare parts.
- End-of-life recycling or disposal beyond observed scrap.
- Capital equipment, buildings, employee commuting, and IT infrastructure.
- Full carrier lifecycle emissions beyond the provided `transport_kgco2e` estimate.
- Customer-side consequences such as lost production cost from downtime, except where service risk is visible in the log.

Key assumptions and uncertainties:

1. The log is **flattened by order**, not by line item, so batch-level and item-level effects are inferred from `batch_id`, `batch_size`, and path evidence.
2. `energy_kwh` is treated as process energy, while transport impacts are assessed through `transport_kgco2e`.
3. `transport_kgco2e` is a useful operational indicator but not a full lifecycle assessment.
4. Rework and returns are only partially visible: `rework_flag`, rework loops, and packaging rework are observed, but hidden customer returns may be undercounted.
5. Obsolescence is represented by the evidence that **6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days** when forecasts are wrong.
6. Emergency paths and partial shipments create many variants, reducing discovered-model precision; therefore, interventions should be validated with event-level conformance checks, not only with a single process model.

# 3. Intervention options

**S1. Green shipping recommender using promised lead time, route reliability, and customer segment**

- **Target path or segment:** Shipment decisions across all segments, especially Standard-stock Service-contract air shipments and Made-to-order air paths.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** The recommender uses `promised_lead_time_hours`, current route reliability, `case_type`, and `customer_type` to recommend ground or consolidated truck when the probability of meeting the promise is above 85%. It reduces avoidable parcel air and unjustified dedicated-van use.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service is protected by using promise-based slack rather than a blanket ban on fast modes. Resilience improves if the recommender respects R1 and keeps an alternative route available. Quality risk is limited if packaging-route compatibility is checked at the same decision point.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `route_mode` versus promised slack, SP1 violation rate, late delivery by mode, override rate, `expedite_flag`, carrier reliability, and alternative-route availability for Service-contract customers.

**S2. Dynamic batching for CNC and heat treatment with maximum waiting-time caps by segment**

- **Target path or segment:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production`, especially Made-to-order Regular and Service-contract cases; also heat treatment where batch size above 10 reduces energy per item.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Batching increases CNC_A energy efficiency from 2.8 kWh/item at batch size 1–3 to 1.3 kWh/item at batch size 12–20. Heat treatment also becomes more efficient above batch size 10.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Cycle time increases because batch waiting can rise from 4h to 38h. Heat-treatment batch waiting carries a 1.7x higher late-delivery risk. Therefore, batching must be capped by segment and due-date slack. Large batches also raise obsolescence risk, with 6% of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days when forecasts are wrong.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `batch_size`, batch waiting time, `energy_kwh` per item, late delivery by batch-wait band, obsolescence rate, post-batch expediting, and heat-treatment waiting effects.

**S3. Shift Made-to-order production from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and due-date slack allow**

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order cases currently routed through CNC_B, especially the high-rework path `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** CNC_B has a higher rework rate than CNC_A, 18% versus 9% for Made-to-order cases. Reducing CNC_B rework lowers repeated CNC energy, scrap, and delayed deliveries. The problematic CNC_B rework path shows 32 kWh/order and 1.8 kg scrap/order.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Quality improves because fewer defects enter rework. Cycle time may increase if CNC_A queues are longer, so routing should depend on due-date slack and machine load. Resilience requires avoiding excessive concentration on CNC_A.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `machine_group` queues, rework rate by machine, `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, late delivery by machine routing, and capacity utilization.

**S4. Replace air for Standard-stock Service-contract orders with priority pick + ground unless slack is insufficient**

- **Target path or segment:** Standard-stock Service-contract orders currently shipped by air, especially where SP1 is violated. SP1 is violated in 22% of these cases.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Parcel air emits about 4–6x more kgCO2e per order than ground. The path evidence shows stock-to-air transport at about 18.5 kgCO2e/order versus 3.6 kgCO2e/order for stock-to-ground.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service can remain acceptable if priority picking and ground transport are used only when the promised lead time can still be met with high probability. Cycle time may increase from the current 18h median for this segment toward the ground path median, but late delivery should remain controlled if slack rules are enforced. Resilience requires maintaining an alternative ground or carrier option under R1.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Air share for Standard-stock Service-contract, SP1 violations, priority-pick lead time, ground cycle time, late delivery, customer complaints, and override reasons.

**S5. Consolidate truck departures twice daily for non-urgent stock orders**

- **Target path or segment:** Non-urgent Standard-stock orders, especially `Stock -> standard pack -> ground` variants where promised lead time has slack.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCO2e by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels. Current consolidated-truck volume is only 850 events, so there is room to shift suitable ground parcels.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Cycle time increases by the 12–24h consolidation wait. Late-delivery risk rises if consolidation is applied to orders without enough slack. Resilience risk appears if the consolidated lane becomes a single point of failure; a backup route is needed for Service-contract customers.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Consolidated-truck share, consolidation waiting time, `transport_kgco2e`, late delivery by segment, missed truck departures, recovery paths, and alternative-route availability.

**S6. Improve packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment**

- **Target path or segment:** `Package standard` and `Package reinforced` paths feeding `Ship ground`, `Ship air`, `Dedicated van`, and especially `Rework packaging`.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Packaging rework occurs when packaging choice is incompatible with `route_mode` or part geometry. Preventing incompatibility reduces repeated packaging, extra material, and delayed shipments. The DFG shows 260 standard-package rework events and 360 reinforced-package rework events.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Quality improves because damage-related returns and repackaging loops fall. Cycle time can improve because fewer orders enter rework packaging. Material impact is mixed: reinforced packaging may increase `package_material_kg`, but it reduces damage-related returns by 40% for Made-to-order heavy parts.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** `Rework packaging` frequency, `package_material_kg`, damage-related returns, Q1 compliance, route-package mismatch events, and late delivery after packaging rework.

**S7. Add inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations**

- **Target path or segment:** `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`, especially CNC_B and rushed-inspection cases.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Inspection_cell queues are responsible for many rework-production loops because rushed inspections miss defects. A quality gate reduces repeated CNC processing, scrap, and later packaging or customer-return problems.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Rework and scrap fall. The gate may add a small inspection delay, but net cycle time can improve if fewer orders loop back into production. Late-delivery risk should fall if the gate is capacity-planned rather than added as an unmanaged bottleneck.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Inspection queue time, rework loops by `machine_group`, `material_scrap_kg`, `energy_kwh`, late delivery, gate pass/fail rates, and return-related events.

**S8. Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**

- **Target path or segment:** Critical-downtime cases using `Emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van`, where transport is about 52 kgCO2e/order and vans often return empty.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Shared emergency courier lanes can improve load factor and reduce empty running compared with single-order dedicated vans.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** This is only acceptable for Critical-downtime cases where the shared lane can still meet the downtime window. For true downtime, dedicated vans may remain necessary. Resilience can improve if shared lanes create multiple emergency options, but it can worsen if the shared lane is unreliable.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Dedicated-van justification, shared-courier reliability, Critical-downtime late rate, empty-return rate, `transport_kgco2e`, and exception overrides under SP2.

**S9. Split Critical-downtime into true downtime and precautionary urgent categories**

- **Target path or segment:** Critical-downtime Service-contract cases, which are 11% of orders but have high average transport emissions, about 38 kgCO2e/order.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Some Critical-downtime orders may be precautionary rather than true downtime events. True downtime cases retain emergency production and dedicated van where justified; precautionary urgent cases can use priority ground or air only where necessary.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service protection is improved by reserving the most expensive and fastest modes for genuine downtime. The risk is misclassification, which could cause unacceptable downtime. Governance and customer confirmation are required.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** New criticality category, transport mode by category, late delivery, downtime confirmation, SP2 justification, emergency-production rate, and customer escalation events.

**S10. Introduce carbon-aware promise-date negotiation for Regular made-to-order customers**

- **Target path or segment:** Made-to-order Regular cases, which have 18% late delivery, 18.5 kWh/order, and 0.90 kg scrap/order.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** If Regular MTO customers accept slightly longer or more flexible promise dates, the process can use larger batches, CNC_A instead of rushed CNC_B, and ground or consolidated transport instead of air.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service can improve if promises become more realistic. Late delivery may fall if the negotiated promise reflects actual process capability. The risk is customer rejection or later expediting requests.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Accepted promise-date changes, batch size, `route_mode`, `expedite_flag`, late delivery, cancellation rate, and energy per order.

**S11. Reduce partial shipments by changing availability-check rules**

- **Target path or segment:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment`, including `Partial shipment -> Backorder wait` and second-shipment paths.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** Partial shipments create extra picking, packaging, transport, and customer-contact effort. The partial-shipment ground/backorder path has 7.2 kgCO2e/order, higher than the standard stock-ground path at 3.6 kgCO2e/order.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Complete shipments can reduce customer effort and second-transport emissions. However, if availability rules become too strict, backorder waiting or cancellations may increase. The rule should allow partial shipment for Critical-downtime or customer-approved cases.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Partial-shipment rate, backorder waiting time, second shipment frequency, `transport_kgco2e`, late delivery, cancellation rate, and customer-contact events.

**S12. Create a sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**

- **Target path or segment:** All exceptions to SP1, SP2, and Q1, including unjustified air, unjustified dedicated vans, and packaging-policy violations.
- **Mechanism of environmental improvement:** The exception log makes overrides visible, auditable, and reviewable. It reduces hidden displacement of environmental burden into expedited modes or rework loops.
- **Likely service/quality/resilience effect:** Service and resilience improve if exceptions are used for genuine operational reasons rather than habit. Quality improves if Q1 overrides are reviewed. Administrative burden increases slightly.
- **Required process-mining evidence to monitor:** Exception count by policy, reason code, approver, segment, post-exception late delivery, post-exception emissions, repeat offenders, and conformance trends over time.

# 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching rebound: energy savings are offset by later expediting.**  
   Larger CNC or heat-treatment batches reduce energy per item, but longer batch waiting can threaten promised dates. If delayed orders are later recovered by air freight or dedicated vans, the transport emissions can erase the production energy savings.  
   **Monitoring:** batch waiting time followed by air/van use, late delivery by batch-wait band, and post-batch `expedite_flag`.

2. **Obsolescence displacement: efficient batches create unsold inventory.**  
   Batch-produced items may be energy-efficient per unit, but if demand forecasts are wrong, 6% of batch-produced items are not shipped within 14 days. This converts an energy saving into inventory waste, storage effort, and potential scrap.  
   **Monitoring:** batch size, forecast accuracy, days from batch completion to shipment, and scrap or write-off events.

3. **Air-to-ground displacement: mode shift creates later exceptions.**  
   Replacing air with ground for Standard-stock Service-contract orders can reduce transport emissions, but if customers or internal service teams later perceive the service as too slow, they may override the rule and request urgent shipment. The environmental gain is displaced into exception traffic.  
   **Monitoring:** SP1 overrides after ground adoption, air share for Service-contract cases, and repeated customer escalations.

4. **Consolidation delay causing emergency recovery.**  
   Consolidated trucks reduce emissions by 35–55%, but the 12–24h consolidation wait can push orders close to their promise limit. If a consolidation window is missed, the recovery path may be more carbon-intensive than the original ground parcel.  
   **Monitoring:** missed consolidation departures, recovery route mode, late delivery after consolidation, and emergency production following consolidation delay.

5. **Reinforced packaging material burden.**  
   Reinforced packaging reduces damage-related returns by 40% for Made-to-order heavy parts, but it doubles package material. If applied too broadly to light or low-risk parts, the material and transport burden may outweigh the damage-reduction benefit.  
   **Monitoring:** `package_material_kg`, damage-related returns, route mode, part weight or geometry, and Q1 compliance.

6. **CNC_A routing displaces delay into machine queues.**  
   Shifting work from CNC_B to CNC_A reduces rework and scrap, but CNC_A may become congested. The environmental benefit can be displaced into longer lead times, overtime, or rushed production elsewhere.  
   **Monitoring:** CNC_A queue length, CNC_B emergency use, machine utilization, late delivery by machine routing, and rework rate by machine.

7. **Inspection gate bottleneck causing rushed later inspections.**  
   Adding an inspection quality gate can reduce rework loops, but if inspection capacity is not increased, orders may pile up. Later, rushed inspections may again miss defects, recreating the original problem.  
   **Monitoring:** inspection queue time, gate pass/fail rate, rework loops after gate introduction, and late delivery for inspected batches.

8. **Partial-shipment reduction worsening backorders or cancellations.**  
   Reducing partial shipments lowers second-shipment emissions, but if complete availability takes too long, customers may experience longer backorder waiting or may cancel. The environmental gain can be accompanied by service deterioration and wasted prior processing.  
   **Monitoring:** partial-shipment rate, backorder waiting time, cancellation rate, customer contacts, and complete-order cycle time.

# 6. Balanced recommendation

The recommended redesign is not a single green initiative but a **segment-based order-to-delivery control package** that separates true urgency from flexible demand, reduces rework and packaging waste, and uses batching and consolidation only where promise slack allows.

**Recommended redesign package**

1. **Create four service lanes based on case type, customer type, and downtime criticality.**
   - **Critical true downtime lane:** retain emergency production and dedicated van where SP2 justification is present. Use shared emergency courier lanes only where reliability evidence supports them.
   - **Service-contract priority lane:** for Standard-stock Service-contract orders, use priority picking plus ground or consolidated truck when the promise can still be met with high probability. Air is used only when slack is insufficient or during carrier disruption.
   - **Regular stock consolidation lane:** non-urgent Standard-stock Regular orders are moved to twice-daily consolidated truck departures when slack exists.
   - **Made-to-order planned lane:** MTO orders are routed through carbon-aware promise negotiation, dynamic batching, CNC_A where slack allows, and an inspection quality gate.

2. **Use promise-based routing rather than blanket mode bans.**
   - The green shipping recommender should apply SP1: use ground or consolidated truck when promised lead time can still be met with probability above 85%.
   - Air and dedicated vans should not be treated as equally unjustified. They remain valid for Critical-downtime, insufficient slack, disruption recovery, and approved exceptions.

3. **Batch dynamically, but cap waiting by segment.**
   - Batch CNC_A and heat treatment when due-date slack and forecast confidence are sufficient.
   - Do not allow long batch waits for Service-contract MTO orders or orders close to p90 delay thresholds.
   - Limit large batches where demand forecast uncertainty is high, to avoid obsolescence.

4. **Reduce rework as a core environmental lever.**
   - Shift MTO work from CNC_B to CNC_A when queue and slack allow.
   - Add an inspection quality gate for high-rework machine/part combinations.
   - Monitor the `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production` loop as a sustainability defect, not only a quality defect.

5. **Make packaging route-compatible and risk-based.**
   - Enforce Q1: Made-to-order parts over 8 kg should use reinforced packaging unless inspection certifies low damage risk.
   - Add packaging-route compatibility checks before shipment to reduce the 620 observed packaging-rework events.
   - Avoid expanding reinforced packaging to low-risk light parts, because package material would increase without proportional damage reduction.

6. **Reduce partial shipments unless service-critical.**
   - Change availability-check rules to favor complete shipment within the promised window.
   - Allow partial shipment for Critical-downtime cases, customer-approved splits, or cases where the missing item has a reliable short replenishment time.

7. **Govern exceptions explicitly.**
   - Implement a sustainability exception log for SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides.
   - Every air shipment, dedicated-van dispatch, or Q1 deviation should carry a reason, approver, slack calculation, and alternative-route check where relevant.

**Why this package is balanced**

- It reduces the largest visible environmental burdens: avoidable air transport, dedicated-van emptiness, inefficient small batches, rework loops, scrap, packaging rework, and second shipments.
- It protects service by using `promised_lead_time_hours`, segment-specific rules, and high-probability promise checks instead of forcing the slowest low-carbon option on all orders.
- It protects quality by retaining reinforced packaging where damage risk is high and by strengthening inspection where rework is concentrated.
- It protects resilience by requiring alternative routes for Service-contract customers, by not making consolidation a single point of failure, and by preserving dedicated-van capacity for true downtime.
- It avoids burden shifting by monitoring obsolescence, batch waiting, exception overrides, packaging material, and post-intervention expediting.

The package deliberately avoids the following one-sided actions:

- Forcing all air shipments to ground, because some Standard-stock Service-contract and MTO cases have insufficient slack.
- Maximizing batch size, because obsolescence and late-delivery risk rise.
- Removing reinforced packaging, because damage-related returns and hidden rework would increase.
- Eliminating dedicated vans, because true Critical-downtime cases require protected service.
- Shifting all production to CNC_A, because queue congestion and resilience risks would increase.

# 7. Validation and governance plan

**Baseline and post-implementation measurement**

Before rollout, compute baseline indicators by segment and path:

- Median and p90 cycle time versus `promised_lead_time_hours`
- Late-delivery rate
- Cancellation rate
- Partial-shipment and backorder rates
- `energy_kwh` per order and per item
- `transport_kgco2e` per order by `route_mode`
- `material_scrap_kg` per order
- `package_material_kg` per order
- Rework-loop frequency
- Obsolescence rate for batch-produced items
- SP1, SP2, and Q1 violation rates

After implementation, compare the same indicators using segmented dashboards.

**Conformance monitoring**

1. **SP1: ground or consolidated truck when promise probability is above 85%.**
   - Monitor the share of Standard-stock Service-contract orders shipped by air despite sufficient slack.
   - Track the current 22% SP1 violation rate and reduce it through the green shipping recommender and exception log.
   - Investigate cases where air is used despite high promise probability.

2. **SP2: dedicated vans require explicit Critical-downtime justification.**
   - Monitor dedicated-van events for missing justification.
   - Track the current 31% missing-justification rate.
   - Require justification fields for Critical-downtime category, downtime confirmation, and alternative-route check.

3. **Q1: reinforced packaging for MTO parts over 8 kg unless certified low risk.**
   - Monitor Q1 violations and correlate them with damage-related returns, packaging rework, and customer complaints.
   - Track `package_material_kg` to ensure reinforced packaging is not overapplied to low-risk items.

4. **R1: alternative route availability for Service-contract customers.**
   - During carrier disruptions, verify that at least one alternative route remains available.
   - Monitor route-mode concentration by customer type and region.
   - Test recovery paths for consolidated-truck failures and ground-carrier outages.

**Segment-level service monitoring**

For each segment, monitor:

- Standard-stock Regular: late delivery, consolidation wait, cancellation, and partial-shipment rate.
- Standard-stock Service-contract: air-to-ground shift, late delivery, priority-pick performance, SP1 compliance, and customer escalation.
- Made-to-order Regular: batch waiting, CNC_A/CNC_B routing, rework loops, late delivery, and promise-date negotiation acceptance.
- Made-to-order Service-contract: due-date slack, batching caps, rework, and service-contract penalties.
- Critical-downtime Service-contract: true-downtime classification accuracy, dedicated-van justification, emergency-production rate, and late/downtime impact.

**Exception governance**

Create a sustainability exception register linked to the event log. Each exception should contain:

- Policy violated: SP1, SP2, or Q1
- Order segment and customer type
- Promise slack or downtime justification
- Selected route or packaging deviation
- Reason code
- Approver
- Expected recovery or mitigation action
- Post-event outcome: late delivery, rework, emissions, customer escalation

Review exceptions regularly to identify structural causes, such as:

- Repeated air use because ground reliability is poor
- Dedicated-van overuse because criticality classification is weak
- Packaging rework because route compatibility rules are missing
- Batch waiting causing expediting
- Inspection delays causing rushed quality checks

**Process-mining validation approach**

Because the discovered model has low precision for emergency paths and partial shipments, validation should use event-level checks rather than relying only on a single discovered model:

- Variant analysis for emergency production, partial shipment, and backorder paths.
- Conformance checking against SP1, SP2, Q1, and R1 rules.
- Path-level comparison of expected versus actual routes after intervention.
- Rework-loop detection using `Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production` and `Package -> Rework packaging` patterns.
- Batch-level analysis using `batch_id`, `batch_size`, and waiting time.
- Rebound monitoring: check whether energy-efficient batching is followed by air freight, dedicated vans, or emergency production.

**Governance cadence**

- Weekly operational review: late delivery, air/van exceptions, batch waiting, packaging rework, and inspection bottlenecks.
- Monthly sustainability-service review: energy per item, transport kgCO2e per order, scrap, packaging material, obsolescence, and SP1/SP2/Q1 trends.
- Quarterly resilience review: alternative-route availability, carrier disruption performance, dedicated-van justification quality, and emergency-path variants.

The redesign should be accepted only if it delivers environmental improvement without unacceptable deterioration in service, quality, or resilience. If any guardrail is breached—especially Critical-downtime late delivery, Service-contract cancellations, rework loops, or obsolescence—the affected intervention should be recalibrated before further rollout.
