# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Method to separate core vs peripheral:** Core = high frequency *for that resource* (>15% of their events) **AND** high share within activity **AND** aligned with formal cert/home team **AND** consistent case_complexity; Peripheral = low frequency, occasional substitution, or routed via `temporary_substitution=True` flag / escalation_level>1.

**R-A Automation Boundary:** Core A1/A2 is *automation* (690 low cases, 0.11). No peripheral. Evidence that >60% of all A1/A2 originates from Nora. Naïve interpretation would count A1/A2 for humans as "they register" — but formatted log shows Norahuman handover; automation should be excluded from human role mining or modeled as distinct system resource.

**R-B1 Front-Office Core:** Core A2/A4/A12 dominate >75% of their events. A1 is fallback when Nora incompleteness. Peripheral A3/A7/A8 are low-volume, low-complexity triage or, for Amira, 18 pricing events on medium cases where she drafts note before Fatima/Giorgio. Complexity signals seniority: Amira's median 0.34 > Ben's 0.29 and handles 2x high cases. Peripheral work does not imply competence for high-risk pricing/security.

**R-B2 Seasonal Contingent:** Core identical to B1 but *capped* at Low complexity (0.18). Peripheral is effectively absent — they do not do A5/A6/A7/A9/A11/A14. The 17 reopens after CloseReopenrework path show peripheral failure: Validate notes incomplete. Frequency alone suggests interchangeability with B1; complexity + service time + rework rate shows not.

**R-C Technical Specialists:** Core A3/A5/A10 = ~85% of their work. Peripheral A6 is key discriminator: Chen 24 vs Dina 6, plus Chen 42 Critical vs 9, median gap 0.23. Dina's peripheral quality/KB (10-11) is junior exposure. Authority: both can Configure/Implement, only Chen routinely trusted for security-adjacent diagnostics and critical Enterprise cases. Giorgio's 72 A7 events are peripheral for him but represent ~37% of Fatima's 10-week volume — only during substitution, not permanent. Substitution  qualification.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Shadow = performs formal activity without formal authority/certification, or informal coverage; Bridging = connects otherwise separate functional clusters in handover network. Both are beneficial for resilience but create risk if mistaken for permanent capacity.

**1. R07_Giorgio — Commercial Shadow (Senior Service Agent shadowing Pricing):**
*   Formal: Front Office Senior Agent (entitlement + pricing certs, 7.4y). Core Front-Office (A1/A2/A4/A12).
*   Shadow: 72 A7 Pricing Exception Review + 88 Negotiations, 20 preliminary Approvals. Covers Fatima's absence weeks 3-4: 31 A7 events, median duration almost doubled (3874min), indicating slower, more consultative execution and likely queuing.
*   Evidence: similarity 0.71 to Fatima but 0.84 to Amira; activities span both clusters; product_line commercial exceptions on complex front-office cases.
*   Benefits: Prevents queue collapse during absence, retains customer continuity (front-office relationship + pricing), enables mentoring.
*   Risks: Lower efficiency, higher error pricing risk (no formal pricing authority depth), masks understaffing of Commercial. If management counts his 72 events as Commercial capacity, understates single point of failure (only Fatima fully qualified). Training need: formal pricing delegation limits, escalation thresholds.

**2. R13_Mateo — Cross-Functional Escalation Broker:**
*   Formal: Incident Coordinator, escalation cert, 5.9y, day shift.
*   Bridging: Only resource touching A1-A14 with flat distribution (max 52 events on any activity). 42 Reopens, 44 A9 coordinations, 16 Security filters, 10 Pricing filters, 20 QC. Sequence fragments 3 & 5: `DiagnoseErik SecurityMateo CoordinateImplement` and `ReopenMateospecialistHana Approval`.
*   Network: High out-degree/in-degree in handover-of-work and working-together networks; likely highest betweenness centrality; subcontracting network shows Mateo as subcontractor who spawns child cases to specialists and returns.
*   Benefits: Filters low-risk security referrals (reduced false escalations weeks 7-8), triages reopens, bridges Front/Technical/Risk/Commercial/Management silos, contains temp-agent rework.
*   Risks: Becomes hidden bottleneck/filter with decision impact without formal approval authority; weeks 7-8 filtering left 2 critical security cases waiting >2 days; week 5 prelim A9 required 62% recheck — authority boundary blurred. Naïve clustering with Hana (0.54) mistakes coordination for approval. Management must distinguish `broker` (routing) from `governor` (ratification).

**3. R05_Erik — Rare but Critical Security Specialist:**
*   Formal: Security Specialist, 6.8y, Risk team, security cert.
*   Core: A6 96 events only but median complexity 0.86, 55 critical cases, concentrated in product_line=Enterprise Security. Peripheral Diagnose/Implement only in critical path.
*   Shadow pattern: Not a shadow performer, but is *shadowed* by broker — Mateo filters referrals to him, creating implicit gatekeeping.
*   Benefits: Deep expertise prevents catastrophic security misconfiguration; low volume = high leverage.
*   Risks: Single point of failure; low frequency leads capacity planners to underfund; broker filtering trade-off (fewer false positives vs delay on true positives). Naïve frequency pruning would label him outlier/inefficient. Must protect with SLA on escalation_level=Critical and backup coverage (Chen does 24 A6 but at lower complexity).

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format - PM4Py expects case:concept:name, concept:name, time:timestamp, org:resource
# ------------------------------------------------------------
df = pd.read_csv("order_to_resolution_log.csv",
                 parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])

# Enrichment columns in scenario: case_complexity, customer_segment, product_line,
# temporary_substitution (bool), escalation_level (Low/Med/High/Critical)

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    timest_key="time:timestamp"
    # org:resource stays as resource column
)

# optional: ensure sorted
df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Organizational Roles discovery (activity frequency)
#    - Naïve baseline to be interrogated, not trusted as formal structure
# ------------------------------------------------------------
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    df,
    # typical params: each role needs at least 1 activity, clustering threshold
    # use default hierarchical clustering on resource-activity matrix
)

for i, role in enumerate(roles):
    print(f"Role {i}: {role}")

# roles is List[List[resource]] - e.g. [[R01,R02,R11,R12], [R03,R04], ...] -> over-merged

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Activity-based resource similarity (cosine over normalized counts)
# ------------------------------------------------------------
similarity_matrix = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(df)
# returns dict {(res1,res2): cosine}
print(similarity_matrix[("R03_Chen","R04_Dina")])  # 0.94
print(similarity_matrix[("R06_Fatima","R07_Giorgio")])  # 0.71

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Handover-of-work, Working-together, Subcontracting networks
#    To separate frequency from authority / coordination
# ------------------------------------------------------------
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)          # Directed: res A -> res B
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)  # Undirected co-presence per case
subcontracting = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)      # A -> B -> A pattern

# Inspect centrality - Mateo should dominate betweenness despite low volume
import networkx as nx
G_h = nx.from_pandas_edgelist(handover, source="source", target="target", edge_attr="weight", create_using=nx.DiGraph)
print(nx.betweenness_centrality(G_h, weight="weight"))  # Expect R13_Mateo high

# Working together shows QC pair, Tech pair, Temps together
# Subcontracting reveals Nora->FrontOffice and Mateo as broker: Front->Mateo->Specialist->Mateo->Close

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Join role output with case_complexity & temporary_substitution to avoid over-interpretation
# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Aggregate complexity and substitution per resource-activity
agg = df.groupby(["org:resource","concept:name"]).agg(
    n=("case:concept:name","count"),
    median_complexity=("case_complexity","median"),
    mean_escalation=("escalation_level", lambda x: (x=="Critical").mean()),
    substitution_rate=("temporary_substitution","mean"),
    critical_cases=("case_complexity", lambda x: (x>0.8).sum())
).reset_index()

# Pivot to resource profile with complexity context
resource_profile = df.groupby("org:resource").agg(
    median_case_complexity=("case_complexity","median"),
    high_critical_rate=("case_complexity", lambda x: (x>0.7).mean()),
    product_security_share=("product_line", lambda x: (x=="Enterprise Security").mean())
)

# Example: unmask Chen vs Dina despite 0.94 similarity
print(resource_profile.loc[["R03_Chen","R04_Dina"]])
# Illustrate Giorgio shadow: high substitution_rate on A7
print(agg[(agg["org:resource"]=="R07_Giorgio") & (agg["concept:name"]=="A7 Pricing Exception Review")])

# Filter substitution episodes to test temporary vs permanent competence
sub_episode = df[df["temporary_substitution"]==True]
print(sub_episode.groupby(["org:resource","concept:name"]).size())
# Expect: R07_Giorgio A7=31 (weeks 3-4), R13_Mateo A9=21 (week5)

# Service time analysis to see efficiency gap during substitution
from pm4py.statistics.service_time import get as service_time_get
# or use pm4py.get_service_time
service_times = pm4py.get_service_time(df, agg_by_activity=True)  # per activity case duration
# Compare A7 duration with vs without substitution flag
import seaborn as sns
# median 38 vs 74 min story would emerge

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Safeguards: do NOT treat frequency clusters as authority
# ------------------------------------------------------------
# - Exclude Automation Bot from human clustering or tag is_system=True
df_human = df[df["org:resource"]!="R14_Nora"]

# - Re-run similarity weighted by complexity/escalation, not raw counts
# - Threshold on n but also on median_complexity before merging
# - Validate roles with interviews on formal certs/contract/tenure
# - Use variants and waiting time to surface rework loops
variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)
waiting = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(df)  # reveals >2 day waits weeks 7-8 for Erik queue

# Filter to critical cases to see who actually handles risk
critical_df = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(df, "escalation_level", ["Critical"], level="case")
print(critical_df["org:resource"].value_counts())  # Erik, Hana, Mateo, Chen dominate
```

*Pseudocode summary:* `format_dataframe`  baseline `discover_organizational_roles`  `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` to explain 0.94/0.97 similarities  `handover/working together/subcontracting` to expose broker and gatekeeping centrality  join aggregated `case_complexity`/`escalation_level`/`temporary_substitution`/`product_line` to stratify inside apparent clusters  exclude `R14_Nora` system actor and weight by authority, not just count.

# 6. Operational interpretation

**How management should use the structure:**

*   **Staffing:** Keep **R-A automation** for low-complexity A1/A2 but do not count as FTE. Plan human Front-Office capacity on **post-automation** volume. Maintain **B1 core (Amira/Ben)** for low-medium, **B2 contingent** strictly for simple seasonal overflow with validation checklist and mandatory review before Close to prevent the 17 reopens. Protect **R-C two-tier**: Dina needs mentoring, not equal critical allocation; Chen late shift covers complex diagnostics — avoid scheduling gap. Protect **R-F Erik** as single point of failure for Enterprise Security; cross-train Chen for Level-1 security while retaining Erik for Critical; set SLA and on-call cover for product_line=Enterprise Security.
*   **Training:** Formalize **R-E Giorgio shadow** into bounded delegation: pricing cert != pricing authority. Provide pricing exception masterclass and define threshold where Giorgio must escalate to Fatima. Use temporary substitution episodes as training labs but measure service time (3874min gap) to set realistic throughput. Develop **R-I Mateo** coordination playbook: routing criteria, filtering rules, and escalation to Hana — train on product risk triage to avoid 2-day critical waits.
*   **Risk Control:** Separate **broker (Mateo) vs governor (Hana)** decision rights: log prelim vs final A9, enforce recheck workflow (as in week 5). For **R-D Commercial**, require dual sign-off when shadow handles >X value pricing. For **R-H Quality**, enforce independent QC (Implementer  QC Checker on same case) to preserve gate integrity; track KB Update as leading indicator.
*   **Resilience:** Build roster with **absence simulation**: if Fatima absent, buffer with Giorgio + delay tolerance, not full replacement; if Hana absent, limit Mateo to triage + defer approvals, communicate audit workshop risk. For security surge (weeks 7-8), apply filter + priority queue, not blanket throttling. Maintain Front Office day/late and QC late/day coverage.

**Cautions — do not change staffing based only on mined roles:**

1.  **Frequency  Authority or Competence.** Similar profiles (R03-R04 0.94, R01-R02 0.93, R06-R07 0.71, R08-R13 0.54) hide complexity, certification and decision-rights. Treating substitution episodes (Giorgio's 31 pricing reviews, Mateo's 21 prelim approvals) as proof of permanent competence would legitimize shadow roles without legal/commercial risk coverage and would overestimate capacity while underestimating rework (62% recheck) and duration inflation (74 vs 38 min). Any staffing reduction in Commercial or Management based on shadow coverage would create hidden single point of failure.

2.  **Ignoring Case Complexity and Product Risk Underestimates Tail Risk.** Cutting Rare-Critical (Erik) or downgrading Technical Seniority (Chen vs Dina) because of low event counts or high similarity ignores that 55 critical cases depend on Erik and that Chen handles 5x critical cases of Dina. Likewise, removing temp quality controls because temps "close as many requests as permanent staff" ignores that their 0% critical exposure and 17 reopens create downstream load for coordinators, technical and quality teams (ReopenMateospecialist). Staffing models must be stratified by `case_complexity`/`escalation_level`/`product_line`, weighted by service time and rework cost, not raw activity frequency, and validated with handover network bottlenecks and waiting-time analysis before any headcount change.
