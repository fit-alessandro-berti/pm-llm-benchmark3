## 1. Feasibility model

Partial-order relation set (a < b means b may not start before a completes; a || b means no ordering is imposed):

```
GLOBAL (every case)
  Register < Triage
  Triage < Identity Check
  Triage < Document Check_1
  Identity Check < Assessment_1
  Document Check_1 < Assessment_1
  Assessment_final < Decision
  Decision < Notify

CONCURRENCY PERMITTED
  Identity Check || Document Check                  when originals are not required   (K-104, K-411)
  Identity Check || Originals Receipt               (K-219, K-337)
  Escalation Advice (S1) || {Identity, Document, Assessment}      advisory work may overlap
  Preliminary Compliance Screen (C1) || {Identity, Document, Assessment}

ORIGINALS REQUIRED (K-219, K-337)
  Originals Receipt < Document Check_k              for every k (including rework checks)

REWORK LOOP (factual in K-337; latent in K-219 and K-104)
  Assessment_k  or  Compliance Review  -> Request More Info_k
  Request More Info_k < Document Check_(k+1) < Assessment_(k+1)

HIGH RISK / HIGH VALUE (K-104, K-337)
  Assessment_final < Compliance Review_final < Decision
  Preliminary Compliance Screen < Compliance Review_final          (screen never replaces the final review)
  Escalation Advice < Compliance Review_final                      (when the advice is what triggers the review)

CASE-SPECIFIC ORDERS ACTUALLY OBSERVED
  K-104  Reg < Tri < {Id || Doc} < Asmt < Esc < CR < Dec < Not
  K-219  Reg < Tri < {Id || OrigRcpt} ; OrigRcpt < Doc < Asmt < Dec < Not
  K-337  Reg < Tri < {Id || OrigRcpt} ; OrigRcpt < Doc1 < Asmt1 < RMI1 < Doc2 < Asmt2 < RMI2 < Doc3 < Asmt3 < CR < Dec < Not
  K-411  Reg < Tri < Id < Doc < Asmt < Dec < Not     (Id < Doc is a system-lock artefact, not a normative rule)
```

Conditional and declarative constraints:

```
D1   high_risk OR high_value  =>  EXISTS Compliance Review_final with Assessment_final < CR_final < Decision
D2   originals_required       =>  Originals Receipt < every Document Check_k
D3   NOT originals_required   =>  Identity Check and Document Check may overlap in time
D4   Request More Info_k issued  =>  Document Check_(k+1) AND Assessment_(k+1) must precede Decision
D5   (high_risk OR high_value) AND RMI issued after a Compliance Review  =>  new or explicitly reaffirmed CR before Decision
D6   resource(Assessment_final) != resource(Compliance Review_final)                     [four-eyes]
D7   Preliminary Compliance Screen does NOT discharge D1; it may reduce CR_final duration by roughly 1-3 h
D8   FOR ALL resources r: service intervals performed by r are pairwise disjoint          [rule 11]
D9   Compliance Review only by C1; Decision and Notify only by M1; Escalation Advice only by S1 (this window)
D10  RMI may be issued only from Assessment or Compliance Review; a Document Check can flag but not issue
D11  Decision in {approve, approve-with-conditions, reject}; a known unresolved discrepancy must be
     either resolved via D4 or explicitly addressed in the decision record (never silently ignored)
D12  start(a) >= max(ready(a), free(resource(a))), ready(a) = max complete(predecessors); queue hours are forecasts
D13  complete(a) - start(a) should lie inside the service-time guidance ranges (soft, wide)
D14  Concurrency claims require real-time timestamps; batch-entered times cannot evidence overlap (K-411)
```

Which interventions change what:

- Timing only (control flow and decision set unchanged): K-411 moves Document Check earlier within a partial order that D3 already permits; K-104 moves S1 and part of C1's work earlier and inserts a preliminary screen that D7 already contemplates. K-104 has a secondary priority/queue-discipline effect (a C1 reservation reorders other cases), but it does not change routing of K-104's own activities or its decision options.
- Routing: K-219 re-assigns one activity from D1 to D2. Every precedence stays the same; the service-time distribution and the rework probability change because the resource changes.
- Decision policy: K-337 removes an allowed transition (Assessment_2 -> RMI_2) and forces the process into D11 with incomplete evidence. This changes the reachable outcome set (approve-with-conditions or reject instead of a later unconditional approve), not merely the timestamp of an unchanged decision.

The four cases must not be forced into one sequence: K-104 and K-411 have an Identity/Document parallel block, K-219 and K-337 have an external Originals Receipt gate, K-337 has loops, and only K-104 and K-337 carry the compliance gate.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: The case carried a sanctions ambiguity from Triage (Mon 08:50) that was not acted upon. The binding delays were three queues: D1 (case entered at 08:50, started 21:40, about 13 h, matching the Monday snapshot), A1 (ready Tue 05:40, started 15:10, about 9.5 h, matching Tuesday's 9 h forecast) and C1 (ready Tue 21:20, started Wed 13:10, about 16 h, matching Tuesday's 16 h forecast). The compliance review itself took 4.5 h because C1 had no prior exposure to the evidence. Escalation advice (2 h) sat serially between Assessment and Compliance because nobody had requested it earlier.

Minimal intervention: At Triage T1 forwards a preliminary packet to S1 and books a provisional C1 slot. Nothing else changes: Identity stays with I1 Mon 10:40-12:50, Document Check stays with D1 Mon 21:40-Tue 05:40 (the packet does not change D1's queue), Assessment stays with A1 Tue 15:10. S1's advice moves from Tue 19:20 to Mon morning and becomes a parallel activity; C1 spends 1.5-2 h on a preliminary screen before the case is assessment-ready.

Temporal propagation: The case becomes ready for final compliance at Tue ~19:00 in both worlds; the intervention cannot move readiness earlier because it does not touch D1 or A1. What it moves is (a) the removal of the serial 2 h S1 step (Assessment may finish slightly earlier, ~18:40, because A1 already has S1's reading of the ambiguity), (b) C1's start, from Wed 13:10 to the reserved slot, and (c) C1's service time, from 4.5 h to 2-4 h because the sanctions question and the corporate structure were pre-read. If the slot is Wed 08:00, review ends ~10:30-12:00, M1 signs by ~12:30 and Notify lands ~12:40, so cycle time falls from 58.5 h to ~52.5 h. If C1 can take the case Tue evening right after Assessment, Compliance ends ~22:00 and the Decision waits only for M1 (Wed 08:30 or Tue evening), giving 38-49 h. The saving therefore is not "16 h of queue disappears": most of the queue reduction is converted to when C1's calendar allows the slot and when M1 is available.

Process-feasibility check: Register < Triage < {Identity || Document} < Assessment < CR_final < Decision < Notify holds. The preliminary screen precedes and does not replace CR_final (D7). Four-eyes holds: A1 assessed, C1 reviewed, S1 only advised. D8 holds if C1's preliminary slot does not overlap C1's other intervals; the provisional slot necessarily displaces other C1 work by its duration. S1's early packet is compatible with rule 4/3 because S1's work is advisory and overlaps with checks by design.

Best/base/worst case: Best: -9 to -20 h (C1 Tue evening, M1 available same evening or first thing Wed). Base: -5 to -8 h (reserved Wed morning slot, 2-4 h review). Worst: 0 to +2 h when the packet lacks D1's document findings, the reserved slot falls before Assessment completes and is lost, and the case rejoins C1's queue behind the two urgent cases; a further tail of +8 to +36 h if the preliminary screen issues an RMI that final review would not have needed.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: C1 logs showing preliminary reads rarely shorten final reviews (double reading instead of saving 1-3 h); evidence that the reserved slot repeatedly displaces genuinely urgent compliance cases with larger loss exposure; S1's own queue exceeding a few hours; or A1's assessment being the true information source, so that S1 cannot advise usefully from a Triage-level packet.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: Triage chose D1 for quality. Originals arrived Mon 17:30, when D1's queue was ~11 h and D1 was committed to complex cases overnight (including K-104 Mon 21:40-Tue 05:40); K-219 therefore started only Tue 09:30. The 4 h check was accurate. Assessment readiness at Tue 13:30 met a ~6.5 h A2 backlog; the 20:00-22:30 assessment then missed M1, who signed Wed 08:30. Two waits dominate: 16 h for D1 and ~10 h for A2 plus overnight.

Minimal intervention: At Mon 17:30 the Document Check is assigned to D2 (queue ~5 h at 16:00) instead of D1. Identity, Assessment and Decision resources are unchanged.

Temporal propagation: D2 can start once its 5 h backlog clears, about Mon 22:30 (assuming the Documents pool works overnight, as D1 demonstrably did). A generalist on handwriting takes 5-8 h, so completion falls Tue ~03:30-06:30 instead of 13:30: readiness for Assessment moves forward 7-10 h. Entering A2's queue before Tuesday's 6 h backlog builds, the case is likely assessed Tue ~11:00-16:30, so M1 can sign the same afternoon; the overnight wait before the Decision disappears. This is why the cycle-time gain (12-18 h) exceeds the document-stage gain (7-10 h): the earlier readiness crosses the M1 end-of-day boundary. If instead D2 flags illegible entries, D10 means A2 must issue an RMI at ~Tue 13:00; the external response (8-36 h) pushes the second Document Check into Wed-Thu and the Decision to Wed evening-Fri, later than the factual Wed 09:00. If D2 works only daytime, D2 starts Tue ~12:00 and the case ends up 3 h later than factual.

Process-feasibility check: Register < Triage < {Identity || Originals Receipt}; Originals Receipt (Mon 17:30) < Document Check (D2 start >= 17:30) < Assessment < Decision < Notify holds (D2). No compliance gate (low risk, low value). D8: D2's Mon-night interval does not collide with D2's factual K-411 interval Tue 15:00-19:30. The rework branch satisfies D4 (new Document Check and Assessment before Decision).

Best/base/worst case: Best: -18 h (D2 5 h, A2 late morning, M1 early afternoon). Base: -12 to -16 h with probability roughly 0.65-0.85. Worst: +12 to +47 h with probability roughly 0.15-0.35 (rework), or +3 h if D2 is day-shift only; plus a small, unmeasured probability of a misread that passes to Assessment.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2 clarification rates on handwritten originals above ~30% or D2 service times above ~8 h for handwriting; D2 having no overnight coverage; D2's actual queue on Monday evening being far above the 16:00 forecast (Monday arrivals were high); or post-decision error reviews showing D1's accuracy on handwriting is what prevented downstream corrections.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: A slow, inconsistent supplier drove two full rework loops. Each loop cost external waiting (20.5 h, then 70.5 h across the weekend), a D1 check (5 h, 3.5 h) and an A1 assessment (2.5 h, 2.5 h) plus A1 overnight queue positions (each Document Check finished in the afternoon; A1 restarted next morning). Compliance and Decision then followed normally. The weekend is what made loop 2 so expensive: ~50 of its 70.5 h were non-working.

Minimal intervention: At Fri 10:30 A1 may not issue RMI_2; the assessment must close with a recommendation that explicitly addresses the remaining inconsistency (conditional approval or rejection). All upstream activities and loop 1 are as observed.

Temporal propagation: The case becomes compliance-ready at Fri 10:30 instead of Tue(2) 10:30, a 96 h shift. C1's queue is uncertain (Thu snapshot 5 h, Friday unknown) and the review is likely longer than the factual 4 h because C1 must evaluate incomplete evidence and the proposed conditions, and rule 6 would normally let C1 ask for more information, which the intervention forbids. Compliance therefore completes Fri evening or Mon(2) morning, the Decision follows when M1 is next available (Fri ~19:00 or Mon(2) 08:30), and Notify follows within minutes. Cycle time falls from ~214 h to ~104-167 h. The saving is large precisely because the suppressed loop was the weekend one; the same intervention applied to loop 1 (midweek, 20.5 h) would have saved roughly one day.

Process-feasibility check: Register < Triage < {Identity || Originals}; Originals < Doc_1 < Asmt_1 < RMI_1 < Doc_2 < Asmt_2 < CR < Decision < Notify satisfies D2, D4 (loop 1 was closed properly), D1 (CR after the latest Assessment, Asmt_2, and before Decision), D6 (A1 vs C1) and D11 (discrepancy addressed in the decision). D5 is not triggered because no CR preceded RMI_1. The prohibition must bind C1 as well as A1, otherwise C1 could reopen the loop and the counterfactual collapses back to the factual timeline. Note also that if K-219 and K-337 belong to the same calendar week, D1's intervals Tue 08:30-14:30 (K-337) and Tue 09:30-13:30 (K-219) violate D8; either the weeks differ or one log was batch-entered, a caution about timestamp reliability.

Best/base/worst case: Best (time): -110 h, approve with well-specified conditions that the later factual confirmation would have satisfied anyway. Base: -48 to -100 h, approve with conditions; slightly higher C1 effort; post-decision monitoring of the condition. Worst (value): rejection of a case that the factual confirmation showed was acceptable, appeal or resubmission, resolution later than the factual Wed(2) and reputational cost; or a conditional approval of a case where the unresolved total masked a material problem, i.e. a rare large loss and a weak audit record.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Historical data showing second-round confirmations frequently change the assessment outcome (material discrepancy rate well above ~10%); regulator or audit guidance that approvals with open supplier discrepancies are not defensible for high-value cases; appeal rates and resubmission costs exceeding the saved days; or C1 refusing conditional sign-off in practice, converting the policy into a rejection machine.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: Triage created only the identity task; the legacy case lock made the document task unclaimable until I2 finished at Tue 13:20. D2 then started at 15:00 (short queue) and finished 19:30. Readiness at 19:30 fell after A2's Tuesday capacity (A2 was occupied until 22:30 that evening on K-219 if the weeks coincide) so the case waited until Wed 09:30, and M1 signed at 14:00. The lock cost ~2 h of serial time directly and ~14 h indirectly through the overnight assessment wait.

Minimal intervention: Triage creates both tasks at 09:35 and a controlled workaround lets D2 take ownership while I2 holds the case; all timestamps are captured in real time. Resources and service times are unchanged.

Temporal propagation: D2's queue was 4 h at 08:00, so D2 reaches K-411 around 12:00-13:30 and finishes ~16:30-18:00 after 4-5 h of service, overlapping Identity (11:30-13:20). Readiness improves by 1.5-3 h, not 5.5 h, because D2's own queue, not the lock, is the second constraint. Whether this translates into a large cycle-time gain depends entirely on A2's evening availability: if A2 has an open window before ~20:00, Assessment finishes Tue evening and M1 (who demonstrably signs into the evening, Wed 18:00-18:40 for K-104) can decide the same night, saving up to ~18 h; if not, the case simply sits higher in Wednesday's queue, Assessment starts ~08:00 instead of 09:30, and the saving is ~2-3 h. Earlier readiness is certain; earlier completion is a threshold event.

Process-feasibility check: Register < Triage < {Identity || Document} < Assessment < Decision < Notify is exactly the partial order D3 permits for a case without originals. No compliance gate. D8 holds for I2 and D2 (distinct resources). D14 is the critical constraint: the workaround must produce system-generated start/complete events; if D2's start is entered after the fact, the log will show concurrency that may not have existed (D2 waiting for the verified identity, for instance).

Best/base/worst case: Best: -6 to -18 h (A2 evening slot; M1 same evening or Wed 08:30). Base: -1.5 to -3.5 h. Worst: 0 to +2.5 h when D2's realised queue is longer than forecast, the lock workaround causes an ownership conflict and the task must be re-created, or a duplicate/lost edit forces re-verification.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Real-time logs showing D2 could not begin until identity data was available (no true concurrency); A2 assessment queues that grow by more than the readiness gain when many cases are made ready earlier; lock-conflict incidents corrupting case data; or system-vs-manual timestamp comparisons revealing that the "overlap" appears only in manually entered times.

Rolling each policy out to 100 similar cases in one week changes the problem from a queue position to a capacity balance.

Reservation of scarce compliance capacity: C1 is the only certified reviewer and already carries 16-20 h of backlog early in the week. A hundred cases each reserving 1.5-2 h of preliminary screen and 2-4 h of final review would demand 350-600 h of C1 time against a working week of perhaps 40-50 h. Reservations then stop meaning anything: when everyone holds a provisional slot, the queue reverts to first-come or to whoever shouts loudest, and the two "urgent" cases that arrived Monday 16:00 are displaced by cases that are merely earlier-flagged. Early S1 packets alone scale better, because S1's advice overlaps other work, but S1 is also a single resource; sending 100 packets is 50-150 h of S1 reading. The policy is only viable if triage filters to genuine sanctions ambiguity, perhaps 10-15 cases.

Substitution between D1 and D2: Redirecting all originals-required standard cases to D2 moves 400-800 h of weekly work onto a resource with a 2-5 h queue; D2 becomes the bottleneck within a day while D1 idles on standard work and the complex corporate queue shrinks. A shortest-expected-completion rule (queue plus expected service by resource and document type) captures most of the K-219 gain without inverting the bottleneck.

Additional or avoided rework: D2 on handwriting adds 15-35 rework loops per 100 cases, each consuming a second Document Check, a second Assessment and 8-36 h of external waiting; K-337's cap removes the second loop in the affected subset but adds conditional reviews, monitoring, appeals and resubmissions that re-enter Register.

Assessment-queue bottlenecks: K-411 and K-219 make cases ready earlier and closer together; the Tuesday assessment queue already "had grown" and one assessor was in training. If 100 cases land at A2 in late afternoon, the queue absorbs the readiness gain and the shift-boundary upside disappears for most of them.

Weekend and external-response effects: K-337's saving is inflated by a Friday RMI; a Monday cap saves one day, not four. External response times (8-36 h, weekends longer) dominate any internal rebalancing.

Timestamp behaviour versus true concurrency: If workaround timestamps are batch-entered, the log will show 100 concurrent identity/document pairs whether or not D2 actually waited, producing a policy "success" that no downstream queue ever sees.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411's dual-task creation. It operates entirely inside the permitted partial order, adds no service time, changes no decision, and produces a certain readiness gain of 1.5-3 h with an occasional large payoff when that gain crosses the assessment or decision end-of-day boundary. Its failure modes are informational rather than procedural: a badly logged workaround can manufacture concurrency on paper, and at scale the earlier arrivals compress the assessment queue. Both are observable in a short pilot and both are reversible.

The most dangerous intervention despite its speed is K-337's prohibition of the second rework loop. Its two-to-four-day saving is real, but it is a change of decision policy dressed as a timing change. The factual record shows the second confirmation was benign, yet the counterfactual decision maker does not know that; the policy converts an information request into either a conditional approval carrying a known open item on a high-risk, high-value case or a rejection that may generate an appeal and a longer path to resolution. It also raises C1's per-case load and creates monitoring work that the process model does not even contain.

Local counterfactuals transport worst for K-104 and K-219. K-104's benefit is a queue position gained at the only certified compliance reviewer; give the same reservation to a hundred cases and the reservations cancel while the urgent cases lose. K-219's benefit exists because D2 happened to have a 5 h queue on a Monday evening; a fixed routing rule would erase that slack within a day, whereas a dynamic shortest-completion rule preserves the logic.

The most valuable additional data are system-generated timestamps for task creation, ownership and start (to separate true concurrency from batch entry), per-resource shift calendars for C1, A2 and M1 (the shift-boundary thresholds drive most of the variance), D2's service-time and clarification distribution on handwritten originals, and, for K-337, the historical rate at which second confirmations changed a decision.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural`: The normative rules are complete; in particular a preliminary compliance screen never discharges the final review, and a Document Check cannot itself issue Request More Info (it flags, Assessment or Compliance issues).
2. `temporal`: The Documents pool works overnight (D1's Mon 21:40-Tue 05:40 interval), whereas Assessment, Compliance and Decision capacity is concentrated in daytime with some early-evening coverage (A2 to ~22:30, M1 to ~18:40); this determines whether readiness gains cross a shift boundary.
3. `resource`: Queue hours in the snapshots hold approximately in the counterfactual world, apart from the target case's own removal or insertion; C1 remains the single certified reviewer all week and S1 the single escalation adviser.
4. `behavioral`: D2's probability of requesting clarification on difficult handwriting is 15-35%, versus roughly 5% for D1, and a clarification becomes a formal RMI at Assessment with an 8-36 h external response.
5. `quality`: The unresolved K-337 discrepancy was ex post benign, but the counterfactual decision maker holds only a prior (about 70-90% benign); A1's recommendation and M1's decision under the cap are therefore conditional approval more often than rejection, not certain approval.
6. `interference`: Queues are approximately FIFO within a resource; a reserved or inserted service interval delays every case behind it by the inserted duration, and no other resource spontaneously absorbs the displaced work.
7. `temporal`: Service times stay within the guidance ranges in the counterfactual; the only intervention-induced service-time change is C1's 1-3 h saving from a complete preliminary screen and a possible 30 min shorter A1 assessment in K-104, and 1-2 h longer C1 reviews under the K-337 cap.
8. `interference`: The four cases are treated as if their weeks coincide for spillover reasoning (K-219's freed A2 slot could benefit K-411), but D1's overlapping Tuesday intervals for K-219 and K-337 show that either the weeks differ or one log was batch-entered; cross-case interactions are therefore indicative, not established.
