## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501  COMPLIANT.** *Premise that must change:* B2 imposes no timing requirement (its text says only "require Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve"), and A6's "pre-commitment approval" language is specific to GPIP-4's 50k rule and does not inform the local SOP; combined with a genuine B4 emergency (4h  6h) that excuses PO sequencing, all required controls exist and the purchase is below every GPIP threshold. *Credibility:* **partially credible** on the timing-text point — the silence is real, and P-501 is the case where a literalist reading most plausibly produces compliance. It is **weak** on the actor point: the express condition "absent Plant Manager … actively delegated Shift Lead" is unmet on stated facts, so SHIFT-1's authority defect survives even under the literalist reading. Overall the alternative would need *both* premises to change.

**P-502  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (or COMPLIANT).** *Premise that must change:* ECD-2 must count as a GPIP-4 exception — either A7's issuer element is read as non-exhaustive (any senior joint operational signature suffices for a written, scoped, time-limited instrument) or C4's "takes precedence" is read as general supremacy over GPIP-4 rather than over purchasing timing/onboarding. *Credibility:* **moderate for the general supremacy claim** — the organization has prior practice treating signed directives as temporary policy overlays, and a directive co-signed by the COO carries real institutional weight. It is **weak specifically as to A3**, for three independent reasons: A7 names the CRO expressly; the available precedent is stated to exclude new-supplier compliance clearance; and D3 confirms that no officer email or directive of this class is recognized as reaching Compliance. The alternative is strongest if a CRO countersignature or later ratification were produced — that single document would flip P-502 to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.

**P-504  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** *Premise that must change:* (i) B4's "imminent safety harm" must be read to include food-spoilage/food-safety risk within six hours, **and** (ii) ECD-2's scope must be read to cover site-continuity purchases (or C6's undefined term read expansively), with C3's authorization requirement treated as non-essential. *Credibility:* (i) is **weak-to-moderate** — plausible on policy purpose, but contradicted by the numeric/subject-matter specificity of B4 and by the form's own "site continuity" label. (ii) is **very weak**: the stated scope is sterilization-line lots, and C3 is a condition precedent the actors never attempted to meet. Decisively, even the *strongest* version of this alternative leaves the B3 register breach standing, because B5 expressly says emergency does not waive the approved-supplier rule. So the best available alternative changes severity, not classification.

**P-505  NONCOMPLIANT.** *Premise that must change (two independent routes):* **Route A (timing):** the 17:59:31 acknowledgment must be read as acknowledging *receipt of an inquiry* rather than *acceptance of an order*, leaving the ERP 18:06 value (declared possibly fast, but ON-44 does not cover 07-18) as the only commitment evidence  commitment falls outside the window  C1/C2 lapse  B1 breach plus unwaived approval requirements. **Route B (authority):** D1 must be held *ultra vires* as to A2 — the CFO may interpret Finance controls but may not relieve a board-policy clause (A5/A7 structure; uncontrolled email; D4 hedge)  Finance_Approve at 19:00 is an uncured post-commitment approval. *Credibility:* Route A is **credible but evidence-dependent** — it turns entirely on the acknowledgment's wording, which is the single missing document; internal consistency (call 17:57  acknowledgment 17:59:31  ERP ~6–9 min fast) favours my reading, but not overwhelmingly. Route B is **moderately credible and is the more dangerous alternative**, because it also flips P-507; it is weakened only by D3's positive grant of Finance interpretive authority, which would be pointless if it could never substitute a control.

**P-507  NONCOMPLIANT.** *Premise that must change:* either (a) the signed PDF of 12:40 is *not* the authorization record (so C3's four-hour recording was missed, ERP 14:30 being 5h35 late), or (b) D1 is invalid as to A2 (no Finance event exists at all — a gap, not a late act), or (c) the ERP is accurate and no Create_PO/authorization sequence can be shown. *Credibility:* (a) is **moderate** and cheap to resolve — produce the PDF. (b) is the same Route B premise as P-505 and is **moderately credible**, though P-507 is *more* defensible than P-505 because nothing was ratified after the fact: every substantive control preceded the commitment.

**P-508  COMPLIANT.** *Premise that must change:* D2's "as well" must be read as incorporating D1's substitution for the >150k tier (i.e., "joint IC authorization, *plus* Legal, satisfies approvals above 150k"), rather than as an additional requirement layered on top of ordinary approvals. *Credibility:* **genuinely balanced.** The structural two-tier reading (150k: IC alone; >150k: IC + Legal) is coherent and would make P-508 exemplary. My contrary reading rests on expressio unius (an expressly bounded sufficiency clause should not be extended by implication, especially where relief from a board policy is at stake) and on D4's conservative posture. This is the single most balanced fork in the file; CFO confirmation of intent would resolve it either way.

**P-506  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** *Premise that must change:* verbal extension of a signed directive by Incident Command must be accepted as effective, with the recording obligation suspended. *Credibility:* **weak.** ECD-2 carries an explicit end timestamp; its own C3 requires recording within four hours and *no* record exists (not even late); and, fatally, there is no two-member authorization for this purchase at all, so coverage fails even on the most generous reading of the window. The alternative fails on the *authorization* axis before it reaches the *time* axis.

| **Inability to prove compliance** (neither breach nor proof) | Independent budget-owner approvals in **all** 50k cases (no discrete process activity exists; IC charter silent); B6 emergency forms for P-502, P-505, P-507, P-508 (not in the record set — may be excerpting); A1 actor identities for P-502, P-505, P-506, P-507; P-505 Create_PO; P-504 form timestamp | Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — subject to the declared burden rules |

**Note on burden asymmetry, applied consistently:** an *unproven approval* is treated as presumed unmet but is recorded here as category 4 rather than category 1, because the process model contains no budget-owner activity type at all — that is a control-design/mapping gap, not a case-level act. An *unproven prohibition* (A1) never becomes a breach. An *unproven waiver claim* **does** fail (claimant's burden), which is why P-506's verbal extension and P-503's scope label do not help their actors.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

### 7.1 Replacement precedence and emergency-authority clause (draft)

**§1 Precedence.** In descending order: (a) GPIP-4 clauses A1–A3 and A7 (*Reserved Controls*); (b) a written exception issued by the Chief Risk Officer under A7; (c) a written incident directive co-signed by the COO and Incident Commander *only* as to the matters expressly enumerated in §3; (d) site SOPs; (e) individual-officer guidance within that officer's documented control ownership. **No instrument of class (c), (d) or (e) may relieve a Reserved Control.** Silence in a subordinate instrument never relieves a superior one.

**§2 Reserved Controls (never waivable in an emergency).** Segregation of duties (requester  committer  final approver); the dual approval requirement at  EUR 50,000, both approvals **before** commitment; Compliance clearance before commitment for > EUR 100,000 or any supplier lacking a current register entry; and the requirement that a supplier's register status be assessed before commitment. The only relief is a written, scoped, time-limited CRO exception; the CRO may delegate pre-issuance authority to a named alternate in writing, published in the controlled repository.

**§3 Matters an incident directive *may* cover (closed list).** (i) sequencing of PO creation relative to commitment; (ii) use of a supplier not yet in the register, subject to onboarding within 10 business days; (iii) substitution of the plant signature by two Incident Command members who are not in the requesting or buying chain; (iv) suspension of competitive tendering. Each directive must state: start and end timestamp to the minute; a defined scope term; the amounts covered; and the required authorizers. **No verbal extension is effective.** Extension requires a written amendment co-signed as the original and logged in the controlled repository within one hour.

**§4 Defined scope term.** "Critical lot" = a part whose unavailability stops, idles, or renders non-sterile an *affected* production line within 24 hours, evidenced by a dated engineering or operations determination attached to the commitment. Backup-line and site-continuity items are excluded unless an affected line is implicated.

**§5 Approval substitution and caps.** Up to EUR 150,000 and only inside a valid directive window, joint authorization by two qualifying Incident Command members **before** commitment substitutes for Finance-side approval, provided the authorization is recorded within four hours by at least two independent media (one non-ERP). Above EUR 150,000: Finance Controller **and** Legal clearance before commitment, with no substitution. Budget-owner approval is never substituted; where a directive is in force, the standing budget owner named in the delegation schedule (not an ad-hoc actor) must approve before commitment.

**§6 Segregation in emergencies.** A person who requests, buys, or sits in the requesting/buying chain may not be one of the authorizing Incident Command members, and may never be a final approver. Incident Command members are not budget owners unless named in the delegation schedule.

**§7 Evidence and time.** The commitment time is established by the earliest reliable contemporaneous record (supplier acknowledgment, call recording, signed instrument) where ERP integrity is in doubt; ERP timestamps are rebuttable. Clock drift during an outage must be diagnosed and attached. Post-event records may evidence a timely act but may not substitute for an approval that did not occur before commitment; late approvals are recorded as *ratification*, never as *compliance* (codifying A6).

**§8 Post-event obligations.** Emergency form within 24 hours (content must cite §4 scope basis); Compliance clearance retrospective review within 24 hours where a new supplier was used, escalating to the CRO if not completed; complete control package filed within 5 business days.

**§9 Expiry and anti-normalization safeguards.** (a) Maximum validity 72 hours, **non-renewable** without a fresh CRO exception; (b) automatic lapse at the stated end timestamp — no retroactive or verbal extension, and any purchase after expiry is assessed under ordinary rules; (c) a public count of directive-covered commitments and of post-commitment ratifications reported monthly to the Audit Committee, with mandatory suspension of the emergency route if it is used for more than 10% of site purchases in any 30-day period or for three consecutive months; (d) an independent 30-day post-expiry audit of every covered purchase, with findings unsealed regardless of outcome; (e) a prohibition on declaring an emergency for purchases that fail §4; (f) any use of the emergency route to bypass a Reserved Control is a reportable integrity event, not an operational exception.

**§10 Definitions harmonization.** "New supplier" (GPIP-4 A3) = any supplier without a *current, valid* register entry on the commitment date, expressly including suppliers previously used but lapsed, suspended, or never screened — closing the present gap between A3 and SOP B3.

### Closing note on the overall posture

The eight cases do not sort into a single moral. Two (P-507, P-505) show a control path that worked — one ex ante, one by valid substitution under contemporaneous third-party time evidence — and should not be sanctioned merely because an ERP clock disagreed. Four (P-501, P-502, P-506, P-508) are sequencing/approval-timing failures of low-to-moderate gravity where necessity was real and integrity controls were not evaded; they call for ratification marked non-curative, not discipline. Two (P-503, P-504) are different in kind: P-503 collapses the requester/approver/committer distinction on the largest contested purchase with no compliance clearance at all, and P-504 asserts emergency status for an event the local definition does not cover while using an unapproved vendor. The single most consequential open question in the file — whether the COO/IC directive or the CFO email can reach GPIP-4's Reserved Controls — is answered "no" here on textual and structural grounds, but it is answerable the other way by one document: a CRO countersignature or ratification. That document does not exist, and its absence is the hinge of this assessment.
