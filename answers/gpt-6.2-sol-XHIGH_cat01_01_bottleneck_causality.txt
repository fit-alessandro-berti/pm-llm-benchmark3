## 1. Executive causal assessment

My provisional verdict is **not identifiable from the supplied evidence**. Confidence is **95/100** that these data cannot establish the direction of a routing effect for future Orion cases. Orion averages 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, yet Orion’s mean is lower in all three observed complexity strata. Applying the same pooled complexity mix gives 17.14 hours for Orion and 19.65 for Nova. The crude comparison combines markedly different assignments. High-complexity cases account for 6 of 14 Orion cases and 2 of 16 Nova cases, and night shifts account for 13 of 14 and 4 of 16, respectively.

The strongest indication of a real Orion advantage is that its cycle time is also shorter in all four observed comparisons that jointly align complexity, shift, load, and compliance requirement. Much of the difference is in time before first review. Each of those comparisons rests on a single case in one group, and the data omit both finer routing risk and cases that never reached Close. The direction is suggestive, and a harmful effect in some eligible cases or under heavier demand remains possible.

Transferring 50% could help if Nova has spare qualified capacity when the cases arrive and the transfer relieves a binding Orion queue. It could do little if both groups depend on the same constrained compliance step. It could backfire if Nova’s night coverage cannot absorb the work, cases wait for the day shift, or queues grow for Nova’s existing cases. The proposed volume change therefore needs evidence on both eligible cases and shared capacity.

## 3. Variable-role table

Define the **total assignment effect** at classification among cases eligible for both groups, for a specified staffing and routing regime. Compare time from assignment to Close under Orion and Nova with queues, reviewer selection, repeat reviews, and other downstream steps allowed to respond. Pre-routing elapsed time belongs to the recorded cycle but precedes this intervention. A **controlled direct effect** would compare assignments under the same feasible settings for specified mediators, such as initial queue access and post-review handoffs. Its meaning depends on what is held fixed. A **mediated effect** could run through queue access, active review, or information requests and return visits. Separating those pathways needs credible variation in the mechanisms and control of mediator–outcome confounding. Adjusting for the observed mediators in a regression does not supply that identification. A policy that moves half the cases also changes other cases’ queues, so its system-wide effect differs from a case-level assignment effect under a small pilot.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Establish the baseline | Leave the routing rule in place during an intake-cohort measurement period. Record eligibility, route reasons, queue state, lifecycle events, and every disposition | Coverage, overlap, availability, and timing boundaries | Every classified case, linked to its queue and shift | Verify the eligible reviewer roster and compliance rules before selecting pilot blocks | DFG and variant frequencies by risk and shift, conformance of required checks, lifecycle reconciliation, queue age, open and abandoned cases, and comparable appeal follow-up | Distinguishes lack of overlap from a queue bottleneck and defines a population where either route is operationally feasible | Hold the pilot if route eligibility, required-check execution, or intake-to-disposition linkage cannot be verified |
| 2. Routing pilot | Among eligible cases otherwise routed to Orion, randomize a small share to Nova and keep the rest on usual routing. Block by staffed shift and, where feasible, risk tier, load, and required-check status | Incremental reassignment under present capacity | Individual case within prespecified dual-eligible blocks | Use a Nova transfer cap per shift, qualified capacity in both arms, unchanged compliance, and logged safety overrides | Intention-to-treat time to Close over a common horizon, completion and abandonment, tail delays, wait by stage, review visits, rework, quality, and spillovers on existing cases | Estimates the assignment-policy effect at low transfer volume and tests whether the time difference is concentrated in particular stages | Suspend transfers immediately for a missed required check attributable to the pilot or a breach of prespecified backlog, tail-delay, or quality guardrails. Revert to usual routing |
| 3. Capacity pilot | Test protected compliance slots with eligible staff during comparable peak periods, retaining the routing rule | Delay around required checks and downstream queue displacement | Compliance queue by shift block, with randomized order and carryover accounted for | Keep every required check and review qualification in force. Monitor access for cases outside the tested blocks | Check queue-entry to start, active check time, check finish to first review, end-to-end closure, and backlog in adjacent queues | Tests whether capacity relief reduces delay independently of the resource-group assignment | Withdraw protected slots if required checks are missed or tail delays or backlog cross guardrails in any affected queue |
| 4. Test volume before scaling | If pilot results support Nova transfers, test capped increases in transfer share across comparable operating blocks | Congestion and spillovers as transferred volume rises | Queue and shift blocks large enough to contain most spillovers, with a washout or explicit carryover measurement | Expand only where both groups can cover the work. Track all traffic that shares review and compliance capacity | Case mix, arrivals versus service capacity, queue age, closure and abandonment, and decision-quality signals for transferred and existing cases | Estimates how sensitive system performance is to transfer volume. The individual pilot alone cannot establish the effect of a 50% shift | Return to the last tested safe cap if a higher share breaches guardrails or worsens system-wide tail delay |

Decline the blanket 50% reassignment. Start by measuring all cases from classification and identifying the shifts and claim types where both groups can legally and practically do the work. In those blocks, test a capped Nova transfer against usual Orion routing. Analyze cases as assigned, including overrides, and compare the probability of Close by a common horizon and mean time without Close through that horizon. Treat abandonment as a competing outcome, report it separately, and check whether Nova’s existing cases incur longer delays. Use the same period of 30-day follow-up after Close for appeals, supplemented by a blinded audit of decision quality.

If lifecycle data identify a shared compliance bottleneck, test protected check capacity with the current routing rule. Continue a transfer only if it reduces time to Close without breaching the agreed guardrails for compliance, decision quality, and delays across both groups. Test higher volumes in stages before committing to them. Keep cases outside the pilot’s eligibility or staffing coverage on their usual route until there is evidence for those operating conditions.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual case mix.** The recorded bands may hide meaningful differences in risk within a routing cell. Retrieve the routing engine’s prerouting case-risk score.
2. **Capacity at transfer volumes.** A small pilot may miss congestion and spillovers that emerge as more cases move. Randomize the transfer share across eligible queue and shift blocks.
3. **Source of pre-review delay.** The combined wait field obscures time in dispatch, compliance, and handoffs. Instrument stage-level lifecycle logging for queue entry, start, and finish.
4. **Selection into the extract.** Differential closure, abandonment, or censoring can change the group contrast. Use a classification-time inception cohort that follows every eligible case to a common horizon.
5. **Decision quality.** Appeals provide a sparse signal affected by events after closure. Use a blinded audit of claim-decision accuracy.
