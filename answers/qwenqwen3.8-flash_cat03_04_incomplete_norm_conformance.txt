## 1. Conformance semantics

I interpret the observed object-centric summary as a set of diagnostics, not as a final legal verdict. The normative model is deliberately incomplete, so classification depends on whether a relation is explicitly governed, whether the observed abstraction matches the normative scope, and whether the graph edge is likely true business behavior or a data artifact.

- **Forbidden behavior**: A relation explicitly marked `forbidden` is observed, and the observation is accepted as a valid object-centric trace. The norm is closed-world for forbidden relations: if the edge is real, it is noncompliant. Example: `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` for 2 Orders under **N3**.

- **Missing mandatory behavior**: A relation explicitly marked `mandatory` is absent within its stated scope, after checking that the object or event is in scope and that the abstraction can actually detect the relation. Example: missing `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` for 2 externally shipped Packages under **N4** may be missing mandatory behavior, but only if the two packages are truly externally shipped and no intermediate modeling abstraction explains the gap.

- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: An observed relation is not covered by the norm. Because the model is open-world except for forbidden relations and mandatory columns, presence alone is not a violation. Example: `Reopen Order -> Close Order` for 1 Order under **N6** is unspecified.

- **Typical-pattern absence**: A `typical` relation is not observed. This is not automatically noncompliance. Example: `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` occurs for 92 of 110 Invoices under **N5**; the 18 non-payment outcomes may be credit notes, write-offs, open credit, or data-quality issues, not violations by themselves.

- **Likely data-quality error**: The graph shows an edge or missing edge that conflicts with known source-system behavior, identifier reuse, or event labeling. The correct conclusion is “repair the data,” not “the business process violated the norm.” Example: `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` for 1 event under **E10** is suspected to be a join artifact.

- **Abstraction mismatch**: The observed summary uses a proxy that is narrower or broader than the normative rule. Example: **N1** says Orders should *eventually* enter validation, but the OC-DFG summary uses a directly-follows count. A missing direct edge may still be conforming if validation occurs after an unrecorded or intermediate event.

- **Insufficient evidence**: The summary lacks event-level identity, timestamps, object scope flags, or labels needed to decide. Example: the two Orders lacking direct `Create Order -> Validate Order` may have had an unrecorded review event, so we cannot confidently classify them as missing mandatory behavior.

**Open-world versus closed-world effect.** For `forbidden` relations, absence of the edge is good and presence is bad, assuming the edge is real. For `mandatory` relations, absence within scope is bad, but only if the scope and abstraction are correct. For `typical` and `unspecified` relations, the norm is open-world: absence is not automatically violation, and presence is not automatically compliance. Therefore, every finding needs a semantic overlay: business behavior, data quality, and model incompleteness must be separated before assigning severity.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

The OC-DFG family can establish observed directly-follows relations on object lifecycles: for example, 98 of 100 Orders show `Create Order -> Validate Order`, 2 Orders show forbidden `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, and 198 of 200 externally shipped Packages show `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`. It cannot, by itself, prove that an event never occurred, that a mandatory eventuality was missed, or that a direct edge is the only valid path.

Counts and scope matter. **N1** is mandatory but semantically eventual: every newly created standard Order should eventually enter validation. The summary approximates this with a direct-follows count. The two missing direct edges are therefore not confirmed violations. They may be abstraction mismatches if validation occurred after an unrecorded review or intermediate event. Similarly, **N4** is mandatory for externally shipped Packages, but if the OC-DFG abstraction is too strict, a package could have been packed, inspected, staged, then dispatched; the missing direct edge may reflect abstraction, not process failure.

The danger of interpreting an “eventually expected” business rule as a directly-follows rule is central here. If we treat **N1** as “Create Order must immediately be followed by Validate Order,” we would mark 2 Orders as noncompliant. But the norm says “eventually enter validation,” and the additional context explicitly says the two Orders may have an unrecorded review event. A direct-follows diagnostic is therefore only a candidate signal. It becomes a confirmed missing mandatory behavior only after event-level traces show no validation at any later point for in-scope standard Orders.

Other OC-DFG observations are more benign. `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` for 72 Orders is not a violation; **N2** acknowledges intermediate invoice, review, or fulfilment events. `Reopen Order -> Close Order` for 1 Order is unspecified under **N6**. `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` for 4 Invoices and `Issue Invoice` with no successor for 14 Invoices are typical exceptions or open-credit patterns under **N5**, not automatic noncompliance.

### OTG

The OTG family can establish object-to-object relations such as `Order object_interaction Item`, `Package object_interaction Delivery`, `Invoice object_interaction Payment`, `Order object_cobirth Payment`, and `Package object_codeath Delivery`. It cannot establish event ordering, event-level mandatory links, or whether an edge represents one event, many events, or a unique object pair.

The most important OTG finding is the forbidden `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` edge with weight 1 under **T6**. If the cobirth derivation is correct, this is a clear violation because Orders and Payments cannot be born in the same business event. However, cobirth can be sensitive to event definitions and identifier joins, so the edge needs verification. The mandatory OTG edge `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` has weight 200, which appears to satisfy **T2** at the object-pair level. But the ET–OT summary shows only 198 `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` links, and the additional context says two Dispatch events lack a Delivery relation despite carrier attributes. This illustrates abstraction mismatch: OTG may show a package-delivery relationship somewhere, while ET–OT shows missing event-level links.

Typical and unspecified OTG edges should not be overinterpreted. `(Invoice, object_interaction, Payment)` with weight 92 is typical under **T3**, and unapplied cash can exist. `(Order, object_interaction, Customer)` with weight 100 and `(Order, object_interaction, RiskCase)` with weight 3 are unspecified under **T4** and **T5**. `Package object_codeath Delivery` with weight 7 is outside the norm; the norm says nothing about codeath, so it is not a violation solely because it is absent from the normative edge set.

### ET–OT

The ET–OT family can establish whether an event type relates to a required object type. It is the best family for checking event-level mandatory links such as **E1**, **E4**, **E5**, **E6**, **E7**, and **E8**. It cannot establish object-to-object relationships, sequence, or business intent.

The strongest ET–OT violation candidate is `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` with weight 1 under **E10**. The norm explicitly forbids linking sensor streams to financial objects. But the additional context says this edge was produced by a join on a reused numeric identifier and is suspected to be a data-quality error. Therefore the correct classification is not “confirmed forbidden behavior” but “forbidden if true; likely data-quality error.” This distinction matters because the immediate remedy is data lineage repair, not process containment.

Other ET–OT findings are mandatory-gap candidates. `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` is mandatory for every Dispatch Package event under **E6**, but only 198 of 200 events have the link. The carrier attribute suggests the missing links may be data-quality or modeling issues. `Record Payment -> Invoice` is mandatory except for explicitly typed unapplied cash under **E8**, but none are typed as such; the missing Invoice link for 1 of 93 payments is therefore a mandatory gap unless the label is missing. `Add Item -> Order` has weight 1448 while `Add Item -> Item` has 1450 under **E2** and **E3**, suggesting two possible missing Order links. But if weights count unique object pairs rather than event occurrences, this may be an abstraction mismatch.

A relation can be conforming at one abstraction and suspicious at another. `Invoice -> Payment` at OTG is typical and can legitimately be absent for unapplied cash. But `Record Payment -> Invoice` at ET–OT is mandatory unless the event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash. Thus, the same business situation can look acceptable in one model family and noncompliant in another depending on event typing and abstraction.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **Eventual versus direct validation under N1**  
   The norm says Orders should eventually enter validation, but the summary uses direct-follows. Operational risk: false positives for missing validation and false negatives if validation occurs much later. Clarification: define whether validation is mandatory within a time window and specify allowed intermediate paths. Proposed status: **mandatory** for eventual validation; direct path should be **unspecified**.

2. **Undefined “standard Order” scope under N1**  
   The norm applies to “standard Orders,” but the summary says “newly created Orders.” Operational risk: nonstandard orders may be incorrectly judged or real gaps may be missed. Clarification: enumerate order types covered by mandatory validation. Proposed status: **mandatory** only for explicitly defined standard Orders.

3. **Ambiguous “packages intended for external shipment” under N4**  
   The summary says 198 of 200 externally shipped Packages have pack-to-dispatch, but the scope of “intended for external shipment” may depend on attributes, not event sequence. Operational risk: returns, internal moves, or split shipments may be misclassified. Clarification: require an external-shipment flag or event attribute. Proposed status: **mandatory** for flagged externally shipped Packages.

4. **Event-level versus object-pair mandatory links under T2/E6**  
   OTG shows 200 Package-Delivery interactions, while ET–OT shows 198 Dispatch-Delivery links. Operational risk: missing event-object links may be hidden by aggregate object relationships. Clarification: state whether the mandatory link must exist for every Dispatch Package event or only for the Package object lifecycle. Proposed status: **mandatory** per Dispatch Package event.

5. **Unapplied cash exception under E8/T3**  
   E8 requires Record Payment to link to Invoice except when explicitly typed as unapplied cash, but none are so typed. Operational risk: legitimate unapplied cash appears as a violation, or missing labels hide reconciliation issues. Clarification: require an explicit event type or attribute for unapplied cash. Proposed status: **mandatory** to link Invoice unless explicitly flagged; the flag itself should be **mandatory** for unapplied cash events.

6. **Forbidden cobirth under T6 lacks operational explanation**  
   Orders and Payments cannot be born in the same business event, but the norm does not explain whether prepaid orders, deposits, or payment-on-order flows are legitimate. Operational risk: important financial flows may be blocked or falsely flagged. Clarification: define allowed payment creation patterns and explicitly forbid only the unsupported cobirth mechanism. Proposed status: **forbidden** for the current system unless a future exception is specified.

7. **Data-quality handling for forbidden edges is missing**  
   E10 is forbidden, but the observed Sensor Heartbeat-Invoice edge is suspected to be a join artifact. Operational risk: data-quality errors are escalated as business violations. Clarification: add a rule that forbidden edges must be validated against identifier lineage before conformance reporting. Proposed status: **mandatory** for data-quality validation of forbidden-edge candidates.

8. **Codeath, customer, and risk-case relations are unregulated**  
   Package codeath Delivery, Order-Customer, Order-RiskCase, and Manual Compliance Review-Customer are observed but not governed. Operational risk: blind spots for privacy, risk, and lifecycle alignment. Clarification: decide whether these relations are intentionally outside the norm or need governance. Proposed status: **unspecified** if intentionally outside; **typical** if desirable but not required; **forbidden** only if a specific policy prohibits them.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

**Immediate containment**

- Prioritize **N3**: the 2 Orders with `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`. Stop any further dispatch for cancelled Orders, quarantine the associated Packages, and determine whether recall or customer remediation is needed.
- Prioritize **T6**: the 1 forbidden `Order object_cobirth Payment`. Freeze or hold the related payment until the creation event is verified, because this could indicate an unauthorized or system-prohibited financial event.
- Do not escalate benign rare behavior such as `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, `Package object_codeath Delivery`, `Order -> Customer`, or `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` solely because it is rare or unmodeled.

**Data repair**

- Repair the suspected `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` join. Quarantine the edge, correct identifier mapping, and rerun ET–OT discovery.
- Link the two Dispatch Package events with carrier references to proper Delivery objects under **E6/T2**.
- Add or verify an unapplied-cash label for the one Record Payment event lacking Invoice under **E8/T3**.
- Investigate the two apparent Add Item Order-link discrepancies under **E2/T1** to determine whether they are missing links or unique-pair abstraction artifacts.
- Backfill or verify any unrecorded validation or review events affecting the two Orders missing direct `Create Order -> Validate Order` under **N1**.

**Process investigation**

- For the two cancelled Orders with dispatch, investigate whether cancellation occurred after picking, whether packages were reassigned, or whether dispatch controls failed.
- For the two Packages missing `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package`, inspect warehouse records to determine whether packing occurred but was not logged, whether the package was not externally shipped, or whether an intermediate event breaks the direct-follows abstraction.
- For the 14 Invoices with no successor and the 4 write-offs, review aging, open credit, and write-off approvals under **N5**.
- For the 3 Orders with `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`, check whether they eventually enter validation; do not assume violation from the direct-follows count alone.

**Norm revision**

- Replace or supplement **N1** with an eventual validation rule plus a time window and allowed intermediate paths.
- Define “standard Order” and “externally shipped Package” scopes for **N1** and **N4**.
- Specify whether mandatory object links under **T2/E6** are event-level or object-pair-level.
- Define explicit event typing for unapplied cash under **E8**.
- Add data-quality preconditions before treating forbidden edges such as **E10** as business violations.
- Decide whether codeath, customer, and risk-case relations should remain **unspecified** or be moved into **typical** or **mandatory** governance.

**Monitoring**

- Real-time alerts for forbidden relations: **N3**, **T6**, and confirmed **E10**.
- Coverage metrics for mandatory relations: validation eventualities, pack-to-dispatch, dispatch-to-delivery, payment-to-invoice, and Add Item links.
- Data-quality metrics: orphan event-object links, reused identifier joins, missing exception labels, and direct-follows versus eventual-follows discrepancies.
- Operational aging metrics for typical patterns: unpaid Invoices, write-offs, open credit, and delayed validation.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

The robust conclusions are that the two `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` cases are the clearest confirmed forbidden behavior under **N3**, and that the forbidden `Order object_cobirth Payment` edge under **T6** requires urgent verification because, if true, it is a direct norm violation. The `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` edge under **E10** is high-risk only if real; the available context strongly suggests a data-quality join error, so it should not be escalated as business noncompliance without lineage review.

Several conclusions depend on norm incompleteness: whether missing direct validation under **N1** is a violation, whether missing pack-to-dispatch under **N4** is a scope or abstraction issue, whether missing Delivery links under **T2/E6** are event-level gaps, and whether missing Invoice links under **E8** are legitimate unapplied cash or label omissions. Other conclusions depend on suspected data-quality issues, especially the sensor join, carrier-reference links, unapplied-cash labels, and Add Item link counts.

From graph summaries alone, we cannot decide whether missing edges represent absent events, unrecorded events, incorrect event typing, unique-pair versus occurrence counting, object misassignment, or legitimate exceptions. Therefore, the correct posture is to treat forbidden edges as high-priority containment candidates, mandatory gaps as investigation candidates, typical absences as operational monitoring items, and unregulated rare patterns as benign unless another policy or evidence changes their classification.
