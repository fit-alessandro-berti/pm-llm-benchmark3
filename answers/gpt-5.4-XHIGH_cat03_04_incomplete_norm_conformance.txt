## 1. Conformance semantics
Forbidden behavior means an observed in-scope relation matches a norm edge explicitly marked forbidden. Because the norm is closed-world for forbidden edges, a real occurrence is nonconforming even if it is rare. Here that applies most directly to N3, T6, and E10, subject to checking whether the observation itself is genuine.

Missing mandatory behavior means an in-scope relation or link required by the norm is absent. Mandatory rules are also closed-world within scope, but scope and representation matter: a missing ET-OT link is stronger evidence than a missing OC-DFG directly-follows edge, because OC-DFG may miss legitimate intermediate steps.

Unexpected but unregulated behavior means a relation is observed but the norm does not govern it. Under the norm’s open-world reading, absence from the normative edge set is not a violation. `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, `Order -> Customer`, and `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` fall into this category unless another policy says otherwise.

Typical-pattern absence is weaker than a violation. Typical edges describe common, desirable paths, but legitimate exceptions are already acknowledged by the norm. Therefore N2, N5, and T3 support monitoring and interpretation, not enforcement.

Likely data-quality error is the right class when there is concrete evidence that an observed forbidden or missing relation may be produced by extraction, joining, or identifier problems rather than real business behavior. The suspected reused-identifier join behind `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` is the clearest example.

Abstraction mismatch arises when the summary model is stricter than the business rule. N1 explicitly says validation is an eventual expectation, approximated by directly-follows. Therefore `98 of 100` on `Create Order -> Validate Order` is not automatically two business violations.

Insufficient evidence applies when graph summaries lack the labels, timestamps, case-level traces, or exception markers needed to decide. The unlabeled `Record Payment` without `Invoice`, and the 14 invoices with no observed successor, cannot be settled from summary counts alone.

Open-world versus closed-world is the key boundary: forbidden edges are violations if real; mandatory edges are potential violations if missing and in scope; typical edges are only soft signals; unspecified areas stay unregulated unless another norm is brought in.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family
### OC-DFG
OC-DFG can establish which directly-follows relations are observed for each object type and how often they recur. That makes it useful for spotting hard contradictions such as `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` (N3), and for checking whether common lifecycle steps appear broadly consistent with the norm. It also provides case-count context: `98 of 100` on N1 and `198 of 200` on N4 show that any issue is concentrated in a small subset, not necessarily systemic.

What OC-DFG cannot establish is just as important. It cannot prove eventual reachability when an intermediate event exists, cannot show whether a missing edge is caused by late-arriving events, and cannot distinguish a true skipped activity from a directly-follows abstraction error. That danger is explicit in N1: the business rule is “should eventually enter validation,” but the summary uses a directly-follows approximation. Therefore the two Orders without observed `Create Order -> Validate Order` are not automatically two violations. The same caution applies to N4 and to the typical edges N2 and N5: an invoice, review, fulfilment, relabel, or customs step can legitimately sit between the two observed labels.

OC-DFG is also open-world except for forbidden and mandatory-in-scope edges. So `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review`, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` are not violations merely because they are absent from the normative edge set. A relation may be conforming at the business level yet look suspicious in OC-DFG, or vice versa. For example, an Order may eventually validate and still miss the direct-follow edge; conversely, a direct-follow edge can exist while ET-OT links underneath are incomplete.

### OTG
OTG establishes whether object types are connected in the object graph and with what aggregate weight. It is strong for structural expectations such as T2 `(Package, object_interaction, Delivery)` and T6 `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)`. The single `Order`-`Payment` cobirth is therefore serious: if the edge is real, it violates a closed-world forbidden structural invariant.

But OTG cannot establish event sequence, causal direction, or per-event completeness. A graph-level `Package`-`Delivery` interaction weight of 200 looks healthy, yet ET-OT still shows two `Dispatch Package` events without `Delivery`, meaning graph-level presence can hide event-level failures. Likewise, the OTG `Invoice`-`Payment` interaction weight of 92 cannot tell whether the missing payment-side invoice relation is a legitimate unapplied-cash exception, a labeling problem, or a broken join.

Scope also matters. T4 and T5 are explicitly unspecified, so `(Order, interaction, Customer)` and `(Order, interaction, RiskCase)` are not nonconformances. The seven `Package`-`Delivery` codeath edges are also unregulated because the norm says nothing about codeath. OTG therefore supports structural anomaly detection, but not legal or business judgment by itself.

### ET–OT
ET-OT establishes whether a given event type is linked to the object types the norm expects. It is the strongest model family here for mandatory link completeness. The two missing `Add Item -> Order` links directly challenge E2; the two missing `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` links directly challenge E6; the one `Record Payment` linked to `Payment` but not `Invoice` directly challenges E8; and `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` directly conflicts with E10.

What ET-OT cannot establish is process order or exception legitimacy without extra labels. It cannot tell whether the missing invoice link on `Record Payment` is actually allowed unapplied cash, because the snapshot does not label it as such. It also cannot tell whether a complete event-object assignment still participates in forbidden order-level behavior such as N3; a `Dispatch Package` event can satisfy E5 and E6 while still being attached to a cancelled Order.

Counts must therefore be read with scope and exception handling in mind. ET-OT gaps are stronger than OC-DFG gaps because they are not merely about directly-follows abstraction, but they are still not self-interpreting. A relation can look conforming at ET-OT and suspicious at OTG or OC-DFG, and the reverse is also true. The central pitfall across all three families is turning an eventual business obligation into a direct-follow obligation; that overstates nonconformance for N1 and can also distort reading of N4, N2, and N5.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior
Mandatory:
- `Create Order -> Validate Order` at `98/100` under N1 is a mandatory-gap candidate only if direct-follow is intended literally; if eventual validation with an intervening review is acceptable, it becomes abstraction mismatch.
- `Pack Package -> Dispatch Package` at `198/200` under N4 is a mandatory-gap candidate unless the two packages had legitimate intermediate package events and the rule is only eventual precedence.
- `Dispatch Package -> Delivery` missing on 2 events, `Add Item -> Order` missing on 2 events, and `Record Payment -> Invoice` missing on 1 event are mandatory-link gaps unless an explicit exception label exists.

Forbidden:
- `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` for 2 Orders is forbidden under N3 if the Order association and timestamps are correct.
- `(Order, object_cobirth, Payment)` weight 1 is forbidden under T6 if the single birth event is real rather than a batching artifact.
- `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` is forbidden under E10 if the link is genuine; with the suspected reused-ID join bug, it is better treated as a likely data-quality error first.

Typical:
- `Validate Order -> Close Order` observed for 83 Orders is a typical pattern under N2; the remainder are not violations unless another rule makes closure mandatory within a horizon.
- Invoice outcomes under N5/T3: `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` for 92/110 is typical, `Issue Invoice -> Write Off` for 4 is a legitimate exception, and 14 with no observed successor remain typical-pattern absence or insufficient evidence unless aging rules say otherwise.

Unregulated:
- `Validate -> Issue Invoice` 72, `Create -> Manual Compliance Review` 3, `Reopen -> Close` 1, `Order -> Customer` 100, `Order -> RiskCase` 3, `Package codeath Delivery` 7, `Manual Compliance Review -> RiskCase` 3, and `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` 3 are open-world observations, not violations.
- Any of these would change class only if another policy later marks them mandatory, forbidden, or scoped-typical.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment
1. N1 mixes an eventual business rule with a directly-follows proxy. Risk: false positives on perfectly valid orders with an intermediate review step. Clarification: state that validation is mandatory eventually within the order lifecycle, and that direct-follow is only a monitoring heuristic. Status: mandatory.

2. N4 does not say whether packing must be the immediate predecessor of dispatch or merely precede it. Risk: the two `198/200` exceptions may be over-escalated if label-printing or customs events legitimately intervene. Clarification: require `Pack Package` before `Dispatch Package`, not necessarily directly before, unless the process truly forbids intermediate package events. Status: mandatory.

3. “Standard Order” is undefined. Risk: special orders, test orders, or internal transfers may be counted against N1-N3 incorrectly. Clarification: define the order subtypes in scope and require explicit flags for excluded classes. Status: mandatory.

4. The Delivery-object policy is ambiguous when a dispatch has a carrier reference attribute but no `Delivery` object. Risk: the two missing E6/T2 links remain disputable and traceability can silently degrade. Clarification: either mandate a `Delivery` object for every dispatch or define a narrow explicit exception where carrier-reference-only is acceptable. Status: mandatory.

5. The unapplied-cash exception is conceptually acknowledged in N5/T3/E8 but not operationalized. Risk: finance exceptions cannot be distinguished from broken links. Clarification: require explicit labeling of unapplied-cash payments and tie E8’s exception to that label. Status: mandatory.

6. No observation-horizon rule is given for “no observed successor” invoices. Risk: the 14 open invoices may be mislabeled as anomalous simply because the snapshot ends too early. Clarification: define an aging window before typical-pattern absence is escalated. Status: typical.

7. `Manual Compliance Review` semantics are incomplete: its temporal placement relative to validation and its `Customer` linkage are not defined. Risk: benign risk reviews are repeatedly misread as deviations. Clarification: either declare these relations explicitly unspecified because another policy owns them, or document them as allowed/typical variants. Status: explicitly unspecified.

8. Codeath semantics are absent. Risk: analysts may interpret the seven `Package`-`Delivery` codeath edges as suspicious although they arise from synchronized completion events. Clarification: mark codeath between these types as explicitly unspecified, or typical if the shared confirmation event is a designed pattern. Status: explicitly unspecified.

9. The norm says nothing about escalation thresholds versus case-level severity. Risk: rare but critical forbidden behavior may be underplayed, while rare benign behavior may be overplayed. Clarification: define that forbidden edges trigger case investigation immediately, mandatory gaps trigger targeted review, and typical deviations are trend-monitored. Status: mandatory.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan
Immediate containment:
- Priority 1: stop or hard-block dispatch release for any Order already in cancelled state, then reconstruct the 2 N3 cases first.
- Priority 2: isolate the single T6 `Order`-`Payment` cobirth event from automated posting and reconciliation until its source transaction is understood.
- Suppress the E10 `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` linkage from compliance dashboards until the suspected join defect is resolved.

Data repair:
- Create or restore `Delivery` objects for the 2 dispatches missing E6/T2 links, or formally annotate them as approved exceptions if that policy is adopted.
- Repair the 2 missing `Add Item -> Order` links and resolve the 1 `Record Payment` event that lacks `Invoice`, including unapplied-cash relabeling if applicable.
- Rebuild the identifier-matching logic that produced the `Sensor Heartbeat -> Invoice` link and add namespace checks to prevent reused numeric keys from colliding.

Process investigation:
- Perform case-level replay for the 2 cancelled-yet-dispatched orders and the 1 order-payment cobirth event; these are the highest business-risk findings.
- Inspect the 2 `Create -> Validate` and 2 `Pack -> Dispatch` exceptions with full traces before calling them control failures; they may be abstraction or logging artifacts.
- Age the 14 invoices with no observed successor against credit terms before escalating them.

Norm revision:
- Clarify eventual-versus-direct-follow semantics for N1 and N4.
- Define scope flags for “standard Order,” Delivery-object requirements, and unapplied-cash labeling.
- Mark review/customer relations and package-delivery codeath as explicitly unspecified if they remain outside this norm’s governance.

Monitoring:
- Maintain separate dashboards for forbidden behavior, mandatory-link gaps, typical deviations, open-world observations, and suspected data-quality errors.
- Alert immediately on N3, T6, and E10-class findings; review E2/E6/E8 gaps daily or weekly; trend N2/N5/T3 without counting them as violations.
- Track whether rare mandatory-gap candidates recur in the same system component; recurrence matters more than their current small counts.

## 8. Uncertainty statement
Robust conclusions are limited to the clearly forbidden or clearly missing mandatory patterns if the observed links are genuine: N3 `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`, T6 `Order`-`Payment` cobirth, E6/T2 delivery-link gaps, E2 add-item order-link gaps, and the E8 payment-invoice gap. Conclusions about N1 and N4 depend heavily on the norm’s incompleteness around eventual versus directly-follows semantics. Conclusions about E10, and possibly some mandatory-link gaps, depend on suspected data-quality issues and missing exception labels. What cannot be decided from graph summaries alone is whether the two N1 cases and two N4 cases eventually complied, whether the 14 invoices are legitimately still open, and whether unguided relations such as `Manual Compliance Review -> Customer` are acceptable under other governing policies.
