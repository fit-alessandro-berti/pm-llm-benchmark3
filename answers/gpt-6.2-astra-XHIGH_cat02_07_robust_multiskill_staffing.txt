# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Decision.** Use the hybrid rule for triage if P1 protection, the 24-hour P3 commitment and the upper tail of elapsed time all matter. Pair it with a staffing escalation. Under the service model below, even a perfectly efficient roster has less daily capacity than the mean base workload. Routing can change which cases miss their deadlines. It cannot make this roster sustainable at 90% planned utilization.

**Demand and clocks.** The supplied counts are expectations. For the estimates, assume independent Poisson counts by block, uniform arrival minutes within a block, and independent draws of priority and type conditional on block. Assume the diagnostic type is known at Intake. For U1/U5, add eight *percentage points* to P1 and remove that share proportionally from P2 and P3. For U2, keep the arrival count distribution fixed and double the weights of E-only and ES before normalizing all five weights. The clock runs from arrival to end of Validation through breaks, outages and overnight. The deadlines are 120, 480 and 1,440 minutes.

**Service and calendars.** Choose the ordinary or tail interval with the indicated probability, then draw uniformly within it. Draw independently by activity, including the two branches, since correlations are unspecified. Multiply the draw by the assigned worker's time multiplier. The simulator rounds active durations up to whole minutes. The load calculation uses unrounded means. A worker performs one operation at a time. The experiment starts empty at 08:00, admits one day's arrivals, then drains them to completion on repeating normal shifts with no new arrivals. Absence and outage end on the shock day. This isolates one day's shock and measures spillover without censoring slow cases. Recovery with fresh arrivals can be considerably slower.

**Transition assumptions.** A transfer costs 7 elapsed minutes per changed dependency edge. For the extra 5 minutes, assume Ada and Ben have a mechanical home team, Chen, Deepa and Emil an electrical/software home team, and Farah is a neutral floater. Team membership is unspecified and merits sensitivity testing. A neutral transfer still costs 7. Elapsed transfers can overlap waiting and each other. At a fork/join, use the longest applicable incoming transfer after the stage becomes ready. Transfers and the 8-minute parallel join use no staff in this model. Diagnostic setup occupies the receiving worker for 5 minutes when their previous diagnostic case used a different family. Intervening Intake or Validation does not erase that history. Same-case changes incur no setup. A P1 interruption adds 10% of the *current operation's remaining active minutes*, rounded up, to the victim. The victim resumes on the same worker and can be interrupted by priority only once across both branches combined.

**Choices.** Assign a ready stage to qualified staff, choose sequential or parallel execution at diagnostic dispatch, decide whom to reserve and when to interrupt. Sequential ME/ES follows the listed branch order on one dual-skilled worker. Parallel dispatch acquires two distinct workers together and Validation waits for both finishes plus coordination. Calendar closures pause assigned work on its owner without a priority restart charge. An active Intake is held during the IT outage. Planned overtime is zero in all three comparisons. This is an admissible common baseline under the 30-minute limit.

**Two objectives.** An efficiency objective minimizes base mean arrival-to-completion time. For the robust decision, let $a_{ps}$ be the fraction of priority $p$ on time in scenario $s$, $d_{ps}=\max(0,0.90-a_{ps})$, and $Q_s$ the all-case 90th percentile of elapsed minutes. Let $M_B$ and $G_B$ be the mean daily peak utilization and the mean daily maximum-minus-minimum utilization gap in Base, in fractions. Compare the six environments by minimizing

$$
\max_{s\in\{B,U1,\ldots,U5\}}
\left(4d_{1s}+d_{2s}+d_{3s}+0.25Q_s/1440\right)
+0.5\max(0,M_B-0.90)+0.25G_B .
$$

The weights are decision assumptions. A percentage point of P1 shortfall costs four times a point of P2 or P3 shortfall. The maximum avoids guessing scenario probabilities. For actual staffing approval, screen each individual's **planned** load against 90% in Base and enforce the overtime cap. The score ranks ways to manage a shortage when the capacity screen fails. A low score does not certify adequate staffing.

# 3. Candidate policies

The roster and all three dispatchers use only revealed arrivals, current queues, calendars and base mean durations. Shock-day service draws are hidden. At each minute, scan cases in dispatch order and skip a case if it has no admissible route. A transfer is eligible once its delay from stage readiness has elapsed. Workers already assigned remain on their operation across calendar breaks. Stable ties use arrival, case ID and staff initials as specified in the code.

**Fixed specialization.** Use one standing queue for each skill and the time-dependent ownership below. The letters denote Ada, Ben, Chen, Deepa, Emil and Farah. A queue follows its rostered owner when the block changes. An operation already started keeps its owner. Among eligible cases choose P1, then P2, then P3, oldest arrival first within class. There is no discretionary overflow, P1 holdback or priority interruption. This supplies a predictable handoff plan and protects uninterrupted work.

| Block | I | M | E | S | V |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 08–10 | F | A | C | C | B |
| 10–12 | F | A | D | C | B |
| 12–16 | A | B | D | E | C |
| 16–18 | D | D | D | E | E |
| 18–20 | E | closed | E | E | E |

If Chen is absent, the predeclared cover is Ada for Chen's E, Farah for Chen's morning S and Ben for Chen's V. A break holds the corresponding queue. Two different diagnostic owners must both be available to launch a pair. A common owner performs the pair sequentially, preserving continuity.

**Full pool.** Keep the same priority/FIFO scan, open all qualified routes, and reserve nobody. Among immediately admissible routes, choose the smallest predicted calendar time to finish the stage, including setup, shift gaps and parallel coordination. Break ties on fewer changed-resource edges, then deterministic route order. Thus a returning owner can start before an incoming worker has cleared the handover lag. If a P1 diagnostic has no free feasible route and its estimated slack is negative, it can interrupt one lower-priority active diagnostic for a single-worker route. Slack is elapsed time until deadline less estimated remaining service. Use mean Validation, mean Intake if still at Intake, and the largest applicable branch mean if diagnostics remain. This deliberately simple slack forecast is optimistic for serial pairs. No policy interrupts setup, Intake or Validation.

**Hybrid.** Give P1 first access. Within P1 and within the remaining group, choose least estimated slack first. For dispatch order alone, age P3 by a virtual due date of arrival plus 600 minutes. Its contractual deadline remains 1,440. Use Ada/Ben as primary M, Chen/Deepa as primary E and Chen/Emil as primary S. Open a non-primary diagnostic route when the case is P1, its stage has waited 15 minutes, its real slack is at most 40 minutes, or the primary skill has no staff on duty. I and V can use any qualified worker.

With more than one worker on duty, reserve one free qualified responder while a known P1 is in or awaiting Intake or waiting for diagnostics. Use the oldest such P1 and select its fastest free responder for S, otherwise E, otherwise M, according to the skills it needs. Let Intake use the reserve. Release it for another class once that case's stage wait reaches 40 minutes or real slack falls to 30 minutes. Recompute the reserve each minute. A P1 diagnostic with slack below 30 minutes may interrupt under the same single-worker, no-free-route and once-per-victim rules as the pool. The reserve is a contingent hold on a named person, with a reason and an expiry condition.

Among feasible routes, minimize predicted stage duration plus 0.30 times expected active worker-minutes, 3 minutes per changed dependency edge, and a fairness charge. The charge is zero up to 75% of the worker's full-day net capacity already used, rises linearly to 15 minutes at 90%, and continues linearly above that. Use the largest charge among workers on a paired route. Free routes take precedence over interrupting ones. Operators can inspect the individual time, effort, transfer and fairness contributions. These are tunable policy parameters.

**Why the branch choice matters.** At the mean inputs, Deepa can retain an ME case for about $1.25(26.30)+32.82=65.7$ active minutes. With Ben on M and Chen on E, the plug-in parallel critical path is $\max(26.30,32.82)+8=40.8$ minutes once both are ready. The pair also occupies two people at once and can create another transfer at Validation. If waiting to assemble the pair or its additional handovers consume 25 minutes, the nominal gain is gone. For ES, Chen alone needs about 80.9 minutes versus a plug-in 46.5 on Chen and Emil in parallel. These are dispatch comparisons for fixed mean inputs. A stochastic parallel finish depends on the expected *maximum* of branch times. The simulation takes the maximum of the two realized finish times.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Choose Hybrid with a capacity escalation.** Rounded robust losses are 2.44 for Hybrid, 2.61 for Pool and 4.49 for Fixed. U5 sets the maximum for all three. The ranking rests on a material trade-off. In U5, Hybrid cuts p90 by 132 minutes and raises P3 attainment by 29.2 percentage points relative to Pool, while losing 11.2 points on P2. P1 attainment is close and its sampling intervals overlap. There is little basis for claiming a P1 advantage under combined stress. Full pooling wins on mean time, fraction carried past close and P2 in every environment. An operator whose primary objective is base mean delay would choose Pool. Increasing the P2 shortfall weight from 1 to 3 also favors Pool under the stated robust score. Fixed gives the lowest setup burden and an easy-to-operate roster, but its unused capacity and extra handovers are costly at these loads.

**Staff the shortfall explicitly.** With all existing shifts planned to 90%, the base lower bound of 479.6 missing productive minutes corresponds to at least 533 extra net roster minutes per day if relief is also planned to 90%. Actual skill, time of day, transfers and variability can raise this requirement. At 90% the analogous U5 gap is about 1,606 productive minutes. Prioritize testing E/S cross-cover for a morning absence, a second finisher qualified for Validation from 16:00 onward, and M coverage after 18:00. Combining roles is possible if the timing works. Adding only early mechanical hours cannot serve a newly arrived evening M case. The estimates use only the six named staff. The timing and skills of any relief need a separate capacity test.

**Operating thresholds for a pilot.** At shift planning and every 15 minutes, project the next two hours' work by skill, including remaining work, expected new arrivals, both branches and due Validations. Allocate it to named qualified people on their actual calendars, counting each minute once. Activate the lead's capacity-escalation mode immediately if the base plan requires any individual above 90% for the shift, a P1's predicted Validation finish exceeds its deadline even on the fastest available route, or a required skill has no viable start before its case's latest safe start. Request qualified relief or an authorized transfer of work to a staffed service, with the original SLA clock retained. Keep Hybrid P1 reservation and aging in effect. Record the size, skill and timing of any uncovered deficit and flag affected cases to the duty lead. Overtime, if locally approved, is limited to 30 minutes per person and was not used to obtain the table. Stand down after two consecutive checks with coverage restored, a feasible base plan and no forecast P1 breach. These thresholds and the effect of any relief require pilot testing.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Priority and estimate gaming.** A P1 label buys access to the reserve and interruption rights. Record the priority evidence and timestamp, use shared service estimates, retain original priority and audit overrides against incident outcomes. Compare like types and hours before attributing differences to staff speed.
- **Starvation and displaced deadlines.** Strict priority can leave P3 waiting into the second day. The hybrid's virtual P3 due date improves that tail, but the U5 P2 result shows who pays. Show the oldest and least-slack case in *each* class. Escalate projected misses against actual deadlines. Tune the 600-minute aging rule against both P2 and P3.
- **Interruptions and teamwork.** In U5 Hybrid interrupts about 1.2 times per simulated cohort, versus 0.6 for Pool. The once-per-victim rule prevents repeated cuts on different branches of one case. Count restart minutes, additional setup and victim delay along with any P1 time saved. A dispatch late in a nearly complete operation may save little. Test a minimum predicted saving before interruption.
- **Coordination and Validation.** Parallel work ties up two specialists and may produce a burst of simultaneous Validation requests. Track time waiting to form pairs, actual overlap, join delay and the ready-to-validate queue. If workers actively attend the eight-minute coordination, budget those worker-minutes too. Keeping Emil on E after 16:00 can leave nobody for S or final sign-off.
- **Fairness and skill retention.** A small utilization gap in U5 can mean that nearly everyone is saturated. Track absolute load, interruptions, unfavorable late work and overtime per person as well as dispersion. Fixed assignments can erode backup skills. Rehearse cross-cover on low-risk cases during documented slack and update eligibility only after proficiency is verified.
- **Interpretability.** Show the case's real deadline, dispatch deadline if aged, expected finish, qualified alternatives, reserve owner and trigger for overflow or interruption. Give the lead an auditable override. Distinguish calendar holds, policy holdback and lack of qualified staff so a queue owner can act on the cause.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

**Event contract.** Use a stable case ID, activity-instance ID, branch and predecessor IDs, and an event/segment ID. Record arrival, type, priority history, actual SLA deadline and calendar/time zone. Emit queue entry when prerequisites are met, then assignment decision, transfer start/end, setup, productive start, pause with cause, resume, branch finish, join ready, Validation finish and disposition. Keep both branch-complete time and post-coordination Validation queue-entry time. Capture skill used, worker, team and prior owner, complete qualified set, on-duty/free set, policy-admissible routes and rejection reasons. At assignment store forecast duration, expected finish, score components, reserve status, chosen route, policy and matrix versions, shock flag, break/outage calendar and any override. At completion store actual active intervals, interruption and preemptor IDs, restart minutes, coordination time and overtime. A shift pause and a P1 interruption have different consequences.

**Indicators.** Compute end-to-end flow from arrival, SLA attainment and distance to deadline by priority, type and arrival block. Track unfinished arrivals at 20:00, next-day carry-in, oldest wait, remaining skill-minutes and time to restore a normal queue. Compute assignment minus queue-entry time per decision, productive-start minus assignment, active minutes per activity instance across resumes, and time between last branch finish and Validation start. A transfer delay may overlap queue residence. Split waits using logged causes before summing. Report transfer counts on dependency edges, critical-path transfer minutes, total diagnostic setup and restart labor, utilization against net calendars and the individual 90% exceptions. Reconcile arrived = completed + open and keep open cases visible in SLA reporting.

**PM4Py use.** Format timestamped interval records and retain stable ordering/IDs. On activity instances, inspect directly-follows frequencies and performance by policy, calendar block and case type. Use variants for M/E/S and fork/join-aware Petri-net replay or alignments for ME/ES. Check for missing branches, premature Validation and illegal resumes. A completion-ordered sequence can turn simultaneous branches into an apparent handover. Compare PM4Py's resource handover network to the explicit predecessor/recipient edges and their recorded transfer intervals. A resource pair's frequency alone does not give the time cost.

Use PM4Py service-time summaries by activity, then aggregate productive segments across resumes and divide by the resource multiplier to examine base-service quantiles and tail frequency by comparable case mix. Monitor rolling changes in arrival mix, variants, multiplier-adjusted service and team-to-team transfers. Plot stage-specific queue-entry to assignment p50/p90 and ready workload per staffed skill-hour to see a bottleneck move from Intake to E/S or Validation. Examine model conformance and staffing calendars at the change point before attributing cause.

The Python example passes an explicit start timestamp to the PM4Py APIs. In the pandas interval calculation, case service is the sum of recorded interval durations, including setup if supplied. Case sojourn spans the first recorded start to the last completion, and reported case waiting is sojourn less service, in seconds. These case values repeat on rows. Overlapping branch intervals can make the residual negative, and pre-Intake queueing lies outside that span. For elapsed inactive time, subtract the *union* of occupied intervals from arrival-to-completion time. For queue delay, use the queue-entry and assignment events directly. The example prints this latter measure by stage.

owners = ["FACCB","FADCB","ABDEC","ABDEC","DDDEE","E-EEE"]

def population(s, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    surge,inc,weight,_,tail,_ = shocks[s]
    cases = []
    for b,row in demand.iterrows():
        pp = row[["P1","P2","P3"]].to_numpy(float)/100
        mix = row[types].to_numpy(float)
        n = row.n
        if b<2:
            n *= surge
            pp[1:] *= (1-pp[0]-inc)/(1-pp[0])
            pp[0] += inc
        if b in (2,3):
            mix[[1,4]] *= weight
        for _ in range(rng.poisson(n)):
            arr = 120*b + int(rng.integers(120))
            p = int(rng.choice([1,2,3],p=pp))
            typ = str(rng.choice(types,p=mix/mix.sum()))
            draws = {}
            for k in K:
                lo,hi,a,z,prob = service.loc[k]
                in_tail = rng.random() < prob*(tail if k in home else 1)
                draws[k] = rng.uniform(a,z) if in_tail else rng.uniform(lo,hi)
            cases.append(dict(i=len(cases),arr=arr,p=p,typ=typ,draw=draws,stage="I",
                              q=arr,parents=(),cut=False,edges=0,par=0))
    return cases

# Each option is a tuple of (worker, ordered skills). Pair dispatch is atomic.
routes = {}
for stage in ["I","V"]+types:
    routes[stage] = [((r,stage),) for r in staff if all((r,k) in v for k in stage)]
    if len(stage)==2:
        routes[stage] += [((r,stage[0]),(z,stage[1])) for r,z in permutations(staff,2)
                          if (r,stage[0]) in v and (z,stage[1]) in v]

def lag(a,b):
    if a==b:
        return 0
    return handoff + team_move*(team[a]!=team[b] and "neutral" not in (team[a],team[b]))

def simulate(s,policy,seed,save=False):
    cases = population(s,seed)
    arrivals = defaultdict(list)
    for c in cases:
        arrivals[c["arr"]].append(c)
    absent,outage = shocks[s][3],shocks[s][5]
    duty = {r:np.array([i for a,b in windows[r] for i in range(a,b)]) for r in staff}
    def forecast(r,t,n):
        # Last predicted occupied slot + 1; future days have ordinary shifts.
        day,m = divmod(t,1440)
        j = duty[r].searchsorted(m)+max(1,ceil(n))-1
        return (day+j//cap[r])*1440 + int(duty[r][j%cap[r]])+1
    stack = {r:[] for r in staff}
    last = dict.fromkeys(staff)  # (case, last diagnostic family); I/V do not reset it
    busy = dict.fromkeys(staff,0)
    first_day = dict.fromkeys(staff,0)
    pending, rows, last_row = {},[],{}
    setup_min = cuts = finished = t = 0
    while finished<len(cases):
        day,m = divmod(t,1440)
        if m>=720:
            t += 1440-m
            continue
        if day>=12:
            raise RuntimeError("cohort failed to drain")
        if m==0:
            busy = dict.fromkeys(staff,0)
        on = {r for r in staff if any(a<=m<b for a,b in windows[r])
              and not(day==0 and absent and r=="C")}
        for c in arrivals.get(t,()):
            pending[c["i"]] = c
        down = bool(day==0 and outage and outage[0]<=m<outage[1])
        b = m//120
        fixed = dict(zip(K,owners[b]))
        if absent and day==0:
            fixed = {k:({"E":"A","S":"F","V":"B"}[k] if r=="C" else r)
                     for k,r in fixed.items()}
        def slack(c):
            st = c["stage"]
            remain = mu["V"] + (mu["I"] if st=="I" else 0)
            if st!="V":
                remain += max(mu[k] for k in c["typ"])
            return c["arr"]+target[c["p"]]-t-remain
        def order(c):
            if policy!="Hybrid":
                return c["p"],c["arr"],c["i"]
            aging = 840 if c["p"]==3 else 0  # virtual 600-minute due date for dispatch
            return c["p"]!=1,slack(c)-aging,c["arr"],c["i"]
        def change(r,k,c):
            return setup if k in home and last[r] and last[r][0]!=c["i"] and last[r][1]!=k else 0
        def can_cut(r):
            j = stack[r][-1]
            return (j["k"][0] in home and j["worked"] and not j["c"]["cut"]
                    and j["c"]["p"]>1 and j["su"]==0)
        def start_part(j):
            k = j["k"][0]
            j.update(rem=ceil(j["c"]["draw"][k]*v[j["r"],k]),su=None,worked=False,
                     segment=None,penalty_due=0)

        free = {r for r in on if not stack[r]}
        reserve = None
        if policy=="Hybrid" and len(on)>1 and free:
            # Only known P1s in/awaiting intake or waiting for their diagnostic route.
            watch = [c for c in cases if c["p"]==1 and c["arr"]<=t and
                     (c["stage"]=="I" or (c["stage"] in types and c["i"] in pending))]
            if watch:
                lead = min(watch,key=lambda c:(c["arr"],c["i"]))
                k = next(k for k in "SEM" if k in lead["typ"])
                fit = [r for r in free if (r,k) in v]
                if fit:
                    reserve = min(fit,key=lambda r:(v[r,k],r))
        # Ready cases are reconsidered each minute. Never see a latent duration in scoring.
        for c in sorted(pending.values(),key=order):
            st,q = c["stage"],c["q"]
            if t<q or (st=="I" and down):
                continue
            urgent = c["p"]==1
            allow_cut = (urgent and st in types and policy!="Fixed" and
                         slack(c)<(30 if policy=="Hybrid" else 0))
            if not free and not allow_cut:
                continue
            choices = []
            for route in routes[st]:
                rs = [r for r,ks in route]
                if not all(r in on for r in rs):
                    continue
                victims = [r for r in rs if r not in free]
                if victims and not (allow_cut and len(route)==1 and can_cut(victims[0])):
                    continue
                if policy=="Fixed" and any(fixed[k]!=r for r,ks in route for k in ks):
                    continue
                if policy=="Hybrid" and not urgent and t-q<15 and slack(c)>40:
                    if any(k in home and r not in home[k] and any(z in on for z in home[k])
                           for r,ks in route for k in ks):
                        continue
                if (reserve in rs and not urgent and st!="I" and t-q<40 and slack(c)>30):
                    continue
                transfers = [lag(a,r) for a in c["parents"] for r in rs]
                if t < q+max(transfers,default=0):
                    continue
                ends,effort = [],0
                for r,ks in route:
                    n = change(r,ks[0],c) + sum(mu[k]*v[r,k] for k in ks)
                    ends.append(forecast(r,t,n))
                    effort += n
                count = sum(x>0 for x in transfers)
                cost = max(ends)-t + join*(len(route)==2)
                if policy=="Hybrid":
                    cost += .30*effort+3*count+15*max(
                        max(0,(busy[r]/cap[r]-.75)/.15) for r in rs)
                choices.append(((bool(victims),cost,count,route),route,victims,count))
            if not choices:
                continue
            _,route,victims,count = min(choices)
            eligible = "|".join("+".join(r+":"+ks for r,ks in opt) for _,opt,_,_ in choices) if save else ""
            del pending[c["i"]]
            c.update(left=len(route),outs=[],par=int(len(route)==2),edges=c["edges"]+count)
            for r in victims:
                j = stack[r][-1]
                j["penalty_due"] = ceil(restart*j["rem"])
                j["rem"] += j["penalty_due"]
                if save:
                    j["segment"].update(interrupt_at=t,interrupted_by=c["i"])
                j["c"]["cut"] = True
                j["su"] = None  # recompute setup when this worker resumes the victim
                cuts += 1
            for r,ks in route:
                j = dict(c=c,r=r,k=ks,decision=t,q=q,eligible=eligible,edges=count)
                start_part(j)
                stack[r].append(j)
                free.discard(r)
            if reserve in (r for r,ks in route):
                reserve = None

        for r in sorted(on):
            if not stack[r]:
                continue
            j = stack[r][-1]
            c,k = j["c"],j["k"][0]
            if k=="I" and down:
                continue
            if j["su"] is None:
                j["su"] = change(r,k,c)
            is_setup = j["su"]>0
            if is_setup:
                j["su"] -= 1
                setup_min += 1
            else:
                if k in home:
                    last[r] = (c["i"],k)
                j["rem"] -= 1
                j["worked"] = True
            busy[r] += 1
            if day==0:
                first_day[r] += 1
            if save:
                label = ("Setup-" if is_setup else "")+k
                row = last_row.get(r)
                if row and (row["id"],row["act"],row["end"])==(c["i"],label,t):
                    row["end"] += 1
                else:
                    prev = j["segment"]
                    reason = ("resume_after_P1" if j["penalty_due"] else
                              "resume_after_closure" if prev and prev["end"]<t else "start")
                    row = dict(id=c["i"],act=label,r=r,start=t,end=t+1,queue=j["q"],
                               transition=reason,restart_min=j["penalty_due"],version=1,
                               decision=j["decision"],eligible=j["eligible"],skill=k,
                               p=c["p"],typ=c["typ"],arrival=c["arr"],scenario=s,
                               policy=policy,stage=c["stage"],parents="".join(c["parents"]),
                               stage_edges=j["edges"],plan_min=setup if is_setup else mu[k]*v[r,k])
                    rows.append(row)
                    j.update(segment=row,penalty_due=0)
                    last_row[r] = row
            if j["rem"]:
                continue
            j["k"] = j["k"][1:]
            if j["k"]:
                start_part(j)
                continue
            stack[r].pop()
            c["outs"].append(r)
            c["left"] -= 1
            if c["left"]:
                continue
            st = c["stage"]
            c["parents"] = tuple(c["outs"])
            if st=="V":
                c.update(stage="done",finish=t+1)
                finished += 1
            else:
                c["stage"] = c["typ"] if st=="I" else "V"
                c["q"] = t+1 + join*c["par"]
                pending[c["i"]] = c
        t += 1
    util = [first_day[r]/cap[r] for r in staff if not(absent and r=="C")]
    daystats = dict(maxU=max(util),gap=max(util)-min(util),setup=setup_min,cuts=cuts)
    caseout = [dict(p=c["p"],flow=c["finish"]-c["arr"],edges=c["edges"],
                    carry=c["finish"]>720) for c in cases]
    return caseout,daystats,rows

def mining(rows):
    import pm4py
    df = pd.DataFrame(rows).rename(columns={"id":"case:concept:name","act":"concept:name",
                                            "r":"org:resource"})
    df["case:concept:name"] = df["case:concept:name"].astype(str)
    epoch = pd.Timestamp("2000-01-01 08:00",tz="UTC")
    for src,dst in [("start","start_timestamp"),("end","time:timestamp")]:
        df[dst] = epoch+pd.to_timedelta(df[src],unit="m")
    df["actual_segment_min"] = df.end-df.start
    df["deadline_min"] = df.arrival+df.p.map(target)
    for _,g in df.sort_values("start").groupby("org:resource"):
        assert (g.start.iloc[1:].to_numpy()>=g.end.iloc[:-1].to_numpy()).all()
    assert (df.actual_segment_min>0).all()
    df.to_csv("simulated_intervals.csv",index=False)
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(df,start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    args = dict(start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    stats = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log,**args)
    print(stats.drop_duplicates("case:concept:name")[
        ["case:concept:name","@@service_time","@@waiting_time","@@sojourn_time"]].head(3))
    print("Mean segment seconds:",pm4py.get_service_time(log,**args))
    network = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
    print("Observed resource links:",len(network.connections))
    # One row per stage decision. Both simultaneous branches share a decision.
    adm = df.drop_duplicates(["case:concept:name","decision"]).copy()
    adm["queue_to_assign_min"] = adm.decision-adm.queue
    print(adm.groupby("stage").queue_to_assign_min.agg(["mean","max"]).round(1))

def experiment(nseed=64):
    table = []
    for s in shocks:
        for pol in policies:
            records,days = [],[]
            for seed in range(nseed):
                save = (s,pol,seed)==("Base","Hybrid",0)
                cases,day,rows = simulate(s,pol,seed,save)
                records.extend(cases)
                day["n1"] = sum(c["p"]==1 for c in cases)
                day["ok1"] = sum(c["p"]==1 and c["flow"]<=target[1] for c in cases)
                days.append(day)
                if save:
                    mining(rows)
            x,d = pd.DataFrame(records),pd.DataFrame(days)
            sla = {f"P{p}":100*(g.flow<=target[p]).mean() for p,g in x.groupby("p")}
            boot = np.random.default_rng(802).integers(nseed,size=(1000,nseed))
            denom = d.n1.to_numpy()[boot].sum(axis=1)
            num = d.ok1.to_numpy()[boot].sum(axis=1)
            lo,hi = np.quantile(100*num[denom>0]/denom[denom>0],[.025,.975])
            table.append(dict(s=s,policy=pol,mean=x.flow.mean(),p90=x.flow.quantile(.90),
                              **sla,P1lo=lo,P1hi=hi,maxU=100*d.maxU.mean(),gap=100*d.gap.mean(),
                              edges=x.edges.mean(),setup=d.setup.sum()/len(x),
                              cuts=d.cuts.mean(),carry=100*x.carry.mean(),
                              U10=100*d.maxU.quantile(.1),U90=100*d.maxU.quantile(.9)))
    print(pd.DataFrame(table).round(1).to_string(index=False))

if __name__=="__main__":
    experiment()

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected arrivals and service ranges do not identify an SLA percentage, a stable backlog, a joint priority/type distribution or the staffing cost of a handover. The simulated table depends particularly on empty opening queues, a quiet drain day, simultaneous pair starts, team definitions, fixed sequential order, calendar holds on the same owner, and the ability to know the diagnostic type at Intake. Dispatch considers immediately feasible routes. It can start a slow worker shortly before closing even if waiting for a different worker would be better. Correlated electrical incidents, correlated branch tails, service already in progress at 08:00 and a prolonged absence could all change the ranking. No staffed overnight recovery, staggered parallel starts, discretionary handoffs at shift end, new relief workers, rework, quality differences or overtime responses were simulated.

1. **Reconstruct the baseline.** Match arrival, eligibility, assignment, service and completion records across overnight cases. Reconcile productive, setup and idle time to shift/break calendars. Check that no worker is double booked, both required branches precede Validation, no victim is interrupted twice, and the outage has zero active Intake. Confirm whether coordination ties up people and whether a cross-team handoff adds 5 minutes on the observed edge. Review exceptions with operators.
2. **Backtest on held-out days.** Fit interarrival clustering, joint type/priority, worker-adjusted service and branch-tail dependence on earlier days. Check whether measured times include waiting or restart work. Replay complete arrival streams with real carry-in, calendars and next-day competition, using common latent work across policies and resampling whole days or incident clusters. Compare the simulated incumbent against actual queue profiles, individual loads, SLA distributions and spillover before trusting counterfactuals. For workers rarely assigned a skill, retain a sensitivity range for their speed.
3. **Stress the decision.** Re-run with correlated tails, endpoint service means, resource-occupying handovers, staggered starts, bounded overtime, long runs of consecutive base days and successive shock days. Measure backlog growth and time to recovery alongside P1/P2/P3 and fairness. Test the proposed relief by skill and clock time. Estimate paired confidence intervals for policy differences, especially Hybrid versus Pool on P1 and P2. Separate uncertainty from finite samples from uncertainty about model structure.
4. **Protect live cases.** Start in shadow mode with the experienced dispatcher retaining control. Log every disagreement and review projected P1 misses and their feasible alternatives. Once a feasible capacity plan is in place, pilot on selected staffed shifts with a qualified lead and relief available. Keep P1 escalation live in both arms. Compare matched or randomized shift blocks, tracking carryover between them, and evaluate *all* arrival cohorts through their deadlines. Stop the pilot immediately for a blocked P1 with a faster safe route, an eligibility/clock failure or a proposed overtime-cap violation. Expand only after uncertainty intervals support the agreed P1 safeguard, P2/P3 targets and individual planned-load limit across the tested conditions. If the incumbent also misses targets, use shadow evidence to revise staffing before expanding either dispatcher.
