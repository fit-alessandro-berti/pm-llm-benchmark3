# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

- **Upstream data quality dependencies**
  - `Receive application -> Register application -> Classify product and exposure` has very high volume, but classification errors are consequential. A misclassified product or exposure can send a case down the wrong KYC, collateral, committee, or contract-control path.
  - `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review` is a major rework loop. A5 should precede or accompany A6, because extraction/normalization cannot work reliably on incomplete files.
  - `Financial document review -> Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis` means A6 is a prerequisite for cleaner credit analysis and committee packs.
  - Committee packaging waits for multiple artifacts: KYC status, normalized statements, collateral valuation if required, and credit-analysis notes. A8 should not be deployed before A4/A6/A7 have stabilized those inputs.

- **Sequentiality caveat**
  - KYC check and financial document review are often concurrent, even though the information system records them sequentially. Automation should not create an artificial hard dependency between them. The real hard dependency is that **KYC clearance must precede customer signing**, and contract terms must follow the committee decision.

- **Legal/compliance controls**
  - KYC clearance must prevent illegal provisional signing; 6.5% of cases show provisional signing and 1.1% required remediation.
  - Contract terms must match committee decision; remediation loops are 650 cases in the DFG and 8.5% overall, rising to 14% for Credit-line renewal.
  - High-exposure SME cases require four-eyes separation between credit analysis and committee packaging; violations occur in 5.8% during month-end rush.
  - All model-assisted candidates must preserve an auditable trail of: input data, model suggestion, confidence, human override, final decision, user ID, timestamp, and rule/model version.

- **Human escalation capacity**
  - Compliance specialists are only 3 FTE, with high dependency on two senior specialists. A3 and A4 must not flood this pool with low-value false positives.
  - Credit analysts are 9 FTE and central in the collaboration network. A6 may improve input quality but increase clean inflow to credit analysis; queue caps and prioritization are needed.
  - RMs trigger missing-document loops through incomplete instructions. A5 should change RM templates and customer messaging, not merely send more nudges.
  - Legal ops is underutilized when KYC is slow but overloaded at month end. A9 can help month-end capacity, but only if the rule library and upstream decision data are trusted.

- **External valuation dependency**
  - Collateral valuation is required in 71% of Equipment finance cases and 18% of Working-capital cases.
  - `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request -> Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` creates a synchronization point with 58h mean wait.
  - A7 can triage and parallelize requests, but cannot fully control valuer turnaround. It needs partner SLAs, fallback routes, and a quality-dispute process.

- **Core-banking integration constraints**
  - Only two modules can be deeply integrated with core banking in the first six months.
  - Deep integration should be reserved for modules that alter hard gates or authoritative state. In the recommended portfolio, these are A4 for KYC-clearance state and A6 for validated normalized financial data. A5 is portal/CRM-oriented; A7 is external valuer routing and need not be a deep core-banking integration.
  - A1, A2, A10, and A11 should not consume scarce early core-integration capacity merely because they are visible or high-frequency.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

**Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A5, A9**

- **Cycle time:** Modest improvement. `Receive application -> Register application` and `Register application -> Classify product and exposure` become faster, and A5 reduces document loops. However, cases arrive faster at unresolved KYC, financial review, normalization, and credit-analysis queues, so end-to-end cycle time may improve less than local service time.
- **Waiting time:** Registration wait falls; missing-document response delay falls; contract preparation may improve if A9 has clean decision data. Waiting before KYC, normalize financial statements, credit analysis, and external valuation remains largely unchanged.
- **Rework:** A5 reduces the 52% repeated document-request problem. A9 can reduce contract remediation if rules are current. A2 may introduce new misrouting rework if recommender errors are not controlled.
- **Handovers:** Fewer back-office clerical handovers, but more exception handoffs into compliance because A1 accelerates inflow. A9 shifts some legal-ops work into rule maintenance and exception review.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office utilization is already low after 15:00; A1 may further reduce visible demand there while compliance and analysts absorb more pressure. Legal ops improves at month end if A9 is reliable.
- **Control violations:** A9 can enforce decision/KYC checks for contracts, but four-eyes violations and provisional-signing risk remain unaddressed unless manual controls are added.

**Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A7 — recommended**

- **Cycle time:** Largest expected end-to-end improvement. A4 shortens cases on the critical `KYC check -> Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` path; A5 reduces `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review`; A6 improves the normalization-to-credit chain; A7 attacks the long `Credit analysis -> Collateral valuation request -> Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` synchronization point. Remaining delays include committee cadence, especially weekly high-exposure decisions, and the 44h customer-signing wait.
- **Waiting time:** Direct reduction in the 31h entity-mismatch wait, repeated customer-document delay, 20h normalization wait, and 58h external valuation wait. KYC hit resolution outside entity mismatch and the 28h credit-analysis wait remain material.
- **Rework:** A4 reduces entity-mismatch loops; A5 reduces repeated missing-document loops; A6 reduces normalization defects; A7 reduces 19% late valuations. Rework may shift toward analyst overrides and valuer quality disputes.
- **Handovers:** Fewer RM/back-office/compliance ping-pong loops. More deliberate handovers to compliance specialists, credit analysts, and external valuers. A6 changes analyst work from manual preparation to exception review.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance specialists need escalation capacity because they are only 3 FTE. Credit analysts need queue controls to avoid overload from cleaner inflow. External valuation demand rises in Equipment finance and Working-capital cases requiring collateral. Legal ops may see more downstream work once cases reach contract preparation faster.
- **Control violations:** A4, if configured with audit and KYC-clearance gating, reduces provisional-signing exposure. It does not by itself solve four-eyes violations or contract-term mismatches; those need hard workflow controls now and A9/A12 later.

**Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12**

- **Cycle time:** Uneven. KYC-related cases may move faster, and contract-remediation loops such as `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue -> Contract preparation` should fall. However, document loops, normalization, credit analysis, and external valuation remain major delays.
- **Waiting time:** Entity-mismatch and KYC hit queues may improve; contract remediation waits improve. The 18h financial-document wait, 20h normalization wait, 28h credit-analysis wait, and 58h external valuation wait remain largely untouched.
- **Rework:** Fewer KYC and contract-control rework loops. A3 false positives may increase specialist workload; A12 may surface policy ambiguity that temporarily increases review cycles.
- **Handovers:** More structured handoffs to compliance, legal, and control owners. More approval steps may be introduced for high-exposure and Credit-line renewal cases.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance specialists and senior specialists become the critical constraint. Legal ops shifts from correction work to rule administration. Committee and RM workloads may rise because of added control checks.
- **Control violations:** Strongest reduction in four-eyes breaches, provisional signing, contract-term mismatches, and missing audit trails. Requires strict thresholds to avoid the false-negative risk of KYC pre-classification.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

| risk area | where it appears | compensating controls |
|---|---|---|
| Auditability | A2, A3, A4, A6, A8, A9 | Log model suggestion, evidence used, confidence, human override, final decision, user ID, timestamp, and model/rule version. Make audit records immutable and reviewable by compliance. |
| False positives and false negatives | A3 KYC pre-classifier, A4 mismatch assistant, A2 classification recommender, A6 extraction | No auto-clearance for KYC or high-exposure cases. Use high-recall thresholds, human confirmation, dual review for severe cases, and segment-specific validation for Micro, SME, and High-exposure SME. |
| Model drift | A2, A3, A4, A5, A6, A9 | Monitor by applicant type, product, document quality, month-end period, and exception type. Recalibrate when override rates, false clears, or rework rates rise. Use champion/challenger testing before replacing rules. |
| Exception handling capacity | A4, A6, A7, A9, A12 | Define specialist SLAs, overflow queues, escalation to senior compliance specialists, and queue caps before enabling automation. Do not allow automated modules to push silent exceptions into backlog. |
| Four-eyes compliance | High-exposure SME cases during month-end; A8/A9 if packaging or contracts are automated | System-enforced separation of user IDs for credit analysis and committee packaging. Block same-user completion; log any emergency override and review it daily during month end. |
| KYC/signing control | A3, A4, A10, A11 | Hard-stop customer signing and activation unless KYC state is cleared and auditable. Treat provisional signing as a control exception, not a normal path. |
| Contract legal risk | A9, especially Credit-line renewal | Legal-approved, versioned rule library; automated check that terms match committee decision; human legal approval for non-standard clauses and renewals. |
| Customer friction and RM resistance | A5, A10 | Use RM-approved instruction templates, configurable nudges, opt-out/escalation paths, and monitoring of complaint rates. Avoid rigid checklists that reject valid documents unnecessarily. |
| Downstream bottleneck shifting | A1, A2, A5, A6, A9 | Rate-limit automated inflow into constrained queues. Use work-queue prioritization and capacity gates before pushing cases into credit analysis, committee packaging, or activation. |
| External valuation dependency | A7 | Valuer SLAs, parallel routing, fallback valuers, quality checks, and a dispute queue. Track late-rate and quality disputes by product and valuer. |
| Incomplete controls in downstream bots | A11 | Do not deploy loan activation automation until KYC, contract, signing, and remediation states are reliable. A11 remains out of the first-year portfolio. |

# 7. Final recommendation

Recommend the **bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A7**.

This portfolio best fits the process evidence. The highest-frequency activity, `Receive application -> Register application`, is attractive politically and operationally, but registration is not where the end-to-end process loses the most time. Automating A1 first would mostly push more cases faster into KYC hit resolution, financial document review, normalization, and credit analysis. Because those queues already have long waits and high exception rates, the visible local saving would likely expose rather than solve the real constraints.

By contrast:

- **A4** addresses a low-frequency but high-impact exception: entity mismatch has only 1,750 cases but a 31h wait, 38% exception rate, and is on the critical path in 82% of cases. With audit and human escalation, it also strengthens KYC control.
- **A5** reduces repeated missing-document loops, where customer response delay dominates and RMs currently trigger rework through incomplete instructions.
- **A6** attacks analyst preparation time and is a prerequisite for cleaner credit analysis and committee packs, but must be paired with analyst-capacity gates.
- **A7** addresses the largest synchronization point: the 58h external valuation wait feeding committee packaging, especially for Equipment finance.

This portfolio deliberately excludes A11 because a loan-activation bot is unsafe while upstream KYC, contract, signing, and remediation states remain imperfect. It also postpones A8 and full A9 because they depend on clean upstream artifacts. Contract-term and four-eyes controls should be enforced manually in workflow during year one, with A9/A12 considered for the next wave if control metrics remain weak.

The defensible trade-off is therefore: **do not automate the highest-frequency activity first; automate the dependencies, exceptions, and synchronization points that govern flow, while preserving human accountability and control integrity.**
