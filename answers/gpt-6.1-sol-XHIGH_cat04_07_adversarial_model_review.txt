# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

Neither submission is ready to serve as the authoritative compliance model. Model S has demonstrable safety violations; Model C preserves several important invariants but confuses its eight enumerated variants with the policy’s permitted behavior. The reported measurements are diagnostic evidence, not a substitute for policy semantics or reliable event data.

I distinguish four uses:

| Use | Appropriate trade-off |
|---|---|
| Exploration and discovery | High fitness and some permissiveness can expose possible relationships and logging gaps. An exploratory model must identify its overapproximations; skipped mandatory work must not become a business recommendation. |
| Conformance enforcement and release authorization | Mandatory obligations and ordering constraints are gates. High aggregate fitness cannot compensate for accepting a prohibited release. Missing evidence requires investigation, rather than automatic approval or an automatic finding of misconduct. |
| Simulation | The model needs plausible permitted behavior, correction dynamics, attribute distributions, probabilities, durations, and resources. Neither language coverage nor high token-based generalization establishes these. |
| Stakeholder communication | A small, understandable backbone helps, but its explanation must include conditional obligations, recheck decisions, and uncertainty. Readability cannot replace correctness. |

I interpret the expressions as standard process trees: sequence orders its children, parallelism permits their interleavings, exclusive choice selects one child, and tau is invisible. In Sections 3–5, “accepts” means exact membership of the complete visible trace in the tree’s language. An alignment with deviations is not acceptance. Neither original expression contains a loop.

I assume the events represent completed activities, with reliable within-case ordering. Equal timestamps, lifecycle events, case mixing, and event loss must be investigated before compliance conclusions. Completion labels alone do not establish satisfactory check results, appropriate approval authority, segregation of duties, or authentic execution.

P1–P7 and the probes are the current policy evidence. Some details remain open:

- P3 establishes an external-order obligation; it does not unambiguously prohibit discretionary Supplier Review on an internal order. I permit that review unless stakeholders specify otherwise.
- I propose that required Supplier Review and all correction work finish before approval. P3 does not explicitly state the supplier deadline; this needs ratification.
- The proposed correction protocol starts after the initial mandatory checks and uses a nonempty affected set drawn from Credit Check and Technical Review, following F6. Earlier corrections, impacts on other reviews, and consecutive corrections without intervening rechecks need explicit policy decisions.
- Whether correction invalidates an earlier Expedite Review is unresolved. The third alternative withholds certification for that situation until the rule is settled.
- No rule establishes eligibility for Auto Approval versus Manager Approval, or requires urgency before Expedite Review. I will not invent either restriction.

These assumptions bound the third alternative’s certification claims. Unsupported situations must be distinguished from established policy breaches.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

The strongest case for S is structural economy. It preserves registration first, release last, and one approval. Parallelism avoids falsely privileging Credit Check over Technical Review. It accepts B2 and B5’s new combination without adding branches, uses no repeated visible labels, and is easy to explain. For exploratory replay of an incomplete log, allowing an unrecorded event can help identify logging problems.

Its strongest weakness is that noise tolerance is encoded as executable business behavior. Both mandatory checks are choices between execution and tau. Expedite Review is an independent parallel branch, so it can happen before either check. Supplier Review is optional without consulting the order attribute.

Concrete admitted traces include:

| Admitted trace | Undesirable or ambiguous behavior |
|---|---|
| Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order | Both mandatory checks omitted: B3, forbidden. |
| Register Dossier, Credit Check, Auto Approval, Release Order | Technical Review omitted, forbidden. |
| Register Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order | Credit Check omitted, forbidden. |
| Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order | Expedite precedes both checks: B4, forbidden. |
| Register Dossier, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order | Expedite precedes Technical Review, forbidden even though both checks eventually occur. |
| Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order, with external = true | Supplier obligation omitted. Identical labels are valid internally, demonstrating the missing data distinction. |

Missing Supplier Review might reflect intentional tolerance for F4’s event loss. Missing mandatory checks may explain the discovery rationale. That warrants an observation model, alignment explanation, or uncertainty flag; it does not warrant authorizing an order without required evidence. Early Expedite Review is an additional ordering flaw. Missing events do not justify removing precedence between events that are present.

S is restrictive in the wrong places too. Each review occurs at most once, and Correct Dossier is absent. It rejects legitimate corrections, including conditional B6, while accepting unsafe omissions. It rejects B8 rather than permitting arbitrary repeated checks. Its visible language contains 130 traces: 65 permutations of subsets of four reviews, times two approvals. This finite language has no correction cycles, regardless of its generalization score.

F7 is defensible within a bounded exploratory use. Low precision can be acceptable for hypotheses, but mandatory-check and ordering violations and missing correction behavior must be visible. S should not define compliance, generate unfiltered operational recommendations, or represent the approved process in training materials.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

The strongest case for C is conservative containment. Every branch has registration first, release last, both mandatory checks before approval, and one approval. Where Expedite Review occurs, it follows both checks. Mandatory repeats occur only in the correction branch. C rejects B3, B4, and B8; that matters more for enforcement than aggregate score alone.

Its strongest weakness is presenting an eight-trace language as a policy model. It enumerates observed combinations instead of independently valid choices:

- Auto Approval exists only after Credit-then-Technical, without Supplier Review, Expedite Review, or correction.
- Expedite branches begin Credit-then-Technical; the opposite order cannot combine with expedition.
- When supplier and expedite coexist, supplier must come first.
- Supplier appears only after both checks, although P3 imposes no such order.
- Correction appears once, after Credit-then-Technical, repeats both checks in that order, and finishes with Manager Approval.

C therefore rejects valid B2 and plausible B5, and B6 even when Technical Review alone was affected. Its correction branch can accept an unnecessary repeated Credit Check when only Technical Review was affected. Without impact data, it cannot distinguish that violation from a legitimate two-check correction.

Duplicated labels are not inherently wrong: an activity can occur in initial and corrective phases. Here, much duplication comes from copied complete prefixes and suffixes. Adding an approval alternative or changing a supplier rule requires multiple edits; omissions produce inconsistent behavior. Multiple transitions with the same label can create replay ambiguity and dilute visitation frequencies. Ownership and impact analysis become harder.

The 0.941 training precision is compatible with memorizing common variants. That is not proof of memorization, but it requires testing: allowlisted variants expose little additional behavior while legitimate unseen recombinations are excluded. The 0.744 holdout fitness and F4 provide converging evidence. Missing supplier events may further harm replay, but do not explain all rejected combinations.

F8 correctly recognizes that genuinely new policy needs approval. Combining already permitted choices is not automatically new policy. B2 is explicitly valid; P2 and P5 establish separate choices of check order and approval alternative. Rejecting their combination because it was absent from training substitutes frequency for policy.

C can record eight familiar paths or support a deliberately narrow allowlist with exceptions. “Audit-ready” additionally requires validated semantics, trace integrity, conditional obligations, approval authority, and explained deviations. Resembling frequent variants establishes none of those by itself.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

These ranks reflect likely governance impact, not a claim that every suspected artifact is demonstrated.

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior.** S’s language demonstrates this: missing both mandatory checks fits exactly. High fitness can reward a safety defect. Inspect activity-specific deviations and impose policy gates independently.

2. **Missing-event and missing-attribute bias.** F4 identifies possible supplier event loss; P3/P6 identify missing conditions. Absence can mean a lawful internal case, an external breach, or logging failure. A correction can look incomplete or excessive depending on unknown impact. Neither optional labels nor fitted control flow reliably recover these distinctions.

3. **Positive-log-only precision and variant memorization.** Alignment precision can detect excess enabled behavior relative to observed prefixes, but has no policy oracle. Observed forbidden behavior can appear supported; valid unseen behavior can appear imprecise. C’s enumeration is vulnerable. Test curated negatives and valid combinations separately.

4. **Train/holdout leakage and split sensitivity.** Leakage is neither demonstrated nor excluded. Cases sharing a customer, order family, template, batch, or time period may be correlated. Full-log filtering or variant selection can leak holdout information. Random splits may omit rare combinations, while grouped and temporal splits expose them. C’s decline makes these tests consequential.

5. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Costs determine whether deviations are explained by dropping log events, inserting model events, or choosing another branch. Publish costs, algorithms, and aggregation. A model move suggesting a missing check is not evidence that the check occurred.

6. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness.** Token-based generalization is a transition-visitation surrogate, not future policy coverage. Frequently used optional branches can score well while admitting unsafe omissions. C’s copied transitions disperse visits. Policy-labeled holdouts and recombination tests are stronger evidence.

7. **Duplicated-label effects.** Copies increase alignment ambiguity, disperse replay counts, and complicate ownership. Conversion reductions can change these effects without changing visible behavior. The undocumented count of 33 is uninterpretable until its convention and representation are fixed.

8. **Silent-transition effects.** S’s tau skips erase obligations; conversions also introduce legitimate routing transitions. Invisible routing affects search, silent closures in precision, and simplicity. Its treatment in replay/generalization must be documented. Silence is not intrinsically wrong, but silently bypassing a mandatory check is.

9. **Runtime confounding by model size.** C’s copied structure and ambiguous choices can increase search; its holdout deviations can make search harder too. Compare identical cases, algorithms, costs, net sizes, and deviation severity. Include tails and timeouts; speed is not correctness.

10. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** An informal mean can hide disagreements among compliance, operations, architecture, and novices. Familiarity, display, explanations, and questions affect scores. Measure correct answers on B3/B4, selective rechecks, and sourcing obligations, alongside confidence and change-analysis time.

These effects interact. Duplication can worsen runtime, simplicity, and token-based generalization simultaneously. Optionalization can improve fitness and speed while weakening compliance. Treating those numbers as independent demonstrations would overstate the evidence.

# 7. Third Alternative

I propose a procedural backbone plus explicit data and temporal guards. It retains mandatory initial checks, composes independent reviews instead of enumerating complete variants, and supports arbitrarily many correction rounds. The guarded composite—not the bare tree or converted net—is the candidate compliance model.

Let R = Register Dossier, L = Release Order, c = Credit Check, t = Technical Review, s = Supplier Review, e = Expedite Review, k = Correct Dossier, m = Manager Approval, and a = Auto Approval. “||”, “?”, and “*” mean interleaving parallelism, optional occurrence, and zero or more repetitions.

~~~text
backbone =
    R ;
    shuffle(
        (c || t) ; (k ; (c? || t?))*,
        s?,
        e?
    ) ;
    (m XOR a) ;
    L

model_third_composite =
    backbone AND conditional_supplier_obligation
             AND expedite_readiness
             AND correction_affected_set_match
             AND trusted_evidence_or_explicit_review
~~~

Optional rechecks represent alternative affected sets; initial mandatory checks remain mandatory. A guard excludes correction rounds with no required recheck. The optional supplier branch represents sourcing conditioning. The independently positioned expedite branch requires a readiness guard. These explicit overapproximations must never authorize release without the guards.

This self-contained PM4Py construction assigns the main model to model_third:

~~~python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def activity(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def silent():
    return ProcessTree()  # No label and no operator: tau.

def block(operator, *children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator, children=list(children))
    for child in children:
        child.parent = node
    return node

checks_and_corrections = block(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    block(
        Operator.PARALLEL,
        activity('Credit Check'),
        activity('Technical Review'),
    ),
    block(
        Operator.LOOP,
        silent(),  # LOOP(do, redo) means do (redo do)*.
        block(
            Operator.SEQUENCE,
            activity('Correct Dossier'),
            block(
                Operator.PARALLEL,
                block(
                    Operator.XOR,
                    activity('Credit Check'),
                    silent(),
                ),
                block(
                    Operator.XOR,
                    activity('Technical Review'),
                    silent(),
                ),
            ),
        ),
    ),
)

model_third = block(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    activity('Register Dossier'),
    block(
        Operator.PARALLEL,
        checks_and_corrections,
        block(
            Operator.XOR,
            activity('Supplier Review'),
            silent(),
        ),
        block(
            Operator.XOR,
            activity('Expedite Review'),
            silent(),
        ),
    ),
    block(
        Operator.XOR,
        activity('Manager Approval'),
        activity('Auto Approval'),
    ),
    activity('Release Order'),
)

net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
~~~

Nodes have one parent and are not reused across positions. Each mandatory label has one initial and one recheck occurrence. This phase-specific duplication is deliberate; entire end-to-end variants are not copied.

The external checker below is executable. It does not claim that PM4Py automatically enforces a declarative dictionary. Events are labels or dictionaries; correction dictionaries carry an affected_checks collection. The external argument must come from trusted dossier data, not be inferred from supplier-event presence.

~~~python
MANDATORY = frozenset({'Credit Check', 'Technical Review'})
APPROVALS = frozenset({'Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval'})

policy_config = {
    'affected_attribute': 'affected_checks',
    'supplier_on_internal': 'permitted',
    'unknown_data_action': 'manual_review',
    'correction_after_expedite': 'manual_review_pending_policy',
}

def check_third_policy(events, external=None, evidence_complete=False):
    if external is not None and not isinstance(external, bool):
        raise ValueError('external must be True, False, or None')

    records = [
        {'concept:name': event} if isinstance(event, str) else dict(event)
        for event in events
    ]
    names = [event.get('concept:name') for event in records]
    violations = []
    unresolved = []

    if (
        len(names) < 2
        or names[0] != 'Register Dossier'
        or names[-1] != 'Release Order'
    ):
        violations.append('Register must be first and Release must be last')

    seen = set()
    expected = set(MANDATORY)
    supplier_count = 0
    expedite_count = 0
    approval_count = 0
    approved = False

    def finish_round(location):
        if expected is None:
            if not seen:
                violations.append(
                    'No recheck recorded in correction round: ' + location
                )
        elif seen != expected:
            violations.append(
                'Incomplete or excessive check round: ' + location
            )

    for event in records[1:-1]:
        name = event.get('concept:name')

        if approved:
            violations.append('Activity after approval: ' + str(name))
            continue

        if name in MANDATORY:
            if name in seen:
                violations.append('Repeated check within round: ' + name)
            if expected is not None and name not in expected:
                violations.append('Unaffected check repeated: ' + name)
            seen.add(name)

        elif name == 'Correct Dossier':
            finish_round('before correction')
            if expedite_count:
                unresolved.append(
                    'Correction after Expedite Review needs policy resolution'
                )
            seen = set()
            raw = event.get(policy_config['affected_attribute'])
            if raw is None:
                expected = None
                unresolved.append('Correction affected set is missing')
            elif not isinstance(raw, (list, tuple, set, frozenset)):
                expected = None
                violations.append('Affected set has invalid representation')
            else:
                expected = set(raw)
                if not expected or not expected.issubset(MANDATORY):
                    violations.append('Affected set is outside supported policy')

        elif name == 'Supplier Review':
            supplier_count += 1
            if supplier_count > 1:
                violations.append('Repeated Supplier Review unsupported')

        elif name == 'Expedite Review':
            expedite_count += 1
            if expedite_count > 1:
                violations.append('Repeated Expedite Review unsupported')
            finish_round('before expedite')

        elif name in APPROVALS:
            approval_count += 1
            finish_round('before approval')
            approved = True

        else:
            violations.append('Unexpected activity: ' + str(name))

    finish_round('at release')

    if approval_count != 1:
        violations.append('Exactly one approval is required')

    if external is True and supplier_count != 1:
        violations.append('External order lacks Supplier Review')
    elif external is None and supplier_count == 0:
        unresolved.append('Sourcing attribute is missing; supplier duty unknown')

    if not evidence_complete:
        unresolved.append('Event completeness and integrity are not established')

    # Reject concerns the trusted observed trace under the candidate rules.
    # Unreliable evidence retains findings but requires investigation.
    if not evidence_complete:
        decision = 'indeterminate'
    elif violations:
        decision = 'reject'
    elif unresolved:
        decision = 'indeterminate'
    else:
        decision = 'accept'

    return {
        'decision': decision,
        'violations': violations,
        'unresolved': unresolved,
    }
~~~

The semantics and operating boundary are:

1. **Mandatory checks:** both initial checks complete once, in either order. Correction does not erase that obligation. Approval requires completion of the current correction round. Repeats within a round are rejected on trusted evidence.
2. **Supplier Review:** one is required externally; it is optional internally under the stated interpretation. It may precede, interleave with, or follow initial checks, and occur before or after expedite, but before approval. Unknown sourcing without supplier evidence is indeterminate. With supplier evidence, the conditional obligation is satisfied under either sourcing value.
3. **Expedite Review:** at most once, before approval, when initial checks and currently affected rechecks have completed. Later correction creates an unresolved question about expedite validity; certification is withheld.
4. **Approval:** exactly one alternative, independent of check order and supplier presence. Resource and eligibility requirements must be supplied before stronger authorization claims.
5. **Correction:** each selects credit only, technical only, or both. Exactly that set is rechecked once before the next correction, expedite, or approval. Both affected checks may finish in either order. Unaffected initial outcomes remain valid. Multiple rounds are permitted.
6. **Unknown affected set:** one observed recheck might be correct selective handling or incomplete two-check handling. Missing metadata produces indeterminate status, not guessed compliance.
7. **Evidence integrity:** missing mandatory events remain visible deviations. Model moves cannot prove execution. Independent dossier/source-system evidence must resolve suspected event loss; incomplete-evidence cases never receive automatic acceptance.

At runtime, enforce guards at routing and activity-enable decisions, including invisible skips. Post-hoc diagnostics alone cannot prevent release. Authorization requires exact visible backbone replay, an accept decision from the same checker, and trusted evidence. Exact replay permits invisible routing but no log moves or visible model moves.

The checker embodies the proposed correction-phase and review-before-approval assumptions. Rejection under those added assumptions is not an established P1–P7 breach until ratified. Corrections affecting other reviews, changing sourcing, repeated optional reviews, and early corrections need broader rules or an exception route. Its dictionary documents the policy; the function supplies the enforcement logic.

This does not manufacture missing attributes. Trusted enrichment is preferred. Otherwise preserve an accountable uncertainty queue, distinguishing candidate-model rejection, observed policy deviation, and confirmed real-world breach.

# 9. Validation Experiments

Freeze the submissions and guards before evaluation. Separate reproduction of reported results from evaluation against a new policy-labeled suite. The existing numbers cannot be reproduced from this dossier alone.

**Splits and preprocessing.** Publish source-log version/hash, anonymized case manifest, split membership, preregistered seeds, dependency versions, discovery settings, and replay parameters. Publish handling of case IDs, lifecycle events, equal timestamps, incomplete cases, duplicates, missing events, and administrative events. Evaluate raw and cleaned logs separately; never delete deviations merely to improve fitness.

Use paired 80/20 case splits, grouped splits for correlated customer/order/template families, and temporal holdouts. Discovery, variant selection, filtering thresholds, and data-driven guard design use training only. If the holdout informed a model edit, obtain a fresh untouched evaluation set. Report split uncertainty and paired case differences.

**Attribute-stratified holdouts.** Enrich sourcing and correction impact independently where possible. Stratify external/internal/unknown, supplier recorded/missing, approval type, initial order, expedite, correction count, and affected sets. Publish outcomes and sizes per stratum. Do not exclude unknown-attribute cases; report uncertainty coverage instead of invented labels.

**Curated positive and negative suites.** Compliance and operations label cases independently of the models. Include B1–B8 with their data conditions and A1–A6. Add missing each mandatory check, early expedite before zero or one completed check, both approvals, no approval, release before approval, external supplier omission, repeats without correction, missing affected rechecks, and unnecessary unaffected rechecks. Keep unresolved policy cases separate. Measure false acceptance, false rejection, and indeterminate rates; abstention is not a correct rejection.

**Unseen combinations.** Withhold combinations while retaining their components in training: Technical-then-Credit plus Auto Approval; supplier plus auto; expedite-before-supplier; each selective correction set with both initial orders; and multiple rounds. First verify policy permission. Test compositional coverage, not indiscriminate novelty tolerance.

**Perturbations.** From verified complete cases, remove supplier, each mandatory check, correction, or approval one at a time. Inject duplicate mandatory events, duplicate corrections, repeated approvals, and ordering/timestamp errors. Retain original truth and perturbation identity. Compare visible deviations, classification, and evidence-recovery behavior. With deliberately untrusted completeness, the third alternative must withhold certification and explain why. Track whether “robustness” conceals missing mandatory work.

**Alignment costs and runtime.** Use identical conversions, algorithms, hardware, and cases. Sweep uniform visible costs and higher penalties for missing mandatory/supplier evidence; record selected alignments. Specify synchronization costs, invisible costs, and timeout treatment. Distinguish log from visible model moves. Publish medians, p95/p99, memory, and failures by length and deviation class. Costs may change explanations, never policy invariants.

**Soundness and reachability.** Check workflow-net soundness, both approvals’ reachability, absence of extra releases, and both check orders’ feasibility. Explore guard states plus tokens for each sourcing value and affected set. Ensure invisible skips cannot bypass supplier/rechecks; every operational state can complete or reach an exception route; legitimate work cannot deadlock. Net soundness alone does not establish composite soundness.

Bound correction counts for systematic generation and supplement with loop invariants: initial checks mandatory; only correction opens another round; affected outcomes block approval until rechecked; no excess checks within a round. Verify beyond the finite bound. Unknown attributes need a reachable resolution/escalation state, not a silent release route.

**Separate metrics and documented comprehension.** Report fitness, precision, token-based generalization, and simplicity separately on identified logs, with model sizes and defined duplicate counts. For the third alternative, PM4Py net metrics measure the backbone, not its guards. Also report guard count, uncertainty burden, valid-combination coverage, and change effort.

Use a counterbalanced study sampled by stakeholder role, with equal explanation/time budgets. Ask participants to predict acceptance, identify supplier conditions, explain B6/A5, and implement a small policy change. Measure accuracy, time, confidence calibration, and clarity; report role-specific outcomes and uncertainty. Show the guards, not just the diagram.

def seeded_split(log, seed, fraction=0.20):
    # Baseline case split; grouped/temporal tests need separate splitters.

def costed_alignment(trace, net, im, fm,
                     visible_cost=10000, mandatory_multiplier=1):
    # Experimental costs, not asserted library defaults.
    model_cost = {}
    for transition in net.transitions:
        if transition.label is None:
            model_cost[transition] = 1
        else:
            factor = (
                mandatory_multiplier
                if transition.label in MANDATORY else 1
            )
            model_cost[transition] = visible_cost * factor
    parameters = {
        alignments.Parameters.PARAM_TRACE_COST_FUNCTION:
            [visible_cost] * len(trace),
        alignments.Parameters.PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION: model_cost,
        alignments.Parameters.PARAM_SYNC_COST_FUNCTION:
            {transition: 0 for transition in net.transitions},
    }
    return alignments.apply_trace(
        trace, net, im, fm,
        parameters=parameters,
        variant=alignments.Variants.VERSION_STATE_EQUATION_A_STAR,
    )

def exact_visible_replay(diagnostic):
    # Default label-pair format. Test against the pinned release and options.
    if diagnostic is None or diagnostic.get('alignment') is None:
        return False
    for log_label, model_label in diagnostic['alignment']:
        if log_label == '>>' and model_label is None:
            continue  # Invisible routing.
        if log_label == model_label and log_label != '>>':
            continue
        return False  # Log move or visible model move.
    return True

def timed_alignments(log, net, im, fm):
    seconds, diagnostics = [], []
    for trace in log:
        start = time.perf_counter()
        diagnostic = costed_alignment(trace, net, im, fm)
        seconds.append(time.perf_counter() - start)
        diagnostics.append(diagnostic)
    return {
        'seconds_by_case': seconds,
        'median_seconds': statistics.median(seconds) if seconds else None,
        'diagnostics': diagnostics,
    }

# 10. Governance Recommendation

Retain S only as a labeled exploratory baseline if useful. Its presentation and downstream uses must expose optional mandatory checks, early expedition, and unsupported corrections. Retire it from enforcement, approval automation, authoritative process documentation, and unfiltered operational simulation.

Retain C as a frozen catalog of familiar variants and regression reference. Retire its claim to be the comprehensive audit model. A narrow allowlist might be defensible with approved exceptions and separate checks, but submitted C cannot independently authorize releases: conditional supplier enforcement and affected-set semantics are absent, and known valid behavior is rejected.

Select the guarded third alternative for development and shadow evaluation, not immediate deployment. Before deployment:

- Ratify correction handling, supplier deadline/internal interpretation, corrections after optional reviews, and approval eligibility/authority rules.
- Establish trusted sourcing/impact data, completeness checks, provenance, and an accountable uncertainty-resolution route.
- Version and enforce backbone and guards together; prove routing and invisible skips cannot bypass obligations.
- Pass curated safety/valid-combination suites, soundness and augmented-state checks, reproducibility review, and documented comprehension testing.
- Demonstrate acceptable runtime, false rejection, and manual-review load in a shadow period with independently adjudicated cases.
- Assign policy/activity owners, publish decision explanations, and monitor drift, missingness, new combinations, and corrections.

The recommendation composes established choices, enforces mandatory rules, and withholds decisions that evidence cannot support. It equates neither all unseen behavior with compliance nor all unseen behavior with misconduct.

# 11. Dissenting View

> The proposed hybrid may replace an obvious limitation with a harder-to-audit one. Model C visibly lists the paths it authorizes; the third alternative distributes meaning across a tree, event metadata, a Python checker, and a runtime enforcement layer. Any mismatch between those components could authorize a release the board never intended. Its net metrics would describe only an overapproximation, weakening continuity with existing audit reports. Missing sourcing and correction attributes are known facts, whereas successful enrichment, dependable completeness flags, and an efficient uncertainty queue remain promises. Operations may experience substantial delays if every selective correction requires metadata unavailable in current systems. A conservative allowlist with documented human exceptions could therefore be the safer interim choice. Its low holdout fitness would transparently identify exceptions instead of hiding uncertainty behind apparently general behavior. The probes also leave policy details unresolved: early supplier review, repeated correction rounds, and the validity of expedition after correction may require more governance work than the recommendation admits. Before introducing another executable representation, the board should repair logging and ratify policy. It should keep C for explicitly approved paths, retain S for investigation, and require human authorization for every exception until comparative evidence demonstrates that the hybrid improves safety and operational burden together.

Evidence that would change my decision includes an independently approved closed-world policy limiting production to C’s variants, separate supplier and correction-impact enforcement, reliable human exceptions, and acceptable measured operational burden. That could justify constrained interim C deployment, but would change the present policy interpretation and would not validate submitted C alone.

I would defer or redesign the third alternative if guard integration cannot be audited, enrichment is unreliable, composite reachability reveals deadlocks, or shadow operation produces excessive uncertainty or false rejection. Conversely, reliable case-level enrichment, zero accepted safety negatives, verified guard invariants, and strong valid-combination coverage would support deployment. New evidence should change a rule or decision explicitly; an improved aggregate score would not suffice.
