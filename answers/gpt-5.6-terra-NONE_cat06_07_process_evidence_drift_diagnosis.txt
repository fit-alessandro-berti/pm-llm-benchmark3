## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March: stable reference period.**  
The model appears operationally stable: AUROC is 0.81–0.82, Brier about 0.13, ECE about 0.02, data-quality failures are negligible, labels are mature, and variant mix is stable. V3 is intrinsically higher risk than V1/V2, but its breach rate is stable around 0.30–0.32. These months are the strongest candidate training/evaluation reference set.

**April: primarily a schema and feature-pipeline incident, not persuasive evidence of business deterioration.**  
The migration renamed the prediction anchor activity. Since scoring should occur immediately after `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, failure to recognize the new `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` can cause a prediction prefix to be absent, misanchored, or constructed after the wrong number of events. This is directly corroborated by:

- `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` jumping from 0.3% to 17%;
- missing queue feature jumping from 1% to 14%;
- unmapped/other variants jumping to 18%;
- AUROC falling to 0.67 and ECE to 0.110;
- mean predicted risk rising to 0.28 while observed breach remains 0.19.

The last point is important: the observed outcome rate did not materially change, while predictions and feature integrity did. That pattern is more consistent with pipeline corruption than a sudden business-process change. April should therefore be excluded from ordinary model-performance trend interpretation until features are rebuilt from raw events.

**May: partial technical recovery, but not proof that the incident is fully resolved.**  
The mapping patch reduced unknown activity to 2%, missing queue to 3%, and unmapped variants to 1%. AUROC improved from 0.67 to 0.78, while ECE improved from 0.110 to 0.050. However, metrics did not return to the January–March baseline, and the audit states that cached “number of events before diagnosis” was not fully backfilled. The right interpretation is partial recovery with possible residual feature contamination, not a clean model recovery.

**June: a real routing/process mix change, but not necessarily concept drift.**  
V2 rises from 34% in May to 52% in June due to the remote-routing policy. The aggregate breach rate falls from 0.18 to 0.15, while V2’s own breach rate falls to 0.09 and remote-fix timing remains stable. This is coherent with a mix shift toward a lower-risk, faster pathway.

The mean predicted risk remains 0.20, above the observed 0.15, and ECE rises to 0.060. That could be ordinary calibration misalignment due to changed prevalence or variant mix. It does not, by itself, establish concept drift. If the model still ranks cases within V2/V1/V3 similarly and only overall prevalence changed, reweighting or intercept recalibration may be sufficient after validating labels and features.

**July: early warning of genuine parts-flow deterioration.**  
The supplier issue begins on 10 July. The `Order Part -> Part Received` median jumps to 65 h, and V3 breach rate jumps to 0.46. Aggregate AUROC falls to 0.76. Although labels are 98% mature, this month is not fully mature and should be revisited at a later vintage. Still, the joint movement in V3 outcome rate and parts-edge delay makes a real operational change plausible.

**August: strong evidence of a process-performance shock, with likely model relationship failure in V3.**  
V3 share rises to 35%, V3 breach rate reaches 0.64, and the median parts wait doubles again to 130 h. Aggregate observed breach rate rises to 0.28 while mean predicted risk remains only 0.19. AUROC falls to 0.68, Brier rises to 0.197, and ECE reaches 0.120.

This is not adequately explained by simple prevalence change: the model is underpredicting and discriminating worse, especially plausibly among parts-dependent cases. There may be:

1. **process drift:** supplier delays alter the process;
2. **covariate drift:** the distribution of path/parts-delay-related features changes;
3. **concept drift:** the breach relationship conditional on the model’s available prefix/features changes because unobserved supplier reliability has become decisive.

The distinction requires conditional testing. The operational response should begin immediately regardless: mitigate the supplier bottleneck and ensure risk operations recognize V3/parts exposure.

**September: overlapping supplier/process deterioration and population shift.**  
V3 reaches 42% of cases, parts wait reaches 148 h, and V3 breach remains very high at 0.61. At the same time, Partner Z enters at 24% of volume, has a much more V3-heavy mix, and serves customers with longer travel distances and older devices. Observed breach is 0.45 for Partner Z versus 0.27 elsewhere, but Partner Z labels mature more slowly.

Thus, September cannot be attributed to one mechanism. The evidence supports genuine V3 deterioration independent of Partner Z because V3 had already worsened in July–August. But Partner Z plausibly adds a new population mix and potentially unmodeled risk factors. With only 82% label maturity, exact segment performance remains provisional.

**October: heavily confounded by outcome backlog; do not compare observed breach rate directly with prior mature cohorts.**  
The observed breach rate declines to 0.16 despite continued severe parts delays and V3 still at 41%. That direction is implausible as evidence of true recovery when the outcome API backlog disproportionately delays long-resolution paths—the same cases most likely to breach. With only 55% label maturity and a 52-day median label delay, October’s observed labels are selectively incomplete.

The October AUROC/Brier/ECE values are also not final because the evaluation set is censored and outcome availability is related to path length. October may contain continued process and population drift, but it is unsafe to infer either model recovery or deterioration magnitude from the currently available labels.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Immediate containment

1. **Quarantine April as a model-evaluation and training cohort** until raw events are normalized, deduplicated consistently, and all derived features are rebuilt.
2. **Treat May as conditionally usable only after recomputation.** It should not automatically be accepted as clean merely because the mapping patch was deployed.
3. **Do not use October labels for final model-performance decisions or supervised training.** Its 55% maturity and path-dependent API backlog make the labels informative-censored.
4. **Operationally flag V3/parts-dependent cases now.** The supplier delay is an operational threat even before model retraining is complete. A temporary business rule may be appropriate, such as additional monitoring or escalation for V3 cases associated with delayed parts, provided it is evaluated for workload and fairness effects.
5. **Publish dual dashboards:** one for operational process deterioration and one for mature-label model performance. Do not let one substitute for the other.

### Short-term recalibration

Recalibration is appropriate only after feature repair and after using reasonably mature labels.

- **June is the strongest candidate for recalibration analysis**, because the change is largely a known V2 mix shift with stable remote timing and apparently stable within-variant outcomes.
- A **global intercept-only recalibration** may be adequate if:
  - discrimination remains stable within V1/V2/V3;
  - calibration slope remains near one;
  - the main change is prevalence or variant composition.
- A **variant-specific calibration layer** is more appropriate if V1, V2, and V3 have stable ranking but different intercept shifts. For example, V3 may require a higher baseline risk after supplier deterioration, while V2 may need a lower baseline after the routing policy.
- Recalibration should be evaluated by fixed-vintage cohorts, not using provisional October labels.

Recalibration alone is not enough if V3 AUROC remains materially degraded after clean features and mature labels, or if calibration slope changes sharply. Those outcomes indicate feature inadequacy or changed conditional relationships rather than a simple base-rate shift.

### Feature repair

Feature repair is the priority before full retraining.

Required repairs:

- canonical activity mapping for both diagnosis labels;
- versioned event-label normalization;
- consistent duplicate handling for old and new source records;
- recomputation of all cached, sequence-derived, and diagnosis-anchor-dependent features;
- lineage checks proving no post-diagnosis information enters a diagnosis-time score;
- explicit representation of path/variant and current operational state;
- consideration of features such as part category, supplier/service-level status, historical supplier delay, travel distance/rurality, device age, and source/partner where governance permits.

The model should not rely on a raw activity label as an unversioned business concept. The business anchor should be canonicalized before prefix construction.

### Full retraining

Full retraining is justified if, after pipeline repair and maturity controls:

- V3 discrimination remains materially below baseline;
- conditional residual breach risk differs by month/supplier period;
- new features have predictive value and are available at the diagnosis-time decision point;
- the production population now includes a durable Partner Z-like segment not represented in the historical training population;
- a recalibration layer does not restore acceptable calibration and operational utility.

A retrained model should use **time-based splits**, not random splits, and should include a holdout covering a supplier-stress period if the business expects that condition to recur.

### Training data safety

| Cohort | Training status | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| January–March | Safe | Fully mature labels, stable pipeline, stable variant mix, good baseline metrics. |
| April | Unsafe pending reconstruction | Schema rename, wrong/missing anchors, duplicate inconsistency, corrupted derived features. |
| May | Unsafe/conditional | Mapping corrected but cached feature backfill incomplete; usable only after full raw-data recomputation and validation. |
| June | Conditionally safe | Labels mature and process mix change is real; include after verifying pipeline repair, but model with variant/mix explicitly. |
| July | Conditionally safe after final-vintage confirmation | 98% mature and genuine supplier shock may be useful training signal, but confirm missing labels and causal feature availability. |
| August | Conditionally safe after maturity and lineage checks | Strong deterioration signal, but only useful if labels are finalized and feature availability at prediction time is assured. |
| September | Unsafe for immediate training; later conditional | 82% mature, Partner Z shift, slower label maturity by source, overlapping mechanisms. Use only after maturity adjustment/final labels and stratified validation. |
| October | Unsafe | 55% mature, outcome API backlog is path-dependent, and current labels are biased. |

### Segment-specific or variant-specific model

A **variant-specific strategy is justified for investigation and may be justified in production** because V1, V2, and especially V3 have different baseline risk, process structure, and failure mechanisms. The strongest case is V3: supplier delay and parts availability are structurally relevant and may not be captured by a generic diagnosis-time model.

A practical sequence is:

1. retain a common model if it can include variant and interaction terms;
2. test whether separate V3 calibration/modeling materially improves calibrated utility;
3. deploy a V3-specific model only if it has sufficient volume, stable feature availability, and demonstrable out-of-time benefit;
4. do not create a Partner-Z-only model merely because observed breach is higher. First determine whether the difference is transported population mix, censoring, or a source-specific conditional process.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

The monitoring system should maintain separate but linked layers. Each metric should be versioned by model version, feature-pipeline version, event-mapping version, and outcome-extract version.

### A. Predictive-performance monitors

For mature, fixed-vintage cohorts:

- AUROC and PR-AUC;
- Brier score;
- top-decile/lift and alert precision-recall at operational thresholds;
- score distribution and risk-decile counts;
- observed-minus-predicted risk;
- threshold workload, escalation volume, and downstream action rates.

Break down by:

- case-start month;
- event-calendar month;
- V1/V2/V3;
- source/partner;
- geography/rurality;
- parts supplier/part category where available;
- label-maturity stratum.

### B. Calibration monitors

Track:

- reliability curves;
- ECE and adaptive ECE;
- calibration intercept and slope;
- Brier decomposition where feasible;
- calibration by risk band, variant, source, and month;
- calibration after standardizing to a reference variant mix.

A declining aggregate calibration metric should be interpreted only after checking label maturity and data quality.

### C. Label-maturity and censoring monitors

Track:

- percent final labels by cohort age;
- median, p75, p90, and p95 label delay;
- outcome API queue/backlog size;
- label availability by path length, variant, source, predicted risk, and observed elapsed time;
- number of evaluation-eligible cases missing outcomes;
- stability of cohort performance across fixed vintages.

**Dependency rule:** if label maturity is below the defined threshold, or outcome availability is associated with risk/path after conditioning on cohort age, label the performance evaluation “provisional.” This should trigger data/outcome investigation, not automatic retraining.

### D. Data-quality and lineage monitors

Track:

- canonical activity mapping coverage;
- old/new diagnosis-label occurrence;
- `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` share;
- unmapped variant share;
- missing queue feature and all critical-feature null rates;
- duplicate rate by source and timestamp window;
- number of raw diagnosis events versus feature rows versus prediction rows;
- scoring latency relative to canonical diagnosis event;
- feature freshness and feature-pipeline version;
- post-anchor leakage checks.

Alert thresholds should be especially strict for prediction-anchor activities and critical features.

### E. Process-variant monitors

Track:

- monthly variant mix;
- transition/edge frequencies;
- rare/unmapped pathway share;
- case-start versus event-calendar variant distributions;
- source-specific route allocation;
- V2 routing-policy adherence;
- V3 entry rate and parts-order prevalence.

Interpretation:

- **event-calendar cohorts** show what happened operationally during a month, including events from cases that started earlier;
- **case-start cohorts** show the process experience and eventual outcomes of cases entering in a given month.

Both are needed; they answer different questions.

### F. Performance-DFG monitors

For high-value edges and variants, track median and tail duration:

- `Order Part -> Part Received`;
- diagnosis to remote fix;
- diagnosis to dispatch;
- dispatch to repair;
- repair to closure.

Use p50 and p90/p95, not only medians, and retain edge-volume denominators. Alert on statistically material shifts relative to seasonally appropriate baselines and stratify by supplier, partner, geography, and part type.

### G. Population monitors

Track:

- source/partner proportions;
- rural/urban distribution;
- travel distance, device age, product type, region;
- supplier/part mix;
- feature distribution shift metrics;
- variant mix conditional on source;
- feature availability by source.

For Partner Z, monitor both raw outcomes and maturity-adjusted/transported outcomes before concluding that the model is biased or that the partner is intrinsically higher risk.

### H. Intervention and deployment monitors

Track:

- event-mapping and ETL deployments;
- duplicate-cleaning rule versions;
- model and calibration versions;
- policy changes, including remote routing;
- supplier incidents and recovery dates;
- partner onboarding;
- changes to promised-date policy or outcome API behavior;
- alert acceptance, override, and operational capacity.

Every drift alert should be joined to a deployment/change calendar.

### Alert dependencies and response logic

1. **Data-quality alarm first.**  
   If anchor mapping, missingness, duplicate rate, or prediction-eligibility reconciliation fails, suppress automated model-drift conclusions and open a pipeline incident.

2. **Label-maturity alarm second.**  
   If labels are immature or non-randomly missing, mark metrics provisional and defer retraining triggers. Continue operational process monitoring.

3. **Process-performance alarm independently.**  
   Severe changes in parts-edge duration or V3 rates should trigger operational action even when labels are immature.

4. **Predictive/calibration alarm after gates pass.**  
   Trigger recalibration/retraining review only when data quality is acceptable and labels are sufficiently mature or appropriately corrected.

5. **Population-shift alarm prompts stratification, not automatic segmentation.**  
   New-source drift should initiate transport and fairness/performance checks before a partner-specific model is considered.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Expected columns (adapt names as needed):
# case_id, activity, timestamp, case_start_timestamp
raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv", parse_dates=["timestamp", "case_start_timestamp"])

# Canonicalize the business event before prefix/feature/process extraction.
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
})

# Auditable duplicate policy: remove exact repeated event records, then retain the
# original row count for reconciliation. In production, prefer a stable event_id
# where available and log the chosen policy/version.
raw["event_row_id"] = range(len(raw))
dedupe_keys = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
events = (raw.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp", "event_row_id"])
             .drop_duplicates(subset=dedupe_keys, keep="first")
             .copy())

# PM4Py event-log dataframe formatting.
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp"
)

# Temporal features are extracted for audit/feature-lineage review, not to fit a model.
temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
    events,
    case_id_key="case_id",
    timestamp_key="timestamp"
)

monthly_rows = []
for month_start in pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-10-01", freq="MS"):
    month_end = month_start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)

    # Event-calendar cohort: events occurring during the calendar month.
    # Useful for operational workload and edge-performance monitoring.
    event_month = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        events, month_start, month_end, mode="events"
    )

    # Case-start cohort: all events for cases whose case start is in this month.
    # Useful for cohort outcomes and the full process paths of cases entering then.
    started_case_ids = events.loc[
        (events["case_start_timestamp"] >= month_start) &
        (events["case_start_timestamp"] < month_end),
        "case:concept:name"
    ].unique()
    start_month = events[events["case:concept:name"].isin(started_case_ids)].copy()

    for cohort_name, cohort_df in {
        "event_calendar": event_month,
        "case_start": start_month
    }.items():
        if cohort_df.empty:
            continue

        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(cohort_df)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
            cohort_df,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort_df)

        # PM4Py DFG keys are activity pairs; duration units depend on PM4Py settings,
        # so preserve raw values and document the installed PM4Py version/configuration.
        part_edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        remote_edge = ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")

        monthly_rows.append({
            "month": month_start.strftime("%Y-%m"),
            "cohort_interpretation": cohort_name,
            "n_cases": cohort_df["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
            "n_events": len(cohort_df),
            "n_variants": len(variants),
            "order_part_to_received_frequency": dfg.get(part_edge, 0),
            "order_part_to_received_performance": perf_dfg.get(part_edge),
            "diagnosis_to_remote_fix_frequency": dfg.get(remote_edge, 0),
            "diagnosis_to_remote_fix_performance": perf_dfg.get(remote_edge),
            "unknown_activity_share": (
                cohort_df["concept:name"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()
            ),
        })
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Why it is harmful | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retraining on immature October labels | The API backlog disproportionately hides longer-resolution paths, likely including breaches. Training on available labels would teach the model that difficult cases are safer than they are and could degrade future detection. | Exclude October from supervised training until fixed-vintage maturity criteria are met. Model label-observation probability and compare later outcome vintages before admitting September/October cohorts. |
| Calibrating around the April pipeline bug | An April-based recalibration could absorb incorrect prefixes, missing queue features, duplicated events, or wrong diagnosis anchors as if they were real risk changes. Once the pipeline is fixed, that calibration would become wrong. | Rebuild April–May features from normalized, deduplicated raw events first. Require source-to-score lineage reconciliation before using these months for calibration. |
| Ignoring genuine supplier-driven deterioration because October labels are immature | Label uncertainty does not erase the observed process evidence: parts waits and mature V3 breach rates worsen sharply in July–September. Waiting for perfect labels before acting could allow avoidable breaches. | Separate operational action from model action. Escalate supplier management, improve stock/routing decisions, and add temporary V3 safeguards while final labels mature. |
| Treating June’s aggregate breach reduction as model improvement | The lower aggregate breach rate is plausibly due to more V2 remote resolutions, not improved prediction. A model could have unchanged or worse within-variant calibration despite a favorable overall rate. | Always report variant-standardized and variant-stratified performance. Compare within-V1/V2/V3 calibration and discrimination before concluding improvement. |
| Treating Partner Z’s higher observed breach rate as evidence of unfair model behavior | Partner Z has more V3 cases, rural travel, older devices, and slower label maturity. A raw source comparison conflates population mix, process exposure, and censoring with model behavior. | Use maturity-comparable cohorts, transport/reweighting analyses, and calibrated performance by comparable path/covariate strata. Conduct fairness review on errors and intervention consequences, not only raw outcomes. |
| Treating Partner Z’s difference as “just mix” and missing a real segment gap | If the model lacks travel distance, device age, or partner-specific operating constraints, a residual source-specific calibration failure may persist even after variant adjustment. | Test conditional residuals after reweighting and include decision-time operational features where appropriate. Consider segment/variant-specific calibration only after sufficient sample size and stability checks. |
| Concluding all August–September degradation is concept drift | The apparent relationship change may actually be caused by missing supplier, parts, or geography features. Calling it pure concept drift can lead to unnecessary frequent retraining without fixing observability. | Run conditional same-path tests and feature-availability audits. Add timely operational covariates before deciding whether the conditional outcome mechanism truly changed. |
| Concluding all August–September degradation is only covariate drift | V3’s breach rate rose sharply alongside supplier delay, and AUROC fell. If conditional relationships changed, an intercept adjustment will underperform and create false assurance. | Compare calibration slope and discrimination within V3 and matched paths across time. Escalate to retraining when residual month/supplier effects persist after feature repair. |
| Using event-calendar evidence as if it were case-start outcome evidence | An event-calendar DFG includes cases that began earlier, while breach labels and predictions are tied to case cohorts. Mixing them can create misleading temporal attribution. | Maintain both cohort definitions and label each dashboard clearly. Use event-calendar cohorts for operational flow and case-start cohorts for prediction/outcome evaluation. |
| Overreacting with a broad model replacement during a localized V3 issue | A global retrain may sacrifice performance in stable V1/V2 populations and obscure the need for supplier operations fixes. | Evaluate V3 separately, use targeted calibration or a V3-specific model where justified, and preserve a stable baseline for unaffected variants.
