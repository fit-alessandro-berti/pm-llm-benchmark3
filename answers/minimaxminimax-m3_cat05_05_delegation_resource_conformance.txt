## 2. Rule Interpretation

- **RR1 (Drafter/approver separation).** The human who performed Draft_Change_Request must not perform Approve_Change for the same case. Aliases belonging to the same human (e.g., u_bk and b.koch) must be merged before evaluation. Service-account approvals cannot be evaluated unless session evidence maps to an authorized human.
- **RR2 (Implementer/verifier separation).** The human who performed Implement_Change must not perform Verify_Implementation for the same case. Same alias-merging rule.
- **RR3 (High-risk approval authority).** For HIGH-risk cases, Approve_Change must be performed by:
  1. the standing QM (Asha) acting in person, **or**
  2. a valid QM delegate acting under a delegation whose (a) effective interval covers the event timestamp, (b) scope covers the case scope and risk, (c) principal is absent or formally unavailable when required, and (d) the delegate did not subdelegate where prohibited. A later database record that post-dates the event does not retroactively prove that a delegation was in force at the event; the controlling evidence is the signed PDF, registration timestamp, and any system-of-record entry made contemporaneously.
- **RR4 (Emergency substitution).** A HIGH-risk emergency change may be implemented after approval by the IC or valid IC delegate **only if** the emergency flag and safety rationale are credible. Retrospective_QM_Review by a QM (or valid QM delegate) must occur within **two business days** of implementation. Business days are counted using the site calendar excluding weekends and declared holidays; the period 2026-08-15  2026-08-16 is a weekend with no holiday, so those days do not count.
- **RR5 (Identity evidence).** A service account is not a human identity; it supports conformance only with reliable session evidence (badge, FIDO, individual re-authentication) mapping the action to an authorized human. Aliases are normalized prior to SoD checks.
- **RR6 (Suspicion vs. proof).** Repeated pairs, reciprocal approvals, shared workstations, and concentrated handovers are indicators, not proof. Each requires supporting evidence before a finding of collusion.

**Action by a principal while on leave.** An event logged under the principal's user ID while the principal is on leave is not automatically invalid; it may reflect credential sharing, an authorized off-leave intervention, or a logging artifact. Authority attaches to the human, not the session, so RR3 is met only if the human is confirmed to have acted and was formally available when required by the delegation. In CHG-504, Asha's principal status makes the action authorized regardless of her leave status (she is the delegating principal, not the delegate); however, the leave conflict raises a secondary integrity question.

**Later database entry.** A delegation recorded in the QMS database after the event cannot prove that the delegation existed at the event timestamp. The signed PDF and any contemporaneous system record control. D-QM-01's PDF was signed 2026-08-09 18:00; the DB record at 2026-08-12 09:15 is corroborative but was created during a migration and is weaker evidence of pre-existence.

## 4. Collusion and Alternative-Explanation Analysis

**Repeated pair: Clara (drafter)  Ben (approver).** 18 of 23 HIGH-risk production changes in 60 days.
- *Benign explanation:* Clara is the cell Process Engineer and Ben is the cell Quality Engineer under a valid standing QM delegation (D-QM-01); the pair is the natural control structure for that cell. Concentration reflects cell ownership, not coordination.
- *Adverse explanation:* The pair has internalized a rubber-stamp dynamic; Ben approves without independent challenge because Clara drafts the controls he is meant to challenge.
- *Discriminating evidence:* approval-comment substance (length, specificity, deviations raised), rework rate on Clara's drafts, sampling of rejected drafts, independence markers (does Ben ever reject or require changes?).

**Reciprocal pattern: Ben drafts  Clara approves (LOW-risk training).**
- *Benign explanation:* Cross-training and load balancing; LOW-risk approvals are routine and rotation across the team is normal.
- *Adverse explanation:* Reciprocal logrolling — each approves the other's drafts to ensure their own pass; classic collusion signature.
- *Discriminating evidence:* whether approval comments differ in depth when roles are reversed, whether either party ever rejects the other, time-to-approval distribution, and any external audit findings on training-change quality.

**Shared workstation WS-DM-04 (CHG-503 svc_qms).**
- *Benign explanation:* Diego is the implementer and Eva uses the same physical workstation as a generic verifier station; lack of re-authentication is a usability oversight, not misconduct.
- *Adverse explanation:* Diego performed the verification under his own account to bypass RR2 (he implemented CHG-503), and the service account masked the identity.
- *Discriminating evidence:* individual re-authentication log, biometric binding, USB-token presence, application-level session binding to a named verifier, CCTV.

**Absence conflicts.**
- *Ben on sick leave but u_bk used (CHG-506):* Benign — credential reuse by a colleague covering approvals under D-QM-01. Adverse — credential compromise or Ben acting covertly from leave. *Discriminator:* token/FIDO presence, IP geolocation consistency, keystroke/cadence biometrics.
- *Asha on leave but u_as VPN session (08-13):* Benign — Asha handled one urgent case as permitted. Adverse — credential sharing by a delegate to extend Asha's apparent authority. *Discriminator:* Asha's device fingerprint, simultaneous second-factor, statement of which case she handled.

**Service-account architecture.** svc_qms is a shared identity without individual re-authentication. This is itself an RR5 control failure regardless of intent: any conformance claim under svc_qms is structurally weak.

**Network signals (manual interpretation).** A ClaraBen handover network will dominate because Clara drafts and Ben approves. Working-together edges will be dense for {Clara, Ben, Diego, Eva} on production changes; an unusual density on emergency cases (CHG-505/510) involving Clara and Farid/Clara is a red flag for emergency-channel abuse.

## 6. Control Improvements

1. **Delegation creation.** Require a four-eyes creation step (requester  authorizer) and a unique delegation ID stored in the system of record **before** the effective start. Reject any delegation whose DB record post-dates its effective start unless a contemporaneous audit trail (signed PDF + workflow log) is attached.
2. **Effective-time recording.** Use a tamper-evident, append-only delegation log (WORM storage) with cryptographic chaining; forbid migrations that rewrite `created_at` without preserving the original. Reconcile DB record with PDF timestamps nightly.
3. **Principal availability.** Automate calendar integration: a delegation is only "armed" when the principal's calendar shows Out-of-Office or a formal unavailability flag; weekend/holiday coverage requires a named on-call principal.
4. **Alias management.** Maintain a single canonical human ID per person; all event-log resource strings must resolve to that ID via a versioned alias map that is itself change-controlled and reviewed quarterly.
5. **Individual authentication on shared terminals.** Mandate per-session re-authentication (FIDO2 + biometric) on any shared workstation; bind each event to the authenticated principal at write time; retain 90 days of CCTV at all shared verification stations.
6. **Emergency review (RR4).** Auto-generate a Retrospective_QM_Review task with a hard SLA timer at implementation; block case closure until completed within two business days; escalate breach to the Site Director with audit log.
7. **Pair rotation.** Institute a formal pair-rotation policy for high-risk drafter/approver pairs (max 60-day continuous pairing; min 3 active approvers per cell); require a sampling audit of approval-comment quality per pair.
8. **Network monitoring.** Continuously compute handover and working-together densities; alert on (a) any single approver handling >70% of a drafter's HIGH-risk cases, (b) reciprocal drafter/approver swaps in the same population, (c) service-account verifications on shared workstations without re-authentication.
9. **Evidential retention.** Retain signed PDFs, workflow logs, badge/FIDO logs, and CCTV for 7 years; require time-sync (NTP) attestation for all authentication sources.
10. **Subdelegation discipline.** Enforce machine-checkable `allows_subdelegation = False`; any attempt to create a subdelegation should be rejected at the workflow layer, not merely documented as prohibited.
