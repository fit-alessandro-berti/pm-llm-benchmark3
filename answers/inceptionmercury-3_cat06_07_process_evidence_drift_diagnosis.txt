## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March** are the baseline. The model is stable: AUROC ~0.81, ECE ~0.025, and variant mix is relatively flat. There is no evidence of label delay or process change.

**April** is primarily a **data-pipeline failure**, not a process or concept drift. Labels are 100% mature, yet mean predicted risk jumps to 0.28 while observed breach remains 0.19. The 17% unknown-activity share and 14% missing queue feature indicate that prediction prefixes were built from the wrong event or not built at all. The appropriate falsifier is simple: after normalizing the renamed diagnosis label, removing duplicates, and recomputing features, April’s metrics should return to near-baseline.

**May** is a partial recovery. The mapping patch reduced unknown activity to 2%, but some derived features were not recomputed and duplicate-event removal was not applied retroactively. The metrics improve but do not fully normalize. May should be treated as a cleanup month, not a new process regime.

**June** is a **real process-mix change** caused by the remote-routing policy. V2 usage rises to 52%, lowering the overall observed breach rate to 0.15. The per-variant rates remain close to baseline: V2 0.09, V1 0.16, V3 0.35. The model’s mean prediction stays near 0.20 because it is not adjusting for the new process composition. That is not concept drift; it is covariate/process drift.

**July** is the early stage of the **supplier-driven process/concept shift**. The `Order Part -> Part Received` edge time rises to 65h, and the matured V3 breach rate rises to 0.46. Label maturity is still 98%, so censoring is minor. This is the beginning of genuine workflow deterioration, especially for parts-dependent cases.

**August** is where the deterioration becomes severe. V3 share increases to 35%, and the matured V3 breach rate reaches 0.64. The overall observed breach rate is 0.28, and the model’s AUROC drops to 0.68. Although label maturity is 93%, censoring cannot explain the sharp increase; if anything, full maturity would raise the observed breach rate further. August is best explained by overlapping **process drift and concept drift** caused by the supplier failure.

**September** is where **population shift** joins the picture. Partner Z is onboarded and accounts for 24% of starts, with 58% of its cases routed through V3. V3 share rises to 42%. Partner Z has higher observed breach rates, but its labels mature more slowly. The model’s deterioration is therefore a combination of supplier-related concept/process drift and a new high-risk population that was not represented in training.

**October** is heavily confounded by **label delay and outcome-API backlog**. Only 55% of labels are mature, median label delay is 52d, and the observed breach rate of 0.16 is not comparable with mature months. Process evidence still shows severe parts delays (155h on `Order Part -> Part Received`), and Partner Z remains at 27% of starts. October should not be interpreted as an improvement or as an overprediction problem until labels mature.

The months are not one homogeneous failure: April is a pipeline artifact, June is a policy-driven mix shift, August–September involve real process/concept/population shifts, and October is primarily a measurement/label-maturity problem.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

### 1. Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting
For each case-start month, plot the cumulative breach rate as a function of days since case start. Compare months at fixed horizons such as 60, 90, and 120 days, rather than at reporting cutoff.

- **If label delay/censoring is dominant:** October and September will show lower early breach rates that rise sharply as labels mature. Partner Z and V3 cases will show slower maturity curves. The apparent gap between predicted and observed risk will shrink after waiting.
- **If real concept drift is dominant:** The terminal matured breach rate for August and September will remain higher than earlier months even after fixing the vintage. August’s V3 rate will not revert to 0.35.

### 2. Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning
Rebuild the event log from raw `field_service_events.csv`, map `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` back to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, remove exact and near-duplicate events, and recompute all derived features. Then rerun prediction and evaluation for April/May.

- **If April was a pipeline failure:** April’s mean predicted risk should move from ~0.28 toward ~0.19, missing queue/unknown activity should drop, and AUROC/ECE should return toward baseline.
- **If concept drift or covariate drift was real:** The cleaned features will not restore performance, and the drift will persist after repair.

### 3. Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month
Compute AUROC, Brier score, ECE, and reliability diagrams separately by month, V1/V2/V3, and Partner Z/non-Partner-Z, using only mature labels.

- **If June’s issue is process-mix drift:** Overall calibration degrades, but per-variant calibration remains good. The model is mis-specified for the new mix, not for the underlying relationships.
- **If supplier-driven concept drift exists:** The V3 stratum will show systematically degraded calibration and discrimination starting in July/August, while V1 and V2 remain stable.
- **If Partner Z is a population shift:** Partner Z may show different calibration until rural/device-age features are added, but reweighting should explain much of the difference.

### 4. Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges
Discover DFGs and performance DFGs per month and per variant. Compare edge frequencies and median times, especially `Order Part -> Part Received` and `Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix`.

- **If process drift is the cause:** The variant distribution changes, and the `Order Part -> Part Received` edge duration increases. The process topology may show new or more frequent parts-dependent paths.
- **If the April incident is a pipeline failure:** April’s DFG will contain `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, duplicated events, or disconnected paths that disappear after normalization.
- **If the problem is concept drift rather than process drift:** The same path will produce a higher probability of breach even when the process duration is controlled.

### 5. Tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed
Fit a logistic or Cox model on mature labels with variant, known covariates, month, and month × variant interactions. Compare coefficients over time.

- **If there is no concept drift:** Month effects should disappear once variant and covariate mix are controlled.
- **If supplier-driven concept drift exists:** The V3 month interaction will become significant in July/August, and the effect will remain after adjusting for `Order Part -> Part Received` delay.
- **If label delay confounds:** Apparent month effects will weaken as labels mature and censoring is handled.

### 6. Population reweighting or transport-style checks for Partner Z
Use entropy balancing or propensity-score weights to reweight non-Partner-Z September/October cases to match Partner Z’s covariate distribution. Compare mature breach rates.

- **If Partner Z is a pure population shift:** The breach-rate difference will shrink substantially after reweighting.
- **If Partner Z has a genuine different risk relationship:** A large difference will remain after reweighting, suggesting additional features or a segment-specific effect.
- **If labels are immature:** Reweighting on incomplete labels will be unstable; repeat the test after full label maturity.

### 7. Feature missingness and lineage audits
Track missingness by feature, month, and source. Audit where every derived feature came from, especially after the April rename and May patch.

- **If the pipeline is the problem:** Missingness should concentrate in April and early May, and the repaired pipeline should eliminate it.
- **If new processes/populations are the problem:** Missingness will appear in genuinely new variables such as rural distance or device age, requiring new data collection rather than backfill.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Stage 0: Immediate containment
Do not retrain on immature labels. Do not compare October’s observed breach rate to mature months. Freeze automatic evaluation and retraining triggers until label maturity is verified. If predictions are needed now, apply a provisional variant-level correction using the most recent matured cohort, but mark the model as not fully calibrated for August–October.

### Stage 1: Short-term recalibration
After normalizing activity labels and cleaning duplicates, refit only the calibration layer using clean, mature data from January–July. Use variant-specific calibrators because V1/V2/V3 have different base rates. Validate on March, June, and July held-out matured cases. Do not include April until its reconstructed features are verified.

### Stage 2: Feature repair
Recompute all derived features affected by the April rename, duplicate events, and incomplete backfill. Add process-variant labels, partner/source, rural travel distance, device age, supplier delivery performance, and queue completeness. Until these features are repaired, model evaluation on April and May is unreliable.

### Stage 3: Full retraining
Retrain only when August and September cohorts reach sufficient label maturity and the outcome-API backlog is cleared. Use fixed-vintage mature labels, not cutoff labels. Consider a survival or interval-censored objective for cases whose labels are still maturing.

**Safe training months now:** January, February, March, and conditionally June/July once per-variant label maturity is confirmed.  
**Unsafe training months now:** April until repaired; August–October until labels mature and the outcome backlog is resolved.  
**May:** usable only after the feature-lineage audit and recomputation are complete and residual missingness is removed.

A **variant-specific or segment-specific component is justified**. V3 has a materially different risk profile and a different process path. Partner Z adds a new population with different covariates and label maturity. Rather than immediately building fully separate models, use a shared model with variant × month interactions and Partner-Z features, and consider a dedicated V3 model if the V3 data volume and drift are sustained.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

The monitoring system should separate measurement from action.

- **Predictive monitor:** tracks predicted-risk distribution, mean predicted risk, PSI, AUROC, Brier, and ECE on matured vintages only.
- **Calibration monitor:** evaluates reliability diagrams by month, variant, and source, but only when the label-maturity threshold is met.
- **Label-maturity monitor:** tracks median label delay, percentage matured, maturity curves by cohort, and outcome-API backlog.
- **Data-quality monitor:** tracks unknown-activity share, missing feature share, duplicate-event rate, schema mapping failures, and backfill completeness.
- **Process-variant monitor:** tracks V1/V2/V3/unmapped shares and routing-policy flags.
- **Performance-DFG monitor:** tracks median and percentile edge times, especially `Order Part -> Part Received` and diagnosis-to-remote-fix.
- **Population monitor:** tracks Partner Z share, rural/device-age covariate balance, and segment-specific label maturity.
- **Intervention/deployment monitor:** records model version, feature version, calibration version, mapping patches, policy changes, and outcome-definition changes.

**Alert dependencies:**

- A **label-delay alarm** suppresses predictive, AUROC, Brier, and calibration alarms. It must not trigger retraining; it triggers waiting, label-backlog resolution, and maturity-curve review.
- A **data-quality alarm** quarantines affected months and triggers feature repair, not model retraining.
- A **process-variant alarm** triggers stratified calibration and DFG analysis, but does not automatically trigger retraining.
- A **population alarm** triggers reweighting and feature-engineering review, not immediate retraining.
- Retraining is allowed only when data quality is clean, labels are mature, and the stratified performance degradation is sustained after all those checks.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

DIAG_CANONICAL = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
DIAG_RENAMED = "Diagnostic Assessment Completed"
UNKNOWN = "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY"

def clean_events(path):
    df = pd.read_csv(path, parse_dates=["time", "start_time"])
    # Normalize renamed diagnosis activity before any feature extraction.
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace({DIAG_RENAMED: DIAG_CANONICAL})

    # Duplicate handling: exact duplicates are dropped; for migration duplicates,
    # prefer the older source and keep the earliest timestamp.
    if "source_priority" not in df.columns:
        df["source_priority"] = df.get("source", "old").map(
            {"old": 0, "new": 1}).fillna(2)
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "time", "source_priority"])
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity", "time"], keep="first")
    return df

def monthly_process_evidence(path, months):
    raw = clean_events(path)
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="time",
        start_timestamp_key="start_time",
    )

    summaries = []
    for start, end in months:
        # Event-calendar cohort: what happened in this calendar month.
        cal_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode="events")
        calendar_events = len(cal_log)
        cal_df = raw[(raw["time"] >= start) & (raw["time"] < end)]
        cal_unknown = (cal_df["activity"] == UNKNOWN).mean()
        cal_missing_queue = (
            cal_df["queue"].isna().mean() if "queue" in cal_df else None
        )

        # Case-start cohort: full traces whose first event occurred in this month.
        # This is the correct cohort for case-level outcome analysis.
        cohort = pm4py.filter_log(
            lambda trace: start <= min(e["time:timestamp"] for e in trace) < end,
            log,
        )

        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort)
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(cohort)
        perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cohort)
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
            raw,
            case_id="case_id",
            activity_key="activity",
            timestamp_key="time",
        )

        summaries.append({
            "month": start.strftime("%Y-%m"),
            "event_calendar_events": calendar_events,
            "case_start_cohort_cases": len(cohort),
            "unknown_activity_share": cal_unknown,
            "missing_queue_share": cal_missing_queue,
            "variant_counts": {k: v for k, v in variants.items()},
            "dfg": dfg,
            "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
            "temporal_features": temporal,
        })

    return summaries
```

The code deliberately does not fit a model. It produces auditable monthly summaries: event-calendar evidence for workload/process state, and case-start cohorts for case-level evaluation. The two cohorts have different interpretations: event-calendar reflects current operations, while case-start is the correct unit for outcome prediction and label-maturity analysis.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

### 1. Retraining on immature labels
If we retrain on October or September cutoff labels, the model will learn artificially low breach rates, especially for Partner Z and V3 cases, whose labels mature more slowly. This could produce confident under-predictions in the next months.

**Mitigation:** Enforce a minimum label-maturity threshold before any training or evaluation. Use fixed-vintage backtests and survival-style censoring corrections. Do not use October data until the outcome-API backlog is cleared and maturity is verified.

### 2. Calibrating around a pipeline bug
If April’s corrupted features are used to recalibrate the model, the calibration may look good under the bug but break after the activity label is normalized and features are recomputed.

**Mitigation:** Repair the pipeline first. Exclude April and possibly May from calibration until lineage and feature recomputation are verified. Validate calibration on a clean holdout month.

### 3. Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift
If all August–September degradation is attributed to data quality, label delay, or population shift, the V3 model will remain dangerously under-predicting while parts delays continue.

**Mitigation:** Use process evidence such as the `Order Part -> Part Received` edge time. Track matured V3 breach rates separately from overall rates. Retrain with supplier-delay variables and variant interactions once labels mature.

### 4. Attributing Partner Z’s population shift to model bias or unfairness without stratified evidence
Partner Z has higher observed breach rates, but also slower labels, more V3 cases, and different covariates. If we act without reweighting or full label maturity, we may either unfairly penalize Partner Z or miss a real operational problem.

**Mitigation:** Stratify by variant and source, wait for label maturity, and use reweighting/matching before making fairness or policy conclusions. Add Partner Z, rural, and device-age features before judging the model.

### 5. Treating October’s low observed breach rate as real improvement
October’s observed breach rate of 0.16 looks like a recovery, but only 55% of labels are mature and longer-resolution cases are disproportionately delayed. Acting on that number could cause a premature deployment or inappropriate risk appetite.

**Mitigation:** Suppress October scorecards until labels mature. Report label maturity alongside breach rate and require maturity-adjusted comparisons for all months.
