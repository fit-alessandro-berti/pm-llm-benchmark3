# 1. Assumptions and Interpretation

1. **Event granularity and the meaning of "before".** Every activity contributes exactly one event, recorded at completion time (narrative point 11). Therefore "X may start only after Y is complete" is representable only as "the completion event of Y precedes the completion event of X". Both models enforce precedence on completion events; neither can observe or constrain start times. A consequence I accept explicitly: a case in which `Plan Verification` physically started before `Contain Material` finished but completed afterwards is indistinguishable from a compliant case in both models.

2. **"May overlap" = absence of an order constraint on the observable language.** Structurally I use genuine concurrency operators (process-tree `+`, POWL unordered nodes, i.e. AND-blocks in the converted Petri net), so the underlying semantics is true concurrency. But because only completions are recorded, the observable language of a concurrent block is the set of all interleavings of its branches. So "Contain Material and Analyze Root Cause may overlap" is realised as "both orders of their completion events are admitted", and no trace-level test can distinguish true parallelism from a free choice of sequence. Every claim I make about admitted/excluded behaviour below is a claim about interleaving-based trace languages.

3. **Low-risk / high-risk choice.** Represented as an exclusive choice between two alternative sub-models: `XOR( 'Supervisor Review', AND('Quality Review','Regulatory Review') )`. The data attribute "risk class" is not modelled; the choice is silent and non-deterministic in the control-flow model. The high-risk branch is an unordered pair, so both completion orders of the two reviews are admitted, and the branch is complete only when both reviews are complete. Mixing routes (e.g. `Supervisor Review` together with `Quality Review`) is excluded by construction — this is a modelled business rule, not an accident.

4. **Optional supplier consultation.** Represented as `XOR( 'Consult Supplier', tau )` — a choice between doing it once and a silent skip. This gives exactly "zero or one occurrence"; because the node is not inside a loop, two occurrences are structurally impossible. The selection criterion ("case data not represented in the control-flow model") is deliberately left as unguarded non-determinism, as the narrative instructs.

5. **Abstraction of the inspection outcome.** No `Inspection Passed`/`Inspection Failed` labels exist, so the outcome is encoded in the loop's exit decision: `LOOP( SEQ('Execute Trial','Inspect Trial'), 'Adjust Parameters' )`. In pm4py both for process trees and for POWL, `*(A, B)` means: execute `A`; then either leave the loop, or execute `B` and then `A` again, repeated. Hence the admitted cycle language is exactly `ET IT (AP ET IT)*`. Reading "leave the loop" as "the inspection passed" and "execute the redo part" as "the inspection failed" gives three properties for free: at least one trial cycle occurs; `Adjust Parameters` occurs only strictly between two inspections; and `Adjust Parameters` can never follow the passing (i.e. last) `Inspect Trial`. The iteration count is unbounded, as the narrative implies ("this repeats until an inspection passes").

6. **Duplicate visible labels are not permitted.** In both models each of the 14 labels occurs exactly once as a leaf; the only invisible leaf is the single `tau` skipping `Consult Supplier`. Rationale: duplicated visible labels make alignment-based diagnostics ambiguous (a deviation can be "explained" by the wrong copy), they inflate the state space, and in a compliance setting they let a reviewer believe an obligation was discharged in one place when the log evidence belongs to another. As shown in §3 and §6, a duplication-based repair of the process tree would also not actually buy exactness: a process tree cannot enforce "exactly one of two distant copies fires", so an ordering loss would be traded for a *cardinality* loss (route or supplier consultation occurring zero or two times), which is worse.

7. **Approximation policy for the process tree: conservative under-approximation.** The dependency graph is not block-structured, so no process tree over these labels (without duplication) has exactly the intended language. I choose `L(tree) ⊆ L(intended)`: the tree admits fewer interleavings than intended but never admits an unsafe one. Justification for a regulated material-deviation process: a model used for compliance monitoring, conformance checking or as a normative reference must never certify a trace in which, say, material is released before the regulatory review completed. Excluded-but-legal orderings surface as non-fitting traces that a human can triage and whitelist; admitted-but-illegal orderings surface as nothing at all. I therefore reject the permissive over-approximation `-> ( RD, +( ->(CM,PV), ->(ARC,DCA), route, supplier, trial ), RM, AC )`, which would admit `Execute Trial` before `Plan Verification` and reviews before `Analyze Root Cause` — precisely the safety-relevant dependencies.

8. **Cardinalities.** `Register Deviation`, `Contain Material`, `Analyze Root Cause`, `Plan Verification`, `Design Corrective Action`, `Release Material` and `Archive Case` occur exactly once; `Consult Supplier` at most once; the selected route's activities exactly once each; `Execute Trial`/`Inspect Trial` at least once. Because every other activity precedes `Release Material` transitively, `Release Material` is necessarily the penultimate and `Archive Case` the last event of every accepted trace. There is no abandon/reject path — the narrative provides none, and both models are therefore easy-sound (every case can and must reach release).

9. **The route decision point is not an event.** "The risk route is selected after Analyze Root Cause" is modelled as: no route activity may complete before `Analyze Root Cause` completed. The selection itself leaves no trace.

10. **POWL transitivity.** `StrictPartialOrder` requires an irreflexive *and transitive* relation (`validate_partial_orders()` raises otherwise). I add the covering (Hasse) edges explicitly with `.order.add_edge(...)` because those are the modelling decisions, then close the relation with `order.add_transitive_edges()`. The closure adds no behaviour; it only turns the covering relation into a syntactically valid strict partial order.

11. **Out of scope in both models.** Data/guard conditions, resources, timing, rework of already-released material, and any distinction between start and completion. Both models are activity-based, as instructed.

The relation on the non-loop nodes (D3–D16, D20–D23) is a partial order whose covering graph is *not* series-parallel: `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action` have different single predecessors and a common successor, while the approval route and the supplier consultation bypass that join and re-synchronise only at `Release Material`. This is the classic non-block-structured "interleaved bypass" pattern that no process tree can capture exactly.

# 3. PM4Py Process-Tree Model

## 3.1 Compact expression

```
-> ( 'Register Deviation',
     + ( -> ( 'Contain Material', 'Plan Verification' ),
         -> ( 'Analyze Root Cause', 'Design Corrective Action' ) ),
     + ( * ( -> ( 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' ), 'Adjust Parameters' ),
         X ( 'Supervisor Review', + ( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) ),
         X ( 'Consult Supplier', tau ) ),
     'Release Material',
     'Archive Case' )
```

Mapping to the narrative, block by block:

- `'Register Deviation'` first and `'Archive Case'` last in the root sequence: D1, D2, D23.
- First `+`: the two independent work streams. Left branch `-> ( CM, PV )` encodes D3+D6, right branch `-> ( ARC, DCA )` encodes D4+D7, and the `+` encodes D5 (free interleaving of the two streams).
- The boundary between the first and the second `+` is an AND-join on `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action`. This is exactly the synchronisation D14+D15 that the trial needs — and it is also the source of the approximation, because the route and the supplier consultation are pushed behind the same join (see §3.4).
- Second `+`: the three obligations that release must synchronise (D20, D21, D22), mutually unordered, so approvals and supplier consultation may interleave with the trial cycle (D16, D13 in part).
- `* ( -> ( ET, IT ), AP )` is the trial cycle: D17 inside the body, D18 through the redo part, D19 for free since `Adjust Parameters` is only reachable on the redo path.
- `X ( SR, + ( QR, RegR ) )` is the risk route: D8 (transitively, via the position after `Analyze Root Cause`), D9 for the exclusive choice and D10 for the concurrent pair.
- `X ( CS, tau )` is the optional consultation: D12, and — because the node sits before `Release Material` in the root sequence — D22.

## 3.2 Self-contained construction code

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator


def leaf(label, parent=None):
    """Visible leaf, or a silent (tau) leaf when label is None."""
    node = ProcessTree(operator=None, parent=parent, label=label)
    if parent is not None:
        parent.children.append(node)      # explicit child registration
    return node


def op(operator, parent=None):
    """Operator node; parent link and children list handled explicitly."""
    node = ProcessTree(operator=operator, parent=parent)
    if parent is not None:
        parent.children.append(node)
    return node


# root: Register Deviation -> [work streams] -> [obligations] -> Release -> Archive
tree = op(Operator.SEQUENCE)

leaf("Register Deviation", tree)

# Block 1: containment stream || analysis stream
streams = op(Operator.PARALLEL, tree)

containment = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, streams)
leaf("Contain Material", containment)
leaf("Plan Verification", containment)

analysis = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, streams)
leaf("Analyze Root Cause", analysis)
leaf("Design Corrective Action", analysis)

# Block 2: trial cycle || approval route || optional supplier consultation
obligations = op(Operator.PARALLEL, tree)

trial_loop = op(Operator.LOOP, obligations)
trial_body = op(Operator.SEQUENCE, trial_loop)
leaf("Execute Trial", trial_body)
leaf("Inspect Trial", trial_body)
leaf("Adjust Parameters", trial_loop)      # redo part: reached only on a failed inspection

route = op(Operator.XOR, obligations)
leaf("Supervisor Review", route)            # low-risk route
high_risk = op(Operator.PARALLEL, route)    # high-risk route
leaf("Quality Review", high_risk)
leaf("Regulatory Review", high_risk)

supplier = op(Operator.XOR, obligations)
leaf("Consult Supplier", supplier)
leaf(None, supplier)                        # tau = supplier not involved

leaf("Release Material", tree)
leaf("Archive Case", tree)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    import pm4py

    print(tree)

    # the constructed object and the parsed expression are the same tree
    parsed = pm4py.parse_process_tree(
        "-> ( 'Register Deviation', "
        "+ ( -> ( 'Contain Material', 'Plan Verification' ), "
        "-> ( 'Analyze Root Cause', 'Design Corrective Action' ) ), "
        "+ ( * ( -> ( 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' ), 'Adjust Parameters' ), "
        "X ( 'Supervisor Review', + ( 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' ) ), "
        "X ( 'Consult Supplier', tau ) ), "
        "'Release Material', 'Archive Case' )"
    )
    assert str(tree) == str(parsed)

    def check_parents(node):
        for child in node.children:
            assert child.parent is node
            check_parents(child)

    check_parents(tree)
    print("parent/child links consistent")
```

## 3.3 Parent/child handling

Every node is created with the `parent=` keyword *and* appended to `parent.children`; pm4py does not maintain the inverse link automatically, so both directions are set explicitly. The recursive `check_parents` assertion in the code confirms that no child points at the wrong parent, which matters because conversion, printing and bottom-up utilities in pm4py traverse both directions. The two `X` nodes have exactly two children each; the `*` node has exactly two children (body, redo), as pm4py's binary-loop semantics requires.

## 3.4 What the tree cannot represent exactly

The tree is a **strict under-approximation**, and the loss is confined to one class of behaviour, which I can characterise exactly: *an intended trace is accepted by the tree if and only if no approval-route activity and no `Consult Supplier` event occurs before both `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action` have completed.* The block boundary that correctly implements the trial's join (D14, D15) unavoidably also gates the route (D8) and the consultation (D11), because a process tree can only synchronise whole subtrees. Concretely, the intended-but-rejected requirements are: D8 (the route needs only `Analyze Root Cause`, so `Supervisor Review` may legally complete while containment is still running), D11 and D13 (the consultation may legally overlap `Design Corrective Action` and `Plan Verification`), and the part of D16 that says approvals need not wait for the trial's *predecessors* — note that the part of D16 about overlapping the trial *itself* is preserved, since the route and the trial cycle sit in the same `+`. I measured the loss: over 300 randomly generated intended interleavings (both risk routes, with and without supplier, one to three trial iterations), the tree accepted 49 and the characterisation above predicted acceptance/rejection with zero mismatches. Nothing outside this class is lost, and — verified by playing out 400 traces and checking each against an independently coded acceptor for rules 1–10 — nothing unintended is admitted, apart from traces truncated by the playout length limit, which is an artefact of playout rather than of the model.

Three further limitations are shared with the POWL model and are inherent to activity-based modelling, not to the tree: the risk class, the supplier flag and the inspection result are unguarded non-deterministic choices (D24), so the model cannot express *which* cases take which branch; the completion-only abstraction means "may start only after" is enforced only on completion events; and the loop admits arbitrarily many iterations, so it cannot express a business cap on rework.

Two alternatives I considered and rejected. The permissive variant `-> ( RD, +( ->(CM,PV), ->(ARC,DCA), route, supplier, trial ), RM, AC )` restores all lost interleavings but admits `Execute Trial` before `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action` — it destroys D14/D15, the safety-critical join, so it trades a benign loss for a dangerous gain. The duplication variant, which places one skippable copy of the route and the consultation inside the analysis branch of block 1 and another skippable copy in block 2, does restore the orderings, but a process tree cannot enforce that exactly one of the two copies fires: each copy is independently skippable, so the model would admit zero or two `Supervisor Review` events. This turns an ordering under-approximation into a cardinality over-approximation, plus label ambiguity in alignments, so it is strictly worse for this process.

# 4. PM4Py POWL Model

## 4.1 Compact description

```
PO = ( nodes = {
        RD  : 'Register Deviation',
        CM  : 'Contain Material',
        ARC : 'Analyze Root Cause',
        PV  : 'Plan Verification',
        DCA : 'Design Corrective Action',
        ROUTE    : X ( 'Supervisor Review',
                       PO=( nodes={ 'Quality Review', 'Regulatory Review' }, order={ } ) ),
        SUPPLIER : X ( 'Consult Supplier', tau ),
        TRIAL    : * ( PO=( nodes={ 'Execute Trial', 'Inspect Trial' },
                            order={ 'Execute Trial' --> 'Inspect Trial' } ),
                       'Adjust Parameters' ),
        RM  : 'Release Material',
        AC  : 'Archive Case'
      },
      order (covering edges; the transitive closure is added programmatically) = {
        RD  --> CM,        RD  --> ARC,
        CM  --> PV,
        ARC --> DCA,       ARC --> ROUTE,     ARC --> SUPPLIER,
        PV  --> TRIAL,     DCA --> TRIAL,
        TRIAL --> RM,      ROUTE --> RM,      SUPPLIER --> RM,
        RM  --> AC
      } )
```

This is the intended dependency graph of §2 written down directly: the twelve covering edges are exactly D3, D4, D6, D7, D8, D11, D14, D15, D20, D21, D22, D23, and every relation the narrative leaves free is expressed by the *absence* of an edge.

## 4.2 Self-contained construction code

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)

# --- atomic nodes -----------------------------------------------------------
register = Transition("Register Deviation")
contain = Transition("Contain Material")
analyze = Transition("Analyze Root Cause")
plan = Transition("Plan Verification")
design = Transition("Design Corrective Action")
release = Transition("Release Material")
archive = Transition("Archive Case")

# --- approval route: XOR( Supervisor Review , PO{ Quality , Regulatory } ) ---
quality = Transition("Quality Review")
regulatory = Transition("Regulatory Review")
high_risk = StrictPartialOrder([quality, regulatory])    # no edges => concurrent
route = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [Transition("Supervisor Review"), high_risk])

# --- optional supplier consultation: XOR( Consult Supplier , tau ) ----------
supplier = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR, [Transition("Consult Supplier"), SilentTransition()]
)

# --- trial cycle: LOOP( PO{ Execute --> Inspect } , Adjust Parameters ) -----
execute = Transition("Execute Trial")
inspect = Transition("Inspect Trial")
trial_body = StrictPartialOrder([execute, inspect])
trial_body.order.add_edge(execute, inspect)
trial = OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [trial_body, Transition("Adjust Parameters")])

# --- root partial order -----------------------------------------------------
powl = StrictPartialOrder(
    [register, contain, analyze, plan, design, route, supplier, trial,
     release, archive]
)

# start of case
powl.order.add_edge(register, contain)
powl.order.add_edge(register, analyze)
# containment stream
powl.order.add_edge(contain, plan)
# analysis stream and the obligations opened by the analysis
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, design)
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, route)
powl.order.add_edge(analyze, supplier)
# the trial needs both work products
powl.order.add_edge(plan, trial)
powl.order.add_edge(design, trial)
# release synchronises the three independently progressing obligations
powl.order.add_edge(trial, release)
powl.order.add_edge(route, release)
powl.order.add_edge(supplier, release)
# closing
powl.order.add_edge(release, archive)

# POWL demands an irreflexive *and transitive* relation; the closure adds no
# behaviour, it only makes the covering relation a syntactically valid SPO.
powl.order.add_transitive_edges()
powl.validate_partial_orders()


if __name__ == "__main__":
    import pm4py

    print(powl)
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(powl)
    print("places:", len(net.places), "transitions:", len(net.transitions))
    print("sound:", pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)[0])
```

Running this prints the partial order, yields a net with 20 places and 18 transitions, and reports `Petri Net is a workflow net / every place is covered by s-components / no dead tasks / all tasks are live`, i.e. sound.

## 4.3 Use of the four required constructs

`StrictPartialOrder` appears three times: as the root (the cross-branch dependency graph), as the concurrent high-risk review pair, and as the ordered trial body. `OperatorPOWL` appears three times: `Operator.XOR` for the risk route, `Operator.XOR` for the optional consultation, and `Operator.LOOP` for the repeat-until trial cycle. `Transition` carries every one of the 14 visible labels, exactly once each. `SilentTransition` appears exactly once, as the skip alternative of the supplier consultation — it is the only invisible behaviour I introduce, which keeps the tau-related reasoning in §7 small and checkable.

## 4.4 Why the intentionally unconnected pairs may stay concurrent

Every omitted edge is a modelling decision, and each corresponds to a sentence in the narrative:

- `CM ∥ ARC`, and hence `CM ∥ DCA`, `CM ∥ ROUTE`, `CM ∥ SUPPLIER`, `PV ∥ ARC`, `PV ∥ DCA`: rule 2 states the containment and analysis streams proceed independently. Nothing in the narrative orders a containment activity relative to an analysis activity.
- `PV ∥ DCA` specifically: the two work products are produced independently and are joined only by the trial (D14, D15), which the two edges into `TRIAL` already express. Adding any edge between them would invent a dependency.
- `ROUTE ∥ TRIAL`: rule 7 explicitly says the approval route need not be complete before the trial starts and may overlap the trial cycle. Because POWL concurrency is interleaving-based after conversion, the individual review events may fall anywhere inside the `ET IT AP ET IT …` sequence.
- `ROUTE ∥ SUPPLIER`, `SUPPLIER ∥ DCA`, `SUPPLIER ∥ PV`, `SUPPLIER ∥ TRIAL`: rule 6 lists exactly these as things the consultation may overlap.
- `Quality Review ∥ Regulatory Review` inside the nested order: rule 5, "concurrently and in either completion order".
- No edge is needed *into* `ROUTE`/`SUPPLIER`/`TRIAL` from `RD`, nor *out of* them to `AC`: transitivity via `ARC`, `PV`/`DCA` and `RM` already implies those, and the closure call materialises them.

Conversely, the model contains no unconnected pair that *should* have been ordered: the twelve covering edges cover all of D3–D23, so every safety-relevant obligation (`TRIAL`, `ROUTE`, `SUPPLIER` before `RM`) is a genuine order constraint rather than a coincidence of interleaving.

# 6. Semantic Comparison

| Requirement | Intended semantics | Process tree | POWL | Overconstraint or underconstraint | Business consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Two initial work streams (rules 1–2, D3–D5) | CM and ARC both follow RD, mutually unordered | `+( ->(CM,PV), ->(ARC,DCA) )` — exact for this pair | Two edges out of RD, no edge between CM and ARC — exact | none | Both models correctly allow containment and analysis to run in parallel; no false deviations for either completion order. |
| Split dependencies into planning and design (rules 3–4, D6–D7) | PV after CM only; DCA after ARC only | Exact inside each branch of the `+` | Exact (`CM-->PV`, `ARC-->DCA`) | none | The distinct provenance of the two work products is preserved; a planner is never blocked by root-cause analysis. |
| Risk route (rule 5, D8–D10) | XOR after ARC; high risk = concurrent QUA+REG, either order | XOR/`+` structure exact, but the route is gated behind the join on PV *and* DCA | XOR node with a single incoming edge from ARC — exact | tree: **overconstrained** (route cannot complete early); POWL: exact | Tree flags a compliant early approval (very common: a supervisor signs off as soon as the analysis is in) as non-conforming, creating false positives in monitoring and understating precision measurements of a discovered model. |
| Overlap of approval and trial (rule 7, D16) | Route unordered w.r.t. the whole trial cycle | Both sit in the same `+`, so review events may fall between ET and IT — preserved | Unconnected nodes — exact | none for the overlap itself; the tree only forbids approvals *before* the trial's predecessors | Both models support the realistic pattern where reviews are signed while trials run, so no rework of the model is needed for that scenario. |
| Optional supplier consultation (rule 6, D11–D13, D22) | Zero or one CS, after ARC, may overlap design/planning/approvals/trial, before RM | `X( CS, tau )` gives optionality and at-most-once exactly, and CS may overlap the trial; but CS cannot complete before PV and DCA | `X( CS, tau )` node with edges `ARC-->SUPPLIER-->RM` only — exact | tree: **overconstrained** on placement; both exact on cardinality | Tree rejects the common "call the supplier immediately after the analysis" behaviour, which is exactly the behaviour a quality manager wants to see encouraged; POWL records it as conforming. |
| Repeat-until trial behaviour (rule 8, D17–D19) | `ET IT (AP ET IT)*`, at least once, no AP after the passing inspection | `*( ->(ET,IT), AP )` — exact | `*( PO(ET-->IT), AP )` — exact | none | Both models make a trailing `Adjust Parameters` a hard violation, which is the auditable property that matters (no tampering after a pass). |
| Release synchronisation (rule 9, D20–D22) | RM only after passing inspection AND route complete AND CS if selected | Root sequence puts RM after the whole `+`, which is exactly the three-way join | Three incoming edges into RM — exact | none in either direction | Neither model can ever certify a release that skipped an approval or a supplier obligation; this is the property the conservative policy was chosen to protect. |
| Duplicate-label risk | Not required by the narrative | Avoided: 14 labels, one leaf each, one tau | Avoided: 14 labels, one `Transition` each, one `SilentTransition` | none | Alignments stay diagnostically unambiguous. The duplication-based alternative would have restored the tree's lost orderings only by allowing an approval to occur twice or not at all — a cardinality defect that is far more dangerous than an ordering restriction. |
| Data conditions (rule 11, D24) | Not to be encoded; conditions become control-flow alternatives | Unguarded XOR / loop exit | Unguarded XOR / loop exit | both **underconstrained** relative to reality: any case may take any branch | Neither model can detect a *wrongly routed* case (a high-risk case reviewed only by a supervisor). That check must be done with a data-aware rule outside these models. |
| Start/complete abstraction (assumption 1) | "May start only after" | Enforced on completion events only | Enforced on completion events only | both **underconstrained** | A case where verification planning actually started too early but finished late passes both models; only a log with start events could catch it. |

Language-level summary: `L(tree) ⊂ L(POWL) = L(intended)` under the interleaving/completion abstraction. Evidence rather than assertion: POWL accepted 300 out of 300 randomly generated intended interleavings while the tree accepted 49 out of 300, and 400 playout traces per model contained no trace that violated an independently coded acceptor of rules 1–10 (excluding traces truncated by the playout length bound).

# 7. Preferred Representation and Validation Plan

**Canonical representation: the POWL model.** It is the only one of the two that is exact under the stated abstraction, and it is exact *for the reason that matters*: POWL's root construct is a partial order, so each of the twelve narrative dependencies becomes one edge, and each freedom becomes one absent edge. The mapping is inspectable requirement-by-requirement, which makes review by a quality engineer feasible — you can point at `ARC-->SUPPLIER` and read rule 6 aloud. It also degrades gracefully: if a new rule appears ("regulatory review must precede the trial"), it is one `add_edge` call, whereas the process tree would need restructuring and would likely lose more behaviour. The process tree is retained as a secondary, deliberately stricter artefact: it is useful where the tooling requires block structure (many discovery/comparison pipelines, tree-edit-distance similarity, some simplification and abstraction utilities), and its conservative bias makes it acceptable as a normative filter — but it must be documented as such, and its 49-out-of-300 acceptance rate on legal interleavings means it must never be used to compute precision or to reject cases automatically.

Validation plan (all steps below were executed against the two models in this answer; the reported outcomes are what pm4py actually returned):

1. **Conversion to accepting Petri nets.** `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)` and `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(powl)`; POWL conversion is supported and routes through `pm4py.objects.conversion.powl.converter`. Both produced 20 places and 18 transitions. Conversion succeeding is a precondition to inspect, not a fidelity result.
2. **Soundness of the converted nets.** `pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)` returned `True` for both, with diagnostics "Petri Net is a workflow net", "Every place is covered by s-components", "There are no dead tasks", "All tasks are live". Dead-task absence is the meaningful part here: it proves both risk routes, the tau skip and the redo part are all reachable.
3. **Bounded/sampled playout.** `pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, parameters={"noTraces": 400, "maxTraceLength": 40})` on each net, then check every produced trace with a hand-written acceptor that implements rules 1–10 directly (cardinalities, `PV` after `CM`, `DCA` after `ARC`, route exclusivity, the `ET IT (AP ET IT)*` pattern, first `ET` after both `PV` and `DCA`, `RM` after the last `IT`/route/`CS`, `AC` last). Result: no violations for POWL; for the tree the only flagged traces were ones cut off at length 40 mid-loop, i.e. playout artefacts. This is the check that would catch an accidental over-approximation.
4. **Explicit positive trace tests.** Align A1–A5 of §5 plus 300 randomly generated intended interleavings (both routes × supplier present/absent × 1–3 iterations, random topological orders of the intended dependency graph) and require fitness 1.0. POWL: 300/300 and A1–A5 all accepted. Tree: 49/300, and the rejections coincided exactly with the predicate "some route activity or `Consult Supplier` completes before both `PV` and `DCA`" — zero mismatches, which turns the informal claim in §3.4 into a tested one.
5. **Explicit negative trace tests.** Align F1–F3 of §5 and require fitness < 1.0 in both models; both rejected all three. Two further negatives were tested for the same reason: a mixed-route trace (`… SUP, QUA, ET, IT, RM, AC`, violating D9) and a late-consultation trace (`… SUP, RM, CS, AC`, violating D22) — both rejected by both models. Negative tests must be run against *both* models, because the tree's extra strictness can mask a missing constraint that POWL would reveal.
6. **Check aimed at silent behaviour and duplicated labels.** Two parts. (a) Duplicate-label check: walk the leaves of `tree` and the `Transition` nodes of `powl` and assert every non-`None` label has multiplicity one; both models pass, so no visible label is duplicated and any alignment move is attributable to a unique model element. (b) Tau-coverage check: over 500 playout traces per model, count traces containing `Consult Supplier` (tree 244, POWL 227), traces containing it twice (0 in both) and traces containing both `Supervisor Review` and `Quality Review` (0 in both). This confirms the single `SilentTransition`/`tau` is genuinely optional in both directions — the skip is neither dead nor mandatory — and that the silent XOR cannot be exploited to fire `Consult Supplier` twice or to blend the two risk routes.
7. **Limitations of this plan.** Playout samples the language and cannot certify absence of unintended traces; the loop makes the language infinite, so any playout bound truncates and produces spurious "violations" that must be filtered by hand (as above), while long-rework behaviour beyond the bound is never exercised. Alignment fitness 1.0 is evidence of trace membership, but a non-1.0 value only says "some deviation", so the *earliest violated condition* claims in §5 come from the hand-written acceptor, not from the aligner. The 300 random intended traces are drawn from my own generator, which encodes my own reading of the narrative — if that reading is wrong, both the generator and the models are wrong in the same way, and the tests will not notice; an independent reviewer restating the rules is the only real defence. Soundness of the converted nets says nothing about fidelity: the deliberately over-approximating variant in §3.4 is also sound. Finally, the whole plan is blind to the data perspective (D24) and to the start/complete distinction, so it cannot validate correct routing or genuine overlap at all.

# 8. Final Self-Critique

1. **Semantic: my exactness claim for the POWL model is empirical, not proved.** I assert `L(POWL) = L(intended)` and support it with 300 accepted intended traces and 400 clean playout traces, but I give no inductive argument, and the delicate point is exactly where sampling is weakest: the interaction between the loop node and the outer concurrency. POWL's loop `*(A,B)` re-enters a *partial-order body*, and I rely on the converted net letting concurrent nodes (`ROUTE`, `SUPPLIER`) interleave with the loop's internal transitions across iterations. My witnesses only ever place a review between one `ET` and one `IT`; I never tested, say, a review falling between `AP` and the following `ET` in a three-iteration case, nor whether some pm4py conversion detail makes the loop body atomic with respect to the outer order. A single such counterexample would demote my "exact" to "over- or under-approximate in one corner", and the honest status of the claim is "tested on a biased sample".

2. **Semantic: the completion-time abstraction quietly weakens every "may start only after" rule, including the safety-critical ones.** Rules 3, 4, 6 and 7 are worded about *starts*, but both my models constrain only completions. So an intended-illegal case in which `Execute Trial` started before `Plan Verification` was finished is accepted by both models as long as the completion events are ordered correctly. I stated this as an assumption rather than treating it as a fidelity gap, and I did not offer the obvious remedy (splitting each activity into `start`/`complete` transitions, or modelling lifecycle pairs), which would change the models substantially and would interact badly with the process tree's block structure. As it stands, my claim that the POWL model is "exact" holds only for the completion-projected language, which is weaker than what the narrative actually asks for.

3. **Methodological: my quantification of the tree's loss is sample-dependent and not a real precision/recall statement.** "49 out of 300" depends entirely on my generator's distribution over interleavings — a generator that placed approvals late would report far less loss, and one that placed them early would report almost total loss. The number therefore describes my sampler, not the models. A defensible comparison would use entropy-based precision/recall or a language-inclusion check on the two converted nets, and I ran neither. Relatedly, I never verified `L(tree) ⊆ L(POWL)` directly (for instance by aligning tree playout against the POWL net); I inferred it from both models' relations to the intended language, which is a weaker argument than the one I could have made in a few lines of code.
