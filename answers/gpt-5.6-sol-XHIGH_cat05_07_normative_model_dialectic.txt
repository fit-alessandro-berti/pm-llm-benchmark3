## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts

1. **M0’s control-flow semantics**
   - `Register_Supplier` precedes the composite `checks`.
   - Within `checks`, `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review` are concurrent: either may occur first, but both must complete before the XOR decision.
   - The approved branch requires `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`.
   - The alternative branch is `Reject_Supplier`.
   - Because `checks` precedes the XOR as a whole, M0 also requires both checks before rejection.
   - `Notify_Requester` must follow the selected branch, and `Close_Case` must follow notification.
   - M0 admits no `Auto_Approve`, emergency route, repeated information requests, label aliases, or duplicate events. It also has no data, temporal, monetary, identity, or authority guards.

2. **Controlled baseline**
   - CS-9, effective from 2025-01-01, requires sanctions and quality review before activation, manual procurement approval before every activation, and requester/manual-approver separation.
   - M0 does not encode the separation-of-duties rule, so a fitting trace can still violate CS-9.

3. **Temporary operational materials**
   - PD-3 had a stated 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31 window and narrow eligibility criteria. It was documented but not incorporated into CS-9 or M0.
   - FD-7 expressly covered 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12, had a narrow critical-relief scope, and imposed compensating controls.
   - Neither factual continuation of the flood nor expectation of a permanent pilot amendment extended those stated dates.

4. **Known data and model defects**
   - After 2026-07-01, only approximately 70% of CRM notifications were mirrored into the onboarding log. The rise in missing notification events from 2% to 29% is quantitatively consistent with that collection change.
   - `Request_More_Info` is common, considered desirable, and absent from M0.
   - M0 has unresolved scope for rejected pre-screen leads and reactivations.
   - M0 uses the old `Quality_Review` label and has no semantic mapping for `QA_Clearance`.
   - SUP-711 contains a known migration duplicate with the same source transaction ID.

5. **Clear risk facts**
   - SUP-703 was high-risk and foreign, was not PD-3 eligible, used auto-approval, and incurred EUR 180,000 in first spend.
   - SUP-708 was activated before sanctions clearance and manual approval, without an applicable exception.
   - No transactions before sanctions in SUP-708 mitigates realized exposure but does not cure the prerequisite violation.
   - SUP-712 has requester and manual approver aliases resolving to the same human.
   - SUP-715 has a missing required conflict disclosure, although no rule expressly prohibits the relationship itself.

### Disputed or unproved propositions

| Proposition | Status |
|---|---|
| The steering committee had authority to derogate from CS-9 through PD-3 | **Disputed.** PD-3 was a real decision and the observer allowed a “monitored exception,” but it was not incorporated into CS-9 and the observer denied approving a weakening of CS-9. |
| SUP-702 completed automated master-data validation before activation | **Unproved on the supplied record.** It is required by PD-3 but is not in the trace or accompanying evidence. |
| All 289 apparently eligible auto-approved cases were valid exceptions | **Unproved.** Eligibility is not the same as competent authorization or proof of every compensating control. |
| A missing onboarding-log notification means no business notification occurred | **Not supported without corroboration.** N4 supplies a known missing-event mechanism. |
| The mutable CRM link in SUP-706 identifies the case reliably | **Unproved.** The message exists, but the link was entered later in a mutable field. |
| Rejected pre-screen leads are governed by M0 | **Disputed.** The process owner and model owner use different scope boundaries, and the scope document is ambiguous. |
| Reactivation is a new onboarding under CS-9 | **Unproved.** CS-9 has no explicit reactivation procedure. |
| A still-valid prior quality certificate can substitute for a new `Quality_Review` | **Unproved as a normative rule**, even though it may be operationally sensible.
| `QA_Clearance` was formally equivalent on the case date | **Partly supported.** Content equivalence is supported by audit records; the formal mapping was approved only afterward. |
| The spouse/reporting-line relationship in SUP-715 invalidated the approval | **Not supported.** Only disclosure is expressly required. |
| Desirable practice, absence of loss, or widespread use amended policy | **Not supported.** Those facts affect model revision and severity, not retroactive authority. |

### Burden-of-proof approach

- A finding of **NONCOMPLIANT** should require, on the balance of reliable evidence:
  1. an applicable and effective rule;
  2. a case within that rule’s scope;
  3. reliable event, identity, or transaction evidence;
  4. a failed obligation; and
  5. no established valid exception.

- A missing log event is not automatically proof that the business activity was omitted where a documented collection failure exists. Such cases should remain data-indeterminate unless another source proves or disproves performance.

- The party relying on an exception should establish:
  1. competent authority;
  2. effective time and scope;
  3. complete eligibility;
  4. explicit derogated baseline rules;
  5. satisfaction of compensating controls; and
  6. an immutable invocation record.

- An exception receives a higher evidentiary burden because it relaxes preventive controls. Informal belief, operational normalization, or an anticipated amendment is insufficient.

- **Trace conformance and business compliance must be decided separately.** A trace deviation can be a model, vocabulary, scope, or logging defect; conversely, an M0-fitting trace can violate identity or conflict-of-interest rules.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that CS-9 is the only clearly controlled, permanent standard, while the operation allowed temporary or informal practices to become de facto policy without adequate authority controls.

### Formal authority and expiry

PD-3 was recorded in minutes and email but was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. The compliance observer’s statements are internally limiting: she permitted a monitored pilot but later denied approving a weakening of CS-9. The prosecution can therefore reasonably argue that PD-3 authorized experimentation but did not establish a competent waiver of the manual-approval and pre-activation-quality requirements.

Even if PD-3 is treated as a valid temporary exception, it was narrow. The aggregate evidence contains:

- 14 clearly ineligible auto-approved cases;
- 9 cases with missing eligibility attributes;
- six additional auto-approved cases after expiry;
- SUP-703, a particularly serious instance of selecting the pilot despite clear ineligibility.

FD-7 is similarly bounded. It justified activity during its stated window, not whenever operations believed the underlying emergency continued. SUP-705 occurred on 2026-07-13, after automatic expiry, with no written extension. SUP-714 occurred after PD-3 expired. In both cases, operational belief cannot replace the authority instrument.

### High-risk and material deviations

- **SUP-703** bypassed manual approval for a high-risk foreign supplier, delayed quality review until after activation, and incurred EUR 180,000 in initial spend. It was neither eligible for PD-3 nor covered by FD-7.
- **SUP-708** was activated before both sanctions clearance and manual approval. The absence of transactions before sanctions reduces loss but not the risk created by opening an active supplier record.
- **SUP-712** fits M0 but violates CS-9’s explicit requester/approver separation rule. This demonstrates why merely improving M0 fitness would not be enough.
- **SUP-715** fits M0 but omitted a conflict disclosure required by another organizational policy. The prosecution should not claim the marriage itself was prohibited, but the disclosure failure is still a real control failure.

These are not harmless process-mining artifacts. They concern sanctions, approval authority, identity, and conflicts—the controls with the greatest preventive significance.

### Normalization of exceptions

The operation’s desire for a permanent low-risk auto route is not itself evidence of authorization. The six post-pilot cases show that the pilot became normalized before approval. SUP-714 is the clearest example: the supplier may have been substantively low-risk, but the authorization had expired.

Likewise, SUP-705 demonstrates unauthorized continuation of an emergency privilege. A factual emergency can support a request for extension, but not a self-executing legal extension.

### Desirable behavior does not retroactively amend policy

SUP-707’s information-request cycles were beneficial, and the prosecution should concede that M0 is defective for not representing them. Nevertheless, stakeholder approval after the fact does not make every unmodeled activity normatively authorized. The proper response is controlled model revision, not treating the model as optional.

Similarly:

- SUP-713’s semantic equivalence may show that quality work occurred, but late approval of the label mapping exposes change-control weakness.
- SUP-710’s valid old certification may be sensible evidence reuse, but no controlled reactivation rule authorizes it to replace a new review.
- SUP-709’s abbreviated rejection route may be legitimate, but scope ambiguity should be formally resolved rather than selected case by case.

### Why the acknowledged defects do not defeat enforcement

The prosecution must acknowledge that:

- M0 wrongly excludes repeated information requests;
- it may overstate requirements for rejected pre-screen leads;
- it has no reactivation logic;
- it cannot detect identity violations;
- raw notification events became unreliable after the CRM migration;
- SUP-711 is a migration duplicate; and
- exact label matching mishandles SUP-713.

Those defects require targeted exclusions, data repair, and model revision. They do not justify disabling the core rules that sanctions and approval must precede activation absent a valid exception. At minimum, CS-9 should be enforced as the default, auto and provisional routes should be technically disabled outside approved overlays, and missing eligibility should fail closed.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense is not that all deviations are acceptable. It is that raw M0 conformance substantially overstates misconduct because M0 is simultaneously stale, incomplete, data-blind, and ambiguously scoped.

### M0 does not represent the actual authorized process landscape

M0 contains only the permanent standard route and rejection. It cannot represent:

- the PD-3 auto route;
- the FD-7 provisional route;
- retrospective approval;
- post-activation quality review;
- spend caps or monitoring;
- repeated `Request_More_Info`;
- reactivation;
- abbreviated pre-screen rejection; or
- renamed but semantically equivalent activities.

Thus every valid temporary exception appears deviant by construction. SUP-704 is the clearest example: its trace violates M0 ordering, yet the case satisfies the stated FD-7 scope, date, sanctions prerequisite, spend cap, monitoring requirement, and retrospective checks. Treating it as misconduct would confuse baseline-model fitness with policy compliance.

SUP-702 likewise closely matches PD-3: it was in-window, low-risk, DE, part of an approved corporate group, had next-business-day quality review, and has a stable CRM notification ID. The unresolved issues are authority hierarchy and proof of master-data validation—not the raw M0 deviations themselves.

### Concurrency is already broader than a single displayed sequence

M0 does not prescribe an order between sanctions and quality. Any analysis that flags a case merely because quality preceded sanctions would misread the POWL semantics. The violation in SUP-708 is not that quality came first; it is that activation came before sanctions and manual approval.

This distinction matters because process-mining visualizations often turn partial-order models into a representative sequence and then over-report deviations.

### Missing loops and desirable evidence gathering

Forty-seven cases contain `Request_More_Info`, and none activated before the requested evidence arrived. SUP-707 used the requests to obtain certificates before approval. This is not evidence of control avoidance; it is evidence that M0 lacks a normal evidence-remediation loop.

Under exact-label alignment, both requests become log moves. That is a model-language defect unless a rule actually prohibits such requests.

### CRM migration and event completeness

Notification missingness rose to 29% after a migration expected to mirror only about 70% of notifications. This strongly supports a collection defect rather than a sudden twenty-seven-point increase in requester-notification misconduct.

- SUP-702 has strong external evidence: a stable `notification_id`.
- SUP-706 has weaker evidence: a message exists, but its mutable, delayed link is insufficient for a definitive case-level conclusion.

The defense should not claim SUP-706 is proven compliant. It should claim that “no event in the onboarding log” is not proof of “no notification.”

### Scope and lifecycle defects

- **SUP-709:** CS-9 expressly contemplates abbreviated pre-screen rejection, but M0 requires quality review before every rejection. If rejected leads are pre-acceptance intake cases, M0 should not be applied. If they are in the same process, M0 needs a separate abbreviated rejection route.
- **SUP-710:** Reactivation is not expressly governed. Requiring a new registration and quality review despite a still-valid certification may be reasonable, but that is not established by the current documents.
- **Withdrawals:** M0 has no withdrawal outcome at all, indicating that its lifecycle scope is not comprehensive.

### Vocabulary and data artifacts

- SUP-711 is a migration duplicate, not two genuine registrations.
- SUP-713 performed audit-equivalent quality work under a renamed UI label. A stable activity taxonomy should map both display names to the same semantic activity.
- SUP-712 proves the opposite problem: an exact M0 trace can conceal a serious resource violation.

These examples show that raw alignment cost is neither a reliable misconduct score nor a complete compliance score.

### Limits the defense must acknowledge

The defense cannot reasonably excuse:

- SUP-703’s deliberate use of the pilot for an ineligible high-risk supplier;
- SUP-708’s activation before sanctions and approval;
- SUP-705’s reliance on an expired directive;
- SUP-714’s reliance on an expired pilot; or
- SUP-712’s same-human requester/approver arrangement.

Operational desirability does not create authority. Widespread practice may prove that a model is stale, but it does not prove that the practice is safe or approved. Missing eligibility attributes also cannot be treated as positive eligibility.

### System-level judgment

The operation is **materially noncompliant at system level**, but **M0 is not a valid sole measure of that noncompliance**.

Material noncompliance is established by:

- at least 14 clearly ineligible auto-approved cases;
- six post-pilot auto-approved cases;
- unresolved eligibility in another nine cases;
- severe examples SUP-703 and SUP-708;
- a confirmed segregation failure in SUP-712; and
- weak technical enforcement of overlay eligibility and expiry.

The record does not provide the total onboarding population, so it does not establish that most cases were noncompliant. Materiality rests on the importance of the controls and known serious failures, not on an unsupported overall percentage.

M0 remains valid as a description of the **standard manual route**, but it is invalid as a complete normative architecture because it:

- over-constrains rejected leads;
- omits temporary route families and information loops;
- has no reactivation or withdrawal scope;
- treats labels as semantics;
- cannot enforce identities, dates, amounts, eligibility, or authority; and
- is being applied to a log with known notification and migration defects.

### Where the prosecution is rejected

The prosecution is rejected insofar as it would:

- equate every raw M0 deviation with misconduct;
- treat SUP-704 as noncompliant despite a documented emergency directive;
- infer missing notification directly from missing onboarding-log events;
- treat SUP-707’s information requests as harmful;
- count SUP-711’s duplicate as two real registrations;
- disregard semantic equivalence in SUP-713; or
- assume rejected leads and reactivations are unambiguously within M0.

### Where the defense is rejected

The defense is rejected insofar as it would:

- use M0’s obsolescence to excuse SUP-703, SUP-708, or SUP-712;
- extend FD-7 based on factual emergency rather than written authority;
- extend PD-3 based on expectation of permanent approval;
- treat missing eligibility as eligibility;
- claim absence of transactions cures an activation-order violation; or
- treat widespread or desirable behavior as a retroactive policy amendment.

The appropriate response is therefore **not blind enforcement of every M0 label and sequence**. It is immediate enforcement of CS-9’s hard prerequisites and valid overlay boundaries, combined with controlled model, scope, and data revision.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### Layer 1: Revised POWL control-flow model

The revised POWL should describe route families:

1. **Standard route**
   - Register.
   - Concurrent sanctions and quality checks.
   - Zero or more information requests before approval.
   - Manual approval before activation.
   - Notification before closure.

2. **Conditional low-risk auto route**
   - Sanctions, automated master-data validation, auto-approval, and resolved information requests before activation.
   - Quality review must eventually occur and may occur before or after provisional activation.
   - Notification follows activation and precedes closure.
   - The structural branch does not itself grant eligibility.

3. **Emergency/provisional route**
   - Sanctions and monitoring enrollment before activation.
   - Quality and manual approval retrospectively after provisional activation.
   - Notification before closure.
   - Deadlines, spending, scope, and authority remain external constraints.

4. **Abbreviated rejection route**
   - Sanctions pre-screen.
   - Optional quality review depending on the intake procedure.
   - Rejection, notification, and closure.

5. **Withdrawal route**
   - Separate voluntary withdrawal outcome, notification, and closure.
   - It should not be counted as sanctions rejection or activation failure.

The POWL should not merge reactivation into first-time onboarding without policy. A separate reactivation model should be approved. Until then, future reactivations that create a new active purchasing relationship should default to the CS-9 standard route.

### Layer 2: DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints

Cross-route invariants are better represented declaratively:

- Every `Activate_Supplier` must be preceded by a same-case `Sanctions_Check`.
- Standard activation must be preceded by manual approval.
- Every auto activation must coexist with `Auto_Approve` and automated validation.
- Emergency activation must eventually be followed by quality review and retrospective approval.
- Every rejection, activation, or withdrawal must eventually be followed by notification and closure.
- `Notify_Requester` must precede `Close_Case`.
- No activation may occur after rejection or withdrawal.
- Closure must be terminal, except controlled correction events.
- Every information request must be resolved before activation; an outstanding-request counter must be zero.
- Duplicate visible activities should be checked by source transaction identity rather than label cardinality alone.

Numeric deadlines such as “within two business days” should not be left to an untimed DECLARE relation; they require calendar-aware data checks.

### Layer 3: pandas/data checks

Data-aware rules should evaluate:

- PD-3 risk, country, approved-group membership, and activation date;
- FD-7 critical-relief designation, invocation time, and emergency scope;
- cumulative EUR spend from provisional activation until retrospective approval;
- daily transaction-monitoring coverage;
- quality-review deadline using an approved business calendar;
- policy version effective at event time;
- master-data-validation result and timestamp;
- notification linkage and timing;
- whether an old certificate was valid and normatively reusable;
- unresolved eligibility fields;
- deduplication using stable source transaction IDs; and
- label normalization using effective-dated activity mappings.

Missing eligibility must produce `INDETERMINATE` or fail closed, not `ELIGIBLE`.

### Layer 4: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules

Resource rules require canonical human identities, not raw usernames:

- requester must differ from manual approver;
- delegation must identify delegator, delegate, authority, scope, and effective dates;
- delegation must not be used to bypass requester/approver separation;
- service accounts used for auto-approval must map to an authorized rule engine and version;
- conflict relationships must generate a disclosure and an independent disposition;
- the spouse/reporting-line relationship should not be treated as automatically prohibited unless policy is amended to say so;
- alias resolution must be temporal and append-only so that SUP-712-type conflicts remain detectable.

Identity comparisons can be implemented through pandas or database checks, but role authorization, recusal, and exception approval are organizational controls.

### Layer 5: Policy-overlay governance

Every pilot or emergency overlay should contain:

- immutable overlay and rule-version IDs;
- issuing authority and proof of competence;
- approval, record, effective-start, and effective-end timestamps;
- case-scope predicate;
- baseline rules expressly derogated;
- compensating controls;
- invocation requirements;
- expiry behavior;
- owner and monitoring obligations; and
- revocation or extension version.

No overlay should become effective merely because a POWL branch exists. Expiry should technically disable new invocations. Extensions must be new signed versions; they must not modify the prior record. Post-activation obligations survive expiry for cases validly invoked before the end date.

Where overlays overlap, a case must invoke one approved route. It may not combine the most favorable elements from several overlays.

### Explicit scope decisions

- **Rejected leads:** Define the process as beginning at supplier intake and provide a distinct pre-screen rejection route, or explicitly exclude pre-screen leads from onboarding conformance. The present ambiguity should not continue.
- **Reactivations:** Use a distinct lifecycle type. Until a controlled reuse rule exists, treat activation as requiring the standard route. A future rule may allow `Validate_Existing_Quality_Certification` to satisfy quality requirements.
- **Withdrawals:** Use a separate terminal status and activity. Require notification and closure, prohibit subsequent activation in the same lifecycle instance, and exclude withdrawals from rejection metrics.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following model is a **route-family model**. Its language permits auto and emergency branches structurally, but a trace is compliant only when the external overlay rules also authorize the selected route.

The loop uses a silent “do” child and `Request_More_Info` as the redo child. Under process-tree/POWL loop semantics this yields zero or more visible requests. It does not represent responses, outstanding-document state, or evidence arrival; those must be enforced through event and data constraints.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def zero_or_more_information_requests():
    """
    LOOP semantics execute the first child once and may then repeat
    redo -> do. A silent first child and visible redo therefore
    approximate Request_More_Info* (zero or more requests).
    """
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[
            SilentTransition(),
            Transition(label="Request_More_Info"),
        ],
    )


def add_edge(po, source, target):
    po.order.add_edge(source, target)


# ---------- Standard manual route ----------
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality])
# No edge between the checks: they are concurrent.

std_info = zero_or_more_information_requests()
std_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
std_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        std_checks,
        std_info,
        std_manual,
        std_activate,
        std_notify,
    ]
)
add_edge(standard_route, std_checks, std_manual)
add_edge(standard_route, std_info, std_manual)
add_edge(standard_route, std_manual, std_activate)
add_edge(standard_route, std_activate, std_notify)


# ---------- Conditional low-risk auto-approval route ----------
auto_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
auto_master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
auto_info = zero_or_more_information_requests()
auto_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
auto_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
auto_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        auto_sanctions,
        auto_master_data,
        auto_approve,
        auto_info,
        auto_activate,
        auto_quality,
        auto_notify,
    ]
)
# These controls must be complete before activation.
add_edge(auto_route, auto_sanctions, auto_activate)
add_edge(auto_route, auto_master_data, auto_activate)
add_edge(auto_route, auto_approve, auto_activate)
add_edge(auto_route, auto_info, auto_activate)

# Notification follows activation. Quality is intentionally unordered
# relative to activation and notification, but the route cannot complete
# and the case cannot close until Quality_Review has occurred.
add_edge(auto_route, auto_activate, auto_notify)


# ---------- Emergency/provisional route ----------
em_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
em_monitoring = Transition(label="Start_Daily_Transaction_Monitoring")
em_info = zero_or_more_information_requests()
em_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
em_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
em_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
em_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        em_sanctions,
        em_monitoring,
        em_info,
        em_activate,
        em_quality,
        em_manual,
        em_notify,
    ]
)
add_edge(emergency_route, em_sanctions, em_activate)
add_edge(emergency_route, em_monitoring, em_activate)
add_edge(emergency_route, em_info, em_activate)

# This branch represents actual use of provisional activation.
# Quality and manual approval may proceed concurrently afterward.
add_edge(emergency_route, em_activate, em_quality)
add_edge(emergency_route, em_activate, em_manual)
add_edge(emergency_route, em_activate, em_notify)


# ---------- Abbreviated rejection route ----------
rej_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
rej_optional_quality = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        SilentTransition(),
        Transition(label="Quality_Review"),
    ],
)
rej_info = zero_or_more_information_requests()

reject_screen = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[rej_sanctions, rej_optional_quality, rej_info]
)

reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
reject_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

rejection_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[reject_screen, reject, reject_notify]
)
add_edge(rejection_route, reject_screen, reject)
add_edge(rejection_route, reject, reject_notify)


# ---------- Voluntary withdrawal route ----------
withdraw = Transition(label="Withdraw_Request")
withdraw_notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")

withdrawal_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[withdraw, withdraw_notify]
)
add_edge(withdrawal_route, withdraw, withdraw_notify)


# ---------- Route choice and closure ----------
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")

route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_route,
        auto_route,
        emergency_route,
        rejection_route,
        withdrawal_route,
    ],
)

close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, route_choice, close])
add_edge(M1, register, route_choice)
add_edge(M1, route_choice, close)
```

### Event and data-collection proposal

1. **Immutable event identity**
   - `event_id`
   - `case_id`
   - `supplier_id`
   - stable `activity_code`
   - human-readable `activity_label`
   - `source_system`
   - `source_transaction_id`
   - `occurred_at`, `recorded_at`, and `ingested_at`
   - correction reference rather than destructive update

2. **Stable notification linkage**
   - CRM-generated immutable `notification_id`
   - direct immutable `case_id` foreign key
   - recipient, template/version, send status, and send time
   - mirrored and non-mirrored CRM records included through a federated extraction
   - mutable text links treated only as corroborative evidence

3. **Policy and eligibility evidence**
   - `applicable_rule_set_id`
   - `overlay_id` and `overlay_version_id`
   - signed `overlay_invocation_id`
   - immutable eligibility snapshot containing risk, country, group membership, and snapshot time
   - explicit result for each eligibility predicate
   - authority, approval time, effective interval, and derogated-rule IDs

4. **Approval and delegation evidence**
   - decision ID and type: manual, auto, retrospective
   - canonical approver person ID or authorized service identity
   - requester person ID
   - raw actor alias
   - temporal alias-resolution version
   - role and organizational unit
   - delegation ID, delegator, scope, and validity interval

5. **Validation, quality, and taxonomy evidence**
   - automated-validation ID, result, engine version, and timestamp
   - quality evidence ID and reviewer
   - stable semantic activity code such as `QUALITY_REVIEW`
   - effective-dated mapping from `Quality_Review` and `QA_Clearance`
   - mapping approval time recorded separately from mapping effective time

6. **Emergency financial controls**
   - provisional-activation timestamp
   - transaction IDs, immutable amounts, currency, and posting times
   - cumulative spend before retrospective approval
   - monitoring-enrollment ID and daily monitoring results
   - cap-release event tied to retrospective approval

7. **Lifecycle and scope**
   - explicit lifecycle type: intake, first onboarding, reactivation, rejection, withdrawal
   - prior supplier and certification references for reactivation
   - withdrawal and rejection reasons kept distinct
   - no reuse of an old case ID for a new activation lifecycle

8. **Data-quality controls**
   - enforce uniqueness of source-system/source-transaction pairs in the canonical event view;
   - preserve duplicate raw rows for lineage;
   - report source-specific completeness and late-arrival rates;
   - distinguish `NOT_PERFORMED`, `NOT_COLLECTED`, `UNLINKED`, and `UNKNOWN`;
   - never treat an absent event or eligibility field as affirmative compliance.
