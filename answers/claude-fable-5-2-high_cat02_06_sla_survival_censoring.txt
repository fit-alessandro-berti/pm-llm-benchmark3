# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

Labels used throughout: **[Obs]** read directly from the table, **[Est]** model-based estimate, **[Bound]** assumption-free bound, **[Hyp]** hypothesis, **[Asm]** assumption, **[Rec]** recommendation. All numbers come from the script in section 8, which was executed on the 32 supplied rows.

**Time definitions**

- Origin: `Submit` (t = 0 for every case). Time scale: elapsed hours.
- Horizon: h = 72 h. A secondary horizon of 96 h (SLA + 24 h) is used for late resolution.
- Event time T: time of the first terminal internal disposition. Event type D ∈ {Resolve, Withdraw, Reject, Transfer}.
- Censoring time C: age at the observation cutoff, which applies only to `open` rows. Observed data are (min(T, C), status).
- Functions: S(t) = P(T > t) is the probability of no disposition yet; F_k(t) = P(T ≤ t, D = k) is the cumulative incidence of exit k. S(t) + Σ_k F_k(t) = 1.

**Assumptions**

- **A1 [Asm]** The SLA clock is wall-clock time without pauses, and "breach" means strictly more than 72 h. Needed because the prompt gives no pause or business-hours rule.
- **A2 [Asm]** `open` rows are censored only by the common cutoff (administrative censoring). Needed for any Kaplan-Meier or Aalen-Johansen estimate; questioned in sections 3 and 6.
- **A3 [Asm]** The extract contains every case submitted in an intake window, not a sample selected on status or on being active at the cutoff. Otherwise there is length-biased sampling.
- **A4 [Asm]** Terminal states are absorbing (no reopening), and each activity marks entry into a stage.
- **A5 [Asm]** For open rows, `duration_or_age_h − last_event_gap_h` is the hour at which the current stage was entered. For terminal rows the gap is 0 by construction and carries no information.
- **A6 [Asm]** Priority is fixed at `Submit`. This is doubtful: all 8 P1 cases are escalated, against 4 of 14 P2 and 0 of 10 P3 **[Obs]**.
- **A7 [Asm]** Two analyst-chosen thresholds: "stale" means an open case idle for at least 48 h, and the late-resolution window ends at 96 h.

**Estimands**

| ID | Definition | Pooled value | Operational decision it matches |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P(not resolved by 72 h) = 1 − F_Resolve(72) | 0.696 **[Est]**; 0.563–0.719 **[Bound]** | Effectiveness reporting: what share of intake gets the successful outcome within the SLA. Must be shown decomposed, because it mixes lateness with exits. |
| E2 | P(no terminal disposition at 72 h) = S(72) | 0.380 **[Est]**; 0.281–0.438 **[Bound]** | Headline "SLA breach" (clock still running at 72 h): penalty exposure, staffing, backlog. |
| E3 | Late resolution: F_Resolve(96) − F_Resolve(72); conditional form E3c = E3 / S(72) | 0.143 and 0.378 **[Est]** | Recovery planning: how many breached cases are still recoverable in the next 24 h. |
| E4 | Cause-specific cumulative incidence F_k(72) for Withdraw, Reject, Transfer, plus cause-specific hazards | 0.140, 0.101, 0.075 **[Est]** | Process design: demand leakage, screening quality, routing to the external organization. |
| E5 | Dynamic risk P(T > 72 given T > s and history up to s) for an open case of age s < 72 | per case, section 7 | Alerting and triage of work in progress. |

E1 decomposes exactly: 0.696 = 0.380 (still pending) + 0.140 (withdrawn) + 0.101 (rejected) + 0.075 (transferred). Almost half of "not resolved by 72 h" is therefore exits, not lateness.

**[Rec]** Use E2 as the governed breach metric, because a case withdrawn at 27 h cannot meaningfully breach a 72 h clock. Report E1 beside it, because E2 can be improved by pushing cases into Withdraw, Reject or Transfer shortly before the deadline.

**Three different kinds of "incomplete" case**

- **Right-censored for the 72 h question:** open and younger than 72 h (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31). Their 72 h status is unknown.
- **Competing event:** Withdraw or Reject (S05, S06, S12, S14, S22, S23, S30), and Transfer under the primary encoding. Nothing is missing: the case is known never to be resolved internally. Censoring it would pretend it could still resolve.
- **Already-observed breach:** open and older than 72 h (S09, S11, S25, S26), or terminal after 72 h (S03, S13, S20, S28, S32). The 72 h indicator is fully observed for E1 and E2. Only the eventual time and type of exit remain censored for the four open ones, which matters for E3 and the tail.

**Contribution of the four named cases** (primary encoding, transfer as a competing exit)

| Case | E1 not resolved by 72 | E2 no disposition at 72 | E3 late resolution | E4 cause-specific |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Resolved at 76 h (S03) | Breach; in the risk set through 72 | Breach (pending at 72) | Counts as a late-resolution event at 76 | Resolve event at 76; at risk for every cause until then |
| Open at 92 h (S09) | Observed breach, even though the row is censored | Observed breach | At risk for late resolution from 72 to 92, then censored | At risk for all causes until 92, then censored |
| Withdrawn at 27 h (S06) | Counted as not resolved, in the withdrawal component; never censored | Not a breach (disposition at 27) | No contribution to the numerator; stays in the denominator | Withdraw event at 27 |
| Transferred at 68 h (S08) | Not resolved, in the transfer component | Not a breach (internal disposition at 68) | No contribution | Transfer event at 68 |

S08 changes under the alternative transfer treatments of section 2:

- **Censored at handoff:** unknown for both E1 and E2; its probability mass is redistributed to cases still at risk after 68 h.
- **Resolved at handoff:** an on-time success for E1; not pending for E2.
- **Never resolved:** not resolved for E1; pending at 72 h, so a breach for E2.

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Cause codes: 0 = right-censored, 1 = Resolve, 2 = Withdraw, 3 = Reject, 4 = Transfer.

| Observed status | n | Any-disposition indicator (E2) | Resolve indicator (E1, E3) | Competing-risk code | Censoring rule | Sensitivity alternatives |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| `resolved` | 13 | 1 at T | 1 at T | 1 | Never censored | None; a duration above 72 is an observed breach, not a censoring |
| `withdrawn` | 4 | 1 at T | 0 | 2 | Not censored (competing) | W1: censor at T (hypothetical "no withdrawals"); merge with Reject into "exit without resolution" |
| `rejected` | 3 | 1 at T | 0 | 3 | Not censored (competing) | W1: censor at T; W2: count as code 1 if a rejection is a delivered decision that satisfies the SLA |
| `transferred` | 3 | 1 at T | 0 | 4 | Not censored in the primary encoding | T1–T4 below |
| `open`, age < 72 | 5 | 0 | 0 | 0 | Censored at age | Best and worst case imputation (bounds) |
| `open`, age ≥ 72 | 4 | 0 | 0 | 0 | Censored at age, but the 72 h status is known | C1: if idle ≥ 48 h, recode as a silent exit (code 5) at age − gap |

**Transfer treatments**

| ID | Assumption about a transferred case | Encoding | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 (primary) | Internal responsibility ends; no internal resolution | Code 4 at T | Internal view. The breach metric stops at handoff. |
| T2 | External outcome is like that of cases still at risk internally | Code 0 at T | Requires non-informative censoring, which is implausible here (section 3). |
| T3 (optimistic bound) | Handoff discharges the SLA, or the external party resolves at once | Code 1 at T | Lower bound on E1. |
| T4 (pessimistic bound) | The customer's clock keeps running and the case is never resolved in the window | Code 0 with time set beyond all horizons | Upper bound on E2. Practically identical to T1 for E1 (0.697 against 0.696). |
| T5 (not coded) | External resolution hazard = k × internal hazard of escalated cases | Multiple imputation or inverse-probability-of-censoring weights, k varied from 0 to 1 and above | Tipping-point analysis once any external follow-up exists. |

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

The naive analysis keeps the 13 resolved cases and discards 19 of 32 (59%) **[Obs]**.

| Naive, resolved only | n | Mean h | Median h | Share over 72 h |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Cohort A | 6 | 54.7 | 47.5 | 33.3% (S03, S13) |
| Cohort B | 7 | 45.6 | 39.0 | 28.6% (S20, S28) |
| Pooled | 13 | 49.8 | 41.0 | 30.8% |

**Survivor bias.** Conditioning on "resolved before the cutoff" selects fast cases.

- S09 (92 h), S11 (81 h), S25 (79 h) and S26 (104 h) are open and already past 72 h, and they are absent from the naive rate. If they ever resolve, each will be late.
- The naive count of late cases is 4. The observed count of cases without disposition at 72 h is 9 (S03, S13, S20, S28, S09, S11, S25, S26, S32) **[Obs]**.
- The quantity the naive rate imitates is "share of eventual resolutions that are late". Estimated on all cases it is (0.605 − 0.304) / 0.605 ≈ 0.50 at 110 h, or 0.32 if only resolutions up to 96 h are counted **[Est]**. The tail beyond 96 h rests on one to three cases, so this is indicative only, but it is not 0.31.

**Competing risks.** All seven withdrawals and rejections occurred before 72 h (9 to 70 h).

- Dropping them inflates the apparent success share; censoring them is also wrong.
- With every non-resolve exit censored, 1 − Kaplan-Meier gives 0.361 resolved by 72 h against 0.304 from Aalen-Johansen, and 1.000 against 0.605 by 110 h **[Est]**. The naive curve claims everyone eventually resolves.
- S14 (rejected at 63 h) and S30 (withdrawn at 70 h) leave just before the deadline. They are compliant under E2 and failures under E1. Whether late exits are caused by slow service is a **[Hyp]**.

**Informative censoring: transfers.**

- All three transfers (S08, S21, S32) are P1, and `Transfer` is reached only from `Escalate` **[Obs]**.
- Of 12 visits to `Escalate`, 4 end in Resolve, 2 return to Review, 3 end in Transfer and 3 are still open **[Obs]**.
- Censoring transfers therefore lets them be "represented" by the remaining cases, which are mostly P2 and P3 review cases. For P1 the choice moves E2 from 0.469 (T1) to 0.729 (T2) **[Est]**.
- S08 was transferred at 68 h, four hours before the deadline. Clock-stopping by transfer is a **[Hyp]** the data cannot test.

**Informative censoring: long gaps.** The nine open cases are not exchangeable.

| Group | Cases (state, idle hours) |
|---|---|
| Stalled | S26 (Review, 72), S11 (Escalate, 55), S25 (Escalate, 48), S09 (Review, 31), S10 (Escalate, 19) |
| Recently touched | S29 (Review, 6), S24 (Review, 4), S31 (Screen, 3), S15 (Review, 2) |

- The cutoff itself is administrative, but stalled "open" cases may be unrecorded exits: a silent withdrawal, an unlogged transfer, or a wait on an external party **[Hyp]**.
- If so, the censoring mechanism is fine and the status is misclassified, so the tail of S(t) is a mixture of worked and abandoned cases.
- Censoring is credible only conditional on stage and idle time.

**Cohort imbalance.**

- Sizes are 15 and 17. The priority mix is P1/P2/P3 = 3/7/5 in A and 5/7/5 in B, so B has more of the transfer-prone P1 cases **[Obs]**.
- In B every P1 and P2 case starts `Submit>AutoCheck`, and every P3 case starts `Submit>Screen` **[Obs]**. Exposure to the new path is perfectly confounded with priority inside B.
- The naive view says B is better (28.6% against 33.3% late; mean 45.6 against 54.7 h).
- With all cases, B is worse on E2 (0.416 against 0.339) and marginally better on E1 (0.684 against 0.708) **[Est]**.
- Bootstrap 95% intervals for B − A are −0.34 to +0.31 (E1) and −0.32 to +0.45 (E2). The assumption-free bounds also overlap: E2 is 0.267–0.400 in A and 0.294–0.471 in B **[Bound]**. No ranking is supported.

**Open below versus above 72 h.**

- S09, S11, S25 and S26 are certain breaches. They belong in the numerator of E1 and E2 now and need recovery, not prediction.
- S10 (47 h), S15 (35 h), S24 (67 h), S29 (51 h) and S31 (23 h) are genuinely censored. Counting them as compliant gives the lower bound and counting them as breaches gives the upper bound.
- Their remaining risk differs sharply: S24 has 5 hours left and S31 has 49.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

Event counts limit everything: 13 Resolve (9 by 72 h), 4 Withdraw, 3 Reject, 3 Transfer and 9 censored, with no tied times.

| Method | What it estimates here | Verdict for these data |
|---|---|---|
| Kaplan-Meier | All-cause S(t), which is E2 when the event is "any disposition". | Valid for E2 only. As 1 − KM for Resolve with exits censored it targets a hypothetical world without exits and overstates resolution (0.361 against 0.304 at 72 h). |
| Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence | S(t) and F_k(t) for the four exits from one risk table, so E1 to E4 are mutually consistent. | **Primary.** Nonparametric, needs no proportional-hazards assumption, and handles the five censored-before-72 cases transparently. |
| Cause-specific hazards | Rate of each exit among cases still pending; the natural scale for stage and time-varying covariates. | Explanatory use inside the multi-state model. With 3 to 13 events per cause, at most one covariate or a stratified Nelson-Aalen. A covariate can raise the Resolve hazard without raising F_Resolve if it also raises a competing hazard: P1 has no withdrawals or rejections but a 0.281 transfer incidence. |
| Subdistribution hazards (Fine-Gray) | Direct covariate effects on F_Resolve(t). | Not now. Nine Resolve events by 72 h cannot support regression, and the risk set keeps exited cases, so internal time-varying covariates such as current stage are undefined. Later: baseline-only prediction of E1, or pseudo-value regression of the 72 h indicator. |
| Restricted mean time | Area under S(t): 50.4 h pending per case within the first 72 h (A 49.6, B 51.1), and 6.9 overdue hours per submitted case between 72 and 96 h (P1: 11.3) **[Est]**. | Supporting metric. Robust to crossing curves and directly a workload unit (case-hours). |
| Landmarking | E5: at s = 24, 48, 60 h, risk of breach among cases still pending, using only information available at s. | **Secondary (operational).** Removes immortal time by design. Today only a baseline-only version is possible; at the 72 h landmark, 9 cases are pending and E3c = 0.378. |
| Multi-state model | Transition hazards between stages, with a clock-reset (semi-Markov) time scale so idle time matters. E1 to E5 are all functionals of it. | Target design. Currently only transition counts are identifiable, because stage sojourn times are missing for completed stages. |

**[Rec] Primary analysis.** Aalen-Johansen state-occupation probabilities at 72 h on all 32 cases, with four competing exits and the cutoff as the only censoring. Report pooled and by cohort, with priority shown descriptively. Each figure carries assumption-free bounds, a cohort-stratified bootstrap interval, and the transfer scenarios T1 to T4. No cohort p-values: the intervals are about ±0.35 wide.

**[Rec] Secondary analysis.** Landmark dynamic prediction built on a multi-state cause-specific model once event timestamps exist. Until then, the pooled conditional curve plus stage and idle-time rules (section 7).

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

| Feature | Derivation from the prefix or columns | Type | Safe use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cohort | Column | Baseline | Stratifier. It is a proxy for calendar period, not for treatment received. |
| Priority | Column | Baseline only if A6 holds | Stratifier. If priority is raised on escalation it is post-baseline and leaks. |
| Entry path | First two activities: `Submit>AutoCheck` (12 cases, all B) or `Submit>Screen` (20) | Near-baseline, post-treatment relative to cohort | Compliance proxy and mediator; usable from the first activity onwards. |
| Current stage | Last non-terminal activity | Time-varying state | State in the multi-state model or covariate as of a landmark. |
| Repeated review | Count of `Review`: 2 in S03, S07, S13 | Time-varying | Only its value as of the landmark; the final count is leakage. |
| Rework | `rework_count`: S07, S13, S27 | Time-varying | Same. The supplied column is an end-of-observation value. |
| Escalation | `Escalate` in prefix (12 cases) | Time-varying, post-treatment under B's escalation policy | Time-dependent covariate or state. Never a baseline flag. |
| Escalation timing | Known only where `Escalate` is the last event of an open case: S10 at 28 h, S11 at 26 h, S25 at 31 h. Otherwise only its ordinal step. | Time-varying | Cannot be reconstructed for the other nine escalated cases without inventing timestamps. |
| Inactivity gap | `last_event_gap_h`; hours in the current stage for open cases | Time-varying | Only as time since last event at the landmark. On the full table it is pure leakage: gap = 0 exactly when the case is terminal. |
| Path pattern | Returns from escalation, Escalate→Review (S03, S29), and the B-only AutoCheck→Escalate shortcut (S18, S29) | Time-varying | Prefix as of the landmark. A full variant contains the terminal activity and is the outcome itself. |

**Immortal time and leakage, concretely.**

- A naive split on the `escalated` flag shows 2 of 12 escalated cases resolved by 72 h against 7 of 20 non-escalated, and 6 of 12 against 3 of 20 known pending at 72 h **[Obs]**. This is not an effect of escalation.
- **Immortal time:** a case must stay undisposed long enough to be escalated. S13's 110 h includes two reviews and a rework before `Escalate`; crediting all 110 h to "escalated" assigns event-free time to the exposure.
- **No opportunity:** early exits such as S05 (9 h), S23 (11 h) and S12 (15 h) could never join the escalated group.
- **Reverse causation:** escalation is plausibly triggered by ageing or difficulty **[Hyp]**.
- **Design remedy:** split each case at its escalation time into a not-yet-escalated and an escalated interval, or landmark, or model `Escalate` as a state.

**Proposed state-transition structure** (counts are observed transitions; "open" is the number censored in that state at the cutoff)

| From state | To state (observed count) | Open here at cutoff |
|---|---|---:|
| Submit | AutoCheck (12), Screen (20) | 0 |
| AutoCheck | Screen (10), Review (2), Escalate (2), **Resolve** (1) | 0 |
| Screen | Review (17), Escalate (5), AutoCheck (3), **Reject** (2), **Resolve** (1), **Withdraw** (1) | 1 |
| Review | **Resolve** (6), Escalate (5), Rework (3), **Withdraw** (3), **Reject** (1) | 5 |
| Rework | Review (2), **Resolve** (1) | 0 |
| Escalate | **Resolve** (4), **Transfer** (3), Review (2) | 3 |
| **Resolve**, **Withdraw**, **Reject** | Absorbing | — |
| **Transfer** | Absorbing internally; latent external continuation Transfer → ExternalPending → ExternalResolved or never, unobserved | — |

- **SLA overlay:** every transient state is split into a pre-72 h and a post-72 h copy with a deterministic move at t = 72. E2 is the probability of occupying any post-72 h transient state at that instant.
- **Stage hazards:** the hazard from stage j to k is transitions j→k divided by total hours spent in j. The numerators are in the table; the denominators need timestamps.
- **What the extract does give:** censored sojourns of open cases only, 122 idle hours in `Escalate` (S10, S11, S25) and 115 in `Review` (S09, S15, S24, S26, S29). That is enough to flag `Escalate` as the stage where cases stall, not to estimate a rate.

**Conclusions that could reverse**

- **Cohort ranking.** The naive view favours B. E2 disfavours B in every scenario except C2, but the gap shrinks to +0.015 once stale cases count as exits. - **Priority ranking.** P1 is the second-worst class for E1 if a transfer is a non-resolution (0.750) and the best if a transfer discharges the SLA (0.469). On E2, P1 is worst in every scenario, and far worse if transfers remain pending (0.750).
- **Size of the breach problem.** E2 ranges from 0.271 (C1) to 0.558 (W1) across the encodings above, and reaches 0.639 under the indefensible C2. Within the primary definition the bounds are 0.281 to 0.438, or 0.500 if transfers stay pending **[Bound]**.

- **Recoverability.** E3c is 0.25 in A and 0.47 in B, from 4 and 5 cases pending at 72 h. It would change with a single event.

**Conclusions that do not reverse**

- At least 28% of submitted cases have no disposition at 72 h.
- At most 44% of cases are resolved within 72 h, and at most half even if the two early transfers are credited as resolved **[Bound]**.
- The resolved-only analysis understates tail risk.
- The data cannot rank the cohorts.

# 7. Operational interpretation

**Alerts (E5).** The pooled curve gives conditional risks for the five open cases younger than 72 h **[Est]**. It uses age only, not stage.

| Case | Age h | State, idle h | P(pending at 72) | P(not resolved by 72) |
|---|---:|---|---:|---:|
| S24 (B, P2) | 67 | Review, 4 | 0.82 | 1.00 |
| S10 (A, P2) | 47 | Escalate, 19 | 0.66 | 0.87 |
| S29 (B, P1) | 51 | Review, 6 | 0.66 | 0.87 |
| S15 (A, P2) | 35 | Review, 2 | 0.56 | 0.79 |
| S31 (B, P2) | 23 | Screen, 3 | 0.47 | 0.74 |

- The 1.00 for S24 only reflects that no resolution was observed between 67 and 72 h in 32 cases; it is not a certainty.
- The expected number of further breaches is 3.15 of 5. Added to the 9 observed, this gives 12.15 of 32 = 0.380, which reproduces E2.
- A stage-aware model would rank S10 (idle 19 h in `Escalate`) above S29 (touched 6 h ago). The pooled curve cannot make that distinction **[Hyp]**.
- **[Rec]** Use three tiers:
  - *Breached and open* (S26, S09, S11, S25): a recovery queue with status verification, starting with the P1 cases idle 55 h and 48 h in `Escalate`.
  - *Imminent:* conditional E2 risk above a threshold chosen from alert capacity.
  - *Watch:* idle time in `Escalate` or `Review` above a stage-specific limit.

**Workload.**

- Restricted mean pending time (50.4 h per case in the first 72 h) and overdue hours (6.9 per case to 96 h; 11.3 for P1) convert intake volume into case-hours of capacity.
- The transition table locates queues: 5 open cases in `Review` and 3 stalled in `Escalate`.
- E3c (0.378) sizes the post-breach recovery effort.

**Governance. [Rec]**

- Publish E2 as the headline, with the transfer band (0.380 under T1 to 0.456 under T4) and the E1 decomposition.
- Report by intake cohort using the estimator or bounds, and prohibit resolved-only averages.
- Keep observed breaches separate from predicted ones.
- Monitor dispositions in the last hours before 72 h (S08 transferred at 68 h, S30 withdrawn at 70 h), since E2 rewards them.

**Not causal.**

- Risk scores rank cases for attention. A high score for an escalated or P1 case does not mean escalation or priority causes delay.
- The cohort contrast is not the policy effect: assignment was not randomized, the cohorts differ in calendar time and priority mix, and compliance is confounded with priority.
- A policy effect needs a designed rollout, or a target-trial emulation of rules such as "escalate by hour x".

**Calibration checks.**

- Compare predicted and observed 72 h risk within each priority and each cohort, with the Aalen-Johansen estimate as the observed value and censoring weights for cases open below 72 h.
- Track the censoring-weighted Brier score and time-dependent discrimination per landmark.
- Validate on later intake periods, not on random splits.
- With 8 to 17 cases per group, only calibration-in-the-large is feasible now.

**Fairness checks.**

- Compare alert rates, missed-breach rates and time-to-first-touch after an alert across priority and cohort.
- **P3:** check that alerts do not systematically de-prioritize it. S26 has been idle 72 h, and the P3 breach share is 0.2 in A and 0.4 in B.
- **P1:** check that its measured performance does not depend on the transfer convention.
- **Cohort B:** it must not be scored with curves fitted to cohort A if their paths differ.

CAUSES = {1: "resolve", 2: "withdraw", 3: "reject", 4: "transfer", 5: "stale_exit"}      # code 0 = right-censored
BASE = {"resolved": 1, "withdrawn": 2, "rejected": 3, "transferred": 4, "open": 0}

## === 2. Encoding: each keyword choice is an explicit estimand/assumption; no case is ever dropped ===
def encode(d, transfer="competing", wd_rej="competing", stale_gap=None):
    """Return one row per case with analysis time `time` and cause code `ev` (0 = censored).
    transfer : competing | censor | resolved_at_handoff (optimistic bound) |
               never_resolved (pessimistic bound: clock keeps running, case stays pending)
    wd_rej   : competing | censor (hypothetical 'exits eliminated') | reject_is_decision (Reject counted as Resolve)
    stale_gap: if set, open cases idle >= stale_gap hours become a silent exit at their LAST RECORDED event,
               i.e. at t - gap, which is derivable from the supplied columns (no timestamp is invented)."""
    o = d.copy()
    o["time"], o["ev"] = o["t"].astype(float), o["status"].map(BASE)
    tr, wr = o["status"].eq("transferred"), o["status"].isin(["withdrawn", "rejected"])
    if transfer == "censor":
        o.loc[tr, "ev"] = 0
    elif transfer == "resolved_at_handoff":
        o.loc[tr, "ev"] = 1
    elif transfer == "never_resolved":
        o.loc[tr, ["time", "ev"]] = [NEVER, 0]
    if wd_rej == "censor":
        o.loc[wr, "ev"] = 0
    elif wd_rej == "reject_is_decision":
        o.loc[o["status"].eq("rejected"), "ev"] = 1
    if stale_gap is not None:
        st = o["status"].eq("open") & (o["gap"] >= stale_gap)
        o.loc[st, "time"] = o.loc[st, "t"] - o.loc[st, "gap"]
        o.loc[st, "ev"] = 5
    return o

## === 3. Risk sets and Aalen-Johansen estimator in plain pandas/numpy (no survival library needed) ===
def risk_table(o):
    """One row per distinct time: n at risk, cause-specific event counts, censorings, all-cause survival
    S_all(t) (Kaplan-Meier for 'any disposition') and the cumulative incidence F_k(t) of every cause."""
    rows, S, cif = [], 1.0, {k: 0.0 for k in CAUSES}
    for t in np.sort(o["time"].unique()):
        n = int((o["time"] >= t).sum())
        d = {k: int(((o["time"] == t) & (o["ev"] == k)).sum()) for k in CAUSES}
        for k in CAUSES:
            cif[k] += S * d[k] / n                  # P(pending just before t) * cause-specific hazard at t
        S *= 1 - sum(d.values()) / n
        rows.append({"time": t, "n_risk": n, **{f"d_{CAUSES[k]}": d[k] for k in CAUSES},
                     "n_cens": int(((o["time"] == t) & (o["ev"] == 0)).sum()),
                     "S_all": S, **{f"F_{CAUSES[k]}": cif[k] for k in CAUSES}})
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def at(rt, t, col):
    r = rt[rt["time"] <= t]
    return (1.0 if col == "S_all" else 0.0) if r.empty else float(r[col].iloc[-1])

def area_S(rt, a, b):
    """Integral of S_all over [a, b] = expected hours spent without disposition in that window."""
    grid = np.r_[a, rt.loc[(rt.time > a) & (rt.time < b), "time"], b]
    return float(sum((grid[i + 1] - grid[i]) * at(rt, grid[i], "S_all") for i in range(len(grid) - 1)))

def estimands(o):
    rt = risk_table(o)
    f72, f96, s72 = at(rt, SLA, "F_resolve"), at(rt, TAU, "F_resolve"), at(rt, SLA, "S_all")
    return pd.Series({
        "n": len(o),
        "E1_not_resolved_by_72": 1 - f72,                                   # 1 - CIF_resolve(72)
        "E2_no_disposition_at_72": s72,                                     # state-occupation prob. of 'pending'
        "E3_resolved_late_72_96": f96 - f72,                                # CIF_resolve(96) - CIF_resolve(72)
        "E3c_resolve_by_96_given_pending_72": (f96 - f72) / s72 if s72 > 0 else np.nan,   # landmark at 72 h
        "E4_withdraw_by_72": at(rt, SLA, "F_withdraw"), "E4_reject_by_72": at(rt, SLA, "F_reject"),
        "E4_transfer_by_72": at(rt, SLA, "F_transfer"),
        "RMST_pending_h_0_72": area_S(rt, 0, SLA), "overdue_h_72_96": area_S(rt, SLA, TAU)})

def bounds(d):
    """Assumption-free bounds at 72 h. Unknowns are ONLY open cases younger than 72 h (and, where stated, the
    external fate of transfers). Open cases older than 72 h are already-observed breaches, not unknowns."""
    n = len(d)
    res72 = int((d.status.eq("resolved") & (d.t <= SLA)).sum())
    term72 = int((~d.status.eq("open") & (d.t <= SLA)).sum())
    unk = int((d.status.eq("open") & (d.t < SLA)).sum())
    trf = int((d.status.eq("transferred") & (d.t <= SLA)).sum())
    return pd.Series({"n": n, "resolved<=72": res72, "other_exit<=72": term72 - res72, "open_age<72": unk,
                      "known_pending@72": n - term72 - unk,
                      "E1_low": (n - res72 - unk) / n, "E1_high": (n - res72) / n,          # transfer = not resolved
                      "E1_low_if_transfers_resolved": (n - res72 - unk - trf) / n,
                      "E2_low": (n - term72 - unk) / n, "E2_high": (n - term72) / n,        # transfer = disposition
                      "E2_high_if_transfers_pending": (n - term72 + trf) / n})

# 9. Limitations and additional data

**Limitations of the present analysis**

- 32 cases and 23 terminal events. Interval widths of about ±0.17 (pooled) and ±0.35 (cohort difference) dominate every comparison.
- Estimates beyond about 90 h rest on one to three cases.
- The bootstrap is crude at n = 15 and 17, and the priority-standardized figures use cells of 3 to 7 cases.
- The stale-case rule (48 h) and the 96 h window are analyst choices (A7).
- Stage hazards, stage-aware landmark models and time-split escalation analysis are specified but not estimable from this extract.

**Additional data, in priority order**

1. **Exact timestamps for every event** (ideally start and complete). They unlock stage sojourn times, transition hazards, landmark covariates, and time-split handling of `Escalate`, `Rework` and repeated `Review`. This is the largest single gain.
2. **External outcomes after `Transfer`** (resolution time, or confirmation of none). This collapses T1 to T4 into an observed quantity. It is decisive for P1, where E2 ranges from 0.469 to 0.750.
3. **Submit dates and the cutoff date.** They verify administrative censoring (A2), reveal calendar trends, show whether cohort B had shorter potential follow-up, and expose the sampling frame (A3).
4. **True status of stalled open cases** (S26, S11, S25, S09) and the reasons for withdrawal and rejection. They separate real backlog from unrecorded exits, and delay-induced exits from unrelated ones.
5. **The assignment mechanism.** How cases entered cohort B, when priority is set and whether it can change (A6), and what triggers escalation under each policy.
6. **Policy rollout compliance.** A per-case record of whether automated screening and the new escalation rule were applied, and whether P3 was out of scope or non-compliant. This allows as-assigned and as-treated contrasts that are not confounded with priority.
7. **Time-varying workload and capacity.** Queue lengths, staffing and handler identity per stage and hour, to separate case risk from congestion and to make the workload conversion actionable.
8. **SLA rule details.** Clock pauses, business hours, and whether rejection or transfer contractually discharges the SLA. These fix the choice among E1, E2, W2 and T1 to T4 instead of leaving it to sensitivity analysis.
9. **More cases over several intake periods,** for temporal validation, calibration by priority and cohort, and any regression modelling.
