# 1. Model scope and state variables

**Boundary:**
The digital twin models the administrative lifecycle of a building permit from submission ("Submit application") to final decision ("Issue permit" / "Reject application"). It excludes physical construction phases post-permit and pre-submission consulting unless part of the intervention (e.g., D1 Pre-submission checker). External actors (Applicants, Architects, Third-party consultants) are modeled as stochastic generators of delays or dependencies rather than active agents within the municipal system.

**Entities:**
*   **Case:** A unique permit application instance (`case:concept:name`). Attributes include `case_type`, `district`, `architect_id`, `application_value`, `requires_*` flags.
*   **Activity:** Process steps (e.g., "Planning review"). Has associated duration, resource requirement, and routing logic.
*   **Resource:** Human or System workers (e.g., `planner_1`, `scheduler_1`, `system`). Attributes include FTE, calendar, skill set.
*   **Bundle:** A temporary grouping of cases for geographically efficient inspection (cross-case dependency).
*   **Queue:** Holds cases waiting for specific activities or resources.

**State Variables:**
*   **Case State:** Current activity, elapsed time, waiting time, accumulated rework loops, bundle ID (if applicable).
*   **Resource State:** Busy/Idle, current task, remaining shift time, scheduled leave/holidays.
*   **System State:** Current simulation time, global arrival queue, district-specific travel buffers, active bundles.
*   **Dependency State:** Set of open cases linked by `architect_id` and `district` eligible for inspection bundling (D3).

**Resources & Calendars:**
Resources are mapped to specific calendars (e.g., Fire reviewers work Tue-Thu). Calendars account for public holidays and council meeting blocks (Legal check). Capacity is defined in FTE hours per day.

**Cross-Case Dependencies:**
*   **Bundling:** Cases from same `architect_id` + `district` arriving within a configurable window (e.g., 5 days) may be merged into an inspection bundle to reduce travel time (Intervention D3).
*   **Contagion:** Neighborhood objections (proxy via Public Notice duration) affect multiple cases in the same district simultaneously.
*   **Resource Contention:** Shared specialized resources (Fire, Environmental) create coupling between case types.

# 2. Parameter-estimation plan

We utilize **PM4Py** for log mining to derive parameters for the Discrete Event Simulation (DES) engine.

1.  **Log Loading & Formatting:**
    *   Load raw CSV using `pandas`.
    *   Use `pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id='concept:name', activity_id='concept:name', timestamp='time:timestamp')` to create a PM4Py event log object.
    *   Handle missing timestamps by inferring start times from preceding activity completion times where start timestamps are absent, flagging these records for sensitivity analysis.

2.  **Arrival Processes:**
    *   Extract inter-arrival times using `pm4py.log_statistics.get_case_timestamps`.
    *   Fit a non-homogeneous Poisson process (NHPP) or seasonal regression model to capture the 1.7x peak (March-May, Sept-Nov) and Monday spikes.
    *   Cluster arrival bursts by `architect_id` to model correlated submissions (top 20 architects submit 31% of cases).

3.  **Service & Waiting Times:**
    *   **Service Time:** Calculated as `end_time - start_time`. Where start is missing, use the median service time for that activity/case-type from the log summary as a fixed value or sample from a fitted distribution (LogNormal/Weibull) matching the reported Median/p90.
    *   **Waiting Time:** `start_time_of_activity - end_time_of_previous_activity`.
    *   Use `pm4py.performance.analysis.get_performance` to extract empirical distributions.

4.  **Routing & Branching:**
    *   Discover variants using `pm4py.get_variants(log)`.
    *   Calculate transition probabilities (e.g., Probability of `Request missing documents` after `Completeness check`) using `pm4py.statistics.transition_occurrence`.
    *   Map conditional routing (Fire/Env checks) based on attributes (`requires_fire`, `district`).

5.  **Resource Calibration:**
    *   Map `org:resource` to resource pools.
    *   Validate calendar constraints against HR data (e.g., Fire reviewers Tue-Thu).
    *   Estimate travel times for inspectors by district using spatial distance proxies if GPS data is missing, calibrated against "Site inspection" duration outliers.

6.  **Data Gap Handling:**
    *   **Objections:** Since objection events are missing, fit a mixture distribution to `Public-notice period` duration. The main mode represents standard notices; the long tail represents objection extensions.
    *   **Rework:** Identify rework loops by detecting repeated activity occurrences within a case trace. Estimate rework probability per loop point (Planning, Inspection, Legal).

# 3. Assumptions

### Arrivals
*   **Non-stationarity:** Arrival rates follow a seasonal pattern derived from the 18-month log (1.7x multiplier in peaks).
*   **Burstiness:** Architect-driven bursts are modeled as cluster processes; individual applications within a burst arrive within a 24-hour window.
*   **Distribution:** Inter-arrival times follow a Gamma distribution to allow for variance clustering.

### Service Times
*   **Missing Start Timestamps:** For human activities lacking start times, we assume the recorded timestamp is the completion time. Service duration is sampled from the empirical distribution for that activity type, independent of the specific resource (unless resource-specific outliers exist).
*   **Heavy Tails:** Long-duration activities (e.g., Environmental Review) are modeled with Log-Normal distributions to preserve the p90 impact.

### Routing
*   **Deterministic Conditions:** Requirements for Fire/Environmental/Public Notice are determined by `case_type`, `district`, and `application_value` thresholds known at submission.
*   **Parallelism:** Fire and Environmental reviews proceed in parallel once triggered, constrained only by resource availability.

### Resources/Calendars
*   **Calendar Accuracy:** Reconstructed HR calendars are assumed accurate for availability windows but do not account for unscheduled sick leave (modeled as random unavailability ~5%).
*   **Capacity:** FTE values translate directly to hourly capacity during working hours. Lunch breaks are ignored for simplicity or aggregated into effective working hours.

### Rework
*   **Trigger Logic:** Rework is probabilistic based on variant share (e.g., 9% Inspection Rework). If rework occurs, the case returns to the specific predecessor activity.
*   **Reasoning:** Rework reasons (unstructured text) are abstracted into "Quality Defect" or "Regulatory Conflict" probabilities.

### Applicant/Third-Party Response
*   **External Delays:** "Receive missing documents" waiting time is modeled as a separate external delay generator (Applicant response time), independent of municipal resource queues.
*   **Response Curve:** Modeled as a Weibull distribution to capture the slow tail of applicant responsiveness.

### Cross-Case Dependencies
*   **Bundling:** Bundles form dynamically. If a new case arrives matching an existing pending inspection bundle (same district/architect), it joins the bundle. The first case in the bundle waits for the bundle trigger time.
*   **Travel Time:** Travel time is added to Site Inspection duration based on district adjacency matrices.

### Non-Stationary Demand
*   **Seasonality:** Demand multipliers apply to the base Poisson rate.
*   **Day-of-Week:** Monday submissions are weighted 2.2x relative to Wednesday.

### Missing Data
*   **Objections:** Unobserved objection events are not simulated as discrete interruptions but as extended durations in the Public Notice activity distribution.
*   **Emergency Decisions:** Removed from the log; excluded from the simulation scope to avoid biasing average metrics.

# 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

**Warm-up Period:**
*   **Duration:** 6 months (one full seasonal cycle half-period).
*   **Justification:** Ensures the system reaches steady-state behavior regarding resource queues and seasonal demand adaptation before collecting statistics. Data from Jan-Jun discarded if running full-year simulation starting Jan 1.

**Replications:**
*   **Count:** 30 independent replications per experiment.
*   **Random Seeds:** Fixed seeds for reproducibility across experiments, varied per replication (Seed = Base + i).
*   **Run Length:** 24 months simulation time (18 months calibration + 6 months forecast horizon).

**Uncertainty Treatment:**
*   **Confidence Intervals:** Report 95% Confidence Intervals (CI) for all KPIs (Mean ± 1.96 * StdErr).
*   **Heavy-Tailed Durations:** Do not rely solely on mean lead times. Report Median and p90/p95 to capture outlier risks (e.g., Complex variance appeals).
*   **Sensitivity Analysis:** Vary key uncertain parameters by ±20%:
    *   Rework probability (currently inferred from log).
    *   Applicant response time distribution shape.
    *   Objection frequency (proxy via Public Notice tail).
*   **Scenario Envelope:** Present results as bands (Min/Median/Max) across replications rather than single point estimates.

    *   Run the simulation with historical arrival traces.
    *   Compare simulated activity frequencies and variant shares against the original event log (Fitness metric).

3.  **Distributional Validation:**
    *   Perform Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests on simulated vs. empirical lead-time distributions per case type.
    *   Verify resource utilization rates match historical HR reports (e.g., Planners ~85% busy).

4.  **Bottleneck Validation:**
    *   Identify bottlenecks in simulation (e.g., Inspection Scheduling).
    *   Compare with historical bottleneck identification (e.g., "Inspection scheduling" waiting time spikes).

5.  **Out-of-Sample Validation:**
    *   Hold out the last 3 months of the 18-month log.
    *   Train/Calibrate on first 15 months.
    *   Simulate the held-out period and compare predicted vs. actual throughput and lead times.

6.  **Constraint Checking:**
    *   Verify no negative waiting times occur.
    *   Verify resource utilization never exceeds 100% (unless overtime is explicitly modeled).

# 7. Decision criteria

Interventions will be selected based on a multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA):

1.  **Throughput Efficiency:**
    *   **Metric:** Median Lead Time (Days).
    *   **Threshold:** Reduction  10% compared to BASE for priority case types (New Residential).

2.  **Service Level Agreement (SLA) Compliance:**
    *   **Metric:** % of cases completed within statutory limits (e.g., 60d for Simple, 120d for Commercial).
    *   **Threshold:** Increase  5 percentage points.

3.  **Tail Risk Management:**
    *   **Metric:** p90 Lead Time.
    *   **Threshold:** No increase in p90 even if Median decreases (avoiding "fast average, slow worst-case").

4.  **Resource Utilization & Cost:**
    *   **Metric:** Average Resource Utilization (%).
    *   **Range:** 70% - 85% (Avoid under-utilization or burnout).
    *   **Cost:** Net FTE cost change (including temporary staff costs).

5.  **Fairness / Equity:**
    *   **Metric:** Coefficient of Variation of Lead Times across Districts (D1 vs D3/D5).
    *   **Threshold:** CV reduction or maintained below 0.25 (prevent regional disparity).

6.  **Robustness:**
    *   **Metric:** Performance degradation under STRESS1 (+40% demand).
    *   **Threshold:** Lead time increase < 20% compared to BASE under stress.

**Selection Rule:** An intervention is recommended if it improves Efficiency and SLA Compliance without violating Utilization or Fairness thresholds. COMBO policies require additive benefit verification.
