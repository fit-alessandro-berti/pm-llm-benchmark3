# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this case, **resilience** means adapting the process under system failure in a way that preserves critical supply, respects product and patient risk, captures manual evidence, and enables later reconciliation. **Concealed noncompliance** means preserving apparent throughput by skipping, weakening, or deferring required controls so that the risk becomes invisible until audit, complaint, recall, or regulatory inspection.

The central distinction is not whether the process deviated from baseline. Deviation was unavoidable. The distinction is whether the deviation was:

1. **Risk-proportionate**: controls were relaxed only where product and customer risk allowed it.  
2. **Documented**: manual evidence captured who, what, when, why, and by what authority.  
3. **Time-boxed**: the workaround had a clear start, end, and recovery path.  
4. **Reconcilable**: the organization could later prove what happened and correct errors.  
5. **Visible to management**: the hidden workload and residual risk were measured, not buried after invoice close.

## Adaptations that indicate true resilience

True resilience here includes:

- **B. Manual authorization check**: preserved the authorization control despite system loss.  
- **A. Hospital-critical fast lane**, when combined with emergency authorization and retrospective validation. It protected hospitals without necessarily abandoning compliance.  
- **F. Temporary release control**, especially for controlled-substance orders, provided the control is expanded and documented.  
- **I. Defer noncritical order**, if performed using explicit triage rules and communicated fairly.  
- **J. Backlog prioritization**, if governed by transparent scoring rather than informal judgment.  
- **K. Reconcile manual reservation**, if performed quickly and visibly.  
- **L. Audit remediation**, if bounded by SLA and used to restore evidence rather than pretend the control occurred in real time.

These adaptations preserved essential function while still attempting to retain or restore control evidence.

## Adaptations that are risky shortcuts or concealed noncompliance

The clearest risky shortcuts are:

- **C. Skip system authorization check**: this is a direct authorization gap unless converted into a documented emergency manual authorization with retrospective validation. Shipping based on assumed standing authorization is throughput preservation, not resilience.  
- **G. Dispatch without scan**: this created the largest traceability gap. The manual DFG shows approximately 3,250 dispatch-without-scan events but only 2,600 immediate phone POD events downstream. That gap suggests missing custody evidence or delayed proof. This is concealed noncompliance for any product where dispatch and delivery evidence are required.  
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot check after the snapshot became stale**: reasonable early in the incident, but risky as time passed. It can create false confidence in stock availability, lot allocation, and expiry control.  
- **E. Manual pick list without disciplined scan reconstruction or lot verification**: physical movement continued, but traceability quality suffered. The rise in rework to 12.5% during T2 is consistent with this risk.  
- **H. Phone proof of delivery for high-risk products**: acceptable for low-risk ambient orders if properly scripted, but weak for controlled-substance, cold-chain, or recall-sensitive orders.  
- **Direct manual pack to dispatch without temporary release control**: in the manual DFG, 1,800 cases moved from manual pack directly to dispatch without scan, bypassing even the temporary release control branch. That is a missing quality/release control.  
- **Spreadsheet invoice hold used as an informal endpoint**: if spreadsheets are closed without reconciliation, they become a shadow process that hides incomplete compliance.

## Ambiguous adaptations depending on risk appetite

Some adaptations are neither clearly resilient nor clearly noncompliant; they depend on governance, product risk, and evidence quality:

- **A. Hospital-critical fast lane**: resilient if emergency authorization is documented; noncompliant if used to bypass authorization for controlled or cold-chain products.  
- **D. Manual inventory snapshot**: resilient in the first hours; risky if used for days without freshness controls.  
- **E. Manual pick lists**: resilient if paired with verified reconstruction; noncompliant if scans are backfilled without evidence.  
- **H. Phone POD**: acceptable for low-risk ambient; insufficient for controlled/cold-chain.  
- **I. Deferral of noncritical orders**: resilient capacity protection; unfair if pharmacy-standard customers absorb disproportionate harm without rules.  
- **L. Audit remediation**: necessary recovery work; concealed noncompliance if management treats invoice close plus remediation as equivalent to real-time compliance.

The conformance results support this interpretation. The manual workaround’s fitness of 0.71 and 44% fitting traces do not automatically mean failure; they show that the process necessarily changed. But the move-on-model deviations—missing quality/release, missing scan, missing proof of delivery, missing temperature-control record—are the dangerous subset of changes. They are not merely “different”; they remove evidence required for compliance.

The temporal deviations also matter. `Backlog queue  Manual pick list` exceeded baseline p95, showing delayed recovery for deferred work. `Dispatch without scan  Phone proof of delivery` had highly variable delay, meaning delivery evidence was not contemporaneous. `Invoice close  Audit remediation` created a long after-the-fact compliance tail, showing that operational closure did not equal compliance closure.

# 4. Recovery process model

Below is a redesigned recovery process for future disruptions. It uses a simple BPMN-like notation:

- `` means sequence.  
- `[M]` means mandatory control.  
- `[SLA: x]` means time-bound requirement.  
- `` means decision gateway.  
- `!` means hard stop: do not proceed unless a formal exception is granted.

## 4.1 Operating states and trigger conditions

| State | Trigger conditions | Operational meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **NORMAL** | Systems available; WMS, scanning, authorization, inventory sync, temperature monitoring, controlled-substance register, and courier interfaces are trusted. | Standard process only. Baseline controls apply. |
| **DEGRADED-MANUAL** | Cyber incident declared; WMS or scanning unavailable >2 hours; inventory sync untrusted; authorization system unavailable; cold-chain monitoring partially unavailable; controlled register unavailable. | Activate manual workaround with mandatory evidence capture. Triage and risk gates apply. |
| **PARTIAL-RECOVERY** | Order intake returns, but inventory synchronization, scanning, or traceability systems remain unreliable; manual records still open; reconciliation mismatch > defined threshold. | Hybrid process. Dual verification required. High-risk products still subject to manual evidence and hard stops. |
| **STABILIZATION / REMEDIATION** | Systems mostly restored, but backlog, manual reservations, missing scans, missing PODs, temperature documentation, or authorization exceptions remain open. | Backlog prioritization, reconciliation, audit remediation, and formal compliance closure required. |
| **RECOVERED-AUDIT-CLOSED** | All manual events matched; exceptions closed; audit remediation complete or QA risk assessment approved; backlog age returned to target; fairness metrics acceptable. | Incident can be declared closed from both operational and compliance perspectives. |

### Explicit declaration triggers

1. **System integrity trigger**:  
   - WMS unavailable, inventory sync unreliable, or suspected data compromise.  
   - Scanning unavailable for >2 hours.  
   - Controlled-substance register unavailable.  
   - Cold-chain temperature monitoring unavailable.

2. **Service-risk trigger**:  
   - Hospital-critical backlog age exceeds baseline p90 threshold.  
   - Courier availability threatens cold-chain stability windows.  
   - Recall-sensitive or controlled orders are at risk of missing required evidence.

3. **Backlog trigger**:  
   - Backlog exceeds 1.5 times normal daily demand or segment-specific criticality threshold.  
   - Pharmacy-standard fill forecast falls below minimum service floor.

4. **Recovery trigger**:  
   - Systems return but manual-event reconciliation mismatch >1% or high-risk exceptions remain open.  
   - Do not move from partial recovery to stabilization until inventory and dispatch evidence are sufficiently reconciled.

## 4.2 Triage rules

Use explicit priority classes. Product risk can upgrade a case; customer criticality alone does not remove mandatory product controls.

### Priority classes

| Priority | Scope | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **P1: Clinical / hospital critical** | Hospital-critical orders, urgent patient need, recall replacements needed for patient continuity, urgent controlled or cold-chain clinical supply. | Process immediately. Manual authorization allowed under emergency protocol. Mandatory evidence capture required. |
| **P2: Essential non-hospital** | Pharmacy-standard essential medicines, short-expiry replenishment, wholesale orders supporting pharmacy continuity. | Process under protected capacity quota. No silent indefinite deferral. |
| **P3: Deferrable / non-urgent** | Wholesale-bulk stock building, non-urgent promotions, low-risk ambient replenishment where delay is acceptable. | Defer, reschedule, or cancel with customer communication. |

### Triage gate rules

1. **Customer and product triage**  
   - Identify customer type: Hospital-critical, Pharmacy-standard, Wholesale-bulk.  
   - Identify product risk: Ambient, Cold-chain, Controlled-substance, Recall-sensitive.

2. **Product-risk override**  
   - Controlled-substance, cold-chain, and recall-sensitive products cannot move solely on customer priority. They require mandatory product controls regardless of P1/P2/P3.

3. **Authorization rule**  
   - If authorization can be manually verified: proceed.  
   - If authorization is unknown: hold.  
   - For P1 hospital-critical only: allow emergency manual authorization by trained compliance staff, with retrospective validation `[SLA: 24h]`.  
   - Do not skip authorization for controlled-substance, cold-chain, recall-sensitive, or new customers.

4. **Inventory rule**  
   - Use timestamped inventory snapshot.  
   - Snapshot age limit: high-risk products require fresher evidence than low-risk ambient products.  
   - If snapshot is stale, perform targeted cycle count or availability-to-promise check before release.  
   - Manual reservation tokens must be unique and reconciled later.

5. **Capacity fairness rule**  
   - Protect a minimum share of capacity for Pharmacy-standard orders.  
   - Do not allow Pharmacy-standard fill to collapse while hospital fill is protected, unless management explicitly approves the trade-off.

## 4.3 Minimum controls by product and customer risk

| Segment / product | Mandatory real-time controls | Permitted degraded-mode substitute | Hard stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hospital-critical ambient** | Customer/product authorization, inventory reservation, pick/pack identity, dispatch record, proof of delivery. | Manual authorization; manual pick list; paper dispatch manifest; phone POD if scripted and time-bound. | Do not ship if customer authorization cannot be manually evidenced and retrospective validation is impossible. |
| **Hospital-critical cold-chain** | All ambient controls plus temperature documentation, qualified packaging, monitor activation, stability-window check. | Manual temperature record, paper packing certificate, offline POD with cold-chain acceptance. | `!` Do not dispatch if temperature evidence or stability window cannot be established, unless QA grants documented clinical emergency exception. |
| **Controlled-substance, any customer** | Authorization, dual control, pharmacist/QA release, security seal, lot/serial or register record, dispatch custody, recipient verification. | Manual controlled register, dual signatures, paper manifest with courier signature, offline authorization, retrospective system upload. | `!` Do not dispatch without dual control and custody evidence. Phone POD alone is insufficient if recipient identity/security evidence is missing. |
| **Pharmacy-standard ambient** | Authorization, inventory reservation, pick/pack, dispatch, POD. | Manual authorization, manual pick list, paper dispatch manifest, delayed POD if captured within SLA. | Do not assume authorization. Do not close invoice without reconciliation or remediation task. |
| **Wholesale-bulk ambient** | Authorization, inventory reservation, pick/pack, dispatch, POD. | Deferral allowed. If processed, use same manual evidence discipline as pharmacy-standard. | Do not consume P1 emergency capacity unless explicitly approved. |
| **Recall-sensitive items, any customer** | Lot/expiry confirmation, traceability, authorization, release check, dispatch evidence. | Manual lot/expiry record, QA release, paper manifest. | `!` Do not dispatch if lot traceability is uncertain. |

## 4.4 Manual evidence capture requirements

Every manual action must create a recoverable evidence object.

Required evidence fields:

- Disruption case ID.  
- Order ID.  
- Customer type and customer identifier.  
- Product identifier, lot/expiry where applicable.  
- Operator ID.  
- Verifier or witness ID where required.  
- Timestamp.  
- Action performed.  
- Exception or deviation reason.  
- Authorizing person for deviations.  
- Link to later reconciliation task.

Evidence artifacts should include:

- Paper or e-form authorization sheets.  
- Offline customer authorization extracts with version/timestamp.  
- Manual inventory snapshot printout showing snapshot time.  
- Manual pick list with picker signature.  
- Manual pack/release sheet.  
- Controlled-substance manual register entry.  
- Cold-chain temperature log or manual temperature record.  
- Courier manifest or signed paper handoff as scan surrogate.  
- Phone POD script record: caller ID, recipient name, time, product accepted, condition accepted.  
- Spreadsheet invoice hold entries locked, versioned, and matched to reconciliation tasks.

Evidence upload SLA:

- High-risk products: evidence uploaded or filed within 24 hours.  
- Low-risk ambient products: within 2 business days.  
- Any missing mandatory evidence becomes an exception case immediately.

## 4.5 Backlog prioritization model

When systems recover, backlog should not be processed purely first-in-first-out. Use an explicit score:

```text
Backlog score =
  40% clinical criticality
+ 25% product risk
+ 15% time in backlog
+ 10% shelf-life or expiry urgency
+ 10% fairness / customer impact
```

Rules:

1. Hospital-critical clinical orders receive highest priority.  
2. Controlled-substance and cold-chain orders receive product-risk upgrade.  
3. Recall-sensitive replacement orders receive priority to avoid patient safety impact.  
4. Pharmacy-standard orders receive a protected minimum processing share.  
5. Wholesale-bulk orders may remain deferred, but deferral age and cancellation impact must be reported.  
6. Customers affected by deferral receive proactive communication.  
7. Backlog prioritization decisions are logged and auditable.

## 4.6 Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow

```text
Manual work completed
  Daily three-way reconciliation [M]
    Order / inventory / dispatch / POD / invoice evidence complete?
       Yes:
         Move to operational close.
       No, low-risk ambient:
         Provisional close with remediation task [SLA: 5 business days].
       No, controlled / cold-chain / recall-sensitive / authorization missing:
         Compliance hold [M]
          QA review
          Evidence reconstruction or documented risk assessment
          Final close only after remediation or exception approval.
```

Required reconciliation checks:

1. **Authorization reconciliation**  
   - Every shipped order must have authorization evidence or documented emergency authorization.  
   - Skipped authorization cases must be retrospectively validated or escalated.

2. **Inventory reconciliation**  
   - Manual reservation tokens matched to system reservations once available.  
   - Manual inventory snapshot discrepancies investigated.  
   - Lot/expiry allocation confirmed for cold-chain, controlled, and recall-sensitive products.

3. **Dispatch and custody reconciliation**  
   - Every dispatch must have system scan, surrogate scan, or signed courier manifest.  
   - Missing dispatch evidence must be treated as traceability exception.

4. **Proof-of-delivery reconciliation**  
   - Phone POD accepted only where risk permits.  
   - Controlled/cold-chain orders require stronger POD or QA exception.

5. **Invoice and audit reconciliation**  
   - Do not allow `Spreadsheet invoice hold  END` without matching evidence.  
   - Do not allow `Invoice close  Audit remediation` to remain invisible.  
   - Audit remediation queue should have age, owner, and closure SLA.

The redesigned process separates **operational close** from **compliance close**. Operational close means the goods moved, the customer received them, and the invoice can be processed. Compliance close means the control evidence is complete or formally dispositioned. For high-risk products, compliance close must be explicit.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should accept temporary, documented deviations that preserve hospital-critical supply and substitute comparable manual controls, including manual authorization, paper manifests, timestamped inventory snapshots, temporary pharmacist release, deferred non-urgent bulk orders, and bounded after-the-fact reconciliation, provided evidence is captured and remediated within defined SLAs. Management should not accept silent skipping of authorization, dispatch without custody evidence for controlled or cold-chain products, missing temperature documentation, missing dual-control, or treating invoice close plus delayed audit remediation as equivalent to real-time compliance. The evidence that should change this recommendation is clear: if a future manual phase maintains hospital fill while traceability exceptions, cold-chain documentation gaps, controlled-substance dual-control gaps, and audit-remediation age remain within pre-approved tolerances, then more flexible manual pathways can be authorized. If, however, dispatch-without-scan events, stale inventory decisions, or unresolved audit remediation remain high after operational metrics recover, management should tighten controls, restrict fast-lane authorizations, and treat the recovery as incomplete until compliance evidence is restored.
