# 1. Cluster diagnoses

Assumption A1: clock fields written as `HH:MM` are minute-aligned point stamps (or already rounded to the source’s stated precision); `recorded_at` values with seconds are used as given. Assumption A2: `sequence_no` is insertion order, not independent proof of execution order. Assumption A3: a repeated resource ID is the same worker except where the prompt marks a shared account.

# 2. Expected temporal signatures

The matrix is what the raw fields should look like if that explanation is the data-generating process. “Smaller tolerance” means shrinking `merge_distance` toward 0, or refusing to treat timestamps as equal once they are coarser than the stated precision.

| explanation | start equality | end equality | adjacency (`start_{i+1}≈complete_i`) | overlap | recording lag (`recorded_at−complete`) | source precision | resource identity reliability | queue-arrival pattern | under a smaller time tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| genuine simultaneous batch | required, same cycle open | required, same cycle close | absent | total (identical intervals) | short; inserts often nearly simultaneous and ordered only by `sequence_no` | equality at **fine** precision (G1, 1 s) is strong; equality only at a coarse bin is weak | usually one station/machine; capacity should match batch size | staggered arrivals that pile up **before** a common start (fixture load) | exact identical bounds remain one batch at `merge_distance=0` |
| start batching / common setup | required (shared “begin”) | absent; completes staggered | completes often form a near-adjacent chain | recorded intervals nest from the common start | typically **per case**, tracking each complete (G2: +1 min) | common on tablet/UI minute clocks | one worker; concurrency is a recording artefact | arrivals before the group start, not necessarily during work | the start-equal group survives if starts are literally identical; inferred sequential starts would split it |
| end batching / bulk submission | absent, or dummy | required (folder/shift submit) | starts often near-adjacent if work was serial | recorded intervals share a common end | **shared**: many cases get the same `recorded_at` just after the common complete (G3, G6) | common on forms | one submitter | arrivals can be spread over the whole working window | the end-equal group survives any sub-minute merge; it is a logging batch, not proof of a common finish |
| sequential batching | absent | absent | required, touching or near-touching | none except an instantaneous touch | **per case**, a few seconds after each complete on scanners (G4, G8) | 1 s scanners make adjacency credible | one worker | either a short assembled queue then a drain (G4) or a long blocked queue then a drain (G8) | exact adjacency still groups at `merge_distance=0`; slack adjacency splits |
| concurrent work or multitasking | absent | absent | absent | **partial**, staggered opens and closes | per case, not a single dump (G5) | 1 min stamps can invent small overlaps; large overlaps survive | reliable only with badge-level IDs; a pool ID cannot separate people from multitasking | roughly independent arrivals | tiny overlaps vanish; a 4-way overlap of several minutes does not |
| shared-account artifact | any | any | any | often looks like concurrency or >100% utilization under one ID | whatever the real people did, funneled through one login | any | **defining failure**: the ID is not a worker | any | tighter time tolerance does not restore identity |
| timestamp coarsening | frequent, snapped to bin edges | frequent, snapped to bin edges | destroyed or invented by rounding | often total inside one bin | `recorded_at` may stay finer and **serial** (G7: 14:16–14:20) | **defining**: precision ≫ true event spacing (15 min) | any | arrivals may fall **inside** the start bin → negative waits | equality below the bin width is meaningless; analysis must become interval-censored |
| queue accumulation after capacity loss | absent (unless mixed with another mechanism) | absent | typical **after** release: a sequential drain | none during the drain | per case, live | any | the idle resource is real if clocks agree | a tight arrival cluster **during** the idle window, then a long arrival→first-start gap, then adjacency | the drain is stable at `merge_distance=0`; the idle gap is the diagnostic, not the merge distance |
| another: dummy / end-of-shift admin close | start forced to a round hour | complete equal to start (zero duration) | none | degenerate (points) | one bulk insert (G6: all 17:01) | even a 1 min clock is “exact” for a dummy | one closer | hours-long arrival span ending well before the dummy stamp | identical dummy stamps never split |

Reading the eight clusters against that matrix:

- G1 matches genuine simultaneous **and** “one PLC cycle written to four serials.” Capacity context is what prefers the physical-batch reading.
- G2 is start-equal, end-staggered, per-case recording: start batching. Completes 8 / 6 / 7 / 6 min apart are the sequential-work signature underneath the common start.
- G3 is start-staggered, end-equal, one common `recorded_at`: end batching. Starts themselves look like a 7–8 min sequential chain.
- G4 is textbook sequential batching / single-piece drain; arrivals 11:58–12:02 and first start 12:05 are a short assembled queue, not a downtime pile.
- G5 is partial overlap with per-case recording: concurrent **or** a pool ID. It is not start/end/simultaneous.
- G6 is dummy zero-duration end-of-shift bulk submit. PM4Py will still call it simultaneous.
- G7 is coarsening: bin-aligned simultaneous pattern **plus** serial `recorded_at` **plus** negative waits.
- G8 is sequential drain after a ~30 min hold that lines up with a safety window; the hold is queue accumulation, the drain is sequential batching.

# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

Bias directions below take the naive estimators waiting = `start − queue_arrival`, service = `complete − start`, utilization = busy time of the resource ID / calendar time, throughput = completes per clock minute, bottleneck = longest mean wait or longest mean service. Conformance inherit the same distorted sojourn and resource-occupancy tokens.

**G1.** Waiting to the 09:00 cycle start is the right *batch* wait if the fixture is not released earlier; treating four cases as independent adds no extra wait bias beyond that. Service is a 3 min **cycle** shared by four serials. Counting four parallel 3 min services does not inflate utilization of RBT-7 (still one 3 min busy interval) but it **does** inflate case-level work content and can create a false “four concurrent Apply label” conformance variant. Throughput is 4 pieces per cycle, not 4 independent 3 min machines. Bottleneck detection should sit on fixture cycle time and load-build wait (08:54–09:00), not on a 3 min per-case service.

**G2.** Recorded services are 8, 14, 21, 27 min; if work was serial after one “begin group,” actual services are about 8, 6, 7, 6 min. Later cases have **inflated service** and **deflated waiting** (their wait is hidden after 10:00 inside the open interval). If T-B is treated as concurrently busy on four overlapping intervals, utilization is 8+14+21+27 = 70 min of work in a 27 min window (~259%), a false overload. Wall-clock busy time 10:00–10:27 is about right if they worked straight through. Bottleneck signals flip from “inspection is slow” (service view) to “one inspector, sequential” (resource view).

**G3.** If starts are true first-touches and 11:30 is a folder submit, waiting is roughly usable (~18–22 min) but service is **inflated for early folder members** (E01 recorded 28 min vs ~8 min if the next start is the true complete) and possibly about right only for the last case. Utilization of T-C becomes a single 11:02–11:30 occupancy if one interval is unioned, or a stack of overlapping intervals if not. Conformance “review duration vs SOP” will fail early cases. Bottleneck: a long recorded service on E01 is an end-batch artefact.

**G4.** Little lifecycle bias. Waiting 7, 10, 13, 16, 19 min is mostly **queueing behind the previous 4 min pack**, which is a real, well-measured sequential bottleneck. Utilization ~20 min busy in the 12:05–12:25 window is high and honest. Throughput 5 / 20 min is the right station rate. The only analytic mistake is to feed the adjacent chain into a “sequential batch” KPI and then also count each case’s wait as an independent arrival process without a remaining-work term.

**G5.** Waiting ~15 min looks stable (possibly a post-midday pickup). Service 25/15/20/10 min may be real open times, including idle-in-the-form. Utilization of `analyst_pool` as one resource exceeds 100% and hits four-way overlap — unusable. If three analysts are assumed, the 13:12–13:20 window still over-subscribes the stated headcount, so either headcount, multitasking, or stamps are wrong. Bottleneck “analyst pool is overloaded” is directionally plausible and numerically unidentified. Conformance on “one resource, one case” will fire continuously.

**G6 — why waiting explodes and service goes to zero.** The form writes the same 17:00 into both lifecycle fields. Service = `complete − start` = 0 for every case, so the station looks infinitely fast and utilization looks like 0 (or like a Dirac spike of seven completes). Waiting = `17:00 − queue_arrival` is 35 min (C07) to 175 min (C01). That wait is **time from upstream arrival to administrative close**, not time in a repair or review queue. Throughput shows a 17:00 burst of seven closes. Bottleneck detection reports a huge queue in front of a zero-service resource: the classic “infinite server after a long delay” mirage. Any conformance rule that requires a positive processing time, or that compares close latency to an SLA, will either auto-pass (zero work) or auto-fail (multi-hour close), both for a logging reason.

**G7.** Naive service is 15 min for all five; naive waits include **negative** values (M02–M05 arrive after 14:00). Utilization looks like five simultaneous 15 min jobs (500%) or one 15 min job, depending on whether overlaps are believed. Both are unidentified inside a quarter-hour bin. Bottleneck and throughput at a resolution finer than 15 min are not identified. Interval-censored analysis can only bound service in (0, 15+] min and start in a bin that already contains some arrivals.

**G8.** Service 7 min × 5 is credible. Waiting 38–58 min is **mostly the safety hold plus residual sequential wait**. If the safety clock is behind the scanner, wait is overstated; if ahead, understated. The 15:00–15:08 arrival cluster during the hold is genuine queue accumulation; treating it as ordinary congestion without the downtime event mis-labels the bottleneck as “triage is slow to start” rather than “triage was blocked.” Utilization 15:38–16:13 is a clean busy spell; 15:00–15:38 should be idle or “paused,” not missing data.

Cross-cutting: start-batches move delay from wait into service; end-batches and dummy closes move work out of service and, when start is also replaced, into wait; coarsening both censors and can flip the sign of wait; shared IDs break utilization and any resource-based conformance; sequential drains are the one pattern whose case-level service is usually safe if precision is fine.

def parse_clock(value: str) -> datetime:
    fmt = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S" if value.count(":") == 2 else "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"
    return datetime.strptime(f"{DAY} {value}", fmt)


def pandas_pattern(g: pd.DataFrame) -> str:
    """Independent label; never drop zero-duration or coarsened rows."""
    n = len(g)
    zero_svc = int((g["complete_dt"] <= g["start_dt"]).sum())
    same_start = g["start_dt"].nunique() == 1
    same_end = g["complete_dt"].nunique() == 1
    if zero_svc == n and same_start and same_end:
        return "dummy_or_zero_duration_bulk"
    if same_start and same_end:
        return "simultaneous_or_coarsened"
    if same_start and not same_end:
        return "start_equal_end_staggered"
    if same_end and not same_start:
        return "end_equal_start_staggered"
    ordered = g.sort_values(["start_dt", "sequence_no"])
    gaps = (
        ordered["start_dt"].iloc[1:].reset_index(drop=True)
        - ordered["complete_dt"].iloc[:-1].reset_index(drop=True)
    ).dt.total_seconds()
    if len(gaps) and (gaps == 0).all():
        return "exact_sequential"
    if len(gaps) and (gaps >= 0).all() and (gaps <= 60).all():
        return "near_sequential"
    pairs = list(itertools.combinations(range(n), 2))
    overlaps = 0
    for i, j in pairs:
        a, b = g.iloc[i], g.iloc[j]
        if a["start_dt"] < b["complete_dt"] and b["start_dt"] < a["complete_dt"]:
            overlaps += 1
    if pairs and overlaps / len(pairs) >= 0.5:
        return "overlapping_concurrent_or_shared"
    return "mixed_or_unbatched"


def cluster_diagnostics(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    rows = []
    for cluster, g in df.groupby("cluster", sort=True):
        g = g.sort_values(["start_dt", "sequence_no"]).reset_index(drop=True)
        n = len(g)
        pairs = list(itertools.combinations(range(n), 2))
        n_pairs = max(len(pairs), 1)
        overlaps = 0
        max_overlap = 0
        for t0 in pd.unique(pd.concat([g["start_dt"], g["complete_dt"]])):
            open_n = int(((g["start_dt"] <= t0) & (g["complete_dt"] > t0)).sum())
            max_overlap = max(max_overlap, open_n)
        for i, j in pairs:
            a, b = g.iloc[i], g.iloc[j]
            if a["start_dt"] < b["complete_dt"] and b["start_dt"] < a["complete_dt"]:
                overlaps += 1
        ordered = g
        adj = 0
        if n >= 2:
            adj = int(
                (
                    ordered["start_dt"].iloc[1:].reset_index(drop=True)
                    == ordered["complete_dt"].iloc[:-1].reset_index(drop=True)
                ).sum()
            )
        wait_s = (g["start_dt"] - g["queue_dt"]).dt.total_seconds()
        svc_s = (g["complete_dt"] - g["start_dt"]).dt.total_seconds()
        lag_s = (g["recorded_dt"] - g["complete_dt"]).dt.total_seconds()
        rec_span = (g["recorded_dt"].max() - g["recorded_dt"].min()).total_seconds()
        arr_span = (g["queue_dt"].max() - g["queue_dt"].min()).total_seconds() / 60.0
        gap_after_arr = (g["start_dt"].min() - g["queue_dt"].max()).total_seconds() / 60.0
        prec_sec = {"1 sec": 1, "1 min": 60, "15 min": 900}[g["precision"].iloc[0]]
        start_mod = int((g["start_dt"].dt.minute.mod(15).eq(0) & g["start_dt"].dt.second.eq(0)).all())
        rows.append(
            {
                "cluster": cluster,
                "n": n,
                "resource": g["resource"].iloc[0],
                "precision": g["precision"].iloc[0],
                "pandas_pattern": pandas_pattern(g),
                "same_start_n": int(g.groupby("start_dt").size().max()),
                "same_end_n": int(g.groupby("complete_dt").size().max()),
                "adj_pairs": adj,
                "overlap_pair_rate": round(overlaps / n_pairs, 2),
                "max_concurrency": max_overlap,
                "mean_svc_min": round(svc_s.mean() / 60.0, 2),
                "min_wait_min": round(wait_s.min() / 60.0, 2),
                "max_wait_min": round(wait_s.max() / 60.0, 2),
                "neg_wait_n": int((wait_s < 0).sum()),
                "zero_svc_n": int((svc_s <= 0).sum()),
                "mean_rec_lag_s": round(lag_s.mean(), 1),
                "rec_span_s": int(rec_span),
                "arr_span_min": round(arr_span, 1),
                "start_after_last_arr_min": round(gap_after_arr, 1),
                "bin_aligned_15min": bool(start_mod and prec_sec >= 60),
                "suspect_lifecycle": bool((svc_s <= 0).any() or (wait_s < 0).any() or prec_sec >= 900),
            }
        )
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)


def flatten_pm4py_batches(obj, path=""):
    """Walk unknown PM4Py return shapes and collect (path, type_name, size)."""
    found = []
    if obj is None:
        return found
    if isinstance(obj, dict):
        for key, val in obj.items():
            key_s = str(key)
            if isinstance(val, list) and val and isinstance(val[0], tuple):
                for item in val:
                    btype = str(item[0]) if item else "unknown"
                    payload = item[1] if len(item) > 1 else None
                    size = len(payload) if hasattr(payload, "__len__") and not isinstance(payload, str) else None
                    found.append((f"{path}/{key_s}".strip("/"), btype, size))
            else:
                found.extend(flatten_pm4py_batches(val, f"{path}/{key_s}".strip("/")))
    elif isinstance(obj, list):
        for item in obj:
            if isinstance(item, tuple) and item:
                btype = str(item[0])
                payload = item[1] if len(item) > 1 else None
                size = len(payload) if hasattr(payload, "__len__") and not isinstance(payload, str) else None
                found.append((path, btype, size))
            else:
                found.extend(flatten_pm4py_batches(item, path))
    return found

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

**If resource IDs are trusted as workers.** G1 (RBT-7) and G4/G8 (scanners on T-D / T-H) stay stable: one station, one stream. G2 and G3 become “one person, logging batch,” which is already the preferred reading. G5 becomes internally inconsistent: one trusted worker cannot honestly occupy four overlapping analyses unless the explanation flips to multitasking or bad timestamps; utilization is then a data-quality flag, not a staffing KPI. G6 remains an admin close regardless of who T-F is.

**If resource IDs are treated as possibly shared.** G5 is the designed case: overlap is then **not** evidence of multitasking by one analyst and **not** a clean headcount of three. G2/G3 could in theory be two people sharing T-B / T-C, which would revive a concurrent-work reading — but the UI/form context and the single common start (G2) or single common submit (G3) still favor logging batches over two people. G1’s machine ID is not a shared human account; sharing is the wrong alternative there.

**If timestamps are taken as exact points.** G1’s identical 1 s bounds, G4/G8 adjacency, G3’s common 11:30, and G6’s common 17:00 are all sharp. Negative waits in G7 become a hard contradiction (work started before arrival). G5’s 4-way overlap is a hard contradiction of the “≤3 analysts” note.

**If timestamps are interval-censored by stated precision.** G7’s entire simultaneous pattern can be produced by any set of starts in [13:45, 14:15) and completes in [14:15, 14:30) under an undocumented snap rule; sequential, simultaneous, and start/end batching are not separable. G2/G3/G5/G6 (1 min) still have structure larger than one minute, so their qualitative labels survive, but G5’s exact overlap seconds do not. G1/G4/G8 (1 s) change little: equality and adjacency remain informative.

Three (actually five) patterns that support more than one physical story:

1. **Identical start and complete (G1 and G7, and PM4Py-G6).** This is genuine fixture concurrency (G1), quarter-hour coarsening (G7), or a dummy admin stamp (G6). The discriminator is not the equality; it is precision, `recorded_at` seriality, arrival-vs-start sign, source type, and capacity context.

2. **Common start, staggered ends (G2).** This is a “begin group” logging batch, genuine parallel inspection, or sequential work whose start was overwritten. Separate complete buttons plus ~6–8 min complete spacing prefer overwritten sequential work, not four-up physical inspection.

3. **Common end, staggered starts (G3).** This is bulk folder submit, a true common finish after interleaved review, or sequential work with only the last complete retained. One shared `recorded_at` and the stated T-C habit prefer submit-batching.

4. **Exact adjacency (G4 and G8).** This is sequential batching, ordinary single-piece flow, or a scanner that will not open a second case. G8 adds a long pre-start idle that can be downtime, clock skew versus the safety system, or an unrecorded setup. Same drain, different waiting-time story.

5. **Partial overlap under one ID (G5).** This is two-to-three analysts on a pool login, one analyst with several forms open, leftover VDI sessions, or 1 min stamps on nearly sequential work. The 8 min 4-way window is too wide for 1 min coarsening of a clean serial schedule, so the remaining ambiguity is identity, not coarsening.

A further alternative for G5 only: a midday break with a ~13:00 pickup can explain the common ~15 min wait without explaining the overlaps.

# 6. Data-collection redesign

Priority is “what most reduces the specific confusions in G1–G8,” not a generic observability wishlist.

1. **Execution vs recording.** Keep `recorded_at`. Require `start` and `complete` to be execution lifecycle or explicitly null. Forbid writing submit-time or shift-end into both fields (G6, G3). A third field `submitted_at` belongs on forms.

2. **No silent defaults.** Reject or flag `start == complete`, `complete < start`, and `start < queue_arrival` at ingest. G6 and G7 should never enter a performance mart as point intervals.

3. **Identity.** Badge or session-level worker ID in addition to station / pool / machine. Retire `analyst_pool` as the only resource grain. Shared VDI can remain a device field.

4. **Group / batch / fixture identifiers.** When RBT-7 loads a fixture, write `batch_id`, `fixture_id`, and `batch_size` (capacity 4, actual load). Distinguish physical batch membership from “times happened to match.”

5. **Workstation capacity and UI semantics.** Persist “begin group” as `group_start` not as each case’s `start` (G2). Persist “submit folder” as `folder_submit` not as each case’s `complete` (G3). Store button names with the event.

6. **Pause / resume and downtime on the process clock.** Safety-check, breakdown, and break events must share the scanner/MES timeline (G8). A parallel safety clock is only usable with a measured offset.

7. **Clock discipline.** NTP (or a documented offset table) across PLC, tablet, desktop, scanner, VDI, MES, and safety. Store timezone and clock-source ID.

8. **Precision and accuracy metadata.** Keep the current `precision` field; add `rounding_rule` (especially G7’s undocumented quarter-hour snap), `accuracy_unknown` flag, and store timestamps at native resolution rather than pre-rounding to 15 min.

9. **Lifecycle completeness.** Optional `pause`, `resume`, `setup_start`, `setup_end`. Sequential scanners already give good process times; the missing piece is the blocked interval before the first scan.

10. **`sequence_no` plus execution order.** Insertion order is useful (G1, G7). It is not a substitute for execution timestamps.

Until those exist, downstream rule: run the pandas diagnostics in §4, trust PM4Py only as a pattern detector, and apply the cluster-level corrections in §1 before any waiting/service KPI.

# 7. Final adjudication

**High confidence (usable after a stated transformation).**  
G4 can be used as-is for service, wait, utilization, and bottleneck (sequential 4 min pack). G6 is confidently **not** execution: exclude its start/complete from queue and service; at most treat `recorded_at` as an administrative close time. G1 is a physical/machine cycle of four: analyze one 09:00–09:03 fixture cycle, not four independent jobs. G2 is a start-logging batch: infer sequential starts from completion order before computing wait/service. G3 is an end-logging batch: do not use 11:30 as four true completes; starts may be kept as first-touch only after field confirmation.

**Exclude or interval-censor in queue analysis.**  
G6: exclude lifecycle entirely. G7: interval-censor to the 15 min MES bins; do not publish point waiting times (they include negative waits). G2 recorded starts and G3 recorded completes: do not enter uncorrected sojourn formulas. G1: do not explode one cycle into four concurrent service times.

**Require operational validation before any performance conclusion.**  
G5: how many people were on `analyst_pool`, and was 4-way overlap multitasking or leftover sessions? No utilization or staffing claim is identified. G8: measure the safety-vs-scanner clock offset and confirm the 15:00–15:35 hold; service times can be used now, waiting times only as bounds. G7: recover the MES rounding rule; until then only censored bounds. G1: confirm the fixture was actually loaded with four that cycle (capacity is not occupancy). G3: confirm whether 11:02/10/17/24 are work starts or form-open times.

Net rule for this extract: identical timestamps are a **logging pattern** until capacity, source, recording lag, arrival sign, and precision say otherwise. G4 is process. G6 is paperwork. G1 is a machine batch. G2/G3 are UI/form batches. G7 is a coarse clock. G5 is an identity failure. G8 is a downtime pile followed by a clean sequential drain.
