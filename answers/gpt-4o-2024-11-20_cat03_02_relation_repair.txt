# 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

Below is the classification and explanation of the rules and heuristics employed for reasoning about the dataset.

### Hard invariants
1. **Lifecycle order**: Activities must follow a plausible lifecycle order for object types (e.g., Orders must exist before they are invoiced or delivered). Illogical lifecycle sequences (e.g., Delivery before the corresponding Packaging) invalidate a repair.
2. **Cardinality bounds**: Activities such as `Add Item` (`one Order, one Item`), `Pack Item` (`one Item, one Package`), and `Record Payment` (`one Payment, one Invoice`) suggest strict cardinality, barring exceptional circumstances.
3. **Consistency of attributes**: Key attributes (e.g., customer, declared value, amounts, carrier identifiers) must align across related objects and events.
4. **E2O/O2O cross-validation**: Relations deduced from events (E2O) must be consistent with O2O links, avoiding circular reasoning.

### Strong defaults
1. **Identifier prefix matching**: Events and objects with similar prefixes (e.g., `ORD-A`, `IT-A1`, `PK-7`) are strongly indicative of a connection when other factors (e.g., timestamps, lifecycle order) agree.
2. **Calibrated amounts**: Arithmetic matches (e.g., declared values and invoice totals) provide evidence but are not conclusive on their own.
3. **Temporal adjacency**: Events occurring within close time frames are likely to relate to each other unless other evidence suggests otherwise (e.g., `Dispatch Package` immediately following `Pack Item` for the same package).

### Weak heuristics
1. **Route matching**: Shared routes (e.g., `NORTH`) between deliveries, packages, and their associated events weakly suggest potential relations.
2. **Behavioral exceptions**: Scanner outages or bulk dispatches might create ambiguous or missing relations, but these should be reconciled when possible without over-repair.

### Evidence against a repair
1. **Ambiguity without resolution**: If multiple plausible connections exist (e.g., between a delivery and several packages), forcing one arbitrarily is discouraged.
2. **Excessive cardinality violations**: Adding relations that inflate many-to-many mappings (e.g., multiple deliveries for one package without lifecycle justification) weakens plausibility.

### Combining evidence
- Match identifiers first and ensure lifecycle compatibility, then weigh time and attributes for E2O and O2O relations.
- Use weak heuristics only as tiebreakers or when hard/stable evidence is insufficient.
- Leave unresolved cases when uncertainty remains high (e.g., multiple plausible targets with equal confidence).
