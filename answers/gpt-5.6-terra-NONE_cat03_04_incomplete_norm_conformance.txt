## 1. Conformance semantics

A conformance judgment depends on both the observed relation and the status, scope, and abstraction of the applicable norm.

- **Forbidden behavior**: an observed relation explicitly prohibited by a norm, within its stated scope. This is a strong nonconformance finding unless the observation is shown to be erroneous. Examples: N3 (`Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package`) and T6 (`Order object_cobirth Payment`).

- **Missing mandatory behavior**: absence of a required relation where the norm’s scope clearly applies. This is a conformance gap, but not necessarily proof of operational failure: it may arise from incomplete logging, an incorrect object association, or an abstraction mismatch. Examples: N4 gaps for externally shipped packages and E6 gaps for dispatch events.

- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior**: an observed relation absent from the normative graph but not forbidden and not covered by a mandatory expectation. Under the open-world portions of the norm, it is not a violation. It may be benign, a candidate for future modeling, or evidence that the norm is incomplete. Examples: `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, `Reopen Order -> Close Order`, Package–Delivery `object_codeath`, and Manual Compliance Review–Customer.

- **Typical-pattern absence**: deviation from a common/desirable relation, not an automatic breach. It should be investigated proportionately and interpreted against known legitimate exceptions. Examples: invoices without observed payment under N5 and invoice-payment interaction variation under T3.

- **Likely data-quality error**: a relation or gap for which contextual evidence indicates an extraction, join, identity-resolution, timestamp, or event-object linkage problem. The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge is the clearest case: it is forbidden by E10, but the stated reused-identifier join creates a strong data-quality explanation.

- **Abstraction mismatch**: a discrepancy caused by the model representing a business expectation at a different level than the underlying rule. N1 says validation should eventually occur, while the OC-DFG summary approximates this using directly-follows. An intervening review event can make a direct edge absent even when the business rule is satisfied.

- **Insufficient evidence**: a graph summary alone does not establish whether an exception is legitimate, whether objects belong to the stated scope, whether the sequence is temporal, or whether a relation is complete. The summaries lack event-level ordering, lifecycle state, object identity lineage, and completeness guarantees.

The norm is **closed-world only for explicit prohibitions and mandatory relations in their stated scope**. Therefore, an observed forbidden relation is presumptively nonconforming, and a mandatory relation gap needs explanation. The norm is otherwise **open-world**: absence of a relation from the normative edge set does not make an observed relation noncompliant. Typical relations are expectations, not hard constraints; unspecified relations are intentionally outside this norm.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG-style summary establishes observed directly-follows relations for object lifecycles and their aggregate counts. It can identify explicit forbidden sequences, such as the two N3 Order paths from cancellation to dispatch, and it can flag missing direct relations against a mandatory approximation.

It cannot, by itself, prove full business-path compliance. It does not establish whether all relevant events were logged, whether timestamps are reliable, whether the relation is directly consecutive versus eventually reachable, or whether the package involved is the same operational fulfilment unit intended by the Order.

The central issue is N1. The business wording is that a newly created standard Order should **eventually enter validation**. A directly-follows edge is only an approximation. If `Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review -> Validate Order` occurred, then the Order satisfies the eventual business expectation but lacks the direct OC-DFG edge. The two apparent N1 gaps therefore require trace-level review rather than immediate classification as failed validation.

N4 is stronger because it concerns packaging before dispatch for externally shipped packages. Yet the summary still requires scope confirmation and sequence inspection: a missing direct edge may reflect an intervening event or an omitted packing scan. By contrast, N3 is an explicit prohibited ordering and is substantially more serious, although object matching and timestamps still need validation.

N2 and N5 are typical, not mandatory. The 18 invoices without observed payment are not automatically defects; four write-offs are expressly compatible with N5, while the remaining 14 may be open credit, later payments outside the observation window, or missing data.

### OTG

The OTG captures relationships among object types, not event-level participation or exact temporal sequencing. It can establish that Order–Item, Package–Delivery, Invoice–Payment, Order–Customer, Order–RiskCase, and Order–Payment relationships occur in the observed graph.

T6 is unambiguous at the semantic level: an Order and Payment may not be co-born. The observed one edge is therefore a high-priority prohibited relation, subject to verification that the cobirth was not manufactured by ETL event grouping.

The 200 Package–Delivery interaction edges support the presence of relationships overall, but they do not prove that every dispatch event has the required Delivery association. That event-level gap appears under E6. Similarly, 92 Invoice–Payment interactions do not establish that every Record Payment is linked to an Invoice; the ET–OT table shows one exception.

The 100 Order–Customer edges and three Order–RiskCase edges are not violations because T4 and T5 are unspecified. The seven Package–Delivery codeath edges are likewise unregulated. A relation can be acceptable in OTG while suspicious in ET–OT: carrier-reference-only dispatches may look operationally connected in attributes but still fail the mandatory Delivery object linkage required by E6 and T2.

### ET–OT

ET–OT relations establish whether an event type was connected to required object types. This is the most direct model family for E1–E10. It shows:

- Create Order–Order: 100, satisfying E1 at aggregate level.
- Add Item–Item: 1450, satisfying E3.
- Add Item–Order: 1448, revealing two apparent E2 gaps.
- Pack Package–Package: 200, satisfying E4.
- Dispatch Package–Package: 200, satisfying E5.
- Dispatch Package–Delivery: 198, revealing two E6 gaps.
- Record Payment–Payment: 93, satisfying E7.
- Record Payment–Invoice: 92, revealing one E8 gap.
- Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice: one explicit E10 violation, likely caused by the described bad join.

Counts matter, but aggregate counts do not always identify exactly which events are missing links unless event cardinalities are known. For example, 1450 Item links and 1448 Order links suggest two Add Item events lack Orders only if each event is expected to have one link of each type and there are no multi-object events. Event-level inspection is necessary.

ET–OT cannot establish that a Dispatch followed packing, that payment settled the right invoice, or that a Sensor Heartbeat truly referenced an Invoice rather than a reused identifier. Those require event content, object identity rules, and source-system provenance.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

**Mandatory**
- E1: 100 Create Order–Order links observed; apparently satisfied.
- E3: 1450 Add Item–Item links observed; apparently satisfied.
- E4/E5/E7: Pack–Package, Dispatch–Package, and Record Payment–Payment are observed at expected aggregate levels.
- N1: two apparent Create-to-Validate gaps are mandatory only if “eventually” is not satisfied through intervening or unrecorded events.
- N4: two Pack-to-Dispatch gaps are mandatory if both packages are externally shipped and direct-following is the intended abstraction.
- E2: two apparent Add Item–Order gaps require reconciliation.
- T2/E6: two dispatches lack Delivery links and are mandatory gaps unless the Delivery object exists but was omitted by extraction.
- E8: one payment lacks Invoice; it is a mandatory gap unless it is correctly established and typed as unapplied cash.

**Forbidden**
- N3: two `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` relations.
- T6: one Order–Payment object cobirth edge.
- E10: one Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge; operationally likely a data-quality defect, but still prohibited if the relation is real.

**Typical**
- N2: 83 Validate-to-Close paths support a normal but non-mandatory closure pattern.
- N5: 92 invoice-to-payment paths are typical; four write-offs are legitimate stated exceptions; 14 no-successor invoices need aging/reconciliation, not automatic breach.
- T3: 92 Invoice–Payment interactions are typical; the unmatched payment may be valid unapplied cash only when properly classified.

**Unregulated**
- 72 `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice` relations.
- One `Reopen Order -> Close Order` relation under N6.
- 100 Order–Customer interactions under T4.
- Three Order–RiskCase and three Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase interactions under T5/E9.
- Three Manual Compliance Review–Customer interactions.
- Seven Package–Delivery codeath edges.
- These become governed only if another policy introduces a mandatory, forbidden, or typical requirement.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 conflates eventual validation with direct-following.**  
   Risk: legitimate reviewed Orders appear nonconforming, or genuinely unvalidated Orders are obscured.  
   Clarification: state that validation must occur eventually before cancellation/closure, with intervening review allowed.  
   Status: **mandatory**.

2. **“Standard Order” is undefined.**  
   Risk: impossible scope determination for N1.  
   Clarification: define standard versus exempt Order categories and the authoritative attribute.  
   Status: **mandatory scope definition**.

3. **N4 does not define whether intervening events are allowed.**  
   Risk: direct OC-DFG gaps may be mistaken for unpacked shipment.  
   Clarification: require a Pack Package event before Dispatch Package, not necessarily immediately before it.  
   Status: **mandatory**.

4. **Delivery carrier references are not reconciled with Delivery objects.**  
   Risk: operational shipment evidence may exist while mandatory traceability fails structurally.  
   Clarification: specify whether carrier reference may serve as a temporary Delivery surrogate and require eventual Delivery object creation.  
   Status: **mandatory**.

5. **Unapplied cash exception lacks a controlled event type or flag.**  
   Risk: missing Invoice links cannot be distinguished from broken payment allocation.  
   Clarification: require `Record Unapplied Cash` or a mandatory unapplied-cash classification attribute.  
   Status: **mandatory**.

6. **N3 does not distinguish cancellation timing, reversal, or post-cancellation recovery handling.**  
   Risk: a cancelled Order with already-dispatched goods may be misread as a prohibited post-cancellation dispatch.  
   Clarification: define temporal precedence, reversal events, and allowed return/recovery flows.  
   Status: **forbidden** for new dispatch after effective cancellation; related recovery flows **explicitly unspecified** or separately modeled.

7. **T6 does not define “same business event.”**  
   Risk: ETL batching or shared technical event IDs can create false cobirth violations.  
   Clarification: define authoritative business-event identity and exclude technical batch events.  
   Status: **forbidden**.

8. **No lifecycle semantics exist for Package–Delivery codeath.**  
   Risk: analysts may treat normal confirmation closure as anomalous.  
   Clarification: explicitly state that common confirmation-event codeath is permitted.  
   Status: **explicitly unspecified** or **typical** if it is expected.

9. **No observation-window policy exists for invoice payment.**  
   Risk: open invoices near the extraction cutoff appear deficient.  
   Clarification: define aging thresholds and distinguish open, overdue, written-off, credited, and unobserved-later-payment states.  
   Status: **typical**, with separate mandatory accounting controls if needed.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

### Immediate containment

1. Prioritize the two N3 cancellation-to-dispatch cases. Confirm whether goods left custody after cancellation; pause further fulfilment, refund, replacement, and inventory actions where needed.
2. Validate the T6 Order–Payment cobirth case before automated settlement, order release, or accounting posting.
3. Quarantine the E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice association from financial reporting and downstream analytics.

### Data repair

- Repair the reused numeric-identifier join causing the suspected E10 defect; introduce namespaced identifiers and referential-integrity checks.
- Reconcile the two missing Dispatch–Delivery links using carrier references and create or restore Delivery objects.
- Investigate the two Add Item events without Order links.
- Reconcile the unmatched payment; either allocate it to an Invoice or formally type it as unapplied cash.
- Validate whether the two apparent N1 and N4 gaps are missing events, intervening events, or genuine process exceptions.

### Process investigation

- For N3, inspect cancellation effective time, warehouse release, carrier pickup, staff overrides, and customer communication.
- For T6, inspect transaction boundaries, payment initiation mechanics, and event-generation design.
- For N4, compare warehouse scan logs with OCEL events to determine whether packing was skipped or merely unrecorded.
- Review the 14 invoices with no successor using terms and aging rather than treating them as compliance failures.

### Norm revision

- Replace direct-following approximations for N1 and N4 with precedence/eventuality rules where operationally intended.
- Define standard Order scope, external-shipment scope, unapplied-cash typing, business-event identity, and observation-window treatment.
- Explicitly document codeath and Customer/RiskCase relationships as outside this norm unless governance needs them.

### Monitoring

- Alert immediately on N3, T6, and E10 after data-quality validation.
- Monitor dispatches without Delivery objects, Add Item events without Orders, and payments without Invoice/unapplied-cash classification.
- Track invoice aging and exception outcomes rather than raw absence of payment.
- Publish separate dashboards for hard-control violations, data-quality defects, typical-pattern exceptions, and unregulated behavior.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are that N3 has two prohibited cancellation-to-dispatch observations, T6 has one prohibited Order–Payment cobirth observation, E10 has one forbidden Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice observation, and E6/T2 show two missing Delivery associations. The classification of `Validate Order -> Issue Invoice`, Manual Compliance Review–Customer, Package–Delivery codeath, and `Reopen Order -> Close Order` depends on the norm’s intentionally open and unspecified areas and is not nonconformance. The E10 conclusion strongly depends on the suspected bad identifier join, while N1 and N4 gaps depend on direct-following abstraction, scope, and log completeness. Graph summaries alone cannot decide actual event chronology, lifecycle scope, object identity correctness, whether validation eventually occurred, whether a shipment was truly post-cancellation, or whether unmatched payments were legitimate unapplied cash.
