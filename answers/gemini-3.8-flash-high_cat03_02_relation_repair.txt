## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

In OCEL 2.0, relational repair must balance observational fidelity against missing data. Events act as temporal hyperedges connecting entities via Event-to-Object (E2O) relations, while persistent structures are captured via Object-to-Object (O2O) relations. Domain assumptions are classified into four epistemological tiers:

### Classification of Rules

#### 1. Hard Invariants
Strict physical, temporal, and relational constraints; violations require immediate rejection:
- **Temporal Causality:** Events cannot act on objects prior to creation or post-termination. Packing cannot precede order/item creation; dispatch cannot precede packing; delivery confirmation cannot precede dispatch.
- **Referential Integrity:** E2O edges require $e \in \mathcal{E}, o \in \mathcal{O}$; O2O edges require $o_1, o_2 \in \mathcal{O}$ with typed qualifiers. Dangling references are prohibited.
- **Physical Exclusivity:** An item can occupy at most one container at any timestamp. Simultaneous multi-package containment without unpacking is impossible.
- **Containment Acyclicity:** Containment hierarchies must be DAGs ($o \not\to_{contains}^+ o$). Items cannot contain orders, nor packages carry deliveries.

#### 2. Strong Defaults
Expected business rules in nominal executions unless explicitly overridden:
- **Cardinality Defaults:** `Pack Item` relates to 1 Item and 1 Package (Domain Rule 3); `Dispatch Package` relates to 1 Package and 1 Delivery (Domain Rule 4).
- **Entity & Route Alignment:** Commercial events (invoicing, payments) and portal confirmations align with purchasing customer `C17`. Packages and deliveries require compatible routes (`route_hint=NORTH` matches `route=NORTH`).
- **Commercial Settlement:** Payments normally settle active customer invoices rather than floating indefinitely (Domain Rule 7).

#### 3. Weak Heuristics
Circumstantial indicators providing Bayesian support that cannot independently prove relations:
- **Identifier Similarity:** Lexical matching (`scanner_ref=PK8-2` to `PK-8`, `external_ref=N-421?` to `DEL-Y`, `N-42*` to `N-420`/`N-421`) suggests operational links.
- **Arithmetic Balance:** Exact numeric equivalence ($130 + 100 = 230$) suggests consolidation, but Domain Rule 6 notes fees, taxes, and discounts make arithmetic non-demonstrative.
- **Temporal Proximity & Hints:** Clustered events (`ev07`/`ev08` 1 min apart; `ev09`/`ev10` at 10:00) indicate batch processing. Attributes like `order_hint=ORD-A` reflect initial planning.

#### 4. Evidence Against a Repair
Negative signals contraindicating edge creation:
- **Cross-Order Contamination:** Packaging items across distinct orders (`IT-A2` from `ORD-A`, `IT-B1` from `ORD-B`) into one box (`PK-8`) merges independent customer lifecycles.
- **Contradictory References:** Mapping `DEL-X` (`carrier_ref=N-420`) to `ev10` (`external_ref=N-421?`) contradicts logged telemetry.
- **Unilateral Wildcard Disambiguation:** Attributing wildcard `external_ref=N-42*` exclusively to one delivery invents artificial certainty.
- **Forced Completionism:** Adding edges solely to eliminate open or in-progress lifecycles.

### Evidence Combination Framework
Candidate relations are evaluated across four orthogonal channels: administrative ($S_{id}$), physical ($S_{phys}$), temporal ($S_{temp}$), and financial ($S_{fin}$). A candidate is **accepted** only when corroborated by at least two independent channels without violating hard invariants. Single-heuristic evidence yields **provisional** or **unresolved** status. Invariant violations are **rejected**.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

To represent epistemic uncertainty, two coherent bundles are formulated alongside an edge-case baseline, reflecting distinct organizational interpretations across warehouse packaging, carrier dispatch, portal confirmation, and billing.

### Bundle A: Conservative / Minimum-Change Repair
Bundle A exercises epistemic restraint, repairing only operational dropouts supported by multi-channel evidence while preserving recorded ambiguities:
- **ev08 and PK-8:** Accepts PR-01 and PR-02. Scans `PK8-1` (09:04) and `PK8-2` (09:05) reflect packing continuity, reconciling scanner dropouts per Domain Rule 3. Weight (2.9 kg) accommodates both cables.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Accepts PR-03 and PR-04. Telemetry `N-421?` matches `DEL-Y` (`carrier_ref=N-421`). `DEL-X` is rejected due to conflicting reference `N-420`.
- **ev11 and Delivery Objects:** Left unresolved. Remains linked solely to `ORD-A`. Per Domain Rule 5, portal confirmations frequently lose carrier IDs; wildcard `N-42*` spans both deliveries.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Left unresolved. Remains linked solely to `ORD-A`. Per Domain Rule 6, arithmetic equality is not proof; `ORD-B` billing may be pending.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Left unresolved. Remains unapplied cash per Domain Rule 7; `INV-44` is partially settled by `PAY-1` ($100).

*Lifecycle Coherence:* Physical fulfillment completes for packages and deliveries. Commercial tiers honestly reflect in-flight fulfillment and unapplied cash.

### Bundle B: Consolidated Enterprise Hypothesis
Bundle B assumes an automated enterprise consolidation agreement for customer C17:
- **ev08 and PK-8:** Accepted identically to Bundle A (cross-order packaging).
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Accepted `DEL-Y` identically to Bundle A.
- **ev11 and Delivery Objects:** Linked to `DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`, and `ORD-B` as a bulk portal receipt under `N-42*`.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Accepts PR-08 and PR-09, consolidating `ORD-A` ($130) and `ORD-B` ($100) into `INV-44` ($230).
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Accepts PR-10 and PR-11, modeling two installment disbursements settling `INV-44` in full.

*Lifecycle Coherence:* All entities achieve terminal completion, but tightly entangles `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` across delivery, billing, and payment.

### Bundle C: Strict Segregated Logistics (Baseline)
Strict segregation baseline rejecting co-packaging: `ev08` is treated as an aborted pack or unrecorded package `PK-9`, leaving `IT-B1` unpacked. `ev10` is assigned to `DEL-Y`, but `PK-8` carries only `IT-A2`. Commercial relations remain unrepaired.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

Evaluating the log against object-centric lifecycle integrity requires auditing every object type under Bundle A, assessing orphan status, checking cardinalities, and verifying evidence independence.

### Object-by-Object Lifecycle Audit
1. **Order (`ORD-A`, `ORD-B`):** `ORD-A` completes a full lifecycle from creation (`ev01`) to billing (`ev12`). `ORD-B` exhibits an active in-flight lifecycle from creation (`ev02`) to dispatch (`ev10`). Lacking delivery confirmation and invoicing reflects normal in-progress fulfillment, not a data defect.
2. **Item (`IT-A1`, `IT-A2`, `IT-B1`):** All items achieve valid lifecycles (item addition followed by packaging). Cardinality is strictly $1:1$: each item belongs to one order (`ORD-A contains IT-A1/IT-A2`, `ORD-B contains IT-B1`) and resides in one package (`PK-7 packs IT-A1`, `PK-8 packs IT-A2/IT-B1`).
3. **Package (`PK-7`, `PK-8`):** `PK-7` packs one item (`IT-A1`) and dispatches via `DEL-X` (`ev09`). `PK-8` packs two items (`IT-A2`, `IT-B1`) across `ev07` and `ev08`, dispatching via `DEL-Y` (`ev10`). Both packages successfully transition to transit.
4. **Delivery (`DEL-X`, `DEL-Y`):** In the raw log, `DEL-Y` was an orphan with no events or O2O relations. Bundle A links `DEL-Y` to `ev10` (`DEL-Y carries PK-8`), resolving the orphan via carrier reference matching (`N-421?` to `N-421`). Neither delivery receives an artificial portal confirmation.
5. **Invoice (`INV-44`):** Issued in `ev12` for `ORD-A` ($230); receives partial settlement `PAY-1` ($100) in `ev13`. The open balance of $130 reflects active accounts receivable.
6. **Payment (`PAY-1`, `PAY-2`):** `PAY-1` ($100) settles `INV-44`. `PAY-2` ($130) recorded in `ev14` remains unlinked, correctly modeling unapplied cash per Domain Rule 7.

### Orphan Audit and Structural Cardinalities
- **Orphans:** No orphan events remain. `DEL-Y` is integrated into dispatch. `PAY-2` remains unapplied cash, representing a valid business state rather than a defect.
- **Cardinality Shifts:** `PK-8` co-packing introduces a $1:N$ package-to-item relation, inducing an $M:N$ entanglement between Orders and Packages (`ORD-A`/`ORD-B` with `PK-7`/`PK-8`). This is common in consolidated shipping but alters object-centric process discovery.

### Circular Reasoning Audit
1. *Relational Fallacy:* Inferring E2O edge `ev08 -> PK-8` from scanner data, synthesizing O2O edge `PK-8 packs IT-B1` from that event, and then citing the O2O edge as independent confirmation of the E2O edge is circular. Here, no independent O2O manifest exists; the O2O edge is strictly downstream.
2. *Financial Fallacy:* Inferring `INV-44 bills ORD-B` because $130 + 100 = 230$, and using `ORD-B` to claim `ev12` is verified, commits circular reasoning. Bundle A explicitly avoids this trap.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

Repair strategies directly alter graph topology, metrics, and discovered models across the Unrepaired Log, Bundle A, and Bundle B.

### 1. Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph (OC-DFG)
- **Unrepaired Log:** Package and Delivery graphs are disconnected. `Pack Item` (`ev08`) dead-ends for Item. Delivery has only `Dispatch Package (DEL-X)`; `DEL-Y` is absent. `Record Payment` (`ev14`) floats isolated.
- **Bundle A:** Connects `Pack Item (ev08)` $\to$ `Dispatch Package (ev10)` for `PK-8`; instantiates `Dispatch Package` across both `DEL-X` and `DEL-Y`. Flow diverges: `Confirm Delivery` follows dispatch only along `ORD-A`/`DEL-X`.
- **Bundle B:** Proliferates edges. `Confirm Delivery` becomes a multi-object join node from `Dispatch Package` across both deliveries and orders. Adds `Dispatch Package` $\to$ `Issue Invoice` for `ORD-B`. Adds cyclic loop `Record Payment` $\to_{Invoice}$ `Record Payment` (`ev13` $\to$ `ev14`).

### 2. Object Type Graph (OTG)
- **Unrepaired Log:** `DEL-Y` isolated; 1:1 `Invoice - Order` and `Payment - Invoice`.
- **Bundle A:** `Package - Item` ($1:N$) and `Delivery - Package` ($1:1$) fully connected. `Invoice - Order` remains $1:1$.
- **Bundle B:** `Invoice - Order` transitions to $1:N$; `Payment - Invoice` transitions to $N:1$, introducing a multi-object cycle between Order, Package, Item, Invoice, and Payment.

### 3. Event Type to Object Type (ET–OT) Graph
- **Unrepaired Log:** Partial participation for `Pack Item` $\to$ `Package` (2/3), `Dispatch Package` $\to$ `Delivery` (1/2), `Record Payment` $\to$ `Invoice` (1/2).
- **Bundle A:** `Pack Item` and `Dispatch Package` reach $100\%$ participation; `Record Payment` remains $50\%$.
- **Bundle B:** All event types reach $100\%$ participation; `Confirm Delivery` signature expands to $\{\text{Order}, \text{Delivery}\}$; `Issue Invoice` signature expands to $\{\text{Invoice}, \text{Order}_{multi}\}$.

### 4. Flattenings on Concrete Object Types
- **Order Flattening:** `ORD-A` shows 7 events in all versions. In Bundle A, `ORD-B` gains `Dispatch Package (ev10)` via `PK-8`. In Bundle B, `ORD-B` gains `Confirm Delivery (ev11)` and `Issue Invoice (ev12)`, duplicating `ORD-A`'s terminal sequence.
- **Package Flattening:** `PK-8` trace correctly adds `ev08` pack event in Bundles A & B: $\langle \text{Pack Item (ev07)}, \text{Pack Item (ev08)}, \text{Dispatch Package (ev10)} \rangle$.
- **Delivery Flattening:** In Unrepaired, `DEL-X` has `Dispatch Package`; `DEL-Y` is empty. In Bundle A, `DEL-Y` contains `Dispatch Package (ev10)`. In Bundle B, both deliveries contain $\langle \text{Dispatch Package}, \text{Confirm Delivery} \rangle$.
- **Invoice and Payment Flattening:** In Bundle A, `INV-44` shows a single payment (`ev13`); `PAY-2` shows an isolated payment (`ev14`). In Bundle B, `INV-44` contains $\langle \text{Issue Invoice}, \text{Record Payment (ev13)}, \text{Record Payment (ev14)} \rangle$.

### 5. Activity Frequencies Across Semantic Views
- **`Pack Item` (3 events):** Relates to 3 Items and 2 Packages. Total E2O edges increase from 5 (Unrepaired) to 6 (Bundle A & B), increasing Package participation by $50\%$.
- **`Issue Invoice` (1 event):** In Unrepaired and Bundle A, relates to 1 Invoice and 1 Order (2 E2O edges). In Bundle B, relates to 1 Invoice and 2 Orders (3 E2O edges), doubling Order participation.
- **`Confirm Delivery` (1 event):** In Unrepaired and Bundle A, relates to 1 Order (1 E2O edge). In Bundle B, relates to 2 Orders and 2 Deliveries (4 E2O edges), quadrupling participation.

### 6. Equivalent-Execution Clustering Around Order
- In the **Unrepaired Log**, `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` split into two distinct clusters: Completed Order ($50\%$) and Aborted Order ($50\%$).
- In **Bundle A**, `ORD-B` clusters as an *In-Flight Shipped Order*, cleanly separating physical logistics from commercial completion.
- In **Bundle B**, `ORD-A` and `ORD-B` are forced into the same cluster (*Completed Standard Order*), concealing delivery delays or unbilled balances.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

The script below demonstrates programmatic repair for Bundle A, executes referential-integrity validation, constructs an OCEL 2.0 object, discovers standard process models (OC-DFG, OTG, ET-OT), and flattens onto target types.

```python
"""
PM4Py-oriented validation script for OCEL 2.0 relational repair.
Applies Bundle A (conservative repair) and executes discovery and flattening.
"""

import pandas as pd
import pm4py


def validate_and_repair_ocel(
    events: pd.DataFrame,
    objects: pd.DataFrame,
    relations: pd.DataFrame,
    o2o: pd.DataFrame,
) -> pm4py.OCEL:
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 1. Defensively copy original tables prior to repair
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    events_rep = events.copy()
    objects_rep = objects.copy()
    relations_rep = relations.copy()
    o2o_rep = o2o.copy()

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 2. Append selected additions from Bundle A
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # E2O additions for Bundle A:
    # ev08 -> PK-8 (qualifier: 'packs')
    # ev10 -> DEL-Y (qualifier: 'dispatches')
    e2o_new = pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {
                "ocel:eid": "ev08",
                "ocel:oid": "PK-8",
                "ocel:qualifier": "packs",
            },
            {
                "ocel:eid": "ev10",
                "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",
                "ocel:qualifier": "dispatches",
            },
        ]
    )

    # O2O additions for Bundle A:
    # PK-8 -> IT-B1 (qualifier: 'packs')
    # DEL-Y -> PK-8 (qualifier: 'carries')
    o2o_new = pd.DataFrame(
        [
            {
                "ocel:oid": "PK-8",
                "ocel:qualifier": "packs",
                "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1",
            },
            {
                "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",
                "ocel:qualifier": "carries",
                "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",
            },
        ]
    )

    relations_rep = pd.concat([relations_rep, e2o_new], ignore_index=True)
    o2o_rep = pd.concat([o2o_rep, o2o_new], ignore_index=True)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 3. Referential integrity and duplicate validation checks
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    valid_eids = set(events_rep["ocel:eid"])
    valid_oids = set(objects_rep["ocel:oid"])

    # Validate E2O integrity
    e2o_dangling_events = relations_rep[~relations_rep["ocel:eid"].isin(valid_eids)]
    e2o_dangling_objects = relations_rep[~relations_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(valid_oids)]

    if not e2o_dangling_events.empty:
        raise ValueError(f"Referential integrity failure: Dangling event references in E2O: {e2o_dangling_events}")
    if not e2o_dangling_objects.empty:
        raise ValueError(f"Referential integrity failure: Dangling object references in E2O: {e2o_dangling_objects}")

    # Validate O2O integrity
    o2o_dangling_source = o2o_rep[~o2o_rep["ocel:oid"].isin(valid_oids)]
    o2o_dangling_target = o2o_rep[~o2o_rep["ocel:oid_2"].isin(valid_oids)]

    if not o2o_dangling_source.empty:
        raise ValueError(f"Referential integrity failure: Dangling source object in O2O: {o2o_dangling_source}")
    if not o2o_dangling_target.empty:
        raise ValueError(f"Referential integrity failure: Dangling target object in O2O: {o2o_dangling_target}")

    # Check for duplicate relations
    if relations_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]).any():
        raise ValueError("Duplicate E2O relations detected after repair.")
    if o2o_rep.duplicated(subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier", "ocel:oid_2"]).any():
        raise ValueError("Duplicate O2O relations detected after repair.")

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 4. Construct OCEL object
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # NOTE: In pm4py >= 2.7.x / 2.8.x, OCEL constructor arguments accept
    # events, objects, relations (E2O), and o2o (O2O table).
    # Version-dependent instantiation:
    try:
        ocel = pm4py.OCEL(
            events=events_rep,
            objects=objects_rep,
            relations=relations_rep,
            o2o=o2o_rep,
        )
    except TypeError:
        # Fallback for earlier PM4Py versions lacking native o2o parameter in OCEL constructor
        ocel = pm4py.OCEL(
            events=events_rep,
            objects=objects_rep,
            relations=relations_rep,
        )
        ocel.o2o = o2o_rep

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 5. Discover Process Models across standard OCEL views
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Discover Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graph
    ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)

    # Discover Object Type Graph (shows relations between object types)
    otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)

    # Discover Event Type to Object Type Graph (ET-OT participation)
    etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # 6. Flatten on target object types (Order and Delivery)
    # -------------------------------------------------------------------------
    # Note: We deliberately avoid pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment here.
    # Synthesizing E2O edges purely from O2O graph traversal conflates physical
    # containment with event execution provenance, creating circular evidence.
    flattened_order = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Order")
    flattened_delivery = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Delivery")

    return ocel
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

Aggressive or uncalibrated relational repair introduces severe distortions into process models:

1. **Fabricating Artificial Multi-Order Synchronization Bottlenecks:**
   Linking `ev11`, `ev12`, and `ev14` to both orders creates artificial Petri net joins/forks, falsely asserting that `ORD-B` cannot proceed without synchronizing with `ORD-A` at portal confirmation and invoicing.

2. **Concealing Unbilled Revenue and Invoicing Failures:**
   Assuming `INV-44 bills ORD-B` balances ledgers through conjecture, blinding auditors to potential ERP billing blocks or unbilled orders.

3. **Masking Accounts Receivable Gaps and Unapplied Cash Risks:**
   Tying `PAY-2` to `INV-44` conceals unapplied cash, distorting AR aging metrics and masking poor payment matching.

4. **Distorting Carrier SLA and Delivery Tracking:**
   Disambiguating wildcard `N-42*` to `DEL-Y` synthesizes on-time delivery records, masking potential transit delays or freight loss.

5. **Normalizing Dangerous Warehouse Co-Packaging Violations:**
   Treating `PK-8 packs IT-B1` as nominal ground truth masks potential packaging compliance violations where cross-order mixing is prohibited.

6. **Inducing Phantom Concurrency and Cyclic Payment Loops in Flattening:**
   Forcing both payments onto `INV-44` induces an artificial $\text{Record Payment} \to \text{Record Payment}$ loop, inflating rework metrics and distorting straight-through processing rates.
