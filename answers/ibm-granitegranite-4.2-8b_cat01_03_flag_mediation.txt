**Pilot selection** – We recommend a limited pilot of **P3** (protected manualreview capacity).  

- **Eligibility**: All arriving cases with `flag = 1` during `arrival_load = High` periods in the next two weeks.  
- **Randomization / assignment rule**: With probability 0.5, allocate the daily protected manualreview slot (1 reviewer hour) to the pilot group; the control group receives the current policy (no dedicated slot). Slot usage is recorded automatically.  
- **Queuecapacity guardrail**: If the manual queue (pending `manual_start` cases) reaches size2, the protected slot must be allocated first to a flagged manual case; otherwise the slot is kept idle.  
- **Primary outcomes** (measured per case):  
  1. Triage completion time (`triage_done – arrival`).  
  2. Total cycle time (`close – arrival`).  
  3. Average `manual_queue_wait` (`manual_start – triage_done`) for flagged manual cases.  
- **Secondary outcomes**: Rejection rate (`outcome = Reject`), average `escalation_queue_wait` (`escalation_start – manual_done` for escalated cases), and reviewer satisfaction (simple 1item Likert collected at case close).  
- **Minimum process data needed**: All timestamps (`arrival`, `triage_start`, `triage_done`, `manual_start`, `manual_done`, `escalation_start`, `escalation_done`, `close`), `flag`, `complexity`, `arrival_load`, `triage_resource`, `review_resource`, and the binary `manual_review_occurs`/`escalation_occurs` derived from timestamps. No extra instrumentation required.  
- **Stopping conditions**: After observing **30 flagged cases** in the pilot period *or* after **7 calendar days**, whichever occurs first, to ensure sufficient data while limiting rollout size.  

If pilot data show a 20% reduction in flagged total cycle time without a rise in rejection rate or reviewer complaints, we would roll P3 to all highload periods. Otherwise we would iterate the protectedslot size or refine the assignment rule.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk** – An unobserved component of `U_underlying_risk` systematically differs between flag=1 and flag=0 (e.g., some highrisk cases never receive a flag due to a gap in the riskscore algorithm). This would generate intrinsic longer processing for a subset of flag=1 cases, reversing the observed speedup at triage.

2. **Queue emptiness under high load** – If during the observed High `arrival_load` periods the manual and escalation queues are never backlogged (i.e., `manual_start` always occurs within a few minutes of `triage_done`), then the downstream delay seen for flag=1 cases would disappear; the flag would have no queuerelated effect.

3. **Decisionquality improvement** – If mandatory manual review for flagged cases demonstrably raises approval accuracy (lower rejection rate) for the majority of flagged cases, the longer total cycle might be justified; a policy that removes manual review (P2) could reduce quality, reversing the preferred recommendation.

4. **Timestamp misrecording** – If `triage_start` or `manual_start` timestamps are recorded after the actual start due to batch logging, the apparent “faster initial triage” could be an artefact, reversing the interpretation that flag speeds triage.

5. **Positivity violation for mediators** – If fewer than ~10% of flag=1 cases experience manual review *or* escalation, the natural direct/indirect decomposition would be invalid, potentially inflating or masking effects.

6. **Reviewer behaviour change** – If reviewers allocate consistently more time to flagged cases when the flag is visible (e.g., spend extra minutes checking), `manual_service` could increase for flag=1 even without queue delay, reversing the expectation that total cycle would shorten under P2 or P3.

## 9. Final synthesis

**What is directly observed:** Flagged cases (`flag = 1`) typically start triage sooner (`arrivaltotriagestart` 0.2h vs 2–3h for flag0) and finish triage slightly faster (`triage_service` 30min vs 60min). Conversely, many flagged cases undergo manual review and, for a subset, escalation. Manual review often begins several hours after triage completion, especially when `arrival_load = High`, leading to long `manual_queue_wait` and, for escalated cases, an additional `escalation_queue_wait`. Consequently total cycle time for many flagged cases exceeds that of many flag0 cases.

**What is a plausible mechanism:** The flag triggers two policy actions: (i) entry into a priority triage lane (faster triage) and (ii) mandatory manual review for most flagged cases. Both actions place the case into the shared manual (and possibly escalation) queues. Because the manual and escalation queues are FIFO and shared across all incoming cases, inserting more flagged cases increases queue length for subsequent cases, especially during highload periods. This queueinterference raises downstream waiting times (`manual_queue_wait`, `escalation_queue_wait`) for flagged cases and can also delay flag0 cases that arrive while the queue is backlogged. Complexity and arrival load further influence manualreview occurrence and escalation likelihood, creating confounding if not accounted for.

**What remains unidentified:** The exact capacity limits of the manual review team and the senior escalation team are not recorded; we only observe whether escalation occurs. The precise rule for “most flagged cases are then sent to manual review” (whether it is strict or reviewerdiscretionary) is not documented in detail. The effect of simply suppressing the flag display (`P1`) on reviewer behaviour (e.g., whether reviewers rely more on the risk score) is unknown. Finally, the extent to which unobserved underlying risk (`U_underlying_risk`) differs between flag groups is unmeasured.

**Which intervention produces the most useful next evidence:** A limited pilot of **P3** (add a protected onehour manualreview capacity per day for flagged cases) is recommended. It directly targets the downstream queueinterference pathway while preserving the beneficial fast triage of the flag and the qualitypreserving manual review. The pilot is small (30 flagged cases), uses only existing timestamps, stratifies by `arrival_load`, and includes clear guardrails to avoid overloading the manual queue. Primary outcomes (triage completion time, total cycle time, manualqueue wait) directly test the speedup vs delay tradeoff, and secondary outcomes (rejection rate, reviewer satisfaction) monitor quality and side effects. If the pilot shows 20% reduction in flagged total cycle time without a rise in rejection or reviewer complaint, the mechanism interpretation (queue interference driving downstream delay) is supported and P3 can be rolled out. Otherwise the pilot will reveal whether protected capacity alone suffices or whether additional changes (e.g., adjusting escalation criteria in P4) are needed.
