## 1. Current-process diagnosis

### What the process-mining evidence says—and what it does not

The frequency DFG shows a dominant fast backbone: `Intake claim -> Auto eligibility screen` occurs 3,600 times; `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` 2,550 times; `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` 2,300 times; and `Dispatch replacement -> Quality/compliance close` 2,320 times. This is the operationally attractive route.

However, an aggregated DFG does not prove that every edge belongs to one clean end-to-end variant, nor that an edge causes the next outcome. It must be read alongside the performance DFG, conformance alignments, customer outcomes, and rework data. Taken together, those artifacts show that the apparent fast path shifts material work and risk downstream rather than simply eliminating it.

### Main performance problems

- **The missing-evidence loop is a major cycle-time trap.** `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` takes a mean 34 hours and p90 of 88 hours. The loop is structurally visible in the DFG: 1,430 entries into missing-evidence requests (`650 + 780`) and 890 returns to manual review. Moreover, 26% of cases entering missing-evidence requests do so at least twice.
- **The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment is disproportionately slow and fragile.** It represents 22% of cases, has a median cycle time of 140 hours, and has the highest abandonment rate. This is not simply a manual-review problem; it is a poorly designed information-acquisition process.
- **Reviewed repair paths have long waiting tails.** `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` has a mean of 22 hours and p90 of 60 hours; `Repair quote -> Customer approval` adds a mean 30 hours and p90 of 80 hours. The temporal-profile analysis specifically identifies the manual-review-to-repair-quote delay as too slow for Premium cases.
- **Reopens create a second, expensive process.** A complaint/reopen takes a mean 40 hours to reach manual review and 54 hours to reach the escalation board. This is especially concerning because complaints frequently occur long after apparent completion: `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` averages 72 hours and `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` averages 96 hours, with a p90 of 180 hours for the latter.

### Main quality, compliance, and customer-experience problems

- **Fast approval is correlated with materially worse downstream outcomes.** Complaint/reopen occurs in 14.1% of Fast approval cases, versus 3.3% of cases following `Repair quote -> Customer approval -> Repair device`. That does not mean every repair route is preferable, but it is strong evidence that the current fast route is often approving the wrong remedy, insufficient evidence, or incorrect replacement.
- **Replacement selection is a specific downstream quality defect.** The long-tail `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` pattern is linked to incorrect replacement-part selection. This matters economically because expected wrong-replacement cost is EUR 95 for low-risk accessories and EUR 410 for high-value devices.
- **Repair is not automatically the quality answer.** Rework repair occurs in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices, versus 4.0% for repaired low-risk devices. A slower reviewed route can still deliver a poor result if the repair diagnosis, parts, or quality check are weak.
- **The process has material policy-conformance risk.** Token-based replay fitness of 0.91 looks reasonable at first glance, but only 68% of traces fit the normative model. The alignment diagnostics identify frequent move-on-log deviations for:
  - Fast approval before evidence completeness;
  - Customer complaint/reopen after close;
  - Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices.
- **The observed model is not a safe design specification.** Low precision means that the discovered model allows many low-frequency combinations of auto approval, risk review, and repair that policy does not allow. The organization should not simply automate the most common observed path.
- **The current process does not reliably enforce the three key controls.**
  - R1 is exposed where claims above EUR 400 receive Fast approval without prior Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review.
  - R2 is exposed where replacements are dispatched before evidence completeness, outside the narrow Premium low-risk accessory exception.
  - R3 is exposed unless every reopened case is forced through Manual eligibility review before its eventual close or denial. The DFG shows 310 complaint/reopen transitions to manual review but also 120 direct escalations; case-level audit is needed to establish whether those escalation cases subsequently satisfy R3.

### The central contradiction: fast locally, costly and risky end-to-end

The frequent fast route is attractive for good reasons:

- Auto screening costs only EUR 0.20 per case and has effectively unlimited capacity.
- Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework.
- Premium low-risk accessory claims have a median of 8 hours on the fast path versus 46 hours on a manual path; service-credit exposure becomes high after 24 hours.
- Standard low-risk accessory customers also see a substantial speed gain: 13 hours fast versus 52 hours manual.

But this advantage weakens once the process is measured through final customer resolution rather than first closure:

- Fast approval is only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits are included.
- High-value customers have especially poor fast-path outcomes: Premium high-value claims have a 22% complaint/reopen rate on the fast path versus 5% on reviewed/repair routes; Standard high-value claims show 18% versus 6%.
- The compliance deviations are concentrated around the same activities that make the path fast: approval before evidence completion and dispatch without required review.

The implication is not “make everything manual.” That would be harmful for Premium low-risk accessories and unnecessarily costly for many Standard low-risk claims. It is to make the fast path **risk- and segment-specific**, while attacking the information and fulfillment failures that cause downstream rework.

Resource constraints reinforce that conclusion. Updating auto rules may increase Manual eligibility review inflow by 15–35%, while the seven reviewers have only 252 productive hours per week and queue growth rises sharply above 82% utilization. Fraud/risk review is also a small, expensive bottleneck with two specialists and Monday/campaign peaks. A defensible redesign must therefore reduce inappropriate fast approvals without indiscriminately sending all claims to scarce reviewers.

## 2. Redesign A: Policy-as-code, risk-tiered straight-through routing

### Redesigned process flow

1. **Intake captures and validates routing attributes:** customer tier, product category, claim value, defect type, evidence status, replacement model, and any fraud/risk trigger.
2. **An executable policy gate replaces the current broad auto-screen rules.**
   - Claims above EUR 400 cannot reach Fast approval unless a completed Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review is recorded, satisfying R1.
   - Dispatch is blocked until an `Evidence complete` event exists, satisfying R2, except where an explicit Premium low-risk accessory exception code is present.
   - A reopened claim enters a `Reopen review` state and cannot be closed or denied until Manual eligibility review is completed, satisfying R3.
3. **Segment-specific routing is used rather than one auto/manual split.**
   - **Premium low-risk accessories:** retain expedited straight-through handling where eligibility and product match are clear; use the permitted R2 exception only when the claim is genuinely low risk and Premium.
   - **Standard low-risk accessories:** allow automated approval after evidence-completeness confirmation, with proactive status visibility rather than unnecessary manual review.
   - **High-value devices and claims above EUR 400:** require prioritized Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before approval; use targeted fraud review for risk triggers rather than routing every high-value case to the two specialists.
   - **Unclear defect or incomplete evidence:** prevent Fast approval until the evidence state is resolved; route to the evidence-resolution mechanism rather than allowing approval on an ambiguous record.
4. **Premium high-value cases receive priority queue treatment, not policy exemption.** They can receive faster reviewed decisions, but do not bypass R1 or R2.

### Segments, paths, and activities targeted

- The redesign directly targets the `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` backbone where it is unsafe.
- It is primarily aimed at high-value devices, claims above EUR 400, unclear defects, and non-exempt replacement claims.
- It deliberately preserves a narrow rapid path for Premium low-risk accessories, where the 8-hour median and 24-hour service-credit threshold make blanket manual review unattractive.
- It also improves control of `Customer complaint/reopen -> Manual eligibility review` by making the review mandatory before final disposition.

### Mechanism of improvement

This redesign changes the decision architecture, not merely the staffing level:

- It prevents the specific alignment deviations currently observed in the log: approval before evidence completion, high-value dispatch without required risk review, and improper final treatment of reopened claims.
- It concentrates scarce manual and fraud capacity on cases where the expected cost of a wrong replacement is high and fast-path complaint rates are 18–22%.
- It preserves low-cost automation for genuinely low-risk cases rather than treating all manual paths as inherently higher quality.
- It creates auditable event data for evidence completeness, policy exception use, claim value, and review completion, making future conformance analysis more reliable.

### What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- It accepts slower initial decisions for high-value and ambiguous cases. That is a deliberate trade-off against high complaint, compliance, and wrong-replacement exposure.
- It may increase Manual eligibility review inflow by the stated 15–35%; there is no basis in the supplied data to assume the current reviewer capacity can absorb that increase while staying below 82% utilization.
- It does not by itself solve repeated evidence requests, low digital literacy, part-price delays, or incorrect part selection after a valid eligibility decision.
- It may create a worse Premium experience if priority review is implemented as a label rather than a genuinely protected service lane.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first, assisted customer completion

### Redesigned process flow

1. **At intake, identify the evidence package needed by product, defect type, and remedy.** Rather than issuing a generic missing-evidence request, the case receives a defect-specific checklist: proof of purchase, serial number, photographs/video, diagnostic indicators, prior repair history, or other required evidence.
2. **Create one consolidated evidence request and one visible case state.**
   - The portal validates simple fields immediately, such as serial format, product match, image requirements, and required-document presence.
   - The customer sees exactly what is missing, why it is needed, and the next decision date.
3. **Offer an assisted completion channel.**
   - Customers with low digital literacy, inaccessible devices, repeated failed uploads, or Premium service needs can use an agent-assisted upload, callback, retail/service partner, or concierge route.
   - The assisted path is not a second manual eligibility review; it is a focused evidence-completion service.
4. **Generate an explicit `Evidence complete` event.**
   - Once complete, the claim proceeds to the existing eligibility/risk decision route.
   - Dispatch remains blocked until this marker exists, except for the authorized Premium low-risk accessory exception.
5. **Break the repeat-request loop.**
   - If the customer cannot provide the requested evidence after a structured attempt, the case goes to a defined assisted-resolution or exception decision rather than cycling repeatedly through `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review -> Request missing evidence`.

### Segments, paths, and activities targeted

- This redesign targets the 22% unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment and the repeated-evidence population.
- It focuses on the DFG loop between `Request missing evidence` and `Manual eligibility review`, especially the 34-hour mean and 88-hour p90 delay from request to manual review.
- It is relevant to Standard low-risk customers, who can tolerate up to 72 hours if status visibility is good, and to Premium customers who need active communication rather than opaque waiting.
- It is not primarily a high-value risk-routing redesign, although it supports R2 compliance for all non-exempt dispatches.

### Mechanism of improvement

- The portal has the stated potential to reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%; the redesign makes that potential credible by replacing fragmented requests with a single structured evidence contract.
- It removes manual reviewers from routine document chasing and lets them focus on eligibility and exception decisions.
- It reduces avoidable abandonment by giving customers a clearer path to completion, while treating assisted completion as a first-class path rather than an afterthought.
- It improves customer experience through visibility: a customer who knows what is missing and when a decision will occur is less likely to perceive the process as stalled.
- It creates a reliable evidence-completion event that can be used to enforce R2 and to distinguish genuine evidence problems from reviewer queue delay.

### What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- It requires portal investment, customer-support capacity, integration with product/serial data, and careful accessibility design.
- A digital portal can increase abandonment among customers with low digital literacy if the assisted route is weak or hard to find.
- It does not decide whether a high-value claim should be approved, repaired, denied, or sent to fraud review; those are eligibility and technical-decision problems.
- It does not directly solve incorrect replacement-part selection or high-value repair rework. A case can have complete evidence and still receive the wrong fulfillment decision.
- A rigid evidence checklist may add unnecessary friction to straightforward Premium low-risk claims unless the exception and fast-completion routes are deliberately designed.

## 4. Redesign C: Risk-tiered right-part release and repair assurance

### Redesigned process flow

1. **Insert a fulfillment-release decision between approval and dispatch.** Approval no longer automatically creates a shipment. The release record combines:
   - product and serial number;
   - approved remedy;
   - evidence-completeness status;
   - required Manual eligibility or Fraud/risk review status;
   - proposed replacement part/model;
   - stock and compatibility data.
2. **Apply a risk-tiered pre-dispatch check.**
   - **Premium low-risk accessories:** use automated compatibility matching and only escalate on a mismatch signal. This preserves rapid treatment and avoids routinely adding a 6–18 hour manual gate to cases with a 24-hour service-credit threshold.
   - **High-value devices and ambiguous replacements:** require a technical compatibility check before dispatch. If evidence, review, or product-match conditions are not met, release is blocked and the case returns to the appropriate evidence, eligibility, or technical-decision activity.
3. **Use repair as a controlled alternative to wrong replacement.**
   - Where a technically suitable replacement cannot be confirmed, move the case to repair quote/repair rather than dispatching a speculative replacement.
   - For repaired high-value devices, apply strengthened, product-specific quality criteria before return to the customer, given the 12.5% high-value rework rate.
4. **Make final close reflect fulfilled outcome, not shipment alone.**
   - Capture delivery/repair completion and compliance evidence before close.
   - Reopened claims enter Manual eligibility review before final close or denial; the escalation board can decide exceptional remedies after review, rather than serving as a bypass around R3.

### Segments, paths, and activities targeted

- This redesign targets `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` and `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen`.
- It is primarily for high-value devices, replacement-sensitive products, ambiguous defects, and repair cases with high rework exposure.
- It uses the underused Quality check resource where appropriate, but does not assume that two inspectors can absorb all campaign peaks.
- It is less suitable as a universal gate for low-risk accessories because the expected wrong-replacement cost is lower and the customer-speed penalty is more consequential.

### Mechanism of improvement

- It addresses a specific observed failure mechanism: the 96-hour mean, 180-hour p90 dispatch-to-complaint tail associated with wrong replacement-part selection.
- It converts a customer-visible failure after shipment into a controlled internal decision before shipment.
- The added 6–18 hours can be justified for high-value devices where a wrong replacement has expected cost of EUR 410 and fast-path complaint rates are 18–22%.
- It supports R1 and R2 at the last point where a noncompliant outcome can become irreversible: the release of a replacement shipment.
- It reduces the likelihood that apparent fast-path savings are consumed by reshipment, service credits, escalation, and repeated handling.

### What it deliberately sacrifices or leaves unresolved

- It intentionally adds time before dispatch for higher-risk claims. Its value depends on avoiding a larger downstream complaint/replacement tail; that net value cannot be calculated exactly from the supplied data.
- It risks delaying Premium cases if risk rules are over-broad or if the manual gate is applied to low-risk accessories.
- It may shift cases into Repair quote, where part-price confirmation already causes delay, and into Repair device, where high-value rework remains material.
- It does not eliminate poor evidence capture or poor eligibility routing upstream; it is a release control and quality-containment design, not an evidence-collection redesign.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A: Policy-as-code, risk-tiered straight-through routing

- **Manual-review overload.** The anticipated 15–35% increase in Manual eligibility review inflow could push utilization beyond 82%, causing queue growth and eroding Premium service performance. The existing `Manual eligibility review -> Repair quote` p90 of 60 hours could become worse rather than better.
- **Misclassification or bad master data.** Incorrect claim value, product category, customer tier, or defect data could route high-risk cases into the low-risk fast lane or send low-risk cases into unnecessary review.
- **Exception misuse.** The Premium low-risk accessory exception could become an informal shortcut used for non-Premium, non-low-risk, or high-value claims. Exception codes require audit and periodic case sampling.
- **Overuse of fraud review.** If risk triggers are too broad, the two fraud specialists become a gate for ordinary claims, especially on Mondays and after campaigns.

**Likely bottleneck migration:** from Auto eligibility screen to Manual eligibility review and, during peak periods, to Fraud/risk review. If review volume grows, repair-quote delay may become the next visible bottleneck.

### Redesign B: Evidence-first, assisted customer completion

- **Digital exclusion increases abandonment.** The portal may reduce repeated requests overall but raise abandonment for customers with low digital literacy, poor connectivity, or inaccessible devices. An aggregate improvement could conceal harm to a vulnerable subgroup.
- **False evidence completeness.** Superficial validation may mark a case complete even when the evidence is insufficient for the technical decision. The failure would then reappear as incorrect approval, wrong replacement, or later complaint.
- **Evidence demand becomes over-engineered.** A checklist that requests too much evidence can turn simple low-risk claims into unnecessary customer effort and slow Premium cases.
- **Customers may still not respond.** Better portal design does not eliminate genuine nonresponse or unavailable documentation; the process still needs an assisted or exception disposition rather than endless reminders.

**Likely bottleneck migration:** from Manual eligibility review toward evidence-support agents, human evidence validators, and possibly Repair quote/part-price confirmation once more cases become decision-ready.

### Redesign C: Risk-tiered right-part release and repair assurance

- **Pre-dispatch queue harms fast-service commitments.** A universal or poorly targeted 6–18 hour gate could push Premium low-risk accessory claims beyond the 24-hour service-credit threshold without producing enough quality benefit.
- **False assurance from bad part data.** If product/serial data, replacement catalogues, or compatibility rules are wrong, the gate may confidently approve the wrong shipment.
- **Repair diversion creates a new delay.** Cases blocked from replacement may flow into Repair quote, where part-price confirmation already drives delay, or into repair operations with a 12.5% high-value rework rate.
- **Campaign spikes overwhelm inspection capacity.** The two Quality check inspectors are underused in normal periods but may become a severe bottleneck after repair campaigns or product recalls.

**Likely bottleneck migration:** from post-dispatch complaints and escalation to pre-dispatch Quality check, technical compatibility review, Repair quote, and part-price confirmation.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### Instrument before changing routing

Before scaling any redesign, improve the event log so that the organization can distinguish a real improvement from a hidden transfer of work. Add or validate case-level events for:

- claim value and product-risk tier;
- Premium/Standard segment and defect type;
- evidence requested, evidence received, evidence-complete decision, and reason for incompleteness;
- Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, and policy-exception codes;
- selected replacement part, compatibility decision, shipment, delivery, repair completion, and quality result;
- close, reopen, complaint, reshipment, service credit, and final disposition.

Measure outcomes through at least a 30/60/90-day post-close window. The alignment diagnostic already shows that a nominal close can be followed by complaint/reopen; therefore, close rate alone is not a success metric.

### First pilot: Redesign A for a controlled high-value cohort

Start with a limited, controlled rollout of the policy-as-code routing design for newly arriving high-value claims and claims above EUR 400 in one region, product family, or review team. Do not include the Premium low-risk accessory exception in the first cohort unless its exception logic is separately tested.

Use random assignment among eligible cases where operationally and legally feasible; otherwise use a stepped rollout with a contemporaneous matched control cohort. Avoid comparing the pilot with a historical period alone, because campaigns, product mix, and Monday fraud peaks can distort results.

Monitor:

- R1, R2, and R3 conformance at case level;
- percentage of fitting traces and alignment move-on-log deviations;
- high-value 30/60/90-day complaint/reopen rate, with the current 18–22% fast-path rate and 5–6% reviewed-path rate as directional reference points rather than guaranteed targets;
- first-decision time, end-to-end resolution time, and Premium queue delay;
- Manual eligibility review utilization, queue age, and whether utilization remains sustainably below 82%;
- Fraud/risk review queue and Monday/campaign peak behavior;
- replacement accuracy, reshipment, service credits, and total cost per completed claim.

**Stop or rollback conditions:** any unauthorized R1/R2/R3 breach introduced by the new workflow; sustained reviewer overload above the 82% queue-growth threshold; a material increase in high-value complaints, wrong replacements, or Premium service failures.

**Go condition:** compliance deviations are eliminated or materially reduced, high-value downstream complaints move materially downward without pushing reviewer capacity into persistent overload, and total end-to-end cost does not deteriorate after complaints, reshipments, and credits are included.

### Second pilot: Redesign B for unclear-defect and missing-evidence cases

Once the high-value controls are stable, pilot the evidence-first, assisted completion model for cases entering `Request missing evidence`. Randomize the offer of the portal plus assisted channel against the current process, or phase it in by comparable service teams.

Monitor:

- repeated evidence-request rate, using the current 26% repeat rate as baseline;
- median and p90 time from missing-evidence request to decision-ready status;
- the 140-hour median cycle time for unclear/missing-evidence cases;
- abandonment rate overall and by digital-literacy proxy, age band where lawful, channel, language, and accessibility need;
- rate of cases marked evidence-complete that later require additional evidence, are denied for evidence reasons, or reopen;
- Manual eligibility review demand, evidence-support demand, and spillover into repair quote;
- customer status-viewing, assisted-channel use, and complaint contacts.

**Stop or redesign conditions:** a material increase in abandonment, especially concentrated among less digitally able customers; no meaningful reduction in repeated requests; evidence-complete cases producing new R2 or downstream quality failures.

**Go condition:** a large reduction approaching the stated 30–45% repeat-request potential, lower evidence-loop time, and no material worsening of abandonment or downstream decision quality. The assisted channel must be expanded rather than treated as optional if the portal improves averages while harming a specific customer group.

### Third pilot: Redesign C for high-value fulfillment release

After the organization can reliably record evidence and review status, pilot the right-part release gate for high-value replacement decisions and selected ambiguous product matches. Keep Premium low-risk accessories on automated compatibility matching unless a risk signal is present.

Monitor:

- added time between approval and dispatch, including whether the observed 6–18 hour burden is concentrated in appropriate high-risk cases;
- wrong-replacement rate, first-shipment correctness, reshipment rate, and dispatch-to-reopen time;
- high-value 30/60/90-day complaint/reopen rates;
- repair diversion rate, repair rework rate, and Quality check outcomes;
- Quality check, technician, Repair quote, and part-price-confirmation queue behavior;
- service credits, total fulfillment cost, and customer satisfaction after final resolution.

**Stop or redesign conditions:** pre-dispatch delay rises without a visible reduction in wrong replacements or downstream reopens; the gate causes avoidable Premium low-risk service-credit breaches; inspection or technician queues become the new dominant source of delay.

**Go condition:** the reduction in wrong shipments, reopens, reshipments, and service credits outweighs the added release time and inspection cost for high-value cases. This should be assessed by segment, not only as an all-claims average.

### Sequencing logic and proof of real improvement

The sequence should be:

1. **Control unsafe high-value routing first** through Redesign A, because R1/R2 compliance and high-value wrong-replacement exposure are immediate risks.
2. **Reduce evidence-loop demand next** through Redesign B, both to improve the 22% unclear/missing-evidence segment and to avoid overwhelming reviewers with avoidable evidence chasing.
3. **Contain fulfillment and repair defects third** through Redesign C, after evidence and eligibility states are reliable enough to support a meaningful release check.

Do not deploy all three changes across all segments at once. That would make it impossible to know whether lower complaints came from better eligibility, better evidence, less risky case mix, or a slower fulfillment gate.

For every stage, compare pilot and control cohorts by customer segment, product risk, claim value, defect type, and campaign period. Evaluate:

- first-touch time and first-close time;
- final resolution time after reopen;
- complaint/reopen and reshipment within 30/60/90 days;
- policy conformance and exception use;
- queue and utilization movement across Manual review, Fraud/risk review, Quality check, Repair quote, and escalation;
- full cost including labor, replacement, reshipment, service credits, and repair rework.

The decision criterion should be end-to-end customer resolution with compliant control execution—not a lower average time to first closure or a lower apparent cost before downstream failures appear.
