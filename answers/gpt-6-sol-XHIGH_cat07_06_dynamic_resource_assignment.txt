# 1. Objectives and constraints

The policy aims to finish cases before their SLAs, maintain quality, limit avoidable handovers, distribute work according to remaining capacity, develop skills, and preserve coverage when a specialist is absent. Throughput matters, but an extra completion is not worth an unauthorized review or a predictable rework loop. There is no single best assignment independent of service-time estimates and worker availability.

**Safety and compliance gates.** Skill 0 cannot perform an activity. Security Review requires skill 3, or skill 1–2 with an explicitly recorded supervision arrangement with R7_Gita or R8_Hugo. Final Approval requires skill 2–3 to finalize; skill 1 may only prepare it. High/urgent priority work cannot go to skill 1 without supervision. A supervisor must have available time and must review/sign off the specified work. No resource may receive more than four assignments in this decision batch; count a substantial supervisory commitment against this limit as well. An assignment is released only if its estimated work and supervision fit an actual capacity reservation. If no compliant assignment exists, escalate rather than silently relax a gate.

**Preferences and conditional targets.** Prioritize imminent SLA misses using predicted completion time, not priority labels alone. Prefer a capable current owner when handover cost or complexity is high, especially after repeated reassignments. Penalize high overtime risk and workload per remaining hour; reserve scarce Security and Approval capacity for work that needs it. Give at least 20% of normal/low assignments a documented training purpose when projected SLA risk and supervision capacity permit. Batch Quality Check only for normal/low cases with more than 24 hours remaining. Worker input, breaks, and the actual remaining hours can change the recommendation. The historical 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeal associations for cases with over two reassignments are warning signals, not proof that any particular transfer causes those outcomes.

# 2. Assignment policy

At each assignment decision, build candidates from the current skill matrix, owner, team, open work, remaining hours, overtime risk, and supervisor availability. Use historical service and waiting-time distributions by activity, complexity, and team; use conservative estimates for urgent or complex cases. Recompute the queue after each reservation.

```text
for case in descending predicted SLA risk (then priority, then deadline):
    candidates = workers or supervised pairs with skill > 0
    remove candidates violating Security, Approval, high-priority,
        four-assignment, or supervisor-sign-off rules
    remove candidates whose reserved work cannot fit available hours
    for candidate in candidates:
        finish = predicted queue wait + service time + handover time
                 + supervision time
        sla = calibrated P(finish before deadline); urgency rises sharply
              as predicted slack approaches or falls below zero
        skill = fit to complexity (competent may suffice for routine work;
                specialist preferred when error cost is high)
        continuity = owner bonus minus handover band and extra penalty
                     for high complexity or a third reassignment
        fairness = remaining-capacity score minus overtime-risk penalty
        development = documented priority match with a bounded training
                      task, feasible supervisor, and safe SLA margin
        resilience = reward for preserving scarce specialist capacity
                     and building supervised backup coverage
        throughput = expected service time and avoidable waiting
        score = 30*sla + 20*skill + 15*continuity + 10*fairness
                + 10*development + 10*resilience + 5*throughput
    choose highest score; reserve worker and supervisor time; record
        component scores, forecast, assignment reason, and alternatives
```

Each component is normalized to 0–1. The weights are an explainable starting point to calibrate against outcomes, not an asserted optimum; hard gates remain independent of the score. Train only when the forecast leaves a defensible margin after supervision and handover. For a near tie, prefer the current owner, then the lower predicted SLA risk, then lower capacity-adjusted workload, then a stable resource identifier. A worker can flag unavailable time or case context for human review; record the override and its reason. If a required supervisor or capacity estimate fails at dispatch, rerun the choice. If no safe candidate can meet an SLA, escalate the case and communicate the risk rather than assigning it in violation of a rule.

The baselines are useful comparisons, not deployable exceptions to safety rules. Literal first-available routing could select skill 1 for urgent work or Security without supervision. Compare policies on case-mix-adjusted outcomes, including delayed rework and appeals, not just tasks started.

# 5. Explanation to workers

“We look at the deadline, the skill the task needs, who already knows the case, and how much time people have left today. Keeping a case with its owner usually saves handover time, especially for complicated work. We sometimes transfer a case when the current owner is stretched or the task requires a different qualification; the reason is recorded and the receiving person gets a handover.

“Some normal-priority diagnosis work is paired with an experienced reviewer so colleagues can build that skill without carrying the quality risk alone. Security and final approval still require the specified qualified sign-off. We check urgent deadlines first, but we also watch workload and avoid routinely pushing work onto people already at overtime risk. Please flag missing context, inaccurate capacity, or an assignment you cannot safely finish. A coordinator can revise it and explain the change.”

# 6. Process-mining audit indicators

Use assignment-decision records alongside work events, with decision time, candidate set, skill and availability snapshot, predicted finish, supervisor and sign-off, score components, assignment reason, override reason, and actual owner. Otherwise an event log records who worked but may not reveal who was offered or declined a task. Report indicators by priority, complexity, activity, team, and time period; compare similar cases and account for unfinished cases.

| Indicator | Definition and audit use |
|---|---|
| Handover frequency | Resource changes between consecutive events or assignment owners per 100 eligible transitions; break out high handover bands and owner changes. Handover-of-work network reveals concentrated paths. |
| Reassignment count | Mean, median, and share of cases with more than two owner changes; compare downstream rework/appeal with appropriate case-mix controls. |
| Skill mismatch | Share of assignments below the required level, plus any Security/Approval or high-priority gate breach; verify skill at the assignment date and recorded supervision/sign-off. Report hard breaches separately from supervised training. |
| Workload distribution | Assigned effort and open WIP per available hour by person/team, with dispersion (for example, percentiles or Gini), not just raw case counts. |
| Overtime risk exposure | Count and estimated/actual hours assigned to high-overtime-risk workers, with urgent exceptions and later overtime hours. |
| SLA misses | Share completed after `sla_deadline`, plus predicted-at-assignment miss risk and median/p90 slack; stratify by priority and complexity. |
| Rework/appeal after assignment | Rework Correction or appeal events within a defined follow-up window, divided by eligible assigned cases; track repeated diagnosis loops and ownership changes. Appeal needs an event definition if not already logged. |
| Training assignment share | Normal/low assignments matching a documented worker training priority with feasible supervision and acceptable predicted SLA risk, divided by all normal/low assignments. Target at least 20%; audit completion and skill progression too. |
| Specialist bottlenecks | Security/Approval queue length, median/p90 wait versus service time, specialist utilization, concentration of assignments, and delays during absences. |
| Policy deviations | Number/rate of hard-gate violations and of score/owner recommendations overridden, with reasons, actor, and outcomes. Missing `assignment_reason` or supervisor sign-off is itself a finding. |

Audit batch waiting for C006/C012, supervisor load for R3, and the forecast-versus-actual time added by high-cost handovers. Review the policy with workers and quality leads: unexplained disparities or recurring overrides may indicate bad duration estimates, missing case context, or an unfair workload measure.

# 7. PM4Py implementation sketch

The following is pseudocode: PM4Py return shapes and optional arguments vary by installed version. Keep the decision ledger and dated skill/capacity tables beside the event log; do not infer an assignment decision solely from the eventual `org:resource` event.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

log = pd.read_csv("events.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp", "sla_deadline"])
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    log, case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
similarity = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
batches = pm4py.discover_batches(log)
service = pm4py.get_service_time(log)
case_times = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)
performance_edges = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)

# Use event/case filters for focused, comparable cohorts. Version-specific
# signatures and returned data shapes should be checked locally.
security = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "concept:name", ["Security Review"], level="case", retain=True
)
slow_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
    log, min_performance=24 * 3600
```

Handover and working-together networks describe observed behavior, not proof that a person is qualified or available. Service/waiting times, performance DFG edges, variants, and filtered cohorts help calibrate the forecast and reveal loops and queues. A prospective trial with monitored overrides is needed to assess whether the policy improves outcomes without shifting burden onto particular workers.
