## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I use the following rules, ordered by strength.

- **Hard invariants**  
  - Every E2O relation must refer to an event that exists in `events` and an object that exists in `objects`.  
  - Every O2O relation must refer to two existing objects.  
  - Relations of the same kind, with the same source, qualifier, and target, must not be duplicated.  
  - E2O and O2O edges are directional; I do not add reverse edges.  
  - I do not invent new events or new objects.

- **Strong defaults, drawn from the domain semantics**  
  - `Create Order`: one event, one Order.  
  - `Add Item`: one event, one Order and one Item.  
  - `Pack Item`: one event, one Item and one Package, but item-only rows may be legitimate scanner outages.  
  - `Dispatch Package`: one event, one Package and one Delivery, but bulk dispatch may cover multiple packages.  
  - `Confirm Delivery`: may be one delivery, several deliveries, or only a customer/order when the carrier identifier is lost.  
  - `Issue Invoice`: one Invoice and one or more Orders.  
  - `Record Payment`: one Payment and normally one Invoice, but unapplied cash is possible.

- **Weak heuristics**  
  - Identifier similarity: `PK8-2` looks like `PK-8`; `N-421?` looks like `DEL-Y.carrier_ref`; `N-42*` is a prefix, not an exact match.  
  - Timestamps: packaging before dispatch, dispatch before delivery confirmation, invoice before payment.  
  - Amounts: `130 + 100 = 230` and `230 - 100 = 130` are supportive, not proof.  
  - Customer/route evidence: `customer=C17`, `route=NORTH`, and `route_hint=NORTH` can corroborate, but do not uniquely determine relations.  
  - Lifecycle/cardinality defaults: if adding a relation creates a multiple-order package or multi-order invoice, that is not automatically invalid, but it is a warning sign.  
  - Existing O2O structure: I use qualifiers in the same direction as observed edges, e.g. `Package packs Item`, `Delivery carries Package`, `Invoice bills Order`, `Payment settles Invoice`.

I combine these by starting with observed E2O relations. I only add an E2O edge when an event attribute points clearly to an existing object and the lifecycle order is plausible. I add O2O edges only after accepting the corresponding E2O hypothesis, and I do not treat the derived O2O edge as independent confirmation of the E2O edge.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

### Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair

Bundle A makes only the repairs with the strongest direct identifier support:

- **ev08 and PK-8**: add P1 and P2. The scanner reference `PK8-2` is strong enough to hypothesize that PK-8 is the package scanned in ev08, but I keep the confidence below 1 because a scanner outage or separate package remains possible.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y**: add P3 and P4. I assign ev10 to DEL-Y, using the near-exact carrier reference `N-421?` and matching route. DEL-X is not touched.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects**: leave ev11 as observed, related only to ORD-A. I do not add ORD-B, DEL-X, or DEL-Y. The wildcard `N-42*` is too ambiguous, and the customer-level attribute suggests an order-level confirmation.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B**: leave unresolved. The arithmetic match is suggestive but not decisive, and ORD-A alone could have been invoiced with fees, taxes, or discounts.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44**: leave unresolved. PAY-2 remains unapplied cash, which is an allowed legitimate state.

Bundle A is coherent but intentionally incomplete: it repairs the clearest physical object omissions, while leaving several financial and confirmation questions open.

### Bundle B: customer-wide consolidation interpretation

Bundle B accepts the same physical repairs as A and then adds a customer-centric interpretation of the commercial events:

- **ev08 and PK-8**: same as Bundle A, P1 and P2.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y**: same as Bundle A, P3 and P4.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects**: add P5, `ev11 -> ORD-B`, but still do not add `ev11 -> DEL-X` or `ev11 -> DEL-Y`. The `N-42*` prefix and `portal_customer=C17` make it plausible that both orders of customer C17 were acknowledged, but the carrier identifiers are too vague to attach the confirmation to specific delivery objects.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B**: add P8 and P9. This treats the invoice as covering both C17 orders. The amount equality supports this, but it is not proof.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44**: add P10 and P11. This makes PAY-2 the second payment on INV-44, leaving the invoice fully paid.

Bundle B creates a more complete lifecycle for ORD-B: create order, add item, pack item, dispatch package, confirm delivery as an order-level event, issue invoice, and pay invoice. It does so by assuming that the customer-level context in the invoice and confirmation events is broader than what is explicitly recorded.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

The following audit refers to the selected Bundle A, with Bundle B differences noted.

- **Order**  
  ORD-A has a direct, mostly complete lifecycle: created, items added, items packed, order-level delivery confirmation, invoiced, and paid through INV-44. ORD-B is created and an item is added, and through IT-B1/PK-8 it is indirectly shipped, but ORD-B is not directly confirmed or invoiced in Bundle A. This is acceptable if the process records delivery confirmation at the order level only for ORD-A and if ORD-B is still waiting for commercial closure. In Bundle B, ORD-B also receives Confirm Delivery and Issue Invoice.

- **Item**  
  In Bundle A, IT-A1, IT-A2, and IT-B1 all have an `Add Item` event and a `Pack Item` event. No item is an orphan. IT-B1's package relation depends on P1/P2 and is not independent evidence.

- **Package**  
  PK-7 has a clean lifecycle: packed with IT-A1, dispatched, and carried by DEL-X. PK-8, after Bundle A, contains IT-A2 and IT-B1, and is carried by DEL-Y. This creates a package with items from two orders. That is not forbidden by the given semantics, but it is the most suspicious one-to-many relation in the repair. If the real business rule is “one package only contains items from one order,” then P1/P2 is wrong.

- **Delivery**  
  DEL-X and DEL-Y both have a dispatch event and a `carries` O2O relation to their packages. Neither has an explicit `Confirm Delivery` event in Bundle A or Bundle B. This is a deliberate unresolved point: the confirmation event is order-level in the observed data, not delivery-level. The delivery lifecycles therefore end at dispatch and remain open with respect to confirmation.

- **Invoice**  
  In Bundle A, INV-44 bills ORD-A and has one observed payment edge from PAY-1. ORD-B is not connected. In Bundle B, INV-44 bills both orders and receives both payments. The one-to-many invoice-to-order and invoice-to-payment shapes are plausible under the domain semantics.

- **Payment**  
  In Bundle A, PAY-1 settles INV-44; PAY-2 remains an unapplied cash event. PAY-2 is not an orphan object because it has a `Record Payment` event, but it has no O2O relation. In Bundle B, both payments settle INV-44.

- **Circular reasoning warning**  
  P2, P4, P11, and P8 are O2O edges inferred after accepting E2O edges. They are not independent confirmation of those E2O edges. For example, `DEL-Y carries PK-8` should not be cited as separate evidence that `ev10 -> DEL-Y` is correct. Confidence should be derived from the E2O evidence first.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

### Unrepaired log

- DEL-Y is disconnected; it has no events and appears nowhere in event/object graphs.
- ev10 is package-only, so the package-to-delivery lifecycle for PK-8 is missing.
- ev08 is item-only, so IT-B1 is not visibly packaged.
- PAY-2 is unapplied, so the payment lifecycle has no invoice connection.
- ORD-B has almost no direct relations beyond creation and item addition, so Order flattening and Order-based clustering are highly asymmetric.

### Bundle A

- **OC-DFG**: `Pack Item -> Pack Item` appears because ev07 and ev08 now share PK-8 and are consecutive for that package. The existing `Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` edge is strengthened by the second PK-8 packing event.
- **OTG**: edge weights for `Package -> Item` and `Delivery -> Package` increase; no new object-type edge appears.
- **ET–OT graph**: `Pack Item` now has an additional Package incidence from ev08; `Dispatch Package` gains its first Delivery incidence for DEL-Y.
- **Order flattening**: largely unchanged because none of the added Bundle A edges directly touch an Order object.
- **Package flattening**: PK-8 now includes ev08, so the package-level event sequence becomes `Pack Item -> Pack Item -> Dispatch Package` instead of `Pack Item -> Dispatch Package`.
- **Delivery flattening**: DEL-Y now has one event, `Dispatch Package`, whereas before it had none.
- **Invoice/Payment flattening**: unchanged in Bundle A because PAY-2 is intentionally left unresolved.

### Bundle B

- **OC-DFG**: `Confirm Delivery -> Issue Invoice` is supported by two order objects instead of one. `Record Payment -> Record Payment` appears because ev13 and ev14 now share INV-44.
- **OTG**: `Invoice -> Order` and `Payment -> Invoice` weights increase.
- **ET–OT graph**: `Confirm Delivery` and `Issue Invoice` gain an additional Order incidence; `Record Payment` gains an additional Invoice incidence.
- **Order flattening**: ORD-B now includes `Confirm Delivery` and `Issue Invoice`, making its variant much more similar to ORD-A.
- **Equivalent-execution clustering around Order**: unrepaired logs and Bundle A keep ORD-A and ORD-B far apart. Bundle B brings them closer because both orders then have the same high-level commercial milestones, although ORD-A still has two `Add Item` events while ORD-B has one.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Assigning ev11 to DEL-X and/or DEL-Y**  
   The `N-42*` reference is a prefix, not an exact carrier reference. Treating it as proof of delivery-level confirmation would falsely close delivery lifecycles and insert `Confirm Delivery` into Delivery flattening for deliveries that may not have been individually confirmed.

2. **Forcing ev08 into PK-8 as certain**  
   If `PK8-2` is a scanner identifier rather than a package identifier, PK-8 falsely receives IT-B1. This creates a multi-order package, changes Package flattening, and generates a false `Pack Item -> Pack Item` edge in the OC-DFG.

3. **Using arithmetic equality to prove INV-44 bills ORD-B**  
   `130 + 100 = 230` is consistent with one invoice covering both orders, but fees, discounts, taxes, or surcharges could explain the total for ORD-A alone. Adding ORD-B as a fact would hide an unbilled-order exception.

4. **Using the payment balance to prove PAY-2 settles INV-44**  
   PAY-2 may be unapplied cash. If it is forced onto INV-44, the invoice appears fully paid and the payment is no longer flagged as an exception. This changes Payment/Invoice lifecycle views and may hide cash-application problems.

5. **Counting derived O2O edges as independent evidence**  
   P2, P4, P11, and P8 are derived from E2O assumptions. If these are treated as independent confirmation, the same evidence is counted twice, inflating confidence and making the repaired log look more certain than it is.

6. **Completing every object lifecycle for its own sake**  
   A repair that connects PAY-2, ORD-B, DEL-X, and DEL-Y just to close every open lifecycle will erase legitimate uncertainty. Some open lifecycles, such as unapplied cash or an order-level confirmation, may be true business behavior rather than missing data.
