| 1 — Small randomized routing pilot | Randomize 20% of Medium-complexity Day Normal-load cases with compliance flag balanced to Orion vs Nova (1:1) | Estimate direct group effect at good overlap (exchangeable subgroup) | Case at Classify routing decision, stratified by compliance | Cap at 20%, preserve Orion night/certified coverage for High; monitor appeals, SLA breaches; Nova cannot exceed 110% day capacity | Trace variant frequencies, DFG sojourn times, queue_wait by hour, rework_rate, cycle_time distribution, 30-day appeal | Unbiased ATE for feasible subgroup; tests beneficial vs harmful hypotheses; informs power for larger trial | Stop if Nova mean wait +2h vs control, or rework +10pp, or appeal rate doubles (Fisher p<0.1), or positivity fails |
| 2 — Queueing/capacity fix, not label swap | Add night compliance staffing + queue triage for High-complexity night arrivals; keep group labels | Reduce queue_wait around compliance, the dominant +14.8h mechanism | Night shift compliance queue (resource capacity), not case label | No reassignment; maintain certification requirements; measure staffing cost vs wait reduction | Event-log performance: time RegisterComplianceReview, queue length per arrival_load, compliance throughput, wait_before_review by hour, variant `...Compliance Check...` cycle | Distinguishes mediation (compliance capacity) from group label; high learning even if no group effect; safer than blind move | Rollback if wait not  15% in 4 weeks or service time  or quality degrades; revert staffing |
| 3 — Conditional scale with monitoring | If phase 1 shows Orion  Nova and phase 2 cuts wait, expand randomization to 40% Medium+Low day cases; still exclude High-night-complex | Scale only where evidence of non-inferiority + capacity exists | Case, with dynamic balancing by shift/load | Real-time dashboard: SLA, backlog, rework, appeals; Nova day load caps; preserve Orion for High/certified | Continuous DFG comparison, drift detection, queue-state, reviewer-level performance, case-mix standardized cycle (phase 1 weights) | Generalizability test; heterogeneous treatment effects by complexity/shift; builds decision rule for routing policy | Stop/rollback if standardized cycle worsens >1h, or High-complexity spillover, or positivity drops (Orion-Day <5 cases/week) |

**Final recommendation (158 words):** Do not implement a blind 50% reassignment. Crude evidence is strongly confounded by severity and night work, and within-stratum data suggest Orion is currently faster conditional on type. Moving complex night work to a day-oriented, less-certified pool risks longer queues, compliance backlog, and quality failure without learning.

Instead, invest 2–4 weeks in proper event-log repair (phase 0), then run a small randomized pilot limited to Medium Day cases where overlap exists, while separately addressing the queueing/compliance capacity that likely drives most delay. This sequence preserves process semantics (queue vs service), respects positivity, and lets you estimate a credible effect where you can actually randomize. Use process-mining dashboards (DFG variants, wait decomposition, rework) and pre-registered stop rules to ensure safety. Only scale reassignment if the pilot shows non-inferior wait/service and the capacity fix reduces wait meaningfully; otherwise, optimize routing policy and staffing, not the label.

## 8. Residual uncertainty
1. **Residual within-complexity severity:** 3-level `complexity` likely coarsens true risk. Need continuous case-risk score (claims amount, fraud flags, prior review history) plus reviewer certification at assignment to enable proper overlap weighting and sensitivity analysis.
2. **Queueing vs compliance time conflation:** `wait_before_review_h` mixes queueing after Classify with waiting around compliance. Need per-activity timestamps (start/end for Classify, Compliance Check, Review) to decompose queue state by hour and true service time.
3. **Night capacity and reviewer skill:** Cannot separate label from bundle. Need reviewer-level logs (who worked, certification, concurrent cases) and hourly staffing/capacity to test reviewer-skill vs shift-fatigue mechanisms.
4. **Selection into completed-case sample:** Only completed cases observed. Need open/abandoned/still-open cases with censoring times and reasons to correct collider bias via survival analysis and inverse-probability-of-completion weighting.
5. **Rework and quality mechanism:** `review_service_h` includes repeat review and appeals are coarse/post-process. Need `Request Info` reason codes, rework timestamps, and case-level quality audits to distinguish efficiency from quality-shirking and to power non-inferiority monitoring.
