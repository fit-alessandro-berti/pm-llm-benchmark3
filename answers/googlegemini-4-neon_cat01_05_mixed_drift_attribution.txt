## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The divergence between raw and mapped fitness in March–April (0.51–0.55 raw versus 0.90–0.91 mapped, recovering 93–96% of the decline from the 0.925 baseline) implies that the initial conformance shock was predominantly a logging and semantic artifact of the March 1 migration—specifically, renaming `Validate Eligibility` to `Eligibility Screen` in ~78% of March cases and emitting duplicated `Open Case` events in 4–5% of cases. It does not, however, prove that the business process remained invariant.

Label mapping forces distinct event labels onto one normative transition, restoring replay fitness without verifying semantic equivalence. `Eligibility Screen` could implement altered decision rules, automation logic, or execution times while still replaying cleanly. Moreover, replay fitness is insensitive to variant frequencies and waiting times; even in March–April, mapped fitness dipped by 0.015–0.025, consistent with minor unmapped routing changes.

Technical duplicates and missing resources bias different analyses asymmetrically. Duplicated `Open Case` events inflate event counts, fragment trace variants, create spurious start edges (`Open Case -> Register`), and depress raw conformance, yet barely affect cycle times if logged near registration. Conversely, non-random resource missingness (rising from 1.05% to 4.8–6.5% in migrated activities) leaves control-flow fitness untouched but distorts handover networks, workload utilization, and team-level attribution.

Finally, post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift. Even after normalization and deduplication, mapped fitness drops further to 0.86–0.88, normalized `Assess -> Request Info` more than doubles (12 to 29 per 100 cases), `Assess -> Senior Assessment` reaches 22 in October, and median/p90 cycle times climb to 49/121 hours—demonstrating genuine behavioral, population, and queueing drift.

Aggregate data cannot identify exact causal shares because queueing is nonlinear in utilization (rework, volume, complexity, and staffing loss interact multiplicatively). Using pre-staffing-cut May (+4.5 h median / +13 h p90 vs April) and August's easing-rework / rising-p90 divergence as empirical anchors, we bound the approximate contributions across two peak regimes relative to Jan–Feb baseline (31.15 h median, 68 h p90):

| component | Jul–Aug deterioration (+13.5 h med / +39 h p90) | Oct–Nov deterioration (+17.3 h med / +50.5 h p90) | quantitative justification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Service time** | ~5–15% of median (~0.5–2 h) | ~5–15% of median (~1–2.5 h) | Median `Assess` service time grew only ~0.6 h (4.8  5.4 h); modest extra service work from `Senior Assessment` in Oct–Nov cannot explain double-digit cycle-time inflation. |
| **Control-flow / rework** (direct loop execution) | ~25–40% of median (~3.5–5.5 h); higher share of p90 | ~20–35% of median (~3.5–6 h) | In May (pre-staffing cut), a +11 pp rework jump coincided with +4.5 h median (+13 h p90). Direct rework adds extra `Request Info -> Collect Docs -> Assess` cycles primarily to the upper half and right tail. |
| **Queueing** (capacity deficit, load spillover, priority dispersion) | ~45–65% of median (~6–8.5 h); ~50–70% of p90 | ~40–60% of median (~7–10.5 h); ~50–65% of p90 | Between May and Aug, rework rose only +3 pp while median climbed +8.5 h and p90 +26 h; in Sep, median stayed +10.35 h above baseline despite lower volume and rework. ### 3.4 Candidate change points by drift type
- **Logging / semantic drift:**
  1. **2026-03 (primary, abrupt):** Raw fitness -0.375, labels 9  11, duplicate `Open Case` 0  4%, missing resources 1.05%  4.8%.
  2. **2026-06 / 2026-07 (secondary, gradual):** Missing resources climb further to 6.2–6.5% and duplicate `Open Case` ticks up to 6% during workload redistribution.
  3. **2026-10 (plausible semantic/logging shift):** Ratio of `Senior Assessment` to `high_complexity_pct` doubles from ~27% (Sep) to ~54% (Oct), suggesting either changed logging compliance for a historically inconsistent activity or altered escalation rules.
- **Behavioral / control-flow drift:**
  1. **2026-05 (primary, abrupt):** Rework jumps 13%  24%, fast variant drops 55%  45%, normalized DFG step L1 = 37, mapped fitness drops 0.91  0.88.
  2. **2026-09 (partial reversal):** Rework drops 27%  20%, fast variant rebounds 42%  50%, DFG step L1 = 31.
  3. **2026-10 (secondary, abrupt/seasonal):** `Assess -> Senior Assessment` triples (7  22), rework rebounds to 26%, DFG step L1 = 36 (global peak L1 = 64 vs Jan).
- **Performance drift:**
  1. **2026-05 (initial step):** Median +4.5 h (+14.1% MoM), p90 +13 h (+18.3% MoM) tied to rework surge.
  2. **2026-06 / 2026-07 (queueing acceleration):** Median climbs +4.3 h (Jun) and +3.4 h (Jul) to 44.2 h; p90 accelerates to 104 h and 110 h (Aug) with p90/median widening to 2.44.
  3. **2026-10 (seasonal load shock):** Median jumps +6.3 h (41.5  47.8 h) and p90 +20 h (96  116 h) to peak 49.0 h / 121 h in November.

### 3.5 Global versus mechanism-specific change points
The data **decisively reject a single global change point** and support **multiple, staggered, mechanism-specific change points**. March shifts raw conformance and provenance metrics with zero impact on cycle time or normalized DFG; May shifts rework and normalized DFG before staffing cuts; June–August shifts queue latency and right-tail dispersion even as rework stabilizes and eases; and October shifts volume, complexity, and `Senior Assessment` routing independently of Supplier Z. Running a single aggregate change-point detector would either trigger falsely on March's logging artifact or conflate supplier, staffing, priority, and seasonal mechanisms.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L0: Global / Schema Health** | None (full log, raw vs normalized) | Monitor ingestion integrity, label vocabulary, duplicates, missingness, and total arrivals/WIP. | All cases (typically $\ge 500$/month); alert immediately on any unseen label ($n \ge 5$ events). | Raw vs mapped conformance; total volume and global queue utilization. | None (no conditioning), but suffers severe ecological aggregation bias (Simpson's paradox across priority/supplier). |
| **L1: Core Population Strata** | `supplier_group` (`Z` vs `Non-Z`) $\times$ `complexity_tier` (`Standard` vs `High`) | Separate external supplier defects and seasonal complexity mix from internal process behavior. | $\ge 50$ completed cases per cell per month; if $< 50$, pool into rolling 8-week windows. | Within-tier rework, `Senior Assessment` rate, and complexity-standardized global indices. | Low if variables are assigned strictly at registration; **high** if `complexity_tier` is updated *after* `Assess` (collider/post-treatment bias). |

**Primary monitoring population:** Treat **`L1: Non-Supplier Z × Standard Complexity`** (evaluated on normalized, deduplicated traces) as the **primary canary monitoring population**, supplemented by L0 schema monitors and tracked L1–L2 panels for `Supplier Z` and `High Complexity`. Why: `Non-Supplier Z × Standard` is large (~40–65% of monthly volume, ensuring stable monthly support of ~250–450 cases), avoids both Supplier Z's external document disruptions and the seasonal high-complexity renewal surge, and sits directly in the non-prioritized assessment queue. Any shift in rework, conformance, or waiting time inside this clean core cohort immediately signals genuine internal behavioral, capacity, or priority-spillover drift rather than external mix noise.

**Handling seasonality and multiple testing:**
- **Seasonality:** Until $\ge 2$ full annual cycles exist, flag known calendar regimes (e.g., Oct–Nov renewal) using **direct standardization** (reweighting monthly KPIs to baseline complexity/supplier shares) and **capacity-normalized queue curves** (expected waiting time given observed arrivals and complexity). Once $\ge 2$ years of clean data accumulate, switch reference windows for volume, mix, and escalation monitors to **same-calendar-month prior-year baselines** (or STL seasonal-adjusted residuals) so recurring renewal surges trigger capacity-scaling playbooks rather than false "new drift" alerts.
- **Multiple testing:** Running 11+ monitors across multiple strata inflates family-wise false alarms. Mitigate via: (1) **hierarchical gating**—Monitors 1, 2, and 9 (schema, duplicates, missingness) act as data-quality gates before behavioral/resource alerts page operations; (2) **Benjamini–Hochberg FDR control** ($q = 0.05$) across secondary stratified diagnostic tests (e.g., per-supplier, per-team, per-edge); (3) **persistence filtering**—require 2 consecutive weekly breaches or an EWMA/CUSUM signal for non-critical behavioral/performance alerts while reserving single-window paging for hard schema breaks or SLA-critical queue surges.

        assert ((monthly[col] >= 0) & (monthly[col] <= 100)).all(), f"Out-of-range pct in {col}"

    assert (monthly["fast_variant_pct"] + monthly["rework_variant_pct"] <= 100).all(), (
        "Fast + rework variant shares exceed 100%."
    )
    assert (monthly["p90_cycle_h"] >= monthly["median_cycle_h"]).all(), "p90 < median detected."
    assert ((monthly["raw_model_fitness"] >= 0) & (monthly["raw_model_fitness"] <= 1)).all()
    assert ((monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] >= 0) & (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] <= 1)).all()
    assert (monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] >= monthly["raw_model_fitness"]).all(), (
        "Mapped fitness unexpectedly lower than raw fitness."
    )

    return monthly, dfg


def analyze_aggregates(monthly: pd.DataFrame, dfg: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    """Compute baseline deltas, fitness recovery, DFG distances, and simple change points."""
    base_mask = monthly["month"].isin(["2026-01", "2026-02"])
    base = monthly.loc[base_mask].mean(numeric_only=True)

    # 1. Baseline-relative changes & derived ratios
    out = monthly[["month"]].copy()
    out["cases_pct_vs_base"] = (monthly["cases"] / base["cases"] - 1.0) * 100.0
    out["hc_pp_vs_base"] = monthly["high_complexity_pct"] - base["high_complexity_pct"]
    out["supz_pp_vs_base"] = monthly["supplier_z_pct"] - base["supplier_z_pct"]
    out["rework_pp_vs_base"] = monthly["rework_variant_pct"] - base["rework_variant_pct"]
    out["med_h_delta"] = monthly["median_cycle_h"] - base["median_cycle_h"]
    out["p90_h_delta"] = monthly["p90_cycle_h"] - base["p90_cycle_h"]
    out["p90_to_med"] = monthly["p90_cycle_h"] / monthly["median_cycle_h"]

    # 2. Raw-to-mapped fitness recovery
    out["raw_fit_drop"] = base["raw_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
    out["fit_recovery_abs"] = monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
    out["fit_recovery_share"] = np.where(
        out["raw_fit_drop"] > 0.05,
        out["fit_recovery_abs"] / out["raw_fit_drop"],
        np.nan,
    )

    print("=== BASELINE-RELATIVE CHANGES & FITNESS RECOVERY ===")
    print(out.round(3).to_string(index=False))

    # 3. Normalized DFG distances (L1, L2, relative L1, and step-to-step)
    edge_cols = ["Reg_Elig", "Elig_Col", "Col_Ass", "Ass_Req", "Req_Col", "Ass_Sen", "Dec"]
    base_vec = dfg.loc[dfg["month"] == "2026-01", edge_cols].to_numpy(dtype=float)[0]
    base_l1_norm = float(np.sum(np.abs(base_vec)))

    dfg_rows: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []
    prev_vec: Optional[np.ndarray] = None
    for _, row in dfg.iterrows():
        vec = row[edge_cols].to_numpy(dtype=float)
        diff_base = vec - base_vec
        l1_base = float(np.sum(np.abs(diff_base)))
        l2_base = float(np.sqrt(np.sum(diff_base ** 2)))
        rel_l1 = (l1_base / base_l1_norm) * 100.0
        if prev_vec is None:
            l1_step, l2_step = np.nan, np.nan
        else:
            diff_step = vec - prev_vec
            l1_step = float(np.sum(np.abs(diff_step)))
            l2_step = float(np.sqrt(np.sum(diff_step ** 2)))
        prev_vec = vec

        hc_val = float(monthly.loc[monthly["month"] == row["month"], "high_complexity_pct"].iloc[0])
        dfg_rows.append(
            {
                "month": row["month"],
                "L1_vs_Jan": l1_base,
                "L2_vs_Jan": round(l2_base, 2),
                "Rel_L1_pct": round(rel_l1, 1),
                "L1_vs_Prev": l1_step,
                "L2_vs_Prev": None if math.isnan(l2_step) else round(l2_step, 2),
                "SenAss_per_HC_pct": round((row["Ass_Sen"] / hc_val) * 100.0, 1),
            }
        )

    dfg_dist = pd.DataFrame(dfg_rows)
    print("\n=== NORMALIZED DFG FRAGMENT DISTANCES ===")
    print(dfg_dist.to_string(index=False))

    # 4. Transparent rule-based candidate change-point detection (MoM step thresholds)
    m = monthly.copy()
    m["d_raw_fit"] = m["raw_model_fitness"].diff()
    m["d_miss_res"] = m["missing_resource_pct"].diff()
    m["d_dup_open"] = m["duplicate_open_case_pct"].diff()
    m["d_rework"] = m["rework_variant_pct"].diff()
    m["d_map_fit"] = m["mapped_model_fitness"].diff()
    m["d_med_h"] = m["median_cycle_h"].diff()
    m["d_p90_h"] = m["p90_cycle_h"].diff()

    logging_cps = m.loc[
        (m["d_raw_fit"].abs() >= 0.10) | (m["d_miss_res"].abs() >= 1.5) | (m["d_dup_open"].abs() >= 2.0),
        "month",
    ].tolist()
    behavioral_cps = m.loc[
        (m["d_rework"].abs() >= 5.0) | (m["d_map_fit"].abs() >= 0.025),
        "month",
    ].tolist()
    performance_cps = m.loc[
        (m["d_med_h"].abs() >= 3.0) | (m["d_p90_h"].abs() >= 8.0),
        "month",
    ].tolist()

    print("\n=== CANDIDATE CHANGE POINTS (TRANSPARENT STEP RULES) ===")
    print(f"Logging drift flags     : {logging_cps}")
    print(f"Behavioral drift flags  : {behavioral_cps}")
    print(f"Performance drift flags : {performance_cps}")
    print(
        "DFG snapshot step flags (L1_vs_Prev >= 25): "
        f"{dfg_dist.loc[dfg_dist['L1_vs_Prev'] >= 25, 'month'].tolist()}"
    )

    # 5. Mandatory caveats
    print("\n=== ANALYTICAL CAVEATS ===")
    print(
        "1. Omitted edges: DFG distances use only 7 supplied edges and implicitly treat omitted edges "
        "as unchanged, making L1/L2 strict lower bounds on true control-flow divergence."
    )
    print(
        "2. Monthly aggregation & censoring: Aggregate rows hide within-month transitions (e.g., Jun 15 "
        "staffing cut, Sep 10 recovery), priority-class delay redistribution, and right-censoring of "
        "slow cases across month boundaries."
    )
    print(
        "3. Attribution limits: Non-fitness metrics come from unnormalized operational tables, and "
        "Supplier Z's +25 pp May share jump with +25 total cases requires raw case-ID audit."
    )

def _require_pm4py():
    """Import pm4py lazily so Part A executes even without pm4py installed."""
    try:
        import pm4py  # type: ignore
    except ImportError as exc:
        raise ImportError("pm4py is required for Part B event-level analysis.") from exc
    return pm4py


def prepare_event_log(
    raw_df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id_col: str = "case_id",
    activity_col: str = "activity",
    timestamp_col: str = "timestamp",
    timest_format: Optional[str] = None,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Format a raw event dataframe for PM4Py without mutating the caller's dataframe."""
    pm4py = _require_pm4py()
    df_copy = raw_df.copy()
    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_copy,
        case_id=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
        timest_format=timest_format,
    )
    return formatted


def slice_time_window(
    log_df: pd.DataFrame,
    start_ts: str,
    end_ts: str,
    mode: str = "traces_contained",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Slice a formatted event log with pm4py.filter_time_range.
    Use 'traces_contained' to avoid right-censoring truncation, or 'traces_intersecting' /
    'events' when auditing WIP and active monthly arrivals.
    """
    pm4py = _require_pm4py()
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log_df, start_ts, end_ts, mode=mode)


def apply_semantic_normalization(
    log_df: pd.DataFrame,
    label_map: Optional[Dict[str, str]] = None,
    dedup_activity: str = "Open Case",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Return a normalized copy of the event log (label mapping + consecutive/per-case deduplication
    of technical events) without modifying the original log.
    """
    norm_df = log_df.copy()
    mapping = label_map or {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
    norm_df["concept:name"] = norm_df["concept:name"].replace(mapping)

    if dedup_activity:
        norm_df = norm_df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
        is_target = norm_df["concept:name"] == dedup_activity
        is_dup_target = is_target & norm_df.duplicated(
            subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name"], keep="first"
        )
        norm_df = norm_df.loc[~is_dup_target].reset_index(drop=True)

    return norm_df


def compute_monthly_artifacts(
    log_df: pd.DataFrame,
    month_bounds: Iterable[Tuple[str, str, str]],
    window_mode: str = "traces_intersecting",
    include_skeleton: bool = False,
    include_declare: bool = False,
    skeleton_noise_threshold: float = 0.05,
) -> Dict[str, Dict[str, Any]]:
    """
    Compute monthly DFGs, variants, temporal profiles, and optional log skeletons / DECLARE models.
    `month_bounds` is an iterable of (label, start_ts, end_ts), e.g. ('2026-01', '2026-01-01 00:00:00', '2026-01-31 23:59:59').
    """
    pm4py = _require_pm4py()
    results: Dict[str, Dict[str, Any]] = {}

    for label, start_ts, end_ts in month_bounds:
        sub_log = slice_time_window(log_df, start_ts, end_ts, mode=window_mode)
        if sub_log.empty:
            continue

        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub_log)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(sub_log)
        temporal_profile = pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(sub_log)

        entry: Dict[str, Any] = {
            "n_cases": int(sub_log["case:concept:name"].nunique()),
            "n_events": int(len(sub_log)),
            "dfg": dfg,
            "start_activities": start_acts,
            "end_activities": end_acts,
            "variants": variants,
            "temporal_profile": temporal_profile,
        }

        if include_skeleton:
            entry["log_skeleton"] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(
                sub_log, noise_threshold=skeleton_noise_threshold
            )
        if include_declare:
            entry["declare_model"] = pm4py.discover_declare(sub_log)

        results[label] = entry

    return results


def compare_raw_vs_normalized_logs(
    original_log_df: pd.DataFrame,
    month_bounds: Iterable[Tuple[str, str, str]],
    label_map: Optional[Dict[str, str]] = None,
    dedup_activity: str = "Open Case",
    window_mode: str = "traces_intersecting",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Compare raw vs normalized monthly artifacts side by side without overwriting original_log_df.
    """
    raw_view = original_log_df.copy()
    norm_view = apply_semantic_normalization(
        original_log_df, label_map=label_map, dedup_activity=dedup_activity
    )

    bounds_list = list(month_bounds)
    raw_art = compute_monthly_artifacts(raw_view, bounds_list, window_mode=window_mode)
    norm_art = compute_monthly_artifacts(norm_view, bounds_list, window_mode=window_mode)

    rows: List[Dict[str, Any]] = []
    for label, _, _ in bounds_list:
        if label not in raw_art or label not in norm_art:
            continue
        r, n = raw_art[label], norm_art[label]
        rows.append(
            {
                "month": label,
                "cases": r["n_cases"],
                "raw_events": r["n_events"],
                "norm_events": n["n_events"],
                "removed_dup_events": r["n_events"] - n["n_events"],
                "raw_variants": len(r["variants"]),
                "norm_variants": len(n["variants"]),
                "raw_dfg_edges": len(r["dfg"]),
                "norm_dfg_edges": len(n["dfg"]),
                "raw_temporal_pairs": len(r["temporal_profile"]),
                "norm_temporal_pairs": len(n["temporal_profile"]),
            }
        )

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusions (**high confidence**) separate the March schema shock from real post-May operational drift. First, the March–April raw conformance collapse (0.925  0.51–0.55) is predominantly **logging and semantic drift** from the March 1 migration: label mapping and deduplication recover 93–96% of the fitness drop while cycle times and normalized DFG edges remain virtually unchanged (L1 = 3). Second, the May–July doubling of rework (12–13%  24–29%) and normalized `Assess -> Request Info` surge (12  29 per 100 cases) represent genuine **behavioral drift**, with Supplier Z's May 5 incomplete-bundle disruption as the most plausible primary trigger (**moderate-to-high confidence**). Third, the persistent July–September cycle-time deterioration (median 41.5–45.0 h, p90 96–110 h despite only +0.6 h growth in `Assess` service time and easing August rework) is driven primarily by **resource/organizational queueing drift** following the unreplaced June 15 departure of four assessors (**high confidence**).

Several secondary mechanisms remain **plausible (moderate to low-moderate confidence)** rather than proven. The widening right tail after July 1 (p90/median rising from 2.18 to 2.44–2.47) plausibly reflects **priority-induced delay redistribution** harming standard cases, but monthly aggregates cannot verify class-level waiting times. October–November's volume (760–790) and complexity (41–45%) surge clearly reflects **seasonal population drift** (**high confidence**), whereas the disproportionate jump in `Assess -> Senior Assessment` (rising from 27% to 54% of high-complexity cases) plausibly mixes stricter escalation with improved logging of a historically inconsistent activity (**low-moderate confidence**). Similarly, May's +25 pp jump in `supplier_z_pct` alongside only +25 total cases plausibly reflects an unrecorded intake mix shift or split case registration (**low-moderate confidence**).

Clean attribution is prevented by three interactions: rework revisits and seasonal arrivals **mediate** queue growth under reduced staffing; December's partial recovery **confounds** seasonal offloading with overtime and the document-quality campaign; and non-random resource missingness (4.8–6.5%) obscures team-level productivity.

I would revise this account if trace-level data showed: (1) May–July rework rising equally inside non-Supplier-Z cases; (2) standard and high-complexity pre-`Assess` waiting times moving identically after July 1; (3) stable `Senior Assessment` rates per complexity tier after auditing case notes; or (4) external document-wait or active service times—rather than pre-`Assess` internal queues—accounting for July–September latency growth.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF `raw_model_fitness` drops by $\ge 0.05$ or unseen activity labels exceed $0.5\%$ of events while `mapped_model_fitness` stays $\ge 0.89$ and core cycle times remain stable, THEN quarantine behavioral drift alerts and audit ETL/schema mappings and duplicate event emitters, BECAUSE the pattern indicates logging or semantic drift rather than operational process change.
2. IF `Assess -> Request Info` increases by $\ge 4$ percentage points within a single supplier stratum (e.g., Supplier Z) while remaining within baseline bounds for the primary `Non-Supplier Z × Standard` cohort, THEN escalate a targeted supplier document-quality remediation and isolate supplier-specific KPIs, BECAUSE localized rework signals an external upstream defect rather than global internal behavioral drift.
3. IF reconstructed pre-`Assess` p90 waiting time rises by $> 15\%$ for `Standard` cases while `High-Complexity` waiting time falls or remains flat with stable `Assess` service times, THEN review queue starvation caps on the priority rule and evaluate assessor capacity allocation, BECAUSE diverging class-level waits reflect priority-induced delay redistribution compounding a queueing capacity deficit.
4. IF `Assess -> Senior Assessment` increases faster than `high_complexity_pct` (raising the `Senior Assessment / High Complexity` ratio by $\ge 10$ percentage points), THEN audit a stratified sample of high-complexity case notes before retraining models or changing escalation policy, BECAUSE a disproportionate surge may represent improved logging of a historically inconsistent activity rather than genuine behavioral escalation.
5. IF `missing_resource_pct` exceeds $2.5\%$ overall or concentrates non-randomly ($> 5\%$ in migrated activities), THEN suppress automated team-productivity rankings and handover-of-work alerts and open a provenance defect ticket, BECAUSE non-random resource missingness biases workload attribution and creates false organizational drift signals.
6. IF rework rates and arrival volumes revert toward baseline (within $+5$ pp / $+10\%$) while median and p90 cycle times remain $> 20\%$ above baseline for two consecutive weeks, THEN trigger a backlog-clearance and staffing-capacity review rather than further quality campaigns, BECAUSE persistent post-recovery latency indicates structural capacity deficit and queue backlog lag.
7. IF operational KPIs improve during a seasonal wind-down that coincides with active interventions (such as overtime or quality campaigns), THEN defer declaring permanent intervention success until complexity-standardized and within-stratum rework/wait rates confirm gains over at least two post-intervention windows, BECAUSE seasonal demand relaxation confounds direct evaluation of operational countermeasures.
