**Why “same expected loss” is insufficient as the sole standard:**

The four-eyes principle serves organizational, legal, and procedural functions beyond pure loss expectation. First, low-frequency, high-impact fraud (tail risk) may have a low expected value but catastrophic organizational impact; procedural separation is required regardless of calculated expectation. Second, collusion and control-culture degradation are structural failures that expected-loss models treat as parameters rather than outcomes to be prevented. Third, regulators and auditors typically require demonstrable separation of duties, not risk-adjusted equivalence. Fourth, recoverability is time-bound and probabilistic; “same expected loss” ignores the difference between a recoverable near-miss and an irrecoverable completed fraud. Therefore, the standard must include **timing, independence, coverage, override resistance, recoverability, and structural collusion resistance**, not only a risk-equivalence calculation.

### Semantics of manager action after Release_Payment but before settlement

Under the FEP-1 framework, **Release_Payment** is the authorization event. Once executed, the nominal separation has been breached: the same actor who initiated has also triggered the authorization mechanism. Therefore, a manager who acts **after Release_Payment** has not performed a **purely preventive** control relative to the authorization event. The breach has already occurred.

However, in industrial payment processes, **Bank_Settlement** is the point of financial irrevocability. If settlement is delayed or can be halted, the manager review functions as a **late preventive control relative to settlement**—it can still stop the financial loss from becoming final. Simultaneously, it is **detective relative to the authorization event**, because it examines whether the release was erroneous or fraudulent. Thus, it is best classified as **hybrid**: **detective for the four-eyes separation failure, and late-preventive/corrective for the settlement outcome**.

This distinction is critical for conformance evaluation. A hybrid manager review does **not** restore the structural independence required by FEP-1; it only mitigates the consequences. Therefore, cases relying solely on post-release manager approval (e.g., AP-201, AP-207) remain **nominal violations**, and their control-objective status depends on whether the hybrid review, combined with AV-2 and timely audit, sufficiently reduces risk within the specific case context. In AP-207, the combination satisfies ED-1, making the package equivalent for that exception context. In AP-201, the lack of an emergency basis and the post-release timing mean the package is only partially equivalent.

- **Most plausible loss mechanism**: U12 and u13 collude within a dedicated payment cell. U12 prepares with a fabricated service receipt; u13 releases without genuine review because they share a workstation, approve 37 payments together, and have an implicit or explicit agreement.
- **Chain breaker**: The audit eventually detects the fabricated receipt, but this occurs 4 days after release—well beyond the recall window—so detection does not prevent loss.
- **What remains exposed**: The full amount is likely unrecoverable; the structural pairing means other payments in the 30-day window may also be compromised; organizational design documents are missing, so independence cannot be verified.
- **Under a truly independent second releaser**: A genuinely independent second actor (different workstation, not repeatedly paired with u12, no shared reporting line) would review u12’s documentation with fresh eyes. The fabricated receipt would likely be questioned before release, breaking the collusion chain. The shared-workstation risk would be eliminated.
- **Qualitative risk**: High without structural fix (illustrative: 40–60% of paired payments may carry hidden issues).

### AP-206 (EUR 95,000; same user u52; token ambiguity; manager before release)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism**: The emergency token is logged late (13:02 vs. prepare 13:00), suggesting either a logging delay or an attempt to invoke ED-1 retroactively. If retroactive, a non-emergency payment is released without separation.
- **Chain breaker**: AV-2 passes; manager m11 approves **before release** (13:04); audit is timely (next morning); settlement is 15:00, leaving a window for recall.
- **What remains exposed**: The ED-1 eligibility ambiguity means the exception may be technically invalid. If the emergency was fabricated, the single-operator pathway is an unjustified bypass. The manager review and AV-2 still provide substantial protection, but the governance framework is undermined.
- **Under a truly independent second releaser**: U52 would prepare; another operator would release. The token timing issue would become irrelevant for separation, though ED-1 conditions would still govern whether the exception applies. Risk shifts to availability of a second user during the outage.
- **Qualitative risk**: Low if emergency is real; medium if token is fabricated (illustrative: 10–20% ambiguity).

### AP-208 (EUR 900,000; same user u72; AV-2 unavailable; manual checklist unsigned; manager after settlement)
- **Most plausible loss mechanism**: U72 directs a very large payment to a vendor with a bank account changed 3 days earlier. With AV-2 offline, no automated verification of bank ownership or sanctions occurs; the unsigned manual checklist provides no reliable evidence; u72 controls both initiation and authorization.
- **Chain breaker**: Essentially none. The manager acts after settlement; the audit is 4 days later; the recall window is long expired.
- **What remains exposed**: The full EUR 900,000 is exposed with near-certain unrecoverability; there is no reliable bank-ownership evidence at the time of release; the vendor’s 8-month history and recent bank change are high-risk indicators.
- **Under a truly independent second releaser**: An independent releaser would examine the manual checklist, notice it is unsigned, observe the 3-day bank change, and likely halt the release pending verification. The separation would force a second pair of eyes on a high-value, high-change transaction.
- **Qualitative risk**: Critical (illustrative: >90% exposure to unrecoverable loss if fraudulent).

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Distinguish **alerts** (statistical or temporal anomalies requiring investigation) from **proof of misconduct** (convergent evidence linking an anomaly to a specific defect or loss, such as a fabricated receipt combined with a conflicted manager and unrecovered funds).

| Monitoring Rule | What to Measure | Alert Threshold | Proof of Misconduct / Control Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Frequency of cases where `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` resources match, by user and department. | Alert if rate exceeds baseline (e.g., >5% of payments) or spikes for a specific user. | Proof requires correlation with other anomalies: AV-2 override, bank-change proximity, conflict in manager, and detected defect/unrecovered funds. |
| **AV-2 override rate** | Count of `Override_Warning` or manual checklist substitution per user and supervisor. | Alert on any override; critical if same user overrides their own warning. | Proof requires showing that the warning conditions were real (e.g., actual sanctions hit, actual bank change within 7 days) and were ignored by a conflicted party, with resulting loss. |
| **Late manager approval** | Time delta `Release_Payment`  `Manager_Ratify` and `Bank_Settlement`  `Manager_Ratify`. | Alert if manager acts after settlement; warning if after release but before settlement in non-ED-1 cases. | Proof requires showing the manager did not review invoice/bank/receipt evidence (blank fields, rubber-stamp patterns) and that a defect was present but not caught. |
| **Audit-before-recall-window** | For completed payments, compute `Audit_Review` – `Bank_Settlement`. | Alert if >24 hours for high-value or emergency-single-operator cases. | Proof requires that the audit eventually found a defect (e.g., fabricated receipt) but recovery was impossible due to delay. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Co-occurrence matrix of `Prepare_Payment` and `Release_Payment` resources; also `Prepare_Payment` and `Manager_Approve` pairs. | Alert if a pair exceeds statistical expectation (e.g., >20% of one user’s payments) or if shared workstation is detected. | Proof requires shared workstation logs, organizational absence of cell-design justification, detected fabrication/conflict, and unrecovered funds (as in AP-204). |
| **Shared workstations** | Link session/workstation IDs to `org:resource` in access logs. | Alert if the same workstation is used for both roles in a four-eyes pair within a short interval, especially for high-value payments. | Proof requires access logs showing the same session/user context controlling both steps, combined with a defect. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Days between bank-account-change event and `Prepare_Payment`. | Alert if <7 days; critical if combined with same-user release and AV-2 warning/block. | Proof requires linking the bank change to a vendor relationship or employee-relative connection, absence of independent verification, and resulting unrecoverable transfer. |
| **Control outages** | Availability of `Automated_Validate` (vs. `Validation_Service_Unavailable` or manual checklist usage). | Alert on any outage with concurrent completed payments; critical if combined with same-user release and high amount. | Proof requires showing payments were released without automated verification (unsigned checklist) and that a defect existed (e.g., wrong bank account) but was not caught. |
| **Emergency token eligibility** | Validate `Emergency_Token` timestamp < `Prepare_Payment`, amount < EUR 100k, vendor age  180 days, no bank change in 7 days, AV-2 full pass without override. | Alert if any condition fails; critical if token is logged after prepare or if conditions are violated but single-user release proceeds. | Proof requires demonstrating no genuine emergency (missing outage documentation) combined with a single-user release, manager conflict, or unrecovered funds. |
| **Evidence quality tracking** | For each `Manager_Approve` or `Manager_Ratify`, record whether fields for invoice, bank details, and receipt/service report review are completed and non-generic. | Alert if review fields are empty, identical across cases, or completed in implausibly short time. | Proof requires pattern analysis (rubber-stamp signatures across many high-risk cases) combined with a detected defect that should have been caught. |

**Organizational action**: The repeated u12/u13 pattern (AP-204, AP-209) should trigger a structural review, not just case-level audit, because the risk is organizational (shared workstation, possible dedicated cell) rather than transactional.

**Notes on PM4Py behavior that may vary across versions:**
- `format_dataframe` expects specific column name patterns; it generally returns a formatted DataFrame, but some versions modify in place.
- `filter_four_eyes_principle` is designed to return cases where the same resource performs both specified activities. In some releases it returns a DataFrame of violating cases; in others it may return a dictionary or raise an exception if activities are absent in certain traces. The code above includes a defensive manual fallback.
- The function does **not** assess manager timing, audit quality, AV-2 results, or emergency eligibility; those must be joined from the case-facts table, as shown.
- All outputs are labeled as preliminary evidence. They should not be interpreted as automatic proof of conformance or non-conformance.
