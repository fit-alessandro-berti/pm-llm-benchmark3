# 1. Network-by-network interpretation

## Handover-of-work network

A handover network connects resource A to resource B when B performs the next recorded event in the same case. It captures sequential position, not necessarily a deliberate person-to-person transfer.

**What it suggests about Mira**

- Mira is a major junction between automated triage and human exception handling. The `Bot_Rules  Mira_Coordinator` edge occurs 920 times, followed by routing to medical, legal, and adjustment resources.
- Her high normalized out-degree (0.82) indicates that she sends work to a broad range of resources. Her high in-degree (0.51) indicates that work also reaches her from several parts of the process.
- Betweenness of 0.77 is consistent with many exception paths crossing her node. She connects the rules engine, specialists, adjusters, temporary staff, reopening, and complaint handling.
- This centrality is concentrated rather than universal: she touches only 22% of claims. She appears central to the exception sub-process, not to routine claims as a whole.
- The automated resources are organizationally important here. `Bot_Rules` is itself central and is the principal upstream source of Mira’s work. The observed architecture is partly a bot-to-human exception pattern, not merely a human social network.

**What it does not imply**

- A direct-follow relation does not prove an explicit assignment, conversation, or reporting relationship.
- High degree and betweenness do not establish formal authority, good performance, poor performance, or excessive workload.
- Edge frequency is event-based and may include repeated transfers within one case.
- The mean elapsed time between events is not necessarily Mira’s processing time. It can include queueing, calendars, asynchronous vendor behavior, or delayed logging.
- Centrality depends on graph construction. Removing automated or vendor resources, changing edge thresholds, or restricting the graph to exception cases could materially change the normalized values.

## Working-together network

A working-together network usually connects two resources when they both appear in the same case. It is undirected and ignores order, the number of transfers, and whether the resources interacted directly.

**What it suggests about Mira**

- Mira is not embedded in a stable production team in the same way as Alice–Carla or Bruno–Diego.
- Her low weighted degree and low pairwise co-case rates fit a short-intervention role: route, clarify, reopen, or escalate, and then leave the case.
- The all-case denominator suppresses her apparent co-occurrence. If Mira appears in 22% of claims and Mira–Carla appears in 8% of all claims, then, under the stated denominator, Carla could appear in roughly 36% of Mira-touched cases. Thus 8% is low globally but not necessarily low within her exception workload.
- A switchboard or broker can have many directed transfers across different cases without repeatedly belonging to the same case teams.

**What it does not imply**

- Low working-together centrality does not mean organizational irrelevance, poor collaboration, or absence of coordination.
- It does not show that Mira is peripheral within the exception-only sublog. That requires recalculation after filtering.
- Co-occurrence does not establish communication. Conversely, lack of co-occurrence may miss off-log consultations and queue-management work.
- It should not be compared directly with team-pair rates without accounting for case mix and the fact that Mira touches only a minority of cases.

## Subcontracting network

With \(n=2\), an A–B–A pattern identifies cases where A performs an event, B intervenes, and A returns within the next two events. “Subcontracting” is a structural process-mining term; it does not necessarily mean legal outsourcing or a managerial delegation.

**What it suggests about Mira**

- The Mira–Omar–Mira and Mira–Priya–Mira patterns indicate that Mira often retains or resumes coordination after specialist input.
- Her subcontracting betweenness of 0.69 supports a consultation or dispatch-and-return mechanism: detect or clarify an issue, obtain specialist input, and then reopen, assign, or redirect the case.
- This is qualitatively different from a one-way handoff. It suggests continuity of exception ownership, at least in the recorded sequence.
- The pattern also distinguishes internal specialist consultation from actual external-provider patterns such as Temp_1–Vendor_Verify–Temp_1.

**What it does not imply**

- It does not prove that Omar or Priya reports to Mira, that Mira controls their substantive decisions, or that a formal subcontract exists.
- A–B–A can also result from rework, mandatory logging, shared queues, or coarse event granularity.
- The result is sensitive to the selected value of \(n\). Longer specialist work or intervening system events may hide genuine consultation loops, while a short window may overemphasize logging conventions.
- The pattern alone cannot determine whether the return to Mira adds value or creates avoidable routing churn.

## Performance/time statistics

**What the evidence suggests**

- Mira’s median service time is short at 4.2 minutes. Her role therefore appears transactionally light but potentially consequential.
- Her case-cycle contribution has high variance, which is consistent with heterogeneous exception cases rather than uniform routine work.
- The six-day absence is stronger evidence of operational dependency than centrality alone: ordinary low-value claims were stable, while exception queues accumulated and the exception-case 90th percentile rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days.
- Medical, legal, and vendor resources have much longer service or waiting components. Delay on a Mira-touched case may therefore arise before Mira, at a specialist, or at a vendor rather than from Mira’s own event duration.

**What it does not establish**

- The absence period is not a controlled experiment. Volume, case mix, weekday effects, specialist availability, bot changes, and vendor incidents could partly explain the increase.
- High cycle-time contribution is not causal unless the attribution method controls for downstream waits and case complexity.
- A short recorded service time does not exclude a bottleneck. Cases may wait before Mira starts the logged event, and some coordination may occur off-log.
- Conversely, the evidence does not show that Mira is a steady-state bottleneck: top outgoing elapsed times are relatively short, and routine claims were unaffected.

## Role-discovery or activity-profile similarity

Activity-based similarity and organizational-role discovery compare resources according to the activities they perform. Mira’s combination of missing-information requests, specialist assignment, reopening, and complaint escalation is likely to form a coordinator or exception-handling profile distinct from adjusters and team leads.

Her profile is nevertheless hybrid:

- `Request Missing Information` overlaps with junior adjusters and Temp_1.
- `Reopen Claim` overlaps with Alice.
- Routing and specialist assignment distinguish her from settlement approvers.
- Bots, vendors, and single-activity specialists are likely to form separate or singleton roles.

This can support the existence of a functional coordinator role, but it cannot establish reporting lines, formal decision rights, competence, or whether the role is performed by one person or a shared account. Results will also depend on whether activity frequencies are normalized.

There is a taxonomy issue to validate: `Assign Specialist` appears in Mira’s activity profile and sequence fragments but not in the numbered activity list. It may be a genuine additional activity, a derived label, or a logging inconsistency.

**Reconciliation:** the networks answer different questions. Handover and subcontracting networks emphasize sequence and return paths, so they make a short-lived routing node highly visible. The working-together network emphasizes repeated membership in the same cases, so it favors stable North and South production teams. A broker who briefly intervenes in a concentrated minority of difficult cases can therefore be central in the first two networks and peripheral in the third without contradiction.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

## Hypothesis A: Formal coordinator / exception desk

**Mechanism**

Bot_Rules routes cases that fail ordinary rules to a designated desk. Mira clarifies the issue, selects an eligible specialist or adjuster, and retains responsibility for reopening or escalation until the exception is placed correctly.

**Supporting evidence**

- Strong `Bot_Rules  Mira` flow and broad outgoing handovers.
- Main activities are typical of an exception desk.
- Mira touches 64% of reopened claims and 71% of complaints.
- Specialist consultations frequently return to her.
- The resource name and description identify her as a coordinator, although names should not be treated as conclusive labels.
- Routine claims were not affected by her absence, which is compatible with a specialized desk rather than general team leadership.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- The process owner describes her as a “queue cleaner,” which may imply a narrower administrative function.
- There is no direct evidence of documented authority, service levels, assignment rules, or an official role charter.
- She has little working-together connection with the team leads.
- Some routing could be an artifact of a mandatory queue account rather than person-level coordination.

**Predictions in future logs**

- Most defined exception categories should pass through a coordinator role, while ordinary cases should bypass it.
- Assignment targets should follow relatively stable rules by case type, amount, region, and exception reason.
- A trained substitute should reproduce Mira’s activity profile and network position during leave.
- If the desk is formalized, reason codes and authorization records should accompany specialist assignments and overrides.

## Hypothesis B: Organizational boundary spanner or triage broker

**Mechanism**

Mira uses informal knowledge of teams, specialist availability, and difficult case types to bridge boundaries that the rules engine and formal team structure do not handle well.

**Supporting evidence**

- Very high handover out-degree and betweenness combined with low team co-membership is a classic broker-shaped pattern.
- She connects automation, North and South adjusters, medical and legal specialists, temporaries, reopenings, and complaints.
- Adjusters describe her as the person who knows where hard cases should go.
- The specialist A–B–A patterns imply that she translates or reintegrates specialist advice rather than merely forwarding cases.
- Low co-occurrence with team leads is consistent with crossing teams rather than belonging to either one.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- The same pattern could be produced by a formally designed queue rather than informal expertise.
- Event logs do not capture the content or quality of routing decisions.
- High betweenness may partly arise mechanically from Bot_Rules sending all exceptions to one recorded node.
- Low co-working rates may reflect the global denominator rather than true social distance.

**Predictions in future logs**

- Mira’s target-resource diversity should remain high after controlling for case mix.
- She should route across both team boundaries and to rare specialist combinations more often than team leads do.
- Comparable cases routed without her may exhibit more reassignment, specialist rejection, or formal escalation—after adjustment for complexity.
- If her knowledge is informal, a nominal replacement may initially show more routing entropy, longer queues, or higher bounce-back rates.

## Hypothesis C: Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency

**Mechanism**

A large share of difficult cases depends on one person’s availability or tacit knowledge. Her individual actions are short, but cases cannot progress until she classifies or dispatches them.

**Supporting evidence**

- Exception queues accumulated during a six-day absence.
- The exception-case tail increased substantially while routine low-value claims remained stable.
- High handover and subcontracting betweenness indicate limited alternative paths in the recorded process.
- She is involved in most reopened claims and complaints.
- No peer coordinator with a similar activity profile is visible.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- Mira handles only 22% of claims, so this is not an enterprise-wide bottleneck.
- Her median recorded service time is only 4.2 minutes, and outgoing elapsed times are generally measured in minutes.
- Medical, legal, and vendor delays may be responsible for much of the eventual cycle-time tail.
- A single six-day absence may coincide with unusual intake or downstream capacity constraints.
- The dependency may be on the exception-desk function rather than on Mira personally.

**Predictions in future logs**

- Queue age immediately before Mira’s first event should rise with her workload or absence.
- Exception waiting time should correlate with her availability after controlling for intake, specialist capacity, priority, and case type.
- A trained backup should reduce or eliminate the absence effect.
- If the problem is capacity rather than knowledge, adding equivalent coverage should reduce waiting without materially changing assignment patterns.

## Hypothesis D: Quality gatekeeper and automation monitor

**Mechanism**

Mira reviews cases that automated rules cannot resolve, checks the problem, chooses the next expert, and occasionally returns information to the fraud or rules process.

**Supporting evidence**

- The `Bot_Rules  Mira` edge is the principal entry to her work.
- The Bot_Rules–Mira–Bot_Rules subcontracting fragment explicitly contains a manual exception and fraud-screen update.
- Reopenings and complaints are plausible signals of failed prior decisions or insufficient information.
- The compliance concern about detecting a problem and selecting the specialist is consistent with gatekeeping discretion.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- Only 48% of Mira’s cases are reported as exception-flagged, so her work is not identical to the recorded exception category.
- There is no direct evidence that she validates the substance of medical, legal, fraud, or settlement decisions.
- Alice and Bruno, not Mira, perform settlement approval and payment release.
- The exception flag itself may be inconsistent or may change during the case.

**Predictions in future logs**

- Mira events should be preceded by identifiable rule failures, overrides, complaints, or missing-data conditions.
- Her intervention should predict fewer subsequent misroutes or repeated rule failures after matching on case complexity.
- Rule versions with better exception handling should reduce Mira’s intake or change its composition.
- Audit records should reveal whether she recommends, assigns, or authorizes specialist selection.

## Hypothesis E: Logging or shared-account artifact

**Mechanism**

`Mira_Coordinator` may partly represent a queue, role account, or mandatory logging step. Several people or system actions could be recorded under one resource identity, inflating apparent centrality.

**Supporting evidence**

- Many events are short routing, assignment, or clarification records.
- Mira often appears once and then disappears for several events.
- Low working-together centrality is consistent with a recorded routing marker rather than sustained case participation.
- “Queue cleaner” may describe a queue-account function rather than a unique decision maker.

**Contradicting or limiting evidence**

- The absence period had a person-specific operational effect.
- Adjusters attribute routing knowledge to Mira personally.
- Specialists frequently return work to the same recorded resource, suggesting continuing ownership.
- Her activity profile is broader than a single automatic routing event.

**Predictions in future logs**

- There may be impossible overlaps, continuous activity outside Mira’s working hours, identical timestamps, or multiple devices under the same credential.
- Authentication or assignment metadata may reveal several operators.
- If it is mainly a queue artifact, replacing the account name should not alter performance; if it is a person dependency, substitution quality should matter.
- System-generated routing events should align closely with Mira records and may lack human service intervals.

# 3. Risk assessment

| Risk | Calibrated assessment | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Bottleneck risk** | Medium in ordinary operations; medium-high for exception tails and absences | Mira’s own events are short, so there is no clear evidence of slow execution. Nevertheless, a queue can form before a short but mandatory decision. The absence evidence indicates vulnerability in exception flow. Specialist and vendor waits must be separated before attributing delay to her. |
| **Coordination risk** | Medium-high | Removing or bypassing Mira could produce misrouting, formal escalation, or specialist overload. The opposite risk also exists: unnecessary returns to Mira could cause churn, ambiguous ownership, or repeated clarification. The A–B–A patterns should therefore be evaluated for routing quality, not classified automatically as either beneficial or wasteful. |
| **Key-person risk** | High for the exception function, but not yet proven to be personal rather than role-based | No equivalent coordinator is visible, and absence affected difficult cases. Cross-training and substitution evidence are missing. Omar and Priya are also single-specialist dependencies, while Bot_Rules, Vendor_Verify, and Vendor_Pay create important technical and external dependencies. Mira is one part of a wider concentration risk. |
| **Compliance/control risk** | Plausible and material, but unproven | Detecting an exception and selecting the next specialist concentrates discretion. Whether this violates segregation-of-duties policy depends on the decision’s materiality and formal authorization. Mira does not appear to approve settlements or issue payment, which limits the apparent financial conflict. There is no evidence of improper routing or wrongdoing. Sensitive assignments, rule overrides, reopenings, and complaints should nevertheless be auditable. |
| **Workload fairness risk** | Unknown to medium | Event counts are not comparable units of labor: Mira’s median is 4.2 minutes, while medical and legal reviews take 37 and 51 minutes and involve more exceptions. The observed outgoing volumes to Carla, Diego, Elise, and Farid are broadly distributed, but case complexity may differ. Assignment fairness should be evaluated after controlling for case type, priority, amount, region, specialist eligibility, and current workload. |
| **Data-quality risk** | Material | Direct-follows relations can be distorted by start/complete lifecycle events, timestamp ties, asynchronous bots, vendor callbacks, or shared accounts. `Assign Specialist` is absent from the stated activity catalog. Also, Bot_Ingest and Bot_Rules touch almost the same set of cases but have exception-case shares of 19% and 31%; if `exception_flag` is a fixed case attribute, those rates should be much closer. The flag may change over time, be aggregated differently, or be missing inconsistently. |

Network centrality itself is neither a control failure nor a benefit. In this setting, it primarily identifies organizational concentration and a point where both coordination value and operational risk may accumulate.

# 4. Further process-mining analyses

## Data preparation and validity checks

1. Use `pm4py.format_dataframe` with the stated case, activity, timestamp, and resource columns, and sort events deterministically within each case.
2. Inspect `lifecycle:transition`. For handover analysis, either use completed activities or collapse matching start/complete events; otherwise lifecycle events can create misleading direct-follow edges.
3. Validate whether `exception_flag` is fixed at case level or changes during processing. If it is case-level, retain complete traces when filtering rather than deleting only nonmatching events.
4. Resolve the `Assign Specialist` taxonomy discrepancy and determine whether Mira is a personal identity, role account, or shared queue.
5. Keep both a socio-technical graph containing bots and vendors and a human-only sensitivity graph. Bots and vendors should not be silently discarded, but neither should their technical edges be interpreted as human social relations.

## Handover-of-work analysis

Use `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` separately for:

- all claims;
- `exception_flag=True` and `False`;
- reopened and complaint cases;
- North and South regions or teams;
- periods before, during, and after Mira’s absence;
- low- versus high-value or high-priority claims.

Compare edge frequency, target diversity, reciprocity, and elapsed-time distributions. In particular:

- Determine whether Mira remains highly central inside the exception sublog or whether her global centrality is largely generated by `Bot_Rules`.
- Compare graphs with and without bots and vendors.
- Inspect specific activity-conditioned transitions such as `Fraud Screen  Mira`, `Mira  Medical Review`, and `Legal Review  Mira`.
- Bootstrap cases to obtain uncertainty intervals for centrality ranks.
- Use `pm4py.discover_network_analysis` where activity-specific nodes or performance-weighted resource edges are needed.

The visualizations can be reviewed with `pm4py.view_sna` or persisted with `pm4py.save_vis_sna`, but layout prominence should not be treated as statistical evidence.

## Working-together analysis

Run `pm4py.discover_working_together_network` on the same cohorts and report several denominators:

- percentage of all cases;
- percentage of Mira-touched cases;
- percentage of exception cases;
- Jaccard or overlap-normalized co-case rates.

This would show whether Mira is genuinely weakly connected within exception handling or only appears peripheral because ordinary claims dominate the denominator. Compare community structure and modularity for:

- the full socio-technical network;
- human resources only;
- exception cases only;
- cases with medical or legal review.

A useful test is whether Mira belongs weakly to both North and South communities, as a broker hypothesis predicts, or whether she is effectively attached to one hidden team.

## Subcontracting analysis

Use `pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network` with multiple window sizes, such as \(n=1,2,3,\) and \(5\).

For every Mira–specialist–Mira pattern:

- inspect the actual activity fragment;
- measure time from Mira to the specialist and from the specialist back to Mira;
- distinguish successful consultation from repeated routing or rework;
- calculate subsequent reassignment, reopening, complaint, and cycle-time rates;
- compare with similar specialist cases that do not return to Mira.

The same analysis should be applied to Alice–Carla, Bruno–Diego, Temp_1–Vendor_Verify, and Bot_Rules–Mira patterns. This provides organizational baselines and avoids treating every A–B–A loop as problematic.

## Activity-based similarity and role discovery

Apply:

- `pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`
- `pm4py.discover_organizational_roles`

Run the analyses both with raw activity frequencies and normalized resource profiles. Questions include:

- Does Mira form a singleton exception role?
- Is she more similar to Alice and Bruno because of triage/reopening, to junior adjusters because of missing-information work, or to no other human resource?
- Are there latent backup candidates with partial profile similarity?
- Does her role profile change over time or during peak exception volume?
- Are bots and vendors correctly separated from human roles?

A singleton role would support functional specialization but would not, by itself, prove that only Mira is qualified to perform it.

## Filtering, variants, and absence analysis

Use `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values`, `pm4py.filter_variants`, PM4Py time-range filters, or equivalent pandas case masks to create full-trace cohorts for:

- `exception_flag=True`;
- `Reopen Claim`;
- `Escalate Complaint`;
- `Medical Review` or `Legal Review`;
- variants containing Mira versus comparable variants without her;
- the six-day absence and matched pre/post periods;
- case type, priority, amount band, region, and channel.

For each variant, compare:

- where Mira enters and leaves;
- number of handovers and repeated assignments;
- reopen and complaint rates;
- specialist rejection or reassignment;
- final cycle time and tail percentiles.

The absence period can be analyzed as a difference-in-differences design: compare exception and routine cohorts before, during, and after the absence, controlling for intake volume, weekdays, case mix, specialist availability, rule versions, and vendor incidents. Open and censored cases should be included through survival or queue-age analysis rather than examining only completed cases.

## Service- and waiting-time analysis

Use `pm4py.get_service_time` after correctly pairing lifecycle start and complete timestamps. Use `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` to separate active service from waiting and sojourn time.

Measure queue and transition waits around:

- `Bot_Rules  Mira`;
- Mira’s first event  specialist;
- specialist completion  Mira’s return;
- Mira  adjuster reassignment;
- team lead  Vendor_Pay;
- Temp_1  Vendor_Verify and vendor return.

Report medians and tail percentiles using business calendars where possible. External-provider elapsed time should be labeled as external latency or SLA time, not assumed to be active labor. This decomposition can distinguish:

1. waiting for Mira;
2. time Mira actively spends;
3. waiting for a specialist;
4. specialist service;
5. vendor waiting;
6. post-specialist coordination.

# 5. Recommendation

## Change immediately

- **Retain the coordination function but add continuity coverage.** Establish at least one trained backup or a small rotating exception desk with documented handoff procedures. If Mira were removed or unavailable, Bot_Rules must route exceptions to this replacement function rather than directly flooding specialists.
- **Document decision rights.** Define whether Mira classifies, recommends, assigns, authorizes, reopens, or escalates. Distinguish administrative routing from substantive medical, legal, fraud, settlement, and payment decisions.
- **Add auditable routing data.** Capture exception reason, rule version, override flag, eligible specialist set, selected specialist, assignment rationale, queue-entry time, and actor identity.
- **Apply targeted controls to sensitive cases.** For complaints, high-value claims, legal referrals, or unusual overrides, use constrained assignment rules or a second review where required by policy. A universal second approval would be premature and could create another bottleneck.
- **Create queue alerts and absence procedures.** Monitor exception backlog age and tail percentiles. Include Bot_Rules failures and Vendor_Verify/Vendor_Pay SLA breaches because they can create or amplify the same queues.

## Monitor

- Waiting time before Mira, Mira’s active service time, and waiting after assignment.
- Exception backlog, 90th/95th percentile cycle time, and recovery after absences.
- Assignment concentration, specialist workload, bounce-back, reassignment, and repeated A–B–A loops.
- Routing quality after adjustment for case complexity.
- Reopenings, complaints, overrides, and specialist outcomes.
- Differences by case type, priority, amount, region, and channel.
- Bot rule changes, false-exception rates, and vendor response times.
- Conditional network measures over time, with uncertainty intervals rather than centrality rankings alone.

## Do not change until more evidence is available

- Do not remove Mira, bypass the exception desk, or send all difficult cases directly to specialists.
- Do not formally label her a team lead merely because she is central.
- Do not treat low working-together centrality as a collaboration failure or force her into stable case teams.
- Do not infer misconduct, unfair assignment, or personal slowness from the current aggregates.
- Do not fully automate her discretion until routing reasons, outcomes, and rule exceptions are understood.
- Do not optimize for lower centrality as an end in itself. The objective should be resilient, controlled coordination, not a visually flatter network.

# 6. Short executive summary

Mira appears to perform a concentrated exception-coordination function rather than ordinary team production. She receives many cases from the rules bot, routes difficult work to adjusters and medical or legal specialists, and often resumes the case after specialist input. That sequence makes her highly central in handover and subcontracting graphs. Her low working-together score is not contradictory: she touches only 22% of claims, records short routing events, and is not repeatedly embedded in North or South case teams.

The evidence supports several possibilities at once: a designed exception desk, an informal broker whose expertise crosses teams, and a key-person bottleneck. Her six-day absence affected exception tails but not routine claims, which strengthens dependency concerns without proving that her own work is slow. It also does not establish misconduct. Management should retain the coordination function, document decision rights, record routing reasons and overrides, establish trained backup coverage, and add targeted review for sensitive assignments. Monitor exception queues, assignment balance, rework, specialist load, bot routing, and vendor waits. Do not remove or fully automate the role based on centrality alone.
