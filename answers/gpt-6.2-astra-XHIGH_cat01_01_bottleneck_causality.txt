## 1. Executive causal assessment

My verdict is **not identifiable from the supplied evidence**. My confidence in that judgment is **95/100**. Among 30 completed claims, Orion averaged 21.96 hours from registration to close, against Nova’s 15.24. Orion received six of the eight high-complexity claims and handled 13 of the 17 night cases. Its mean and median were lower at every recorded complexity level. Giving both groups the same complexity mix changes the Orion-minus-Nova mean difference from +6.71 to −2.51 hours. Management’s projected saving depends on treating a difference in the cases each team received as a difference in what the teams would do with the same cases.

The strongest evidence for a genuine Orion advantage is that the shorter times extend to some close comparisons. Among medium-complexity night cases at high load requiring compliance, Orion averaged 16.93 hours and Nova 18.20. But that comparison rests on three Orion cases and one Nova case. Only four of ten observed combinations of complexity, shift, load and compliance requirement contain both groups. Each has one case on its smaller side. Finer differences in urgency, staffing and which cases reached closure could change the estimated effect in either direction.

Moving work could help when Nova has an available certified reviewer and Orion has a backlog. It could leave a shared compliance delay untouched, or lengthen waits for both transferred cases and Nova’s existing work. A 50% transfer changes the queues that generated these observations. I would test spare capacity and routing at small scale before committing to that policy.

**Final recommendation**

Decline the blanket 50% transfer. First determine whether a case waiting for its first review is awaiting compliance, a qualified reviewer or a handoff. That distinction changes where an extra staffed hour should go. Preserve mandatory checks and keep high-risk cases on their authorized routes wherever only one team qualifies.

Judge the routing pilot on all eligible assignments over a common horizon agreed before launch. One practical endpoint is mean hours to successful closure capped at that horizon, assigning the cap to cases that never close. Report closure and abandonment separately, along with effects on Nova’s existing caseload. Analyze assignment as randomized even when supervisors override it. Report uncertainty intervals that respect the randomization blocks. Agree on tolerable tail delay and quality deterioration with the committee before enrollment, and wait for 30-day follow-up before expanding. If spare compliance capacity improves closure under current routing, fund that change. If diversion improves system-wide completion with acceptable quality, increase the share only in staffing and eligibility conditions where the gain survives a higher-volume test.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual severity and priority.** Which cases looked equally difficult to the dispatcher within each complexity label? The most valuable field is the engine’s pre-assignment case-priority score.
2. **Location of the wait.** How much of a required case’s wait precedes access to compliance? The most valuable field is elapsed hours from readiness for the mandatory check to its actual start.
3. **Effect of team assignment.** Would the same eligible case close sooner on Orion under comparable staffing? The most valuable design change is randomized initial routing within dual-eligible staffed windows.
4. **Missing slow cases.** Does observing only closures favor either route? The most valuable design change is a consecutive-arrival cohort with a common status ascertainment horizon, retaining open and abandoned cases.
5. **Spillover at scale.** How much transferred work can Nova absorb before incumbent delays erase the gain? The most valuable design change is a block-randomized trial of transfer shares measuring time to closure across both desks.
