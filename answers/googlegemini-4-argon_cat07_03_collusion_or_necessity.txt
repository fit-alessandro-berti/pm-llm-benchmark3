# 2. Benign alternative explanations

### Pattern A: `VendorMaster_Lena`  `AP_Kim` (Fast bank changes  payment approvals, V-77x concentration, manual uploads, 9 four-eyes violations)
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Coordinated legitimate mass-maintenance project.** Vendor family `V-77x` recently changed banking platforms. `VendorMaster_Lena` and `AP_Kim` (assigned to higher-risk suppliers) may have worked together to clear held invoices via `manual_upload` once the new bank details were verified, resulting in rapid sequential timestamps and post-approval cleanup loops.
   - *Supporting/Refuting Evidence:* Supported if an approved master-data change ticket or formal corporate communication from `V-77x` matches the new bank details and predates the uploads. Refuted if no change ticket exists, if bank details revert shortly after payment (in the $n=2$ loops), or if bank accounts do not belong to `V-77x`.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: Role-mapping or cross-training artifact during manual upload.** In the `manual_upload` interface, the person uploading the batch file may be logged as `org:resource` for both the bank change and payment approval rows, even if separate offline sign-offs occurred.
   - *Supporting/Refuting Evidence:* Supported if `entered_by` and `approved_by` fields show distinct individuals, or if attached sign-off forms show independent review. Refuted if system access logs confirm a single user executed both steps without external authorization.

### Pattern B: `Buyer_Carla`  `Approver_Gita` and `Buyer_Dan`  `Manager_Hugo` (High co-case share, 8–14 min approvals, subcontracting loops)
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Legitimate emergency handling and domain specialization.** `Buyer_Carla` handles emergency medical supplies and `Buyer_Dan` handles weekend facilities repairs. During urgent clinical or facility events, the buyer likely calls or messages the designated urgent approver (`Approver_Gita` or `Manager_Hugo`) for immediate sign-off and iterative scope adjustments (explaining the 8–14 minute medians and subcontracting loops).
   - *Supporting/Refuting Evidence:* Supported if work orders, hospital/facility incident logs, or delivery receipts confirm genuine emergencies on those dates. Refuted if the purchased items are non-urgent commodities or lack delivery documentation.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: Shared credentials and administrative delegation.** For 23 emergency approvals, `org:resource` shows `Approver_Gita` while `entered_by` shows assistants. What looks like a tight two-person loop or a same-person violation may actually involve multiple assistants operating under a delegated shared login.
   - *Supporting/Refuting Evidence:* Supported if `entered_by` logs show distinct assistant user IDs and formal delegation matrices exist. Refuted if badge/IP access logs show `Buyer_Carla` logging directly into `Approver_Gita`’s account.

### Pattern C: Four-Eyes Violations in PO Creation/Approval (42 cases) and Retrospective Justifications (17 cases)
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Source-system ETL and mapping defects (`legacy_erp`).** When `Bot_PO` creates a PO, `legacy_erp` sometimes maps the human listed in `approved_by` into the `org:resource` field for the creation event, creating an artificial same-person violation.
   - *Supporting/Refuting Evidence:* Supported if raw tables in `legacy_erp` show `entered_by = Bot_PO` on the creation event. Refuted if both creation and approval were manually keyed by the same human login.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: End-of-quarter administrative batching (`quarter_close_upload`).** 12 of the 17 same-person retrospective justifications share an exact minute timestamp from a spreadsheet migration. A manager or temp (`Temp_Nina`) likely uploaded an offline log of justifications, stamping their own user ID or the original approver's ID onto all rows.
   - *Supporting/Refuting Evidence:* Supported if the original underlying spreadsheet or emails show justifications were authored by different individuals within the 3-day window. Refuted if the manager simply bulk-closed their own overrides at quarter-end without independent review.

### Pattern D: High-Volume Pairs (`Buyer_Aline`  `Approver_Elena`, `Buyer_Boris`  `Approver_Felix`)
1. **Benign Explanation 1: Formal organizational alignment.** Elena is Team A approver and Felix is Team B approver; high co-case counts (510 and 495) and ~2.5-hour approval times reflect standard routing rules for low/medium-value POs.
2. **Benign Explanation 2: Standard workflow queues.** Rework loops (~30 cases each) represent routine PO amendments after approver comments.
   - *Supporting/Refuting Evidence:* Supported by standard ERP routing configuration tables and low rates of high-value or manual overrides (1%–6%).

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

**Executive Summary: Process Mining Review of Procurement-to-Payment Controls**

Our analysis of last quarter’s procurement-to-payment event log identified several clusters of segregation-of-duties (SoD) exceptions, compressed approval timelines, and repeated rework loops. Importantly, process mining indicators highlight control exceptions and workflow anomalies rather than confirmed misconduct; our findings point to a combination of operational specialization, emergency processing, data-migration artifacts, and specific control vulnerabilities requiring substantive verification.

High-volume pairings (`Buyer_Aline`/`Approver_Elena` and `Buyer_Boris`/`Approver_Felix`) reflect standard team alignment and present low risk. Similarly, rapid approvals and iterative loops involving `Buyer_Carla`/`Approver_Gita` and `Buyer_Dan`/`Manager_Hugo` align closely with emergency medical and weekend facility repair workflows (`emergency_flag=True` in 79%–88% of cases). However, oversight in this emergency channel is weakened by late retrospective justifications (18%–21%) and the use of a shared login for `Approver_Gita`.

The most concerning subgroup involves `VendorMaster_Lena` and `AP_Kim` (44 cases). This cluster exhibits a high concentration of high-value transactions (68%), heavy reliance on `manual_upload` (83%), rapid payment approvals following bank changes (17-minute median), and nine four-eyes violations concentrated in vendor family `V-77x`. While a known banking platform migration for `V-77x` may explain these patterns as legitimate mass-maintenance activity, the absence of automated system links between maintenance tickets and procurement cases creates an elevated risk of unverified payment modifications.

Data-quality limitations—including `legacy_erp` bot-to-human mapping errors, shared credentials, and a batch spreadsheet upload (`quarter_close_upload`) affecting 12 retrospective justifications—currently obscure individual accountability in the event log. We recommend: (1) immediate substantive testing of the 44 `VendorMaster_Lena`/`AP_Kim` cases against independent banking documentation for `V-77x`; (2) remediating shared accounts and ERP mapping tables; and (3) enforcing system-level blocks on same-user retrospective justifications.
