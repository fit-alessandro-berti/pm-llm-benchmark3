## 1. Materiality Framework

To prioritize deviations effectively, we distinguish between **Technical Fit** (alignment cost) and **Business Materiality** (severity). A low-cost alignment can hide catastrophic control failures, while a high-cost alignment may represent harmless scope differences.

| Dimension | Definition | Impact on Severity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Control Objective** | Is the deviation related to fraud, financial authorization, or regulatory compliance? | Violations of P1-P3 (Authorization/Fraud) increase severity significantly. |
| **Amount/Exposure** | What is the financial value at risk? | Cases > EUR 50,000 (P2 threshold) or high-risk flags elevate severity. |
| **Reversibility** | Can the action be undone if found erroneous? | Payments (Pay Claim) are hard to reverse; Notifications are easy. |
| **Timing** | Did the event occur out of sequence (e.g., Pay before Approve)? | Sequence violations (P3) are critical regardless of cost. |
| **Evidence Reliability** | Is the missing event truly missing, or just logged elsewhere? | External evidence (CRM, Adjuster reports) reduces severity if verifiable. |
| **Recurrence** | Is this a one-off error or a systemic pattern? | Systemic patterns (e.g., Auto Approve logic) increase priority. |
| **Model Scope** | Does the case belong in the model (Payable vs. Denied/Withdrawn)? | Scope mismatches (P6) are model errors, not operational violations. |
| **Confidence** | How certain are we that the deviation occurred as diagnosed? | **Distinct from Severity.** High severity + Low confidence = Investigate. High severity + High confidence = Remediate. |

**Severity Scale (1–5):**
*   **1 (Noise):** Technical artifacts, telemetry, harmless retries.
*   **2 (Minor):** Label mismatches, evidence found in secondary systems (CRM).
*   **3 (Moderate):** Scope disputes (Denied/Withdrawn), external evidence gaps.
*   **4 (Major):** Data-aware policy violations (Auto Approve limits), missing core evidence.
*   **5 (Critical):** Fraud control bypass, payment before approval, high-value exposure.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost measures the **technical edit distance** between log and model under a uniform cost function. It does not weigh the **business consequence** of the deviation.

1.  **Zero-Cost Policy Violations (CL-609, CL-610):** Both cases have `raw_cost = 0` because the control flow (Auto Approve) matches the model. However, they violate **P1** (Amount/Bank Change limits). The alignment cost is blind to data attributes, hiding severe financial risk.
2.  **Low-Cost High-Exposure (CL-602):** `raw_cost = 1` (one missing Fraud Screen). Technically minor. Businessly, this is a **EUR 480,000** high-risk claim missing a mandatory fraud check. The cost function treats missing "Notify" and missing "Fraud Screen" equally, which is incorrect for risk.
3.  **High-Cost Noise (CL-601):** `raw_cost = 3` due to UI telemetry (`Open Workbench`, etc.). Technically expensive alignment. Businessly, this is **Severity 1** noise. High cost here creates false positives for management.
4.  **Sequence vs. Count (CL-604):** `raw_cost = 3`. The cost accumulates from reordering (Pay before Approve). While the cost is moderate, the **irreversibility** of payment before approval makes this a Severity 5 event. Cost does not capture irreversibility.
5.  **Scope Mismatch (CL-607):** `raw_cost = 4`. High cost suggests major deviation. However, if P6 holds (denied claims out of scope), the risk is **zero**; the model is simply wrong. Cost penalizes the case for not fitting a model it wasn't supposed to follow.

**Conclusion:** Raw cost is a diagnostic for **model fitness**, not **compliance risk**. Risk assessment requires overlaying business policies (P1-P6) and financial context onto the alignment diagnostics.
