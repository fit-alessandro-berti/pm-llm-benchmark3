## 1. Materiality Framework

**Severity Assessment Dimensions:**
Severity is a composite score (1–5) derived from the intersection of business impact, control integrity, and evidence quality. It is independent of alignment raw cost.

1.  **Control Objective Violation:** Does the deviation breach a mandatory policy (P1–P3)? Violations of P3 (Pay before Approval) or P2 (Missing Fraud/Manual for high risk) trigger immediate severity elevation.
2.  **Amount/Exposure:** Claims > EUR 50,000 or HIGH risk carry higher potential financial loss and regulatory scrutiny.
3.  **Reversibility:** Can the outcome be corrected? Payments made before approval (CL-604) are high risk; missing telemetry (CL-601) is reversible/irrelevant.
4.  **Timing:** Immediate control failures (e.g., Pay before Approval) vs. delays (e.g., Notification within 24h but late).
5.  **Customer/Regulatory Harm:** Potential for fraud loss, customer dispute, or audit finding.
6.  **Recurrence:** Patterns indicating systemic issues (e.g., data-aware auto-approval failures) increase severity of individual instances.
7.  **Evidence Reliability:** Core log events vs. external documents vs. mutable CRM links. Missing core evidence for a required control (CL-602) is higher severity than external evidence exists but unlinked (CL-608).
8.  **Model Scope:** Deviations caused by cases outside the model's scope (P6) are model-mismatches, not operational failures.

**Confidence vs. Severity:**
*   **Severity** estimates the actual business risk and policy breach.
*   **Confidence** reflects the certainty of that estimate based on available evidence. Low confidence may arise when evidence is external, mutable, or disputed (e.g., P6 scope), requiring investigation to confirm the narrative. High confidence exists when core logs, timestamps, and policy conditions align clearly.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment raw cost measures edit distance under a uniform cost function. It does not capture business risk, as demonstrated by these cases:

1.  **Zero Cost, High Risk (CL-609, CL-610):** Alignment cost is 0 because the log matches the model perfectly. However, both cases violate P1 (Auto Approve allowed only for LOW risk, amount <= 5k, no bank change). CL-609 has MEDIUM risk and 18k amount; CL-610 has a recent bank change. The model does not encode P1, so the alignment cannot detect the policy violation. Cost 0 implies fitness, but risk is 4.
2.  **High Cost, Low Risk (CL-607, CL-612):** Alignment cost is 4 due to multiple missing model moves. However, these are denied and withdrawn claims, which are out of scope per P6. The high cost reflects a model mismatch, not operational failure. Risk is 2.
3.  **Low Cost, High Risk (CL-602):** Alignment cost is 1 for a single missing `Fraud Screen`. However, this is a HIGH-risk, 480k claim with a recent bank change. P2 requires Fraud Screen. The missing control represents a severe compliance and fraud risk. Cost 1 hides the severity.
4.  **Moderate Cost, Critical Risk (CL-604):** Alignment cost is 3. The deviation involves `Pay` before `Manual Approve` and missing `Fraud Screen`. P3 defines Pay before Approval as a severe control failure. The cost does not reflect the irreversibility and regulatory severity of paying before approval.
5.  **Hidden Moves (CL-605):** Alignment cost is 0 due to a silent transition `T(tau)`. This is a fitting move, cost 0, and risk 1. Here cost aligns with risk, but only because the hidden move represents valid routing. Cost function design matters: if hidden moves had cost, this would be penalized incorrectly.
6.  **Evidence Quality (CL-608):** Alignment cost is 1 for missing `Notify Claimant`. However, notification exists in CRM within 24h. P4 allows CRM if linked. The cost suggests a deviation, but the risk is mitigated by evidence, though the mutable link reduces confidence. Cost does not capture evidence reliability.

**Conclusion:** Raw cost is a measure of log-model fit, not business risk. Policies (P1-P3) add constraints not in the model. Scope (P6) causes deviations for valid business cases. Evidence quality and data conditions (P1 checks) are invisible to alignment cost. A risk-aware assessment must overlay policies, amounts, risk levels, and evidence quality.
