## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The operational benefit of executing P-884 within the deadline is material but bounded: **€20,000–€35,000** in avoided operational loss. That benefit only counts if execution follows a valid approval path. A fast payment that violates sanctions-review controls is not a high-utility action; it is an unlawful or invalid execution with asymmetric downside.

The false-rejection or prolonged-hold harm is broader: **€15,000–€80,000**. This means “reject now” is not automatically safer than “review now.” Given the predicted ultimate approval probability of **0.55–0.75** after conformant review, a premature rejection could destroy value and harm the customer even if it avoids execution risk.

The compliance downside from unlawful execution is much larger and more asymmetric: **€100,000–€500,000+**, plus non-monetary consequences such as sanctions-control failure, regulatory reporting exposure, audit findings, personal accountability, and loss of institutional credibility. Those harms are not cleanly offset by a €20,000–€35,000 deadline benefit.

That produces a dominance argument: **A and B are dominated by conformant review strategies**. They may improve the P-884 SLA metric, but they fail mandatory controls. C and D are preferable because they preserve a chance of on-time execution while keeping the payment inside a lawful approval path. F is only attractive if the evidence threshold for rejection is already met; otherwise it substitutes a fast customer-harming decision for a required risk decision. E is safe from an execution-risk perspective but poor for the deadline. G is not a real execution path until the exception prerequisites exist.

The prediction intervals should be treated as decision support, not authorization. The **0.48–0.64** breach probability under normal processing and **0.55–0.75** ultimate approval probability are observational. They may shift once S2 is interrupted, S4 becomes available, alternate screening is run, or new evidence changes the risk state.

## 4. Conformance constraints

**A — Execute payment immediately:** violates the mandatory second-officer-review requirement because the current risk score is **72** and the fuzzy sanctions match is unresolved. It also violates the rule that `Payment Executed` is conformant only after a valid approval path. Emergency override is not available because neither director authorization nor a valid incident identifier exists.

**B — R17 self-certifies:** violates the four-eyes principle. R17 performed the first review, so R17 cannot be the second officer. A self-certified review is not an independent second-officer review, and any payment execution based on it would not be conformant.

**C — S2 independent review:** conformant if S2 independently reviews the evidence and is different from R17. After S2’s review, the case may be approved, rejected, or held/SAR according to the outcome. If approved, payment execution is valid.

**D — Alternate screening plus second review:** alternate screening is allowed and useful as additional evidence, but it does **not** substitute for the second-officer review while risk remains at least 70 or the fuzzy match remains unresolved. It becomes a valid route only when the final decision follows the required review path.

**E — Request additional evidence:** explicitly allowed by the evidence cycle: `Evidence Requested -> Evidence Received -> Evidence Validated -> Analyst Review`. It does not authorize payment execution. After evidence validation, the case must return to review, and the second-officer rule still applies if the risk/match conditions remain.

**F — Reject now:** potentially conformant only if the institution’s documented evidence threshold for rejection is already satisfied. The required evidence would include a clear rationale for treating the unresolved match or risk factors as sufficient for rejection, appropriate authority to reject at this stage, and an audit trail showing why further review or evidence was not needed. If internal policy requires second-officer review before final disposition of high-risk cases, then F would also need that review.

**G — Emergency override:** not enabled now. It requires both a Compliance Director authorization and a valid incident identifier. The evidence required would be a genuine incident record, justification for why the normal approval path cannot be used in time, explicit director approval, scope limitation, and post-event auditability. A customer deadline alone should not be treated as a fabricated incident.

A favorable numerical forecast cannot rescue A or B. Prediction can estimate breach risk or approval likelihood, but it cannot waive mandatory sanctions controls, the four-eyes principle, or the valid-approval-path requirement. Similarly, filing a Suspicious Activity Report does not authorize execution, and the SAR must not be disclosed to the customer.

## 5. Resource spillovers

The key portfolio tradeoff is **S2 now versus S4 later**.

Using **S2 now** gives P-884 the strongest chance of conformant on-time execution. The review plus recording/execution window is **40–90 minutes**, which fits the 90-minute deadline at the favorable end and still barely fits at the upper bound. The cost is externalized to P-901: interrupting S2 for 45 minutes raises P-901’s breach probability by **0.12–0.28**. Because P-901 is also a high-value payment with a 70-minute deadline, this is not a trivial spillover. It should be mitigated by notifying P-901’s owner, checking whether non-second-officer work on P-901 can continue, and documenting why P-884 received the scarce review slot.

Waiting for **S4** avoids the P-901 spillover but increases P-884’s deadline risk. If S4 starts in 35 minutes, then S4’s review plus execution could finish around **75–125 minutes** from now. That may still meet the deadline if review and recording are short, but it is materially less deadline-protective than S2 now. If S4 starts in 65 minutes, the finish range becomes roughly **105–155 minutes**, which makes the P-884 deadline breach very likely.

The alternate-screening API quota is scarce but justified here. P-884 is high value, high risk, time constrained, and has an unresolved fuzzy sanctions match. Using **1 of 3** expedited calls is proportionate if it runs in parallel and does not delay the mandatory review. It is not justified if teams wait for the alternate screen while doing nothing to secure an independent reviewer.

The emergency-override path has a different spillover: it can consume scarce senior-compliance attention and the remaining clock. Because director response is **20–60+ minutes** and the incident identifier is not yet justified, G should not displace D or C. It can only be pursued as a contingency if it does not slow the valid review path.

If **S4 becomes available in 35 minutes**, the ranking shifts toward **D with S4** as the preferred low-spillover path, especially if P-901’s own breach risk is already high or its value is comparable to P-884. C remains the best P-884-only deadline-protection action, but it is less attractive at portfolio level because it harms P-901.

If **S4 becomes available in 65 minutes**, waiting for S4 falls below immediate S2 review. The ranking becomes: **D with immediate S2 and alternate screening in parallel**, then **C**, then the risk-control exits F/E/G. In that scenario, waiting for S4 is close to accepting a P-884 deadline miss.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

Primary recommendation: **start Action D immediately, but do not let alternate screening delay the independent review.** Run the alternate sanctions screen now, prepare the second-review packet, obtain a firm S4 availability estimate, and route the case to an independent second officer. Use **S4** only if S4 can start early enough to preserve a realistic deadline chance; otherwise use **S2** and document the P-901 spillover rationale.

Fallback: if the alternate screen or second officer cannot resolve the fuzzy match, move to a documented **Hold/SAR or Reject** path as appropriate. Do not disclose any SAR to the customer, and do not execute unless a valid approval or a fully enabled emergency override exists.

Confidence: **medium-high** on the conformance ranking, **medium** on the operational ranking because S4 availability, review duration, and intervention effects are uncertain.

Immediate reranking trigger: **a firm S4 start time, the alternate-screening result, a fresh risk score, or any second-officer indication that the fuzzy match is either resolved or cannot be cleared.**
