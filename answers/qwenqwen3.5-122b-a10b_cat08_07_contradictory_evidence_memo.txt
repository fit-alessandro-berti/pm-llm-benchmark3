# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Leadership Team  
**From:** Process Optimization Lead  
**Subject:** Strategic Direction for Fiber Installation Redesign R  

**Decision Required:** Approve Hybrid Rollout (Option E) with Enhanced Measurement. Do not scale nationally yet.

**Summary**  
Redesign R delivers significant efficiency gains for low-risk cases but introduces severe quality and compliance risks for complex segments. While median cycle time improved by 24% (14.2 to 10.8 days) and handovers decreased by 22%, the 95th percentile duration worsened by 17 days, and complaints rose from 6.1% to 9.8%. Process mining confirms the redesign model fits observed data well (fitness 0.94) but lacks control (precision 0.51), allowing frequent deviations such as billing before activation and skipped permit checks.

**Key Trade-offs**  
The redesign optimizes for speed in simple scenarios (houses) by removing friction (survey skips, merged tasks). However, this simplification fails in complex scenarios (apartments, old buildings) where predictive risk rules misclassify cases, leading to rework, second visits, and downstream billing disputes. Additionally, centralizing ownership via coordinators reduced handovers but created a single point of failure; coordinator utilization hit 93%, causing stalls during absences.

**Recommendation**  
We recommend **Option E (Hybrid)**:
1.  **Scale:** Deploy coordinator ownership nationally to maintain accountability gains.
2.  **Restrict:** Limit survey skipping and post-billing activation strictly to "Simple House/Low Risk" segments. Revert to baseline processes for apartments and older buildings.
3.  **Measure:** Initiate targeted experiments (M1, M6) to validate the risk-prediction logic before relaxing controls further.

**Financial Implications**  
Visible costs appear lower (EUR 184  161), but estimated costs including rework are higher (EUR 198  207). Scaling R nationally without fixing the risk model risks increasing total cost per installation and damaging NPS (+21  +17).

**Conditions for Broader Rollout**  
National expansion of the simplified flow is contingent upon:
*   Complaint rates remaining  6.1% (baseline).
*   Downstream cost estimates stabilizing  EUR 198.
*   Validated accuracy of the building-risk predictive rule > 90% on medium-risk strata.

Immediate national scaling is advised against until the tail-end performance and compliance deviations are resolved.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

**High Fitness but Low Precision**  
*Fitness* measures how well event logs fit the process model. In the pilot, almost every case followed *a* path in the new model (fitness 0.94). However, *Precision* penalizes models that allow paths that shouldn't exist. The low precision (0.51) indicates the model permits deviant paths—specifically "Billing start before Activation test" and "Missing Permit check"—which occurred frequently in the log. The model reflects the chaos rather than constraining it.

**Improved Median but Worse Tail Duration**  
The redesign streamlined the "happy path" (Address validation  Appointment  Install), drastically cutting time for simple houses (median improvement). However, when exceptions occurred (e.g., skipped survey on an apartment requiring permits), cases stalled significantly longer due to manual corrections and rescheduling, inflating the p90/p95 tail.

**Fewer Handovers but Lower Resilience**  
Assigning a single coordinator removed handover wait times. However, this centralized knowledge. When coordinators were absent, cases stalled because context was not shared across a team. Reduced handovers lowered visibility into bottlenecks, creating fragility rather than robustness.

**Lower Visible Cost but Higher Rework Cost**  
Upstream savings came from skipping field surveys and merging tasks (visible cost EUR 161). However, skipping surveys led to incorrect installations, driving up second visits and complaint handling. Since downstream costs were excluded from early reporting, the net economic value appears negative (estimated EUR 207).

**Improvement for Simple Houses vs. Deterioration for Apartments**  
The predictive risk rule correctly identified simple houses, allowing safe streamlining. For apartments and old buildings, the rule falsely assumed low risk. Consequently, necessary checks (permits, surveys) were bypassed, leading to regulatory delays and technical failures unique to complex building types.
