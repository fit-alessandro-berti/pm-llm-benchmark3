## 1. Executive causal assessment

Provisional verdict: **mainly confounded**; confidence **72/100**. The crude Orion–Nova gap is large (21.96 vs 15.24 h, +6.71 h), but it is concentrated in case mix and operating conditions: Orion has 42.9% high complexity vs Nova 12.5%, 92.9% night work vs 25.0%, far more high-load cases, and 64.3% compliance-required vs 25.0%. Within every observed complexity stratum Orion’s mean cycle time is lower: low 7.50 vs 9.64, medium 17.17 vs 19.38, high 31.57 vs 35.00. A crude all-case complexity standardization reverses the contrast by about 2.5 h. That is the strongest evidence against a harmful Orion label.

Evidence for a genuine Orion effect is not absent: the crude gap is mostly queue wait (+4.01 h) and service (+2.00 h), Orion handles night/high-load work where queueing may be structurally worse, and rework/compliance paths differ. But those can be routing-policy or capacity mechanisms rather than a resource-label effect. Stratification is not identification: no medium/high day Orion experience, only two Nova high cases, no reviewer or queue timestamps, completed-case selection, and coarse 30-day quality.

A blind 50% reassignment could succeed if Nova day capacity relieves Orion’s compliance/queue bottleneck. It could fail or backfire if Orion is selected because it processes difficult night cases efficiently; moving them to Nova may raise waits, service, rework, appeals, or open-case accumulation.

| 6 | Completed-case selection and measurement distort the comparison | Only completed cases are observed; if group/shift/risk affects completion, conditioning on completed cases is collider bias | Prompt states only completed cases exist; 30-day quality is coarse and post-process | No direct evidence yet of differential abandonment/open cases | Build full intake cohort including open/abandoned cases; survival or censored model; negative-control outcomes measured before routing | Favored if completed-only gap shrinks or reverses when censoring is handled. Weakened if full-cohort estimates preserve the same contrast |

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline instrumentation | Add event timestamps for Register, Classify, Compliance Check, Review, Request Info, Close; capture routing reason, queue length at Classify, reviewer ID/certification, and open/abandoned status | Measurement, selection, queue-state ambiguity | All incoming cases; no routing change | No operational treatment change; privacy controls for reviewer IDs | DFGs, trace variants, queue wait split, compliance wait, service time, rework loops, completion/censoring | Establishes whether overlap, positivity, and component decomposition are estimable | Stop data collection if it degrades SLA or data quality fails audit |
| Overlap-restricted routing pilot | Randomize or quasi-randomize Orion vs Nova only in cells with prior support; block by complexity, shift, load, and compliance; require senior-reviewer parity | Direct and total effect of resource-group assignment | Individual eligible cases or small balanced blocks | Exclude cases where certification/policy mandates one group; cap high-risk cases; monitor appeals and SLA daily | Wait by segment, review service, Request Info transitions, cycle time, rework, completion status | Small causal contrast under improved positivity; tests whether moving cases to Nova helps or backfires | Rollback if treated arm exceeds control cycle time by pre-set threshold, e.g. 15%, or appeals/abandonments rise |
| Queue/compliance capacity intervention | Add compliance-review capacity or dedicated night/Request-Info triage without changing group labels | Queueing and compliance bottlenecks rather than group label | Shift/queue or compliance pod, with staggered rollout | Maintain certification rules; balance headcount across shifts; cost monitoring | Queue length at Classify and compliance, time around compliance, DFG variant rates, rework loops, cycle time | Tests the mechanism: if capacity relief removes the group gap, the issue is operational bottleneck, not group identity | Stop if queue falls but cycle/quality does not improve after defined review period, or overtime/cost exceeds cap |
| Adaptive scale or no-scale decision | Scale only the variant that improves cycle without quality harm; retain holdout cells | Generalizability and time-varying effects | Weekly blocks of eligible cases across shifts | Keep high-complexity safeguards; independent monitoring of quality and open-case accumulation | Same measurements plus survival analysis of open cases and 30/90-day quality | Distinguishes durable mechanism effects from initial learning or Hawthorne effects | Rollback any stratum with adverse cycle, quality, or completion signal; pause if overlap deteriorates |

**Final recommendation:** Do not implement the proposed blanket 50% transfer. The data do not show that the Orion label causes longer cycle time; the crude gap reverses after complexity standardization, and medium/high day Orion overlap is absent. Keep current routing for high-complexity/night cases until a controlled pilot says otherwise. First add event timestamps, queue state at Classify, reviewer ID/certification, routing reason, and complete open/abandoned records. Then run a small, safety-capped randomized or quasi-random pilot only where both groups have support. In parallel, test the actionable mechanism by adding compliance/queue capacity by shift without changing group labels. Scale only if the pilot identifies a genuine group effect or the capacity change confirms a bottleneck; otherwise reframe the issue as routing/selection and process-capacity management.

def parse_group_log(sub_df):
    compact_lines = []
    simple_lines = []
    for _, row in sub_df.iterrows():
        activities = row["trace"].split(">")
        compact_lines.append(f"{row['case_id']};{','.join(activities)}")
        simple_lines.append(",".join(activities))

    attempts = [
        ("\n".join(compact_lines), {
            "separator": ",",
            "case_id_separator": ";",
            "case_id_key": "case:concept:name",
            "activity_key": "concept:name",
        }),
        ("\n".join(compact_lines), {
            "parameters": {"separator": ",", "case_id_separator": ";"}
        }),
        ("\n".join(simple_lines), {
            "separator": ",",
            "activity_key": "concept:name",
        }),
        ("\n".join(simple_lines), {}),
    ]

    last_error = None
    for text, kwargs in attempts:
        try:
            log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(text, **kwargs)
            activities_seen = [
                event.get("concept:name")
                for trace in log
                for event in trace
            ]
            if len(log) > 0 and any(a is not None for a in activities_seen):

        except Exception as exc:
            last_error = exc

    raise last_error if last_error else RuntimeError("Could not parse PM4Py event log string.")

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Unobserved routing intent and case risk:** the most valuable addition is the routing-engine log containing the priority score, exception reason, and clinically/operationally encoded risk at `Classify`.

2. **Queue and compliance mechanics:** the most valuable addition is event-level timestamps plus queue length at `Classify` and before/after `Compliance Check`, enabling separation of true queueing from compliance waiting.

3. **Reviewer heterogeneity:** the most valuable addition is reviewer ID with certification, tenure, and assignment history, allowing within-reviewer and skill-adjusted comparisons.

4. **Selection into the observed sample:** the most valuable design change is a complete intake cohort including open, abandoned, and transferred cases with censoring dates.

5. **Quality interpretation:** the most valuable addition is a richer quality outcome measured at fixed horizons with appeal basis and post-process factors, rather than a single coarse `outcome_30d` flag.
