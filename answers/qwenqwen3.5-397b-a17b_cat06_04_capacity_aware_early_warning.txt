## 1. Decision rule

**Eligibility Gate**
Intervention is permitted only if `Current Stage` is **Triage** or **Diagnosis Started**. Cases at `Diagnosis Complete` or later are ineligible regardless of breach probability, as the swarm mechanism cannot affect the outcome post-diagnosis.

**Probability and Effect Robustification**
Raw model outputs are observational and segment-biased. We apply segment-specific adjustment factors to the *Estimated Absolute Risk Reduction* (not just $p_{now}$) before scoring:
*   **Strategic Enterprise:** Multiply risk reduction estimate by **0.7** (penalty for historical overprediction of model efficacy).
*   **SME:** Multiply risk reduction estimate by **1.1** (uplift for historical underprediction).
*   **Public Sector:** Use midpoint of range (calibration acceptable).
*   **Non-Native/Unknown:** Use lower bound of range (conservative due to sparse calibration).

**Scoring Function**
Cases are ranked by **Net Expected Value (NEV)**:
$$NEV = (Adjusted\_Risk\_Reduction \times Value) - (Swarm\_Cost + False\_Alarm\_Burden) - Spillover\_Penalty$$
*   $Spillover\_Penalty$ is estimated as: $(Slots\_Used \times 6 \times 0.02 \times Avg\_Value\_Untreated\_Diagnosis)$. For initial ranking, we approximate this as a fixed €1,500 per slot to account for queue externalities.

**Alert Persistence & Evidence Gate**
*   **Cool-down:** 4 hours between alerts for the same case unless stage changes.
*   **Re-entry Condition:** A repeated alert triggers intervention consideration *only if* a materially new event occurred (e.g., new log upload, stage transition, customer contact). Mere model refreshes with stable inputs are suppressed.

**Capacity Reservation**
Maintain **1 reserve slot** for high-priority regulatory incidents or sudden degradation in top-tier cases. Maximum **3 active interventions** per cycle.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down and Suppression Logic**
To prevent alert fatigue and resource waste on model noise, the following logic applies:
1.  **Standard Cool-down:** Once an alert is generated for a case, subsequent alerts within **4 hours** are automatically suppressed unless a **Stage Transition** occurs.
2.  **Evidence Gate:** If a repeated alert occurs within the cool-down window, it is only promoted if a **New Event Flag** is true (e.g., new attachment, customer reply, specialist note).

**Comparison of I-05 and I-07**
*   **I-05 (Suppressed):** This is the **third alert**, yet *no new event* has occurred since the first alert. The probability change (0.55  0.57) is likely model drift or noise. Intervening now would incur €100 burden and €450 cost with high likelihood of redundant effort. **Policy Action:** Suppress alert; reset timer only if stage changes.
*   **I-07 (Considered):** This is also a **third alert**, but a **new customer diagnostic log** was uploaded. This constitutes materially new information that may change the diagnosis path. Despite the lower $p_{now}$ (0.72 vs 0.75), the *uncertainty* has reduced, and the intervention might target the new log's findings. **Policy Action:** Eligible for ranking, but likely outcompeted by higher-value cases (I-04, I-08).

**Escalation**
If a case survives two suppressed alert cycles without resolution and time-to-SLA drops below 2 hours, manual review by a Queue Manager is triggered, bypassing the automatic suppression to prevent silent breaches.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-Based Prioritization Starvation**
*   **Risk:** The NEV scoring heavily favors high-value cases (I-08 €100k vs I-07 €5k). SME cases (I-05, I-07, I-10) may systematically lose slots to Strategic cases, even if their relative risk reduction is higher.
*   **Safeguard:** Implement a **Minimum Allocation Quota**. At least 1 of the 3 active slots must be allocated to a non-Strategic segment if a Tier 1 eligible case exists in that segment. (In this cycle, I-09 fills this role).

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support (I-09)**
*   **Risk:** I-09 is selected despite "sparse calibration." If the model is poorly calibrated for this segment, we may be wasting a slot on a case where the swarm is ineffective, while harming well-calibrated cases via spillover.
*   **Safeguard:** Tag interventions on uncertain segments for **Priority Audit**. If intervention efficacy for Non-Native cases falls below 50% of predicted over 3 months, suspend automated intervention for this segment until recalibrated.

**Differential Calibration and Label Bias**
*   **Risk:** Strategic cases (I-02, I-04, I-08) have overpredicted risk. Intervening on them reinforces the model's belief that they were "risky" (since they won't breach after intervention), preventing the model from learning that its raw probability was too high.
*   **Safeguard:** Store **Counterfactual Labels**. For intervened cases, record the *pre-intervention* probability as the training label for risk models, not the final outcome (breach/no breach), to avoid biasing the model against intervention.

**Spillover Harm Concentration**
*   **Risk:** Spillover delays affect "untreated diagnosis-stage cases." If these are systematically lower-value or specific segments (e.g., Public Sector I-06), the policy effectively taxes them to save Strategic cases.
*   **Safeguard:** Monitor **Spillover Incidence by Segment**. If Public Sector cases bear >80% of spillover delays while Strategic cases receive >80% of interventions, rebalance the queue scheduling algorithm to distribute delay evenly across segments.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational Monitoring**
*   **Slot Utilization:** Track % of reserve slot usage. If >50% of reserves are used for regulatory incidents, increase baseline reserve to 2 slots.
*   **Intervention Duration:** Monitor actual swarm time vs. 45-minute budget. If consistently >60 mins, reduce concurrent slot capacity to prevent queue collapse.

**Predictive and Causal Monitoring**
*   **Calibration Drift:** Weekly Brier Score decomposition by Segment. If Strategic calibration error exceeds 0.10, trigger model retrain.
*   **Causal Effect Estimation:** Use a **Stepped-Wedge Design**. Randomly withhold intervention on 10% of eligible Tier 1 cases (control group) to measure true absolute risk reduction vs. observational estimates. Compare breach rates of Control vs. Treated.
*   **Spillover Measurement:** Track breach rates of *untreated* diagnosis cases correlated with slot utilization hours. If breach rate rises >5% during high-utilization windows, the spillover coefficient (0.01–0.03) must be updated upward.

**Policy Recalibration Triggers**
*   **Threshold Adjustment:** If Net Impact (Base Scenario) becomes negative for two consecutive weeks, raise the NEV threshold for intervention by 20%.
*   **Capacity Rule:** If Adverse Scenario materializes (net negative impact) more than once per month, reduce active slots from 3 to 2 until congestion clears.
*   **Fairness Review:** Quarterly audit of intervention distribution vs. breach outcomes by segment. If SME breach rate increases while Strategic decreases, adjust NEV formula to weight Value less heavily.
