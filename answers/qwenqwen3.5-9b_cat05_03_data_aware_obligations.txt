## 1. Temporal Data Semantics

State reconstruction relies on a **forward-fill** strategy within each case partition, where attribute values are carried forward from the event that set them until superseded. Blank cells in the log indicate no change to the attribute at that specific event index, not a null value.

1.  **Event Time vs. Effective Time:** The log timestamp (`time:timestamp`) is the **record time** (when the system logged the update). The **effective time** is the record time unless evidence (e.g., Exemption Register) explicitly establishes a different `effective_from` time.
2.  **Same-Timestamp Ordering:** When multiple events share a `time:timestamp`, `event_index` acts as the tie-breaker. State at a given index `i` reflects changes from indices `< i` at that same timestamp.
3.  **Material Changes:** An update to `amount`, `risk_level`, `jurisdiction`, or `customer_type` is a material change. These invalidate prior reviews if they trigger R4 or R1/R2/R3 conditions after the review.
4.  **Final Attributes:** The value of an attribute at `Release_Funds` is the latest value recorded before that event. We do not assume the final case state applies retroactively to earlier events.
5.  **Evidence Time:** For exemptions, the **evidence time** (signature timestamp) must precede the **obligation trigger** (Release_Funds). A late database record (`Record_Exemption`) is valid evidence only if the `Evidence quality` column confirms it reflects an earlier approval, not just a late entry.
6.  **Missing Data:** If `amount` is blank at `Release_Funds`, R1 cannot be evaluated as TRUE. Per R6, this forces an `INDETERMINATE` classification unless other evidence resolves the threshold.

    $$ \text{if } S(t, c).\text{risk} = \text{HIGH} \lor S(t, c).\text{customer} = \text{PEP} $$
    $$ \text{then } \exists e_{ER} \in Enhanced\_Review, e_{EA} \in Compliance\_Approve \text{ s.t. } e_{ER} < e_{EA} < e $$
*   **R3:** $\forall e \in Release\_Funds \in E$:
    $$ \text{if } \exists e_{SLU} \in Sanctions\_List\_Update \text{ s.t. } e_{SS} < e_{SLU} < e $$
    $$ \text{then } \exists e_{SS} \in Sanctions\_Screen \text{ s.t. } e_{SLU} < e_{SS} < e $$
*   **R4:** $\forall e_{Review} \in \{Standard, Enhanced\} \in E$:
    $$ \text{if } \exists e_{Inc} \in Update\_Amount \text{ s.t. } e_{Review} < e_{Inc} < e_{Release} \land \Delta A > 20\% $$
    $$ \text{then } Review\text{ Invalid} $$
*   **R5:** $\forall e \in Release\_Funds \in E$ with Exemption:
    $$ Valid \iff \text{ApprovedBy}=HC \land \text{Effective} \leq t \land \text{ScopeMatches} \land \text{NotExpired} \land \text{Evidence}(t_{sig} \leq t) $$
*   **R6:** $\forall e \in Release\_Funds \in E$:
    $$ \text{if } \text{Missing}(Amount \lor Risk \lor Juris \lor Customer) \land \text{CannotResolve} \implies INDETERMINATE $$

## 4. Ambiguities and Alternative Readings

If the log's `Record_Exemption` time (11:00) were treated as the *only* evidence of validity (ignoring the register's metadata), the exemption would be considered late, leading to `NONCOMPLIANT` (R1 violation). The decision relies on trusting the Exemption Register's audit trail over the raw event log timestamp.
*   **TX-305:** The `Settlement_Confirmed` event (Event 4) shows 150,000. If the `Release_Funds` amount is assumed to match the settlement (per "settlement amount may equal release amount"), the case becomes `COMPLIANT` (R1 satisfied if 150k >= 100k, but no Enhanced_Review exists). However, R6 explicitly forbids assuming values. If we assume the missing amount was 0, R1 is not triggered. If we assume 150k, R1 is triggered but not satisfied. Therefore, the only rigorous classification is `INDETERMINATE`.
*   **TX-310:** `EX-88` validity hinges on the "witness" vs. "certificate". If the oral witness is deemed valid evidence (subjective), the exemption might waive R1. If the certificate date (10:30) is the hard constraint, the exemption is invalid. Additionally, R1 requires `Enhanced_Review`. Even if the exemption waived R1, the lack of `Enhanced_Review` (only `Standard_Review`) suggests the exemption must cover the *Enhanced_Review* requirement itself. Since the exemption scope says "waives R1 only", it does not waive the `Enhanced_Review` procedural requirement unless R1 is the *only* barrier. Since R1 requires Enhanced_Review, and the exemption waives R1, the requirement is waived. However, the exemption validity itself is `INDETERMINATE`.
*   **TX-311:** The critical ambiguity is the `event_index` tie-breaker. If `event_index` 4 (Jurisdiction Update) were processed before `event_index` 3 (Enhanced_Review) at the same timestamp, the jurisdiction would be `US` at the time of the review. This would trigger R1 (US != DE). However, the Semantic Facts explicitly state "event_index 3 precedes event_index 4". This confirms the jurisdiction was `US` at review time, but `DE` at release time. R1 triggers on Release_Funds state. Since Jurisdiction is `DE` at Release, R1 does not trigger.

## 6. Why Control-Flow-Only Conformance Is Insufficient

1.  **Final-State Leakage:** Control-flow logs often record the final `Release_Funds` event with the final `Jurisdiction` value. If a jurisdiction update occurred at the same timestamp but a lower `event_index` (as in TX-311), a control-flow checker might see the final state and assume the review was valid, ignoring the temporal sequence.
2.  **Post-Hoc Exemptions:** Control-flow cannot distinguish between an exemption valid *before* release and one recorded *after* release. TX-302 and TX-310 show `Record_Exemption` after `Release_Funds`. Control-flow sees the sequence but cannot validate the *validity* of the exemption based on evidence timestamps without semantic analysis.
3.  **Material Changes After Review:** R4 requires checking if an amount increase invalidated a prior review. Control-flow sees the events but does not inherently calculate "20% increase" or map the "latest review" to the "latest update" without explicit semantic logic.
4.  **Conditional Activation:** R1 only triggers if `Jurisdiction != DE`. If `Jurisdiction` changes from `US` to `DE` between `Enhanced_Review` and `Release_Funds` (TX-311), the obligation disappears. Control-flow often treats the path as fixed, missing the dynamic attribute change that removes the obligation.
5.  **Missing Data:** Control-flow cannot infer that a blank `amount` field means "unknown" rather than "zero". It might default to a safe value, violating R6 which mandates `INDETERMINATE` for missing thresholds.
6.  **Same-Timestamp Ordering:** When `Enhanced_Review` and `Update_Jurisdiction` share a timestamp (TX-311), control-flow must rely on `event_index` to determine which value was active during the review. Without explicit index-based state reconstruction, the checker cannot resolve whether the review happened before or after the jurisdiction change.

## 7. Recommended Logging and Control Improvements

1.  **Immutable Release Payloads:** The `Release_Funds` event should log a snapshot of all relevant attributes (`Amount`, `Risk`, `Jurisdiction`, `Customer_Type`) at the exact moment of release, preventing post-hoc state leakage.
2.  **Effective/Record/Signature Timestamps:** Exemption records must store three distinct timestamps: `Effective_From`, `Recorded_At`, and `Signature_Time`. This allows validation of R5 without relying on database insertion order.
3.  **Rule Versioning:** Log the version of the Policy Rule (e.g., `Rule_Version: 1.2`) at the time of the review. This ensures that a review is judged against the rules active at that time, not the current rules.
4.  **Change Provenance:** Every `Update_Amount` or `Update_Jurisdiction` event should include a `Change_Provenance` field (e.g., "Customer Request", "System Default"). This aids in R4 analysis regarding intentional vs. accidental changes.
5.  **Stable Event Ordering:** Enforce a strict global ordering of `event_index` within the event log, even across cases, or ensure `event_index` is strictly monotonic within a case and visible in the audit trail to prevent ambiguity in same-timestamp scenarios (R7).
6.  **Attribute Audit Trail:** For attributes that change frequently (like `Jurisdiction`), maintain a separate `Attribute_Change_Log` that records the old value, new value, and timestamp, rather than relying on forward-fill inference from the main event log.
