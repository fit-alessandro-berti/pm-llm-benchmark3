## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated Facts
- **M0 Definition and Norms**: M0 requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier on the approved branch, and a post-decision Notify_Requester step.
- **CS-9 Compliance Standard**: Enforces manual approval before activation, concurrent sanctions and quality checks, and separation of duties. Does not explicitly define whether rejected leads are scope-relevant or whether reactivations are new cases.
- **Pilot Decision PD-3**: Temporarily allowed Auto_Approve for low-risk DE suppliers; required Sanctions_Check and master-data validation before activation, with Quality_Review deferred by up to two days.
- **Emergency Directive FD-7**: Temporarily allowed provisional activations before quality approval and manual approval in disaster recovery, provided sanctions checks were completed, retrospective approval was received, and EUR 20,000 spend caps were enforced.
- **CRM Migration**: Post-2026-07-01, 70% of notifications are found in onboarding logs. Stable notification IDs exist but require CRM linkage.
- **Observed Practices**: Request_More_Info cycles and auto-approval beyond the pilot were deemed desirable by operations. Cases 703, 708, 714, and others show policy or process violations. Notification missingness rose following CRM integration.
- **Aggregate Trends**: Systemic gaps in M0 and observable deviations reflect either noncompliance or scope/model inadequacies.

### Disputed Propositions
1. **Rejected Leads in M0 Scope**:
   - **Prosecution**: Reject_Supplier is part of M0; all leads entering Sanctions_Check are cases, even if rejected early.
   - **Defense**: Reject_Supplier applies only after pre-screening for risks is passed; rejected leads are out of M0's scope.
2. **Provisional Activation Justifiability**:
   - **Prosecution**: Provisional activation outside FD-7 is noncompliant (Cases 705, 708). FD-7 expired on 2026-07-12.
   - **Defense**: Contextual recovery operations justified emergency actions, though documentation is imperfect.
3. **Pilot Conformance Post-Expiration**:
   - **Prosecution**: PD-3 strictly expired on 2026-07-31; post-pilot Auto_Approve flows (Case 714) were noncompliant.
   - **Defense**: Operations reasonably assumed continuation due to normalization and imminent policy adjustment.
4. **Request_More_Info Absence in M0**:
   - **Prosecution**: M0’s omission of loops for additional review steps creates gaps but doesn’t legitimize enforcement failures.
   - **Defense**: The desirable practice justifies expanded scope to synchronize with operations.
5. **Manual Approval Resource Integrity**:
   - **Prosecution**: SUP-712 and SUP-715 demonstrate separation-of-duties and conflict-of-interest gaps requiring stricter rule representation.
   - **Defense**: SUP-715 is a gray area, as the conflict-of-interest policy required disclosure but not explicit prohibition.

### Burden of Proof
- **Noncompliance Determination**: Requires clear violations of explicitly stated or formalized policy (CS-9, M0) without valid exception authority.
- **Exception Validation**: Requires explicit or implied authority (e.g., temporary directives, pilot approvals) with evidence supporting the legitimacy of deviation in the operational context.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The operation is materially **noncompliant** in multiple areas:
- **Deviation from Formal Authority**: Post-pilot Auto_Approve usages (SUP-714, SUP-702 aggregate) lack formal authority, disregarding the expiration of PD-3. Similarly, provisional activations in SUP-705 are indefensible without written extensions of FD-7. SUP-703 and SUP-708 violated norms without justification, introducing significant risk (EUR 180,000 purchase and pre-sanctions activation, respectively).
- **Normalization of Exceptions**: Operations has effectively treated expanding Auto_Approve and relaxed Quality_Review (e.g., SUP-714, 289 aggregate PD-3-like cases) as standard policy, undermining CS-9 before formal adoption.
- **Hidden and Scope-Excluded Risks**: SUP-710 lacks registration events for reactivations. SUP-709 treats rejected leads ambiguously; the prosecution reasonably assigns them as M0 scope-relevant, supported by explicit inclusion of Reject_Supplier in M0.
- **High-Risk Violations**: SUP-703 flagrantly bypassed necessary validations, and SUP-708 activated improperly, introducing severe reputational and financial risks.
- **Data and Notification Gaps as Enablers**: CRM migration increases the risk of overlooked notifications, rising from 2% to 29% missingness (SUP-706 as an example). Resource misrepresentations in SUP-712 (aliases) further heighten compliance concerns.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is **obsolete, incomplete, and incorrectly scoped**:
- **Concurrency and Temporal Iteration**: M0 lacks Request_More_Info loops (SUP-707) and sequences for deferred Quality_Review under operational realities (SUP-702). Concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review are inadequately captured when sequencing changes are valid (e.g., emergency directives, SUP-704).
- **Pilot and Emergency Overlays**: FD-7 provided defensible grounds for SUP-704, and its factual emergency justifies SUP-705. Post-pilot Auto_Approve cases reflect operational intent toward policy adjustment, with no high-risk departures in SUP-714.
- **Scope Ambiguities**: SUP-709’s rejected lead is out-of-scope, as M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance. Similarly, reactivation in SUP-710 lacks clear procedural inclusion by CS-9 or M0.
- **Label and Resource Defects**: SUP-713 (QA_Clearance equivalence) highlights M0’s obsolescence for capturing procedural aliases. SUP-715 raises policy-compliance questions but not explicit M0 violations, as conflict-of-interest policies remain external.
- **CRM and Data Gaps**: SUP-706 is rendered uncertain due to CRM link volatility post-migration.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Transitions
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions_check = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality_review = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approval = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_case = Transition(label="Close_Case")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")

# Standard Route
standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions_check, quality_review])

# Approval Branches
manual_approval_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
manual_approval_branch.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

auto_approval_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_approval, activate])
auto_approval_branch.order.add_edge(auto_approval, activate)

decision = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[manual_approval_branch, auto_approval_branch, reject])

# Main Model
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, standard_checks, request_more_info, decision, notify, close_case])
M1.order.add_edge(register, standard_checks)
M1.order.add_edge(standard_checks, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(request_more_info, standard_checks)  # Loop/inquiry articulation
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close_case)
```
