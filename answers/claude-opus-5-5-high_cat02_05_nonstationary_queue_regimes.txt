# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Clock and units.** Everything below uses an *opening-hour clock*: one day = 12 open hours (07:00–19:00) = 720 open minutes. Rates are cases per open hour (or per day). "Flow time" from Little's Law is therefore measured in **open hours**. Closed hours are not counted, because no service happens then and the WIP snapshots are assumed to be taken only while open (assumption A3 below).

**Estimands (what we would like to know, and what the table actually supplies).**

| Symbol | Quantity | Supplied? | How it is proxied here |
|---|---|---|---|
| λ | arrival intensity (cases per open hour) | daily counts; 4 hourly vectors | arrivals / 12; hourly bins for 4 days only |
| X | throughput (completions per open hour) | daily counts | completed / 12. This is **not** capacity unless the system is saturated |
| E[S], CV_S | mean and variability of active handling time | median and p90 only | ranges: a lognormal-implied mean (A1), bounded above by FTE-minutes per completion |
| a = λ·E[S] | offered load (FTE needed) | not directly | λ × E[S] range |
| ρ = a / c | utilisation, with c = `staff_fte` | not directly | range, e.g. "0.95–1.15", never a point |
| L | time-average WIP | `mean_wip` from 15-minute snapshots | used as-is (integer-rounded, ±0.5) |
| W | mean time in system (flow time), open-hour clock | **not supplied** | L/λ, only where the Little's-Law conditions hold (Section 6) |
| B₀ | backlog at 07:00 | not supplied | inferred for 4 days from the hourly cumulative net flow plus `mean_wip` |
| net | arrivals − completed | derived | stock-flow check |

**Labelled assumptions.**
- **A1 (service shape).** Service time is roughly lognormal, so mean ≈ median·exp(σ²/2), with σ = ln(p90/median)/1.2816. I need this because only quantiles are given. I use it only for ranges. Two cross-checks: a quantile lower bound, mean ≥ 0.4·median + 0.1·p90, and an upper bound of staff·720 / completed (the FTE-minutes available per completed case). In the high-mix regime the lognormal mean (≈44 min) slightly exceeds that upper bound (≈42.5 min), so A1 overstates the mean there. I therefore report 36–42.5 min.
- **A2 (mix accounting).** Simple and complex variants each have a stable mean service time over time. I use this only as a falsification device in Section 4.
- **A3 (clock).** `mean_wip` averages the 48 snapshots taken 07:00–18:45, and overnight the system is frozen.
- **A4 (capacity).** `staff_fte` is capacity dedicated to this queue. If staff also do other work, all ρ values are understated.
- **A5 (bins).** The hourly vectors are labelled as given. A one-hour inconsistency is flagged below and not resolved.
- **A6 (exit).** "Completed" is the only exit. This is testable, and it appears to be **violated** in the high-mix period (threat 2).

**Threats to naive inference (ten identified).**
1. **Unknown boundary states and carry-over.** B₀ is not supplied. From the hourly vectors I infer B₀ ≈ 18 on 04-07, ≈ 16 on 04-15, ≈ 27–28 on 04-21 and ≈ 18 on 04-25. So roughly 15–25% of a weekday's volume is already in the system at opening. Daily completions therefore include earlier arrivals, and a day's completions are not the throughput of that day's arrivals.
2. **The stock-flow balance does not close.** Within the same week, the change in mean WIP should be about (net_prev + net_today)/2. That holds within ±2.5 cases in baseline weeks and across the outage (04-15: predicted +24.5, observed +24). In the high-mix weeks, however, the predicted change is about +14 per day against an observed ≈ +1.4 per day, a gap of ≈ 10–16 cases per day. Over 04-20…04-28 the cumulative net is +106 cases, yet weekday WIP rose only from ≈ 23 to 50. Candidate explanations, none of which can be separated here:
   - uncounted exits (cancellations, transfers, merges);
   - arrivals double-counted after a coding or channel change (for example, split sub-cases);
   - WIP snapshots that exclude some states (for example, "waiting for customer" in the multi-step variant);
   - closures outside opening hours.
3. **The WIP scope changes on weekends.** The inferred Saturday 04-25 opening backlog (≈ 18) is far below Friday 04-24's likely closing WIP (mean 43 plus net +10, so roughly 45–50). WIP then rebounds to 49 on Monday. Either the weekend snapshots count a subset (for example, only a weekend-routed queue) or weekday backlog is "parked". Weekend WIP is therefore not comparable with weekday WIP.
4. **Quantiles are not means, and service is not flow time.** Medians and p90s of *active handling* say nothing directly about waiting. The mean handling time is only bracketed, and flow time is not observed at all.
5. **The case-mix change is confounded.** `complex_pct`, `peak_08_10_pct`, service quantiles and intraday burstiness all change on the same day (04-20, a Monday, which fits a deployment over the weekend). A real mix shift, a coding change, a new batch intake channel, or all three are observationally equivalent in these aggregates.
6. **Staffing is endogenous, and the intervention's start time is unknown.** Extra FTE appear exactly when backlog is high (04-16: 7.0 after the outage; 04-29: 7.0 after WIP hit 50). Staffing therefore correlates positively with WIP in levels. The 04-29 daily mean of 7.0 is itself odd for a mid-day start. It implies either an early start or more than one extra FTE later in the day, so 04-29 is treated as a transition day.
7. **The hourly vectors are misaligned with the daily peak metric.** `peak_08_10_pct` matches the bins *labelled* 09–11, not 08–10:
   - 04-07: 31.7% vs 31;
   - 04-15: 33.3% vs 32;
   - 04-25: 21.6% vs 22.

   The bins labelled 08–10 give 16.8%, 18.1% and 11.8%. On 04-21 neither alignment matches: 36.5% or 9.6% against a table value of 47, and no two-hour window reaches 47%. This points to a time-zone or daylight-saving offset, or a mislabelled metric, plus a specific inconsistency on 04-21.
8. **The outage is not a clean 180-minute rectangle.** On 04-15 the 10–11 bin already shows only 8 completions against 22 arrivals (04-07: 14 completions against 20 arrivals). After restart, completions ran at only 5.6 per hour, the same as the ordinary late-afternoon rate (5.8 on 04-07), even though about 55 extra cases were waiting. The team manages 11–15 per hour when work is queued (04-07, 10:00–12:00). The recorded `outage_min` therefore understates the capacity loss.
9. **Serial dependence and small samples.** Lag-1 autocorrelation of weekday WIP is ≈ 0.75, so 28 rows carry only a handful of effectively independent backlog observations (n_eff ≈ n(1−r)/(1+r) ≈ 4). Most regimes have 1–2 days.
10. **Rounding and averaging.** Values are integer-rounded, `staff_fte` is a daily mean (no intraday roster), and only 4 of 28 days have hourly data, with none in the intervention period. If cases can arrive outside opening hours, they enter WIP without being counted as arrivals.

- calendar type: weekday or weekend;
- structural state: low mix before 04-20, high mix from 04-20;
- transient shocks: the outage and its recovery, and the staffing intervention.

**How the outage and recovery are handled.** The outage day is split into three intraday regimes (P3a, P3b, P3c). None of them is pooled with P1. The low-mix weekend after the recovery is kept in P2 but flagged.

# 3. Alternative segmentation

**Alternative A: backlog-state segmentation, ignoring the calendar.** Each day is labelled *accumulating* (net ≥ +5), *balanced* (|net| < 5) or *draining* (net ≤ −5).

| State | Days | Mean arrivals | Mean completions | Net per day | WIP | FTE | Complex % | ρ (A1) | Median service |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Accumulating (04-15, 04-20 to 04-25, 04-27 to 04-28) | 9 | 106 | 89 | +16.6 | 42 | 5.6 | 42 | about 1.1–1.2 | 31–36 |
| Balanced | 14 | 72 (36–107) | 71 | +0.6 | 18 | 4.3 | 26 | about 0.75 | 24–35 |
| Draining (04-16, 04-17, 04-29 to 05-01) | 5 | 111 | 121 | −9.6 | 40 | 6.8 | 37 | about 0.9 | 29–33 |

*Operational story:* backlog state, not the calendar, drives what a case experiences. Service times are elevated in **both** the accumulating and draining states. That fits state-dependent service: slowdown under load, or aged and interrupted cases being worked off during a drain. It is the natural basis for a fluid or Markov-switching backlog model and for recovery planning.

*Strengths:*
- It aligns directly with Little's-Law validity, since only balanced windows qualify (Section 6).
- It exposes that high WIP arises in two different ways: arrival or mix overload, and an exogenous capacity loss.
- It puts days with similar staffing pressure together, whatever their calendar position.

*Weaknesses:*
- It is defined by an outcome (net flow), so it is partly circular, and staffing responds to it.
- It lumps an exogenous shock (the outage) together with a structural overload (P5).
- The balanced state mixes weekdays and weekends (arrivals 36–107), so its averages describe no real day.
- Net flow is unreliable because of the stock-flow gap (threat 2).
- The ±5 threshold is arbitrary. 04-26 (net +4, ρ about 1.0) counts as balanced.

**Alternative B: two structural regimes (split at 04-20) × weekday/weekend, with shocks treated as covariates.**

| Structural regime | Days | Arrivals/day | Completions/day | WIP | ρ (A1) | Median service |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| S1 low-mix weekday | 10 | 101.7 | 99.6 | 27.6 | about 0.7 | 24–33 |
| S1 low-mix weekend | 4 | 41.8 | 41.5 | 13.8 | about 0.75 | 28–30 |
| S2 high-mix weekday | 10 | 114.3 | 106.7 | 42.1 | about 1.1 | 32–36 |
| S2 high-mix weekend | 4 | 48.0 | 45.0 | 21.0 | about 1.05 | 34–36 |

*Story:* the service changed permanently on 04-20. That could be a real mix shift, a new intake channel, or a re-coding. The outage and the intervention are perturbations inside the regimes, to be modelled as indicator covariates (for example, in a regression of net flow on staff and mix).

*Strength:* each cell has more days (4–10), so the estimates are more stable, and the model is simpler.

*Weaknesses:* S1 weekday WIP (27.6) is contaminated by the outage and recovery (the clean P1 value is 20.3). S2 weekday ρ (≈1.1) averages overload (≈1.17) with the intervention (≈0.93), which hides that the intervention is what separates a growing queue from a draining one.

**Comparison with the primary segmentation.** The primary segmentation keeps these distinctions, at the cost of 1–2-day cells with low confidence. I use it for capacity and Little's-Law decisions. Alternative A is the better lens for backlog recovery, and Alternative B for demand forecasting and for the mix and coding investigation.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

**(a) Weekday/weekend composition.**
- Pooled daily arrivals are 90.0 (SD 30.0, CV 0.33). The distribution is bimodal: no day lies between 53 and 92.
- Inside the regimes, day-to-day variation is small. The Fano factor of daily arrivals is 0.26–0.29 for P1 and P5, so their SD is about 5.
- The pooled rate of 7.5 per open hour describes neither weekdays (8.3–9.7) nor weekends (3.5–4.1).
- Arrivals per FTE are almost identical (baseline 16.9 on weekdays, 16.7 on weekends), yet weekend handling is slower (median 28–30 against 24–26). Baseline weekend ρ (≈0.77) is therefore higher than weekday ρ (≈0.66), even though a pooled "arrivals per FTE" ratio suggests equal pressure.
- The pooled mean WIP of 29.9 is above every baseline weekday (maximum 23) and below every P5 weekday (minimum 37).

**(b) Complex-case mix.**
- The pooled complex share is 32.9%, but **none** of the 28 days falls between 28% and 38%.
- Pooled indicative E[S] (≈37 min) and mean staffing (5.14 FTE) give a pooled ρ of about 0.9: tight but "stable". Segmented, it is ≈0.66 in P1 and ≈0.95–1.15 in P5.
- *Mix accounting (A1, A2).* Suppose only the mix changed, from 20.9% to 44.6% complex, with fixed variant means. Reproducing the rise in indicative mean service from ≈28 to ≈44 min then needs simple ≈ 14 min and complex ≈ 81 min. But with 79% simple cases averaging 14 min, the baseline median could not be 24–26 min. So the mix shift alone cannot explain the service increase. Per-variant handling times must also have lengthened (congestion slowdown, a different kind of "complex" case), or the variant labels were re-coded. The mix change is **not** only a composition effect.
- `complex_pct` and `peak_08_10_pct` break on the same day and co-move (weekday r ≈ 0.95; all days r ≈ 0.62). Pooled correlations between peak share and WIP therefore cannot be attributed to burstiness rather than mix.

**(c) Staffing intervention and outage (a Simpson-like reversal).**
- *Levels:* weekday mean WIP is 43.7 on 7.0-FTE days against 33.5 on 6.0-FTE days. The correlation between staff and WIP is +0.65 over all days and +0.29 over weekdays. Taken naively, "more staff, more WIP".
- *Changes within the high-mix regime:* the mean day-over-day ΔWIP is +2.0 at 6.0 FTE and −7.0 at 7.0 FTE, with corr(staff, ΔWIP) = −0.75.

The sign reverses because staff were added *because* WIP was high (reverse causation or confounding by backlog state). The pooled level association and the within-regime dynamic association point in opposite directions.

The outage behaves similarly:
- Week 2 totals (613 arrivals, 592 completions, ratio 0.97) look nearly normal, although the week contained a +47 shock and a −30 drain.
- The pooled maximum of 129 completions per day and the 04-16 rate of 1.54 per FTE-hour are recovery artefacts.
- Pooled completions/arrivals is 0.956, which hides a range from 0.55 (04-15) to 1.15 (04-16).

**(d) Flow-time illusion.** The pooled L/λ is 29.9 / 7.50 = 3.98 open hours. The defensible baseline weekday value is 2.4 open hours, so the pooled figure is 1.6× larger. It is not the flow time of any regime, and in the high-mix period it is not a flow time at all (Section 6).

**(e) Tail shape.** p90/median rises from 1.8–1.9 (baseline) to 2.3–2.4 (high mix). Under A1 that is a service CV of about 0.5 against 0.75. With ρ near 1, waiting-time approximations such as Kingman's scale with (c_a² + c_s²)/2 · ρ/(1−ρ). Pooling therefore underestimates tail waiting in exactly the regime where it explodes. Robust summaries (median, p90, ranges) are reported, because the means are not identified.

**Hourly vectors (12 bins per day).**

**Interpretation.**
- The raw within-day Fano factor mixes a *deterministic* intraday profile with randomness. A Fano of 3.2 on 04-07 does **not** prove overdispersion relative to a non-homogeneous Poisson process. It mostly reflects the morning peak, as the smooth, positive lag-1 ACF (0.62) shows.
- The profile-adjusted index (Pearson χ²/df against the 04-07 shape) is the more honest indicator:
  - 04-21 is about 5× over-dispersed relative to the ordinary weekday shape. Bins of 34, 24 and 14 arrive at 3-hour intervals, giving a negative lag-1 and a positive lag-3 ACF. This periodic signature fits batch releases or bulk logging from an upstream system (hypothesis). From counts alone it cannot be told apart from genuinely clustered demand. Identical timestamps, a source-channel field or a batch id in the raw log would settle it.
  - 04-15 has an index of 0.21, *under-dispersed* relative to the 04-07 shape. The two days follow the same profile more closely than Poisson noise would allow, so either demand is highly regular or the representative vectors are smoothed or stylised. Also, the outage did not change intake at all.
- **Scale contrast.** Daily arrivals within a regime are *under*-dispersed (Fano 0.26–0.8), while intraday counts are *over*-dispersed. Daily totals are predictable, and the risk sits in hourly timing. Staffing must be planned hourly, and a Poisson model at either scale would be wrong in opposite directions.
- **Queue impact of the bursts.** One 34-case burst at a high-mix E[S] of 36–42.5 min is about 20–24 FTE-hours of work, i.e. 3.4–4 hours of the full 6-FTE team. The next burst lands 3 hours later, so the backlog ratchets upward during the day (build-up +27 against +6 at baseline).

**Daily sequence.**
- Lag-1 ACF of WIP is 0.60 over all days and 0.75 over weekdays only: backlog is persistent.
- For arrivals, lag-1 ACF is 0.39 and lag-7 ACF is 0.72, which is dominated by the weekly cycle.
- After removing regime means, the arrival residual lag-1 ACF is −0.41. That is largely the negative bias of demeaning very short segments, not evidence of anti-correlation.
- Standard errors that treat days as independent are too small. For WIP, the effective sample size is roughly 4, not 28.

**Change-point evidence.** I ran a binary segmentation on the 20 weekday rows (mean shifts, minimum segment 2 days, robust noise scale) at two penalty levels. The results are working hypotheses, not ground truth.

The convergent evidence is a transient shock on 04-15 (lasting to about 04-17), a structural break on 04-20 (mix, tail, peak share), a capacity change on 04-29, and possibly a gradual pre-drift in mix from 04-14. How sensitive the breaks are to the penalty is itself a sign of how little data there is.

**Why four representative days cannot establish a stochastic model.**
- There is one day per regime, so between-day variability of the hourly profile cannot be estimated.
- There is no hourly vector for the intervention or high-mix weekend regimes.
- A non-homogeneous Poisson process, a Cox (doubly stochastic) process and a batch-Poisson process cannot be distinguished from 12 counts.
- Completions per hour depend on the unknown B₀ and on the queue discipline.
- The vectors disagree with the daily peak metric (threat 7).
- Service times are not given per hour, so the hourly ρ(t) cannot be computed.

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing.**
- Baseline weekdays at 6 FTE have 30–40% slack (ρ ≈ 0.6–0.7).
- Under the high-mix regime, offered load is ≈ 114 × (36–42.5) / 720 ≈ 5.7–6.7 FTE on weekdays and ≈ 2.4–2.8 FTE on weekends. Keeping ρ ≤ 0.85 needs roughly **6.7–7.9 FTE on weekdays** and **2.8–3.3 FTE on weekends**.
- The temporary 7.0 / 6.5 / 3.0 FTE setup reaches ρ ≈ 0.85–0.95. That is barely sufficient: it drains only about 6 cases per weekday.
- The recurring Friday dip to 5.5 FTE is acceptable at baseline but not under the high mix.
- Handle the weekend on its own terms: handling is slower per case, and ρ was already ≈ 1.0–1.15 at 2.5 FTE in P6.

**Backlog recovery.**
- The 04-15 outage backlog was largely worked off in 2 days (net −30), with the rest absorbed by the weekend, *because* baseline slack existed. The recovery borrowed about 35% idle capacity plus 1 extra FTE, and ran at a busy fraction of about 0.85–0.95.
- The same outage in the high-mix regime would add about 50 cases (roughly 37 arrivals during the outage plus restart losses). With zero slack at 6 FTE, that backlog would never drain. At 7 FTE it would take about 8 weekdays.
- Recovery plans must depend on the regime. Pre-arrange surge capacity of +2 FTE for 1–2 days after any outage, and add a restart procedure. After the outage, completions stayed at the normal late-day level of 5.6 per hour, well below the demonstrated 11–15 per hour, although a large backlog was waiting. That is a fixable loss.
- On current backlog: weekday WIP has fallen from 50 to 32. At −6 per day it needs about 2–3 more weekdays to reach 20–25. **Before declaring recovery, resolve the stock-flow gap.** If the roughly 100 "missing" cases are parked work rather than genuine exits, the true backlog is much larger.

**Peak smoothing.**
- On 04-21 the 3-hourly bursts (34, 24, 14) account for most of the intraday build-up. Check whether they are upstream batch releases, and if so ask for continuous or hourly micro-batches.
- Consider triaging complex cases at intake into a separate lane with dedicated staff, so that simple cases do not queue behind long ones.
- Schedule shift starts and breaks around the peak. First resolve the one-hour label offset (threat 7), so staff are not rostered an hour off the real peak.

**A policy that looks beneficial pooled but is risky segmented.** *"End the temporary staffing and return to 6.0 / 5.5 FTE on weekdays and 2.5 on weekends once WIP is back to about 30."*
- The pooled 4-week view supports it: pooled ρ ≈ 0.9, completions at 96% of arrivals, mean WIP 29.9, and pooled "7-FTE days have *higher* WIP".
- The segmented view shows the high-mix regime at 6 FTE has ρ ≈ 0.95–1.15. Recorded net flow was +13 per day, and observed WIP grew until staff were added. Reverting would rebuild the backlog to about 50 within 1–2 weeks. Any further outage would be unrecoverable without an emergency surge.
- Two related pooled traps:
  - sizing capacity from the 129 completions of 04-16, a recovery surge rather than a sustainable rate;
  - moving weekend staff to weekdays because weekend WIP "looks low", when the weekend WIP scope is doubtful and weekend ρ is about 1.

**Data collection (needed before any queueing-law claim beyond the week-1 window).**
1. A case-level event log with arrival, activity start and complete, final exit and exit type (completed, cancelled, transferred, merged), resource, and channel or batch id.
2. The variant label with its classification version, to tell a real mix shift from re-coding.
3. WIP snapshots at 07:00 and 19:00, with case status and queue scope stated explicitly for weekends.
4. The intervention start time and an hourly roster.
5. Outage start and end, including degraded-mode periods.
6. Confirmation of the time zone and bin labels.
7. Hourly vectors for every day.
8. Regime-aware monitoring: CUSUM charts on weekday `complex_pct`, p90 and net flow, with separate weekday and weekend baselines.

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- 6. change-point heuristic
def binseg(x, mult, min_size=2):
    """Greedy binary segmentation on mean shifts (SSE); BIC-like penalty. Heuristic, not ground truth."""
    x = np.asarray(x, float)
    # robust noise scale from first differences, so one outage-sized jump does not inflate the penalty
    sd = np.median(np.abs(np.diff(x) - np.median(np.diff(x)))) / 0.6745 / np.sqrt(2)
    sd = max(sd, 0.5)                                   # floor: integer rounding of the table
    penalty = mult * sd ** 2 * np.log(len(x))
    segs, cps = [(0, len(x))], []
    while True:
        best = None
        for (s, e) in segs:
            seg = x[s:e]
            base = np.sum((seg - seg.mean()) ** 2)
            for k in range(s + min_size, e - min_size + 1):
                l, r = x[s:k], x[k:e]
                gain = base - np.sum((l - l.mean()) ** 2) - np.sum((r - r.mean()) ** 2)
                if best is None or gain > best[0]:
                    best = (gain, k, (s, e))
        if best is None or best[0] < penalty:
            return sorted(cps)
        _, k, (s, e) = best
        cps.append(k)
        segs.remove((s, e)); segs += [(s, k), (k, e)]
