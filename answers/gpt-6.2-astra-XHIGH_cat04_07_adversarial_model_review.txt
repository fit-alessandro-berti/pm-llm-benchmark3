# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

Neither submission justifies automatic clearance or rejection. Model S can release a dossier after zero checks. Model C rejects an explicitly valid case in which Technical Review precedes Credit Check and approval is automatic. These conclusions follow from the expressions and would stand even if every reported score were reproduced.

I distinguish four uses.

| Use | What constitutes a costly error | Implication for model choice |
|---|---|---|
| Exploration and data-quality triage | Losing an unusual legitimate case or hiding evidence of a missing event | Broad coverage helps. Show the observed events, deviations and case attributes alongside the model. A skipped obligation must stay visible to the analyst. |
| Conformance and release control | Clearing a forbidden order or blocking a legitimate one | Test hard obligations individually. Distinguish a demonstrated violation, a conforming case and a case that needs evidence. False clearance and false referral have different costs. |
| Simulation | Generating impossible orders or giving rare paths unrealistic frequency or duration | Validate the allowed language first. Estimate routing probabilities, case mix, rework and timing separately. Neither a fitness score nor a choice operator supplies probabilities. |
| Stakeholder communication | Readers misunderstanding what must happen, or who decides an exception | Favor a compact rule that readers can apply to a new case. Test this with comprehension tasks. A diagram that is easy to read can still teach the wrong policy. |

In traces, **R** = Register Dossier, **C** = Credit Check, **T** = Technical Review, **S** = Supplier Review, **E** = Expedite Review, **D** = Correct Dossier, **M** = Manager Approval, **A** = Auto Approval, **L** = Release Order. A trace denotes one complete case with activities recorded at completion. In the trees, `->` is sequence, `+` is parallel composition with all children completed in any permissible interleaving, `X` chooses exactly one child, and `tau` consumes no event. **Accepts** means an exact visible execution from start to finish. An alignment can assign a high fitness to a rejected trace. An accepted event sequence can still violate an attribute-dependent rule.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

S factors out registration, approval and release, uses each visible label once, and accommodates both check orders and new combinations such as B5. For an analyst locating partially recorded cases, a permissive projection can preserve cases that a strict variant filter would discard. The favorable runtime and informal readability scores are consistent with that use.

Each of C, T, S and E can independently occur zero or one time. For a chosen subset, every permutation is legal. Thus there are 130 complete visible traces across the two approvals. The following are **exactly accepted**, with no alignment repair.

| Witness | Problem |
|---|---|
| R M L | Both mandatory checks can be omitted. |
| R C A L | Technical Review alone can be omitted, even for automatic approval. |
| R T M L | Credit Check alone can be omitted. |
| R E C T M L | Expedite Review can precede both mandatory checks. |
| R T E C A L | Expedite Review can occur while exactly one check remains outstanding. |
| R C T M L, on a confirmed external order with a complete record | Supplier Review can be omitted despite a known obligation. |
| R S C T M L, on an internal order | The tree admits S unconditionally. Whether a discretionary internal Supplier Review is permitted requires an interpretation of P3. |

An intentionally missing-event-tolerant observation model could record a gap, assign a repair cost and request evidence. Here the silent skip is a normal, fully conforming execution. Putting E in unrestricted parallel also permits the wrong order **when all checks are recorded**. Missing events do not explain that design choice. Conversely, S has no D and no repeated visible check, so it rejects every genuine correction trace. Its robustness is selective.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

C gives an auditor eight inspectable sequences. Every branch registers first, performs both mandatory checks before approval, takes one approval and releases last. Every E is after C and T. The sole branch with repeated checks places D before them. A versioned catalog could be appropriate if each sequence represented a separately authorized procedure and the business actually required variant-level authorization.

As written, one root choice commits the case to a complete sequence. A reversal of C and T is represented for some manager-approved orders. Auto Approval occurs only in R C T A L. Supplier Review never follows Expedite Review. The catalog has no way to infer that an authorized order of checks and an authorized approval route can be combined. B2 is already a counterexample to the proposed enforcement use, independently of the disputed holdout. The one rework branch permits exactly one D, then C followed by T, then M. It rejects single-check repairs and repeated corrections. It also accepts the two-check repair when the correction actually affected only one check. No branch tests the source of an order.

The expression has 48 visible leaves for nine distinct labels, or 39 excess occurrences under a simple tree-leaf definition. Seven distinct names recur. Those counts motivate asking how the team obtained 33. They do not establish a counting error in an undocumented net-level convention. A change to approval or supplier policy has to reach all applicable copies. The same business label may also refer to different transitions in replay diagnostics, making branch attribution and ownership difficult.

High training precision is plausible for a catalog built from frequent training variants. It is compatible both with learning a real restriction and with memorizing an incidental association such as Auto Approval always following C then T. The holdout degradation and F4 favor the latter explanation for at least some cases. Missing events, a shifted case mix or costly alignments could account for others. Preserve the catalog as evidence of observed or approved examples, with provenance for each such claim.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by likely impact on a release-control decision, with the most consequential first. The ranking prioritizes errors that could reverse a case decision.

1. **Reward for optionalizing mandatory behavior.** S can improve fitness by assigning zero visible deviation to a missing C or T. B3 demonstrates the resulting false clearance without any statistical assumptions.
2. **Missing-event and missing-attribute bias.** On R C T M L the control-flow record alone cannot distinguish an internal order, an external order with an unlogged S, and an external order with a genuinely skipped S. Loss of correction impact similarly confounds justified and unjustified repeats. Audit source records before labeling errors.
3. **Positive-log-only precision.** Alignment precision describes escaping continuations relative to observed prefixes. Legal rare continuations can lower it. Forbidden behavior present in the log can support it. A catalog can score highly while rejecting legal novelty, and a high score gives no guarantee about curated forbidden cases.
4. **Leakage, split sensitivity and selection.** A case split can still put snapshots of the same order family on both sides, and all-data variant selection can leak holdout information. A different split can strand rare approvals or corrections in just one partition. Neither leakage nor deliberate cherry-picking has been demonstrated. Obtain case manifests and repeat grouped and temporal tests.
5. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** A missing S, a log-only D or a mismatch near a duplicated label may be repaired in different ways. Relative costs and tied optimal paths affect the fitness and diagnostic story. Compare both the move types and total cost.
6. **Generalization rewarding permissiveness.** S reuses a few heavily visited transitions and offers many continuations. A visitation-based score can be high while B3 and B4 remain executable and B6 is impossible. Use unseen legal and matched illegal combinations to measure the desired generalization.
7. **Duplicate-label effects.** C disperses visits across copies, opens alternative alignment paths and makes replay-based counts sensitive to tie resolution. This can affect generalization, precision diagnostics and runtime in different directions. Audit transition identities and repeat on fixed conversions.
8. **Silent-transition effects.** S has four explicit skip alternatives, and conversion can add routing transitions to either tree. Hidden routing changes enabled sets, search effort, replay and degree counts. A hidden move is neither evidence that a business check occurred nor necessarily a visible deviation.
9. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** Familiarity, presentation order and liking a diagram can dominate an informal rating. Measure correct decisions and inter-rater differences before relying on either mean.
10. **Runtime confounding by model and workload size.** Enumerated branches, shared prefixes, concurrency, silent routing and difficult misfits all change the search space. The median gives a capacity signal, with limited diagnostic value until workloads, caching and timeouts are matched.

# 7. Third Alternative

Use a bounded workflow Petri net with a case-data check. The unit of rework is a mandatory check whose result is outstanding. A correction edits that set. This factors the policy into individual obligations and avoids enumerating whole cases.

**Formal description.** R starts a case with outstanding set `Q = {C,T}`. A check `x` can complete exactly when `x ∈ Q`, removing it. Each D has a nonempty impact `I ∈ {{C},{T},{C,T}}` and updates `Q ← Q ∪ I`. S can occur once anywhere in the review phase. E can occur once when Q is empty. Either M or A closes review when Q is empty and the supplier obligation is satisfied. L must immediately follow that approval. The net projects away the attributes by allowing the three possible impacts and optional S. The full policy also checks which impact and supplier rule apply to this case.

The following design choices need owner sign-off. S and E each occur at most once. A correction can arrive before all initial checks are complete or while another repair is outstanding. A check performed after two corrections can cover both edits. E requires current check results at the time it occurs. A later correction reopens affected mandatory checks without automatically reopening E or S. Both check orders are supported. No evidence about simultaneous execution is required. P3 requires S for external orders. If internal S is expressly forbidden, the internal rule enforces that too. Until that point is settled, a discretionary internal S goes to review.

**Executable structural model.** The unique visible D enters a short edit phase. Each silent exit implements an atomic update of the affected check states. Its identifier records the chosen impact for diagnostics. The active token prevents visible events from occurring halfway through the update.

```python
from itertools import product
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils.petri_utils import add_arc_from_to

R, C, T = "Register Dossier", "Credit Check", "Technical Review"
S, E, D = "Supplier Review", "Expedite Review", "Correct Dossier"
M, A, L = "Manager Approval", "Auto Approval", "Release Order"
impacts = (frozenset([C]), frozenset([T]), frozenset([C, T]))

def build_third():
    net = PetriNet("custom_order")
    def place(name):
        p = PetriNet.Place(name)
        net.places.add(p)
        return p

    def step(name, label, inputs, outputs):
        t = PetriNet.Transition(name, label)
        net.transitions.add(t)
        for p in inputs:
            add_arc_from_to(p, t, net)
        for p in outputs:
            add_arc_from_to(t, p, net)

    start, live, edit, approved, end = map(
        place, ("start", "active", "editing", "approved", "end"))
    checks = (C, T)
    due = {x: place("due_" + x) for x in checks}
    ok = {x: place("current_" + x) for x in checks}
    ready = {x: place("available_" + x) for x in (S, E)}
    closed = {x: place("closed_" + x) for x in (S, E)}

    step("register", R, [start], [live, due[C], due[T], ready[S], ready[E]])
    for x in checks:
        step("complete_" + x, x, [live, due[x]], [live, ok[x]])

    step("correct", D, [live], [edit])
    for impact in impacts:
        affected = [x for x in checks if x in impact]
        for bits in product((0, 1), repeat=len(affected)):
            before = [ok[x] if bit else due[x] for x, bit in zip(affected, bits)]
            name = "apply_" + "_".join(affected) + "_" + "".join(map(str, bits))
            step(name, None, [edit] + before, [live] + [due[x] for x in affected])

    for x in (S, E):
        guard = [ok[C], ok[T]] if x == E else []
        step("perform_" + x, x, [live, ready[x]] + guard, [live, closed[x]] + guard)
        step("skip_" + x, None, [live, ready[x]], [live, closed[x]])
    for x in (M, A):
        step("approve_" + x, x, [live, ok[C], ok[T], closed[S], closed[E]], [approved])
    step("release", L, [approved], [end])
    return net, Marking({start: 1}), Marking({end: 1})

model_third = build_third()  # (net, initial marking, final marking)
```

**Case-data check.** The approval guard takes the history through the last review event and the proposed approval activity. The complete-case wrapper also checks release and can replay the same guard retrospectively. An impact entry is a verified set of activity names keyed by the zero-based event index of D. On a complete and trustworthy record, `pass` means every compatible attribute assignment satisfies the rules, `fail` means none does, and `review` means the result depends on missing evidence or unresolved internal policy. It keeps at most four distinct outstanding sets. An invalid alternative is remembered even when a different alternative can replay the rest of the case.

```python
def approval_verdict(history, approval, external=None, affected=None, internal_supplier_allowed=None):
    events = tuple(history)
    affected = {} if affected is None else affected
    empty = frozenset()
    if not events or events[0] != R or approval not in (M, A):
        return "fail"

    pending = {frozenset([C, T])}
    possible_failure = False
    seen_optional = set()
    for i, x in enumerate(events[1:], start=1):
        if x in (S, E):
            if x in seen_optional:
                return "fail"
            seen_optional.add(x)
        if x == S:
            continue
        if x == D:
            evidence = affected.get(i)
            options = impacts if evidence is None else (frozenset(evidence),)
            if any(option not in impacts for option in options):
                raise ValueError("Unrecognized correction impact")
            pending = {q | option for q in pending for option in options}
        elif x == E:
            possible_failure |= any(pending)  # Some impact hypotheses have a due check.
            pending = pending & {empty}
        elif x in (C, T):
            enabled = {q for q in pending if x in q}
            possible_failure |= enabled != pending
            pending = {q - {x} for q in enabled}
        else:
            return "fail"  # includes a second R, early approval or early release
        if not pending:
            return "fail"

    if empty not in pending:
        return "fail"
    possible_failure |= pending != {empty}
    sources = (True, False) if external is None else (external,)
    internal_modes = ((True, False) if internal_supplier_allowed is None
                      else (internal_supplier_allowed,))
    supplier_seen = S in seen_optional
    supplier_ok = {supplier_seen if ext else (not supplier_seen or allowed)
                   for ext in sources for allowed in internal_modes}
    if True not in supplier_ok:
        return "fail"
    return "review" if possible_failure or False in supplier_ok else "pass"

def policy_verdict(events, external=None, affected=None, internal_supplier_allowed=None):
    events = tuple(events)
    if len(events) < 3 or events[-1] != L:
        return "fail"
    return approval_verdict(events[:-2], events[-2], external, affected,
                            internal_supplier_allowed)
```

For B6, setting `affected={3: {T}}` and `external=False` yields a pass. Removing the impact evidence sends it to review. Knowing that both checks were affected yields a failure because C remains outstanding. For live release, evaluate the guards **before** granting approval, using the order record and the correction attestation. For retrospective review, independently assess log completeness first. A missing supplier event on a suspect feed warrants reconciliation. An alignment or a silent impact choice must never supply the attestation.

# 9. Validation Experiments

1. **Reconstruct the comparison.** Obtain hashes of the raw extract, event schema, preprocessing and discovery code, case manifests, model versions, PM4Py/Python/solver versions, conversions, reductions and cost settings. Specify lifecycle completion, case boundaries, stable tie-breaking for equal timestamps, event IDs and handling of missing labels or duplicate rows. Preserve raw records for adjudication. Attempt the original split first. If it cannot be reconstructed, label the exercise a new paired evaluation.
2. **Use reproducible, independent samples.** Group every event of a case and linked snapshots of the same underlying order together. For each seed (17, 41 and 73), sort one row per case by stable ID and use the first fold of a shuffled five-fold stratified group split as an approximately 20% test set. Group by order family and stratify by source × correction present × approval type, keeping unknowns explicit. Publish the splitter version, sorted case-ID manifests or access-controlled hashes, achieved stratum sizes and rare cells that cannot span independent groups. Fit transforms, discover variants and tune on the training side only. Also lock a later calendar window before model selection. Compare the same cases across models. If the discovery procedures can be recovered, refit them per split. Testing fixed submitted models estimates a different quantity and requires fresh cases outside their discovery data.
3. **Restore the information needed for labels.** Link order master data to external/internal status and correction tickets to a per-D impact set. Check event completeness against independent supplier and approval records. Two reviewers blinded to model predictions adjudicate disagreements with the owner. Keep unknowns and ambiguous policy labels as their own strata. Report denominators by source, urgency, approval type, check order and impact, oversampling rare repairs for diagnostic tests. Reweight to production prevalence for workload estimates. If attributes cannot be recovered, publish the conditional tests on constructed cases and mark the corresponding production error rates unidentifiable.
4. **Challenge the languages.** Reserve the probes, the new witnesses and independently authored cases as labeled tests. Derive one-change negatives from valid traces by deleting C/T, moving E just ahead of the second check, inserting a repeat, omitting an evidenced external S, changing the attested impact, taking both approvals or releasing early. Include valid near neighbors for each. Hold out entire legal combinations while retaining their individual features in training, for example external + reverse order + auto, E before S, and selective repair + auto. Score exact fit, false clearance, false block and review rate separately. Do not relabel all unseen cases as violations.
5. **Perturb observations, preserving an execution oracle.** With fixed perturbation seeds and rates such as 1%, 5% and 10%, delete S, C, T or D from a copy of confirmed complete traces, and independently duplicate events, including adjacent and delayed duplicates. Run targeted one-event tests as well as random batches. Compare the decision on the underlying execution with the decision attainable from the corrupted record. Report unsafe clearances and appropriate uncertainty referrals separately from failures on clean data.
6. **Replay consistently.** Publish all four PM4Py measures separately for the frozen nets and the named evaluation logs, plus exact acceptance, per-case visible moves, transition counts and case-level distributions. Include paired case- or family-bootstrap intervals and rare-stratum counts. Holdout precision remains relative to holdout positives. Structural replay of the third net measures its attribute-erased projection. Report policy verdicts and evidence coverage in a separate table, including failures hidden by a successful structural replay.
7. **Vary alignment costs and measure runtime.** Start with the pinned library defaults. Repeat on the same cases with equal visible log/model costs, a higher cost for model-only C/T, and a lower cost for a model-only S on a feed known to lose supplier events. Vary silent costs, record ties where practicable, timeouts and changes in the preferred repair. Recompute normalization per cost profile before comparing fitness, and publish raw moves and costs. Use identical hardware, solver, worker count, cache policy and warm-up. Time matched cases by length, variant and rework, including failures, and report p50/p95/p99 and timeout fractions.
8. **Check execution semantics.** Run workflow-net soundness and inspect diagnostics for option to complete, proper completion and dead transitions. Enumerate reachable markings for these bounded nets, verify that every reachable marking can reach the final one and that no marking with an end token contains leftover tokens. For the third net, each check has one due/current token during review, each optional review has one available/closed token, and exactly one of active/editing is marked. These invariants bound the state space despite arbitrarily many corrections. Exercise all three impact exits, both approvals, both orders of S/E, early and consecutive edits, and the disabling of approval while a recheck is due. Check reachability again with fixed data guards for each policy mode. A sound projection alone cannot prove that the data-aware system can finish. Missing evidence needs an explicit adjudication route with an owner.
9. **Test understanding and ownership.** Recruit compliance, operations and IT reviewers. Give them matched training time and counterbalance model order. Individually score blinded decisions on matched fresh cases and short explanations of the rule applied. Record accuracy, time, confidence, reviewer role, sample size and inter-rater agreement. Separately have maintainers implement a policy change, such as a new condition on auto approval, and count edits, time and regression failures. Report subjective ease on an anchored scale alongside these results.
10. **Apply an explicit veto.** Reject automatic clearance for any model that passes a confirmed hard-policy negative or can clear an evidence-dependent case solely by guessing its missing attribute. Reject automatic blocking if an agreed valid case such as B2 is classified as forbidden. Require soundness, reachability of required alternatives and zero such failures on the locked challenge suite. Before the field pilot, agree with compliance and operations on tolerated upper confidence bounds for false clearance and false block, minimum sample sizes per critical stratum, and staffed capacity for referrals. A zero count on a small test suite does not establish the field bound.

**PM4Py evaluation skeleton.** Here `model_s` and `model_c` are the two supplied strings parsed with `pm4py.parse_process_tree`. Pass PM4Py event logs containing complete cases and the agreed `concept:name` and case mapping. Record the environment and the actual alignment variant used.

```python
def evaluate(model_s, model_c, train_log, holdout_log):
    systems = {"S": pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_s),
               "C": pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_c),
               "third_projection": model_third}
    report = {}
    for name, (net, im, fm) in systems.items():
        sound, diagnostics = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
        result = {"sound": sound, "soundness_details": diagnostics,
                  "net_size": (len(net.places), len(net.transitions), len(net.arcs)),
                  "simplicity": pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree")}
        for split, log in (("train", train_log), ("holdout", holdout_log)):
            result[split] = {
                "fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
                "precision": pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
                "generalization": pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm),
                "alignments": pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, im, fm)}
        report[name] = result
    return report

from collections import deque
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics

def exact_accepts(labels, system):
    net, im, fm = system
    queue = deque([(im, 0)])
    visited = {(im, 0)}
    while queue:
        marking, i = queue.popleft()
        if i == len(labels) and marking == fm:
            return True
        for t in semantics.enabled_transitions(net, marking):
            j = i
            if t.label is not None:
                if i == len(labels) or labels[i] != t.label:
                    continue
                j += 1
            state = (semantics.execute(t, net, marking), j)
            if state not in visited:
                visited.add(state)
                queue.append(state)
    return False

from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import algorithm as align

def costed_replay(trace, system, model_only, log_only, tau_cost=1):
    # trace is a pm4py Trace. Overrides are keyed by visible activity name.
    net, im, fm = system
    params = {
        "trace_cost_function": [log_only.get(e["concept:name"], 10000) for e in trace],
        "model_cost_function": {t: tau_cost if t.label is None
                                else model_only.get(t.label, 10000) for t in net.transitions},
        "sync_cost_function": {t: 0 for t in net.transitions if t.label is not None}}
    return align.apply_trace(trace, net, im, fm, parameters=params,
                             variant=align.Variants.VERSION_DIJKSTRA_NO_HEURISTICS)
```

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Exploration.** Retain S as a labeled exploratory view, paired with a rule-violation overlay that shows missing mandatory events and early E. Preserve C as a versioned variant catalog and regression fixture. Retire both from consideration as standalone compliance gates. C's sequences can serve as operating instructions only where their authorization is documented. Neither model has validated routing frequencies for simulation.

**Compliance.** Pilot the third alternative in shadow mode. Obtain owner sign-off on internal S, correction timing and accumulation, the scope of affected checks, optional-review frequency and whether a later edit invalidates E. Establish provenance and completeness for the source, impact and event records. Run the locked tests, satisfy the vetoes and field bounds in section 9, and demonstrate comprehension and referral capacity. If an obligation is demonstrably unsatisfied, withhold clearance. If the evidence is incomplete or policy is unresolved, route the case to a named reviewer. Only cases satisfying the signed rules on trustworthy evidence qualify for automated approval routing.

Version the rule set, net, case data used and decision together. Record which check became due, the evidence that discharged it, the approval route and any human override. Monitor source-specific missingness, novel combinations, false decisions from sampled adjudications, referral volume and latency. Suspend automation on a hard-rule false clearance or loss of the evidence feed. During the pilot, existing authorized human release control remains responsible.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal.** A compliance board could reasonably prefer a narrow catalog with human escalation. If delegated authority was granted for eight specific procedures, combining two familiar features can cross a real authorization boundary. Auto approval on an externally sourced order may expose the firm to a contract condition that does not appear in the event log. The third alternative would let that combination through once its explicit obligations were met. An auditor can point to the signed procedure behind a catalog entry. Explaining a decision that depends on a hidden impact choice, case-data provenance and the version of a separate rule checker requires additional controls. Repeated edits are particularly consequential. A single check after two corrections may fail to satisfy a contract that requires a separate verification after each amendment. Meanwhile, a referral queue can turn an apparent gain in coverage into a missed release deadline. The comprehension survey did not test the proposed hybrid, and the holdout has no independent compliance labels. Keeping an approved catalog for straight-through processing, sending other cases to an empowered reviewer and using S to investigate recording gaps could be the most defensible interim arrangement.

**Evidence that would change the decision.** Signed, exhaustive route-specific authorization, reliable source and impact guards, and acceptable escalation outcomes would support a guarded catalog for that scope. A document prohibiting an apparently legal recombination would immediately narrow the third model. Conversely, independent adjudication of novel combinations, authenticated correction scopes and a prospective shadow trial meeting the agreed error and workload bounds would justify moving the third alternative into release control. Until those facts are available, the expressions settle what the models admit and leave the disputed business cases open.
