# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

## Upstream data quality dependencies

- A1 and A2 feed nearly everything. Poor application data or misclassification propagates into KYC, document review, collateral routing, committee path, and four-eyes requirements.
- A5 depends on A2 because the missing-document checklist must vary by product and exposure. Equipment finance often needs collateral documents; Credit-line renewal has higher contract-remediation risk.
- A6 depends on A5 and financial-document completeness. If documents are missing or low quality, extraction/normalization creates garbage that flows into `Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis` and later `Committee packaging`.
- A9 depends on clean KYC, committee decision data, and reconciled artifacts. It should not be used as a downstream patch for upstream control failures.

## Legal/compliance controls

- KYC clearance must precede customer signing. A9 and A10 must enforce a hard gate; any provisional signing remains a controlled exception with remediation, not a default path.
- Contract terms must match committee decision. A9 and A12 should check decision-to-contract consistency; Credit-line renewal has 14% contract-remediation loops, so it needs stricter gate criteria.
- Four-eyes principle applies to High-exposure SME: credit analysis and committee packaging cannot be completed by the same person. A2 should tag High-exposure cases correctly; A8/A12/A9 can enforce audit logging, but the control must be explicit.
- Every model-assisted decision needs an auditable trail: model suggestion, human override, reason, and final decision.

## Human escalation capacity

- Compliance specialists are only 3 FTE, with dependency from KYC ops to two senior specialists. A3/A4 must route only true exceptions, or they will overload the same specialists they are meant to help.
- Credit analysts are 9 FTE and central in the collaboration network. A6 frees normalization time, but faster clean inflow may overload `Credit analysis -> Committee packaging`, especially for High-exposure judgment cases.
- Legal ops has 4 FTE. A9 reduces routine contract generation but shifts work to rule-library maintenance, exception review, and month-end control checks.
- Back office has 12 FTE but low utilization after 15:00. A1/A5 may reduce queue visibility but should not blindly increase upstream throughput if downstream teams are constrained.

## External valuation dependency

- A7 can triage and route, but cannot control external valuers. `Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` remains a synchronization point for 1,980 cases.
- Collateral valuation is required in 71% of Equipment finance and 18% of Working-capital cases. A8 packaging and committee decision should not assume A7 removes the external wait; it mainly reduces routing and late-valuation risk.
- A7 depends on A2 to identify when collateral is required and on partner SLAs for turnaround.

## Core-banking integration constraints

- IT can deeply integrate only two modules with core banking in the first six months. The recommended deep integrations are A6 and A9.
- A4/A5 can run through compliance workflow, CRM, portal, and document-management integrations without deep core-banking work.
- A1/A11 are core-banking-adjacent and should wait. A11 especially must not be integrated before control-completeness gates exist.
- A8/A10 depend on core data but can be staged after A6/A9 prove data quality and auditability.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

## Portfolio 1: Quick-win portfolio

Optimizes: fast deployment, high-frequency labor reduction, customer-facing digitization, and lower back-office touch time. It is politically attractive because A1 and A5 are easy to explain.

Sacrifices: little direct improvement in the 31h entity-mismatch wait, 58h external valuation wait, 20h financial-normalization wait, and contract-remediation controls. It may simply push more cases into KYC, credit analysis, collateral, and legal queues.

## Portfolio 2: Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio

Optimizes: critical-path waiting time and rework. A4 attacks entity mismatch; A5 attacks missing-document loops; A6 attacks normalization and credit-analysis preparation; A7 triages external valuation.

## Portfolio 3: Compliance-first portfolio

Optimizes: KYC control, entity-mismatch resolution, contract-rule checks, auditability, and exception remediation. It directly addresses regulatory and control findings.

Sacrifices: throughput. It leaves missing-document loops, financial-statement normalization, and collateral synchronization largely untouched. A3 also risks overloading 3 compliance specialists if false positives/negatives are poorly tuned.

## Portfolio 4: Recommended balanced bottleneck-control portfolio
Modules: A4, A5, A6, A9.

Optimizes: the most damaging process dependencies while adding one control-strengthening module. A4 reduces the entity-mismatch critical-path wait; A5 reduces repeated missing-document loops; A6 improves financial-statement quality before credit analysis and committee packaging; A9 adds contract-generation control checks and auditability.

Sacrifices: no direct automation of external valuation, KYC hit pre-classification, committee decision, or loan activation. It also does not fully solve four-eyes violations; those require explicit role controls and may justify A12 in year 2.

- Rework: A4 reduces `Resolve entity mismatch -> KYC check` loops; A5 reduces repeated missing-document loops; A6 reduces 16% normalization rework; A7 may reduce late external valuations. Contract-remediation loops remain.
- Handovers: fewer loops to back office and analysts, but A4 still routes to 3 compliance specialists, and A7 routes to external valuers.
- Resource demand: compliance specialists may become a hard bottleneck if A4 surfaces more exceptions. Credit analysts may be freed from normalization but then overloaded by cleaner inflow.
- Control violations: KYC/document completeness improves, but KYC-before-signing, contract-decision matching, and four-eyes are not directly strengthened.

## Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12

- Cycle time: KYC and entity-mismatch delays may fall, and contract remediation may shrink. However, missing-document loops, normalization wait, and external valuation wait remain, so end-to-end cycle time stays uneven.
- Waiting time: KYC wait and entity-mismatch wait improve. Financial-document wait, normalization wait, and `Wait for external valuation -> Committee packaging` remain.
- Rework: A9/A12 reduce contract-remediation loops and improve auditability. A3 creates a new false-negative risk; A4 reduces entity-mismatch loops. Missing-document rework is largely untouched.
- Handovers: more control exceptions may flow to compliance and legal. A12 centralizes remediation, but A3/A4 can concentrate work on 3 compliance specialists.
- Resource demand: compliance specialists and legal ops are heavily affected. Credit analysts remain central and may still wait on missing docs or normalization.
- Control violations: strongest improvement in KYC, contract-decision matching, audit trail, and exception handling. Four-eyes can be enforced if A12 is role-aware. Throughput and customer delay risks remain.

## Recommended balanced portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A9

- Cycle time: A4 reduces the entity-mismatch critical path; A5 reduces missing-document loops; A6 accelerates normalization before credit analysis; A9 reduces contract-preparation and remediation time. External valuation and committee cadence remain.
- Waiting time: major reductions in `Resolve entity mismatch`, `Financial document review`, `Normalize financial statements`, and `Contract preparation`. `Wait for external valuation` remains a synchronization point, especially for Equipment finance.
- Rework: A4 reduces KYC-entity loops; A5 reduces missing-document repetition; A6 reduces normalization rework; A9 reduces `Contract preparation -> Remediate control issue` loops and Credit-line renewal contract mismatches. Four-eyes violations require separate control work.
- Handovers: fewer back-office, analyst, and legal handovers. Compliance specialist handovers remain for entity mismatch, but A4 should triage them. A9 shifts legal work from drafting to rule maintenance and exception review.
- Resource demand: credit analysts freed from normalization can be redirected to High-exposure judgment and four-eyes. Legal ops handle fewer routine contracts but more rule exceptions. Compliance specialists remain constrained. RMs must manage A5 customer friction.
- Control violations: A9 strengthens KYC-before-contract/signing checks, contract-decision matching, and auditability. A4/A5/A6 improve upstream data quality. Four-eyes in credit analysis and committee packaging still needs manual enforcement or a future A12/A8 enhancement.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk | Compensating control |
|---|---|
| False negatives in KYC/entity classification | Human escalation by default for severe risk; no auto-clearance for High-exposure SME; sample QA; regulator-compliant audit trail |
| False positives causing unnecessary escalations | Tune thresholds by product/exposure; monitor precision/recall; allow specialist override; capacity dashboard for 3 compliance specialists |
| Model drift in A2/A3/A6 | Versioned models; drift monitoring; periodic validation; shadow-mode rollout; manual review of changed cases |
| Automation bias | Require override reason; compare model suggestion vs final decision; audit reports; training that humans remain accountable |
| Four-eyes violation | Role-based access; block same user from credit analysis and committee packaging for High-exposure SME; A12/A8 role-aware controls in later phase |
| KYC-before-signing bypass | Hard system gate in A9/A10; provisional signing only through controlled exception; remediation queue and compliance review |
| Contract-decision mismatch | A9 checks contract terms against committee decision; versioned legal rule library; exception routing; no auto-generation if decision data missing |
| Garbage-in/garbage-out for A8/A9 | Data-quality gates; require KYC status, normalized statements, collateral output, and credit notes before packaging/contract generation |
| Customer friction from A5 | Plain-language checklist; RM override; opt-in reminders; monitor complaint rate and abandonment |
| External valuation dependency | A7 triage and SLA monitoring; dual-sourcing where possible; buffer for Equipment finance; do not automate valuation judgment |
| Resource overload after automation | Capacity dashboards; throttle automation if downstream queues grow; redeploy freed FTE to exceptions and High-exposure judgment |
| Auditability failure | Immutable logs for model input, suggestion, override, final decision; link to case ID and control rule version |

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

First-six-month constraint: only two deep core-banking integrations are allowed. Use A6 and A9 as the two deep integrations. A4 and A5 run through workflow, compliance, CRM, and portal layers.

| Phase | Timing | Modules | Focus | Monitoring metrics | Gate criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 0 | Months 0-2 | Foundation | Process-mining baseline, control taxonomy, audit schema, data contracts, exception RACI | Baseline waits for entity mismatch, missing docs, normalization, contract remediation; audit completeness | Control owners signed off; A6/A9 integration scope frozen; no sequential enforcement of concurrent KYC/financial review |
| Phase 1 | Months 1-4 | A5, A4 | Deploy missing-document nudging and entity-mismatch assistant in controlled cohorts | Missing-doc loop rate; `Request missing documents -> Financial document review`; entity-mismatch wait; specialist escalation volume; customer complaints | A5 reduces repeated missing-doc loops without RM/customer backlash; A4 reduces specialist handling time or wait without false clearance; audit trail complete |
| Phase 2 | Months 3-6 | A6 | Deep integration #1: financial-statement extraction/normalization for standard, low/medium-complexity documents | Normalization accuracy; analyst correction rate; `Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis` wait; credit-analyst utilization | Accuracy acceptable on standard docs; override rate controlled; no increase in credit rework; analysts redeployed to High-exposure judgment |
| Phase 3 | Months 6-9 | A9 | Deep integration #2: contract generation with control-rule checks, assisted mode only | Contract-remediation loops; KYC-before-signing violations; contract-decision mismatch; legal exception rate; audit completeness | Zero KYC bypass; contract-decision match above agreed threshold; rule library versioned; no High-exposure SME or Credit-line renewal auto-generation until controls mature |
| Phase 4 | Months 9-12 | A4, A5, A6, A9 | Scale successful modules; extend A6 to more doc types; extend A9 only after control gates | End-to-end cycle time; wait times by activity; rework; handovers; control violations; resource queues | Stable or improved control metrics; no downstream overload; four-eyes violations addressed manually or queued for A12 in year 2 |

Gate principle: do not scale a downstream module like A9 if upstream KYC, document, or decision data quality is unreliable. A9 may draft and check, but humans remain accountable for legal/compliance decisions.

# 7. Final recommendation

Recommend the balanced bottleneck-control portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A9.

This portfolio prioritizes process dependencies rather than the highest-frequency activity. Auto-registration, A1, is high-frequency and easy, but `Receive application -> Register application` is already digital for 72% of cases and registration wait is only 1.5h. Automating it first would mostly accelerate inflow into KYC, classification, document review, credit analysis, collateral, and legal queues. That is exactly the kind of frequency-only win that can worsen end-to-end performance.

The stronger process leverage is in lower-frequency but blocking activities:
- A4 targets entity mismatch: 1,750 cases, 31h wait, 38% exception rate, and critical path in 82% when present.
- A5 targets missing documents: 3,300 requests, 52% repeated, and the `Financial document review -> Request missing documents -> Financial document review` loop.
- A6 targets normalization: 5,200 cases, 20h wait, 75 min service time, and a prerequisite for `Normalize financial statements -> Credit analysis` and committee packaging.
- A9 adds control strength: contract terms must match committee decision, KYC clearance must precede signing, and audit trails must record model suggestion, override, and final decision. It should be gated to clean, low-risk cases first, with human legal review for exceptions.

The trade-off is deliberate: we sacrifice immediate external valuation automation, KYC hit pre-classification, and quick-win registration automation in year 1. Instead, we reduce the waits and rework that dominate the critical path while adding one compliance-strengthening module. Compliance responsibility remains human. Four-eyes enforcement is not fully solved by this portfolio; if violations persist, A12 or a role-aware A8 should be the first year-2 addition.
