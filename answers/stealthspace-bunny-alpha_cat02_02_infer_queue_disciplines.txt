# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

The diagnoses below identify the **best-supported scheduling family**, not a uniquely identified algorithm. Confidence is confidence in that family, including its necessary exceptions—not confidence that every dispatch decision followed one fixed rule.

### Interpretation and bounded assumptions

- **Arrival timing:** A delayed arrival scan ordinarily means that a job could have entered up to two minutes **before** its logged time. I therefore do not assume that logged arrival order establishes physical queue-entry order within the two-minute uncertainty band. Under this interpretation, A02 might already be waiting at QA’s 08:02 start, A05 at 08:20, and C05 might already be waiting when QC becomes available at 10:10. These boundary cases do not change the main conclusions. If “arrival” instead permits physical entry *after* the logged scan, A08 at 09:05 in QA, D06 at 11:30 in QD, and C07 at 11:40 in QC become conditional eligibility cases; the broad diagnoses still hold.
- **Baseline rules:** Unless explicitly marked otherwise, FIFO means non-preemptive selection of the oldest **unstarted** job; priority means strict P1-before-P2-before-P3; and SPT uses the displayed arrival-time estimate. These are deliberately simple baselines.
- **Protected-resume scheduling:** An in-progress or previously started job can retain dispatch priority when it resumes. This is a hypothesized state-dependent rule, not an assumed documented rule.
- **Appointments:** Only exact appointment times are supplied. I do not invent a tolerance. An appointment-time prediction is conditional on a window being open, or on a documented late-dispatch tolerance. An early or late appointment start can also reflect an undocumented hold or exception.
- **Resources:** For QD, eligibility is evaluated **per resource**, using the skills and availability supplied. Eligibility in the other queues is not altered by assuming unlisted resources.

A useful empirical observation is that the total recorded service of every job equals its `est_min`; for B01, \(14+17+14=45\). This does **not** prove that estimates were accurate or used by dispatch. It does mean that pure SPT based on the displayed estimates and ex-post shortest-realized-duration sorting cannot be separated in this small sample.

- B01 is suspended for **41 calendar minutes**: 15 before B02, 12 during B03, and 14 during B05. Of these, 29 minutes displace B01’s own work. Its three recorded segments still total exactly 45 minutes, so there is **no additional recorded resumption/setup time**; that does not establish that preparation or resumption was cost-free.
- B07’s 26-minute-late appointment start is not evidence for a hard appointment rule. It requires a late window, expiry of appointment eligibility, or an explicit appointment exception.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by their plausibility as explanations of the ambiguous observations:

# 7. Final synthesis

**QD is the most identifiable at the feasibility level**: unique qualifications, two competing resources, fixed availability windows, and an honored appointment jointly constrain the rule. That does not identify its local tie-break. **QB is close behind**, with a particularly clear repeated protected-resume pattern. **QC is the least identifiable**, because one short, completed backlog combines SPT-looking choices with age, priority, and already-unrecoverable deadline exceptions.

The most dangerous inference would be to label a queue **“FIFO” merely from much of its start order and then operationalize that label as a pure rule**. FIFO does not explain QB’s explicit preemptions, QA’s appointment behavior and P1 bypasses, QD’s skill constraints, or QC’s deadline trade-offs. The defensible conclusion is a set of **stateful, constraint-aware hybrid rules**, with priority, appointment, skill, and SLA states recorded separately rather than collapsed into an unsupported single-mechanism claim.
