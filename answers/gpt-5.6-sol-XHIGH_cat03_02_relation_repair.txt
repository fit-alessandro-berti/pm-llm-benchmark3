## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

I use the following classifications:

- **Hard invariants**
  - Every E2O endpoint must reference an existing event and object; every O2O endpoint must reference existing objects.
  - Exact duplicate relations should not be introduced.
  - O2O direction and qualifier semantics must be preserved: `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, not the reverse.
  - No new events or objects are created.

- **Strong defaults**
  - Activity-specific cardinalities from the domain semantics: one item and normally one package for `Pack Item`; one package and normally one delivery for `Dispatch Package`; one payment object and normally one invoice for `Record Payment`.
  - Lifecycle order should be temporally plausible: packing before dispatch, dispatch before confirmation, invoice before payment.
  - A normalized near-exact operational identifier match is strong evidence. Thus `PK8-2` strongly favors `PK-8`, and `N-421?` strongly favors carrier reference `N-421`.
  - Existing O2O structure is useful when it predates the repair, but an O2O edge inferred from a proposed E2O edge is not independent confirmation.

- **Weak heuristics**
  - Same timestamp, route, customer, amount, identifier prefix, and lifecycle completion.
  - `N-42*` matches both `N-420` and `N-421`, so it is not a unique identifier.
  - `INV-44.total = 230`, `ORD-A + ORD-B = 230`, and `PAY-1 + PAY-2 = 230` support consolidation, but fees, taxes, discounts, partial payments, and unapplied cash prevent arithmetic from proving any relation.
  - NORTH is not discriminative because both deliveries and both packages use that route.

- **Evidence against a repair**
  - Violating normal cardinality without explicit bulk-event evidence.
  - Selecting one candidate where another has an equally compatible identifier.
  - Introducing a mixed-order package, consolidated invoice, or multi-delivery confirmation solely to make lifecycles complete.
  - Assigning an object despite a better identifier match elsewhere.
  - Treating two relations derived from the same assumption as independent evidence.

Evidence is combined abductively rather than by a mechanical score. Independent signals—such as identifier match plus correct temporal position plus compatible cardinality—receive more weight than correlated signals. Confidence values below are calibrated judgments, not statistical probabilities.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair**

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Add `ev08  PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. The scanner reference, timing, and missing package cardinality jointly make this the strongest missing relation.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Add `ev10  DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. Do not add DEL-X or PK-7 to ev10.
- **ev11 and deliveries:** Add no delivery relation. Preserve DEL-X, DEL-Y, and the joint-confirmation hypothesis as unresolved.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Leave unresolved. The consolidated-invoice explanation is plausible but depends heavily on customer and arithmetic.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Leave unresolved. PAY-2 remains plausible unapplied cash.

This bundle gives PK-8 and DEL-Y coherent logistics lifecycles while allowing finance and delivery confirmation to remain incomplete. No event is orphaned, and incomplete lifecycles are not treated as errors by themselves.

**Bundle B: consolidated customer fulfillment and finance**

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Make the same PK-8 additions as Bundle A, accepting that PK-8 consolidates items from two orders.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Link ev10 to DEL-Y only, with `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. DEL-X remains associated with ev09 and PK-7.
- **ev11 and deliveries:** Link the single portal acknowledgement to both DEL-X and DEL-Y. This treats `N-42*` as an aggregate customer confirmation rather than a damaged unique reference.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Add ORD-B to ev12 and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, treating the invoice as customer-level consolidation.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Add INV-44 to ev14 and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, treating PAY-1 and PAY-2 as two settlements of one invoice.

Bundle B yields a coherent end-to-end interpretation: two orders, one mixed-order package, two deliveries jointly acknowledged, one consolidated invoice, and two payments. Its coherence is not proof; several edges rely on correlated amount/customer evidence, and the aggregate ev11 interpretation can duplicate one physical acknowledgement in delivery projections.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

- **Order:** ORD-A has create, item-addition, confirmation, and invoice activities. ORD-B has create and item-addition activities; its item is packed under Bundle A, but no invoice or confirmation is asserted. ORD-B is incomplete rather than orphaned. No direct Order-to-Package relation is invented.
- **Item:** IT-A1 and IT-A2 already have add and pack events. IT-B1 has an add event and ev08; Bundle A supplies the missing package. PK-8 consequently packs two items from different orders. That is suspicious but not prohibited.
- **Package:** PK-7 has packing and dispatch evidence and is carried by DEL-X. PK-8 has two packing events after repair—ev07 and ev08—followed by ev10. The package trace is plausible, though mixed-order consolidation should be flagged.
- **Delivery:** DEL-X is independently connected to ev09 and PK-7. DEL-Y gains dispatch and carriage relations under Bundle A but no confirmation. Lack of a confirmation is not an orphan condition. ev11 remains a valid order-related event because it already relates to ORD-A.
- **Invoice:** INV-44 has issue activity, bills ORD-A, and is related to PAY-1. Its total being larger than ORD-A’s declared value is suspicious but can reflect non-order charges. Bundle A does not force ORD-B into the invoice.
- **Payment:** PAY-1 has both event and invoice relations. PAY-2 has its own record event but no invoice; it is a plausible unapplied payment. Neither PAY-2 nor ev14 is orphaned.

The new PK-8/IT-B1 and DEL-Y/PK-8 E2O/O2O pairs are **not independent confirmations**. Each pair is derived from the same operational hypothesis. Treating the inferred O2O edge as proof of the E2O edge would be circular reasoning. By contrast, the pre-existing `DEL-X carries PK-7` and ev09 co-occurrence are two recorded structures, although their true source independence is unknown.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**OC-DFG.** In the unrepaired Package projection, PK-8 has `Pack Item(ev07)  Dispatch Package(ev10)`. Bundle A inserts ev08 into that package lifecycle, producing `Pack Item(ev07)  Pack Item(ev08)  Dispatch Package(ev10)`. The PK-8 contribution to the direct `Pack Item  Dispatch Package` edge is replaced by `Pack Item  Pack Item` and a later `Pack Item  Dispatch Package`; PK-7 still contributes the latter edge.

Bundle A adds a second Delivery object participating in `Dispatch Package`, but creates no delivery directly-follows edge because neither delivery is linked to ev11. Bundle B creates two `Dispatch Package  Confirm Delivery` contributions, one for DEL-X and one for DEL-Y, even though both use the same confirmation event. Bundle B also creates `Add Item  Issue Invoice` for ORD-B and, on INV-44, `Issue Invoice  Record Payment  Record Payment`.

**OTG.** Bundle A strengthens Package–Item and Delivery–Package connectivity. Those type pairs already exist, so an unweighted OTG may not change topologically, while weighted counts may increase. Bundle B strengthens Invoice–Order and Payment–Invoice and may introduce Order–Delivery co-occurrence through ev11, depending on whether the PM4Py version derives the OTG from event co-occurrence, O2O edges, or both.

**ET–OT graph.** Bundle A raises `Pack Item–Package` participation from two package associations to three and `Dispatch Package–Delivery` from one to two. Bundle B additionally introduces `Confirm Delivery–Delivery`, increases the order count for `Issue Invoice`, and increases invoice participation in `Record Payment`.

**Flattenings.**

- **Order:** Bundle A leaves the observed order traces unchanged. Bundle B adds ev12 to ORD-B, giving approximately `Create Order  Add Item  Issue Invoice`. ev12 then appears in both order cases.
- **Package:** Bundle A and B insert ev08 into PK-8’s case, creating consecutive pack activities.
- **Delivery:** Unrepaired, only DEL-X has a dispatch event. Bundle A gives both deliveries one dispatch event. Bundle B gives each delivery a dispatch-confirm sequence; ev11 is duplicated across two flattened cases.
- **Invoice:** Unrepaired and Bundle A give INV-44 `Issue Invoice  Record Payment(ev13)`. Bundle B adds ev14, producing a second consecutive payment.
- **Payment:** Each payment already has its own `Record Payment` event. Adding an invoice relation to ev14 does not change Payment flattening unless a separate enrichment or propagation step is applied.

Global event-based activity frequencies do not change because no events are added. Relation- or object-based frequencies do change: ev08 and ev10 each gain an object under Bundle A. Under Bundle B, one confirmation event covers two delivery objects and one invoice event covers two order objects. Consequently, “one event,” “two unique participating objects,” and “two total object-event associations” are different counts.

For equivalent-execution clustering around Order, Bundle A leaves the existing contrast between ORD-A’s long trace and ORD-B’s short trace. Bundle B moves ORD-B into a billed-order pattern, but it still lacks ORD-A’s confirmation and second item-addition. Package and delivery activities should not silently enter Order traces through transitive O2O propagation.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Preserve the source data before any repair.
events_original = events.copy(deep=True)
objects_original = objects.copy(deep=True)
relations_original = relations.copy(deep=True)
o2o_original = o2o.copy(deep=True)

events_r = events_original.copy(deep=True)
objects_r = objects_original.copy(deep=True)
relations_r = relations_original.copy(deep=True)
o2o_r = o2o_original.copy(deep=True)

EID, OID, OID2 = "ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2"
ACT, TS, TYPE, QUAL = (
    "ocel:activity", "ocel:timestamp", "ocel:type", "ocel:qualifier"
)

# Bundle A only.
e2o_additions = [
    {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "package"},
    {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "delivery"},
]
o2o_additions = [
    {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
    {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
]

event_index = events_r.set_index(EID)
object_index = objects_r.set_index(OID)

def make_relation_row(spec):
    # Preserve any implementation-specific columns already present in relations.
    row = {column: pd.NA for column in relations_r.columns}
    row[EID] = spec[EID]
    row[OID] = spec[OID]
    if QUAL in row:
        row[QUAL] = spec[QUAL]
    if ACT in row:
        row[ACT] = event_index.at[spec[EID], ACT]
    if TS in row:
        row[TS] = event_index.at[spec[EID], TS]
    if TYPE in row:
        row[TYPE] = object_index.at[spec[OID], TYPE]
    return row

# Append only relations not already present exactly.
existing_e2o = set(
    relations_r[[EID, OID, QUAL]].itertuples(index=False, name=None)
)
new_relation_rows = [
    make_relation_row(spec)
    for spec in e2o_additions
    if (spec[EID], spec[OID], spec[QUAL]) not in existing_e2o
]
if new_relation_rows:
    relations_r = pd.concat(
        [relations_r, pd.DataFrame(new_relation_rows, columns=relations_r.columns)],
        ignore_index=True
    )

existing_o2o = set(
    o2o_r[[OID, OID2, QUAL]].itertuples(index=False, name=None)
)
new_o2o_rows = [
    spec for spec in o2o_additions
    if (spec[OID], spec[OID2], spec[QUAL]) not in existing_o2o
]
if new_o2o_rows:
    o2o_r = pd.concat([o2o_r, pd.DataFrame(new_o2o_rows)], ignore_index=True)

# Referential integrity.
assert events_r[EID].is_unique, "Duplicate event identifiers"
assert objects_r[OID].is_unique, "Duplicate object identifiers"
assert set(relations_r[EID]).issubset(set(events_r[EID]))
assert set(relations_r[OID]).issubset(set(objects_r[OID]))
assert set(o2o_r[OID]).issubset(set(objects_r[OID]))
assert set(o2o_r[OID2]).issubset(set(objects_r[OID]))

# Exact duplicates and suspicious multiple qualifiers for the same endpoint pair.
assert not relations_r.duplicated([EID, OID, QUAL]).any()
assert not o2o_r.duplicated([OID, OID2, QUAL]).any()

e2o_pair_collisions = relations_r[
    relations_r.duplicated([EID, OID], keep=False)
].sort_values([EID, OID])
o2o_pair_collisions = o2o_r[
    o2o_r.duplicated([OID, OID2], keep=False)
].sort_values([OID, OID2])
print("E2O endpoint-pair collisions:\n", e2o_pair_collisions)
print("O2O endpoint-pair collisions:\n", o2o_pair_collisions)

# VERSION-DEPENDENT CONSTRUCTOR LINE:
# Verify the OCEL constructor signature for the installed PM4Py release.
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL
try:
    repaired_ocel = OCEL(
        events=events_r,
        objects=objects_r,
        relations=relations_r,
        o2o=o2o_r
    )
except TypeError:
    # Fallback for versions where o2o is assigned after construction.
    repaired_ocel = OCEL(
        events=events_r,
        objects=objects_r,
        relations=relations_r
    )
    repaired_ocel.o2o = o2o_r

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(repaired_ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(repaired_ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(repaired_ocel)

order_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(repaired_ocel, "Order")
delivery_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(repaired_ocel, "Delivery")

# Deliberately do not call ocel_o2o_enrichment:
# enrichment can propagate recorded relations but cannot validate business semantics.
print(len(order_flat), len(delivery_flat))
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Assigning ev11 uniquely to DEL-X because ORD-A is observed ignores that ORD-A also has an item in PK-8 and therefore evidence toward DEL-Y.
2. Assigning ev11 to both deliveries can turn one portal acknowledgement into two delivery-case confirmations and double the `Dispatch  Confirm` count.
3. Adding ORD-B to INV-44 solely because `130 + 100 = 230` creates a false consolidated invoice and a new order-level `Add Item  Issue Invoice` path.
4. Linking PAY-2 to INV-44 solely because it equals the apparent remaining balance can erase genuine unapplied cash and create a false `Record Payment  Record Payment` edge.
5. Linking ev10 to both deliveries because ev09 has the same timestamp can manufacture a bulk dispatch and duplicate delivery participation.
6. Adding IT-B1 to PK-8 makes PK-8 a mixed-order package. Although plausible, it could be wrong if `PK8-2` denotes a scanner station rather than the package.
7. Transitively propagating package and delivery events to orders through O2O edges would create order activities that were never recorded as E2O relations.
8. Treating inferred E2O and O2O pairs as mutual confirmation would inflate confidence through circular reasoning.
9. Reversing directional O2O relations—for example storing `PK-8 carries DEL-Y`—would corrupt the OTG even if the underlying object pairing were correct.
