### Why Bidirectional DFG Edges Do Not Prove Concurrency
In a Directly-Follows Graph (DFG), observing both $(A, B) > 0$ and $(B, A) > 0$ only demonstrates that across the event log, $B$ immediately succeeded $A$ in some trace(s) and $A$ immediately succeeded $B$ in some trace(s). It cannot distinguish between three fundamentally different generative mechanisms:
1. **True Concurrency ($A \parallel B$):** $A$ and $B$ execute in parallel with overlapping durations; their completion timestamps interleave depending on stochastic task durations or tie-breaking (S7).
2. **Mutually Exclusive Routing ($X(\rightarrow(A, B), \rightarrow(B, A))$):** $A$ and $B$ never overlap in time, but Case Type 1 strictly executes $A$ then $B$, while Case Type 2 strictly executes $B$ then $A$.
3. **Iteration / Short Loops:** A single trace executes $\langle \dots, A, B, A, \dots \rangle$, creating both $(A, B)$ and $(B, A)$ edges without concurrency or case-type branching.

Only by examining full trace variants (ruling out loops because all activities have cardinality $\le 1$ per trace) and incorporating domain knowledge (S2, D7, S7) can we abductively infer concurrency over sequential XOR branching.

# 2. Shared Assumptions

Across all three candidate models (`model_conservative`, `model_balanced`, `model_permissive`), we establish the following foundational assumptions:

1. **Point-Event Completion Semantics:** Because only completion timestamps exist (D7, S7), a model transition firing represents the *completion* of an activity. A causal edge $A \rightarrow B$ in our models strictly means "$B$ cannot *complete* until $A$ has *completed*." It does not strictly mean $B$ could not have *started* while $A$ was ongoing, except where domain rules (e.g., D2, D6) assert true enabling dependencies.
2. **Abstraction of the Missing Case Attribute (`external` vs. `stocked`):** Because the component-type attribute was not exported (S5), deterministic data-dependent routing (S3, S4) cannot be modeled as a guard condition. It is abstracted into a non-deterministic control-flow choice `X(Supplier Confirmation, tau)`.
3. **Conflation of True Optionality and Data Loss:** Of the 30 traces, 7 ($23.3\%$) lack `Supplier Confirmation` (V3, V4). Given S5 (5–10% missingness due to integration drops, corresponding to $\approx 1.5\text{--}3$ traces), roughly 4 to 5 traces represent legitimate stocked-component skips (S4) and 1 to 3 traces represent missing data. Because our models must replay the observed log without blocking on corrupted traces, the silent skip transition $\tau_{\text{skip\_SC}}$ intentionally absorbs both legitimate business skips and integration drop errors.
4. **Loop-Free Execution:** Every activity appears at most once per trace across all 30 cases, and D6 explicitly bounds `Expedite Review` to at most once. All three models are strictly acyclic (no `*` loop operators).
5. **Fixed Prefix and Suffix:** Based on D1, D2, and 100% variant support, every trace begins with `Register Order` and concludes with the strict sequence $\langle \text{Approve Configuration}, \text{Release Production}, \text{Archive Case} \rangle$. All structural variation is confined to the middle segment comprising `Credit Check` (CC), `Engineering Review` (ER), `Supplier Confirmation` (SC), and `Expedite Review` (ExR).

# 3. Conservative Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement
The conservative candidate (`model_conservative`) applies a strict **empirical-support bias**: it prioritizes precision and refusal of unsupported behavior over structural compactness or broad generalization. Because completion timestamps cannot prove true concurrency (D7) and D5 has low-to-medium confidence, this model refuses to generalize unseen interleavings (such as `<ER, SC, CC>` or `<CC, ER, ExR>` without `SC`). Instead, it embeds the shared prefix (`Register Order`) and suffix (`AC -> RP -> ArC`) while enumerating the 7 observed middle execution sequences via an exclusive choice (`X`). To achieve 1.0 precision on the observed support, it trades away structural simplicity by using duplicate activity labels across the XOR branches—a deliberate modeling choice that prevents state-space cross-bleeding at the cost of scalability if new variants emerge.

### Compact Formal Expression
$$\rightarrow\big(\text{RO},\; \times\big(\rightarrow(\text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}),\; \rightarrow(\text{ER}, \text{CC}, \text{SC}),\; \rightarrow(\text{CC}, \text{ER}),\; \rightarrow(\text{ER}, \text{CC}),\; \rightarrow(\text{CC}, \text{SC}, \text{ER}),\; \rightarrow(\text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}, \text{ExR}),\; \rightarrow(\text{ER}, \text{CC}, \text{ExR}, \text{SC})\big),\; \text{AC},\; \text{RP},\; \text{ArC}\big)$$

### Self-Contained PM4Py Code (`model_conservative`)
```python
import pm4py

# Conservative Candidate: ProcessTree with factored prefix/suffix and XOR over observed middle sequences
tree_str_conservative = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "X( "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation' ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check' ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review' ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review' ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' ) "
    "), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)

model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str_conservative)
```

*Note on Duplicate Labels and Scalability:* In `model_conservative`, activities like `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` are instantiated multiple times across the branches of the `X` operator. While this guarantees that the converted Petri net stays 1-safe, sound, and strictly bound to the 7 observed trace variants, label duplication scales poorly: for $k$ concurrent activities with optional elements, enumerating valid permutations requires $O(k!)$ branches, fragmenting transition frequencies during bottleneck analysis and requiring model edits whenever a harmless new interleaving occurs.

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded
- **Expedite Review without Supplier Confirmation:** Traces where `SC` is skipped (stocked item) *and* `ExR` is executed (e.g., `<RO, CC, ER, ExR, AC, RP, ArC>`).
- **Unobserved valid interleavings of `ExR` and `SC`:** `<RO, CC, ER, ExR, SC, AC, RP, ArC>` (where `CC` precedes `ER`, followed by `ExR` then `SC`) and `<RO, ER, CC, SC, ExR, AC, RP, ArC>`.
- **Early `SC` followed by `ExR`:** `<RO, CC, SC, ER, ExR, AC, RP, ArC>`.
- **Symmetric early `SC` after `ER`:** `<RO, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, ArC>` (suggested by D5, but 0 occurrences in the log).

### Known Underfitting Risks
- **Overfitting (Inverse Underfitting) to Sample Size ($N=30$):** With only 2 observed cases of `Expedite Review` (V6, V7), the absence of `<CC, ER, ExR, SC>` is almost certainly a small-sample artifact rather than a business prohibition (violating S6's symmetry).
- **Brittle Compliance Auditing:** If a stocked-component order (V3/V4) ever requires an `Expedite Review`, `model_conservative` will flag it as non-conformant simply because the joint event $(\neg\text{SC} \land \text{ExR})$ was not observed in the initial 30 cases ($P(\neg\text{SC}) \approx 0.23, P(\text{ExR}) \approx 0.067 \implies E[\text{count}] \approx 0.46$ cases in 30).

# 4. Balanced Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement
The balanced candidate (`model_balanced`) applies a **structural partial-order bias** using a Partially Ordered Workflow Language (POWL) model. It reconciles stakeholder statements (S2, S6) with empirical variant frequencies by distinguishing well-supported concurrency from asymmetric causal preconditions. Specifically, rather than forcing a block-structured tree (which must either forbid V5 `<CC, SC, ER>` or over-generalize to allow `<ER, SC, CC>`), POWL allows us to specify exact pairwise ordering constraints. It generalizes across unseen combinations of parallel activities (`CC || ER` and `SC || ExR`) while preserving every precondition empirically supported by the log (`CC -> SC`, `{CC, ER} -> ExR`).

### Decision Logic: Sequence vs. Concurrency vs. Partial Order
1. **`Credit Check` (CC) and `Engineering Review` (ER) $\rightarrow$ Concurrency (Unordered in POWL):** V1 ($12\times$) and V2 ($7\times$) show frequent completion in both orders ($19\times$ `CC` before `ER`, $11\times$ `ER` before `CC`). Combined with S2 (checks run in parallel) and D7/S7 (completion timestamps only), we reject S1 and model `CC` and `ER` as concurrent nodes with no ordering edge between them.
2. **`Supplier Confirmation` (SC) relative to `CC` and `ER` $\rightarrow$ Asymmetric Partial Order (`CC -> SC`, `ER || SC`):** In all 23 traces containing `SC` (V1, V2, V5, V6, V7), `CC` *always* completes before `SC` ($23/23 = 100\%$). Conversely, V5 ($2\times$) proves `SC` can complete *before* `ER`. While D5 speculates `SC` might start after *either* check, there is zero empirical evidence of `SC` completing before `CC`. A block-structured ProcessTree cannot represent the "N-pattern" / crown dependency (`CC -> SC`, `CC || ER`, `ER || SC`) without label duplication. In POWL, we simply add the edge `CC -> X(SC, tau)` and leave `ER` and `X(SC, tau)` unordered. This admits V1, V2, and V5 while excluding the unsupported `<ER, SC, CC>` ordering!
3. **`Expedite Review` (ExR) relative to `CC`, `ER`, and `SC` $\rightarrow$ Partial Order Synchronization (`{CC, ER} -> ExR`, `ExR || SC`):** Per D6, S6, V6, and V7, `ExR` requires both `CC` and `ER` to complete first (`CC -> X(ExR, tau)` and `ER -> X(ExR, tau)`). Once both checks are complete, V6 (`SC -> ExR`) and V7 (`ExR -> SC`) plus S6 confirm that `ExR` and `SC` can occur in either order, so no edge connects `X(SC, tau)` and `X(ExR, tau)`.

### Compact Formal Expression
Let $\text{SC}_{\text{opt}} = \times(\text{SC}, \tau)$ and $\text{ExR}_{\text{opt}} = \times(\text{ExR}, \tau)$. Define the Strict Partial Order $\mathcal{P} = (V, \prec)$ over nodes $V = \{\text{RO}, \text{CC}, \text{ER}, \text{SC}_{\text{opt}}, \text{ExR}_{\text{opt}}, \text{AC}, \text{RP}, \text{ArC}\}$ with transitive reduction edges:
$$\prec \;=\; \big\{ (\text{RO}, \text{CC}),\; (\text{RO}, \text{ER}),\; (\text{CC}, \text{SC}_{\text{opt}}),\; (\text{CC}, \text{ExR}_{\text{opt}}),\; (\text{ER}, \text{ExR}_{\text{opt}}),\; (\text{SC}_{\text{opt}}, \text{AC}),\; (\text{ExR}_{\text{opt}}, \text{AC}),\; (\text{ER}, \text{AC}),\; (\text{AC}, \text{RP}),\; (\text{RP}, \text{ArC}) \big\}$$

### Self-Contained PM4Py Code (`model_balanced`)
```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Atomic transitions
t_ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
t_cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
t_er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
t_sc = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation")
t_exr = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
t_ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
t_rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
t_arc = Transition(label="Archive Case")

# Optional activities modeled via XOR with SilentTransition
opt_sc = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [t_sc, SilentTransition()])
opt_exr = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [t_exr, SilentTransition()])

# Construct StrictPartialOrder
model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[t_ro, t_cc, t_er, opt_sc, opt_exr, t_ac, t_rp, t_arc]
)

# Start boundaries
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, opt_sc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, opt_exr)

# Middle partial-order constraints:
# 1. Supplier Confirmation requires Credit Check, but can interleave with Engineering Review (V5)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_sc)

# 2. Expedite Review requires BOTH Credit Check and Engineering Review (D6, S6)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_exr)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, opt_exr)

# End synchronization boundaries before Approve Configuration (D2, D3, D4, D6)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_sc, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_exr, t_ac)

# Strict suffix (D1, D2)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_arc)
```

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded
- **`SC` completing before `CC`:** e.g., `<RO, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, ArC>` or `<RO, SC, CC, ER, AC, RP, ArC>`.
- **`ExR` completing before `CC` or `ER`:** e.g., `<RO, CC, ExR, ER, SC, AC, RP, ArC>` (violates D6/S6).
- **Any activity occurring after `AC` other than `RP -> ArC`:** (violates D1, D2, D3, D4, D6).

### Known Underfitting Risks
- **Refusal of D5's Engineering-Triggered `SC`:** If D5 holds in rare cases where `Engineering Review` alone provides enough information to trigger and complete `Supplier Confirmation` before a slow `Credit Check` finishes (`<RO, ER, SC, CC, ...>`), `model_balanced` will reject those traces because of the edge `t_cc -> opt_sc`.

# 5. Permissive Candidate

### Inductive-Bias Statement
The permissive candidate (`model_permissive`) applies a **broad domain-relaxation bias** using a compact, block-structured `ProcessTree`. It prioritizes structural simplicity and tolerance for unseen interleavings (especially D5 and S7 timestamp jitter) over strict precondition enforcement between middle activities. By placing the optional `Supplier Confirmation` in a parallel block (`+`) alongside the `Credit Check || Engineering Review` block and the subsequent optional `Expedite Review`, it accommodates D5 (allowing `SC` to complete after `ER` alone, or even concurrently with both checks) without duplicating labels.

### Boundary Between "Reasonable Generalization" and Forbidden Behavior
- **Reasonable Generalization (Admitted):** Any interleaving of `Supplier Confirmation` with `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, and `Expedite Review` within the middle verification phase, as well as independent combinations of skipping `SC` and executing `ExR`. This reflects the view that `SC` is an external procurement handshake whose completion timestamp may arrive at any point during order verification (D5, S5, S7), while `ExR` strictly follows the two internal checks (`+(CC, ER) -> X(ExR, tau)`).
- **Forbidden Behavior (Excluded):** Any violation of high-confidence phase boundaries and core governance rules:
  1. Starting with anything other than `Register Order` (D1).
  2. Executing `Expedite Review` before *both* `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` have completed (D6, S6).
  3. Executing `Approve Configuration` before `Credit Check`, `Engineering Review`, and any active `Supplier Confirmation` or `Expedite Review` have completed (D3, D4, D6).
  4. Violating the strict post-approval sequence `Approve Configuration -> Release Production -> Archive Case` (D1, D2).
  5. Repeating any activity more than once (D6).

### Compact Formal Expression
$$\rightarrow\Big(\text{RO},\; +\big(\rightarrow(+(\text{CC}, \text{ER}),\; \times(\text{ExR}, \tau)),\; \times(\text{SC}, \tau)\big),\; \text{AC},\; \text{RP},\; \text{ArC}\Big)$$

### Self-Contained PM4Py Code (`model_permissive`)
```python
import pm4py

# Permissive Candidate: Block-structured ProcessTree relaxing SC ordering while preserving ExR preconditions
tree_str_permissive = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "+( "
    "->( +( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ), "
    "X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ) "
    "), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)

model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str_permissive)
```

### Behavior Deliberately Excluded
- **Premature `Expedite Review`:** `<RO, CC, ExR, ER, ...>` or `<RO, ER, ExR, CC, ...>` (forbidden by the inner sequence `->( +(CC, ER), X(ExR, tau) )`).
- **Out-of-phase Approvals or Releases:** `<RO, CC, ER, AC, SC, RP, ArC>` or `<RO, CC, ER, RP, AC, ArC>`.
- **Missing Principal Checks:** Skipping `Credit Check` or `Engineering Review`.

### Known Underfitting Risks
- **Minimal Underfitting Within Domain Scope:** Because `model_permissive` enforces only the highest-confidence causal barriers (`RO` first, `{CC, ER} -> ExR`, all checks before `AC -> RP -> ArC`), it would only underfit if:
  1. `Expedite Review` were executed *before* one of the principal checks in an emergency override (violating D6/S6), or
  2. `Credit Check` or `Engineering Review` suffered from logging drop errors similar to `Supplier Confirmation` (unmentioned in S5).

     - *Earliest Violated Constraint:* At `SC` (position 3). In `model_conservative`, after `<RO, ER>`, the next activity must be `Credit Check` (branches V2, V4, V7).

2. **Balanced Candidate (`model_balanced`):**
   - **Excluded Trace 1:** `<RO, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, ArC>`
     - *Earliest Violated Constraint:* At `SC` (position 3). Violates the POWL partial-order edge `Credit Check -> opt_sc`, which requires `Credit Check` to complete before `Supplier Confirmation`.
   - **Excluded Trace 2:** `<RO, CC, ExR, ER, SC, AC, RP, ArC>`
     - *Earliest Violated Constraint:* At `ExR` (position 3). Violates the POWL partial-order edge `Engineering Review -> opt_exr` (D6/S6: `Expedite Review` requires *both* principal checks to complete first).

3. **Permissive Candidate (`model_permissive`):**
   - **Excluded Trace 1:** `<RO, CC, ExR, ER, SC, AC, RP, ArC>`
     - *Earliest Violated Constraint:* At `ExR` (position 3). Violates the inner sequence `->( +( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) )`, which blocks `ExR` until both `CC` and `ER` have completed.
   - **Excluded Trace 2:** `<RO, CC, ER, AC, SC, RP, ArC>`
     - *Earliest Violated Constraint:* At `AC` (position 4, when `SC` is subsequently fired at position 5). Violates the outer sequence `->( ..., 'Approve Configuration', ... )`, which requires the entire parallel verification block (including `SC` if not skipped) to terminate before `Approve Configuration` can fire.

# 8. Validation Code

The following self-contained Python script reconstructs the 30-case event log, builds all three models, converts them to accepting Petri nets, checks soundness, computes conformance metrics (fitness, precision, generalization, simplicity), runs bounded/sampled playout, and verifies the positive/negative trace audit suite.

*Methodological Note on Playout and Language Inclusion:* While our three models are strictly acyclic (making their theoretical trace languages finite: $|L_{\text{cons}}| = 7$, $|L_{\text{bal}}| = 10$, $|L_{\text{perm}}| = 16$), standard `pm4py.play_out` uses stochastic sampling and cannot exhaustively compare infinite languages or guarantee full coverage of large finite languages without explicit state-space enumeration. Therefore, alongside sampled playout, the script uses **alignment-based trace conformance (`fitness_alignments`)** to deterministically verify language inclusion/exclusion for our positive and negative test suite.

```python
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import Event, EventLog, Trace
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    OperatorPOWL,
    SilentTransition,
    StrictPartialOrder,
    Transition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# =====================================================================
# 1. Reconstruct the 30-case Event Log using parse_event_log_string
# =====================================================================
variants_spec = [
    (
        12,
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ),
    (
        7,
        "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ),
    (
        4,
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ),
    (
        3,
        "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ),
    (
        2,
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ),
    (
        1,
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ),
    (
        1,
        "Register Order, Engineering Review, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
    ),
]

trace_strings = []
for count, trace_str in variants_spec:
    trace_strings.extend([trace_str] * count)

log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string(trace_strings)
print(f"Loaded Event Log with {len(log)} traces.")

# Verify DFG matches problem specification
dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
print(f"Start activities: {start_acts}")
print(f"End activities:   {end_acts}\n")

# =====================================================================
# 2. Construct the Three Candidate Models
# =====================================================================

# --- 2A. Conservative Candidate (ProcessTree) ---
tree_str_conservative = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "X( "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation' ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check' ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Engineering Review' ), "
    "->( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review', 'Supplier Confirmation', 'Expedite Review' ), "
    "->( 'Engineering Review', 'Credit Check', 'Expedite Review', 'Supplier Confirmation' ) "
    "), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)
model_conservative = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str_conservative)

# --- 2B. Balanced Candidate (POWL) ---
t_ro = Transition(label="Register Order")
t_cc = Transition(label="Credit Check")
t_er = Transition(label="Engineering Review")
t_sc = Transition(label="Supplier Confirmation")
t_exr = Transition(label="Expedite Review")
t_ac = Transition(label="Approve Configuration")
t_rp = Transition(label="Release Production")
t_arc = Transition(label="Archive Case")

opt_sc = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [t_sc, SilentTransition()])
opt_exr = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [t_exr, SilentTransition()])

model_balanced = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[t_ro, t_cc, t_er, opt_sc, opt_exr, t_ac, t_rp, t_arc]
)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_cc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, t_er)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, opt_sc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ro, opt_exr)

model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_sc)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, opt_exr)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, opt_exr)

model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_cc, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_er, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_sc, t_ac)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(opt_exr, t_ac)

model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_ac, t_rp)
model_balanced.order.add_edge(t_rp, t_arc)

# --- 2C. Permissive Candidate (ProcessTree) ---
tree_str_permissive = (
    "->( 'Register Order', "
    "+( "
    "->( +( 'Credit Check', 'Engineering Review' ), X( 'Expedite Review', tau ) ), "
    "X( 'Supplier Confirmation', tau ) "
    "), "
    "'Approve Configuration', 'Release Production', 'Archive Case' )"
)
model_permissive = pm4py.parse_process_tree(tree_str_permissive)

candidates = {
    "Conservative": model_conservative,
    "Balanced": model_balanced,
    "Permissive": model_permissive,
}

# =====================================================================
# 3 & 4. Convert to Petri Nets, Check Soundness, and Compute Metrics
# =====================================================================
nets = {}
print("=" * 75)
print(
    f"{'Model':<15} | {'Sound?':<7} | {'Fitness':<8} | {'Precision':<9} | {'Generaliz.':<10} | {'Simplicity':<10}"
)
print("-" * 75)

for name, model in candidates.items():
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    nets[name] = (net, im, fm)

    is_sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=False)
    fit_res = pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    fitness_val = fit_res["average_trace_fitness"]

    prec_val = pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm)
    gen_val = pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm)
    simp_val = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)

    print(
        f"{name:<15} | {str(is_sound):<7} | {fitness_val:<8.4f} | {prec_val:<9.4f} | {gen_val:<10.4f} | {simp_val:<10.4f}"
    )
print("=" * 75 + "\n")

# =====================================================================
# 5. Bounded / Sampled Playout Discovery
# =====================================================================
# Limitation Note: Sampled playout draws stochastic traces from the net and
# does not prove exhaustive language coverage (especially for large or cyclic models).
observed_set = set(trace_strings)

for name, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
    playout_log = pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm)
    sampled_variants = set()
    for trace in playout_log:
        t_str = ", ".join([event["concept:name"] for event in trace])
        sampled_variants.add(t_str)
    unobserved_sampled = sampled_variants - observed_set
    print(
        f"[{name}] Sampled playout generated {len(sampled_variants)} distinct variants "
        f"({len(unobserved_sampled)} unobserved in original log)."
    )
print()


# =====================================================================
# 6. Deterministic Positive / Negative Trace Suite Check via Alignments
# =====================================================================
def is_trace_accepted(trace_str, net, im, fm):
    """Deterministically checks if a single trace has 1.0 alignment fitness."""
    single_log = pm4py.parse_event_log_string([trace_str])
    res = pm4py.fitness_alignments(single_log, net, im, fm)
    return abs(res["average_trace_fitness"] - 1.0) < 1e-6


test_suite = [
    # Observed baseline trace (V1) -> All should accept
    (
        "T1_Observed_V1",
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",

    (

    ),
    # Unobserved: Early SC after CC, plus ExR after ER -> Balanced & Permissive accept, Conservative rejects
    (
        "T4_Unobs_EarlySC_with_ExR",
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Supplier Confirmation, Engineering Review, Expedite Review, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
        {"Conservative": False, "Balanced": True, "Permissive": True},
    ),
    # Unobserved: D5 hypothesis (ER -> SC -> CC) -> Only Permissive accepts
    (
        "T5_Unobs_D5_ER_SC_CC",
        "Register Order, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Credit Check, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
        {"Conservative": False, "Balanced": False, "Permissive": True},
    ),
    # Negative 1: Premature ExR before ER completes (violates D6/S6) -> All must reject
    (
        "T6_Neg_Premature_ExR",
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Expedite Review, Engineering Review, Supplier Confirmation, Approve Configuration, Release Production, Archive Case",
        {"Conservative": False, "Balanced": False, "Permissive": False},
    ),
    # Negative 2: Late SC after Approve Configuration (violates D4) -> All must reject
    (
        "T7_Neg_Late_SC_after_AC",
        "Register Order, Credit Check, Engineering Review, Approve Configuration, Supplier Confirmation, Release Production, Archive Case",
        {"Conservative": False, "Balanced": False, "Permissive": False},

print("Trace Suite Verification (Deterministic Alignment Check):")
print("-" * 75)
for test_id, t_str, expected_map in test_suite:
    results = []
    for name, (net, im, fm) in nets.items():
        accepted = is_trace_accepted(t_str, net, im, fm)
        exp = expected_map[name]
        status = "PASS" if accepted == exp else "FAIL"
        results.append(f"{name}: {accepted} ({status})")
    print(f"{test_id:<26} | " + " | ".join(results))
```

# 9. Recommendation and Data-Collection Plan

### Model Recommendations
1. **Recommended for Immediate Operational Use: `model_balanced` (POWL).**
   - *Why:* It achieves 100% fitness on the 30 observed traces, avoids duplicate transition labels, and honors all high- and medium-confidence domain constraints (D1–D4, D6) and stakeholder rules (S2, S6). Crucially, unlike `model_conservative`, it does not break when a stocked order requires an `Expedite Review` or when an integration drop (S5) removes `Supplier Confirmation` from an expedited order. Unlike `model_permissive`, it does not admit the physically implausible `<Register Order, Supplier Confirmation, ...>` trace where procurement confirmation finishes before any check has occurred.
2. **Recommended for Sensitivity Analysis: `model_permissive` (ProcessTree).**
   - *Why:* Because `model_balanced` enforces `Credit Check -> Supplier Confirmation` based on the 23 observed `SC` cases, it will flag deviations if D5 ("`SC` may begin after *either* `CC` or `ER`") occurs in future production logs. Running `model_permissive` in shadow mode as a sensitivity baseline allows analysts to isolate traces that fail `model_balanced` *solely* due to `ER -> SC -> CC` ordering versus traces that violate hard compliance boundaries (`{CC, ER} -> ExR` or `AC -> RP -> ArC`).

### Concrete Data-Collection Plan to Resolve Ambiguities
To move from abductive inference to deductive certainty, the data engineering and process owners should implement four targeted logging upgrades:

1. **Capture Dual Lifecycle Timestamps (`start` and `complete`):**
   - *Ambiguity Resolved:* D7, S1 vs. S2, and S7.
   - *Mechanism:* Recording `lifecycle:transition = start` alongside `complete` with millisecond precision directly tests whether `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` overlap in time ($t_{\text{start}}(\text{ER}) < t_{\text{complete}}(\text{CC})$ and $t_{\text{start}}(\text{CC}) < t_{\text{complete}}(\text{ER})$), proving true concurrency over mutually exclusive sequential routing (`XOR`). It also reveals whether `Supplier Confirmation` *starts* before `Engineering Review` finishes even when it completes afterward.
2. **Export the Case-Level `component_type` Attribute (`external` vs. `stocked`):**
   - *Ambiguity Resolved:* S3, S4, and S5 (separating true optionality from missing data).
   - *Mechanism:* With `component_type` attached to `Register Order`, every trace without `Supplier Confirmation` can be deterministically classified:
     - `component_type == "stocked"` $\land$ $\neg\text{SC}$ $\implies$ Legitimate business skip (S4).
     - `component_type == "external"` $\land$ $\neg\text{SC}$ $\implies$ Integration data-loss failure (S5).
3. **Log Integration Message Receipts / Outbox IDs for `Supplier Confirmation`:**
   - *Ambiguity Resolved:* Quantifies the exact S5 drop rate and enables automated log repair before conformance checking.

# 10. Uncertainty Statement

### Calibrated Confidence Assessment by Modeling Decision

| Modeling Decision | Confidence Level | Justification & Residual Uncertainty |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fixed Boundaries (`RO` at start; `AC -> RP -> ArC` at end)** | **99% (Very High)** | 30/30 empirical support (V1–V7) and high-confidence domain rules (D1, D2) with zero contradicting stakeholder claims. |
| **Concurrency of `Credit Check` and `Engineering Review` (`CC \|\| ER`)** | **85% (High)** | Strongly supported by V1 ($12\times$), V2 ($7\times$), V3 ($4\times$), V4 ($3\times$), V5 ($2\times$), and S2. Residual 15% uncertainty stems from D7: without `start` timestamps, we cannot mathematically rule out a hidden case-level routing rule that executes `CC -> ER` sequentially for ~63% of orders and `ER -> CC` sequentially for ~37% of orders. |
| **Preconditions for `Expedite Review` (`{CC, ER} -> ExR -> AC` and `ExR \|\| SC`)** | **80% (Moderate-to-High)** | Explicitly stated by D6 and S6 and consistent with V6 and V7. However, empirical support is tiny ($n=2$ traces), leaving statistical uncertainty about edge cases. |
| **Separation of True Optionality vs. Data Loss in `SC` Skips** | **60% (Moderate / Unidentifiable at Trace Level)** | We know with high confidence that the 7 `SC`-free traces (V3, V4) are a mixture of stocked items (S4) and 5–10% integration drops (S5, i.e., $\approx 1.5\text{--}3$ traces), so modeling `X(SC, tau)` is 95% justified for replay. However, identifying *which specific trace* is a drop vs. a valid skip has low confidence without the missing attribute. |
| **Enabling Rule for `Supplier Confirmation` (`CC -> SC` vs. Disjunctive D5)** | **55% (Low-to-Moderate / Key Ambiguity)** | All 23 observed `SC` traces satisfy `CC -> SC`, and V5 ($2\times$) proves `SC` can precede `ER`. Yet D5 suggests `ER` alone might also enable `SC`. With only 2 early-`SC` traces in the sample ($n=2$), the absence of `<RO, ER, SC, CC, ...>` could easily be a sampling artifact (e.g., if `CC` is typically faster than `ER`, as suggested by 19 vs. 11 first-completions). |

### Plausible Alternative Interpretation That Would Materially Change the Recommendation
**Alternative Hypothesis (Case-Routed Sequential Regimes + Fast-Track Engineering):**
Suppose that when `start` timestamps and the missing `component_type` attribute are recovered, we discover that:
1. Finance Manager (S1) and Engineering Lead (S2) were talking about *two different order classes*: Standard External Orders strictly require `Credit Check` to finish before `Engineering Review` begins (`CC -> ER`, accounting for V1 and V3), whereas Repeat-Customer Orders run `ER -> CC` or allow `SC` to start immediately after `ER` (D5).
2. Furthermore, suppose `Supplier Confirmation` is actually **triggered by a Long-Term Or-Join (OR-split/join)** where *either* `CC` *or* `ER` completing is sufficient to launch `SC`, and the only reason `<RO, ER, SC, CC, ...>` was not seen in the 30 cases is that `Credit Check` happens to have a much shorter mean duration than `Engineering Review` (19 vs. 11 first-completions).

**How This Would Change the Recommendation:**
If this alternative interpretation holds, `model_balanced` would systematically reject valid Repeat-Customer orders where `Engineering Review` finishes quickly and `Supplier Confirmation` completes before a delayed `Credit Check` (`<RO, ER, SC, CC, AC, RP, ArC>`). In that scenario, we would **replace `model_balanced`** with a split-regime model (or a POWL/Petri net encoding a disjunctive precondition where `X( (->(CC, opt_sc) || ER), (->(ER, opt_sc) || CC) )` holds), elevating the D5-inclusive behavior of `model_permissive` (while adding a guard to block `<RO, SC, ...>`) to be our primary production model.
