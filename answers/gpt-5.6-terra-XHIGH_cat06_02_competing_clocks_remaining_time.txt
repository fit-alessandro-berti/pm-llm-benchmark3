## 3. Stage-by-stage decomposition

Useful calendar anchors are:

- Chemistry has only 2.5 working hours left on Friday, then pauses until Monday 08:00.
- Primary microbiology becomes technically ready on Sunday 20 September, approximately 12:00-20:00.
- QA cannot act on a Sunday microbiology result because QA is closed Sunday.
- The raw branch join is:

\[
J=\max(\text{chemistry usable-result time},\ \text{microbiology usable-result time})
\]

  QA can begin only at the next QA opening time at or after \(J\). The two branch durations are therefore not added.

- A rough QA backlog calculation is: five existing batches plus about three expected Friday/Saturday arrivals. With only 8.5 one-reviewer hours before Monday, and service times with 1.5 h median but 3.5 h at the 80th percentile, roughly two to five cases may be completed. That leaves approximately three to six cases at Monday opening in a central case, with a wider tail because arrivals are overdispersed. With two Monday reviewers, use roughly 1-5 QA working hours of waiting in a central case, and 0-10 h in a congestion tail.

The key arithmetic shortcut is to carry branch completion clocks forward separately and apply a maximum at the join. For example, a Monday 15:00 chemistry result and a Monday 09:00 microbiology result produce a Monday 15:00 join, not a Monday 24:00 join. Conversely, a Tuesday microbiology confirmation result controls even if chemistry finished Friday.

The most plausible terminal event is **Electronic Release**. The modal operational outcome is release late Monday 21 September or during Tuesday 22 September. **Batch Rejected** is a plausible terminal event only in the abnormal-result/investigation branch; its probability cannot be defensibly quantified because the probability that an abnormality is critical was not supplied.

## 6. Most forecast-changing next event

The single most forecast-changing observation is **resolution of the upstream temperature-excursion flag**.

- If the flag is **confirmed**, update toward a common-cause abnormality regime: increase the chance of a chemistry rerun and contamination indication jointly, use the higher 0.30-0.55 QA-investigation probability, and place more weight on the Wednesday-through-next-week tail. It also makes Batch Rejected a more credible terminal alternative, though still not quantifiable without defect-severity information.
- If the flag is **cleared**, reduce the correlated-tail weight. Chemistry rerun and microbiology contamination remain possible at their ordinary rates, but the smooth release path and ordinary QA-query path become more dominant.

A useful secondary update trigger is the **first recorded microbiology readout**. A normal usable readout removes the confirmation-incubation branch and supports a Monday/Tuesday release forecast. A contamination indication shifts the microbiology branch into a likely Tuesday confirmation result and materially increases the probability of QA investigation.
