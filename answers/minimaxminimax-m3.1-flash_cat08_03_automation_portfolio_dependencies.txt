## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Logical dependency chain

The process is not a simple serial chain. A useful dependency view is:

```text
Application record A1 where needed> A2 product/exposure decision
                                           > A5 document-request workflow
                                           > A6 financial normalization > credit analysis
                                           > A7 collateral decision/routing > external valuation

A3 KYC pre-classification > KYC check > A4 for entity mismatch > KYC clearance
                                                                       
                 normalized financials + credit notes + valuation 
                                                                       
                                                        A8 committee packaging
                                                                       
                                                     committee decision
                                                                       
                                                  A9 contract generation/checks
                                                                       
                                                       A10 customer signing
                                                                       
                                                        A11 loan activation

A12 control workbench, audit trail, role separation, and remediation governance
span every arrow and every exception path.
```

Important implications follow from the event data:

- **Registration and classification:** `Receive  Register` occurs 11,880 times, and `Register  Classify` occurs 11,680 times. A1 may improve the completeness of the record consumed by A2, but registration is not a hard technical prerequisite if the existing record is already sufficiently complete.
- **Routing:** Classification sends 7,250 observed paths to KYC and 3,940 to financial-document review. An A2 error can therefore affect compliance, document handling, credit work, and collateral requirements at once.
- **KYC and entity mismatch:** The `KYC  Resolve entity mismatch` edge occurs 1,750 times, with 1,160 returns to KYC and 380 direct paths to contract preparation. These are loop and routing frequencies, not 1,750 independent additional cases. A4 must update the authoritative KYC milestone and should not create a parallel “locally resolved” status.
- **Concurrent financial and KYC work:** Financial-document review and KYC are often concurrent even though the information system records them sequentially. A6 should not be made technically dependent on KYC merely because of log order. Their waiting-time effects may overlap rather than add.
- **Financial documents:** `Financial review  Request documents` occurs 3,300 times and `Request documents  Financial review` occurs 2,800 times. A5 should produce a complete, product-specific request that allows one successful submission, while retaining a human path for unusual cases.
- **Normalization and credit:** `Normalize statements  Credit analysis` occurs 5,000 times. Committee packaging also waits for normalized financials and credit notes. A6 is therefore both a labor-saving module and an upstream prerequisite for a reliable credit and committee artifact.
- **Collateral synchronization:** `Credit analysis  Collateral request` occurs 2,400 times, followed by 2,350 external-wait observations and 1,980 paths from external wait to packaging. Collateral is required in 71% of Equipment-finance and 18% of Working-capital cases, so A2 and A7 must use the same trigger logic.
- **Committee and contract:** Packaging requires KYC status, normalized financials, credit notes, and valuation when applicable. The committee decision has 3,900 cases; 3,650 observed paths lead directly to contract preparation. A8 and A9 cannot be treated as independent form-generation tools.
- **Control remediation:** The `Contract  Remediate control issue  Contract` loop occurs 650 times on the forward edge and 510 times on the return edge. A12 must distinguish a true policy violation from missing or inconsistent data.
- **Signing and activation:** There are 7,300 signing paths and 7,100 activation paths. Signing requires a legally valid contract, identity/consent evidence, and KYC clearance. Activation requires completed controls, not merely a completed signature.

- **Cycle time:** A1 could remove an eight-minute registration touch from a large population, and A2 removes or shortens a 12-minute classification touch. The effect will be smaller than the frequency suggests because 72% of intake is already digital. A9 may reduce contract-preparation effort for about 7,600 cases, but only if decision data is clean.
- **Waiting time:** Some registration and classification waiting should fall, but the major waits remain: 31 hours for entity mismatch, 58 hours for external valuation, and 44 hours before signing. A9 cannot remove the 22-hour wait before contract preparation when KYC or committee artifacts are late.
- **Rework:** A2 should reduce classification errors and the resulting wrong-route rework below the current 8% rate. A9 can detect term inconsistencies and reduce the 650 observed contract-remediation paths, particularly the 14% remediation rate for credit-line renewals. Missing-document loops of 3,300 requests and 2,800 returns remain.
- **Handovers:** Fewer routine back-office and legal handovers are likely. Conversely, faster intake can increase handovers to classification, KYC, and credit analysis, and month-end legal demand can become more abrupt.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office capacity is freed, but because utilization is already below 65% late in the day, this should be treated as redeployable capacity rather than headcount reduction. Compliance and credit work can increase if the newly accelerated cases are not clean. A12 creates a new control-resolution workload that should be staffed by control owners.
- **Control violations:** A12 can block or route four-eyes, KYC-clearance, and contract-consistency violations; A9 adds contract rule checks. However, broad KYC-hit handling, entity mismatch, and provisional-signing root causes remain largely untouched.

### Portfolio B — A4, A6, A7, A12

- **Cycle time:** The largest improvement will be on affected paths. A4 removes a 31-hour wait on cases where the mismatch is critical in 82% of instances. A6 improves the `Normalize statements  Credit analysis` path of 5,000 observations. A7 can shorten the 58-hour external synchronization point feeding 1,980 observed packaging paths.
- **Waiting time:** A4 directly targets the 31-hour mismatch wait, A6 the 20-hour normalization wait, and A7 part of the 58-hour valuation wait. External valuation time cannot be eliminated, so the residual wait will remain dependent on valuer performance.
- **Rework:** The 38% mismatch exception rate, 16% normalization exception rate, and 19% late-valuation rate should all decline if the modules improve first-pass quality. A12 should also reduce unresolved four-eyes and contract-control loops.
- **Handovers:** A4 routes only genuinely difficult matches to the appropriate specialist rather than sending all hit work through senior review. A6 gives analysts cleaner inputs. A7 can prevalidate and route valuation requests earlier, reducing late committee handovers.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance specialists should spend more time on genuine exceptions and less on information gathering. Analysts should receive cleaner statements, but low-quality PDFs still require protected review capacity. A7 may shift demand toward preferred valuers and require panel-level capacity management.
- **Control violations:** A12 provides the strongest cross-case control improvement, while A4 can prevent unresolved KYC status from reaching contract or signing. Without a broad KYC pre-classifier, the underlying 11% KYC exception rate remains. External valuation quality also remains outside direct control.

### Portfolio C — A3, A4, A9, A12

- **Cycle time:** A3 can reduce routine KYC handling for 11,500 cases, A4 clears the 1,750 mismatch paths, and A9 shortens contract preparation. The direct KYC loop edges—1,750 to mismatch and 1,160 back to KYC—should become faster and more targeted.
- **Waiting time:** The KYC pre-activity wait of four hours and mismatch wait of 31 hours should improve. Contract preparation can become less dependent on manual reconciliation, but the 44-hour signing wait and 58-hour valuation wait remain.
- **Rework:** KYC hit resolution should become more focused, and contract-remediation loops should fall. The module can, however, create false-positive rework and, if poorly calibrated, false-negative control failures; the 11% KYC exception rate is not itself eliminated.
- **Handovers:** Low-risk KYC cases may reach compliance more selectively, while true hits receive a clearer escalation package. Legal preparation should require fewer manual corrections. The workflow becomes more dependent on the three compliance specialists and two senior specialists, which is a material concentration risk.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance effort should shift from routine screening to genuine hits and entity remediation. If hit precision is weak, that shifted workload can exceed current capacity. Analyst demand is largely unchanged, and legal month-end peaks remain.
- **Control violations:** This is the strongest direct response to KYC and contract controls. A12 must enforce four-eyes separation, and signing must remain technically blocked until KYC clearance is recorded. A3 must not be allowed to make an autonomous final KYC decision in the first year.

### Portfolio P — A2, A4, A6, A12

- **Cycle time:** A2 improves the common routing point for 11,820 cases; A4 targets the critical mismatch path; A6 improves 5,000 normalization-to-credit paths and committee artifacts. Because KYC and document work are often concurrent, A4 and A6 improvements can overlap and jointly reduce package readiness time rather than simply add.
- **Waiting time:** Expected reductions are strongest in classification waiting, the 31-hour mismatch wait, the 20-hour normalization wait, and part of the 26-hour committee-packaging wait. The portfolio intentionally leaves the 58-hour external-valuation and 44-hour signing waits for later work.
- **Rework:** A2 should reduce the 8% classification exception rate; A4 should reduce repeated KYC mismatch handling; A6 should reduce normalization corrections and analyst rework. A12 should detect and route the 650 contract-control loops and month-end four-eyes violations. Document loops and provisional-signing cases remain and must stay visible.
- **Handovers:** A2 reduces misrouted handovers, A4 creates a more focused specialist queue, A6 reduces analyst clarification handovers, and A12 makes control exceptions explicit rather than allowing them to surface at activation.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office classification effort falls; compliance capacity is reallocated toward genuine entity exceptions; analyst capacity is redirected from routine normalization to complex and High-exposure work. Because A2 could accelerate inflow, its rollout must be tied to analyst and compliance queue stability.
- **Control violations:** A2 with mandatory exposure review reduces wrong-control routing; A4 improves KYC clearance integrity; A12 addresses four-eyes, KYC-before-signing, and contract-decision consistency. It does not replace compliance accountability, and the existing provisional-signing population must be handled as an explicit remediation queue.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

Automation remains a decision-support and workflow mechanism. Compliance, credit, legal, and operations roles retain responsibility for decisions and exceptions unless regulation expressly permits otherwise.

| Risk | Compensating control |
|---|---|
| **Incomplete audit trail or undocumented override** | Append-only event record containing source evidence, model/rule version, confidence, suggestion, reviewer, override reason, final decision, timestamps, and control status. Every exception should be reviewable without reconstructing state from free-text notes. |
| **A2 misclassification or wrong control path** | Human approval for High-exposure and uncertain cases; separate “routing recommendation” from final exposure classification; reconciliation against product, amount, collateral, and KYC policies; segment-level error monitoring. |
| **A3/A4 KYC false negatives or false positives** | Conservative thresholds, no unsupervised clearance for material cases, mandatory specialist sign-off, negative-control sampling, and stratified precision/recall monitoring. False positives must be capped to protect the three-person compliance pool; false negatives require immediate case quarantine and control review. |
| **A6 extraction errors and poor-quality PDFs** | Confidence thresholds, source-page citations, analyst validation below threshold, sampled quality assurance, and explicit “insufficient evidence” outcomes. The system must not fabricate missing financial fields. |
| **Model or rule drift** | Monitor drift by product, applicant segment, document quality, and exception type; use champion/challenger comparison before rule changes; require periodic Risk and Compliance revalidation; maintain rollback to the prior model or manual workflow. |
| **Automation shifts work rather than reducing it** | Plan for a smaller routine queue and a more complex exception queue. Track handling time, queue age, overtime, and specialist/analyst workload; redeploy capacity to High-exposure work rather than assuming proportional headcount reduction. |
| **Exception handling is overwhelmed** | Capacity-based rollout gates, protected specialist and analyst exception lanes, clear service levels, escalation paths, and trained fallback teams. Straight-through processing should never apply to unresolved exceptions. |
| **Four-eyes violation in High-exposure cases** | A12 must perform role-based conflict checks between credit analysis and committee-pack approval, block completion by the same person, and retain independent-review evidence. Month-end load must be included in testing. |
| **Signing before KYC clearance or provisional-signing leakage** | A hard technical gate keyed to authoritative KYC status; no e-sign or activation event before clearance; any provisional-signing exception requires documented legal/compliance authorization, customer notification, and an activation block until remediated. |
| **A9 generates stale or inconsistent contract terms** | Versioned and legally owned rule library, snapshot of the committee decision used for generation, automated reconciliation of material terms, regression tests, expiration dates, and legal approval before rule updates. |
| **Upstream “garbage in” reaches committee, contract, or activation** | Completeness and consistency checks at each artifact boundary; A8 and A9 should refuse incomplete or conflicting records. A11 should not automate while a material control remains unresolved. |
| **Customer friction from A5 or A10** | Clear, plain-language requests; product-specific but flexible checklists; easy upload/correction channels; RM override and escalation; complaint, abandonment, and repeat-request monitoring. Automation should not pressure customers to accept incomplete or unclear terms. |
| **External valuer quality or capacity disputes** | Approved valuer panel, documented routing criteria, evidence/quality checks, SLA monitoring, dispute traceability, and manual review for high-exposure collateral. |
| **Policy ambiguity exposed by A12** | Assign a named policy owner and resolution deadline; distinguish an actual violation from missing data; preserve the original decision and rationale. Do not let the workbench create an unapproved de facto policy. |
| **Data privacy, security, and access errors** | Least-privilege access, encryption, retention controls, segregation of duties, and audit of access to KYC, financial, and model outputs. |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

### Module order

The recommended first-year order is:

1. **A12 — control and audit foundation**
2. **A2 — upstream routing and exposure decision support**
3. **A4 — targeted entity-mismatch assistance**
4. **A6 — financial extraction and normalization**

A12 is deployed first even though A4 ranks highest on immediate process value, because A12 establishes the control, audit, and exception framework needed by every other module.

### Phased plan

| Phase | Timing | Modules and actions | Integration and capacity plan | Gate criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Baseline and design** | Months 0–1 | Establish end-to-end baselines by product, applicant type, exposure, KYC-hit status, collateral requirement, document-loop status, and month-end/non-month-end periods. Validate concurrency rather than relying on sequential log order. Name policy owners and escalation teams. | Reserve the two first-six-month deep-integration slots for **A12 and A2**. A4 and A6 use workflow/document integrations only. Build the common audit-event model under A12. | Risk, Compliance, Legal, and Operations approve control definitions, service levels, and exception capacity. Baselines include resource demand and customer friction. |

Prepare A4 and A6 data-quality tests. | A12 and A2 are the only deep core-banking integrations. No automated decision is final; all recommendations are shadowed and reviewed. | Complete auditability, role-conflict detection, source-data validation, and rollback tests. A2 shadow quality must be no worse than the current 8% exception/rework baseline on critical segments. |
| **2. Controlled control operation** | Months 3–6 | Put A12 into limited production, initially emphasizing High-exposure and month-end cases. Run A2 in shadow-to-assisted mode. ### Monitoring metrics

| Scope | Metrics and relevant baselines |
|---|---|
| **A12** | Four-eyes violation rate versus the 5.8% High-exposure month-end baseline; percentage of violations detected before case completion; provisional-signing incidence versus 6.5% and remediation incidence versus 1.1%; contract-remediation incidence versus 8.5% overall and 14% for renewals; audit-record completeness; override and rationale rates. |

| **A6** | Analyst touch time versus 75 minutes; wait versus 20 hours; normalization exception rate versus 16%; low-quality-PDF rate; analyst correction rate; credit-analysis queue wait versus 28 hours; downstream correction in credit notes or committee artifacts. |
| **Portfolio** | Median and 90th-percentile end-to-end cycle time by product and exposure; activity waiting times; handovers per completed case; rework loops; specialist/analyst/legal workload and service levels; customer contacts and complaints; external valuation and signing waits retained as explicit residual measures. |

### Mandatory rollout gates

1. **Control gate:** No unremediated control bypass; no signing before authoritative KYC clearance; every four-eyes conflict blocked or routed before completion; complete audit history.
2. **Quality gate:** No material segment performs worse than the current baseline. False-negative and false-positive thresholds must be approved by Risk and Compliance before live use.
3. **Capacity gate:** Compliance and analyst queues, service levels, and overtime do not deteriorate. Automated capacity is used for higher-value exceptions rather than assumed to disappear.
4. **Process gate:** The portfolio must improve end-to-end cycle time and wait—not merely shift work to another activity or team.
5. **Customer gate:** Additional customer contacts, incomplete instructions, and complaints do not materially increase.
6. **Integration gate:** Recommendations and control statuses reconcile with core and workflow-system records; rollback is tested.

If any gate fails, the affected module reverts to shadow or manual operation while the specific upstream data, rule, capacity, or control problem is corrected.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend **Portfolio P: A2 + A4 + A6 + A12** for the first year.

It provides the strongest balance of:

- **Broad upstream coverage:** A2 affects nearly the entire application population and prevents routing errors before they propagate.
- **Critical-path intervention:** A4 is low-frequency but has a 31-hour wait and is critical in 82% of its cases.
- **Analyst and downstream preparation:** A6 removes repetitive normalization work and improves the quality of credit and committee artifacts.
- **Control foundation:** A12 addresses four-eyes separation, KYC-before-signing, contract reconciliation, auditability, and policy ambiguity.
- **Manageable deployment risk:** A2 and A4 retain meaningful human decisions, while A6 confines poor-document exceptions to a protected analyst lane.

The highest-frequency activity should **not** be the sole or first automation target. Receive-application volume is 12,000, but 72% is already digital. A1 concerns 11,880 registrations, yet its incremental manual scope is much smaller than the headline and its eight-minute service time is not the dominant source of delay. Automating it without upstream and capacity controls would mainly move cases into classification, KYC, document review, and the already-central analyst pool. It may become a useful later quick win, but it should not displace A4, A6, or A12.

The recommended portfolio knowingly leaves three major opportunities for the next wave:

- **A7** for the 58-hour external valuation wait.
- **A10** for the 44-hour signing wait, after the KYC gate is proven.
- **A5/A9** for document loops and contract remediation, after upstream artifacts and rule governance are reliable.

That trade-off is preferable to maximizing short-term touch-time savings while worsening specialist queues, analyst overload, or control exposure. Management should judge the program on end-to-end time, rework, handovers, resource demand, and control performance—not on how many high-frequency touches were removed.
