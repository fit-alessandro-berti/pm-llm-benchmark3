# 2. Benign alternative explanations

### 2.1 VendorMaster_Lena  AP_Kim bank-change cluster (R1–R4, R8)
- **B1 — Legitimate mass maintenance after the V-77x banking-platform migration.** *Supports:* a change/maintenance ticket covering the exact account values and timestamps; many V-77x bank changes with **no** subsequent payment; identical oldnew account patterns across unrelated vendors in the family; bank-issued migration notice. *Refutes:* changes confined to cases with imminent payments; beneficiary name/IBAN not matching the bank's confirmation; changes reverted after release.
- **B2 — Role-based allocation.** AP_Kim owns higher-risk suppliers and may be the designated approver for the whole V-77x family; VendorMaster_Lena may be the family's data owner. *Supports:* Kim's V-77x share **outside** this pair being similarly high; a documented assignment matrix. *Refutes:* V-77x concentrated only when Lena is the changer, i.e. concentration is pair-specific rather than role-specific.
- **B3 — Timestamp artefact / prerequisite ordering.** The payment was already scheduled; the bank fix was the blocking prerequisite, so approval follows within minutes. Or both events were posted by one upload batch. *Supports:* payment due dates and pre-existing payment-run IDs; batch IDs shared by both events; original system timestamps differing from log timestamps. *Refutes:* genuinely interactive, separately-sessioned events minutes apart with no batch linkage.
- **B4 — Centralised vendor-master function serving all regions** (explains "split across regions" without evasion). *Supports:* Lena's regional spread across her whole 240-case portfolio. *Refutes:* region-splitting present only in this pair.

### 2.2 Emergency clusters: CarlaGita, DanHugo (R7–R9)
- **B1 — Designed delegated authority.** Gita is the delegated urgent approver; Hugo owns exceptions; weekend repairs cannot wait for the standard queue. *Supports:* delegation-of-authority matrix, on-call rota, out-of-hours timestamps matching real weekends/holidays, clinical urgency requisitions, outage/work-order tickets. *Refutes:* `emergency_flag` set **after** approval, or emergency cases with no independent urgency artefact.
- **B2 — Identity conflation, not self-approval.** Assistants' work posted to the shared "Approver_Gita" account; `Bot_PO` creation merged with a human approver in `legacy_erp`. *Supports:* `entered_by`  `org:resource`, session/terminal IDs, IAM ownership of the shared account, corrected mapping table. *Refutes:* a single verified human genuinely performing both activities after identity resolution.
- **B3 — Pre-agreed framework pricing** makes 8-minute approval rational for repeat medical consumables. *Supports:* framework contracts, catalogues, prior price benchmarks, low price variance. *Refutes:* novel vendors or off-catalogue high-value items approved in minutes.

### 2.3 Same-person / batch retrospective justifications (R5, R6)
- **B1 — Migration artefact.** 12/17 rows carry identical minute timestamps from `quarter_close_upload`; the recorded time is the *ingestion* time, not the decision time, and the recorded resource may be the *uploader*, not the author. *Supports:* the source spreadsheet with author cells and original dates; migration job logs; a distinct `entered_by`. *Refutes:* the spreadsheet also shows the same author as the overrider.
- **B2 — Structural infeasibility of the compensating control.** Out-of-hours, no second authorised human may exist. *Supports:* authority matrix and shift coverage showing a single authorised person; a documented, risk-accepted deviation. *Refutes:* an available second authoriser was bypassed.

### 2.4 Vendor Check + Change Bank Details, 31 cases (R11)
- **B1 — Two-person team design**; the activity pair was never a normative four-eyes rule. *Supports:* process design documents, role descriptions. *Refutes:* a policy that does require separation, or these 31 cases overlapping the 9 payment-related violations.
- **B2 — Onboarding workflow** where check and data entry are one legitimate task. *Supports:* onboarding checklist template; new-vendor status at event time.

### 2.5 Temp_Nina and Bot_MatchAP_Kim (R12, R13)
- **B1 — Scoped, time-boxed data-entry support** with no approval rights (1 event/case). *Supports:* ticket, contract end date, entitlement list showing read/create-only, account disabled after quarter close. *Refutes:* activity after contract end, or any approval/payment event on that account.
- **B2 — Automated tolerance-exception queue** with trivial fixes. *Supports:* match-tolerance config, exception reasons, low exception values. *Refutes:* exceptions above tolerance thresholds, or exceptions clustering on V-77x with bank-mismatch reasons (which would instead *support* B1 in §2.1 — a useful discriminating test).

**4.1 Four-eyes filtering, with bounded rather than point estimates**

```python
# PM4Py operates on org:resource only. Verify the polarity of `positive` on a
# hand-checked case before use; then re-run on a log whose resource column is
# the resolved actor, so shared accounts / bot mappings do not drive the count.
fe_raw = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log, 'Create Purchase Order', 'Approve Purchase Order',
    positive=False, resource_key=RES)

df_eff = df.assign(**{RES: df['actor_eff']})
fe_eff = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df_eff),
    'Create Purchase Order', 'Approve Purchase Order', positive=False, resource_key=RES)

def four_eyes_cases(d, a1, a2, actor):
    d = d[d[ACT].isin([a1, a2]) & ~d[actor].isin(BOTS)]
    g = d.groupby([CASE, ACT])[actor].apply(set).unstack().dropna(subset=[a1, a2])
    return set(g[g.apply(lambda r: bool(r[a1] & r[a2]), axis=1)].index)

for a1, a2 in [('Create Purchase Order','Approve Purchase Order'),
               ('Change Vendor Bank Details','Payment Approval'),
               ('Emergency Override','Retrospective Justification')]:
    raw = four_eyes_cases(df, a1, a2, 'actor_raw')
    eff = four_eyes_cases(df, a1, a2, 'actor_eff')
    clean = four_eyes_cases(df[~df['ambiguous']], a1, a2, 'actor_eff')   # lower bound
    worst = four_eyes_cases(df.assign(actor_eff=df['actor_raw']), a1, a2, 'actor_raw')
    print(a1, a2, 'raw', len(raw), 'resolved', len(eff),
          'unambiguous-only', len(clean), 'attribution-sensitive', len(raw ^ eff))
```

**4.2 Networks, with a null model so frequency is not mistaken for signal**

```python
how = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, resource_key=RES)
wt  = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log, resource_key=RES)
sub = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log, resource_key=RES)

                                      ingoing_attribute=RES, index=0, performance=True)

def edges(g):                                  # NetworkX or dict, version-dependent
    if hasattr(g, 'edges'):
        return pd.DataFrame([(u, v, d.get('weight', 1)) for u, v, d in g.edges(data=True)],
                            columns=['src','dst','w'])
    return pd.DataFrame([(u, v, w) for u, dd in g.items() for v, w in dd.items()],
                        columns=['src','dst','w'])

H, S = edges(how), edges(sub)
co = df.groupby([CASE, RES]).size().reset_index()[[CASE, RES]]
co = co.merge(co, on=CASE).query(f'{RES}_x != {RES}_y')
den = co.groupby([f'{RES}_x', f'{RES}_y']).size().rename('co_cases')
S = S.merge(den, left_on=['src','dst'], right_index=True, how='left')
S['loop_rate'] = S['w'] / S['co_cases']        # normalise: 31% vs 6% is the signal

# Null model: permute resource labels WITHIN activity and team/role, preserving
# each account's per-activity case volume; recompute handover weights.
def null_weights(df, n=500):
    out = []
    for _ in range(n):
        d = df.copy()
        d[RES] = d.groupby(ACT)[RES].transform(np.random.permutation)
        out.append(edges(pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
            pm4py.convert_to_event_log(d), resource_key=RES)))
    return pd.concat(out).groupby(['src','dst'])['w'].agg(['mean','std'])
# z = (observed - mean) / std  -> Aline->Elena should be ~structural; test Lena->Kim.
```

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

**Would increase concern (in rough order of diagnostic value)**
1. **Beneficiary mismatch**: post-change IBAN/beneficiary name in the payment file differing from the vendor's independently confirmed bank record, or a beneficiary name resembling an employee/related party.
2. **Reversion pattern**: bank details changed shortly before a payment and changed back afterwards; or new accounts used for one payment only.
3. **Missing independent verification**: no callback/second-channel confirmation for the 9 same-actor cases, or the confirmation signed by the same account that made the change.
4. **Ticket linkage that fails to explain the cases**: the V-77x migration ticket exists but covers different accounts, different dates, or fewer cases than the log shows.
5. **Pair-specific concentration**: V-77x is ~72% within LenaKim but a small share of AP_Kim's and Lena's other work — i.e. concentration is not explained by role allocation.
6. **Release Payment also unseparated**, or `approved_by` equal to `entered_by` on the release event, or release outside a scheduled payment run.
7. **Same-actor justifications confirmed in the source spreadsheet** (author cell = overrider) for the 5 non-migrated cases — migration cannot explain those.
8. **`emergency_flag` set after approval**, or emergency cases lacking any independent urgency artefact, or "weekend" timestamps falling on weekdays.
9. **Access-log corroboration**: both events in a fast pair from the same session ID, workstation, or IP; or one account's credentials used from two locations concurrently.
10. **Post-termination activity** on Temp_Nina, or any approval/payment event on that account.
11. **Vendor-side anomalies**: V-77x entity newly registered, address/phone shared with other vendors, duplicate bank accounts across vendor IDs, or the family's real trading volume inconsistent with the amounts paid.

**Would reduce concern**
1. A **maintenance ticket + bank confirmation** matching every changed account and timestamp, with a documented reason why the ticketcase link is absent from the log.
2. A **population view** showing many V-77x bank changes with no payment, and payments to unchanged accounts — inconsistent with targeted redirection.
3. An **assignment matrix** showing AP_Kim owns V-77x and Lena is the family's data owner, plus similar concentration in their non-paired work.
4. **Payment-run evidence** that the payment was pre-scheduled and the bank fix was the blocking prerequisite, explaining 17 minutes; or shared batch IDs showing the gap is a posting artefact.
5. A **corrected `legacy_erp` mapping table** and IAM confirmation of the shared account, after which the 42 PO violations fall materially and the residual cases show distinct verified humans.
6. **Original spreadsheet author/date cells** showing a different author and a within-3-working-day date for the 12 migrated justifications.
7. **Delegation-of-authority and on-call records** covering Gita and Hugo, plus framework contracts/catalogue pricing for the fast medical and repair approvals.
8. **Null-model results** showing handover and loop rates within the expected range once team routing, role, emergency share and volume are conditioned on.
9. **Independent goods receipt / three-way match** evidence for the high-value cases: goods actually delivered and used.
10. A **documented, risk-accepted deviation** for out-of-hours single-authoriser justification, with a compensating next-business-day review that demonstrably occurred.

**Decision rule to state in advance:** the working hypothesis for H1 remains *channel/SoD control gap plus missing migration linkage* unless items 1–6 above appear. Conversely, if items 1–4 of the reducing list are obtained, H1 should be downgraded to a data-linkage and configuration remediation item.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

We reviewed the procurement-to-payment event log for the last quarter (approximately 2,300–2,400 cases) using conformance, social-network and timing analyses. The review identifies **control exceptions, not findings of misconduct**. Four-eyes exceptions were recorded for Create/Approve Purchase Order (42 cases, 1.8%), Change Vendor Bank Details/Payment Approval (9 cases, 0.4%), and Emergency Override/Retrospective Justification by the same resource (17 cases, 0.7%).

The subgroup warranting closest attention is the VendorMaster_Lena–AP_Kim cluster (44 co-cases): 68% high-value, 72% concentrated in vendor family V-77x, 83% originating from `manual_upload`, and a 17-minute median interval between bank-detail change and payment approval against a 7.8-hour baseline. This combination is not readily explained by emergency handling (16% emergency) and merits targeted verification. It is equally consistent with legitimate mass bank maintenance following the vendor's banking-platform migration; the log lacks the maintenance-ticket linkage needed to distinguish these possibilities.

Other patterns are plausibly attributable to designed specialisation (team-based buyer/approver routing), documented emergency delegation, and automated exception queues. High co-working volume alone was not treated as a risk indicator.

Reliability is limited: shared-account attribution (23 cases), a `legacy_erp` bot/human resource-mapping error, and 12 retrospective justifications migrated with identical minute-level timestamps. Violation counts and timing statistics should be regarded as provisional ranges until identities and source timestamps are reconstructed.

We recommend document-based follow-up on the bank-change/payment cluster and on late retrospective justifications, alongside remediation of segregation-of-duties configuration, shared accounts and migration timestamp fidelity. No conclusion regarding intent is drawn.
