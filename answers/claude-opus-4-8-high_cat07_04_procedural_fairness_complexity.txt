# 1. Fairness dimensions

Procedural fairness in a benefits process is multi-dimensional. Focusing only on approval parity would miss most of the procedural burden that differentiates groups here. I assess at least the following dimensions.

**Outcome parity (raw).** Approval rates differ substantially: A 78%, C 69%, B 63%, D 58%. Raw parity is violated, but raw parity is *not* the fairness target on its own, because case mix, risk, documentation, and channel differ across groups. Raw gaps are a signal to investigate, not proof of unfairness.

**Adjusted outcome disparity.** After controlling for risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, and month, the approval gaps shrink dramatically: B −5.5 pp, D −3.1 pp, C −2.2 pp. The controls "explain" most of the raw gap, but a residual disadvantage remains for every non-A group. This residual is the more relevant fairness concern than the raw gap — though its interpretation depends on whether the covariates are themselves fair (see §5).

**Procedural burden.** This is the frequency and intensity of extra steps: Request Missing Documents, Supervisor Review, Fraud Review, handovers, and rework. Adjusted probabilities show B and C carry markedly more Request Missing Documents (+9.5, +4.2 pp) and Supervisor Review (+8.0, +5.5 pp) *even after controls*. Two applicants with equivalent documented complexity may experience procedurally different journeys. This is a fairness dimension distinct from whether they are ultimately approved.

**Timeliness.** Cycle time and, more importantly, step-level waiting. Adjusted cycle time is worse for C (+4.1d) and B (+2.6d). The waiting-time table localizes much of this to two steps that are *rights-preserving*: Language Support Contact → Eligibility (3.8d, only 7 bilingual caseworkers) and Accommodation Review → Eligibility (4.7d, specialized-team queue). Delay concentrated in the steps needed to exercise a right is a specific timeliness-fairness concern.

**Consistency of routing.** Fairness requires that similar cases follow similar procedures and that routing rules are applied predictably. Legitimate routing exists (high risk → Fraud Review; accommodation request → Accommodation Review; minority language → Language Support). The question is whether routing is *consistent* for equivalent cases and whether it produces avoidable extra handovers. B and D disproportionately hit Supervisor Review / Fraud Review; some is legitimate, some (the residual after controls) is not clearly explained.

**Quality and accessibility of support.** The support steps exist (good), but there are no separate timeliness thresholds for them, and capacity is thin (7 bilingual staff, 5 accommodation specialists, 2 of whom double as supervisors). Accessibility that is nominally guaranteed but operationally bottlenecked is a degraded form of access. Note also that case_outcome does not capture explanation quality or dignity of treatment, so this dimension is only partially observable.

**Appeal and rework burden.** Appeal rates (A 6%, B 13%, D 16%, C 10%) and reopen/rework rates (A 11%, B 22%, C 24%, D 20%) are far higher for non-A groups, and adjusted appeal probability remains elevated (D +5.7, B +4.4 pp). High rework/appeal can indicate lower first-pass decision quality, weaker explanations, or documentation churn — all of which impose burden regardless of the final outcome.

**Resource-allocation fairness.** A's cases spread across 52 caseworkers; B funnels into 7 bilingual staff + 3 overloaded regional officers; C into 5 specialists. Concentration of specific groups onto scarce, overloaded resources is a structural fairness issue: the disadvantage is produced by staffing design, not by the applicant's characteristics.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

The goal is to separate four things: **disparity** (a measured difference), **legitimate routing** (a difference fully attributable to policy-justified case attributes), **possible discrimination** (a residual difference not explained by legitimate factors), and **uncertainty** (what the data cannot resolve).

**Matching / stratification variables.** Build comparison cohorts matched on the documented-complexity covariates: risk_score_band, documentation_completeness, channel, household_size_band, prior_case_count, region, and month/period. Prefer exact stratification or coarsened exact matching within these cells, then compare group outcomes *within* strata. Estimate residual group effects with these covariates held fixed, and report both point estimates and confidence intervals. Because some covariates are themselves suspect (risk_score_band, documentation_completeness), run the model twice: once including them, once excluding/instrumenting them (see sensitivity).

**Path / variant comparison.** For matched cohorts, compare variant distributions across groups. If two groups with equivalent complexity still take systematically different paths (e.g., B far more likely to be on V4 with Request Docs, or to hit Supervisor Review), that indicates routing not explained by documented complexity. Distinguish *legitimate-by-construction* paths (V4 language support, V5 accommodation review) from *discretionary* escalations (Supervisor Review, Fraud Review, Request Missing Documents) that should be equalized within strata.

**Resource-assignment comparison.** Model the probability that a case is assigned to overloaded/scarce resources as a function of group and covariates. Quantify concentration (e.g., share of a group handled by the top-k busiest workers) and compare handover counts per case across groups within strata. Test whether resource concentration itself predicts longer waits and higher rework after controlling for complexity.

**Waiting-time decomposition.** Decompose total cycle time into (a) service time per activity and (b) waiting time per transition. Then attribute waiting to: legitimate additional steps (accommodation/language/fraud steps that genuinely must occur), queueing/capacity waits at those steps, and waits at *shared* steps (e.g., Supervisor Review → Decision backlog in two regions). The key test: after removing legitimately-required extra activities, do equivalent cases still wait longer at *shared* steps? If yes, that portion is not explained by case complexity.

**Sensitivity analysis for possibly biased covariates.** Treat risk_score_band and documentation_completeness as potentially endogenous. (i) Re-estimate disparities dropping these covariates — if the residual group gap grows a lot, then "controls" were absorbing group-correlated bias. (ii) Test whether risk_score_band predicts outcomes differently across groups (calibration/discrimination checks). (iii) Bound results with an unobserved-confounder sensitivity analysis (e.g., E-value-style reasoning): how strong would an omitted legitimate factor have to be to explain the residual?

**Subgroup checks by region and channel.** Backlogs are localized ("two regions"), so pooled estimates can hide/average out region-specific unfairness. Stratify all comparisons by region and by channel (online vs in-person). Check whether the residual B disadvantage is concentrated in the two backlogged regions and among in-person applicants, and whether C's delay is specific to the accommodation-specialist queue. Interaction terms (group × region, group × channel) plus per-cell estimates make this explicit.

# 3. Path and resource disparities

**Justified largely by case complexity:**
- **V4 (Language Support Contact) for B and V5 (Accommodation Review) for C.** These paths are the direct, policy-mandated consequence of a minority-language request or an accommodation request. Their *existence* for B (24% on V4) and C (31% on V5) is legitimate routing, not unfairness.
- **V3 (Risk Screening → Fraud Review → Supervisor Review) concentrated in D (28%).** D has 51% high risk_score_band and 49% high prior-case-count, so heavier fraud/supervisor routing is consistent with documented risk — *conditional* on risk_score_band being a fair measure (which is not assured).
- **Higher Request Missing Documents on V2 for B/C** partly reflects genuinely higher incomplete-documentation shares (B 46%, C 36%).

**Concerning even after accounting for complexity:**
- **Waiting concentrated in rights-preserving steps.** Language Support → Eligibility (3.8d) and Accommodation Review → Eligibility (4.7d) are the largest single waits, driven by *capacity* (7 bilingual staff; 5 specialists, 2 doubling as supervisors), not by the applicant's complexity. Exercising a right should not systematically slow the case; the absence of service-level targets for these steps makes the delay invisible to management. This is the clearest procedural-fairness concern.
- **Residual Supervisor Review and Request Docs after controls** (B +8.0/+9.5 pp; C +5.5/+4.2 pp). These are *discretionary* escalations that remain elevated after adjusting for documented complexity — so complexity does not fully explain them.
- **Shared-step backlog affecting B disproportionately.** Supervisor Review → Decision wait is B 1.9d vs A 1.0d, "backlog in two regions." Because B is funneled into 3 overloaded regional officers, B absorbs more of a *shared* bottleneck than its complexity alone would justify.
- **Handover intensity.** B's general → bilingual → general and C's general → accommodation → supervisor chains add coordination overhead and rework risk (B rework 22%, C 24%). Some handovers are unavoidable given specialization, but the volume and the concentration onto scarce staff are a design choice, not an applicant attribute.
- **D's elevated adjusted appeal (+5.7 pp)** despite risk-consistent routing suggests possible first-pass decision-quality issues for D that are not captured by outcome parity.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

At least four, spanning legitimate and concerning:

1. **Legitimate complexity-driven routing (mostly benign).** High-risk and high-prior-case cases (D) genuinely require Fraud/Supervisor Review; accommodation and language requests genuinely require their review steps. This mechanism explains a large share of the raw gaps and part of the path differences.

2. **Capacity bottlenecks at specialized steps (concerning, structural).** Only 7 bilingual caseworkers and 5 accommodation specialists create queues. The delay is caused by under-resourcing of rights-preserving steps, so applicants exercising a legal right are penalized in timeliness. This is unfair even though each individual routing decision is "correct."

3. **Regional backlog interacting with group assignment (concerning).** Two regions have Supervisor Review → Decision backlogs, and B is concentrated on the 3 overloaded regional officers. A shared, group-neutral-looking bottleneck lands disproportionately on one group because of how work is assigned.

4. **Discretionary escalation / over-scrutiny (potentially concerning).** Elevated adjusted Request Missing Documents and Supervisor Review for B/C beyond documented complexity could reflect stricter document standards applied to certain applicants, or communication friction that manufactures "incompleteness." This could be a benign response to genuine ambiguity or a discriminatory pattern; the data cannot distinguish these without more evidence.

5. **Measurement bias in covariates (concerning, methodological).** risk_score_band may encode historical disparities, and documentation_completeness may be depressed by language barriers rather than by true applicant behavior. If so, "controlling for" these variables launders bias into the baseline, making the adjusted disparities *understate* the true unfairness.

6. **First-pass decision-quality / explanation gaps (concerning).** High rework and appeal rates for B/C/D suggest decisions may be less well-explained or less accurate on first pass, generating extra procedural burden and appeals regardless of the eventual outcome.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

- **Measurement bias in covariates.** documentation_completeness is plausibly *endogenous* to language barriers and to the process itself (Request Missing Documents loops). Treating it as an exogenous control can absorb the very disparity we are trying to detect. risk_score_band is explicitly flagged as possibly encoding historical disparities; conditioning on it can mask discrimination.

- **Missing applicant-need attributes.** We lack fields for literacy, digital access, transportation, work constraints, trauma/health status, or quality of prior interactions. These unobserved needs may drive both "incompleteness" and delay, so residual gaps could reflect unmeasured need rather than differential treatment — in either direction.

- **Unobserved resource workload.** Workload is observed only at *daily* granularity, so intraday queueing, individual caseworker load, concurrent caseload, and true bottleneck timing are not resolvable. We can see that B/C funnel onto few workers, but cannot precisely attribute wait to congestion vs. task difficulty.

- **Appeal quality is not captured.** appeal_flag records that an appeal occurred, not whether the original decision was wrong, whether the explanation was adequate, or whether the appeal was resolved fairly. High appeal rates are ambiguous evidence.

- **No dignity/explanation quality.** case_outcome ignores clarity of communication, respectfulness, and comprehensibility — central to procedural fairness but entirely unobserved here.

- **Historical bias in risk scores.** If risk_score_band was trained on or reflects historically biased enforcement, then both the routing it drives (Fraud Review) and its use as a control are contaminated. This is why risk_score_band must not be treated as unquestionably neutral.

- **Aggregation and ecological limits.** Group- and step-level summaries can hide within-group heterogeneity and region-specific effects; conclusions drawn from aggregates may not hold at the individual/cell level.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

- **Add capacity for language support / accommodation review.** *Benefit:* directly attacks the largest, clearly-unfair waits (3.8d, 4.7d). *Side effects/trade-offs:* hiring/training bilingual and specialist staff is slow and costly; poorly trained additions could reduce quality; if capacity is added without demand forecasting, it may under- or over-shoot; must not be used as an excuse to keep other steps under-resourced.

- **Service-level targets for support steps.** *Benefit:* makes rights-preserving delays visible and manageable; aligns incentives so exercising a right is not penalized. *Side effects:* targets can induce gaming (e.g., rushing accommodation review, reducing its quality) or superficial compliance; need paired quality metrics, not just speed.

- **Routing redesign to reduce handovers.** E.g., co-locate bilingual capability within primary caseworkers, or reduce general→specialist→general ping-pong. *Benefit:* fewer handovers, less rework, faster cycle time. *Side effects:* consolidating specialization can dilute expertise; poorly designed redesign may just move the bottleneck; do not remove the substantive review, only the unnecessary hops.

- **Decision-explanation improvements.** Standardize, translate, and clarify notifications and reasons. *Benefit:* likely reduces avoidable appeals/rework (esp. for B/C/D) and improves dignity. *Side effects:* added drafting time per case; templates can become boilerplate that reduces genuine individualized reasoning; needs quality review.

- **Monitoring adjusted disparities.** Stand up ongoing, stratified dashboards of adjusted approval, cycle time, escalation, and appeal gaps by group × region × channel. *Benefit:* early detection, accountability. *Side effects:* monitoring is not correction; risk of "metric theater"; choosing the control set is itself a value-laden decision that can hide or reveal disparities.

- **Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness.** Test risk scores for differential calibration/impact by group; audit whether incompleteness is language-driven. *Benefit:* addresses the root measurement-bias problem that could be laundering discrimination. *Side effects:* may require rebuilding or retiring the risk model (operationally disruptive); audits can be inconclusive; fixing documentation measurement may reveal that current controls were masking larger disparities, complicating prior conclusions.

*Not recommended:* removing or weakening language/accommodation steps to improve cycle time — that would trade a timeliness metric for a substantive rights violation.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

**Indeterminate, leaning toward "procedurally burdensome in specific, identifiable ways" for Groups B and C.** The evidence does not support a conclusion of outcome discrimination: most raw approval gaps are explained by documented case mix, and the residual adjusted approval gaps (2–6 pp) are modest and rest on covariates (risk_score_band, documentation_completeness) that are themselves suspect. So on *outcomes*, the finding is "small residual disadvantage of uncertain interpretation."

On *procedure*, the evidence is stronger and more localizable: rights-preserving support steps for B and C carry the largest waits (3.8d and 4.7d) driven by thin, overloaded capacity and the absence of timeliness targets, and B disproportionately absorbs a regional supervisor-review backlog. These are structural, fixable sources of unequal procedural burden that penalize applicants for exercising language and accommodation rights — a genuine procedural-fairness concern even where individual routing decisions are legitimate. Elevated adjusted appeal/rework for B and D also suggests possible first-pass decision-quality gaps.

I stop short of "unfair" as a global verdict because key determinants — the fairness of the risk score, the true cause of documentation incompleteness, intraday workload, and decision-explanation quality — are unobserved, and any of them could shift interpretation. The responsible conclusion is: **outcomes are approximately explained but not exonerated; procedure shows specific, credible, and remediable inequities in timeliness and resource allocation for the groups routed through under-resourced rights-preserving steps.** Priorities are auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness, adding capacity and timeliness targets for support steps, and monitoring adjusted disparities by region and channel — not weakening the support steps themselves.
