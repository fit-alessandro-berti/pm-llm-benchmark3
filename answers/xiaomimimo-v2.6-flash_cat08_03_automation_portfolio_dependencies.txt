- **Constraint check:** control module  · integrations  · RM friction  (A5 must be RM-designed).

### Portfolio B — Bottleneck / synchronization
**Modules:** A4, A6, A7, A8 · **Deep integrations:** A4, A8 · **Control module:** A4 (enforced KYC re-entry + audit trail)  (arguable)

- **Optimizes:** end-to-end cycle time at the two structural bottlenecks — the critical-path block (`Resolve entity mismatch`, 31 h, 82%) and the packaging AND-join fed by KYC, normalization (20 h), collateral (58 h) and credit notes; biggest waiting-time reduction and the largest analyst-time release (6,500 h).

- **Constraint check:** control module  (defensible but weaker than an explicit rule-checking module) · integrations  · RM friction  (no customer-facing change).

### Portfolio C — Compliance-first
**Modules:** A2, A3, A9, A12 · **Deep integrations:** A9, A3 · **Control module:** A3 + A9 + A12

- **Optimizes:** the highest evidence-weighted leverage points — control findings (A9), the critical-path/scarcest-pool block (A4), the largest rework loop (A5), and the analyst bottleneck + packaging input quality (A6) — while keeping all four builds feasible in one year and keeping customer-friction exposure to a single, co-designed module.
- **Sacrifices:** no Year-1 fix for the 58 h external valuation (A7) or the 44 h signing wait (A10); back-office-visible savings are modest (A1 excluded); A8 deferred until inputs stabilize; A5 requires genuine RM change management.
- **Constraint check:** control module  · integrations  (A9 + A4, both inside six months) · RM friction  managed by co-design.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

## 4.1 Portfolio Q (A1, A5, A9, A10)

- **Cycle time:** improvement concentrated at the **front and tail**, not on the critical path. Removing the 1.5 h registration wait and trimming 22 h contract prep / 44 h signing does not move the packaging AND-join, because that join waits for the slowest of KYC, normalization (20 h), collateral (58 h) and credit notes. Expect a visible drop in *front-end* lead time and little change in p50 end-to-end, with p90 unchanged.
- **Waiting time:** `Register` 1.5 h  near zero; `Contract preparation` 22 h  materially lower; `Customer signing` 44 h tail trimmed by reminders (21% loop ); `Financial document review` 18 h  only indirectly via fewer repeats (52%  ~35–40%). `Resolve entity mismatch` (31 h) and `Wait for external valuation` (58 h) **unchanged**.
- **Rework:** duplicate registrations ; `Req. missing docs  Fin. doc review` return edges (2,800) ; contract-remediation loops (650/510)  via A9's decision-match rules. **Counter-effect:** with A1 live and A2 absent, misroutes (8%) are produced *faster*, raising re-entry at `KYC` (11%) and `Financial document review` (24%).
- **Handovers:** RM  customer  back-office document loops reduced; legal-ops remediation ping-pong reduced. Compliance handovers (KYC ops  2 senior specialists) **unchanged** — still ~1,160 loop edges and a 31 h block.
- **Resource demand:** back office releases 1,600 h inside a pool with afternoon slack  **redeploy, do not downsize**; legal ops releases 4,400 h but is starved by upstream KYC, so savings appear as idle time first; compliance specialists unchanged (3 FTE still binding); RMs relieved of chasing but exposed to A5 instruction rigidity.
- **Control violations:** contract-decision mismatch 8.5% / 14% (Credit-line renewal)  substantially; provisional signing 6.5%   **only if** A10 carries a hard KYC interlock, otherwise ; four-eyes violations 5.8% (High-exposure, month-end) **unchanged** unless A9's rule library includes segregation attestation.

## 4.3 Portfolio C (A2, A3, A9, A12)

- **Cycle time:** modest — gains come from *fewer misroutes and fewer remediation loops*, not from shorter waits. Expect `KYC` (4 h) and `Contract preparation` (22 h) to improve; `Entity mismatch` (31 h), `External valuation` (58 h), `Signing` (44 h), `Normalization` (20 h) unchanged.

## 4.4 Portfolio R (A9, A4, A5, A6) — recommended

# 7. Final recommendation

**Recommend Portfolio R: A9 (contract generation with control-rule checks), A4 (entity-mismatch resolution assistant), A5 (missing-document checklist & portal nudges), and A6 (financial-statement extraction & normalization)** — released in that order across the four waves above, with A9 first to satisfy the compliance requirement and to establish the shared audit/rule pattern, and with exactly two deep core-banking integrations (A9, A4) inside the first six months.

**Why this portfolio:**

1. **It buys the largest evidence-weighted leverage per slot.** A4 addresses a 31 h wait on the critical path in 82% of cases inside the scarcest pool (3 FTE, two senior specialists as the choke point) and closes a real control bypass (`Resolve entity mismatch  Contract preparation: 380`). A5 attacks the process's largest rework structure (52% repeat, 2,800 return edges) at its root cause (incomplete RM instructions). A6 removes 6,500 h of the central hub's manual work and repairs the input quality of the packaging AND-join. A9 delivers both the largest safe labor saving (4,400 h in a 4-FTE pool running at ~69% with a month-end spike) and the explicit control module compliance requires (contract-vs-decision 8.5%/14%, KYC-before-signing 6.5%, four-eyes attestation at 5.8%).
2. **It respects every stated constraint:** four modules; a control-strengthening module in wave 1; two deep integrations in six months; a single, RM-co-designed customer-facing change; no downstream bot built on unreliable upstream controls (A8 and A11 are explicitly deferred behind stability gates).
3. **It converts resource demand deliberately rather than assuming automation frees people:** back-office slack (<65% after 15:00) is re-sequenced and redeployed, not downsized; analyst inflow is governed by a WIP gate; legal ops is reassigned to rule-library maintenance and renewal complexity; compliance specialists get capacity plus cross-training.

**Why the highest-frequency activity is not automated first.** `Register application` (A1, 11,880 cases) is the politically obvious choice and is still the wrong first move:

- **Frequency is not leverage here — the pool arithmetic shows a queue artifact, not a bottleneck.** The listed back-office activities total 4,800 h/yr (5,500 h with rework) against 21,000 h of nominal 12-FTE capacity, and utilization falls below 65% after 15:00. Automating registration converts *visible* waiting (1.5 h) into *invisible* idling; the business case built on FTE reduction will not survive contact with the data.
- **The directly-follows relations show queue transfer, not cycle-time gain.** `Register  Classify: 11,680` — faster registration pushes 11,680 cases into a step with an 8% misroute rate and into a document step with 24% rework, neither of which has demonstrated spare capacity. Without A2, A1 *industrializes misrouting*.
- **It sits on no critical path.** Its 1.5 h wait and 4% rework are immaterial next to `Resolve entity mismatch` (31 h, 82% critical path), `Wait for external valuation` (58 h), `Customer signing` (44 h) and `Credit analysis` (28 h). End-to-end p50 and the control findings — 5.8% four-eyes violations, 6.5% provisional signing, 8.5%/14% contract-decision mismatches — are entirely untouched.
- **Safety requires a pair, and the budget allows four.** A1 is only defensible alongside A2, which would consume two of four slots for front-office-visible but process-marginal benefit while leaving the critical path, the rework loop and the analyst bottleneck unaddressed.

So A1 is **deferred, not rejected**: it becomes the right first item of Year 2 as a paired front-door package (A1 + A2), once classification accuracy is under control and analyst capacity has headroom — at which point accelerating the front door genuinely adds throughput instead of moving the queue.

**Explicit trade-offs accepted:** Year 1 leaves the 58 h external valuation (A7) and the 44 h signing wait (A10) unaddressed, so p90 end-to-end improves more than the maximum; the back-office-visible labor story is weaker than the quick-win portfolio; A5 requires real RM change management; and A3/A12/A8/A11 are all postponed. In exchange we get the control findings fixed by automation rather than by exhortation, the scarcest pool relieved, the largest rework loop removed before it is amplified, and a defensible position with examiners: **automation changes how work is prepared and checked — it does not transfer or reduce compliance accountability, and four-eyes, KYC-before-signing and contract-vs-decision remain human responsibilities enforced by system segregation and audit trail.**
