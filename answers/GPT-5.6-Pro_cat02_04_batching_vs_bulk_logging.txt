# 3. Consequences for performance analysis

**Assumption A1 — clock comparability.** Logged waiting is calculated as `start - queue_arrival`. Because `queue_arrival` comes from an upstream system and lifecycle fields come from local sources, all waiting estimates assume the relevant source clocks are sufficiently aligned. A source-specific clock offset would shift every waiting estimate for that source.

**Scope limitation.** These are single-activity fragments. They cannot establish complete control-flow conformance or a persistent organizational bottleneck. The conformance comments below concern timestamp ordering, lifecycle semantics, resource capacity, and effects that would arise when these fragments are joined to fuller case histories.

## G1

The logged waits are 2–6 minutes, with a median of 4.5 minutes. They plausibly represent item waiting for the fixture to fill or for the next robot cycle, although small cross-system clock errors would matter because the waits are short.

Each item has a three-minute elapsed service interval. That duration is useful at item level, but the four intervals are **not additive resource labor**. Summing them gives 12 robot-minutes inside three wall-clock minutes, or an apparent 400% utilization for a single resource. The correct machine measure is one three-minute occupied cycle, potentially with four of four slots used.

The completion burst of four items at 09:03 is probably valid throughput for a batch-capable cell. Bottleneck analysis must therefore use fixture capacity and cycle frequency rather than interpreting four simultaneous cases as impossible overload. Conformance modelling should preferably represent one batch or multi-object execution; four independent single-capacity transitions would generate a false capacity violation and overcount executions.

## G2

The logged waits to 10:00 are 6–18 minutes. Under the leading interpretation, these are waits to **group initiation**, not necessarily waits to physical inspection. For later items, they are lower bounds on the wait to actual work start.

The service intervals are 8, 14, 21, and 27 minutes and sum to 70 minutes over a 27-minute wall-clock span. A naive one-worker utilization calculation is therefore approximately 259%. Under the serial-work hypothesis, the completion gaps suggest possible active increments of 8, 6, 7, and 6 minutes, totalling 27 minutes. That reconstruction is a hypothesis, not an observation, because individual starts were not captured.

Completion-based throughput may still be reasonably timed: four inspections complete progressively from 10:08 to 10:27. Queue and service decomposition is biased, however: later queue waiting is shifted into “service,” making waiting look too low and service too high. A bottleneck algorithm could falsely diagnose slow inspection execution instead of a queue behind a common setup. Temporal or organizational conformance could also infer four-way parallel work by T-B when the interface merely opened four lifecycle intervals together.

## G3

Logged waiting is relatively stable at 18–22 minutes. These values may be usable if the staggered starts are true work starts, but the prompt does not establish that they are active-work timestamps rather than form-opening times.

The service intervals sum to 67 minutes over a 28-minute wall-clock span, an apparent one-resource load of about 239%. If 11:30 is a folder submission time, earlier reviews have their service duration inflated by the delay between actual finish and folder submission. In that interpretation, 11:30 is an upper bound on individual completion rather than an exact endpoint.

Time-bucketed throughput is distorted into four completions at 11:30 even if reviews actually finished throughout the half-hour. This can create a false release bottleneck, a false end-of-period throughput spike, and excessive utilization. Downstream directly-follows and temporal-conformance relations can also be altered: events after 11:30 may appear to follow all four reviews simultaneously, while the true finish order is lost. `sequence_no` only preserves insertion order and should not be substituted for physical completion order.

## G4

The logged waits are 7, 10, 13, 16, and 19 minutes, increasing as a five-item queue is processed. The intervals are each four minutes and form one uninterrupted 20-minute busy period. Subject to source-clock alignment, both waiting and service decomposition appear credible.

Summed service equals wall-clock occupied time, so single-resource utilization is 100% from 12:05 to 12:25. The local throughput rate is one item every four minutes after processing starts. The pattern supports a temporary backlog, but one burst is insufficient to establish T-D as a persistent bottleneck; arrivals may simply have occurred faster than service for a few minutes.

Temporal and resource conformance are comparatively reliable. The only semantic uncertainty is whether the continuous run should be called an operational “batch” or ordinary FIFO work.

## G5

Logged waits are 15–16 minutes. They can describe pool-level delay before analysis begins, but they cannot be attributed to individual analysts.

The four service intervals sum to 70 minutes over a 30-minute wall-clock span. Treating `analyst_pool` as one worker produces an apparent 233% load. If three analysts were present and every interval represented active work, the period offered at most 90 analyst-minutes and 70 would be feasible in aggregate. Nevertheless, from 13:12 through 13:20 all four intervals overlap, exceeding the supplied maximum of three analysts. At least one interval must include waiting, multitasking, an inaccurate boundary, or work by an unaccounted executor.

Aggregate pool throughput—four completions from 13:20 through 13:30—may be meaningful. Individual productivity, utilization, handover, role, and workload measures are not identifiable. A resource-based bottleneck analysis would invent one overloaded super-resource, while organizational conformance could falsely report one person executing incompatible parallel work.

## G6

The logged waits range from 35 to 175 minutes, with a median of 110 minutes. Every service duration is zero. This is the critical dual interpretation:

1. **Genuine bulk-close scenario:** cases really remain ready in a queue until 17:00, and one bulk transaction closes all seven. Waiting is then operationally real, while zero or sub-minute click-level service is plausible.
2. **Delayed-entry scenario:** cases were worked or effectively closed earlier, but the form copied 17:00 into both lifecycle endpoints. Waiting is inflated because all elapsed time from upstream arrival to data entry is classified as queue time, while active service is collapsed to zero.

Thus, G6-like data can simultaneously create **inflated waiting and near-zero service** by assigning the late submission timestamp to both `start` and `complete`. The same transformation produces zero measured utilization, seven completions in one minute, an apparent end-of-shift queue bottleneck, and a throughput spike. These metrics are not correctable from the fragment alone.

In conformance analysis, the common closure time can hide the true order between closure preparation, resolution, approval, and terminal events. It may also create artificial end-of-shift variants and timestamp ties with unrelated activities.

## G7

The logged waits are 2, −1, −4, −7, and −11 minutes. Four negative waits show that the exact recorded start cannot be combined causally with the upstream arrivals. Clock offset could contribute, but the documented 15-minute granularity and undocumented rounding are sufficient to invalidate point estimates.

All five service durations are logged as 15 minutes. Summing them yields 75 worker-minutes inside a 15-minute wall-clock interval, or a nominal 500% one-worker load. This is not evidence of five-way active work; it is chiefly evidence that the same coarse bucket was assigned to all rows.

Waiting, service, utilization, event order, and within-bucket throughput should all be interval-censored. No symmetric error such as “±7.5 minutes” should be imposed without knowing the rounding rule. Bottleneck detection cannot determine whether the delay occurred before work, during measurement, or in data entry. Conformance ordering based on the equal endpoints or on `sequence_no` would be unjustified.

## G8

Logged waits rise from 38 to 58 minutes, with a median of 48 minutes. The five seven-minute services form a 35-minute serial busy period, so post-15:38 service time, ordering, utilization, and throughput appear internally coherent.

If the safety check actually removed T-H’s capacity, much of the waiting is real but its cause is temporary unavailability rather than slow triage. A bottleneck detector that sees only the repair log would blame the triage queue or resource. Incorporating a synchronized capacity-state event would instead identify a downtime-induced backlog followed by normal seven-minute service.

The causal attribution is not yet secure because the safety and repair systems have different clocks; the upstream queue clock also needs comparison with the scanner clock. Temporal conformance between safety completion and repair restart should therefore use an offset or uncertainty interval, not the displayed timestamps as exact cross-system order.

def pairwise_interval_stats(group):
    """Strict overlap excludes intervals that only touch at one boundary."""
    valid = (
        group.loc[
            group["interval_order_valid"], ["start", "complete"]
        ]
        .dropna()
        .reset_index(drop=True)
    )
    total_pairs = len(valid) * (len(valid) - 1) // 2
    overlaps = 0
    touches = 0

    for i, j in combinations(range(len(valid)), 2):
        s1, e1 = valid.loc[i, ["start", "complete"]]
        s2, e2 = valid.loc[j, ["start", "complete"]]
        overlaps += int(max(s1, s2) < min(e1, e2))
        touches += int(e1 == s2 or e2 == s1)

    return {
        "overlap_pair_rate": (
            overlaps / total_pairs if total_pairs else np.nan
        ),
        "touch_pair_rate": (
            touches / total_pairs if total_pairs else np.nan
        ),
    }


def max_positive_concurrency(group):
    """Maximum number of positive-duration intervals open simultaneously."""
    points = []
    for row in group.loc[group["interval_order_valid"]].itertuples():
        if row.complete > row.start:
            points.extend(
                [(row.start, 1), (row.complete, -1)]
            )

    current = 0
    maximum = 0

    # End before start at a tie, so touching intervals are sequential.
    for _, delta in sorted(points, key=lambda item: (item[0], item[1])):
        current += delta
        maximum = max(maximum, current)

    return maximum


def equality_and_adjacency(group):
    valid = group.loc[group["interval_order_valid"]].sort_values(
        ["start", "complete", "sequence_no"]
    )
    n = len(valid)
    start_sizes = valid.groupby("start", dropna=True).size()
    end_sizes = valid.groupby("complete", dropna=True).size()

    adjacent = (
        sum(
            valid.iloc[i - 1]["complete"] == valid.iloc[i]["start"]
            for i in range(1, n)
        )
        if n > 1
        else 0
    )

    return {
        "start_group_max": (
            int(start_sizes.max()) if not start_sizes.empty else 0
        ),
        "end_group_max": (
            int(end_sizes.max()) if not end_sizes.empty else 0
        ),
        "all_starts_equal": bool(
            n > 1 and valid["start"].nunique() == 1
        ),
        "all_ends_equal": bool(
            n > 1 and valid["complete"].nunique() == 1
        ),
        "adjacency_rate": (
            adjacent / (n - 1) if n > 1 else np.nan
        ),
    }


def queue_build_up(group):
    """
    Treat queue arrivals as +1 and starts as -1.
    A negative balance indicates starts before corresponding upstream arrivals.
    """
    valid = group[["queue_arrival", "start"]].dropna()
    if valid.empty:
        return {
            "peak_queue_balance": np.nan,
            "queue_balance_min": np.nan,
            "arrived_by_first_start": np.nan,
            "last_arrival_to_first_start_min": np.nan,
        }

    points = []
    for row in valid.itertuples(index=False):
        # At an exact tie, process the arrival before the start.
        points.append((row.queue_arrival, 0, 1))
        points.append((row.start, 1, -1))

    balance = 0
    peak = 0
    minimum = 0
    for _, _, delta in sorted(points):
        balance += delta
        peak = max(peak, balance)
        minimum = min(minimum, balance)

    first_start = valid["start"].min()
    return {
        "peak_queue_balance": peak,
        "queue_balance_min": minimum,
        "arrived_by_first_start": int(
            (valid["queue_arrival"] <= first_start).sum()
        ),
        "last_arrival_to_first_start_min": (
            first_start - valid["queue_arrival"].max()
        ).total_seconds()
        / 60,
    }


def granularity_stats(group):
    values = pd.concat(
        [group["start"], group["complete"]]
    ).dropna()

    precision = (
        int(group["precision_sec"].dropna().max())
        if group["precision_sec"].notna().any()
        else np.nan
    )

    epoch_seconds = (
        values.astype("int64") // 10**9
        if not values.empty
        else pd.Series(dtype="int64")
    )

    return {
        "precision_sec": precision,
        "minute_boundary_rate": (
            float(
                (
                    values.dt.second.eq(0)
                    & values.dt.microsecond.eq(0)
                ).mean()
            )
            if not values.empty
            else np.nan
        ),
        "documented_grid_rate": (
            float((epoch_seconds % precision == 0).mean())
            if not values.empty and precision
            else np.nan
        ),
        "unique_starts": int(group["start"].nunique(dropna=True)),
        "unique_ends": int(group["complete"].nunique(dropna=True)),
    }

def pandas_signature(row):
    if row["all_starts_equal"] and row["all_ends_equal"]:
        return "same start+end"
    if row["all_starts_equal"]:
        return "common start"
    if row["all_ends_equal"]:
        return "common end"
    if np.isclose(
        row["adjacency_rate"], 1.0, equal_nan=False

        return "exact sequential"
    if row["overlap_pair_rate"] > 0:

    return "separated/near"

def normalize_pm4py_result(result):
    """
    Convert PM4Py's activity-resource result into a cluster/category mapping.
    The event-count extraction works whether PM4Py stores three- or four-field
    event tuples inside each detected batch.
    """
    detected = {}

    for actres, _, batches_by_type in result:
        activity, resource = actres
        details = []

        for kind, batches in sorted(
            batches_by_type.items(),
            key=lambda item: str(item[0]),
        ):
            label = (
                kind.value
                if hasattr(kind, "value")
                else str(kind)
            )
            sizes = [len(batch[2]) for batch in batches]
            details.append(
                f"{label}[sizes={','.join(map(str, sizes))}]"
            )

        for cluster in actres_to_cluster.get(
            (activity, resource), ["UNMAPPED"]
        ):
            detected.setdefault(cluster, []).extend(details)

    return {
        cluster: "; ".join(details)
        for cluster, details in detected.items()
    }

# 5. Robustness and alternative explanations

## Resource identifiers trusted versus treated as shared

| cluster(s) | IDs trusted as individual or single-capacity resources                                                                              | IDs treated as shared accounts or pooled resources                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| G1         | Four-way concurrency is consistent with the supplied four-slot automated fixture                                                    | A shared machine identifier is still possible, but it does not materially change the batch-cycle interpretation                                                                    |
| G2 and G3  | Logged four-way overlaps require multitasking, multiple open cases, or lifecycle endpoints that do not equal active-work boundaries | Parallel work by several people becomes possible, but the group-start and folder-submission evidence still makes lifecycle batching the better explanation                         |
| G4 and G8  | Exact non-overlap strongly supports one-at-a-time execution by the named worker                                                     | A shared ID is possible but unnecessary; multiple people coincidentally forming one exact chain is less parsimonious                                                               |
| G5         | One analyst would appear to execute four overlapping analyses, which is not credible as uninterrupted active work                   | A pool of up to three analysts explains much of the overlap. The four-way interval from 13:12–13:20 still requires waiting phases, multitasking, timing error, or another executor |
| G6         | One worker appears to close seven cases simultaneously, most naturally through one bulk form or copied lifecycle values             | Several workers could have done earlier work under one account, strengthening the late-entry interpretation; the 17:00 transaction could still be one clerk’s bulk action          |
| G7         | One worker appears to perform five full 15-minute measurements in the same quarter-hour                                             | Shared identity could permit physical parallelism, but the 15-minute rounding remains sufficient to prevent a concurrency conclusion                                               |

The robustness result is asymmetric: treating IDs as shared weakens conclusions based solely on overlap, but it does **not** explain source-specific signatures such as G2’s common group start, G3’s common folder submission, G6’s manual bulk form, or G7’s quarter-hour rounding.

## Exact timestamps versus precision-censored timestamps

| timestamp assumption                                               | consequence                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| One-second fields are exact and clocks aligned                     | G1’s simultaneous cycle and G4/G8’s sequential chains are strong. Their short recording lags reinforce immediate event generation                                                                                                                                                                  |
| One-second fields have fine granularity but unknown clock accuracy | Within-source service ordering remains strong, but queue waiting and cross-system causal alignment can be shifted by clock offsets                                                                                                                                                                 |
| One-minute fields are exact to the displayed minute                | G2, G3, G5, and G6 have the exact PM4Py geometries shown                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| One-minute fields are interval-censored                            | G2’s starts and G3’s ends mean “same recorded minute,” not necessarily the same second. G5’s overlaps remain largely robust because they last many minutes. G6’s zero durations may conceal small positive durations, but this does not resolve whether the rows represent execution or data entry |
| G7’s quarter-hour fields are exact                                 | The log produces negative waits and physically implausible five-way load                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| G7’s fields are interval-censored under an unknown rounding rule   | Exact ordering, waiting, service, and concurrency become unidentified. This is the only defensible quantitative treatment until the rounding rule is recovered                                                                                                                                     |

A smaller PM4Py merge tolerance is not a substitute for interval censoring. Rounded values can remain exactly equal at every tolerance.

## Temporal patterns with multiple physical explanations

| same temporal pattern                   | defensible interpretations                                                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Identical starts and ends               | G1: physical machine batch; G6: one bulk transaction or late data entry; G7: quarter-hour coarsening                                                      |
| Common start with staggered ends        | G2: genuine common setup, one UI group-start action, simultaneous starts with different durations, or sequential work whose individual starts are missing |
| Staggered starts with common end        | G3: genuine synchronized release, folder-level completion, delayed submission, or several cases held open until a common decision                         |
| Irregular overlap under one resource ID | G5: several analysts under a shared account, one analyst multitasking, intervals containing inactive waiting, or inaccurate lifecycle fields              |
| Exact sequential adjacency              | G4: intentional serial batch; G8: recovery run after capacity loss; either could also be ordinary uninterrupted FIFO processing                           |

# 6. Data-collection redesign

| priority | redesign                                                 | required fields or controls                                                                                                                                                                           | analytical benefit                                                                                                                          |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| P0       | Separate execution and recording semantics               | Preserve `queue_arrival_at`, `work_started_at`, `work_finished_at`, `submitted_at`, and `recorded_at` as different immutable fields. Never fill an unknown start by copying completion                | Distinguishes queue time, active work, post-work delay, and data-entry delay; directly prevents the G3/G6 ambiguity                         |
| P0       | Make source clocks auditable                             | Record source clock ID, timezone, UTC-converted time, synchronization status, estimated offset, offset uncertainty, and the last synchronization time                                                 | Makes cross-system waits and the G8 safety-to-repair relation testable rather than assumed                                                  |
| P0       | Separate worker, account, pool, and workstation identity | Store pseudonymous badge/person ID, login ID, resource-pool ID, workstation ID, and executing device ID separately                                                                                    | Prevents G5-like shared accounts from being interpreted as individual multitasking or overload                                              |
| P1       | Capture explicit group and batch identity                | Add fixture-cycle ID, inspection-group ID, review-folder ID, bulk-action ID, item position, group size, group start/end, and the relationship of each case to the group                               | Distinguishes a physical batch, a UI lifecycle group, a bulk database transaction, and one multi-object event fanned out into several cases |
| P1       | Record workstation capacity and availability             | Maintain parallel-slot capacity, staffing level, active operator count, machine mode, maintenance, safety checks, outages, breaks, and capacity-change intervals                                      | Allows utilization to be capacity-weighted and separates service bottlenecks from temporary capacity loss                                   |
| P1       | Capture active versus inactive lifecycle phases          | Add pause, resume, awaiting-information, background-processing, handoff, and interruption events with reason codes                                                                                    | Prevents long open intervals from being treated as uninterrupted active labor, especially in G3 and G5                                      |
| P1       | Make precision metadata field-specific                   | For every timestamp field, retain documented precision, observed precision, rounding direction, rounding function, source timezone, and a lower/upper admissible timestamp when exact time is unknown | Supports principled interval censoring rather than arbitrary tie-breaking or assumed symmetric errors                                       |
| P2       | Preserve event lineage and corrections                   | Store immutable source event ID, source sequence number, ingestion ID, schema version, correction version, late-arrival flag, and whether rows were generated by fan-out from one transaction         | Makes it possible to distinguish physical ordering from database insertion order and to audit later corrections                             |
| P2       | Add automated quality controls                           | Flag starts before upstream arrivals beyond clock uncertainty, concurrency exceeding known capacity, excessive exact-tie rates, completion-to-recording lag changes, and mass zero-duration events    | Detects G5–G7-like failure modes before they enter production performance dashboards                                                        |

A small validation study should pair source events with direct observation or a trusted device trace. The objective is not to collect permanent surveillance data, but to establish the semantics of each timestamp, the rounding rules, whether group buttons create copied starts, and whether bulk forms overwrite lifecycle fields.

# 7. Final adjudication

**High-confidence physical interpretations:** G1 is a four-item machine-cycle batch, subject to the distinction between one cycle and four case rows. G4 is a genuine one-at-a-time execution chain. These two clusters can enter performance analysis after using the appropriate capacity model.

**High-confidence data-generation diagnoses but incomplete physical semantics:** G2 is a common-start lifecycle group; G3 is a common-end or submission group; G5 is a shared-identity artifact; G7 is quarter-hour-coarsened data. The temporal diagnosis is strong, but the exact allocation of active work, waiting, and concurrency is not.

**Conditionally usable queue analysis:**

* G1 queue waiting is usable only after checking upstream-to-PLC clock alignment and should be interpreted as waiting for a batch cycle.
* G2’s queue-to-10:00 measure is a wait to group initiation and, under serial execution, a lower bound on later items’ wait to physical work.
* G3’s start-based waiting may be retained provisionally, but individual completion and service should be upper-censored at the folder submission.
* G4’s queue and service measures are the strongest conventional measurements in the data.
* G5 can support pool-level queue analysis, not per-analyst utilization or workload attribution.
* G8’s seven-minute services and post-15:38 sequence are usable; upstream waiting and the safety-check explanation require clock validation.

**Exclude or interval-censor:** G6 should be excluded from service-time, utilization, bottleneck, and queue-delay estimation until investigators determine whether `queue_arrival` means “ready to close” and whether 17:00 is an execution or entry time. G7 should be excluded from exact-time queue analysis and represented only through timestamp uncertainty intervals. Its negative waits must not be repaired by sorting on `sequence_no`.

**Operational validation required before causal conclusions:** G2 needs individual-start validation; G3 needs actual finish timestamps; G5 needs badge and pause/resume evidence; G6 needs bulk-action and readiness semantics; G8 needs clock-offset and capacity-blocking validation. G7 needs the rounding rule before any quantitative reuse.

PM4Py can correctly describe the recorded geometries—especially G1/G6/G7 as “Simultaneous”—while those three clusters represent respectively a plausible physical batch, an ambiguous logging or transaction batch, and a coarsening artifact. Therefore, no PM4Py batch label alone is sufficient evidence of physical batching, concurrency, utilization, or a bottleneck.
