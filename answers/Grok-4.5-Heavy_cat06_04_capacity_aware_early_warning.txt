**## 1. Decision rule**

Eligibility is determined by four sequential filters before any prioritization:

1. Stage must be Triage or Diagnosis Started (the Senior Diagnostic Swarm has near-zero effect after Diagnosis Complete).
2. No prior swarm on the case, or a material new event has occurred since the last assessment (new customer diagnostic log, confirmed live-queue change, major status update, or external escalation). Pure model-refresh noise does not qualify.
3. Data quality is acceptable. Cases with critical missing or heavily imputed live features are eligible only after elevated evidence or explicit human review.
4. Robust net benefit is non-negative after costs and externalities:  
   \( RNB_{lo} = ARR_{lo} \times Value - €450 - False\text{-}alarm\,burden - spillover\,buffer \)  
   where \( ARR_{lo} \) is the lower end of the supplied absolute-risk-reduction range and the spillover buffer is approximately €1.5–2.5 k per slot (reflecting 0.01–0.03 extra breach probability on roughly six untreated diagnosis-stage cases). Extreme urgency (time-to-SLA < 2 h) can override a near-zero \( RNB_{lo} \), but not a clearly negative one.

Calibration robustification of the observational probabilities is applied for secondary urgency scoring only:
- SME: \( p_{adj} = p_{now} + 0.06 \) (midpoint of documented under-prediction).
- Strategic Enterprise: \( p_{adj} = p_{now} - 0.08 \).
- Public Sector: no shift.
- Non-Native-Language Support: no point shift; expand the ARR uncertainty band by ~50 % or use only the lower half of the supplied range and raise the \( RNB \) threshold.

Primary ranking never relies on a single probability threshold. Instead cases are scored as  
\( Score = RNB_{lo} \times urgency\,factor \times confidence\,factor \),  
where urgency factor = 1.4 if time-to-SLA < 4 h or stage = Diagnosis Started (1.2 if < 6 h, else 1.0), and confidence factor = 1.0 (Public / complete data), 0.85 (SE / complete), 0.55 (NNLS or material imputation).  

Eligible cases are ranked by this score. At most three of the four remaining slots are allocated; one slot is retained as reserve unless a single case presents exceptional high-confidence \( RNB > €5\,k \). The rule is fully deployable, treats intervention effects as uncertain ranges rather than known causal quantities, and incorporates stage constraints, value heterogeneity, false-alarm costs, calibration bias, data quality, and queue externalities.

**## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy**

Cool-down / suppression  
- After any alert (intervened or not), suppress further alerts on the same case for a minimum of 90 minutes or two full model-refresh cycles, whichever is longer, unless a material new event is logged.  
- If \( |p_{now}-p_{prev}| < 0.05 \) and no new process event, diagnostic log or status change is recorded, treat the refresh as non-informative and keep the case suppressed.  
- After a swarm is delivered, impose a hard cool-down of at least 4 hours or until the case passes Diagnosis Complete; repeated swarms are forbidden without a material new event.

Escalation and re-entry  
- Re-entry is permitted only on documented material new evidence. On re-entry the full eligibility and \( RNB_{lo} \) calculation is recomputed from the updated features; prior priority is not inherited.  
- Cases that were eligible but unselected because of capacity may be soft-re-evaluated at the next refresh, subject to mild priority decay to avoid thrashing.

Explicit comparison of I-05 and I-07  
- I-05 generated a third alert with \( \Delta p = +0.02 \) and “no new event since previous alert.” Per the rule it is suppressed. Although SME under-prediction raises true risk and the ARR range is attractive, the absence of incremental evidence means the repeated alert adds no new information; intervening would violate the material-event exception, consume scarce capacity, and risk alert fatigue. Ordinary handling continues under cool-down.  
- I-07 also produced a third alert, but a new customer diagnostic log constitutes material new evidence. Suppression is therefore lifted and the case is re-evaluated. Recomputed \( RNB_{lo} \approx 0.20 \times €5\,k - 450 - 50 - \) buffer ≈ €0.5 k after spillover remains too low relative to opportunity cost, so the case stays “do not intervene” under current capacity. The new evidence correctly restored eligibility; future refreshes that raise risk or ARR can re-enter the ranking if a slot becomes available. The modest decline in predicted probability does not override the new-event rule.

The same cool-down, material-change and re-computation logic applies to all future cases, balancing responsiveness against alert fatigue and wasted specialist time.

**## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks**

Value-based prioritization systematically risks starving lower-value segments (SME, lower-tier Public, some NNLS). In the present portfolio I-07 (SME, €5 k, ultra-urgent) and I-05/I-10 are deprioritized even though SME under-prediction implies higher true risk; over repeated cycles this can produce higher realized breach rates for those customers.

NNLS cases (I-09) carry elevated uncertainty in both calibration and effect estimates, so they are more likely to be left untreated even when observational risk is high, potentially compounding existing language-support disparities.

Differential calibration (SME under-prediction, SE over-prediction) means an unadjusted policy would under-serve SME and over-serve Strategic Enterprise; the explicit segment offsets and confidence weights mitigate but do not eliminate the distortion.

Selective intervention alters future training labels: successfully intervened cases exhibit lower realized breach rates, so the observational model may learn that high-\( p \) cases “resolve” more readily, biasing subsequent predictions downward for similar profiles or attributing success to the wrong features. The bias is especially dangerous if intervention propensity is correlated with segment or value.

Spillover harms fall on the untreated diagnosis-stage cases. Because prioritization already de-selects lower-value or higher-uncertainty cases, the residual untreated pool (often SME, Public or NNLS) absorbs the extra 0.01–0.03 breach probability and the 8–20 min (or more) aggregate delay, creating a double disadvantage.

Concrete safeguards  
- Soft floor: at least one of every four slots is preferentially available to eligible SME / Public / NNLS cases whose \( RNB_{lo} \) exceeds −€500 after buffer.  
- Weekly segment-stratified dashboards of intervention rate, breach rate (intervened and untreated), and post-spillover outcomes; automatic flag if SME or NNLS breach rate exceeds SE by a pre-defined margin after calibration adjustment.  
- Causal-effect monitoring via inverse-propensity or doubly-robust estimators; when residual capacity exists, a small randomized non-intervention shadow set preserves unbiased labels. Intervention indicators are retained as features or the model is re-specified causally.  
- Explicit logging of which untreated cases incurred delay; if excess risk concentrates on particular segments, the prioritization weights or the maximum number of slots used are adjusted.  
- Quarterly fairness audit (allocation parity, equalized odds on breaches, value-weighted versus unweighted metrics) with mandatory human review for any rule change that widens disparity beyond a threshold.  
- For NNLS: prioritized feature enrichment and wider uncertainty bands until the validation sample grows.

**## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan**

Operational monitoring (daily dashboard)  
Slot utilization, specialist interruption time, realized aggregate delay per used slot versus the 8–20 min expectation, number of repeated alerts suppressed, reserve-slot usage (especially for regulatory arrivals), false-alarm customer complaints, and stage-at-intervention versus subsequent outcome.

Predictive monitoring  
Calibration plots and expected calibration error by segment (SME, SE, Public, NNLS), by stage and by time-to-SLA. Track \( p_{prev} \) versus \( p_{now} \) stability and imputation frequency. Segment-specific isotonic or Platt recalibration is triggered monthly or whenever ECE exceeds 0.05. The SME / SE offset values themselves are updated weekly from recent realized outcomes.

Causal-effect estimation  
- Matching or difference-in-differences of intervened versus similar non-intervened cases, conditioned on stage, predicted risk and segment.  
- When residual capacity exists, a small randomized fraction of borderline cases supplies an unbiased effect estimate.  
- Slot availability / congestion can serve as an instrument.  
- The supplied ARR ranges are revised quarterly; if the observed mid-effect falls outside the prior range the \( RNB \) thresholds and confidence weights are updated.

Detection of policy-induced drift  
Compare feature distributions and outcome rates before versus after policy deployment. If intervened cases systematically lower the observed breach labels, apply sample re-weighting or explicit intervention modeling before the next retrain.

Capacity and spillover monitoring  
Queue length at Diagnosis, realized spillover magnitude (breach rate of untreated diagnosis-stage cases versus their original predictions), and specialist utilization. If congestion produces spillover > 0.03 or delay > 20 min, temporarily reduce the maximum number of slots to two or raise the \( RNB \) threshold. Track the opportunity cost of unused reserve slots.

Fairness monitoring  
Segment-stratified intervention rates, breach rates (both intervened and untreated), and distribution of net benefit. Alerts fire if the value-based gap widens or if untreated groups show excess harm.

Rule-change triggers  
If cumulative estimated net impact (avoided breaches × value − costs − estimated spillover harm) is negative over two consecutive weeks, if any segment calibration bias shifts by more than 0.05, if fairness disparity exceeds the audit threshold, or if updated ARR estimates reorder the ranking, the \( RNB \) formula, reserve policy or cool-down parameters are re-optimized via offline simulation on recent data. A joint operations + data-science review occurs monthly; emergency recalibration is triggered by regulatory incidents or major process redesigns. Every decision is logged with the full feature vector for auditability.
