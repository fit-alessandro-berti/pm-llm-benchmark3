## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated (evidence establishes):**

- M0 enforces: (1) `Register_Supplier`  (concurrent `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`)  `XOR(Manual_Procurement_ApprovalActivate_Supplier, Reject_Supplier)`  `Notify_Requester`  `Close_Case`. No loop, no `Request_More_Info`, no `Auto_Approve`, no emergency path, no resource/identity rules.
- CS-9 (N1) is the only controlled standard. It mandates: both checks before activation; manual approval before activation; requester  manual approver; it is silent on rejected-lead scope and on reactivation.
- PD-3 (N2) is a *pilot*, in force 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31, with explicit eligibility (LOW-risk, DE-domiciled, in approved corporate group). The Compliance observer did not approve weakening CS-9.
- FD-7 (N3) is a *time-bounded* emergency directive, 2026-07-10 00:00 to 2026-07-12 23:59, with EUR 20,000 spend cap, retrospective approval required, daily monitoring.
- The CRM migration (N4) began 2026-07-01; only ~70% of notifications are mirrored; older case links are mutable text fields.
- Aggregate Auto_Approve usage (312 in June/July): 289 PD-3-eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing eligibility attributes.
- Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% post-migration.

**Disputed propositions (depend on authority, scope, or logging interpretation):**

1. Whether a low-risk pilot (PD-3) authorizes permanent *normalization* of `Auto_Approve` (SUP-714) — depends on whether pilot language can outrun its window.
2. Whether FD-7's factual condition (flood) extends its *legal* effect past 2026-07-12 (SUP-705) — depends on whether emergency directives carry implicit extension.
3. Whether rejected leads are *in scope* of M0 (SUP-709) — depends on a scope document that is itself ambiguous.
4. Whether SUP-710 (reactivation) is a CS-9 case or a separate procedure CS-9 does not cover.
5. Whether SUP-708 (40-minute pre-sanctions activation) is logging error, transaction-misattribution, or genuine violation.
6. Whether SUP-703 (HIGH-risk, EUR 180,000 under "pilot") is a pilot misuse or a CS-9 violation that pilot language cannot excuse.
7. Whether SUP-712 (same human, two aliases) and SUP-715 (spouses in same reporting line) are *control-flow* defects or *resource/CoI* defects M0 cannot express.

**Burden of proof.**

- *Declaring noncompliance:* the prosecution must show, by case, that the observed behavior (a) is within M0's documented scope, (b) deviates from a controlled rule (CS-9, FD-7, PD-3) still in force at the time, and (c) is not better explained by a model/scope/data defect. Mere trace deviation from M0 is *not* enough if M0 is itself defective.
- *Accepting an exception:* the defense must show a *contemporaneous, identifiable authority* (CS-9 carve-out, FD-7 text, PD-3 eligibility), with eligibility evidence, and must show the exception was *bounded* (time, amount, risk class). Subjective belief that a practice is "desirable" or that an emergency "factually existed" is insufficient.

This asymmetry is intentional: deviations from a controlled standard carry the heavier burden because the standard is the only artifact with formal approval. The defense can shift that burden only with documented authority and complete eligibility evidence.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**A. Formal authority and expiry.** CS-9 is the only controlled standard; PD-3 and FD-7 are *time-bounded exceptions* whose effect ends by their own terms. After 2026-07-12 FD-7 is dead text; after 2026-07-31 PD-3 is dead text. SUP-705 (2026-07-13) and SUP-714 (2026-08-02) cannot rely on either. The Compliance observer (N2) explicitly disclaimed weakening CS-9. Where a controlled standard is silent (reactivation, rejected-lead scope, CoI), the absence of a rule is not a permission — it is an open question to be resolved by amendment, not by operator choice.

**B. High-risk and clearly ineligible cases.** SUP-703 is the worst: HIGH-risk foreign supplier, EUR 180,000 spend, marked "pilot" despite clear PD-3 ineligibility (foreign, HIGH-risk). This is not a borderline call — it is a deliberate use of pilot language to bypass CS-9's manual approval. SUP-708 activates 40 minutes *before* Sanctions_Check completes, with no emergency or pilot in effect and no transactions in the gap; if logging is correct, this is the only case in the set that places activation before a *required* control. The 14 "clearly ineligible" cases in aggregate, plus SUP-714, show that pilot eligibility is not a gating mechanism in the operational system — it is a label.

**C. Normalization of exceptions.** The strongest prosecution evidence is *pattern*, not individual case. 312 Auto_Approve cases in 60 days, with 14 clearly ineligible, shows the pilot is not a controlled exception but a default path. Operations' request for a "permanent low-risk auto-approval branch" (N5) confirms that PD-3 is being treated as a precedent rather than a window. Post-pilot usage on 2026-08-02 (SUP-714) demonstrates that the operational system has *already* decided PD-3 is permanent. This is precisely the failure mode CS-9 exists to prevent: informal normalization of an exception into a de facto policy.

**D. Concurrency is real but partial.** M0's `Sanctions_Check || Quality_Review` is process-correct for the standard route. Prosecution concedes (and §3 develops) that SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, SUP-713, and most of SUP-702 are not CS-9 violations on the control-flow dimension. But SUP-706, SUP-712, and SUP-715 are CS-9 violations on *resource/identity* grounds that M0 simply cannot detect — which is an argument for *stronger* enforcement, not weaker.

**E. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy.** Operations' "Request_More_Info is desirable" (N5, SUP-707) is a legitimate *amendment proposal*, not a compliance defense. The 47 cases with `Request_More_Info` and zero premature activations is evidence that the loop should be *formalized*, not that its current informal use satisfies CS-9.

**F. Audit signal.** Internal Audit flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe. The fact that Audit sees these and not the rest of the field is consistent with the prosecution thesis: the dominant failure mode is *not* a uniformly broken process but specific cases where the pilot/exception machinery was abused or bypassed.

**Prosecution conclusion.** The operation is materially noncompliant. CS-9 is the controlled standard; deviations from it require contemporaneous, bounded authority. SUP-703 and SUP-708 are severe; SUP-705 and SUP-714 show exception creep; the 14 clearly ineligible Auto_Approve cases in aggregate show the pilot is not gated. M0's inability to express loops, identity, and overlays is a *reason to strengthen* M0, not a reason to relax CS-9.

## 3. Defense Brief

**A. M0 is incomplete by construction.** M0 encodes only control-flow. It cannot express: loops (SUP-707), the resource rule that requester  approver (SUP-712, N1), CoI rules (SUP-715), label renaming with approved equivalence (SUP-713), time-bounded overlays (PD-3, FD-7), eligibility predicates, or amount caps. A deviation from M0 is therefore *evidence of model incompleteness* until the deviation is checked against CS-9 directly. A naive conformance check against M0 would flag SUP-707 as noncompliant when it is a desirable, well-evidenced deviation; that is a defect in the model, not in the operation.

**B. Concurrency vs. false concurrency.** M0 declares `Sanctions_Check || Quality_Review`, but in practice the two are commonly run sequentially in either order (the "checks" partial order admits it). Treating them as a hard parallel is itself a model artifact. SUP-708's ordering may be a logging artifact of a system that records activity start, not ordering; the prosecution's strongest read depends on whether `Sanctions_Check` in SUP-708's position reflects a *completed* check or a *re-attestation* in CRM. The defense cannot prove this from the evidence, but neither can the prosecution prove the violation.

**C. PD-3 is a controlled overlay with a defined window.** SUP-702 (2026-06-15, LOW-risk DE, approved corporate group, quality review next business day, CRM notification with stable `notification_id`) is *exactly* what PD-3 authorized. Of 312 Auto_Approve cases, 289 — 92.6% — are PD-3-eligible by aggregate evidence. A pilot that achieves 92.6% compliance with its own eligibility rule is not a failed pilot; it is a pilot whose residual error rate is concentrated in a small set of clearly ineligible cases that the prosecution has correctly identified. The defense does not defend SUP-703; it defends the 289.

**D. FD-7 is a controlled overlay with a defined window.** SUP-704 (2026-07-11, EUR 18,000 under cap, daily monitoring, retrospective approval next business day) is *exactly* what FD-7 authorized. The prosecution's strongest read of SUP-705 (2026-07-13, EUR 12,000) is that FD-7 had expired; the defense's strongest read is that the factual emergency (flood) persisted and operations reasonably continued a controlled pattern. The defense *concedes* this is a close call and that a written extension or a CS-9 amendment should have been issued. The concession is that the case is *under-documented*, not that the operation is in bad faith.

**E. Scope and identity disputes are not control-flow violations.** SUP-709 (rejected lead) and SUP-710 (reactivation) are *scope* questions. CS-9 is silent on both. The scope document is ambiguous. Until a controlled scope rule exists, the defense's position is that M0's reach is narrower than the prosecution assumes. SUP-712 and SUP-715 are *resource/CoI* questions; CS-9 names the requester-approver rule and CoI policy names the disclosure rule, but neither is in M0. The absence is a model defect, not operator misconduct.

**F. Data quality is itself a defect.** SUP-706 (notification missing from log, present in CRM by mutable text field entered two days later) and SUP-711 (duplicate `Register_Supplier` from migration) are *logging* artifacts of the N4 CRM migration. SUP-713 is a label rename (QA_Clearance  Quality_Review) approved retrospectively. Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% post-migration (N4). A conformance check that uses the log as ground truth is checking the *log*, not the operation; until the log is reconciled, half of the "deviations" are data defects.

**G. Desirability is evidence of operational reality, not of policy.** Operations' request for a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch is a *legitimate amendment proposal*. The 47 `Request_More_Info` cases with zero premature activations is evidence the loop is well-controlled informally and should be formalized. The prosecution's "desirable  compliant" is correct as a matter of authority, but the defense's counter is that *uncontrolled* good practice is itself a compliance risk — because it depends on individuals, not the model.

**Defense conclusion.** M0 is materially obsolete as a conformance check. It cannot detect resource/CoI violations (SUP-712, SUP-715), cannot express desirable loops (SUP-707), cannot encode overlays (SUP-702, SUP-704), and cannot be safely checked against a log whose missingness is 29% (SUP-706, SUP-711). Most observed cases are either compliant with CS-9, justified by PD-3 or FD-7, or the result of a model/scope/data defect. The handful that remain (SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-714) are real and require action — but the system-level response should be *model and governance revision*, not blanket enforcement of an obsolete M0.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

A single POWL is insufficient. The revised architecture is layered by concern and by enforcement mechanism.

**Layer 1 — Control-flow (POWL).** The base process. Encodes what is *structurally* possible. Cannot enforce risk class, amounts, dates, or identity. This is the only layer M0 currently covers.

**Layer 2 — Resource and identity rules (DECLARE / log-skeleton / organizational controls).** Requester  approver; CoI disclosure gates; actor-alias resolution; delegation evidence. These are *binary* existence rules and are awkward in POWL; they belong in DECLARE-style constraints or in a pre-flight identity check that *gates* the transition (e.g., `Manual_Procurement_Approval` is enabled only if `actor(requester)  actor(approver)` and `CoI_disclosure == present`).

**Layer 3 — Eligibility, amount, and time predicates (pandas/data checks at case-open and at each gated transition).** PD-3 eligibility (`risk_class == LOW  domicile == DE  in_approved_group == True  now  [PD3.start, PD3.end]`); FD-7 amount cap (`cumulative_spend  20000` until retrospective approval); CS-9 risk class; pilot/emergency authority IDs. These are evaluated at the case-open event and re-evaluated at each gate.

**Layer 4 — Policy-overlay governance (organizational controls + versioned registry).** Every overlay (PD-3, FD-7, future amendments) has: `policy_id`, `version`, `effective_start`, `effective_end`, `authority_actor`, `eligibility_predicate`, `amount_cap`, `override_allowed`, `record_timestamp`. The case carries a `policy_snapshot_id` taken at `Register_Supplier` so that mid-flight policy changes do not retroactively alter a case's rule set. An overlay cannot extend silently; expiration is automatic.

**Layer 5 — Scope rules (organizational controls).** Rejected leads: defined as intake cases that terminate at `Reject_Supplier`; the *full* M0 applies only to leads that pass pre-screen and are accepted as cases (or, alternatively, the pre-screen is added as an explicit `Pre_Screen_Acceptance` gate that the partial order requires before `Register_Supplier`). Reactivation: a separate case type with a `Reactivation` transition; requires reference to a prior closed case and re-validation of sanctions and certification only if `cert_valid_until < now`. Withdrawal: `Withdraw_Supplier` as a silent or labeled transition reachable at any gated point before `Activate_Supplier`.

**Layer 6 — Data collection and reconciliation (pandas/event log).** Stable `notification_id` mirrored from CRM with write-once semantics; rule/version IDs on every event; eligibility snapshot at `Register_Supplier`; actor alias table; delegation evidence; emergency/pilot authority ID; immutable effective and record timestamps; de-duplication by source transaction ID.

**What goes where:**

- **POWL:** control-flow only (Layer 1). Loops via `OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, ...)`; concurrency via `StrictPartialOrder`; XOR for the decision; explicit pre-screen gate.
- **DECLARE / log-skeleton:** resource and identity constraints (Layer 2).
- **pandas / data checks:** eligibility, amount, time (Layer 3), and the case-level policy snapshot.
- **Organizational controls + versioned registry:** overlays and scope (Layers 4–5).
- **Event log schema:** Layer 6.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The POWL below encodes the *control-flow* faithfully. The accompanying policy-overlay table and event-log schema encode what POWL cannot.

**Design notes and explicit limitations:**

- `Request_More_Info` is encoded as a `LOOP` with a silent "exit" body so the loop can be taken zero or more times. This is the standard POWL idiom for an optional repeatable step.
- The decision is an `XOR` over three branches: standard manual approval, low-risk auto-approval (pilot/permanent), and emergency provisional. The emergency branch *requires* retrospective approval and quality review; this is encoded as a `StrictPartialOrder` after the provisional activation, not as concurrency, so the *direction* of the dependency is preserved.
- Rejection is encoded as a labeled transition reachable from the post-checks decision. Pre-screen rejection (SUP-709) is *outside* the main case flow and is modeled as a separate small POWL for the pre-screen intake, made explicit so the scope ambiguity is closed.
- The notification-before-closure ordering is preserved. The POWL cannot guarantee notification *occurred*; that is a data-layer check on `notification_id` and `notification_mirror == True`.
- Resource/identity (requester  approver, CoI), eligibility (PD-3, FD-7), amount caps, and time windows are *not* in the POWL. They are in the overlay table and pandas checks. Encoding them in the POWL would either explode the state space or require labeled guards that PM4Py does not evaluate.

```python
"""
Revised supplier-onboarding control-flow model.
POWL encodes structure only. Eligibility, identity, amount, time, and overlays
are enforced by the accompanying policy-overlay table and data checks.
"""

from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Atomic activities
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
register          = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions         = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality           = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
manual_approval   = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve      = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate          = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
retro_approval    = Transition(label="Retrospective_Approval")
notify            = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close             = Transition(label="Close_Case")
reject            = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
withdraw          = Transition(label="Withdraw_Supplier")
reactivation_gate = Transition(label="Reactivation_Reference")  # for reactivation cases


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard checks: concurrent sanctions and quality
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
# No edges: concurrent.


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Standard branch: manual approval then activate
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
standard_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
standard_branch.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Low-risk auto-approval branch: pilot / permanent
# Quality is permitted AFTER provisional activation in the auto branch,
# consistent with PD-3 semantics. The POWL expresses only the ordering
# (auto before activate; quality may be concurrent with notify/close
# only in the sense that the auto-branch does not require QR-before-activate).
# Eligibility is enforced in Layer 3.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
auto_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_approve, activate])
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)
# NOTE: Quality_Review in the auto branch is expressed at the case level
# by placing `quality` as concurrent with `activate` only inside the auto
# route, which we model by reusing `quality` in a dedicated partial order
# below. To keep the XOR children distinct and to make the policy
# distinction visible, we use a dedicated node composition.


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Emergency/provisional branch: activate before QR and before manual
# approval, with retrospective approval and QR required after.
# FD-7 amount cap and authority are enforced in Layer 3.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[activate, retro_approval, quality]
)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(activate, retro_approval)
emergency_branch.order.add_edge(activate, quality)
# No edge between retro_approval and quality: they may be completed in
# either order after activation, both required before notification.


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Decision: XOR over three approved paths + a silent skip for the case
# where the emergency/auto branch is not selected but the standard branch
# is also not appropriate (defensive: should be unreachable in well-formed
# cases; if reached, the case is force-closed).
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_branch, auto_branch, emergency_branch],
)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Request_More_Info loop: a LOOP whose "do" body is the decision
# structure and whose "redo" is a single Request_More_Info transition.
# The silent exit allows zero iterations (i.e., straight to the
# post-decision flow) and the labeled body is the actual case work.
# This is the standard POWL idiom for an optional repeatable step.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
request_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
silent_exit  = SilentTransition()

# The loop body is the checks+decision; the loop "redo" is request_info.
loop_body = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks, decision])
loop_body.order.add_edge(checks, decision)

info_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[loop_body, request_info],
)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Post-decision: notification then closure, with rejection as an
# alternative terminal that also notifies and closes.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
reject_path = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[reject, notify, close])
reject_path.order.add_edge(reject, notify)
reject_path.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# Withdraw may be invoked from the loop or before notification.
# Modeled as a silent transition reachable in parallel with the main flow.
# In POWL this is awkward; we expose it as a labeled transition the case
# may take instead of the decision-notify-close path. Operationally,
# a withdrawal gate is enforced at the data layer; here we keep the
# control-flow explicit by including it as a sibling.
withdraw_path = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[withdraw, notify, close])
withdraw_path.order.add_edge(withdraw, notify)
withdraw_path.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# Closure for the main (non-rejected, non-withdrawn) path:
approved_close = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[notify, close])
approved_close.order.add_edge(notify, close)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Top-level model
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, info_loop, reject_path, withdraw_path, approved_close]
)
M1.order.add_edge(register, info_loop)
# After the loop, the case proceeds along exactly one of three terminal
# paths. In POWL we cannot directly express "exactly one of these three"
# with a single XOR that also contains a loop; instead, we model the
# terminal choice as a post-loop XOR:
terminal_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[approved_close, reject_path, withdraw_path],
)
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, info_loop, terminal_choice])
M1.order.add_edge(register, info_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(info_loop, terminal_choice)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Reactivation: a separate, smaller model entered when a prior closed
# case is referenced. Reactivation skips Register_Supplier and re-validates
# sanctions and (if cert expired) quality. CS-9 does not cover this; the
# model below is the proposed normative reactivation flow.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
reactivation_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])
reactivation_checks  # concurrent by default; cert_validity predicate
                     # (in Layer 3) decides whether quality is required.

reactivation_decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[standard_branch, reject],
)
reactivation_flow = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[reactivation_gate, reactivation_checks,
           reactivation_decision, approved_close]
)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivation_gate, reactivation_checks)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivation_checks, reactivation_decision)
reactivation_flow.order.add_edge(reactivation_decision, approved_close)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Pre-screen intake: a separate small model for rejected leads
# (SUP-709). Closes the scope ambiguity by making pre-screen a
# first-class flow rather than an implicit "before M0" state.
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
pre_screen_accept = Transition(label="Pre_Screen_Acceptance")
pre_screen_reject = Transition(label="Pre_Screen_Reject")
pre_screen_close  = Transition(label="Close_Intake")
pre_screen = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[pre_screen_accept, pre_screen_reject],
)
# If accepted, the case proceeds to M1 (Register_Supplier is the entry).
pre_screen_intake = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[pre_screen, pre_screen_close]
)
pre_screen_intake.order.add_edge(pre_screen, pre_screen_close)
# Accepted leads hand off to M1 by a synchronization that PM4Py cannot
# natively express; this is enforced by the case-id continuity rule in
# Layer 6 (data).
```

### Event-log schema (Layer 6)

Each event row carries, in addition to standard XES attributes:

- `case_id` (stable, propagated across pre-screen and main flow)
- `source_transaction_id` (for de-duplication; SUP-711)
- `event_label` (canonicalized via the label-equivalence table; SUP-713)
- `actor_id`, `actor_aliases[]` (resolved against the alias table; SUP-712)
- `policy_snapshot_id` (immutable; references the overlay row active at `Register_Supplier` or `Reactivation_Reference`)
- `eligibility_snapshot` (JSON: `risk_class`, `domicile`, `in_approved_group`, `coi_disclosure_present`, `reporting_line_conflict`)
- `notification_id` (stable, write-once, mirrored from CRM; SUP-706)
- `notification_mirror` (boolean; N4)
- `amount_cumulative_eur` (for FD-7 cap; SUP-704, SUP-705)
- `effective_timestamp`, `record_timestamp` (immutable; for audit)
- `rule_version_id` (the CS-9 / PD-3 / FD-7 / amendment version that gated this transition)
- `delegation_evidence` (if the actor acted by delegation)
- `emergency_authority_id` / `pilot_authority_id` (which overlay authorized this transition)

### Data checks (pandas, illustrative)

A conformance check should not be a single POWL replay. It should be a join of the event log against the overlay table at each gated transition, evaluated against the case's `policy_snapshot_id`. The minimal checks are:

1. **At `Register_Supplier`:** create the policy snapshot; record the overlay(s) in force; record the eligibility snapshot.
2. **At `Auto_Approve`:** require the active overlay to be PD-3 (or a future permanent amendment) and the eligibility snapshot to satisfy `risk_class == LOW  domicile == DE  in_approved_group == True`. If the overlay is missing or expired, this is a NONCOMPLIANT event regardless of trace shape.
3. **At `Activate_Supplier` (emergency branch):** require `FD-7.in_force(now) == True` *or* a future emergency overlay; require `amount_cumulative_eur  20000`; require `Retrospective_Approval` and `Quality_Review` to occur before `Notify_Requester`.
4. **At `Manual_Procurement_Approval`:** require `actor(requester)  actor(approver)`; require `coi_disclosure_present == True`; require `reporting_line_conflict == False`. SUP-712 and SUP-715 fail here.
5. **At `Notify_Requester`:** require `notification_mirror == True` *and* a write-once `notification_id`. SUP-706 fails here pending reconciliation.
6. **At `Close_Case`:** require a successful notification event in the same case.
7. **De-duplication:** collapse events with identical `source_transaction_id`; SUP-711 collapses cleanly.
8. **Reactivation:** require `Reactivation_Reference` event with a `prior_case_id` whose `Close_Case` exists; re-validate sanctions and certification per the reactivation amendment.

This layered architecture lets POWL do what it is good at (control-flow structure) while pushing the predicates it cannot evaluate into layers that are designed for them. The prosecution's strongest cases (SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, SUP-715) and the defense's strongest cases (SUP-702, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-709, SUP-710, SUP-713) are all reachable by this architecture, and the residual ambiguity is reduced to the question each case actually depends on — *which policy was in force, with which eligibility, for which actor* — rather than to the question of whether M0's structure can express the case.
