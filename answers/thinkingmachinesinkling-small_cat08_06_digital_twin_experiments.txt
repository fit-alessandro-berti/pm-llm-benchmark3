| **D3** | District-batched inspection scheduling | Batch window 48h; bundle by district; travel reduction 30% | Reduced travel/service; increased synchronization wait for first case in bundle |
| **D4** | Temporary peak-month inspector | +1 inspector Mar–May and Sep–Nov; calendar-limited | Shorter inspection queues in peaks; idle cost in low season |
| **D5** | Parallel legal pre-check for Commercial / Complex | Start legal workflow after Planning (not after Drafting) | Reduces legal queue at end; risk of rework if planning changes |
| **D6** | Reserve one planner daily for aging cases >30d | Priority queue for aging | Reduces tail/backlog; may delay new cases slightly |
| **D7** | Architect-facing appointment slots | Smooth Monday burst to spread over week; uptake 80% | Lower queue spikes after Mon; requires applicant compliance |
| **D8** | Environmental-review triage (district + attributes) | Triage to fast/slow track; 30% fast-track for low-risk | Reduced env queue; risk of misclassification |
| **D9** | Public-notice objection early-warning | Early detection reduces mean extension from 46d to 20d | Faster notice closure; requires external monitoring cost |
| **D10** | Combined: D1 + D2 + D3 + D4 | All above jointly | Synergies (D1 reduces loop  D3 bundles cleaner); diminishing returns on resources |
| **S1** | Demand surge (+30% above peak) | Arrival rate 2.2× baseline in peak | Stress on all queues; test robustness |
| **S2** | Resource shock (2 planners on leave 4 weeks) | Planner capacity 25% temporarily | Bottleneck shift to Planning; backlog growth |
| **S3** | Objection crisis (mean notice doubles 3 months) | Objection extension factor 2× | Tests D9 and general resilience; long-tail blowout |

5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

- **Warm-up:** 90 days (approx. 3 months). Initialize with empty queues but seed with historical case mix and resource states from reconstructed calendars. Because demand is non-stationary, statistics are collected over full seasonal cycles; warm-up is used only to reach representative queue depth, not steady-state equilibrium.
- **Run length:** 540 days (18 months) match log; plus optional 180-day extension for tail observation.
- **Replications:** 30 replications per experiment, using Common Random Numbers (CRN) across paired experiments (Baseline vs. intervention) to reduce variance.
- **Random seeds:** Fixed seed streams: `seed_arrival`, `seed_service`, `seed_routing`. Each replication gets `seed + rep`.
- **Uncertainty reporting:** For median and mean durations, report 95% CI via replication batch means or percentile bootstrap. For heavy-tailed metrics (p90, p95, max), report median of p90 across replications with inter-quartile range; do not report mean of extremes because distributions are heavy-tailed.
- **Service/wait split uncertainty:** Use sensitivity ranges around service-time parameters (±20%) and propagate through simulation; report tornado plots for key outputs.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Vary (a) seasonality factor ±10%, (b) missing-doc response mean ±30%, (c) objection probability ±20%. Evaluate which interventions remain robust.

6. Validation strategy

- **Face validation:** Present process tree, resource calendars, and output distribution summaries to municipal staff; validate branch logic (e.g., fire required conditions, inspection batching rules).
- **Event-log replay:** Replay historical cases through the discovered process tree (`pm4py.parse_process_tree`) and compare simulated paths to actual variant sequences; measure path-match rate.
- **Distributional validation:** Compare simulated inter-arrival times to observed monthly/week patterns using chi-square; compare simulated service times (where observed) to empirical median/p90 via Q-Q plots.
- **Resource-utilization validation:** Compare simulated planner/inspector utilization by month to reconstructed HR calendars; ensure peaks align with 1.7× demand.
- **Bottleneck validation:** Identify top 3 waiting activities in both log (via `discover_performance_dfg` waiting time estimates) and simulation; confirm simulation reproduces queue build-up after Monday submissions and in peak months.
- **Out-of-sample validation:** Hold out last 3 months of 18-month log; calibrate on first 15 months; predict aggregate case counts, variant shares, median duration by type, and queue lengths. Target MAPE <15% for case counts and <20% for median durations.
- **Cross-case dependency check:** Validate inspection bundle size and travel-time reduction against district-level aggregated data.

7. Decision criteria

We do not claim exact optimality; we treat results as estimates with model error.

**Primary metrics (by case type):**
- Median end-to-end duration; target reductions relative to baseline (Simple 12d  <10d; Complex 140d  <120d).
- p90 / p95 tail duration.

**SLA / compliance:**
- % cases completed within statutory targets by type (e.g., Simple <30d, Complex <180d).

**Fairness / equity:**
- Coefficient of variation of median duration across districts D3/D5 vs others.
- Gini coefficient of waiting time by `architect_id` (prevent burst penalties).

**Resource health:**
- Utilization by resource type: target 75–85% sustained; flag sustained >95%.
- Queue growth rate in peak months.

**Process quality:**
- Rework rate (rework events / total cases) reduction 20%.
- Backlog size: cases waiting > threshold (e.g., >median duration).

**Robustness:**
- Metric degradation under S1–S3; prefer interventions where p90 increases <10% under stress.

**Selection approach:** Multi-criteria decision analysis (weighted score) or Pareto frontier across median duration, tail, fairness, and resource utilization. If trade-offs conflict (e.g., D3 improves median but hurts fairness for early-bundle cases), present to decision-makers rather than auto-selecting.
