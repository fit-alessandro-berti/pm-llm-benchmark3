## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

**Deadline loss.** The benefit of meeting the deadline is €20–35k in avoided operational loss. Because approval is only 0.55–0.75 and a conformant path may end in rejection, the expected value is about €12–25k. Under plans that miss the deadline, the customer faces a prolonged hold with €15–80k of harm. This matters because the prolonged-hold harm can exceed the deadline value. Plans that miss the deadline are not costless, so they must be compared on the same basis.

**False rejection or hold.** Rejection or a hold on a true positive avoids the sanctions exposure. Rejection or a hold on a false positive costs €15–80k. Rejection is therefore a real trade-off, not a free default. Rank 3 is conditional for this reason.

**Regulatory exposure.** An unlawful execution carries €100–500k+ plus non-monetary consequences. That exposure is a prohibition rather than an ordinary cost, so it should be treated as a dominance constraint rather than as an expected-value term.

**Dominance arguments.**
- A and B are dominated by any conformant path that has a non-trivial chance of resolving the case. Their benefit is €20–35k. The break-even probability of a violation, taking the €100–500k exposure at face value, is about 4–35%. The fuzzy match of 0.46 means a violation cannot be excluded at anything near that level. Non-monetary harm and the prohibition itself push the break-even lower still.
- C+D dominates D-S4 under the base-case cost estimates. It secures the deadline and avoids a prolonged hold, and its P-901 cost is bounded and quantifiable. The flip condition is stated in Section 5.
- E is dominated on deadline terms but dominant on resolution quality. It is a backstop, not the primary route.

**Non-monetary harm.** An unlawful execution and any mishandling of a suspicious-activity report carry asymmetric non-monetary harm. Customer-facing messages must not disclose the report (rule 11).

**Why A and B cannot become recommended through a favorable forecast.** The approval probability of 0.55–0.75 is an observational estimate of outcomes under the normal process, including the second review. It does not describe what happens when the second review is removed, and it cannot satisfy a rule. Rules 6, 7, and 9 are preconditions for execution, not inputs to an optimization. A forecast that says a shortcut is "probably fine" does not create an approval path. Under the stated intervention, the prediction does not transport: removing review changes both the outcome and the detection of a false clear.

**What would make F valid.** A documented rejection threshold that the case actually meets. Examples: a confirmed or sufficiently strong sanctions match, or a rejection rule that applies when a fuzzy match stays unresolved past a defined point. The rationale must be recorded with reason codes. R17 or the second officer should document it. A rejection is an outcome, not a payment authorization.

**What would make G valid.** A Compliance Director's actual authorization, recorded with a genuine incident identifier that is created and justified. The incident must be real. A deadline or operational pressure does not qualify. Manufacturing an incident to unlock an override is not acceptable.

**What constrains C and D.** S2 or S4 must be a person other than R17. The reviewer must review the evidence independently, not simply confirm R17's conclusion. The score refresh must use the standard scoring service, not a selective re-run.

## 5. Resource spillovers

**S2 versus S4.**
- S2 is available now, but only by interrupting P-901. Its review (35–75 min plus 5–15 min to record) fits within the 90-minute deadline (total 40–90 min). The fit is tight: the worst case has no slack.
- S4 becomes available in 35–65 min. Adding a review of similar duration, completion falls at about 70–140 min. The deadline is missed in most cases.
- Using S2 secures P-884's deadline but imposes a P-901 cost of 0.12–0.28 added breach probability. Using S4 avoids that cost but almost certainly causes an SLA breach for P-884.

**Effect on P-901.** Interruption is a real cost that must be quantified against P-901's own exposure. P-901's value is not given. The trade-off is:
- Gain from S2: roughly €12–25k expected deadline value plus reduced prolonged-hold harm.
- Cost of S2: 0.12–0.28 × P-901's breach cost.

If P-901's breach cost is comparable to P-884's, S2 is preferred. If P-901 is much larger, D-S4 becomes preferable.

To reduce P-901 spillover, ask S2 for a checkpoint handoff if P-901 is at a step that permits it. Return S2 to P-901 immediately if the lapse condition is met.

**Alternate-screening quota.** Three expedited calls remain. Use one now, since the screen directly affects whether a second review is needed. Hold the remaining two in reserve for a conclusive follow-up, such as contradicting evidence from the first call. Do not spend them just to shorten the decision.

**Time risk of waiting for an exception.** Waiting on G consumes the same time window as C or D. G should not be the critical path. Escalate to the Compliance Director for awareness in parallel, but do not hold P-884 for an override decision.

**How the ranking changes with S4 timing.**
- **S4 at 35 minutes:** D-S4 becomes more attractive because the decision is earlier and the deadline is reachable in the fast tail only. Rank 2 stays at D-S4 but the gap to Rank 1 narrows. The S2 interruption still gives the deadline its best chance.
- **S4 at 65 minutes:** D-S4 is effectively a multi-hour hold. Rank 2 moves to F (conditional rejection), provided the documented threshold is met and the screen has not cleared the match. If the threshold is not met, E moves to Rank 2.
- **Flip condition for Rank 1:** if P-901's breach cost, multiplied by 0.12–0.28, exceeds the expected deadline gain from S2, D-S4 becomes Rank 1.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary recommendation (Rank 1):** C+D composite.
1. Now: request a fresh score re-run through the standard scoring service. Start the alternate sanctions screen (API call 1 of 3).
2. Now: interrupt P-901 and request S2's independent expedited review of P-884. Confirm S2  R17.
3. At screen return (about 15–30 min): if the match is cleared and the fresh score is below 70, stand S2 down and return them to P-901. R17 repeats the analyst review and, if approved, the case proceeds to execution. Otherwise S2 continues with the screen result.
4. Execution is permitted only after a valid S2 approval (or the lapse branch). Record any decision within 15 minutes of S2's decision.

**Fallback:**
- If S2's review is not complete by about 80 minutes, or if S2 is unavailable: use S4 as the second reviewer.
- If the documented rejection threshold is met and no approval has been recorded, use F (rejection with rationale).
- If neither applies, hold and send a generic delay notice to the customer. Do not disclose the sanctions basis or any report (rule 11).
- Escalate to the Compliance Director for awareness. Do not seek an override unless a genuine incident exists.

**Confidence.** Moderate (about 0.5–0.6) that Rank 1 is the best achievable outcome. High confidence (above 0.9) that A and B are nonconformant and must be excluded. The main sensitivity is P-901's unknown value.

**Events that should trigger immediate reranking:**
1. The fresh score returns. A value below 70 combined with a cleared match triggers the lapse branch and cancels the S2 requirement. A value at or above 70 keeps the S2 review in force.
2. The alternate screen result. A confirmed true match moves the case toward reject or hold and a SAR assessment. A false match with an unresolved residual moves the case toward the lapse or continue branch.
3. S2 confirms the interruption and gives a time estimate. If the estimate exceeds 75 minutes, reconsider Rank 2.
4. S4's confirmed availability time. A time of 35 minutes or less keeps D-S4 in contention. A time of 65 minutes or more promotes F or E.
5. P-901's current status and breach-cost estimate. If P-901's exposure is materially larger than P-884's, reconsider the S2 interruption and move D-S4 to Rank 1.
6. Any evidence that the rejection threshold for F is met, or that the match is confirmed, which would promote F.
