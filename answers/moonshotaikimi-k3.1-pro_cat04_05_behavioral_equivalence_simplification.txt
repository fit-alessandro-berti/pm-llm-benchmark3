# 1. Equivalence Notions

Let \(L(M)\) denote the complete visible language of model \(M\): each word comes from a run from the initial to the final state, with `tau` and other silent transitions omitted. Partial executions are not included.

Duplicate labels are **not** merged. There is no persistent activity-instance identity in these models: comparison is by label, multiplicity, and position in the ordered word. Thus, a word containing two `Check Revision` occurrences differs from an otherwise identical word containing one. P1 may additionally annotate an occurrence with its owning revision attempt.

- **Visible trace equivalence after hiding `tau`:**  
  Models A and B are equivalent iff \(L(A)=L(B)\). Equal activity sets, equal minimum trace lengths, or similar diagrams are insufficient.

- **Bounded visible-language equivalence under P3:**  
  For \(N=\texttt{Analyze Revision}\), define
  \[
  L_{\mathrm{P3}}(M)=\{w\in L(M): |w|\le 12,\ \#_N(w)\le 3\}.
  \]
  The models are P3-equivalent iff \(L_{\mathrm{P3}}(A)=L_{\mathrm{P3}}(B)\). Open and close events count toward the 12-event bound. Bounded equality would not prove unbounded equality; a differing bounded word is nevertheless a genuine unbounded counterexample.

- **Business-relevant equivalence under P1:**  
  The conservative P1 observation retains the complete visible word, attempt boundaries, check ownership, and every approval occurrence and position. A compliant run has attempts separated by `Rework Case`, and every attempt has a check of its own before the `Rework Case` ending that attempt, or before `Close Case` for the final attempt. One check cannot be reused to satisfy two attempts. Approvals are never hidden or merged.  
  Two models are P1-equivalent only if they induce the same business-observable behavior and compliance boundary. A coarser quotient may be adopted explicitly, but it must not erase missing-check violations. Even the weaker question “do both models guarantee P1 compliance?” distinguishes A from B.

- **Abstracted dashboard equivalence under P2:**  
  After checking that a complete word starts with `Open Case`, ends with `Close Case`, and contains both analysis and checking, project it to
  \[
  D(w)=
  \bigl(
    \#_{\texttt{Analyze Revision}}(w),
    \mathbf 1[\#_{\texttt{Check Revision}}(w)>0],
    \mathbf 1[\#_{\texttt{Rework Case}}(w)>0]
  \bigr).
  \]
  Approvals, silent behavior, check multiplicity, and analysis/check interleaving are discarded. P2-equivalence means equality of the sets of possible dashboard projections, not equality of trace frequencies or probabilities.

# 2. Structural Comparison

| Feature | Model A | Model B | Semantic consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loop scope | Each iteration contains `Analyze Revision`  `Check Revision`, followed by optional approval. | The loop contains only analysis followed by optional approval; the single check is outside and parallel to the whole loop. | A repeats checking with each attempt; B never repeats checking. |
| Minimum/maximum checks in a complete trace | Minimum 1; for \(n\) attempts there are exactly \(n\) checks, so the maximum is unbounded. | Exactly 1, independently of the number of attempts. | Check multiplicity immediately separates almost all multi-attempt traces. |
| Possible check placement | Within each attempt, before or after its analysis, but after neither the optional approval nor the attempt-ending rework/close. | Any interleaving position after `Open Case` and before `Close Case`, subject to the loop’s internal order: before the first analysis, between analysis and approval, around reworks, or after the loop has finished. | B can defer its only check until every analysis and rework has occurred. |
| Analysis and rework structure | At least one analysis; \(n\) attempts have exactly \(n-1\) reworks separating them. | Same. | This part is identical and explains why the P2 analysis/rework projections coincide. |
| Optional approval occurrences | Between 0 and \(n\), at most one per attempt; each occurs after both that attempt’s analysis and check. | Between 0 and \(n\), at most one per attempt; each occurs after its analysis, but the global check may occur before or after it. | B admits approval-before-check interleavings impossible in A. |
| Concurrency | Analysis/check concurrency is local to one revision attempt. | One check is concurrent with the entire revision loop, potentially spanning several attempts and reworks. | Similar activity sets conceal substantially different causal scope. |
| Join before `Close Case` | Each local analysis/check join precedes optional approval; the completed loop precedes close. | The outer parallel join requires both the global check and the completed loop before close. | Both guarantee open-first/close-last and at least one check, but for different reasons. |

# 3. Verdicts

The unbounded visible trace languages are also not equivalent.

| View | Verdict | Confidence | Decisive reason | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **not equivalent** | High; structural and trace-based | A provides a per-attempt check before the attempt boundary. B has only one check and admits multi-attempt runs in which an analysis is followed by rework without any intervening check. | A permits check-before-analysis within an attempt. If “check a revision” requires analysis to precede checking chronologically, A itself needs strengthening. |
| P2 | **equivalent only under stated abstraction** | High; exact unbounded projection argument | For either model, the possible projections are \(\{(n,\mathrm{true},\mathbf 1[n>1]):n\ge1\}\). | This concerns possible dashboard records only, not compliance, trace language, probabilities, or frequencies. |
| P3 | **not equivalent** | High; witnesses lie well inside the bound | There are witnesses unique to each model with at most nine visible events and at most two analyses. | Exhaustive bounded equality elsewhere would still not prove unbounded equality. # 4. Counterexample Traces

Use the abbreviations:

- `O` = `Open Case`
- `N` = `Analyze Revision`
- `C` = `Check Revision`
- `P` = `Approve Revision`
- `R` = `Rework Case`
- `Z` = `Close Case`

Every displayed word below is a **complete** visible trace, with `tau` omitted. All four satisfy the P3 bounds.

A useful exact characterization is:

- In A, every attempt block is one of `N C`, `C N`, `N C P`, or `C N P`; blocks are separated by `R`.
- In B, first form the loop word `(N P?) R (N P?) R … (N P?)`, then insert the single `C` at any visible interleaving position.

| Trace ID | Trace | Model A | Model B | First decisive semantic difference | Relevant view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | `O N C R N C Z` | Admits | Rejects | Two attempts require two checks in A; B can emit only one check. | P1, P3 |
| A2 | `O C N P R N C P Z` | Admits | Rejects | A has two attempt-local checks and two post-join approvals; B cannot produce two checks. | P1, P3 |
| B1 | `O N P C Z` | Rejects | Admits | B inserts its global check after `N P`; A cannot execute `P` until both `N` and `C` have completed. | P1 approval placement, P3 |
| B2 | `O N R N C Z` | Rejects | Admits | B defers its only check until after both analyses. The first analysis reaches `R` without a check; A would also need a second check. | P1, P3 |

There is no hidden language inclusion:

- For \(n\ge2\), an A-trace has \(n\) checks while a B-trace has one, so their \(n\)-attempt words are disjoint.
- For \(n=1\), A has four words: the two analysis/check orders, each with or without a final approval. B has those same four plus `O N P C Z`.

These are not P2 counterexamples. For example, A1 and B2 both project to `(2, true, true)`: two analyses, some check, and some rework. B1 projects to `(1, true, false)`, just like an A trace such as `O N C Z`.

`X('Approve Revision', tau)` makes approval independently optional in each attempt. Hiding `tau` removes only the silent event; it does not remove the surrounding analysis, check, rework, or control-flow constraints. Consequently:

- both models allow 0 through \(n\) visible approvals;
- `tau` cannot manufacture B’s missing checks or move A’s approval before its analysis/check join;
- P2 discards approval-only differences, whereas P3 retains them, making B1 especially important.

```python
import pm4py
from collections import deque
from pm4py.objects.petri_net import semantics

TREE_A = """
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      +('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision'),
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
"""

TREE_B = """
->(
  'Open Case',
  +(
    'Check Revision',
    *(
      ->(
        'Analyze Revision',
        X('Approve Revision', tau)
      ),
      'Rework Case'
    )
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
"""

tree_a = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_A)
tree_b = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE_B)

accepting_nets = {}

for name, tree in [("A", tree_a), ("B", tree_b)]:
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    sound = pm4py.check_soundness(
        net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True
    )
    print(f"Model {name} sound:", sound)
    if not sound:
        raise RuntimeError(f"Model {name} failed the soundness check")
    accepting_nets[name] = (net, im, fm)


def marking_key(marking):
    """Canonical, hashable representation of the complete marking."""
    return tuple(
        sorted(
            (str(place.name), int(count))
            for place, count in marking.items()
            if count
        )
    )


def bounded_visible_language(
    net,
    im,
    fm,
    max_visible=12,
    max_analyze=3,
):
    """
    Return exactly the complete visible words within both bounds,
    provided this exploration runs to completion.

    A search state contains:
      - the actual Petri-net marking;
      - the visible word produced so far.

    The visited key contains the canonical marking and the visible word.
    Silent cycles therefore cannot cause indefinite revisiting, while
    distinct visible histories are not incorrectly merged.
    """
    analyze_label = "Analyze Revision"
    final_key = marking_key(fm)

    queue = deque([(im, tuple())])
    visited = {(marking_key(im), tuple())}
    accepted = set()

    while queue:
        marking, word = queue.popleft()

        # Acceptance requires equality with the entire final marking.
        if marking_key(marking) == final_key:
            accepted.add(word)

        enabled = pm4py.get_enabled_transitions(net, marking)

        # Sorting is only for reproducible exploration order.
        for transition in sorted(enabled, key=lambda t: str(t.name)):
            label = transition.label

            # Standard PM4Py conversion uses label=None for silent
            # transitions. The additional normalization covers versions
            # that expose an explicit tau label.
            is_silent = (
                label is None
                or str(label).strip().lower() in {"tau", "silent"}
            )

            if is_silent:
                new_word = word
            else:
                visible_label = str(label)
                new_word = word + (visible_label,)

                # Bounds are monotone, so an over-bound prefix can be pruned.
                if len(new_word) > max_visible:
                    continue
                if new_word.count(analyze_label) > max_analyze:
                    continue

            next_marking = semantics.execute(
                transition, net, marking
            )
            state_key = (marking_key(next_marking), new_word)

            if state_key not in visited:
                visited.add(state_key)
                queue.append((next_marking, new_word))

    return accepted


lang_a = bounded_visible_language(*accepting_nets["A"])
lang_b = bounded_visible_language(*accepting_nets["B"])

print("Bounded complete visible language sizes:")
print("  A:", len(lang_a))
print("  B:", len(lang_b))
print("  Intersection:", len(lang_a & lang_b))


def show_words(title, words):
    print(f"\n{title}: {len(words)}")
    for word in sorted(words, key=lambda w: (len(w), w)):
        print("  " + " | ".join(word))


# Required output: bounded traces unique to each model.
show_words("Unique to A", lang_a - lang_b)
show_words("Unique to B", lang_b - lang_a)

# Independent checks of three short witnesses.
O = "Open Case"
N = "Analyze Revision"
C = "Check Revision"
P = "Approve Revision"
R = "Rework Case"
Z = "Close Case"

witness_a = (O, N, C, R, N, C, Z)
witness_b1 = (O, N, P, C, Z)
witness_b2 = (O, N, R, N, C, Z)

assert witness_b1 in lang_b and witness_b1 not in lang_a
assert witness_b2 in lang_b and witness_b2 not in lang_a
```

This exploration is exhaustive for the stated bounds because future enabled behavior depends only on the current marking, while the remaining visible-event budgets are determined by the stored word. Merging two paths only when both marking and visible word coincide is therefore language-preserving.

By contrast, `pm4py.play_out(...)` would provide a sample unless its particular algorithm and exhaustion guarantees were separately established. An empty difference observed after random or heuristic playout would not prove even bounded language equality.

# 6. Stochastic and Footprint Comparison

**Footprints** can help localize ordering, concurrency, start/end, or loop-related differences. In particular, they may expose that A’s analysis/check relationship is attempt-local while B’s check has a much wider concurrent scope.

Footprints are summaries, not complete trace languages. Even equal footprints would not prove that checks have the right multiplicity, that non-local loop constraints coincide, or that approvals occur in the same positions. Conversely, a footprint difference is useful diagnostic evidence but does not replace exhibiting or exhaustively checking complete traces.

**Stochastic languages and EMD** require an additional probabilistic observation model. The process-tree strings specify neither choice probabilities nor loop-exit probabilities. In particular, treating all choices as equally likely would be an extra assumption, not a fact about the business process.

|---|---:|---:|

Structural counts can be reproduced with:

```python
from collections import Counter


def normalize_label(label):
    if label is None:
        return None
    return str(label).strip().strip("'\"")


def tree_stats(root):
    nodes = []

    def visit(node, depth):
        nodes.append((node, depth))
        for child in (node.children or []):
            visit(child, depth + 1)

    visit(root, 0)
    leaves = [node for node, _ in nodes if not node.children]

    def is_silent_leaf(node):
        label = normalize_label(getattr(node, "label", None))
        return label is None or label.lower() in {"tau", "silent"}

    visible_labels = [
        normalize_label(node.label)
        for node in leaves
        if not is_silent_leaf(node)
    ]
    counts = Counter(visible_labels)

    return {
        "nodes": len(nodes),
        "depth": max(depth for _, depth in nodes),
        "operators": len(nodes) - len(leaves),
        "visible_leaves": len(visible_labels),
        "silent_leaves": sum(is_silent_leaf(x) for x in leaves),
        "static_duplicate_labels": sum(
            count - 1 for count in counts.values() if count > 1
        ),
    }


print("A structural counts:", tree_stats(tree_a))
print("B structural counts:", tree_stats(tree_b))

# Compare converted-net simplicity using the same PM4Py installation.
for name, tree in [("A", tree_a), ("B", tree_b)]:
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)
    simplicity = pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm)
    print(f"Converted-net simplicity {name}:", simplicity)
```

No numerical simplicity values are asserted here: they should be measured with the same PM4Py version. Converted nets may contain additional structural places, arcs, and silent transitions, so tree node count alone does not determine that metric.

# 8. Simplified Model

The chosen target is a **P1-canonical compromise**, while also preserving P2. It is not intended to preserve strict P3 trace languages.

The additional design decision is to stop treating `N C` and `C N` within one attempt as distinct desirable business behavior, and to require the operationally natural order **analysis, then check**. All attempt counts, rework boundaries, and approval choices remain.

```text
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      'Analyze Revision',
      'Check Revision',
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
```

```python
import pm4py

SIMPLIFIED_TREE = """
->(
  'Open Case',
  *(
    ->(
      'Analyze Revision',
      'Check Revision',
      X('Approve Revision', tau)
    ),
    'Rework Case'
  ),
  'Close Case'
)
"""

simplified_model = pm4py.parse_process_tree(SIMPLIFIED_TREE)

    ),

```

Let \(S\) denote this model. Its complete visible words have exactly the form

\[
O\;(NCP?)\;\bigl(R\;NCP?\bigr)^{n-1}\;Z,
\qquad n\ge1,
\]

where each optional `P` is chosen independently.

This gives two useful formal facts:

1. \(L(S)\subsetneq L(A)\): every simplified trace is an A trace, but A additionally admits check-before-analysis interleavings such as `O C N Z`.
2. If \(\gamma\) canonicalizes each A attempt by replacing `C N` with `N C`, leaving approvals and boundaries unchanged, then \(\gamma(L(A))=L(S)\). Thus the simplification is exact under the stated P1-canonical quotient, not under strict visible-trace equivalence.

It is only a **modest structural simplification** relative to A:

| Metric | A | B | Simplified S |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Nodes | 12 | 12 | 11 |
| Maximum depth | 4 | 5 | 4 |
| Operator nodes | 5 | 5 | 4 |
| Visible/silent leaves | 6 / 1 | 6 / 1 | 6 / 1 |
| Static duplicate visible labels | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The main simplification is removal of the inner concurrency operator, not a large reduction in activities.

| Property | Preservation status | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Open first, close last; at least one attempt | **Exact** | Retained by the outer sequence and mandatory first loop body. |
| Rework separates attempts | **Exact** | There are still exactly \(n-1\) reworks for \(n\) attempts. |
| One own check per attempt before rework/close | **Exact, with stronger ordering** | Every attempt now performs `N`, then `C`, before its boundary. |
| Optional approval number and attempt placement | **Exact under the P1-canonical target** | For every \(n\), every approval vector in \(\{0,1\}^n\) remains realizable; each approval remains after analysis/check and before rework/close. |
| A’s analysis/check interleavings | **Abstracted** | `C N` is canonicalized to `N C`; exact concurrency is removed. |
| Strict visible-trace equivalence with A | **Weakened to a proper refinement** | \(L(S)\subsetneq L(A)\). If `O C N Z` must remain legal, S is not an acceptable replacement. |
| P2 dashboard projection | **Exact** | S also has projections \(\{(n,\mathrm{true},\mathbf 1[n>1]):n\ge1\}\). |
| P3 bounded visible language | **Lost as an equivalence claim** | Approval labels remain, but check order and multiplicity differ from A or B on bounded traces. |
| B’s global-check behavior | **Lost intentionally** | Multi-attempt behavior with only one check is excluded. |

Regression traces for S:

| Type | Trace | Expected result for S | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regression 1 | `O N C Z` | Accept | Minimum attempt, auto-approved through silent choice |
| Regression 2 | `O N C P Z` | Accept | Single visible approval |
| Regression 3 | `O N C R N C Z` | Accept | Two attempts, one separating rework, two checks |
| Regression 4 | `O N C P R N C Z` | Accept | Mixed approved/auto-approved attempts |
| Negative 1 | `O C N Z` | Reject | Check-before-analysis order intentionally removed; A admits this |
| Negative 2 | `O N C R N Z` | Reject | Second attempt has no check before close; B admits this visible word |
| Negative 3 | `O N P C Z` | Reject | Approval cannot precede checking; B admits this visible word |

“Reject” means that the word is not a complete accepting visible trace; a prefix of it may still be executable.

No single simplification could be strict P3-language-equivalent to both originals, because A and B already differ within the P3 bound.

# 9. Final Recommendation

| View | Suitable original model(s) | Should S replace an original? |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | **A is the only candidate under the stated per-attempt boundary interpretation.** B is unsuitable as a compliance guarantee because it admits unchecked multi-attempt behavior. If checking must chronologically follow analysis, A also needs strengthening. | Replace A with S **only after stakeholders approve analysis-before-check serialization and the loss of `C N` interleavings**. Otherwise retain A. S is preferable when the stronger chronological check is desired. |
| P2 | **Both A and B** produce the same set of possible dashboard records. This does not make B safe for operational compliance. | S can replace either for P2’s possible-record semantics. It does not guarantee equal dashboard frequencies or probabilities. |
| P3 | Neither original is a substitute for the other. Retain A if attempt-local checking is intended; retain B only if one genuinely global check is intended. | S is not a drop-in P3 replacement for either original. Its bounded and unbounded visible languages must be regression-tested separately. |

Finally, bounded comparison has asymmetric evidentiary force. The short witnesses above prove real trace differences. But if two models’ languages were equal for all complete traces of at most 12 visible events and three analyses, that would still leave every longer or higher-attempt behavior unchecked. Random playout would weaken the evidence further; only a completed exhaustive exploration justifies a claim about the entire bounded language.
