## 2. Process-model consistency checks

The validation suite should combine prefix-time checks, activity-order constraints, and lineage audits.

- **Prefix-time availability checker.**  
  For every feature, compute its value twice: once from the full trace and once from the trace truncated immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. If the values differ, the feature depends on future events. This catches `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and `risk_score_current` unless it is frozen at `t0`. The `t0` prefix should be inclusive of Coverage Check Completed and strictly exclusive of later events.

- **Activity-order feasibility.**  
  A normative automaton can be defined:
  `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed -> (Damage Assessment | evidence cycles + Damage Assessment) -> Settlement Decision Recorded -> Claim Closed`, with optional Fraud Investigation in parallel and the semantics `Appeal` after Partial/Deny before another Settlement Decision.  
  Declarative rules can be encoded:
  - Damage Assessment must precede Settlement Decision.
  - Settlement Decision must precede Claim Closed.
  - Claim Closed is terminal; no activity may follow it.
  - Appeal may occur only after Partial or Deny.
  - A second Settlement Decision is valid only if preceded by an Appeal and a first Partial/Deny decision.  
  These rules will flag `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as impossible.

- **Same-timestamp ambiguity.**  
  Group events by case ID and timestamp. Without a reliable sequence index, the ordering of same-timestamp events is unknown. For an edge such as `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`, where both events share a timestamp, the edge should not be accepted. The only process-consistent order would be Damage Assessment before Claim Closed; if the source order is reversed, the trace should be flagged as ambiguous rather than interpreted as a valid transition.

- **Complete-trace information check.**  
  Scan feature definitions for aggregates over the whole trace: totals, durations, final states, last roles, full variant IDs, and final decisions. Store a feature lineage tag for each feature. In validation, compare the complete-trace value with the prefix-computed value. Any divergence should fail the check.

- **Enabled / impossible / future-possible check.**  
  At `t0`, the enabled activities are Damage Assessment, document requests, and possibly Fraud Investigation if a trigger is already known. Claim Closed, Settlement Decision, and Appeal are not enabled at `t0`. An explanation that refers to them as if they were already observed is not a `t0` explanation. Future-possible events can only be used if their *pre-existing trigger* is available at `t0`, not the event itself.

- **Repeated Settlement semantics.**  
  A second `Settlement Decision Recorded` should be considered legitimate only when the first decision was Partial or Deny and an Appeal occurred between them. Otherwise, it is a conformance violation. This prevents the model team from either treating all repeated decisions as illegal or ignoring the special appeal semantics.

**Limitations of common methods.** A frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule; it can be an artifact of same-timestamp ordering. Alignment-based conformance can hide a small number of impossible edges by assigning them low cost. Declarative checks are exact for this problem but require a correct mapping from raw activities to process phases. Therefore, the suite should use all three: exploratory DFG/footprint analysis for discovery, declarative rules for hard constraints, and prefix-time lineage checks for prediction-time validity.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

### Defensible predictive signals
- **Features:** `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `initial_reserve` (once freeze time is verified), repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and, under a process-state view, `queue_length_at_t0`.
- **May be claimed:** These are available at `t0`; they are predictive correlates of expected processing time; they can be used in risk scoring and case prioritization.
- **May not be claimed:** They cause breach; changing them operationally will reduce breach; they are root causes. They are summary descriptors of the case or environment.

### Process-state or congestion signals
- **Features:** `queue_length_at_t0`; after lineage repair, `same_day_document_count_snapshot` as of `t0`, `team_at_t0`, and the prefix variant state.
- **May be claimed:** They describe current workload and process stage at `t0`; they are useful for operational triage and resource planning.
- **May not be claimed:** That a high queue necessarily causes breach in a causal sense. Queue length may be endogenous to claim mix and demand, so an intervention on queue length needs separate causal or queuing validation.

### Proxies requiring fairness/robustness review
- **Features:** `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and possibly repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`.
- **May be claimed:** They may improve predictive performance under the current historical distribution; their performance should be measured across subgroups.
- **May not be claimed:** Region or channel is a “delay driver.” They may be proxies for network coverage, language, channel service models, or socioeconomic background. They should not be used as causal or discriminatory justifications in automated decisions without a fairness review and transparency analysis.

### Drift/calendar proxies
- **Features:** `calendar_week`, holiday indicators, and policy-change indicators.
- **May be claimed:** Calendar time can be used for monitoring, recalibration, and seasonal capacity planning.
- **May not be claimed:** “Certain weeks are operationally worse” as a stable causal law. The effect may be confounded by a holiday period or a policy change. Model monitoring should detect whether the calendar effect remains stable.

### Unusable until lineage is repaired
- **Features:** `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_handler_team`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` until the SQL is fixed, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `final_decision`, and all future-only sequence patterns.
- **May be claimed after repair:** Only prefix-based versions of these features can be used, e.g., `team_at_t0`, `risk_score_at_t0`, `num_document_requests_before_t0`, and `prefix_variant_id`.
- **May not be claimed:** The current values are `t0` information. Some features, such as `total_case_duration_hours` and `final_decision`, are inherently terminal and can never be repaired as `t0` inputs.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Feature-store design.**  
   Implement a point-in-time feature store. Every feature should carry an `as_of_time`, a source event timestamp, a feature version, and a lineage query. Mutable features must never be overwritten in place; they should have `valid_from`/`valid_to` history. The training and serving feature views should be identical.

2. **Historical feature timestamps.**  
   Join every feature using the state at `t0`, not extraction date. This applies to employee experience, handler team, risk score, document counts, and any warehouse fields. Use SQL predicates such as `claim_created_at < t0`, `event_time <= t0`, and snapshot times that are strictly before or at `t0`.

3. **Prefix-only recomputation.**  
   Regenerate all feature matrices by truncating event logs at the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Define prefix variants rather than full-trace variants. Recompute totals, roles, scores, and document counts from the prefix only. Compare old and new values to quantify how much of the model relied on future information.

4. **Process-conformance tests.**  
   Run the validation suite from Section 2 on every ingested event log. Fail batches on impossible transitions such as `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`. Flag same-timestamp ambiguities. Enforce semantic rules for repeated Settlement decisions and appeals. Use footprint/conformance checks, but do not treat frequent DFG edges as normative rules.

5. **Calibration after feature removal.**  
   Remove leaked features and retrain on a temporal holdout. Evaluate AUROC, precision-recall, log-loss, and calibration curves before and after removal. This quantifies the degree to which the original probabilities were inflated by leakage.

6. **Explanation stability.**  
   Use bootstrap or interval estimates around SHAP values. Monitor ranking stability across folds and time windows. Do not present SHAP importances as causal effects. After removing leakage, re-examine whether the remaining feature importances are stable.

7. **Proxy/fairness analysis.**  
   For `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and any other sensitive proxies, evaluate subgroup calibration, false-positive and false-negative rates, and potential disparate impact. Document whether these features add predictive value beyond legitimate process features. Fairness review should happen before deployment, not after.

8. **Monitoring for schema and process drift.**  
   Monitor event-order changes, new activity labels, timestamp reliability, feature refresh cadence, queue snapshot alignment, and policy/holiday effects. Use feature distribution drift metrics and process conformance dashboards. When drift is detected, retrigger the explanation audit and, if needed, recalibrate the model.
