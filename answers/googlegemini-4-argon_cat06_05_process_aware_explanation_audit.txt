## 2. Process-model consistency checks

To prevent future-leaking and process-inconsistent explanations from reaching production, the auditing pipeline must enforce a six-part **Process-Aware Validation Suite** prior to model training and explanation generation:

1. **Prefix-Time Availability Check (Point-in-Time Contract):**
   - *Mechanism:* Every feature $X_i$ and event $e_j$ in a training or explanation instance must carry an explicit `available_at_ts` (system commit timestamp) in addition to its `business_event_ts`. The validator asserts $\max(\text{available\_at\_ts}) \le t_0$ and $\text{extraction\_filter\_ts} = t_0$.
   - *Role:* Blocks complete-trace aggregations (`total_case_duration_hours`, `full_trace_variant_id`), end-of-day snapshots (`same_day_document_count_snapshot`), and unversioned mutable states (`risk_score_current`, `final_handler_team`).

2. **Prefix-State & Enabled-Activity Feasibility Check (Petri Net / Token Replay):**
   - *Mechanism:* Replay the observed prefix up to $t_0$ (`Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`) on the normative process model (represented as a Petri net or BPMN state machine). At marking $M(t_0)$:
     - **Past/Observed activities:** `{Claim Registered, Coverage Check Completed}`.
     - **Currently Enabled activities at $t_0$:** `{Damage Assessment Completed, Documents Requested, Fraud Investigation Opened}`.
     - **Future-Possible (reachable but not yet enabled) activities:** `{Documents Received, Documents Validated, Settlement Decision Recorded, Appeal, Claim Closed}`.
   - *Role:* Any local or global explanation at $t_0$ referencing an *enabled* or *future-possible* activity as having already occurred is automatically flagged as a temporal leakage violation.

3. **Activity-Order Feasibility & Conformance Checking (Alignments vs. Footprints vs. DFGs):**
   - *Mechanism:* Use **trace alignments** and **declarative constraints (DECLARE)** over a normative model rather than relying on empirical Directly-Follows Graphs (DFGs).
     - *Declarative rules enforced:* `Init(Claim Registered)`, `End(Claim Closed)`, `Precedence(Coverage Check Completed, Damage Assessment Completed)`, `NotSuccession(Claim Closed, *)`.
   - *Role & Limitations:* An empirical DFG merely counts observed timestamp-ordered transitions; if the source system has clock-skew or coarse timestamps, a DFG will display frequent edges like `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` and treat them as valid sequence features. A frequent DFG edge is descriptive of logging noise, not a normative rule. Alignment-based conformance checking against the normative model immediately flags `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as a non-synchronous move (violation of `NotSuccession(Claim Closed, *)`).

4. **Same-Timestamp Ambiguity & Tie-Breaking Audit:**
   - *Mechanism:* Scan traces for event pairs $(e_a, e_b)$ where $t(e_a) = t(e_b)$. Verify the presence of a monotonic, source-generated `ingestion_sequence_index`.
   - *Role:* When `ingestion_sequence_index` is missing (as in Case Z), the validator replaces total ordering with a **partially ordered event trace**. Sequence mining and n-gram extractors are prohibited from generating directional bigrams ($a \to b$ or $b \to a$) across concurrent timestamps unless resolved by a deterministic normative tie-breaker (e.g., `Damage Assessment Completed` must precede `Settlement Decision Recorded` and `Claim Closed`).

5. **Ablation-Based Complete-Trace Leakage Detector:**
   - *Mechanism:* For any trace in the test set, generate two synthetic versions that are identical up to $t_0$ (`Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`), but have different post-$t_0$ suffixes (e.g., Suffix A is fast-track closure; Suffix B has 3 document cycles and an appeal). Run the feature extraction pipeline for $t_0$ on both versions.
   - *Role:* Assert $F(t_0 | \text{Suffix A}) \equiv F(t_0 | \text{Suffix B})$. Any feature whose value changes when the future suffix is perturbed (such as `prior_claim_count_before_t0` under broken SQL, `final_handler_team`, or `risk_score_current`) fails the test and is automatically quarantined.

6. **Semantic Multi-Instantiation & Loop Disambiguation Check:**
   - *Mechanism:* Refine coarse activity labels using state-aware refinement rules during conformance checking:
     - First occurrence of `Settlement Decision Recorded` $\to$ `Settlement Decision Recorded (Initial)`.
     - Occurrence of `Settlement Decision Recorded` preceded by `Appeal` $\to$ `Settlement Decision Recorded (Post-Appeal)`.
     - Verify the guarded transition: `Appeal` is only enabled if the payload of `Settlement Decision Recorded (Initial)` $\in \{\text{Partial}, \text{Deny}\}$.
   - *Role:* Confirms that `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is **semantically and normatively legitimate** as a reconsideration loop (unlike the impossible `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` artifact), while still rejecting it as an admissible feature at $t_0$ because the entire loop lies in the future of $t_0$.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Once complete-trace leakage (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`, and all post-$t_0$ sequence patterns) is removed, the remaining and repairable features must be governed under distinct epistemic standards: **Predictive Validity** (does it forecast the target at $t_0$?), **Causal Interpretation** (does intervening on it change the outcome?), **Operational Actionability** (can a manager act on this specific lever at $t_0$?), and **Fairness/Robustness** (does it encode protected traits or transient regimes?).

### 1. Defensible Predictive Signals (Case Severity & Complexity at $t_0$)
- **Features:** `initial_damage_complexity`, `initial_reserve` (verified frozen at $t_0$), `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (after SQL repair).
- **What may be claimed:** These features capture baseline intrinsic claim complexity known at $t_0$. Higher complexity or reserve amounts are statistically associated with a higher probability of needing multi-step evidence validation or specialist assessment, thus increasing 10-day breach risk.
- **What may NOT be claimed:** They are not operational bottlenecks in themselves, nor should claims handlers artificially downgrade `initial_damage_complexity` or `initial_reserve` to "reduce" predicted breach probability (Goodhart’s Law).

### 2. Process-State and Congestion Signals
- **Features:** `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, `queue_length_at_t0` (with strict $\le t_0$ timestamp join).
- **What may be claimed:** These represent real-time operational state and remaining SLA slack at $t_0$. `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` directly reduces the remaining budget out of the 240-hour target. `queue_length_at_t0` is a valid congestion indicator that plausibly contributes to waiting times before step 3 begins.
- **What may NOT be claimed:** While highly actionable (e.g., triggering dynamic load balancing or overtime capacity when queues are long), `queue_length_at_t0` observational SHAP values should not be treated as exact causal treatment effects without accounting for confounding (e.g., a major weather event simultaneously increasing both queue length and average claim severity).

### 3. Proxies Requiring Fairness and Robustness Review
- **Features:** `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`.
- **What may be claimed:** These features carry genuine predictive signal at $t_0$ because they correlate with structural environmental factors (e.g., rural repair-shop scarcity in Region R4, language translation needs, or complex commercial policies submitted via Brokers).
- **What may NOT be claimed:** One must never claim "Region R4 causes delays" or penalize claimants from specific regions/socioeconomic backgrounds. If used to prioritize fast-tracking (helpful intervention), they may be acceptable; if used to deprioritize or deny service, they risk disparate impact. Best practice is to replace `claimant_region` with explicit operational metrics (`regional_repair_shop_backlog_at_t0`) and monitor group calibration across regions.

### 4. Drift and Calendar Proxies
- **Features:** `calendar_week`.
- **What may be claimed:** Captures historical time-based variance in the training set (holiday slowdowns in Week 52 and a specific historical policy shift).
- **What may NOT be claimed:** Cannot be claimed as a stable, cyclic operational law ("Week $W$ is inherently bad"). Because a one-off policy change is confounded with calendar week, the model will suffer severe concept drift in the following year when the new policy applies to all weeks. It should be decomposed into `is_holiday_period` and `active_policy_regime`.

### 5. Unusable Until Lineage is Repaired
- **Features:** `final_handler_team`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `Employee experience`, and raw unindexed same-timestamp sequences (`Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`).
- **What may be claimed:** Nothing in their current form. Any predictive lift they show in backtesting is contaminated by lookahead bias.
- **What may NOT be claimed:** Cannot be used for either prediction or explanation until reconstructed from immutable point-in-time event logs (`assigned_team_at_t0`, `doc_count_at_t0`, `risk_score_at_t0`, `handler_tenure_at_t0`).

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

To remediate these defects systematically, the engineering and modeling teams should execute the following prioritized plan:

### Priority 1: Point-in-Time Feature Store & Immutable Lineage Design (Immediate)
- **Bitemporal Event & State Logging:** Redesign the data warehouse and feature store around an append-only, bitemporal schema recording both `business_event_ts` (when the activity occurred) and `system_ingestion_ts` (when the database recorded it), alongside a strict monotonic `ingestion_sequence_index` per case to eliminate same-timestamp ordering ambiguity.
- **SCD Type 2 for Mutable Entities:** Convert overwrite fields (`risk_score_current`, `final_handler_team`, `last_resource_role`, `Employee experience`) into Slowly Changing Dimension (SCD) Type 2 tables with `valid_from_ts` and `valid_to_ts` intervals.
- **Strict Point-in-Time (`AS OF`) Joins:** Enforce automated `AS OF t0` joins in the feature store so that `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `document_count_at_t0`, and `queue_length_at_t0` cannot access records where `system_ingestion_ts > t0`.

### Priority 2: Prefix-Only Model Recomputation & Process-Conformance Gating (Short-Term)
- **Prefix Extraction Pipeline:** Truncate all training and evaluation traces strictly at the first occurrence of `Coverage Check Completed` ($t_0$). Any feature requiring aggregation beyond index $k(t_0)$ is structurally uncomputable in the pipeline.
- **Automated Conformance Unit Tests:** Integrate the Section 2 conformance suite into CI/CD. Before training, run alignment checks against the normative Petri net/declarative model to quarantine corrupted historical logs (such as unindexed concurrent timestamps yielding `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`) and verify that multi-instantiated activities (`Settlement Decision Recorded` before vs. after `Appeal`) are properly indexed for downstream checkpoints ($t_k$).

### Priority 3: Model Recalibration & Explanation Stability Auditing (Medium-Term)
- **Probability Recalibration:** Because leaked features (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`) artificially inflated model separation (pushing predictions toward 0 and 1), removing them will substantially reduce raw AUROC/AUPRC and compress log-odds. Re-evaluate whether the $t_0$ model has sufficient signal to be operationally useful, and apply Platt scaling or isotonic regression on an out-of-time validation split to ensure predicted probabilities match empirical breach rates.
- **Explanation Stability & Collinearity Checks:** Compute variance of SHAP values across bootstrap resamples and under conditional expectations (accounting for feature correlations like `initial_damage_complexity` and `initial_reserve`) so explanations do not split importance arbitrarily across collinear prefix signals.

### Priority 4: Proxy Decomposition, Fairness Review, and Drift Monitoring (Ongoing)
- **Fairness & Disparate Impact Audit:** Audit `claimant_region` and `claimant_channel` for False Positive Rate (FPR) and calibration parity across demographic/regional subgroups. Decompose `claimant_region` into explicit operational metrics (`regional_assessor_backlog_at_t0`, `repair_network_utilization`) so explanations highlight actionable supply-side bottlenecks rather than customer location.
- **Schema and Process Drift Monitoring:** Replace `calendar_week` with explicit `policy_version` and `holiday_capacity` features. Monitor production streams for **control-flow drift** (changes in prefix transition frequencies or registration-to-coverage durations), **data/schema drift** (changes in timestamp granularity or missing `ingestion_sequence_index`), and **concept drift** (shifts in base breach rates following operational policy updates).
