# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Opening-hours convention.** The center is open 07:00–19:00, i.e. 12 opening hours/day. All daily arrival, completion, WIP-snapshot, outage, and staffing quantities below refer to opening hours unless stated. Service times are active handling times excluding waiting. WIP is a 07:00–19:00 snapshot mean of cases “in system,” not a case-level queue length. Completed cases need not correspond to same-day arrivals, and cases can carry over overnight when the center is closed.

**Core estimands and units.**
- Arrival rate: `A/12` cases per opening hour, where `A` is daily arrivals during opening hours.
- Completion/throughput rate: `C/12` cases per opening hour, where `C` is daily completed during opening hours.
- WIP: mean cases in system from 15-minute snapshots, `L`.
- Service behavior: supplied median and p90 active handling minutes, `s50` and `s90`. These are not mean service time and not mean flow time.
- Staffed capacity: `staff_fte` concurrently available FTEs; `12*FTE` FTE-opening-hours/day.
- Crude capacity-pressure proxies:
  - Arrival load per FTE-hour: `A/(12*FTE)` cases per FTE-opening-hour.
  - Median-workload ratio: `A*s50/(720*FTE)`. This is a transparent median-time workload proxy, not mean utilization.
  - p90 tail-stress ratio: `A*s90/(720*FTE)`. This is a tail scenario, not average utilization.
- Complex mix: percentage of arrivals on multi-step complex variants.
- Peak share: supplied `peak_08_10_pct`, but its exact reconciliation with representative hourly vectors is not always obvious.

**Limitations and threats to naive inference.**
1. **Nonstationarity and regime shifts.** The four weeks contain at least an outage/recovery, a major case-mix/service-time shift around 2026-04-20, and a staffing intervention starting 2026-04-29. Pooled averages combine different operating regimes.
2. **Carry-over and non-matched days.** Arrivals and completions are daily totals but are not linked. Completed cases may have arrived earlier; overnight backlog is not observed at 07:00 each day.
3. **WIP and flow mismatch.** WIP is a snapshot mean, while arrivals/completions are interval counts. Little’s Law requires consistent boundaries and approximate stationarity.
4. **Medians and p90s are not means.** The supplied service-time summaries cannot be substituted for mean service time or mean flow time. For example, baseline weekday median active service is 25 minutes, but an implied WIP/throughput flow time is about 2.4 opening-hours under strong assumptions.
5. **Composition/Simpson-like issues.** Weekday/weekend mix, case-mix phase, and intervention timing change over the four weeks, so pooled means are weighted mixtures of unlike populations.
6. **Outage truncation and recovery surge.** The 2026-04-15 outage suppresses completions and inflates WIP; the 2026-04-16–17 completions include backlog release. Recovery throughput is not normal capacity throughput.
7. **Intervention timing unknown.** The intervention began during 2026-04-29, but the exact time is not logged. That day mixes pre/post intervention and any same-day backlog effects.
8. **Representative-vector reconciliation.** The hourly vectors are useful for shape, but they do not obviously reconcile with every daily `peak_08_10_pct` if interpreted literally; e.g. for 2026-04-07, bins 08–09 and 09–10 sum to 17/101, not the listed 31%. I use hourly vectors for relative burstiness, not exact daily peak reconstruction.
9. **Small regime samples.** Some regimes have 2–4 days. Four representative hourly vectors cannot identify a full stochastic process.

# 7. Operational implications

- **Use regime-specific staffing rather than a single pooled queue.** High-mix weekdays have roughly 114 arrivals/day, arrival load1.60 cases/FTE-hour, median-workload ratio0.94, and p90 tail-stress2.22. This is materially different from baseline weekdays (arrival load1.41, median-workload ratio0.59) and from weekends (about 42–49 arrivals/day). Separate weekday and weekend schedules, and add cross-trained/backup capacity in high-complex weekdays.
- **Treat outage recovery as a special backlog-recovery mode.** On 2026-04-15 completions were suppressed and WIP rose to 47. On 04-16–17 completions averaged 120.5/day while arrivals averaged 105.5/day, and WIP fell from 52 to 35. Staffing during recovery should be explicitly justified by backlog reduction; recovery completion rates should not be used as normal steady-state capacity.
- **Recognize the 2026-04-20 case-mix/service shift.** Median service rose from about 25 to 35 minutes and p90 from about 47 to 83 minutes for high-mix weekdays, while complex_pct moved from about 21% to about 45%. If this is a real case-mix shift, scheduling and triage should change; if it is partly coding change, the metric must be corrected before using it for staffing.
- **Smooth or staff around intraday bursts.** The 2026-04-21 vector has high arrival Fano (9.28) with large spikes, while completions are smoother (Fano0.90). This suggests queue buffering and burst absorption. Use flexible breaks, cross-trained surge staff, and appointment smoothing around historically bursty hours. Do not rely on daily averages alone for minute-level staffing.
- **Evaluate the staffing intervention with time-stamped data.** The intervention began during 2026-04-29, exactly when backlog was being drawn down. The post weekdays show higher FTE, lower WIP, and completions above arrivals, but causality is not identifiable from these daily aggregates. Record the exact intervention time and use event-level queueing metrics.
- **Policy that looks good under pooled averages but is risky under segmentation.** A manager seeing pooled arrivals90/day and pooled completions86/day, or a pooled median service time around 31 minutes, might keep total staffing flat. But high-mix pre-intervention weekdays average 114 arrivals/day and only 100.6 completions/day, with WIP44 and growing backlog, while weekends are low-demand. Uniform staffing based on pooled averages would understaff high-complex weekdays and overstaff low-demand weekends. Similarly, treating the post-intervention pooled period as evidence of success is risky because the apparent lower pooled arrivals are partly a weekend-composition effect; high-mix weekday demand was actually about the same or slightly higher.
- **Data collection priorities.** Record timestamped arrivals, service start/end, completions, case type/complexity flags, rework, queue length every 15 minutes, exact outage intervals, exact staffing changes, breaks, and 07:00 backlog. Daily aggregates are insufficient for identifying queue discipline, abandonment, rework, true mean service, or within-day bottleneck formation.

# Primary segmentation
def primary_regime(row):
    d = row["date"]
    day = row["day"]
    if d == pd.Timestamp("2026-04-15"):
        return "Outage 4/15"
    if d in [pd.Timestamp("2026-04-16"), pd.Timestamp("2026-04-17")]:
        return "Recovery 4/16-17"
    if d in [pd.Timestamp("2026-04-11"), pd.Timestamp("2026-04-12"),
             pd.Timestamp("2026-04-18"), pd.Timestamp("2026-04-19")]:
        return "Baseline weekend"
    if d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-14") and day not in ["Sat", "Sun"]:
        return "Baseline weekday"
    if d in [pd.Timestamp("2026-04-25"), pd.Timestamp("2026-04-26")]:
        return "High-mix weekend pre"
    if pd.Timestamp("2026-04-20") <= d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-04-28") and day not in ["Sat", "Sun"]:
        return "High-mix weekday pre"
    if pd.Timestamp("2026-04-29") <= d <= pd.Timestamp("2026-05-01"):
        return "Intervention weekday"
    if d in [pd.Timestamp("2026-05-02"), pd.Timestamp("2026-05-03")]:
        return "Intervention weekend"
    return "Other"

def summarize_by(df, group_col):
    out = df.groupby(group_col).agg(
        n=("date", "size"),
        arr_mean=("arrivals", "mean"),
        comp_mean=("completed", "mean"),
        med_mean=("median_service_min", "mean"),
        p90_mean=("p90_service_min", "mean"),
        wip_mean=("mean_wip", "mean"),
        fte_mean=("staff_fte", "mean"),
        complex_mean=("complex_pct", "mean"),
        peak_mean=("peak_08_10_pct", "mean"),
        outage_mean=("outage_min", "mean")
    ).reset_index()
    out["arr_per_h"] = out["arr_mean"] / open_hours
    out["comp_per_h"] = out["comp_mean"] / open_hours
    out["arr_per_fte_h"] = out["arr_mean"] / (out["fte_mean"] * open_hours)
    out["median_load_ratio"] = out["arr_mean"] * out["med_mean"] / (60.0 * open_hours * out["fte_mean"])
    out["p90_tail_ratio"] = out["arr_mean"] * out["p90_mean"] / (60.0 * open_hours * out["fte_mean"])
    out["T_comp_opening_h"] = open_hours * out["wip_mean"] / out["comp_mean"]
    out["T_arr_opening_h"] = open_hours * out["wip_mean"] / out["arr_mean"]
    return out

# Burstiness / dispersion indicators
def dispersion(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    m = x.mean()
    v = x.var(ddof=0)
    return pd.Series({
        "mean": m,
        "var_pop": v,
        "fano": v / m if m else np.nan,
        "cv": np.sqrt(v) / m if m else np.nan,
        "max_over_mean": x.max() / m if m else np.nan
    })

# Simple serial dependence indicator
def lag1_autocorr(x):
    x = np.asarray(x, dtype=float)
    return np.corrcoef(x[:-1], x[1:])[0, 1]

# Transparent change-point/segmentation heuristic: single best mean split for a chosen metric.
# This is exploratory only, not definitive ground truth.
def best_mean_splits(df, col, min_size=3, top=5):
    x = df[col].to_numpy(dtype=float)
    rows = []
    for i in range(min_size, len(x) - min_size + 1):
        left, right = x[:i], x[i:]
        pooled_sd = np.sqrt((left.var(ddof=0) + right.var(ddof=0)) / 2.0)
        score = abs(left.mean() - right.mean()) / (pooled_sd + 1e-9)
        rows.append({
            "score": score,
            "n_left": i,
            "last_left": df["date"].iloc[i-1].date(),
            "first_right": df["date"].iloc[i].date(),
            "mean_left": left.mean(),
            "mean_right": right.mean()
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values("score", ascending=False).head(top)

for col in ["complex_pct", "mean_wip", "completed"]:
    print(f"\nTOP MEAN-SHIFT SPLITS: {col}")
    print(best_mean_splits(daily, col, min_size=3, top=3).round(3).to_string(index=False))
