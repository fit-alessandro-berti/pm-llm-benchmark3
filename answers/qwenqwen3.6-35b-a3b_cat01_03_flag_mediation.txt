**Framework choice:** I use *interventional indirect effects* (Vansteelandt) rather than natural direct/indirect effects. The flag policy bundle creates exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding: the flag triggers mandatory review, which alters queue composition and wait times, which in turn affects escalation likelihood and cycle time. Natural effects assume no such feedback, which is violated by shared queues and human routing discretion. Interventional effects condition on the mediator under a fixed treatment assignment, isolating the policy mechanism from downstream confounding.

| Policy-change/randomized encouragement | Randomize flag display suppression for 10% of cases | Case ID, arrival timestamp | Random assignment, no spillover | Intent-to-treat vs per-protocol gap | Large gap  compliance or spillover bias |
| Mediation-oriented analysis | G-computation for interventional indirect effects | Flag, manual_review, queue_wait, complexity | No unmeasured mediator-outcome confounding | Sensitivity to gamma parameter | Effect vanishes under mild confounding  not robust |
| Negative-control outcome | Use low-risk cases expected to bypass review | Risk_score <0.3, complexity=Low | Negative control unaffected by policy | No effect on control outcome | Effect appears  unmeasured confounding or spillover |
| Sensitivity for unobserved risk | E-value or Rosenbaum bounds on risk_score | Risk_score, flag, outcome | Monotonicity, correct model | E-value < observed effect  robust | E-value small  hidden risk explains association |
| Interference-aware design | Cluster-randomize by shift/queue; analyze with GSIR | Shift, queue ID, case ID | Stable unit treatment value relaxed | Variance inflation across clusters | High cluster variance  interference unaccounted for |

## 6. Policy comparison

| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 (current) | Fast (priority) | Congested manual/esc queues | Long (~15.8h) | High detection, low false negative | Negative spillover to unflagged | Moderate (observational) | Baseline |
| P1 (suppress flag) | Slower (normal lane) | Less manual queue pressure | Shorter (~9h) | Missed high-risk cases | Unflagged triage slows | Weak (no experimental data) | Pilot only |
| P2 (remove mandatory review) | Unchanged | Fewer manual entries, lighter queue | Shorter (~10h) | Lower detection rate | Capacity freed for complex cases | Moderate (historical analogs) | Conditional pilot |
| P3 (protected capacity) | Unchanged | Reduced manual/esc waits | Shorter (~11h) | Maintained detection | Minimal spillover | Strong if capacity added | **Recommended pilot** |
| P4 (relax escalation) | Unchanged | Fewer escalations, senior queue lighter | Shorter (~12h) | Risk of under-escalation | Senior team freed | Moderate (expert judgment) | Pilot after P3 |

**Pilot recommendation (P3: protected manual-review capacity)**
Eligibility: All cases with `flag=1`. Assignment: 1:1 randomization to standard vs protected queue at triage_done. Guardrail: Protected queue capped at 1.5× historical throughput; overflow routes to standard queue. Primary outcome: Manual queue wait time. Secondary: Escalation rate, cycle time, decision accuracy. Minimum data: Timestamps, resource IDs, queue depth snapshots, outcome labels. Stopping conditions: If protected queue wait exceeds standard by >10% (indicating capacity failure), or if decision accuracy drops >5% (indicating quality degradation), or after 30 days/200 cases.

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. Hidden risk: If `risk_score` systematically underestimates true severity for flagged cases, the observed delay may reflect genuine complexity rather than policy inefficiency; correcting risk measurement would reverse the conclusion.
2. Queue interference: If protected capacity for flagged cases causes severe displacement of unflagged cases, the net system cycle time could increase, reversing the recommendation for P3.
3. Decision quality: If mandatory review generates excessive false positives that trigger unnecessary escalations, the quality-adjusted cycle time would worsen, reversing the preference for current policy.
4. Timestamp semantics: If `close` timestamps include post-decision administrative delays unrelated to processing, the total cycle metric would conflate operational performance with paperwork, reversing causal claims.
5. Positivity/overlap: If high-risk cases are exclusively routed to the priority lane and low-risk cases never enter manual review, the treatment and control groups are non-comparable, making mediation estimates unidentified.
6. Behavior change by reviewers: If removing the flag changes reviewer vigilance or escalation thresholds (e.g., reviewers become complacent), the policy effect would be confounded by behavioral adaptation, reversing the intervention design.

## 9. Final synthesis

We directly observe that flagged cases enter triage ~10× faster but spend ~3× longer in manual queues and escalations, yielding longer total cycles. Plausible mechanisms include policy-mandated review increasing queue depth, shared-queue interference amplifying waits, and higher underlying complexity extending service times. What remains unidentified are the true causal weights of triage acceleration versus downstream congestion, and the extent to which unobserved risk drives both flagging and delays. Cross-world dependencies and exposure-induced mediator-outcome confounding prevent clean natural direct/indirect decomposition. The most useful next evidence comes from a randomized pilot of protected manual-review capacity (P3), which isolates queue-wait reduction from routing changes while preserving detection quality. This design yields interventional indirect effects on wait times, clarifies spillover magnitude, and provides actionable capacity allocation rules without sacrificing compliance. Until such experimental or quasi-experimental evidence is gathered, stage-level contrasts should be treated as descriptive signals of bottleneck location, not causal policy levers.
