## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Register ≺ Triage ≺ {Identity Check, Document Check}; if originals are required, Originals Receipt ≺ Document Check.
{Identity Check, latest Document Check} ≺ each relevant Assessment; Identity Check and Document Check have no mutual order when originals are not required.
If Assessment_i or Compliance Review_i issues Request More Info_i:
  issuer_i ≺ Request More Info_i ≺ Document Check_(i+1) ≺ Assessment_(i+1).
For high-risk/high-value cases: final Assessment ≺ final Compliance Review ≺ Decision.
For other cases: final Assessment ≺ Decision. In every case: Decision ≺ Notify.
K-104 option: Triage ≺ Escalation Advice ≺ preliminary compliance screen; the screen can overlap verification and precedes, but cannot replace, final Compliance Review.
```

```text
Conditional: A post-request Assessment needs a new Document Check; high-risk/high-value cases then need a new or explicitly reaffirmed final compliance review after that Assessment.
Declarative: Final Assessment and final Compliance Review have different people; only C1 can provide the certified review in this window.
Declarative: Each resource's service intervals are disjoint. A queue estimate is neither a booked start nor permission to preempt existing work.
Conditional: K-337 may end with rejection, or with explicit conditions only if the unresolved discrepancy can lawfully be left as a condition; the discrepancy must be recorded and considered by C1 and M1.
Declarative: Recorded concurrent work must reflect actual start/end times and separate task ownership. M1 must be available for Decision and Notify.
```

K-104 changes timing and preparation, including a capacity reservation; K-411 changes task creation and timing. K-219 changes routing from D1 to D2 and therefore timing and error risk. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy. The printed factual records cannot all share one literal resource calendar: D1 overlaps on K-219 and K-337 on Tue 09:30–13:30. The single-case scenarios below are conditional illustrations; that collision needs a timestamp or case-scope audit before joint scheduling.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** Triage ends Mon 08:50, but the ambiguity reaches S1 only after A1 finishes Tue 19:10. C1 then starts Wed 13:10 and spends 4 h 30 min on final review; this wait, rather than identity or document verification alone, governs notice timing.

**Minimal intervention:** Send the ambiguity and available documents to S1 at Mon 08:50 and hold a provisional C1 slot with a release deadline. Keep I1, D1, A1, and M1's substantive steps and the four-eyes pairing unchanged.

**Temporal propagation:** One possible calendar puts S1 at Mon 09:00–10:30, C1 preliminary reading at Tue 10:00–11:30, A1 at the factual Tue 15:10–19:10, and C1 final review at Wed 08:30–12:00. If M1 signs Wed 12:15–12:45 and notifies by 12:55, earlier preparation and a usable slot save 5 h 45 min. The packet does not advance Assessment: D1 still completes Tue 05:40, and the forecast C1 backlog makes the earlier slot uncertain.

**Process-feasibility check:** Final C1 work follows final A1 Assessment; preliminary work is not treated as certification. A1 and C1 are different people, and the hypothesized C1 intervals do not overlap each other. The slot must be surrendered if the packet is too incomplete or an urgent case takes precedence.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best is roughly 6–8 h faster with a sound packet and early final slot; base is a few hours faster if preparatory reading reduces final service. Worst is no improvement or about 6 h slower if the reservation is unusable, C1 repeats reading, or M1 cannot sign earlier. A substantive sanctions finding could also change the decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** C1 availability logs showing no earlier slot, repeat-reading times exceeding saved work, or an earlier M1 blackout would erase the proposed gain; evidence that the screen reveals a material concern would change the predicted approval.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** Identity ends Mon 16:20, but originals do not arrive until 17:30. D1 then waits to Tue 09:30, completes an accurate four-hour check, and the case waits again for A2 and M1.

**Minimal intervention:** At receipt, place the check with D2 at D2's first free slot, preserving the original-document prerequisite and the same assessment and decision requirements. Assignment does not itself guarantee an immediate start.

**Temporal propagation:** In a fast branch, D2 starts Mon 22:30, finishes Tue 05:30 after seven hours, and A2 works Tue 14:00–16:30. A Tue 17:00 M1 slot gives notice by Tue 17:30, 15 h 30 min faster; if M1 only signs Wed morning, much of that gain disappears. If handwriting prompts A2 to issue Request More Info, the external response, another check, and another assessment can push notice beyond the factual Wednesday time.

**Process-feasibility check:** D2 begins after receipt, and the first Assessment follows completed identity and documents. A document concern must pass through Assessment for a formal Request More Info; after the request, a new Document Check and Assessment precede M1. No compliance review is required for this case.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best is about 12–16 h faster when D2 reads correctly and A2/M1 slots open; base ranges from several hours faster to unchanged when M1 stays the gate. Worst is one to several days slower with clarification or a hidden transcription error that affects quality.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Actual D2 release times later than D1's, higher handwritten-document error or rework rates, or a fixed Wed M1 window would negate a speed or quality advantage.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** The second request leaves on Fri 10:30 and returns Mon 09:00, after which D1, A1, C1, and M1 must each work or wait. The weekend and four downstream queues account for much of the 214 h 10 min cycle.

**Minimal intervention:** Keep the first information loop and Assessment 2 intact, then prohibit only the second request. A1 records the remaining discrepancy, C1 reviews the latest assessment, and M1 decides with available evidence.

**Temporal propagation:** If C1 reviews Fri 14:00–18:00 and M1 signs by Fri 19:00, notice by 19:10 saves 110 h. If C1 finishes Friday but M1 next signs Mon 08:30–09:00, notice at 09:10 saves 48 h. A C1 queue lasting into Tuesday, or manager congestion, can absorb nearly all first-notice savings; an appeal can make ultimate resolution slower despite faster notice.

**Process-feasibility check:** C1 follows A1's Assessment 2 and is a different person. The final decision can reject on unresolved evidence, or approve with explicit conditions only where the governing standard allows that unresolved point to remain conditional. It cannot quietly assume that the missing confirmation was received.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best is Friday notice with a defensible rejection or lawful conditional approval; base is Monday notice with a changed outcome more likely than in the other cases. Worst is little cycle-time gain plus a disputed rejection or unsafe conditional approval, with appeal, remediation, or loss outside the measured cycle.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** A rule requiring a verified supplier total before *any* final decision, evidence that the discrepancy is material to compliance, or a high observed appeal/loss rate after evidence-limited decisions would make the proposed policy infeasible or unattractive.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** Triage creates only I2's task. The lock holds D2's task until I2 completes Tue 13:20; D2 ends Tue 19:30, but A2 does not begin until Wed 09:30.

**Minimal intervention:** Create separate identity and document tasks at Tue 09:35 with controlled ownership, while preserving the checks and using actual service timestamps. Do not change A2's standards or M1's authority.

**Temporal propagation:** If I2 keeps Tue 11:30–13:20 and D2 works Tue 12:00–16:30, verification readiness advances three hours. A2 may still start Wed 09:30 and notice remains Wed 14:30; if an earlier A2 slot and then M1 slot open, the same readiness can advance notice by several hours. Thus the assessment queue, not the amount of parallelism, determines realized completion.

**Process-feasibility check:** With no originals, I2 and D2 may overlap on different resources; A2 starts only after both finish. The workaround must keep separate task ownership and actual timestamps, and D2's proposed interval must not overlap another D2 job in the realized calendar.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best is roughly five hours faster with both downstream slots available; base is unchanged notice with earlier readiness; worst is 1–3 h slower from reconciliation or rework caused by a faulty workaround. Correctly performed checks should preserve the approval decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Logs showing D2 cannot start before Tue 15:00, a persistently fixed A2 start regardless of readiness, or audit findings that apparent overlap is only batch entry would remove the time claim; duplicate or incomplete checks would undermine quality.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation/screen | Adds early S1 and preliminary C1 work; may save 1–3 h of later C1 reading or duplicate it. | Target's post-Assessment C1 wait if reserved slot works. | C1 queue for urgent unreserved cases; possibly S1. | Other high-risk/high-value cases awaiting C1, then M1. | Ambiguous; can be negative under scarce C1 capacity. | Reserved slots and priority jumps displace work; partial packets can cause repeat service. |
| K-219 D2 routing | Moves a handwritten check from D1 to D2; D2 may take longer and create rework. | D1 queue for complex corporate and other cases. | D2 queue, and perhaps A2 after rework. | D1 clients may gain; D2 clients and their assessors may wait. | Positive only if earlier start outweighs slower service and error/rework. | Load-dependent queue delay and a possible extra external-response loop. |
| K-337 stop second loop | Avoids later D1 and A1 work and the second external wait; may add appeals and remediation. | D1/A1 queues and time to initial decision. | C1/M1 if decisions arrive earlier; appeals/quality teams later. | Cases behind D1/A1 may gain; cases needing C1/M1 may lose. | Fast initial closure, potentially negative total resolution or quality. | Weekend crossing and threshold effect of unresolved evidence. |
| K-411 parallel verification | Similar check service, but D2 demand and A2 arrivals move earlier; logging effort rises. | Identity-to-document handoff wait. | D2 at earlier hours and A2 queue. | Cases already waiting for D2 or A2. | Small positive or neutral if logging is sound. | Synchronization at the later check; queue saturation can absorb readiness. |

For 100 similar cases in one week, the single-case gains cannot simply be multiplied by 100. K-104 would send many packets into the sole certified reviewer’s calendar. A provisional slot is real capacity only when C1 is free; holding slots for incomplete files can delay urgent non-target reviews. Release deadlines, priority rules, and measured C1 service saved per packet determine whether preparation helps throughput. S1 may also become congested. Earlier final reviews can shift the next queue to M1.

Moving K-219 checks from D1 to D2 creates a substitution effect: D1 can process complex cases sooner, while D2’s standard-case queue grows. Handwriting may increase D2 service time or clarification probability, so a modest change in rework frequency can consume the whole saved D1 wait and create new A2 demand. The first external reply may cross a weekend; a one-day service delay can then become several calendar days. K-337 avoids a supplier wait and later D1/A1 visits, but advances evidence-limited decisions to C1/M1 and may create appeals that are absent from the initial cycle metric. It also changes the mix of approvals and rejections.

K-411 moves document work and assessment arrivals earlier without necessarily changing total service. If A2 is saturated, the assessment queue absorbs most verification gain and may delay cases previously ahead of the new arrivals. True concurrency requires overlapping service intervals on I2 and D2; simultaneous batch timestamps alone prove nothing. Across all policies, track non-target wait distributions, occupancy, rework, decision mix, and ultimate resolution. The overlapping factual D1 intervals identified above must be reconciled before using these traces to fit any joint capacity simulation.

## 6. Pilot designs

Intervention 1 is K-411; intervention 2 is K-219.

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| eligibility | Digital, standard documents; no originals; both verification tasks needed; no exceptional lock restriction. | Originals received; standard but handwritten documents; no high-risk/high-value flag; comparable handwriting difficulty graded before assignment. |
| assignment rule | Randomize eligible cases within arrival-time and load strata to controlled parallel tasks or current serial creation; analyze assigned policy even if workaround fails. | Randomize at originals receipt within D1/D2 load and handwriting strata to D2-first or D1-first; no preemption, and record actual worker. |
| sample/support logic | Stage at low volume, then collect roughly 40–60 cases per arm across weekdays and queue states; retain non-target cases for interference analysis. | Collect roughly 40–60 cases per arm over multiple weeks with both resources available in overlapping load states; extend follow-up for rare errors and appeals. |
| primary outcome | Register-to-Notify elapsed time, reported with median, tail, and uncertainty; include an intention-to-treat contrast. | Register-to-Notify elapsed time, reported with median, tail, and uncertainty; include an intention-to-treat contrast. |
| process-path outcome | Actual overlap minutes; time both checks are ready; wait to A2; duplicate or missing task rate. | Receipt-to-check start/finish, check-to-Assessment wait, Request More Info/repeat-check rate, and worker crossover. |
| quality outcome | Blinded audit of verification completeness, decision corrections, and conflicting task ownership. | Blinded reread of a sample of handwriting, transcription errors, decision corrections, and subsequent rework/appeals. |
| queue-spillover outcome | D2 and A2 waits for non-target cases, plus M1 signing delay. | D1 and D2 waits for non-target cases, A2 load, and complex-document throughput. |
| logging validation | Compare system timestamps with task acquisition, edit, and release logs; sample direct observations; reject batch-entered overlap as concurrency. | Match originals receipt, resource start/end, and request timestamps to audit logs; verify all D1/D2 intervals are nonoverlapping. |
| stopping rule | Pause immediately for duplicate ownership, missing evidence, or false overlap; stop expansion if non-target A2/D2 90th-percentile waits rise over 20% for two weekly cohorts. | Pause immediately for a material misread or unsafe decision; stop expansion if clarification rises over 10 percentage points or non-target D2 90th-percentile wait rises over 20% for two cohorts. |

## 7. Final synthesis

Controlled parallel verification in K-411 appears most robust. It preserves the original evidence and decision rules, uses two distinct resources, and can be reversed if the workaround fails. Its likely cycle-time benefit is modest: earlier document completion helps only if A2 and M1 have earlier slots. That modesty is part of the conclusion, since the factual case already spends a long period waiting after verification. A pilot should measure both actual overlap and final notice, rather than treating early readiness as success by itself.

Suppressing K-337’s second information loop is the most dangerous despite its potential speed. It removes a supplier wait that crosses a weekend and later D1/A1 work, but it also forces a final decision with a known discrepancy. C1 must review after Assessment 2, and an approval with conditions is defensible only if the unresolved evidence may legally be handled that way. A rejection can be procedurally sound yet provoke an appeal; an unsafe approval may create losses long after Notify. The large first-notice saving therefore says little about ultimate resolution.

K-104 is the least transportable local counterfactual to a 100-case policy. One case may gain from a reserved C1 slot, while 100 early reservations compete for the only certified reviewer and displace urgent work. The queue can grow even when each prepared review is shorter. K-219 also has strong interference through D1-to-D2 substitution and extra rework, but its two-resource tradeoff is easier to test with randomized routing.

The most valuable added data are actual resource acquisition and release times, priority and reservation logs, arrival rates by risk and document difficulty, external request and response timestamps, A2/C1/M1 availability, and non-target queue waits. Record what discrepancy remained, the legal basis for conditional decisions, corrected decisions, appeals, and final resolution. First reconcile the overlapping factual D1 intervals; otherwise an apparently precise shared-capacity forecast rests on an impossible calendar.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural:** Each row is interpreted as a separate representative case scenario until the overlapping D1 factual intervals are reconciled; no joint schedule is inferred from them.
2. **Temporal:** Overnight service is possible, as the factual K-104 document check itself spans Mon night; proposed overnight D2 work still requires verified availability.
3. **Resource:** S1, C1, A2, D2, and M1 have the illustrative free slots only in the stated conditional branches; queue estimates alone do not establish them.
4. **Behavioral:** Early K-104 packets contain enough reliable detail to save some final reading, and unusable C1 reservations are released promptly.
5. **Quality:** D2 can sometimes read K-219's handwriting correctly, but may require clarification or make a consequential transcription error.
6. **Structural:** In K-337, rejection on the existing record is an available decision; conditional approval is considered only when the governing standard permits it.
7. **Temporal:** The proposed K-411 overlap represents actual concurrent service, while A2 and M1 may still retain their factual starts.
8. **Interference:** Applying a policy to 100 cases changes shared queues, priority order, and possibly rework and appeals, so single-case cycle changes do not add linearly.
