## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity is judged by **control objective impact**, not edit distance. Score 1–5 as follows:

| Severity | Meaning | Typical drivers |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Harmless noise or fully fitting behavior | UI telemetry, transport retries, silent routing (`tau`) with no control meaning |
| 2 | Documentation/model-scope mismatch without payment-control breach | Out-of-scope denial/withdrawal forcing model completions; label aliasing with otherwise complete controls |
| 3 | Evidence gap or delayed/alternate-channel control with residual uncertainty | Missing core event with external artifact; notification only in linked CRM/portal; mutable linkage |
| 4 | Material policy or sequencing failure on non-trivial exposure | Auto-approve against P1/P2; missing mandated fraud screen on high-value/high-risk; approval after pay on medium/high amounts |
| 5 | Severe irreversible control failure on large exposure or clear bypass of mandated dual control | Pay-before-approve on large amounts; high-value HIGH-risk paid without evidenced fraud screen; large auto-approve violations with bank-change/fraud flags |

**Factors that raise severity:** amount/exposure, irreversibility of payment, timing inversion (pay before approval), customer/regulatory harm (notification failure, unfair denial handling), recurrence across the population, and weak evidence reliability.

**Factors that lower severity:** low amount, reversible/no payment, alternate compliant channel with retained evidence (P4), pure telemetry (P5), and disputed model scope for non-payable paths (P6).

**Confidence  severity.** Confidence is the reliability that the interpreted business story is true given logs, overlays, and external artifacts. High severity can pair with medium confidence (e.g., missing Fraud Screen may be “not logged” vs “not done”). Low severity can pair with high confidence (clear retry duplicate). Confidence rises with stable identifiers, immutable linked evidence, consistent population patterns, and corroborating documents; it falls with verbal-only attestations, mutable text links, post-hoc mapping changes, and scope disputes.

Raw alignment cost is only a technical fitness signal under uniform move costs. It deliberately ignores amount, policy predicates, irreversibility, and evidence quality.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

**Pattern A — Notification retry duplicates**  
- **Cases:** CL-603; frequency evidence: 74 month cases with `L(Notify Claimant)` after sync notify, same message ID, one connector.  
- **Business risk:** Low (duplicate technical events; customer usually notified once). Inflated fitness noise.  
- **Cause:** Non-idempotent retrying transport connector.  
- **Confidence:** High.  
- **Owner:** Integration / notifications platform.

**Pattern B — External-adjuster Assess Loss gap**  
- **Cases:** CL-606; frequency: 9 high-value cases with `M(Assess Loss)` + external adjuster doc; 2 lack stable doc-to-case ID.  
- **Business risk:** Medium—may hide skipped assessment or only logging gap; weak audit trail on large losses.  
- **Cause:** External adjuster work outside core BPMN event emission; fragile document linkage.  
- **Confidence:** Medium.  
- **Owner:** Claims ops + document management / core claims IT.

**Pattern C — Event label / vocabulary mismatch**  
- **Cases:** CL-611 (`Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`).  
- **Business risk:** Low–medium (false deviations, broken monitoring, delayed detection of real skips).  
- **Cause:** UI/procedure vs model naming; mapping updated after case.  
- **Confidence:** High for mechanism.  
- **Owner:** Data governance / process mining COE.

**Pattern D — Out-of-scope denial and withdrawal (model completeness)**  
- **Cases:** CL-607, CL-612; note: denied/withdrawn drive most raw_cost  4.  
- **Business risk:** Low for payment loss; medium for misleading conformance dashboards and wasted investigation.  
- **Cause:** Tree designed for payable claims only (P6); no Deny/Withdraw terminals.  
- **Confidence:** High.  
- **Owner:** Process owner + model owner (joint scope decision).

**Pattern E — Data-aware auto-approval failures (sync but non-compliant)**  
- **Cases:** CL-609, CL-610; frequency: 4 fully synchronous Auto Approve paths violating P1/P2.  
- **Business risk:** High—mid/high value or payee-change payments skip mandated controls while looking “perfect.”  
- **Cause:** XOR routing without data guards (amount, risk, bank-change).  
- **Confidence:** High.  
- **Owner:** Claims control owner + rules engine / BPM product.

**Pattern F — Missing or unevidenced Fraud Screen on mandated path**  
- **Cases:** CL-602 (primary); related pressure in CL-604 sequencing.  
- **Business risk:** Very high on large HIGH-risk / bank-change claims.  
- **Cause:** Verbal workaround; optional system step; culture over control.  
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.  
- **Owner:** Fraud & SIU + claims authorization.

**Pattern G — Pay-before-approve (sequencing / irreversible control break)**  
- **Cases:** CL-604.  
- **Business risk:** Severe—funds released before authority.  
- **Cause:** Payment service not gated on approval completion; possible parallel jobs or manual pay API.  
- **Confidence:** High (timestamped bank accept vs approval).  
- **Owner:** Payment operations + application controls.

**Pattern H — UI telemetry treated as business log moves**  
- **Cases:** CL-601 (`Open/Close Workbench`, `Autosave`).  
- **Business risk:** Negligible operationally; pollutes cost and queues.  
- **Cause:** Front-end logging continuum without business filter (P5).  
- **Confidence:** High.  
- **Owner:** Telemetry / process mining data engineering.

**Pattern I — Alternate-channel Notify with weak linkage**  
- **Cases:** CL-608.  
- **Business risk:** Medium on high-value—regulatory/customer duty may be met in spirit but not provable.  
- **Cause:** Multi-channel notify; mutable CRM text reference.  
- **Confidence:** Low-medium on compliance proof.  
- **Owner:** Customer communications + records management.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

**CL-602 — Missing Fraud Screen**  
1. Fraud screen never performed (bypass).  
2. Performed offline/verbally, never recorded.  
3. Performed in another system; correlation failed.  
**Discriminators:** SIU system tickets timestamped before Manual Approve; email/recorded call artifacts; whether Manual Approve UI can proceed without fraud-complete flag; peer cases same approver. *Absence in core log  proof of non-occurrence—but verbal-only is non-compliant evidence.*

**CL-604 — Pay before Manual Approve**  
1. True control bypass (pay API without approval token).  
2. Clock skew / timezone between bank and core.  
3. Approval done earlier under different event name not aligned.  
**Discriminators:** NTP/clock audit; payment service authz logs (approval ID required?); immutable approval object `created_at` vs bank `accepted_at`; any earlier approve events under aliases.

**CL-606 / Pattern B — M(Assess Loss) with external report**  
1. Assessment done externally only (acceptable if policy allows) but not projected to core event.  
2. Assessment skipped; document is template/boilerplate.  
3. Document belongs to another case (weak ID).  
**Discriminators:** Stable document-to-case cryptographic link; adjuster signature time vs fraud/manual times; content specificity to loss; policy text on external assessors; rate of the 2/9 unlinked docs.

**CL-609 & CL-610 / Pattern E — Sync Auto Approve violating P1**  
1. Rules engine never encoded P1 data guards.  
2. Guards exist but bank-change/amount attributes stale or wrong at decision time.  
3. Emergency override used but not logged as Manual.  
**Discriminators:** Decision-service snapshot (amount, risk, bank-change_age) at Auto Approve; override audit table; compare attribute store vs payment file for CL-610 payee IBAN change time.

**CL-607 — Deny vs model completions**  
1. Denied leads in scope; model defective.  
2. Denied leads out of scope; log correctly shows Deny; alignment cost is artifact.  
3. Some “denies” later reopen into payable claims (mixed population).  
**Discriminators:** Process owner charter; % of Deny that later pay; separate intake SPA/log stream; re-open events.

**CL-608 — M(Notify Claimant) with CRM message**  
1. Claimant duly notified via portal; core omit only.  
2. CRM text fabricated/edited later (mutable field).  
3. Message drafted but not delivered.  
**Discriminators:** Portal immutable send receipt, message-ID, delivery status from provider; field audit history; claimant acknowledgment.

**CL-611 — Assess Damage vs Assess Loss**  
1. Pure synonym / label mismatch.  
2. Different activity (damage inspection  loss assessment).  
3. Mapping changed post-hoc to beautify conformance.  
**Discriminators:** UI screenshot/procedure version at case date; whether both events can co-occur; git/history of mapping table; assessor role performing `Assess Damage`.

**CL-603 / Pattern A — Double Notify**  
1. Technical retry, single business notify.  
2. Two real notifications to claimant.  
**Discriminators:** Identical message ID + payload hash + retry connector metrics (already supported by 74-case pattern).

**CL-612 — Withdrawal**  
1. Normal early termination; model gap only.  
2. Withdrawal used to mask irregular pre-pay activity.  
**Discriminators:** Payment attempts after withdraw; user who recorded withdraw vs claimant channel; frequency of withdraw after high assess amounts.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment `raw_cost` under this study is uniform edit distance (every visible L/M costs 1; tau 0). Business risk is a function of **control objective, exposure, irreversibility, evidence quality, and policy predicates**. Using at least five cases:

1. **CL-602 (cost 1) vs CL-601 (cost 3):** CL-602 is a EUR 480k HIGH-risk recent-bank-change payment without evidenced fraud screen—among the worst control failures—yet costs only one model move. CL-601’s cost 3 is three UI telemetry L-moves on a compliant EUR 1.2k claim. **Cost-function design** without business weights inverts priority.

2. **CL-609 & CL-610 (cost 0):** Fully synchronous alignments—including hidden-fit structural path—still violate **data conditions** in P1/P2 (amount 18k auto-approve; bank change + auto-approve). **Fitness can be perfect while policy fails** because the process tree XOR does not encode guards. Zero cost is not zero risk.

3. **CL-604 (cost 3) and P3:** The alignment cheaply “repairs” order with L(Pay), M(Fraud), S(Manual), M(Pay). Technically neat; business-wise payment already left at 10:03 before approval 10:20. **Irreversibility** of funds release is invisible to edit distance. P3 explicitly marks this as severe regardless of cheap repair.

4. **CL-607 & CL-612 (cost 4):** Highest technical costs are mostly **scope/model mismatch**—forced Assess/Auto/Pay(/Notify) after Deny or Withdraw. No payment intended  low payment risk. **Scope** and missing lifecycle branches dominate cost without dominating risk. Population note: denied/withdrawn drive most cost  4.

5. **CL-605 (cost 0, tau):** **Hidden moves** are fitting by design. Cost correctly ignores them; they must not be confused with missing controls (contrast M(Fraud Screen) in CL-602).

6. **CL-603 (cost 1) & 74-case retry pattern; CL-601 telemetry:** **Duplicated telemetry/transport** creates L-moves that are not business activities (P5). Cost accumulates noise.

7. **CL-606 & CL-608 (cost 1 each):** **Missing-but-external evidence** (adjuster report; CRM portal notify) means M-moves may be logging/correlation gaps, not skipped work—or may be real skips when linkage is mutable/unstable. Same cost cannot express evidence reliability or amount (EUR 75k / 250k).

8. **CL-611 (cost 2):** **Activity granularity/labeling**—`Assess Damage` vs `Assess Loss`—creates L+M pair for one synonym. Substance may be complete; cost still 2.

9. **Uniform cost configuration** stated in evidence (“no business-specific weighting”) guarantees decoupling from exposure, customer harm, and recurrence. Therefore raw_cost is at best a diagnostic breadcrumb, never a severity rank.
