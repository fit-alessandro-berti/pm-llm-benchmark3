## 6. Evidential Sufficiency and Remediation

Policy breach vs logging defect: P-501 shows late approvals but emergency justification documented; this is a logging/ timing defect rather than intentional evasion. P-502 and P-503 are policy breaches with missing pre-commitment Compliance and segregation. P-504 is scope misuse of emergency procedures. P-505 is late evidence of a timely act due to ERP clock offset; contemporaneous supplier acknowledgement supports compliance. P-506 is inability to prove compliance due to missing written extension. P-507 and P-508 are policy breaches due to missing Finance approval.

Immediate remediation:
* Freeze further commitments relying on verbal ECD-2 extension; require written CRO exception A7 for any post-18:00 activity.
* Retroactively obtain missing Compliance_Clearance for P-502 and P-503 or formally record deviation with risk acceptance.
* Reconstruct authorization chain for P-501 to confirm delegation or re-approve commitment.
* Obtain signed Incident Command authorizations within 4h for all ECD-2 purchases; reconcile ERP timestamps with telephone recordings and signed PDFs.
* Segregation check: ensure requester/committer cannot be Incident Command authorizer for same purchase.

Monitoring: dashboard of pre-commitment approvals vs commit timestamps; alert on new supplier without Compliance_Clearance; weekly review of emergency form justifications against 6h stoppage evidence.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

Replace A7 and ECD-2 interaction with:
* Emergency exception to GPIP-4 may only be issued in writing by Chief Risk Officer, scoped to purchase type, supplier status, amount cap and expiry, and must reference GPIP-4 clause(s) waived. COO/Incident Commander directives may accelerate workflow but cannot waive GPIP-A1 segregation, GPIP-A2 Finance/budget owner pre-commitment, or GPIP-A3 Compliance clearance for new suppliers or >100k.
* During a declared incident, Incident Command joint authorization satisfies the “business approver” role for GPIP-A2 budget owner provided the authorizer is a named budget owner or formally delegated. Finance Controller approval remains required pre-commitment unless CRO exception expressly waives it.
* Emergency definition for SOP-B4 aligns with GPIP-4: imminent safety harm or production stoppage expected 6h, evidenced by engineering assessment recorded before commitment.
* Supplier-onboarding waiver for unapproved suppliers is limited to incident window and critical lots as defined by a pre-approved list or real-time risk assessment signed by two Incident Command members. Waiver expires with incident directive.
* Retrospective evidence: verbal authorizations must be recorded within 4h with signed PDF or system entry; ERP timestamps after outage are deemed provisional and must be corroborated by contemporaneous evidence.
* Safeguard against normalization: any emergency exception exceeding 72h or 5% of monthly procurement spend triggers automatic CRO review and board reporting.

This resolves authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, retrospective records and expiry while preventing emergency normalization.
