## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

March’s raw-mapped fitness divergence is primarily a measurement and provenance alarm. Raw fitness fell from 0.93 in February to 0.55 in March, whereas mapped fitness was 0.90. The simultaneous jump from nine to eleven labels, 4% duplicated `Open Case` events, and higher resource missingness make migration the most direct explanation. It does not establish that every March case followed the old business process. Mapping assumes `Eligibility Screen` has the same business semantics and permissible position as `Validate Eligibility`; it may conceal changed screen rules, partial adoption, or unmapped activities. Mapped fitness also remained slightly below baseline.

Duplicated technical `Open Case` events create spurious starts and edges, inflating variants, DFG differences, and raw alignment deviations. They should be removed only when an event-identity, timestamp, or adjacency rule identifies the duplicate, not by dropping every `Open Case`. Missing resources need not alter activity sequencing, but they bias team workload, handoff/social-network, and resource-specific service-time analyses. Their concentration in migrated activities makes complete-case resource analysis especially unsafe.

Later drift is not dismissible as logging alone. The supplied post-mapping DFG rates show `Assess -> Request Info` rising from 12 to 24-29 per 100 cases after May, with a parallel return edge. Cycle-time medians and especially p90s rose, while reconstructed pre-assessment waiting rose far more than Assess service time. These are operational signatures that a label substitution cannot create. Nevertheless, because several metrics came from operational tables rather than one fully normalized event log, they should be recomputed from a canonical event log before estimating exact effect sizes.

where \(r_{m,e}\) is the supplied occurrence rate per 100 cases for one of the seven listed relations. This is a mean absolute rate difference, not a probability-based graph distance.

The distance treats the seven listed relations as a fixed fragment. Omitted edges are ignored rather than assumed absent, so this understates drift caused by unseen new edges and cannot establish full-model conformance.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Event/schema universe | Event month, source system, raw activity, mapped activity, event identifier, resource-present flag | Detect label, schema, duplicate-event, and timestamp drift before business interpretation. | No case threshold; report counts and proportions with denominators. | Raw versus normalized logs; pre/post migration provenance. | Low for descriptive schema monitoring, but technical events can distort case starts. |
| 1. Primary registered-case cohort | Business `Register` month, case status, fixed follow-up/maturity flag | Preserve whole traces and avoid DFGs built from clipped monthly event windows. | At least 200 registered cases per month; otherwise use a rolling cohort and flag reduced precision. | Overall standardized trend, conformance, variants, outcomes. | Restricting to completed cases can induce informative censoring; retain open cases in survival-style performance measures. |
| 2. Core risk strata | Supplier Z/non-Z × low/high complexity | Separate supplier-driven behavior from changing composition. | At least 75 cases per cell per month and at least 30 cases exposed to the relevant pathway; otherwise pool three months or use partial pooling. | Supplier effects within complexity; composition standardization. | Supplier and complexity are not randomly assigned and may encode customer/region differences. |
| 3. Priority and complexity | Priority class × complexity × pre/post July | Test whether priority redistributed queue delay. | At least 75 cases per class/month; use rolling three-month estimates where necessary. | High versus standard waiting, routing, and outcomes after policy onset. | Priority may be determined by information acquired after registration; it is not suitable for estimating an unconditioned total effect. |
| 4. Assessment team and resource quality | Team, workload band, resource-present/resource-missing, migrated activity indicator | Diagnose capacity, reassignment, and provenance issues without discarding missing-resource events. | At least 150 assessment events per team-month; otherwise pool teams with documented common staffing. | Team queue/service profiles; missingness-adjusted comparisons. | Team assignment may respond to backlog; resource missingness is plausibly non-random. |
| 5. Supplier-region/customer strata | Supplier, region, customer segment, complexity | Address Supplier Z’s non-random customer and regional coverage. | At least 200 cases per quarter or a pre-specified hierarchical model. | Supplier comparison within similar regions/customer groups. | Fine segmentation can create sparse cells and implicitly condition on variables affected by operational routing. |
| 6. Pathway diagnostics | Rework/no-rework, senior/no-senior, outcome, queue-age band | Diagnose where delay and conformance loss arise after a drift alert. | At least 100 starting cases and 20 pathway occurrences; otherwise descriptive only. | Loop duration, decision completion, route-specific waiting. | High: these are often post-treatment variables or mediators. |

The primary monitoring population should be all cases whose business `Register` event falls in a calendar month, with a pre-specified maturity rule and separate treatment of unresolved cases. It preserves complete traces, avoids event-window clipping, does not select on later rework or outcome, and supports both aggregate monitoring and composition-standardized reporting. Schema monitors should still use the full event universe because technical drift can occur before or outside a valid business-case trace.

| Backlog/capacity monitor | Arrival rate, work-in-process, queue age, staffing FTE, overtime hours | Weekly operational baseline with seasonal forecast | Alert when arrivals plus rework exceed available capacity forecast for a sustained period. | Trigger capacity plan, supplier escalation, or priority-rule review. | Delayed event ingestion, planned leave, holiday closure. | Workforce planning |

Seasonality should be handled with calendar-aware reference windows, preferably the same renewal weeks in prior years. Until multiple years of event-level history exist, operational metrics should be reported both raw and standardized to a fixed supplier/complexity mix, with explicit uncertainty around October-November estimates.

For multiple testing, use a hierarchy rather than a universal threshold. Treat schema failures as immediate high-severity incidents. For behavioral and performance monitors, group related tests into families such as schema, control flow, performance, and outcomes; control false discoveries within each family, require persistence across two windows where operationally safe, and seek corroboration from an independent artifact such as DFG plus queue profile.

try:

    pm4py = None


def _require_pm4py():
    if pm4py is None:
        raise ImportError(
            "Part B requires PM4Py. Install it with: pip install pm4py"
        )
    return pm4py


def format_event_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
):
    """
    Copy and format an event dataframe for PM4Py without mutating the caller's
    dataframe. Resource columns may be missing; they are not required here.
    """
    p = _require_pm4py()
    x = df.copy(deep=True)

    required = [case_id_col, activity_col, timestamp_col]
    missing = [c for c in required if c not in x.columns]
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing required event-log columns: {missing}")

    if x[required].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Case ID, activity, and timestamp cannot be null.")

    x[timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(x[timestamp_col], errors="raise")
    x = x.sort_values([case_id_col, timestamp_col], kind="stable")

    return p.format_dataframe(
        x,
        case_id=case_id_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )


def slice_time_window(
    formatted_df,
    start,
    end,
    case_id_col="case_id",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    mode="events",
):
    """
    Slice a formatted PM4Py dataframe with PM4Py's time-range filter.

    mode='events' can create partial traces and is suitable for event/schema
    monitoring. For control-flow discovery, prefer the cohort_monthly_dfgs()
    function below, which retains full traces for cases registered in a month.

    Use a non-overlapping end timestamp, for example the instant before the
    next month, if adjacent windows must not share boundary events.
    """
    p = _require_pm4py()
    return p.filter_time_range(
        formatted_df,
        pd.Timestamp(start),
        pd.Timestamp(end),
        mode=mode,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
        case_id_key=case_id_col,
    )


def cohort_monthly_dfgs(
    formatted_df,
    case_id_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    registration_activity="Register",
):
    """
    Discover monthly DFGs on whole traces of cases registered in each month.

    The registration_activity parameter avoids assigning a case to a cohort
    using a technical Open Case event where a business Register event exists.
    Cases without Register fall back to their first observed event.
    """
    p = _require_pm4py()
    x = formatted_df.copy()

    case_start = x.groupby(case_id_col)[timestamp_col].min()

    if registration_activity is not None:
        register_times = (
            x.loc[x[activity_col].eq(registration_activity)]
            .groupby(case_id_col)[timestamp_col]
            .min()
        )
        # Replace first-event time only where a business Register is observed.
        case_start.update(register_times)

    cohort_month = case_start.dt.to_period("M")
    dfg_by_month = {}
    support_by_month = {}

    for month in sorted(cohort_month.dropna().unique()):
        case_ids = cohort_month.index[cohort_month.eq(month)]
        cohort_log = x.loc[x[case_id_col].isin(case_ids)].copy()

        # This discovers a frequency DFG. Use discover_performance_dfg separately
        # if pair-level elapsed-time DFG performance is also required.
        dfg_by_month[str(month)] = p.discover_dfg(
            cohort_log,
            activity_key=activity_col,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
            case_id_key=case_id_col,
        )
        support_by_month[str(month)] = int(cohort_log[case_id_col].nunique())

    return dfg_by_month, support_by_month


def discover_window_artifacts(
    formatted_window,
    case_id_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    include_log_skeleton=False,
    include_declare=False,
):
    """
    Discover core artifacts for one already-defined cohort or time window.
    Do not use an event-clipped window for DFG/variant inference unless partial
    traces are explicitly intended and documented.
    """
    p = _require_pm4py()

    artifacts = {
        "dfg": p.discover_dfg(
            formatted_window,
            activity_key=activity_col,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
            case_id_key=case_id_col,
        ),
        "variants": p.get_variants(
            formatted_window,
            activity_key=activity_col,
            case_id_key=case_id_col,
        ),
        "temporal_profile": p.discover_temporal_profile(
            formatted_window,
            activity_key=activity_col,
            timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
            case_id_key=case_id_col,
        ),
    }

    if include_log_skeleton:
        artifacts["log_skeleton"] = p.discover_log_skeleton(
            formatted_window,
            activity_key=activity_col,
            case_id_key=case_id_col,
        )

    if include_declare:
        if not hasattr(p, "discover_declare"):
            raise AttributeError(
                "This PM4Py installation does not expose pm4py.discover_declare."
            )
        artifacts["declare_model"] = p.discover_declare(
            formatted_window,
            activity_key=activity_col,
            case_id_key=case_id_col,
        )

    return artifacts


def build_raw_and_normalized_logs(
    df,
    label_mapping,
    case_id_col="case_id",
    activity_col="activity",
    timestamp_col="timestamp",
    normalized_activity_col="activity_normalized",
    technical_duplicate_mask=None,
):
    """
    Return raw and normalized PM4Py dataframes without overwriting the original
    dataframe or replacing its raw activity column.

    Example label_mapping:
        {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}

    technical_duplicate_mask must be a callable receiving the normalized
    dataframe and returning True only for events proven to be technical
    duplicates, for example the second event in a same-case retry pair with
    matching source-event identity. It must NOT simply remove all Open Case
    events.
    """
    raw_input = df.copy(deep=True)
    normalized_input = df.copy(deep=True)

    normalized_input[normalized_activity_col] = (
        normalized_input[activity_col].replace(label_mapping)
    )

    if technical_duplicate_mask is not None:
        mask = pd.Series(
            technical_duplicate_mask(normalized_input),
            index=normalized_input.index,
        ).fillna(False).astype(bool)

        normalized_input = normalized_input.loc[~mask].copy()

    raw_log = format_event_dataframe(
        raw_input,
        case_id_col=case_id_col,
        activity_col=activity_col,
        timestamp_col=timestamp_col,
    )

    normalized_log = format_event_dataframe(
        normalized_input,
        case_id_col=case_id_col,
        activity_col=normalized_activity_col,
        timestamp_col=timestamp_col,
    )

    return {
        "raw_log": raw_log,
        "normalized_log": normalized_log,
        "normalized_event_dataframe": normalized_input,
        "label_mapping": dict(label_mapping),
    }

## 9. Provisional attribution

March is best treated as a semantic and provenance break, with confidence 95/100 for that component. The abrupt raw-fitness fall, new activity labels, duplicated `Open Case` events, and roughly fivefold increase in missing resources coincide exactly with migration. Mapping restores fitness to about 0.90, close to the 0.925 baseline. This does not prove behavioral invariance: `Eligibility Screen` could have different rules or timing, and mapped fitness remains modestly below baseline.

The strongest explanation for the May control-flow change is Supplier Z disruption, with confidence 80/100. Supplier Z share rose sharply at the same time that rework variants and normalized rework-loop edges roughly doubled. The causal claim remains qualified because Supplier Z cases were already more complex and non-randomly distributed across customers and regions. Supplier disruption plausibly caused both a composition change and a within-case document-quality effect; only the latter is behavioral drift.

The June-August performance deterioration is most plausibly a capacity-and-backlog problem interacting with supplier rework, with confidence 75/100. The staffing departure date, much larger increase in reconstructed waiting than Assess service time, and disproportionate p90 deterioration support queueing. Staffing cannot explain the May onset, however. A likely mediation chain is incomplete documents increasing loops, loops increasing assessment arrivals, reduced staffing lowering service capacity, and backlog amplifying elapsed time. The July priority rule is plausible as a redistributor of delay rather than an aggregate cure, but confidence is only 55/100 until high- and standard-priority queue profiles are compared.

October-November is strongly consistent with recurring seasonal population drift, confidence 82/100. Volume, complexity, and senior-assessment routing all rose. Capacity constraints likely mediated the effect into cycle-time deterioration. Senior-assessment recording inconsistency prevents treating its increase as pure behavioral change.

December improvement has moderate confidence, 60/100, as a joint effect of overtime, document-quality intervention, Supplier Z recovery, and lower seasonal load. I would revise this account if matched Supplier Z cases do not show elevated rework, if high-complexity cases did not receive faster post-July service than standard cases, if staffing/queue timestamps do not show capacity pressure, or if historical renewal seasons show a materially different standardized pattern.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness falls while mapped fitness and normalized DFG distance remain within their expected reference ranges, THEN open a schema/provenance incident and defer business-conformance escalation, BECAUSE a label or technical-event change is the most parsimonious mechanism.

2. IF Supplier Z cases show a sustained increase in complexity-adjusted rework relative to matched non-Z cases, THEN escalate document-quality remediation with Supplier Z and monitor loop duration weekly, BECAUSE incomplete bundles are likely generating real control-flow rework.

3. IF queue waiting before Assess rises while Assess active service time remains comparatively stable, THEN activate capacity and backlog management actions, BECAUSE the deterioration is more consistent with queueing than assessor speed.

4. IF the post-July wait-time gap widens between standard and high-priority cases after controlling for complexity and team, THEN review the priority rule and its service-level trade-off, BECAUSE priority may be redistributing delay rather than reducing total delay.

5. IF October-November raw performance worsens but composition-standardized performance remains within the seasonal reference range, THEN classify the change primarily as recurring population drift and plan capacity proactively, BECAUSE renewal mix rather than new pathway behavior is the likely driver.

6. IF resource missingness rises within particular migrated activities or teams, THEN suspend complete-case resource comparisons and repair identity propagation, BECAUSE non-random missingness can create false workload, handoff, and service-time conclusions.

7. IF standardized rework or normalized DFG distance remains elevated after Supplier Z recovery and backlog normalization, THEN investigate persistent behavioral adaptation or policy drift, BECAUSE a transient supplier or capacity shock should otherwise recede.
