## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands

**The target of intervention should be the handling policy, not an unexplained change in the recorded flag.** The supplied policy combines priority triage, frequent manual review, and possible escalation through shared queues. The score is an imperfect measure of underlying risk, while complexity can influence both flagging and processing. 

I adopt the following operational definitions to distinguish the supplied policies:

* **P0:** retain the current system.
* **P1:** hide the displayed badge, but retain the stored flag, score, available human information, and flag-dependent automated routing. Disabling those automated rules would be a different intervention.
* **P2:** retain priority triage but remove the flag-only compulsory referral to manual review. Human referrals and any independently required controls remain. The supplied material does not establish which reviews can safely be omitted.
* **P3:** retain routing and add one genuinely additional, qualified reviewer during a prespecified operating roster, dedicated to flagged manual-review cases. Existing review and senior capacity are not reassigned.
* **P4:** retain manual review and escalation criteria but add one genuinely additional, qualified senior reviewer to the shared escalation queue. Relaxing escalation criteria is a separate P4 variant requiring its own explicit rule.

These are specific versions of the policy families in the task, rather than interchangeable interpretations of “remove the flag.” 

Let \(F\) denote the original classification under a frozen intake rule, \(M\) manual-review occurrence, \(E\) escalation occurrence, \(T\) arrival-to-triage-completion time, and \(Y\) arrival-to-close time. A policy operates on the **connected queue network**, including its effects on other cases. Target populations include all eligible arrivals, not just cases selected because they have already closed.

**Framework.** I use total dynamic-policy effects and intervention-specific mechanism analogues, with E6 illustrating a controlled direct effect. I do **not** claim identified natural direct or indirect effects. A comparison such as \(Y(\pi_1,M(\pi_0))\) requires additional assumptions about incompatible policy histories, exposure-induced confounders, and shared queues that these data do not establish.

For E3–E5, the intervention must generate an admissible joint process: referral, readiness, service starts, resource availability, escalation, and closure. It must not assign a manual waiting time to a case that never enters review, or give every case a shorter wait while ignoring finite capacity. Their effects need not add to E1; their decomposition depends on intervention definitions and ordering.

A lack of observational overlap between flagged and unflagged cases does **not** prevent randomizing P3 among eligible flagged cases. That experiment changes capacity, not underlying risk or flag status.

Arrows are candidate mechanisms, not identified effects. Interference arises because routing and service completions alter the queue state faced by other cases; those cases’ delays belong to the policy effect. Priority also changes when a case reaches the manual queue, so a shorter triage wait can place it into a different congestion regime.

Triage findings, reviewer assignment, and evolving case readiness may be changed by flag handling and subsequently influence both later mediators and outcomes. They are therefore potential exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounders. Earlier routing changes later congestion, which can change subsequent routing: the graph must be unrolled in time rather than interpreted as a static acyclic case model.

Arrival load is pretreatment for an individual arrival but can be downstream of earlier cases’ policy exposure. Adjusting it away throughout a system-policy analysis can remove genuine spillovers. Conditioning on eventual review or escalation also selects post-policy groups. None of these observations establishes a natural direct/indirect decomposition.

## 3. Stage decomposition

All values below are **arithmetic means in local-clock hours**, calculated from the supplied timestamps. I assume a consistent clock offset over each case; no staffed-hours calendar is inferred. A recorded service interval is elapsed start-to-completion time, not necessarily uninterrupted labor. 

Optional-stage means are conditional on occurrence. The `n` cells show total cases followed by `[manual-review count, escalation count]`. `NA` means that no case in the subgroup performed that stage. Consequently, a mixed subgroup’s displayed conditional means do not simply sum to its mean cycle time.

“Residual-to-close” runs from the **last performed stage’s completion** to Close. Interstage gaps are not automatically pure queue waits: readiness, information gathering, handoffs, and staffing calendars are not separately recorded.

For additive accounting, optional blocks are occurrence-weighted:

$$
\bar Y
=
\bar W_T+\bar S_T
+p_M(\bar W_{M\mid M=1}+\bar S_{M\mid M=1})
+p_E(\bar W_{E\mid E=1}+\bar S_{E\mid E=1})
+\bar R.
$$

Wholly absent blocks are omitted; nonexistent stage measurements are not imputed as observed zeros.

Flagged cases finish triage in 0.84 hours on average, compared with 3.43 hours for unflagged cases, but close in 16.51 versus 8.17 hours. Occurrence-weighted accounting partitions the 8.34-hour difference into −2.59 hours at triage, +7.35 in the manual-review block, +3.65 in the escalation block, and −0.07 after the final stage. This is accounting, not a causal mediation decomposition.

Among reviewed cases, manual service averages 2.70 versus 2.75 hours; recorded service duration does not explain the large manual-block contrast. Even within High complexity, where every case is reviewed, pre-review gaps average 7.80 versus 2.17 hours. Flagged high-load cases also have longer pre-review gaps than flagged normal-load cases. However, risk scores do not overlap, triage resources differ completely, routing selects different cases, and the recorded gaps may include readiness delays or closed staffing periods. These contrasts therefore support a downstream-delay hypothesis without identifying its cause.

**Pilot P3.** **Eligibility:** all newly arriving cases retain the frozen intake score/flag rule and P0 routing; the additional slot serves flagged cases accepted for manual review. No existing reviewer or senior capacity is reassigned.

**Assignment:** randomize seven-day blocks for the entire connected queue network to P3 or P0, stratified by planned staffing and calendar. Record preceding assignments and opening backlog; analyze carryover explicitly and retain cross-block cases. This identifies the implemented switchback policy, not automatically permanent deployment.

**Guardrail:** monitor unflagged manual waits and all escalation waits. A proposed, not evidence-calibrated, guardrail is greater-than-10% deterioration in their 90th percentiles over two adequately supported blocks relative to P0 blocks, using prespecified calendar adjustment.

**Primary outcome:** all-arrival 72-hour restricted mean cycle time, with 72 hours a study horizon, not a supplied SLA. **Secondary outcomes:** flagged cycle time, stage-entry gaps, routing frequencies, tail waits, 30-day blinded audit errors, open-case counts, and staffing cost.

**Minimum data:** every shared-queue case, actual readiness/queue-entry/exit times, routing reasons, assignment times, roster availability, pauses, qualifications, and audit follow-up.

**Stop or pause** for verified control bypass, a critical intervention-related decision error, queue-guardrail breach, non-additional staffing, or unreliable timestamps. Predefine sample-size and stopping boundaries before enrollment.

## 7. Reproducible PM4Py-oriented Python

The script embeds all supplied records and preserves absent stages as missing measurements. It produces **82 interval/anchor events**: Register and Close are point anchors, while Triage, Manual Review, and Escalation retain their recorded start and completion times.

For the requested high-level performance DFG, the script deliberately uses a **separate completion-event projection**. Its edge durations therefore represent completion-to-completion lags, not pure waiting. The original interval dataframe remains unchanged, and stage waiting proxies are calculated explicitly. This avoids relying on the formatter alone to determine performance-DFG interval semantics. The API calls follow the documented PM4Py interfaces. ([Process Intelligence Solutions][1])

The Pandas calculations and reshaping were executed here. The PM4Py calls were checked against documentation but could not be executed because the package is unavailable in this runtime.

TIME_COLS = ["arrival", "triage_start", "triage_done", "manual_start",
             "manual_done", "escalation_start", "escalation_done", "close"]
PAIRS = {
    "triage_wait": ("arrival", "triage_start"),
    "triage_service": ("triage_start", "triage_done"),
    "manual_wait": ("triage_done", "manual_start"),
    "manual_service": ("manual_start", "manual_done"),
    "escalation_wait": ("manual_done", "escalation_start"),
    "escalation_service": ("escalation_start", "escalation_done"),
}
METRICS = list(PAIRS) + ["residual", "total"]


def parse_cases() -> pd.DataFrame:
    d = pd.read_csv(StringIO(CSV), na_values=["-"])
    for c in TIME_COLS:
        d[c] = pd.to_datetime(d[c], format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M", errors="raise")
    required = ["arrival", "triage_start", "triage_done", "close"]
    if d["case"].duplicated().any() or d[required].isna().any().any():
        raise ValueError("Duplicate case or missing required timestamp.")
    if not d["flag"].isin([0, 1]).all():
        raise ValueError("Flag must be 0 or 1.")
    for start, done in [("manual_start", "manual_done"),
                        ("escalation_start", "escalation_done")]:
        if (d[start].isna() != d[done].isna()).any():
            raise ValueError(f"Incomplete stage: {start}, {done}")
    d["M"] = d["manual_start"].notna()
    d["E"] = d["escalation_start"].notna()
    if (d["E"] & ~d["M"]).any():
        raise ValueError("Extend predecessor logic for escalation without review.")
    for metric, (start, done) in PAIRS.items():
        d[metric] = (d[done] - d[start]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    last = (d["escalation_done"].combine_first(d["manual_done"])
            .combine_first(d["triage_done"]))
    d["residual"] = (d["close"] - last).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    d["total"] = (d["close"] - d["arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    d["triage_completion"] = (
        d["triage_done"] - d["arrival"]).dt.total_seconds() / 3600
    if (d[METRICS] < 0).any().any():
        raise ValueError("Negative interval or invalid stage ordering.")
    # Sum present intervals only; absent-stage measurements remain NA.
    reconstructed = d[METRICS[:-1]].sum(axis=1, min_count=1)
    if (reconstructed - d["total"]).abs().gt(1e-9).any():
        raise ValueError("Stage intervals do not reconstruct cycle time.")
    return d


def make_events(d: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    # Register and Close are observed timestamp anchors, not measured services.
    stages = [("Register", "arrival", "arrival", None),
              ("Triage", "triage_start", "triage_done", "triage_resource"),
              ("Manual Review", "manual_start", "manual_done", "review_resource"),
              ("Escalation", "escalation_start", "escalation_done", None),
              ("Close", "close", "close", None)]
    rows = []
    for _, case in d.iterrows():
        for activity, start, done, resource in stages:
            if pd.isna(case[start]):
                continue  # Never create events for absent stages.
            rows.append({
                "case:concept:name": str(case["case"]),
                "concept:name": activity,
                "time:timestamp": case[done],
                "start_timestamp": case[start],
                "org:resource": case[resource] if resource else pd.NA,
                "flag": int(case["flag"]),
                "risk_score": case["risk_score"],
                "complexity": case["complexity"],
                "arrival_load": case["arrival_load"],
            })
    return (pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "start_timestamp"],
        kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True))


def subgroups(d: pd.DataFrame):
    groups = [(f"flag {f}", d[d["flag"].eq(f)]) for f in (0, 1)]
    groups += [(f"{c}, all", d[d["complexity"].eq(c)])
               for c in ("Low", "Medium", "High")]
    for c in ("Medium", "High"):
        for f in (0, 1):
            groups.append((f"{c}, flag {f}",
                           d[d["complexity"].eq(c) & d["flag"].eq(f)]))
    for load in ("High", "Normal"):
        groups.append((f"flag 1, load {load}",
                       d[d["flag"].eq(1) & d["arrival_load"].eq(load)]))
    for escalated in (True, False):
        groups.append((f"flag 1, escalation {escalated}",
                       d[d["flag"].eq(1) & d["E"].eq(escalated)]))
    return groups


def print_summaries(d: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    means, counts, small = [], [], []
    for name, g in subgroups(d):
        support = g[METRICS].count()
        means.append({"subgroup": name, "n": len(g),
                      "M": int(g["M"].sum()), "E": int(g["E"].sum()),
                      **g[METRICS].mean().to_dict()})
        counts.append({"subgroup": name, **support.to_dict()})
        if len(g) < 5 or ((support > 0) & (support < 5)).any():
            small.append(name)
    print("MEANS IN LOCAL-CLOCK HOURS; optional stages conditional on occurrence")
    print(pd.DataFrame(means).to_string(index=False, na_rep="NA",
                                       float_format=lambda x: f"{x:.3f}"))
    print("\nSUPPORT COUNTS FOR EACH METRIC")
    print(pd.DataFrame(counts).to_string(index=False))
    print("\nTRIAGE COMPLETION AND TOTAL CYCLE")
    print(d.groupby("flag")[["triage_completion", "total"]].mean())
    print("\nDECISION LABEL COUNTS (NOT ACCURACY)")
    print(pd.crosstab(d["flag"], d["outcome"]))
    print("\nRISK-SCORE SUPPORT")
    print(d.groupby("flag")["risk_score"].agg(["min", "max", "count"]))
    warnings.warn("Small subgroup or stage support (<5): " + "; ".join(small))
    lo = d.loc[d["flag"].eq(0), "risk_score"]
    hi = d.loc[d["flag"].eq(1), "risk_score"]
    if len(lo) and len(hi) and lo.max() < hi.min():
        warnings.warn("No observed risk-score overlap; no flagged Low cases either.")


def check_resource_intervals(events: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    overlaps = []
    known = events.dropna(subset=["org:resource"])
    for resource, g in known.groupby("org:resource"):
        for a, b in combinations(g.to_dict("records"), 2):
            start = max(a["start_timestamp"], b["start_timestamp"])
            end = min(a["time:timestamp"], b["time:timestamp"])
            if start < end:
                overlaps.append((resource, a["case:concept:name"],
                                 b["case:concept:name"],
                                 (end - start).total_seconds() / 3600))
    if overlaps:
        warnings.warn("Same-resource intervals overlap: service spans are not "
                      "necessarily uninterrupted labor or exclusive occupancy.")
        print("\nRESOURCE OVERLAPS (resource, case1, case2, hours)")
        print(overlaps)


def discover_graphs(events: pd.DataFrame) -> None:
    try:
        import pm4py
    except ImportError as exc:
        raise RuntimeError("Install pandas and pm4py==2.7.22 to run DFG discovery.") from exc
    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        events.copy(), case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp")
    print("\nPM4Py version:", pm4py.__version__)
    keys = dict(case_id_key="case:concept:name", activity_key="concept:name",
                timestamp_key="time:timestamp")
    for flag in (0, 1):
        sub = formatted.loc[formatted["flag"].eq(flag)].copy()
        freq, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub.copy(), **keys)
        # Deliberate completion-event projection, not an interval/waiting DFG.
        # Keep the original interval dataframe and its true starts unchanged.
        completion_view = sub.copy()
        completion_view["start_timestamp"] = completion_view["time:timestamp"]
        perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
            completion_view.copy(), business_hours=False,
            perf_aggregation_key="mean", **keys)
        perf_hours = {edge: float(value) / 3600 for edge, value in perf.items()}
        print(f"\nFLAG {flag}: {sub['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases")
        print("Frequency DFG:", freq)
        print("Start/end activities:", starts, ends)
        print("Mean completion-to-completion DFG hours:", perf_hours)
        warnings.warn("DFG differences are descriptive, not causal. Completion "
                      "lags can include destination service; use explicit stage "
                      "gaps for waiting proxies. Shared queues violate independence.")


def main(run_discovery: bool = True):
    warnings.simplefilter("always", UserWarning)
    d = parse_cases()
    events = make_events(d)
    print_summaries(d)
    print("\nEVENT PREVIEW:")
    print(events.head(8).to_string(index=False))
    check_resource_intervals(events)
    warnings.warn("Only 20 cases, one arrival day, no pre-policy control. "
                  "Pre-stage gaps need readiness, calendar and queue-entry validation.")
    if run_discovery:
        discover_graphs(events)
    return d, events


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()

## 8. Reversal conditions

1. **Hidden risk explains readiness rather than capacity.** If richer pretreatment information shows that flagged cases require substantial, legitimate preparation before they become review-ready, and little delay remains after eligibility, I would replace the manual-capacity-first interpretation with a preparation or information-availability explanation.

2. **Queue interference offsets the benefit.** If P3 reduces flagged review waits but increases senior or unflagged delays enough to eliminate system-wide improvement, I would reject scaling P3 alone and investigate P4 or coordinated capacity changes.

3. **Decision quality deteriorates.** If blinded audits reveal an unacceptable increase in material errors or serious missed issues under P3, I would withdraw the recommendation even if average cycle time falls. Equal Approve/Reject proportions would not override that finding.

4. **Timestamp semantics invalidate the decomposition’s operational interpretation.** If starts denote assignment rather than actual work, completions are batch-entered, or long intervals mainly represent pauses or non-operating periods, I would stop treating those intervals as evidence of a staffed queue bottleneck and redesign measurement before capacity deployment.

5. **Actionable support exists only in a narrower population.** If equivalent qualified capacity can be provided under both arms only for Medium-complexity cases, while High-complexity flagged cases require a different specialist pathway, I would reverse the all-flag pilot scope and restrict inference to the supported population rather than extrapolate.

6. **Reviewers change practice in response to capacity.** If reviewers expand discretionary work or lower escalation thresholds when the additional reviewer appears, producing no completion benefit, I would abandon the interpretation of P3 as primarily a waiting-time intervention and redesign the policy around the observed behavioral response.

## 9. Final synthesis

**Directly observed.** In these 20 completed cases, flagged cases finish triage sooner but close later: mean triage-completion times are 0.84 versus 3.43 hours, and total cycle times are 16.51 versus 8.17 hours. All flagged cases receive manual review, compared with four of ten unflagged cases; six versus two are escalated. Conditional manual-service durations are almost identical, while pre-review gaps differ substantially. 

**Plausible mechanism.** The policy accelerates entry into a downstream process with more review demand and potentially scarce capacity. Faster triage can coexist with longer completion because the bottleneck lies later. Risk-driven work, readiness delays, resource assignment, and escalation can reinforce that pattern. This is a coherent explanation, not an identified decomposition.

**Unidentified.** The data do not establish how much delay is caused by the displayed flag, automatic routing, congestion, underlying risk, or reviewer behavior. There is no observed risk-score overlap, no pre-policy comparison, and no independent decision-quality measure. Shared queues also prevent treating each case’s counterfactual timing as isolated.

**Most useful next evidence.** Pilot P3 with genuinely additional, qualified review capacity and unchanged routing, randomizing the connected queue’s operating regime. Measure actual queue eligibility, service availability, cross-case spillovers, and blinded decision quality. Improvement in system-wide cycle time without quality loss would support scaling that capacity policy—not the broader claim that removing the flag or manual review would be beneficial.
