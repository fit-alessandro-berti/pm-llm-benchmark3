**Convergence.** I use convergence to mean *fusion*: several logically independent executions are routed through one shared object (an event touching several objects of the same type, or several orders sharing a package/delivery/invoice), so flattening on that shared object welds distinct flows into a single case. Here: D2 fuses three ordershipments; V2 fuses two orders' billing; P3 and P4 fuse two orders' packing.

**Divergence.** I use divergence to mean *spread and inflation*: one logical execution fans out into multiple copied cases (e16 copied into O1, O2, O3), and/or one case accumulates repeated sibling activities that look like rework (O1 shows Dispatch twice; O3 shows Confirm Delivery twice) even though nothing was redone. Terminology varies across the literature; these definitions are fixed for this report and applied consistently.

**Copied shared events: evidence or duplication?** Both, in different respects. Structurally, e13, e16, e19, e22, e24, e25 etc. are faithful evidence of genuine multiobject synchronisation — they were performed once and genuinely concerned several objects. As *counting units* they are duplicates: any frequency, variant, or performance figure that adds rows naïvely inflates. The working rule: never delete the copies, never sum them blindly; always know the denominator is 31 true events versus N flattened rows.

**Permitted transformations.**
- T0 (default): strict use of the supplied E2O relations. No silent propagation through O2O.
- T1 (declared derivative, sensitivity only): propagate event participation along O2O paths (e.g., attach invoice/payment events to the orders a bill; attach package events to items via `packs`) with percopy provenance tags. This enlarges lifecycles but reintroduces the very ambiguity being studied (see §3.10).
- T2: qualifierpreserving joins — the O2O qualifiers (`partially_settles`, `completes_settlement`, `packs`, `bills`) survive any reshaping and are treated as analytic facts.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

3.1 **Crossorder package P3 under Order flattening.** e13 (`Pack Package`, O2+O3, I21+I31, P3) is copied into both cases. Pack Package has 4 true occurrences but 6 rows (e11, e12, e13×2, e14×2). Effect: workload metrics inflate ~50%; rework detectors may fire. Harmful for capacity figures; informative as evidence that O2 and O3 were synchronised on purpose.

3.2 **Crossorder package P4 contaminates O4's benchmarks.** Under Order flattening, O4's only Pack (e14), Dispatch (e17) and Confirm (e27) happen at the joint P4 schedule dictated partly by O3's item I32. O4 perorder throughput looks worse than O2's simply because its shipment partners differ. Harmful for perorder servicelevel comparison; informative for spotting coupling that a supplychain redesign could break.

3.3 **Consolidated delivery D2 vanishes or explodes depending on lens.** On Delivery flattening, D2 reads as an ordinary shipment (e16e24); the fact that it carries P2+P3 serving O1, O2, O3 leaves no trace. On Order flattening, e16 appears three times, so Dispatch shows 5 rows for 3 truths. Contextdependent: hidden consolidation is harmful for carrier costing; triple rows are harmful for frequencies but correct signals of who was affected.

3.4 **Consolidated invoice V2 manufactures a payment loop.** e19, e22, e25 are duplicated into O2's and O3's cases. Aggregating O2/O3 yields Record PaymentConfirm Delivery (e22e24) *and* Confirm DeliveryRecord Payment (e24e25), plus a Confirm selfloop on O3 (e24, e27). Harmful: a DFG miner reports a behavioural loop that never existed operationally; the real cause is instalments meeting multiorder billing.

3.5 **Partial payments lose their economics.** Payment flattening renders M2 and M3 as two indistinguishable "Record Payment" tokens; the distinction lives solely in the O2O qualifiers `partially_settles`/`completes_settlement`. Worse, both events bind O2 *and* O3 jointly, so no flattening can allocate instalment amounts per order. Harmful for receivables analytics; the split itself is legitimate evidence of credit terms.

3.6 **Nearsingleton lifecycle: Payment flattening is degenerate.** M1–M4 each yield exactly one event. `discover_dfg` finds no edges; start=end everywhere; elapsed time is undefined. This is precisely the prompt's "artificially short lifecycle" warning. Harmful as an analysis basis; useful only as a reference join key back to invoices.

3.7 **Delivery lifecycle truncation flatters upstream waits.** D3's measured life is e17 (11:15)  e27 (next day 10:00), ignoring that P4 finished packing at 10:20 and nothing shipped until 11:15. Transit KPIs cannot explain customervisible delay origin. Contextdependent: valid as pure transit measure, misleading as "fulfilment speed."

3.8 **Item flattening produces a false conformity story.** All six items realise the identical 4step variant (e.g., I31: e08e13e16e24). A conformance check passes at 100 %, yet I11 travelled alone in D1 while I31 shared P3 and D2 with another order's goods. Harmful if reported as "process is uniform"; the truth is uniform *activity syntax* hiding divergent *physical topology*. Finance is unreachable from item cases altogether.

3.9 **Package flattening doublebooks the same instant.** Because e16 and e24 name both P2 and P3, Dispatch and Confirm each show 4 rows for 3–4 truths, and P2/P3 share identical timestamps. Any utilisation or queueing calculation counts one handling stroke twice. Harmful for operations maths; harmless to flow shape (still PackDispatchConfirm).

3.10 **Transformation trap: propagated Item log.** Applying T1 (attach events via O2O paths) pushes e18–e21 onto I11 (through O1/V1) and e19, e22, e25 onto both I21 and I31 (through O2/O3/V2). Traces grow, and the divergence problem returns through the back door: V2's two payments now sit in two item cases with no allocation rule. Longer traces are not truer histories; propagation must remain a tagged sensitivity experiment, not the headline dataset.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

**Flattenings still worth building.** (a) *Order* — the only projection where every activity category occurs; build it but publish alongside its duplication factor (42/31). (b) *Invoice* — cleanest AR view: DSO per invoice, instalment detection (V2  V1/V3). (c) *Package* — warehouse buildtoship lead times. Optional: *Delivery* for pure transit KPIs. Skip *Payment* and *Item* as standalone analysis bases.

**Answerable safely per flattened log.** Order: which orders closed with unsettled deliveries (none here — all delivered), how often dispatch occurred in multiple waves (O1, O3), CreateClose durations. Invoice: issuefinal settlement latencies; instalment prevalence (1 of 3). Package: packdispatch gaps (e13 10:12  e16 11:05 etc.). Delivery: confirmed transit times per Dn.

**Requires objectcentric views.** Any statement containing "shared," "combined," "consolidated," or "split" needs the OC layer: OCDFG (Dispatch really fires once per delivery serving 1–3 orders), OTG (handover pattern OrderItemPackageDelivery vs OrderInvoicePayment), ET–OT graph (which activity/event pairs systematically bind 2 orders — the e13/e14/e16/e17/e19/e22/e24/e25/e27 club), object interaction summary (quantifies that e16 simultaneously touches O1, O2, O3, I12, I21, I31, P2, P3, D2 — the densest node), and equivalentexecution clustering (shows whether O2's and O4's lives are truly different business behaviour or artefacts of whom they were packed with).

**Reconciliation rule.** Cardinality disputes: the E2O table wins — Dispatch happened 3 times, whatever a flattened row count says. Sequence disputes inside a single unambiguous 1:1 segment: flattened logs win (e.g., invoice issued after dispatch for O1: e15 < e18). Behavioural disputes (loops, variants): objectcentric wins, and the discrepancy is documented as a measurement of consolidation density rather than resolved silently.

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Position B: Order as primary lead object, with explicitly maintained secondary lifecycles (Invoice, Package) and mandatory OC views for crossobject claims.**

Decision criteria: (C1) event coverage — fraction of the 31 events reachable without invented relations; (C2) lifecycle completeness — presence of both opening and closing anchors; (C3) decision relevance — whose KPIs the process owner manages; (C4) manageable, quantifiable artefacts per copy.

Strongest supporting evidence: 31/31 events directly reference an order (C1 = 100 %); only Order offers a real start (Create Order per order) and terminal (Close Order e28–e31) anchor per case (C2); payments and confirmations arrive on order cases via direct E2O, so even money questions stay readable without propagation; duplication is large but finite, known rowbyrow, and fully deductible from the sharedevent set (11 surplus rows, locatable by eid).

Strongest counterargument (points toward C): 9 of 31 events (29 %) touch 2 orders, and the market structure — two crossorder packages, one 3order delivery, one 2order invoice paid in instalments — means the *most economically interesting* episodes are exactly the ones Order flattening mangles (§3.1–§3.5). If crossorder synergy is the analytical centre rather than the exception, Order primacy is cosmetic.

Residual risk: readers of the OrderDFG will mistake instalmentinduced cycles (Record Payment  Confirm Delivery) and wave repetitions (O1's double dispatch) for operational defects; V2's perorder cash allocation remains unsolvable in any flattening; package partners stay invisible.

Conditions to change: reanchor primarily on *Invoice* if the engagement becomes a receivables study; on *Package/Delivery* if freight economics dominate; or move to position C outright if future snapshots push multiorder event share above ~50 % or add allocation data that makes consolidated objects the analytical subject rather than the container. Failing those, B with the published caveats beats A (overclaims authority) and C (abandons a genuine 100 % anchor).

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

**S1 — Down-weight copied shared events.** Recompute all Order/Item/Package frequencies with each event copy weighted 1/k, where k = number of leadtype objects the event touches (e16, e24:  weight on Order flattening; ½ on Package). Validation gate: the Record PaymentConfirm Delivery and Confirm DeliveryRecord Payment edges (§3.4) should fall below skeleton noise once downweighted; if a "loop" persists at truthful weights, it is a real pattern worth escalating.

**S2 — Strict vs propagated relations.** Rebuild the Item flattening under T1 (items inheriting order/invoice/payment events via `contains`/`bills`) and diff against the strict Item log: expect +~10 inherited financial/order rows per item and reemergence of the V2 allocation ambiguity (I21 vs I31 both receiving e22, e25). Also propagate onto Delivery (orders via carriespackscontains) to see whether D2's case grows by exactly the O1/O2/O3 closures. Publish both with provenance columns; headline numbers always come from the strict variant.

**S3 — Leadswap inversion test.** Repeat the whole pipeline with Package and then Invoice as lead. Key falsification check: the payment/delivery "cycle" must disappear on the Invoice lead (each of e22/e25 appears exactly once there) and the Pack/Dispatch inflation must disappear on the Package lead beyond P2/P3. Any conclusion that flips under lead swap is attributed to projection, not process — and its robustness verdict downgraded accordingly.

**S4 — Triangulated counting in the OCDFG.** For each activity × object type, compare three metrics from the OCDFG layer: (i) number of distinct events, (ii) number of distinct interacting objects, (iii) total object references. Dispatch should read 3 events / (1+3+2 order refs) = refs 6 on Order type, exposing consolidation without flattening; divergence > 1 reference per event is flagged as a consolidation marker and crosschecked against the `copy_sources` diagnostic from §6.

**S5 — Structural ablation (bonus).** Temporarily remove V2's instalment events (e25) or treat P3/P4 as orderexclusive hypothetically, remine the Order flattening, and confirm the loop edges and part of the variant splintering vanish. This separates "behaviour installed by the consolidation design" from "behaviour installed by the underlying logistics," which is the central interpretive risk of choosing Order as the lead object.
