# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

**Automated intake (R14_Nora).** *Core:* A1 and A2 at machine scale — roughly 46% of all registrations and 27% of all validations. *Peripheral:* nothing; the bot never appears after A2, which itself defines the automation boundary. Complexity is almost exclusively low (0.11). No substitution or authority dimension applies, but the boundary is contested: fragment 1 shows Nora's own validation feeding straight into standard response, while fragment 6 shows human (temp) validation producing reopens — evidence that *where* validation sits matters more than *how many* validations occur.

**Frontline generalists (R01_Amira, R02_Ben).** *Core:* high-frequency A1/A2/A4/A12 forming the human backbone of the routine flow. *Peripheral:* Amira's 42 A8 and 18 A7 events are negotiation/pricing exposure beyond Ben's (25/5), consistent with her higher median complexity (0.34 vs 0.29) — flexibility earned through experience, not a second formal role. Neither holds approval authority (A9 = 0). During the weeks 8–10 substitution they presumably absorbed overflow, but their rework footprint is far smaller than the temps'.

**Technical resolution (R03_Chen, R04_Dina).** *Core:* A3/A5/A10, where together they own the diagnostic-to-implementation spine of fragment 2. *Peripheral:* Chen's 24 A6 events are security-adjacent work at the edge of his certification ("configuration"), not a security mandate; both do modest A14 KB updates. Complexity splits them: Chen 0.71 with 42 critical cases vs Dina 0.48 with 9 — similar activity mix (0.94), materially different risk exposure. Shift design (Chen late, Dina day) suggests coverage intent, not hierarchy.

**Commercial exception handlers (R06_Fatima, R07_Giorgio).** *Core for Fatima:* A7 (122) and A8 (90) — she is the pricing authority. *Core for Giorgio:* still front-office A1/A2/A4/A12 (90/190/150/130); his A7 (72) and A8 (88) are substantial but secondary. *Peripheral:* Fatima's 16 A9 events show she triggers supervisory approval rather than exercising it. The weeks 3–4 substitution is decisive: Giorgio covered A7/A8 but median pricing duration doubled (38  74 min) — similar-looking work, half the speed, i.e., peripheral competence masquerading as equivalence in a frequency-based view.

**Security assurance (R05_Erik).** *Core:* A6 exclusively — 96 events, but each sits on the highest-complexity cases (median 0.86). *Peripheral:* his A3/A5/A10/A14 activity (16/10/18/9) shows he advises beyond the formal gate. Critically, his *effective* workload is co-determined by R13_Mateo's filtering (weeks 7–8): fewer A6 events than expected was a routing outcome, not a capacity outcome. Authority here is veto-like: cases do not proceed past A6 without him.

**Supervisory authority (R08_Hana).** *Core:* A9 (138). Everything else — 12 A2, 10 A3, 22 A8, 24 A13, 12 A11 — is supervisory sampling, escalation handling, and reopen adjudication. Her authority is directional: the handover network should show converging approval edges into her, not reciprocal exchange. The week 5 episode (Mateo's 21 preliminary approvals, 13 rechecked) confirms that A9 authority is non-delegable by default.

**Quality gatekeepers (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia).** *Core:* A11 (310/298) and A14 (96/104) — they are simultaneously the release gate and the institutional-memory maintainers. *Peripheral:* their A3/A5/A10 counts (16–38) are inspection-driven re-engagement with technical work, not delivery work. Shift complementarity (Ivan late, Julia day) gives continuous QC coverage. They hold gatekeeping power without formal decision authority — a structural tension worth managing explicitly.

**Escalation broker (R13_Mateo).** *Core:* A13 (42, the largest reopen coordination load), A9 preliminary approvals (44), and the connective handovers visible in fragments 3 and 5. *Peripheral:* statistically he touches everything (25 A1 … 18 A14), but at low depth — the signature of a router, not a specialist. His 76 critical cases are the highest absolute count in the organization, yet his median complexity (0.77) reflects *exposure*, not *resolution* skill. His authority is borrowed and conditional (see §3).

**Seasonal capacity pool (R11_Kai, R12_Lina).** *Core:* A1/A2/A4/A12 at high volume, strictly low complexity (4 high cases each, zero critical). *Peripheral:* essentially none. The weeks 8–10 episode is the cautionary tale: 17 reopens traced to incomplete A2 notes means their cheap throughput externalizes cost into a rework loop (fragment 6). Their event counts overstate true contribution.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

**R07_Giorgio — the commercial shadow role.** Giorgio performs ~31% of all pricing reviews and negotiates almost as often as Fatima (88 vs 90), yet his formal title, home team, and primary cluster remain front-office. This is an emergent, uncertified-in-practice deputyship: beneficial as resilience (he carried the queue for six days in weeks 3–4) and as informal succession, but risky because (a) his cycle time doubles under substitution, (b) his pricing decisions are presumably calibrated against Fatima's only intermittently, and (c) the organization does not officially know it depends on him — no backup is designated because, on paper, none is needed. This is not improper conduct; it is unmanaged structure.

**R13_Mateo — the cross-functional escalation broker.** Mateo bridges security, technical, commercial, and management: he coordinates reopens (fragment 5), routes security referrals (fragment 3), and exercises *provisional* approval authority (week 5: 21 preliminary A9 events, 13 rechecked by Hana). Benefits: his filtering of low-risk security referrals cut false escalations measurably. Risks: (a) he is a single point of failure — every cross-functional fragment runs through him; (b) his triage created a hidden failure mode — two critical cases waited >2 days during weeks 7–8 because filtering optimized for false-positive reduction, not tail-risk coverage; (c) his preliminary approvals constitute a shadow authority channel whose reversal rate (13/21 rechecked in one week) is unknown system-wide.

**R05_Erik — the rare-but-critical specialist.** Erik's role is defined less by what he does than by what must not happen without him. His low event count invites misreading as slack capacity; his 0.86 median complexity and 33% critical-case share say otherwise. The shadow aspect: his true capacity is co-managed by Mateo's triage, meaning the security gate's throughput is a *dyad*, not an individual. Knowledge-concentration risk is visible in his A14 count (9 KB updates — the deepest expertise in the firm is the least documented).

**Automation boundary role (R14_Nora).** The bot defines where human judgment begins. The weeks 8–10 reopens show the boundary was effectively pushed onto the least-trained humans (temps doing A2) during peak load — a boundary drift nobody decided deliberately.

**Temporary-workforce shadow pool (R11_Kai, R12_Lina).** They function as a hidden subsidy to frontline metrics: high event counts, low unit cost — and a rework loop (fragment 6) booked to other resources' A13/A12 activity.

**Quality gatekeeping as shadow authority (R09_Ivan, R10_Julia).** Their A11 check blocks release, giving them de facto veto power without formal decision rights; their A14 curation sustains knowledge no one is measured on.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
"""
Organizational role discovery for the order-to-resolution log.
Role mining, resource similarity, interaction networks, and context joins
(complexity, substitution indicators). Return shapes (column names, graph
attributes) vary slightly across PM4Py versions -- adapt accessors.
"""

import pandas as pd
import networkx as nx
import pm4py
from sklearn.cluster import KMeans

LOG_CSV = "order_to_resolution.csv"

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load and format the event log
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
raw = pd.read_csv(LOG_CSV)

log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

# Case-level context (constant within a case) for later joins
case_ctx = (
    log.groupby("case:concept:name", as_index=False)
       .agg({
           "case_complexity": "first",
           "customer_segment": "first",
           "product_line": "first",
           "temporary_substitution": "first",
           "escalation_level": "max",
       })
       .rename(columns={"case:concept:name": "case_id"})
)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Segment flows BEFORE role mining so rare specialists are not diluted
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)                 # variant -> count
print(f"{len(variants)} distinct variants")

# Coarse trace clustering separates the automated mass flow from
# escalated/exceptional flows; run role mining per segment afterwards.
(clustered_logs, _) = pm4py.cluster_log(
    log,
    sklearn_clusterer=KMeans(n_clusters=4, n_init=10, random_state=42),
    activity_key="concept:name",
)

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Organizational role discovery
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
)

role_rows = []
for r in roles:
    for res in r.resources:
        role_rows.append({
            "resource": res,
            "mined_role": r.role_name,
            "origin_evidence": r.origin_ev,   # activities supporting the role
        })
role_map = pd.DataFrame(role_rows)
print(role_map.sort_values(["mined_role", "resource"]).to_string(index=False))

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Activity-based resource similarity
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    log,
    activity_key="concept:name",
    resource_key="org:resource",
    measure="cosine",                # alternatives: jaccard / correlation
)
# Long format: source, target, weight
strong_pairs = sim[sim["weight"] >= 0.85].sort_values("weight", ascending=False)
print(strong_pairs)
# CAUTION: similarity >= 0.9 (e.g., R11-R12 vs R01-R02) means "similar
# activity mix", NOT "interchangeable" or "same authority/risk".

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Interaction networks: handover, working-together, subcontracting
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
handover_mv = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    log, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource"
)   # directed MultiDiGraph: u -> v = u handed work to v
together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(
    log, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource"
)   # undirected co-participation intensity per case
subcontract = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(
    log, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource"
)   # A -> B -> A bounce-backs: delegation / rework signals

# Collapse multi-edges into a weighted DiGraph (version-safe weights)
H = nx.DiGraph()
for u, v, data in handover_mv.edges(data=True):
    w = data.get("value", data.get("weight", 1))
    H.add_edge(u, v, weight=H[u][v]["weight"] + w if H.has_edge(u, v) else w)

btw = nx.betweenness_centrality(H, weight="weight", normalized=True)
broker_tbl = pd.DataFrame({
    "resource": list(H.nodes),
    "betweenness": [btw[n] for n in H.nodes],
    "handover_in": [dict(H.in_degree(weight="weight"))[n] for n in H.nodes],
    "handover_out": [dict(H.out_degree(weight="weight"))[n] for n in H.nodes],
}).sort_values("betweenness", ascending=False)
print(broker_tbl.head())   # expect R13_Mateo on top

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. AUTHORITY GUARDRAIL: approval handovers are directional/hierarchical.
#    Mine the handover network on A9 only and compare with peer clusters.
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
appr_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "concept:name", ["A9 Supervisor Approval"],
    level="events", retain=True,
)
appr_handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(
    appr_log, activity_key="concept:name", resource_key="org:resource"
)
# Converging edges into R08_Hana = genuine authority sink.
# Outgoing A9 edges from R13_Mateo = delegated/preliminary approvals --
# a mined "peer cluster" containing both would hide a reporting line.

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 7. Join mined roles with complexity and substitution context
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
res_case = (
    log[["case:concept:name", "org:resource"]]
      .drop_duplicates()
      .merge(case_ctx, left_on="case:concept:name", right_on="case_id")
)
res_profile = (
    res_case.groupby("org:resource")
            .agg(
                cases_touched=("case_id", "nunique"),
                median_complexity=("case_complexity", "median"),
                share_high_critical=("case_complexity",
                                     lambda s: (s >= 0.75).mean()),
                share_substituted=("temporary_substitution", "mean"),
                max_escalation=("escalation_level", "max"),
            )
            .reset_index()
)
role_context = role_map.merge(res_profile, on="resource", how="left")
print(role_context.sort_values(
    ["mined_role", "median_complexity"], ascending=[True, False]))

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Quantify substitution episodes (availability != competence)
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
sub_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "temporary_substitution", [True], level="case", retain=True
)
timed = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(sub_log.copy())
svc = pm4py.get_service_time(
    timed, activity_key="concept:name", aggregate_measure="median"
)
print(svc.loc[[a for a in ["A7 Pricing Exception Review",
                           "A9 Supervisor Approval",
                           "A2 Validate Entitlement"]
               if a in getattr(svc, "index", [])]])

# Incumbent-vs-substitute service-time gap for the Fatima/Giorgio episode
fat = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "org:resource", ["R06_Fatima"], level="events", retain=True)
gio = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "org:resource", ["R07_Giorgio"], level="events", retain=True)
# Compare median A7 service time between the two frames:
# baseline ~38 min vs ~74 min under substitution => competence gap.

# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. Anti-naive-clustering checklist printed with the report
# ----------------------------------------------------------------------
for g in [
    "A mined role describes co-occurring activities, not a job description "
    "or delegation-of-authority record.",
    "High similarity can hide different error rates: check reopen loops "
    "(fragment 6) per resource before pooling staff.",
    "Directional approval handovers (X -> R08_Hana) indicate hierarchy; "
    "undirected working-together weight does not.",
    "Substitution proves availability under pressure, not equivalent "
    "competence: always compare service times and rework rates.",
    "Rare specialists (R05_Erik) have low frequency but high tail risk; "
    "never size their staffing from event counts alone.",
]:
    print("-", g)
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

**Staffing.** Treat the nine-role structure as a capacity architecture, not an headcount chart: protect the security specialist slot even though its utilization looks low; formalize Giorgio as a trained commercial deputy with an explicit cycle-time target (close the 3874 min gap) rather than leaving the deputyship implicit; keep the existing shift complementarity (Chen late / Dina day; Ivan late / Julia day) which already provides continuity; give the temp pool a validation-note checklist plus sampled QA on A2, because their throughput is currently subsidized by downstream rework.

**Training.** Prioritize (a) pricing calibration for Giorgio, (b) security-triage criteria for Mateo so filtering optimizes tail risk, not just false-escalation volume, (c) entitlement documentation discipline for temporary staff, (d) a development path for Dina if the ambition is a second high-complexity analyst, and (e) KB capture from Erik and Fatima — the deepest expertise currently produces the fewest knowledge-base updates.

**Risk control.** Formalize dual control on A9 whenever Hana is unavailable (the week 5 pattern was sound but accidental); monitor A6 queue aging as a leading indicator; track reopen rate *by validating resource* to catch boundary drift at the automation/temp frontier; watch Mateo's betweenness in the handover network quarter-over-quarter — rising concentration is a single-point-of-failure alarm; require an audit trail distinguishing preliminary from final approvals.

**Resilience.** Document the shadow roles openly (deputy designations, triage rulebooks, gatekeeper charters) so the organization survives the absence of any single incumbent; scenario-test Erik's absence and Hana's absence annually; review the Nora automation boundary with a controlled pilot before expanding it.

**Cautions about changing staffing based only on mined roles:**

1. **Mined roles are behavioral descriptions, not authority or competence records.** Cosine similarity says Amira and Giorgio are close (0.84) and that the temps mirror the generalists (0.98), yet the substitution data shows Giorgio works at half Fatima's speed and the temps generate a measurable reopen loop. A staffing model that treats similar profiles as interchangeable will silently transfer risk — especially if Mateo's provisional approvals are made permanent without redesigning decision rights.
2. **Frequency-based efficiency is an illusion at the boundaries.** The temps and the bot dominate event counts; reallocating volume toward them improves apparent throughput while exporting cost into rework (fragment 6) and quality risk. Always evaluate staffing changes end-to-end, including reopen loops and downstream service times.
3. **Rare-specialist staffing cannot be averaged.** Any utilization-based justification for reducing Erik's coverage ignores that his low frequency coincides with the highest-severity tail; the weeks 7–8 delays show what happens when the gate's effective capacity is quietly reduced by upstream filtering.
4. **The window is a snapshot.** Ten weeks include seasonal peaks, one absence, and one incident surge; roles drift. Re-mine quarterly and compare role stability before restructuring teams on any single mining run.
