## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:**
The case entered the system Monday 08:10 and exited Wednesday 18:40 — a 58.5-hour cycle. The principal delay was the C1 compliance queue (20 h on Monday), meaning C1 could not start until Wednesday 13:10. Additionally, the sanctions ambiguity noticed by T1 at triage was not communicated to anyone until A1 independently discovered it during Assessment on Tuesday. S1 was then engaged for a 2-hour advisory session, and C1 had to read all identity, document, and escalation materials from scratch, consuming 4.5 hours.

**Minimal intervention:**
At Triage (Mon 08:50), prepare a brief escalation packet noting the sanctions ambiguity and transmit it to S1. Simultaneously, reserve a provisional C1 slot for the final compliance review, to be activated once Assessment completes. S1 performs preliminary advisory work (reading the triage packet, researching sanctions exposure, drafting notes) during Monday, overlapping with Identity and Document checks. C1 receives S1's preliminary materials before starting the final review.

**Temporal propagation:**
1. **S1 preliminary work** starts Mon ~09:00. S1 spends 2–3 h on the sanctions question. S1 finishes ~Mon 12:00. This overlaps with I1's Identity Check (Mon 10:40–12:50) — no resource conflict since S1 and I1 are different people.
2. **Identity Check** and **Document Check** proceed at their factual timings. The intervention does not change I1's or D1's queue positions, so Identity Check still starts Mon 10:40 and Document Check still starts Mon 21:40.
3. **Assessment by A1** starts Tue 15:10. A1 now has access to S1's preliminary analysis of the sanctions issue. Instead of discovering the ambiguity from scratch, A1 can build on S1's notes. A1's service time may decrease from 4 h to approximately 3–3.5 h, finishing around Tue 18:10–18:40 rather than 19:10.
4. **Escalation Advice** starts Tue ~19:00. Because S1 already investigated the sanctions question, the advisory session may be shorter — approximately 1–1.5 h instead of 2 h. S1 finishes ~Tue 20:30.
5. **Compliance Review** by C1: the reserved slot is activated. C1 was clearing a backlog through Wednesday morning (queue was 8 h at Wed 08:00 in the snapshot). With the reservation, C1 begins K-104 at Wed 08:00 instead of 13:10. C1 has S1's preliminary materials and the assessment notes with the sanctions analysis already flagged. C1's service time decreases from 4.5 h to approximately 3–3.5 h. C1 finishes ~Wed 11:00–11:30.
6. **Decision** at Wed ~11:30–12:00. **Notify** at ~12:00–12:10.

- The preliminary packet is incomplete (prepared before Identity/Document evidence arrives), but S1 can work on the sanctions question independently and augment notes as evidence arrives.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:**
The case arrived Monday 09:00 and was completed Wednesday 09:00 — a 48-hour cycle. The critical delay was the D1 document queue: originals arrived Mon 17:30, but D1 was backlogged (11 h remaining at Mon 16:00) and could not start until Tue 09:30. D1's service time was 4 h (within the 3–6 h standard range), and the check was accurate with no rework. Assessment by A2 started Tue 20:00, meaning a further ~6.5 h wait after document check completion (Tue 13:30).

**Minimal intervention:**
At Triage, route the Document Check to D2 rather than D1. D2 is a generalist (4–8 h for standard documents) but has a shorter queue (5 h at Mon 16:00 vs D1's 11 h).

**Temporal propagation:**
1. The task is assigned to D2 at Triage (Mon 09:40) but blocked until originals arrive (Mon 17:30). Under FIFO queue discipline, the task enters D2's queue at 09:40 with a blocked status. When it unblocks at 17:30, it retains its original queue position.
2. D2 has been working through ~5 h of queued work since Mon 08:00. By Mon 17:30, D2 has completed approximately 9.5 h of work (since 08:00), consuming most of the queue. D2 finishes the current task around Mon 18:00–19:00 and picks up K-219.
3. D2 service time for standard handwritten documents: 5–7 h (D2 is a generalist; handwritten material pushes toward the upper range). D2 finishes ~Mon 23:00–Tue 02:00.
4. The case enters the A2 Assessment queue. A2's queue was 6 h at Tue 08:00. Since K-219 enters the queue around midnight (Tue 00:00), it is near the front of the queue when A2 starts work at 08:00. A2 begins this case around Tue 08:00–10:00.
5. A2 Assessment service: ~2.5 h (standard case, A2 is faster on standard). Assessment completes ~Tue 10:30–12:30.
6. Decision and Notify: Tue ~13:00–13:30.

Counterfactual cycle time (no-rework scenario): Mon 09:00  Tue 13:30  **28.5 h**, saving approximately **19.5 h** (41%) relative to the factual 48 h.

**However**, D2 has a higher probability of misreading handwritten material. If D2 requests clarification or makes an error caught at Assessment, a rework loop is triggered: external clarification (8–36 h)  new Document Check (4–8 h)  new Assessment (2–3 h). This adds 14–47 h, potentially pushing the cycle time to 42–75 h — **worse than the factual**.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- D2 is a valid document resource. 
- Originals-receipt ordering is preserved (C3). 
- Identity Check (Mon 14:20–16:20) and Originals Receipt (Mon 17:30) proceed as before. 
- No four-eyes issue (no compliance review required for this non-high-risk case). 
- The task may be created at Triage and blocked — this is consistent with standard workflow systems.

**Best / base / worst case:**
- **Best (26 h, saving 22 h):** D2 starts Mon 18:00, takes 5 h, finishes Mon 23:00. Case is first in A2 queue at Tue 08:00. Assessment 2 h, done 10:00. Decision 10:30. No rework.
- **Base (30 h, saving 18 h):** D2 starts Mon 19:00, takes 6 h, finishes Tue 01:00. A2 starts this case ~Tue 09:00, finishes 11:30. Decision 12:00. No rework.
- **Worst (60–75 h, worse than factual):** D2 misreads handwritten material. Assessment catches an inconsistency, requests clarification. External response takes 24 h (including a business-day gap). New Document Check by D2: 5 h. New Assessment: 2.5 h. Total cycle time: ~60–75 h.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
If D2's queue discipline assigns the task to the back of the queue when unblocked (rather than retaining its creation-time position), D2 would not start until Mon 21:00–22:00, and the time saving shrinks to 12–14 h. If D2's error rate on handwritten documents exceeds 25%, the expected cycle time (probability-weighted across rework and no-rework scenarios) could exceed the factual 48 h.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:**
The case entered Monday 11:00 and exited the following Wednesday 09:10 — approximately 214 hours (nearly 9 calendar days). Two Request-More-Info loops were the primary drivers. The external supplier was slow and inconsistent: the first RMI consumed ~20.5 h (Wed 11:30  Thu 08:00), and the second RMI spanned a weekend (Fri 10:30  Mon 09:00 = ~70 h including the weekend gap). After each RMI, a new Document Check and Assessment were required. The third Document Check and Assessment confirmed the evidence, but only after adding approximately 4 extra days to the cycle.

**Minimal intervention:**
After Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), the process is prohibited from issuing a second RMI. The case must proceed to Compliance Review and Decision using the evidence available after two rounds of document checking. The remaining inconsistency (one unresolved supplier total) must be addressed through conditions on the decision, escalated monitoring, or an explicit risk acceptance — but the process may not silently ignore it.

**Temporal propagation:**
1. Everything through Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30) is identical to the factual execution. The first RMI, Document Check 2, and Assessment 2 proceed unchanged.
2. At Fri 10:30, instead of issuing RMI 2, the case moves to Compliance Review. C1's queue had been clearing through the week: 8 h (Wed)  5 h (Thu)  approximately 3–4 h (Fri). C1 begins this case around Fri 14:00–15:00.
3. C1 must evaluate the case with one unresolved supplier inconsistency. This is more demanding than reviewing fully resolved evidence: C1 must assess the materiality of the discrepancy, decide whether conditions can mitigate the risk, and document the rationale. C1 service time: 4–6 h (higher than the 3–4 h for complete evidence). C1 finishes Fri 18:00–21:00.
4. **Decision:** M1 signs the decision. If C1 finishes during business hours (before ~18:00), the decision could be Fri 18:00–18:30. If C1 finishes after hours, the decision waits until Mon 08:00–08:30 (next business day). In the more likely scenario (C1 finishes Fri 18:00–20:00), M1 may not be available, and the decision is deferred to Monday.
5. Decision Mon ~08:30. Notify Mon ~08:40.

Counterfactual cycle time: Mon 11:00  Mon 08:40 (next week)  **166 h**, saving approximately **48 h** (22%) relative to the factual 214 h.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Compliance Review is still required (high-risk, high-value). 
- Four-eyes rule: A1 does final Assessment, C1 does Compliance Review — different resources. 
- The intervention changes the **decision policy**: the case is decided with incomplete evidence. This is not a process violation (C12 explicitly permits it under the intervention), but it is a deliberate policy choice.
- The unresolved inconsistency must not be silently ignored — C1 must document how it was addressed (conditions, monitoring, or risk acceptance).

**Best / base / worst case:**
- **Best (164 h, saving 50 h):** C1 finishes Fri 18:00. M1 is available for a late-Friday decision. Decision: conditional approval with monitoring. Cycle time: Mon 11:00  Fri 18:30  163.5 h.
- **Base (166 h, saving 48 h):** C1 finishes Fri 20:00. Decision deferred to Mon 08:30. Cycle time:  166 h. Decision: conditional approval with enhanced reserves.
- **Worst (166 h cycle time, but adverse decision quality):** C1 determines the unresolved discrepancy is material. Case is rejected. Customer appeals, adding 30+ days to the effective resolution time. Alternatively, C1 approves and the discrepancy later causes a financial loss. The 166 h cycle time is achieved, but the downstream cost is substantial.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
If the remaining inconsistency after Document Check 2 is material (e.g., a 15% discrepancy in a financial schedule), suppressing the second RMI forces a decision that may be indefensible under regulatory scrutiny. If post-hoc analysis shows that the second RMI would have resolved the discrepancy in >80% of similar cases, the intervention's expected quality cost exceeds its time savings.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:**
The case arrived Tuesday 09:00 and was completed Wednesday 14:30 — a 29.5-hour cycle. The critical delay was **sequential execution** caused by a legacy system case lock: Triage (Tue 09:35) created only the identity task. Identity Check by I2 finished at 13:20, but the document task was not created until after identity completion. D2 then had to wait in its own queue (1 h 40 m gap before D2 started at 15:00). Both checks completed by 19:30, and Assessment by A2 did not start until Wed 09:30 (waiting overnight plus A2's queue).

**Minimal intervention:**
At Triage (Tue 09:35), create both the identity and document tasks simultaneously. Use a controlled system workaround (e.g., a proxy task or manual task-creation step) to bypass the case lock. Require real-time timestamps for both tasks rather than deferred batch entry.

**Temporal propagation:**
1. At Tue 09:35, both tasks are created. I2 and D2 each receive their assignment.
2. **I2** has a 2-h queue at Tue 08:00. I2 starts this case ~Tue 11:35, finishes ~Tue 13:25 (1 h 50 m service — unchanged from factual).
3. **D2** has a 4-h queue at Tue 08:00. The task enters D2's queue at 09:35. D2 starts this case ~Tue 13:35 (09:35 + 4 h queue), finishes ~Tue 18:05 (4.5 h service — same as factual).
4. In the factual, D2 started at 15:00 and finished at 19:30. In the counterfactual, D2 starts at ~13:35 and finishes at ~18:05 — approximately **1 h 25 m earlier**, because the task entered D2's queue 5 h 25 m earlier.
5. Both checks are complete by ~18:05 (vs 19:30 factual). The case enters A2's Assessment queue.
6. A2's queue at Tue 08:00 was 6 h. By Tue 18:00, A2 has been processing for 10 h. Interpolating from the snapshot trend (Mon 08:00: 4 h  Mon 16:00: 7 h  Tue 08:00: 6 h), A2's queue at ~Tue 18:00 is approximately 5–5.5 h. The case enters the queue at 18:05 and, if A2 processes it first thing Wed morning, A2 starts ~Wed 08:00–08:30.
7. Assessment by A2: ~2.5 h. Completes ~Wed 10:30–11:00 (vs Wed 12:00 factual).
8. Decision: Wed ~11:00–11:30. Notify: ~11:30–11:40.

Counterfactual cycle time: Tue 09:00  Wed 11:40  **26.7 h**, saving approximately **2.8 h** (9.5%) relative to the factual 29.5 h.

**Process-feasibility check:**
- Both Identity Check and Document Check are valid activities; creating them in parallel does not violate any prerequisite (C3, C4 are not relevant since originals are not required). 
- The controlled workaround must produce an auditable log. If the workaround bypasses the case lock without proper logging, audit findings may result.
- No four-eyes or compliance issues (not high-risk). 
- Real-time timestamps are required — this is a logging discipline change, not a process change.

**Best / base / worst case:**
- **Best (25 h, saving 4.5 h):** D2 clears queue faster (some earlier tasks complete quickly), starts ~12:00, finishes ~16:30. A2 picks up the case at 16:30 if near end of A2's queue. A2 finishes by ~19:00. Decision Tue 19:30. Cycle time: ~24.5 h.
- **Base (27 h, saving 2.5 h):** As described above.
- **Worst (29 h, saving 0.5 h):** D2's queue discipline does not preserve the task's original position. The task goes to the back of D2's queue when created at 09:35, and D2 starts it at ~15:00 (same as factual). The only saving comes from eliminating the 1 h 40 m post-identity gap, yielding ~0.5 h saving. Additionally, the workaround creates inconsistent log entries that trigger an audit flag.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:**
If D2's queue does not respect task-creation time (assigns by unblocking time instead), the intervention provides almost no benefit. If the assessment queue absorbs the case at the same time regardless of when identity/document checks complete (because A2's overnight queue is always processed first thing in the morning), the 1 h 25 m saving on the document check has no downstream effect.

### 100-case weekly rollout analysis

**K-104 (Early escalation):** C1 is the sole compliance reviewer with roughly 40 h of weekly capacity. Reserving provisional slots for 100 high-risk cases is structurally impossible — C1 would need 350–500 h. S1, a single advisory resource, faces a similar constraint (100 packets × 2–3 h = 200–300 h). At scale, this intervention must be gated: only cases where triage identifies a specific compliance concern should receive early escalation. A reasonable gate might select 5–10 cases per week. For those cases, the benefit is real (5–7 h saved per case), and C1 reservation displaces a manageable number of other reviews. Without a gate, C1's queue grows from ~8–20 h to 40+ h, and the compliance backlog cascades into the following week, inflating cycle times for all high-risk cases. The key nonlinearity is C1's singleton capacity: once reserved slots exceed ~20% of C1's available time, marginal queue growth accelerates sharply.

**K-219 (D2 routing):** Redirecting 100 handwritten standard cases from D1 to D2 transfers demand from a resource with a deep queue to one with a shallow queue — initially beneficial. However, D2's single-resource capacity (~40 h/week) cannot absorb 100 cases at 5–7 h each (500–700 h). D2's queue would grow from 3–5 h to 30–50+ h within the first few days, negating the early-start advantage. D1's queue, meanwhile, empties, leaving D1 underutilized on standard work while complex cases continue to require D1. The substitution is beneficial only if the number of redirected cases is small (10/week) relative to D2's spare capacity. Additionally, D2's higher error rate on handwriting compounds the queue problem: each rework loop consumes 8–36 h of external response time and an additional D2 service interval, creating a feedback loop that further congests D2. The net system effect is negative unless case volumes are carefully bounded.

**K-337 (Suppress RMI):** Eliminating the second rework loop saves approximately 25 h of document-check and assessment resource time per case, and eliminates 20–70 h of external-response waiting. For 100 cases, this represents 2,500 h of freed internal capacity — significant. However, 100 cases with unresolved discrepancies enter compliance review simultaneously. C1 must evaluate each case's ambiguity, taking 4–6 h per case instead of the usual 3–4 h (400–600 h total vs C1's 40 h/week capacity). The compliance review becomes the new bottleneck. Moreover, if even 10–15% of cases have a material unresolved discrepancy, that's 10–15 bad decisions per week — potentially generating appeals, losses, or regulatory findings whose cost dwarfs the cycle-time savings. Weekend timing amplifies this: cases reaching Assessment 2 on Friday benefit most (skipping a full weekend) but also have the least compliance-review runway before the weekend. The nonlinearity is in decision quality: a small fraction of adverse decisions can generate outsized downstream costs.

**K-411 (Parallelize verification):** Creating both tasks at triage for 100 standard digital cases pushes work into I and D queues simultaneously rather than sequentially. I resources (I1, I2: ~80 h/week combined) face 100 × 2.5 h = 250 h of identity work; D resources (D1, D2: ~80 h/week combined) face 100 × 6 h = 600 h of document work. Neither queue can absorb this in one week. The critical downstream effect is on Assessment: 100 cases completing both checks 1.5 h earlier each does not create a 100 × 1.5 h = 150 h shift. Instead, it creates a **wave** — cases that would have arrived at Assessment over 2–3 days now arrive within 1–2 days. A1 and A2 (~80 h/week, ~3 h/case) can process ~27 cases/week. A wave of 50+ cases arriving in a single day creates a multi-day assessment backlog that completely offsets the parallelization benefit. The workaround for case locks also scales poorly: 100 concurrent task pairs require robust audit trails, and any logging inconsistency generates compliance risk. The key nonlinearity is the assessment queue's convex response to simultaneous arrivals.

**Summary:** No intervention scales linearly. K-104 collapses due to C1's singleton capacity. K-219 collapses due to D2's limited throughput and error risk. K-337 scales in speed but degrades in quality. K-411 scales most gracefully but shifts the bottleneck to Assessment, whose capacity must be expanded in parallel. The most scalable approach combines bounded application of K-104 (gate on triage-identified compliance concerns) with assessment-capacity planning.

## 7. Final synthesis

**Most robust intervention:** K-411 (parallelize verification) is the most robust across plausible assumptions. Its benefit is modest (1.5–3.5 h per case) but degrades gracefully — even if the queue-discipline assumption is wrong and D2 starts at the same time as in the factual execution, the elimination of the post-identity gap still saves approximately 1 h. The intervention requires no resource reservation, does not change decision policy, and is fully reversible. The primary risk (audit/logging from the system workaround) is manageable with proper controls and does not affect decision quality.

**Most dangerous despite potential speed:** K-337 (suppress rework) is the most dangerous. It offers the largest cycle-time savings (44–50 h), but it changes the decision policy by forcing a judgment on incomplete evidence. The risk is not merely a longer cycle time — it is a materially wrong decision. If the unresolved supplier discrepancy turns out to be a significant financial misstatement, the case may be approved when it should have been rejected, or vice versa. Once signed, the decision is difficult to reverse without triggering customer complaints or regulatory scrutiny. The speed gain is real but the tail risk is severe and irreversible.

**Least transportable local counterfactual:** K-104 (early escalation) is least transportable from a single-case counterfactual to a system-wide policy. In the single-case analysis, reserving one C1 slot and engaging S1 for 2–3 h is straightforward. At scale, C1's singleton capacity (40 h/week) makes reservation infeasible beyond a handful of cases. The single-case benefit depends on a scarce resource being available precisely when needed; the system-wide policy depends on that resource being available for everyone, which it cannot be.

**Most valuable additional data:** (1) C1's actual service-time distribution conditional on whether preliminary compliance materials are available — this directly determines whether K-104's benefit is real. (2) D2's error rate on handwritten documents broken down by document type — this determines whether K-219 is net positive or net negative. (3) The materiality distribution of unresolved supplier discrepancies in cases like K-337 — if most are minor, suppression is safe; if even 10% are material, suppression is costly. (4) Assessment-queue inflow rates by hour of day — this determines whether K-411's parallelization actually feeds assessment earlier or simply shifts the arrival time within the same overnight processing window.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Resource** — C1 is the sole fully certified compliance reviewer during the review window; no temporary compliance staff can be brought in to absorb reserved-slot demand. *(resource)*

2. **Temporal** — Queue forecasts from the system snapshots represent point-in-time estimates that may deviate from actual wait times by ±30%; overnight and weekend gaps affect external-response waits but do not affect internal resource scheduling. *(temporal)*

3. **Behavioral** — S1's preliminary work quality is bounded by the completeness of the triage packet; packets prepared before Identity/Document evidence arrives will contain less detail than those prepared after, and S1's utility to C1 depends on this completeness. *(behavioral)*

4. **Quality** — D2's error rate on handwritten standard documents exceeds D1's; the magnitude is uncertain (ranging from marginally higher to significantly higher) and directly determines whether K-219 produces net positive or net negative cycle time. *(quality)*

5. **Structural** — The case lock in K-411 is a known, stable system limitation; the controlled workaround is technically feasible, produces consistent task-creation behavior, and generates an auditable log entry. *(structural)*

6. **Interference** — Reserving a C1 slot for one case displaces exactly one other case's compliance review by approximately the reserved duration; there is no smoothing, batching, or overtime that would absorb the displacement. *(interference)*

7. **Temporal** — External-response wait times (Request More Info) are dominated by the external party's business cycle and include weekends; a Friday-afternoon RMI will not be answered until Monday at the earliest, while a Monday-morning RMI may be answered within 24 hours. *(temporal)*

8. **Quality** — Suppressing a rework loop changes the decision outcome in no more than 10–15% of affected cases; in most instances, the unresolved discrepancy is minor and can be managed through conditions or monitoring without material adverse consequence. *(quality)*
