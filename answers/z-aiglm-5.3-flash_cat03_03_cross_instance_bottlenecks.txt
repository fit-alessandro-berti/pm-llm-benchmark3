**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting:** utilization averages the server's *own* calendar and is blind to waiting that occurs between calendars. Waits here are produced by matching constraints — a job may advance only when a partner set (cohort, lot, freight, fixture-owner, instrument) is aligned. With several serial synchronization points, the fraction of jobs that pass *every* join without collision shrinks multiplicatively, producing long right-tailed job flow times while each resource remains mostly unoccupied. Group services also inherit the maximum of partner latenesses, not their average — B1's start inherits J3's 11:55 arrival, punishing J1 to 3 h55 while OVEN-1 shows 44%.

- *Feasibility:* TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25 with J6 demonstrably queued — the station was never the issue.
- *Policy:* every Final Test requires CAL-3; recalibration evidently trails specific use, so the *second* user pays for the first user's session — order-sensitivity built into the tooling regime.
- *Speculation:* resequencing J6 ahead of J5 transfers, not removes, the recalibration cost; only schedule-shifting the recalibration (or adding capacity) shrinks the sum.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Dwell-triggered batching (reduce synchronization).** Cure when the oldest ready job exceeds a max-dwell (e.g., 60 min) instead of waiting for fuller cohorts. Benefit: removes most of J1/J2-type waits (§2a/b). Helps: J1, J2, J4, J5. Harmed: none operationally today, but smaller charges raise cure-run count. Risk: quality rules may forbid sub-size batches; OVEN-1 wall-clock load rises. Perverse metric: "maximize batch fill rate" reinstates the pathology. Evaluation: compare the distribution of `ReadyBatchStart` per Job (not per batch) before/after using object lifecycles, plus RACK-7 turnaround incidence.
2. **Fixture relief (address tools).** Add FIX-A unit #2 or time-boxed holds with preemptive handoff when a waiter is present. Benefit: erases up to ~1 h of J4-class stalls and decouples J3's machining from other jobs. Helps: J4, and anyone downstream of B1/B2 partners. Harmed: nobody directly; cost pools elsewhere. Risk: duplicate fixtures gather hoarded reservations. Perverse metric: "fixture utilization %" rewards holding. Evaluation: measure overlap-minutes of FIX-A holds with CNC-2-idle-with-queued-job; expect collapse toward zero.
3. **Calibration scheduling (address tools).** Move CAL-3 recalibration off the critical path (pre-emptive windows, or swap-in instrument). Benefit: J6 saves the identified 50 min; second-mover penalty vanishes. Helps: J6 and every subsequent tester. Harmed: quality assurance if recal discipline slips. Risk: escape/audit exposure. Perverse metric: "% runs with fresh calibration" drives perpetual recal. Evaluation: EToT overlay of `Calibrate`/recal events against TEST-1 idle-with-demand episodes.
4. **Graduated dispatch policy (address shipment policy).** Dispatch by T_pack + SLA (e.g., 90 min) even if incomplete; or pair jobs by predicted completion, not order entry. Benefit: caps packed dwell near the S2/S3-style norms (25–35 min) for the early partner. Helps: J1-type victims. Harmed: late partners lose a free ride; carrier spend rises. Risk: SLAs breach on split freight. Perverse metric: "truck fill %" — retro-optimizing fill recreates hostage-taking. Evaluation: extend the shipment flattening with per-Job `PackedDispatch` (computed via Shipment joins, not truck gaps) and compare tail percentiles.
5. **Logging/integration repair (improve recording, not flow).** Emit `Wait for Tool` (requested-at/acquired-at), attach lot quarantine/release to affected Jobs in the primary pipeline, and fold supervisor overrides into the main OCEL with an `overrides` qualifier. Benefit: no cycle-time change; converts guesses (§2d, §2g) into measurements and stabilizes cross-pipeline frequency comparisons. Helps: analysts; future J4/J5 diagnostics. Harmed: nothing operationally. Risk: schema churn; synthetic-event gaming. Perverse metric: "log completeness score" invites fabricated events. Evaluation: re-extract after change; confirm the Job flattening now contains L7 events relating to J2/J5 and a measurable tool-wait segment on J4; quantify newly attributed waiting share.
6. **Quarantine SLA (administrative coupling).** Time-box lot disposition with escalation. Helps: J5-class components; also protects the L8 latent pairing (J1–J3 share C1/C3 even though no L8 events fired today). Harmed: QC thoroughness margins. Risk: rubber-stamp releases. Perverse metric: "fastest quarantine closure." Evaluation: trace `QuarantineRelease` intervals against downstream first-unblocked-operation per Job in the alternate pipeline linkage.

Spillover warning (visible in the data): fixing lots alone (Intervention 6) buys little while RACK-7 holds B2 to 14:35 (§5 Narrative B); fixing batching alone can *increase* rack turnarounds, tightening Intervention 2/3 resources. Only the OTG/EToT views can price these interactions; each single-lever ROI computed from flattened Job statistics will overstate.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (batch synchronization):**

1. Independent timestamps show `Ready for batch` values (08:10, 08:25) are produced by a planner queue refresh, lagging true physical completion — the 3 h55/3 h40 figures become measurement artifacts.
2. OVEN-1's calendar reveals bookings between 08:10 and 12:05 outside the OCEL — a genuine capacity contribution, reviving the machine-capacity hypothesis.
3. Historical B1/B2-like cohorts show tight readiness skews (minutes, not hours); the sampled day is unrepresentative and recurrence expectations fail.
4. End-to-end per-job decomposition attributes most of each job's excess to `PackedDispatch` (Mechanism 2), not `ReadyCure`.
5. Quality validation forbids sub-sized or split cures, showing waits are accepted policy-optimal trade-offs rather than pathology.
6. More cases like J2 exist — jobs routinely enter batches before full eligibility via overrides — indicating synchronization barriers are porous and weaker than modeled.
7. The alternate integration pipeline shows many job waits coinciding with lot-release windows, promoting Mechanism 4 above batching.

**Fields/events that would discriminate among the hypotheses:**

1. Explicit `Wait for Tool` / `Request Fixture` events with requested-at times — separates tool queueing from all other waiting on J4.
2. `Batch Formed` decision events with min-fill/max-dwell policy parameters and per-job `Cure Eligible` stamps — pins down *when and why* the 12:05 clock was chosen.
3. Lot events with reason codes, intended scope ("freeze machining" vs "freeze only consumption"), and the linked alternative-pipeline mapping — resolves the inconsistent Job linkage and prices Mechanism 4.
4. Per-shipment committed cutoff times and carrier capacity ledger — distinguishes true policy delay from physical freight limits behind S1's 19:25.
5. Calibration certificates per test (instrument state hash, recipe-specific recal triggers) — tells whether CAL-3's 16:35–17:20 was use-triggered, recipe-shift, or routine.
6. Reason-coded setup/changeover durations at CNC-2 keyed to from/to combinations — splits the 10:05–10:47 idle into normal setup versus fixture-induced waiting.
7. Shift calendar and operator assignment per event — checks whether the 48-minute CNC-2 gap is a break-pattern artifact.
8. Queue-length snapshots at each resource plus promised-due dates per job — enables queueing-based attribution and priority-conflict detection for the override cases.
