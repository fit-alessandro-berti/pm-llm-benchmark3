## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Observed vs hypothesized:** Observed are the tables, timeline deployments, variant/edge/source stratifications, and audit findings. Hypotheses needing tests are: whether Apr scores were built at wrong prefixes; whether Jun miscalibration is only mixture; whether V3 risk changed conditional on part/supplier; whether Partner Z gap reflects unmeasured case-mix vs model failure; and Oct's true mature performance.

- **Jan–Mar: stable baseline.** AUROC 0.81–0.82, Brier ~0.131, ECE ~0.024, breach 0.18–0.19, predicted ~0.18–0.19, unknown <0.3%, missing 1%, maturity 100%. Variant mix and V1/V2/V3 breach rates stable. This is the reference regime, not drift.
- **April: schema/pipeline incident, not a business collapse.** The rename coincides exactly with unknown activity 17%, unmapped variants 18%, missing queue 14%, over-prediction (0.28 vs 0.19), and discrimination loss. Real breach by variant changes only modestly (V3 0.300.34). A genuine simultaneous concept, population, and process change is implausible given audit evidence of wrong/no prediction prefixes and duplicates. Treat as corrupted measurement/features.
- **May: partial remediation.** Unknown drops to 2%, missing to 3%, predicted 0.21 vs 0.18, AUROC recovers to 0.78 but not 0.81–0.82. This gradient supports residual pipeline damage — specifically non-backfilled cached prefix-length feature — rather than a new May business drift. May should not be pooled blindly with Jan–Mar.
- **June: real routing/process and covariate-mix change, weak evidence of concept drift.** Remote policy pushes V2 to 52%. Because V2 matured breach is low (0.09) versus V1 0.16/V3 0.35, aggregate observed breach falls to 0.15 while mean predicted remains 0.20: aggregate over-prediction/ECE 0.060 can arise from mixture and stale intercept. AUROC stays 0.79 and within-variant matured rates are stable, arguing against a changed outcome relationship. This is process drift causing covariate drift.
- **July: supplier performance drift begins, early concept signal.** `Order Part -> Part Received` jumps 4265h; V3 breach 0.350.46 while V1/V2 unchanged. Labels 98% mature, so this is largely real. Model mildly under-predicts (0.18 vs 0.19) with AUROC 0.76: emerging V3-specific deterioration.
- **August: performance/process drift becomes outcome-relevant concept drift.** Part edge 130h; V3 breach 0.64; aggregate breach 0.28 vs predicted 0.19; AUROC 0.68, ECE 0.120. V2 share falls and V3 rises to 35%, amplifying aggregate impact, but the within-V3 jump is the key evidence that diagnosis-time risk no longer captures downstream reality.
- **September: three overlaps.** (i) Supplier effect persists: edge 148h, V3 breach 0.61. (ii) Population shift: Partner Z 24%, more V3/rural/older devices, missing model features. (iii) Label immaturity begins: 82% maturity, 42d delay. Aggregate AUROC 0.66/ECE 0.14 therefore mixes genuine V3 concept drift, new-population covariate shift, and incomplete labels. Partner Z's raw 0.45 breach cannot be read as pure model bias because case-mix and slower maturity confound it.
- **October: label-censoring confounds everything.** Maturity 55%, delay 52d, backlog biased against long paths. Observed breach drops to 0.16 while predicted is 0.23 — the opposite direction from Aug–Sep and exactly what censoring of long/V3/breach-prone cases would produce. AUROC 0.64/Brier 0.205 on available labels are vintage-dependent artifacts until maturity catches up. Cases and V3 share remain high, so underlying risk may still be elevated; October neither proves recovery nor quantifies failure.

Thus April is corrupted observation, June is changed mix with stable conditional risk, August–September is changed downstream reality plus changed population, and October is changed observation completeness. One retraining response to all four would be wrong.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

**Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
- Build cohorts by case-start month; for each, plot breach rate, AUROC, Brier, ECE at 30/60/90/120 days after start and at equal maturity fractions. Re-score Sep/Oct only when 95–98% mature or use censoring-aware estimates.
- Pipeline/label-delay hypothesis: Oct breach rises and metrics move materially as vintage matures, especially V3/long cases. Genuine Oct recovery hypothesis: mature Oct retains breach ~0.16 and variant-specific rates fall.

**Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**
- From raw events, map both diagnosis labels to one canonical activity, deduplicate by event ID/case-activity-timestamp, rebuild prefixes exactly at diagnosis, recompute all derived features including cached event-count, and re-score with the frozen baseline model for Jan–May.
- Pipeline hypothesis: Apr AUROC/ECE/predicted mean return toward Jan–Mar (0.81/0.025/0.19) and unknown/unmapped ~0. Real April drift hypothesis: clean recomputation leaves AUROC ~0.67 and over-prediction intact.

**Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**
- Report AUROC, Brier, ECE, calibration intercept/slope, predicted vs matured observed by month × V1/V2/V3 × Partner Z/non-Z, with CIs and maturity.
- Mixture-only hypothesis (Jun): within-variant calibration stable; aggregate error disappears after variant standardization. Concept hypothesis (Aug–Sep): V3 calibration slope/intercept deteriorates within variant and source. Population hypothesis: Partner Z gap shrinks within variant after adding rural/age; residual gap suggests unmeasured factors or segment misfit.

**Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges**
- Clean monthly variant shares, DFG, performance-DFG; focus `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosisdispatch, diagnosis`Remote Fix`, plus queue waits. Use case-start cohorts for outcome linkage and event-calendar cohorts for operational load.
- Process/performance hypothesis: control-flow stable Jan–Mar, V2 jumps Jun, part edge deteriorates Jul+ while remote edge stays flat. Pipeline hypothesis: Apr DFG anomalies collapse after normalization/dedup.

**Tests of whether the outcome relationship conditional on the same path changed**
- Within identical variant and, where possible, identical prefix/part-type/supplier/region strata, compare matured P(breach | diagnosis features) over time; fit month×feature and month×V3 interactions; check whether a lead-time proxy known/forecastable at diagnosis restores stability.
- Concept drift: V3 month effect remains after case-mix controls and is mediated by realized/forecast part delay. Covariate-only: conditional rates stable once composition is controlled.

**Population reweighting / transport-style checks for Partner Z**
- Reweight Sep non-Z to Jan–Aug population and Partner Z to non-Z distribution on variant, rural distance, device age, priority, region; compare standardized breach and model performance. Also evaluate old model trained pre-Sep on matured Partner Z vs matched non-Z.
- Transportable model hypothesis: after reweighting/feature augmentation, Partner Z performance matches. Non-transportable/unmeasured hypothesis: gap persists, justifying new features or segment model, not a fairness conclusion without this stratification.

**Feature missingness and lineage audits**
- Trace each feature to source event/label version; compare missingness, prefix event counts, timestamp order, duplicate rates by source/day around 1 Apr, 20 Apr, 6 May. Check queue feature and cached prefix feature against recomputation.
- Pipeline hypothesis: missingness/duplicates align with source cutover dates and explain score errors case-by-case. Business hypothesis: missingness tracks real queue load/priority and persists in clean raw events.

**Supplier mediation / falsification checks**
- Link part orders to supplier promised vs actual lead time, part type, depot. Test whether breach increase is concentrated in delayed-supplier strata and whether diagnosis-time supplier backlog predicts breach prospectively.
- Supplier concept hypothesis: delayed strata drive V3 increase; on-time strata stable. Alternative case-mix hypothesis: increase is uniform across suppliers after controls.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment (now)**
- Freeze automatic retraining and promotion. Quarantine Apr as training data and Oct as evaluation/training data. Keep the existing model in shadow or with human review for V3/high-risk cases; add a temporary V3 risk uplift only as an explicitly monitored operational rule if matured Aug–Sep evidence supports it, not as silent recalibration.
- Publish dashboards with maturity flags: Oct = provisional, Sep = incomplete, Apr–May = pipeline-suspect.

**Feature repair before calibration**
- Normalize activity labels, retrospectively deduplicate, rebuild prefixes, recompute cached features, and backfill. Re-score frozen model. Only if clean Apr/May performance largely recovers should those months re-enter training; otherwise keep excluded. Calibration fitted around corrupted prefixes would learn the bug.

**Short-term recalibration**
- After clean recomputation and mature labels, recalibrate intercept — possibly variant-specific intercepts for V3 — using Jan–Mar clean + Jun–Jul matured data, validated on matured Aug. This addresses Jun mixture-related aggregate miscalibration and early V3 under-prediction.
- Do not globally recalibrate on Sep/Oct until maturity and Partner Z stratification are resolved; a global intercept would overcorrect low-risk V2 and undercorrect V3.

**Full retraining**
- Retrain when: (a) pipeline features are certified; (b) Aug–Sep cohorts are 95% mature or censoring-weighted; (c) supplier/part backlog features available at prediction time and Partner Z rural/device-age features are included; (d) backtests show conditional V3 drift persists. Use time-based validation, not random splits.
- Training safety: Jan–Mar safe baseline; Apr unsafe until recomputed and verified; May conditionally safe after feature-lineage sign-off; Jun–Jul safe for new routing regime once mature (Jul essentially mature); Aug usable with caution at 93% and sensitivity analysis; Sep unsafe until maturity/reweighting resolved; Oct unsafe.

**Segment/variant-specific model**
- Justified as a staged hypothesis, not immediate fragmentation. V3 has distinct mechanism, volume, and drift; V2 is stable and fast. First try one model with variant, supplier/part, rural, device-age features and variant interactions, plus hierarchical/variant-specific calibration. Move to a separate V3 model — and possibly Partner Z segment calibration — only if stratified mature data show persistent residual miscalibration/discrimination gaps and sample size supports it. Fully separate Partner Z model now would encode immature labels and small-sample noise.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

**Monitors and alert logic**
- **Predictive:** AUROC, Brier, log-loss, predicted-mean vs matured breach by month/variant/source; alert only on cohorts meeting maturity threshold.
- **Calibration:** ECE, calibration intercept/slope, reliability bins by variant/source; require minimum mature positives per bin.
- **Label-maturity:** % labels matured, median/P90 label delay, backlog age, maturity by variant/source/path length; provisional watermark on all downstream metrics.
- **Data-quality:** unknown activity %, unmapped variants %, duplicate rate, missing queue/prefix features, diagnosis-prefix build success, schema vocabulary diff, source lineage version.
- **Process-variant:** V1/V2/V3/unmapped shares on case-start cohorts; routing-policy change log join.
- **Performance-DFG:** median/P90 edge times, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosisdispatch, diagnosis`Remote Fix`; completed-edge censoring note.
- **Population:** Partner Z/source share, rural distance, device age, priority/region mix; standardized vs raw breach gaps.
- **Intervention/deployment:** model/feature/schema versions, mapping patches, supplier incidents, routing policy flags, recalibration/retraining deployments, shadow-vs-champion deltas.

**Alert dependencies / gating**
- Label-maturity alarm (`maturity <90%` or delay > threshold) sets `EVALUATION_PROVISIONAL`: it suppresses auto-retraining, calibration promotion, and “performance collapse” paging; it pages data/outcome-API owners instead.
- Data-quality alarm (unknown/missing/duplicates spike) blocks training ingestion and recalibration, routes to pipeline on-call, and requires clean recomputation before model alarms are actionable.
- Process/population alarms open an investigation and stratified evaluation; they trigger retraining only if mature, clean, stratified tests confirm conditional performance loss.
- Retraining requires all gates green: pipeline clean, labels mature or censoring-handled, drift confirmed within segments, and champion–challenger backtest on fixed vintages.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CANON = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
         "Initial Diagnosis Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}

def monthly_process_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    df = pd.read_csv(path, parse_dates=["timestamp"])
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].astype(str).str.strip().replace(CANON)
    df = df.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"])
    # Duplicate handling: exact source duplicates; keep first and audit count.
    dup_mask = df.duplicated(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "event_id"], keep=False)
    dup_rate = float(dup_mask.mean())
    df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp", "event_id"], keep="first")

    log_all = pm4py.format_dataframe(df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp")
    # Case-start cohort: month = first event month; right for breach/prediction linkage.
    first_ts = df.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min()
    case_month = first_ts.dt.to_period("M").astype(str)

    rows = []
    for month, cases in case_month.groupby(case_month):
        case_log = log_all[log_all["case:concept:name"].isin(cases.index)]
        # Event-calendar cohort: events occurring in month; right for operational load, not outcomes.
        start, end = pd.Period(month).start_time, pd.Period(month).end_time
        cal_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(log_all, start, end, mode="events")
        dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(case_log)
        pdfg, psa, pea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(case_log)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(case_log)
        top_variant, top_n = max(variants.items(), key=lambda kv: len(kv[1])) if variants else ((), 0)
        try:
            temp = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(case_log)
            n_temp_rows = len(temp)
        except Exception:
            n_temp_rows = -1  # audit feature availability; do not fail the evidence job
        edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        rows.append({
            "case_start_month": month, "n_cases": int(cases.size),
            "duplicate_rate_before_clean": dup_rate,
            "unknown_share": float((df[df.case_id.isin(cases.index)]["activity"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()),
            "top_variant": " -> ".join(top_variant), "top_variant_cases": int(top_n),
            "order_to_received_median_h": (pdfg.get(edge, {}).get("median", None) / 3600 if pdfg.get(edge) else None),
            "n_event_calendar_events": len(cal_log), "temporal_feature_rows": n_temp_rows,
            # No pm4py drift detector is assumed: compare these auditable summaries across months explicitly.
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

**Retraining on immature labels (especially Oct)**
- Risk: model learns that long/V3 cases rarely breach because their labels are missing, codifying censoring as low risk and worsening Aug–Sep-type under-prediction.
- Mitigation: maturity gates, fixed-vintage backtests, censoring-aware sampling/weights, Oct quarantine.

**Calibrating around a pipeline bug (Apr–May)**
- Risk: intercept/Platt scaling compensates for wrong prefixes and cached features; when the pipeline is fixed, calibration breaks and corrupted patterns enter training.
- Mitigation: clean recomputation first, frozen-model re-scoring, Apr exclusion until lineage certification, versioned feature rebuilds.

**Ignoring genuine supplier-driven concept drift**
- Risk: dismissing Aug–Sep as “just drift noise” leaves V3 systematically under-predicted while part delays keep breach high; operations misses SLA interventions.
- Mitigation: V3-stratified mature monitoring, supplier lead-time features, temporary V3 alerting uplift with expiry, joint ML–operations incident review.

**Attributing Partner Z's population shift to unfair/biased model behavior without stratified evidence**
- Risk: raw 0.45 vs 0.27 breach gap may reflect V3 mix, rural distance, device age, and slower label maturity; premature fairness conclusions or a separate model could harm a new partner segment or mask missing features.
- Mitigation: matured, variant-stratified, reweighted/transport analyses; add missing legitimate features; review outcomes and errors within comparable strata before fairness claims.

**Treating June routing change as concept drift and retraining aggressively**
- Risk: chasing a mixture change destroys stable within-variant relationships, overfits a policy that may change again, and misprices remote cases.
- Mitigation: standardization by variant, intercept-only recalibration if within-variant calibration holds, policy-change features and scenario monitoring.

**Confusing process/performance drift with a purely ML problem**
- Risk: retraining cannot fix a supplier missing deliveries; predictions may adapt while root-cause SLA harm continues, or model changes mask operational deterioration.
- Mitigation: performance-DFG ownership by process/supplier teams, dual KPIs for prediction quality and actual lead time, interventions targeted at parts logistics.
