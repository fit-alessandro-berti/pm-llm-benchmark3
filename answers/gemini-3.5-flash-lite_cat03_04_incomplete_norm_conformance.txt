## 1. Conformance semantics

To evaluate object-centric process data against a deliberately incomplete normative specification, we must systematically distinguish true noncompliance from benign variation, data-quality artifacts, modeling abstractions, and unmodeled behavior. We define our classification categories as follows:

*   **Forbidden behavior:** Occurs when an observed relation, event-type link, or direct-follow transition explicitly matches a norm marked as *forbidden*. Because the norm is closed-world for forbidden relations, any positive observation of a forbidden link represents an absolute compliance violation.
*   **Missing mandatory behavior:** Occurs when an expected relation or transition marked as *mandatory* within its stated scope is absent in the observed summary. This requires absolute verification that the scope applies and that no abstraction mismatch masks the behavior.
*   **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** Occurs when observed behaviors fall entirely outside the mandatory, forbidden, or typical specifications. Because the norm is open-world, these observations are structurally permitted. They highlight emergent process variants or unmodeled pathways that do not violate policy.
*   **Typical-pattern absence:** Occurs when a transition or relation marked as *typical* is absent. A typical status means the pattern is common and desirable, but exceptions are legitimate. Therefore, the absence of a typical pattern does not constitute a violation; it flags an exception that may warrant business investigation.
*   **Likely data-quality error:** Occurs when an observation violates system constraints or operational logic due to technical failures, erroneous database joins, missing unapplied-cash markers, or identifier collisions. These must be treated as telemetry or ETL issues rather than process noncompliance.
*   **Abstraction mismatch:** Occurs when a normative expectation is specified at one level of granularity (e.g., eventually-follows, business-level execution) while the observed summary or metric operates at another (e.g., directly-follows, event-log adjacency). Failing to account for intervening, unrecorded life-cycle steps produces false-positive violations.
*   **Insufficient evidence:** Occurs when aggregated summary metrics lack the event-level timestamps, attribute payloads, or relational context required to confirm whether an exception falls within a valid normative scope or represents a compliance breach.

### Open-World vs. Closed-World Assumptions

The normative specification operates on a hybrid assumption structure:
1.  **Closed-world for forbidden relations:** Any positive occurrence of an explicitly forbidden relation (e.g., $N3$, $T6$, $E10$) is an automatic violation. The absence of forbidden relations is expected.
2.  **Closed-world for mandatory columns within scope:** Any entity or event within the stated scope that fails to exhibit a mandatory relation (e.g., $N1$, $N4$, $T1$, $T2$, $E1$–$E8$) constitutes a noncompliant deviation.
3.  **Open-world for everything else:** Unspecified relations ($N6$, $T4$, $T5$, $E9$), typical relations ($N2$, $N5$, $T3$), and unmentioned structural combinations are fully permitted. Absence from the norm does not imply noncompliance. Consequently, novel pathways (e.g., compliance reviews, write-offs, co-birth events, and unrecorded intermediate steps) cannot be judged as noncompliant simply because they lack explicit normative sanction.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG (Object-Centric Directly-Follows Graphs)
*   **What it establishes:** OC-DFGs capture direct state transitions within the lifecycle of individual object types (e.g., Orders, Packages, Invoices). They verify whether sequential stepping stones—such as creation leading to validation—occur adjacently in the log.
*   **What it cannot establish:** OC-DFGs cannot establish business process completion when intermediate, unmodeled steps occur. They cannot prove causality across parallel object lifecycles without explicit object interaction edges.
*   **Counts and scopes:** For $N1$, 98 of 100 orders exhibit direct validation, while 2 do not. The summary reveals that 3 orders enter a manual compliance review, suggesting that the missing directly-follows transitions are absorbed by unmodeled intervening activities. For $N2$, intermediate invoicing or fulfillment occurs between validation and closure without violating the typical expectation.
*   **Abstraction and eventually-expected rules:** Interpreting an "eventually expected" rule ($N1$) as a strictly directly-follows rule is a major analytical hazard. In reality, business processes contain loops, parallel paths, and review states. An order that undergoes compliance checks, credit holds, or managerial approvals will not satisfy a rigid directly-follows edge, even though it fully complies with the underlying business policy.

### OTG (Object-Type Graphs)
*   **What it establishes:** OTGs map structural, many-to-many relationships between object types via `object_interaction`, `object_cobirth`, and `object_codeath` edges. They ensure that operational entities link correctly (e.g., packages to deliveries, orders to items).
*   **What it cannot establish:** OTGs cannot establish temporal execution order or business intent; they only prove structural connectivity within the data model.
*   **Counts and scopes:** The observation of 200 package-delivery interactions ($T2$) against 200 pack events confirms structural alignment, though 2 deliveries lack explicit object bindings due to carrier attributes. The single co-birth edge ($T6$) violates the strict prohibition against simultaneous order-payment creation. Codeath edges (e.g., packages and deliveries ending together) are unconstrained by the norm and represent benign structural cleanup.

### ET–OT (Event-Type to Object-Type)
*   **What it establishes:** ET–OT projections establish which event types are legitimately linked to which object types during execution (e.g., ensuring `Pack Package` relates to a `Package`).
*   **What it cannot establish:** ET–OT projections cannot establish whether attributes on those events are valid, nor can they determine whether an event was triggered prematurely.
*   **Counts and scopes:** The critical finding here is $E10$, where a `Sensor Heartbeat` event links to an `Invoice` object ($n=1$). While ET–OT graphs show that an edge exists, semantic interpretation reveals this as a data-quality error driven by a reused identifier. Similarly, unapplied cash ($E8$) lacks an invoice link; without explicit event typing, it appears as a mandatory-edge failure rather than a legitimate unapplied-cash exception.

## 5. PM4Py conformance code sketch

```python
import pm4py

# Assume ocel is loaded, and normative structures are defined as dictionaries/tuples.
# Normative metadata must be maintained externally in policy structures, 
# as bare discovery graphs do not store semantic tags (mandatory/forbidden/typical).

norm_ocdfg = {
    ("Create Order", "Validate Order"): {"status": "mandatory", "scope": "standard Order"},
    ("Validate Order", "Close Order"): {"status": "typical", "scope": "Order"},
    ("Cancel Order", "Dispatch Package"): {"status": "forbidden", "scope": "Order"},
    ("Pack Package", "Dispatch Package"): {"status": "mandatory", "scope": "external shipment"},
    ("Issue Invoice", "Record Payment"): {"status": "typical", "scope": "Invoice"},
    ("Reopen Order", "Close Order"): {"status": "unspecified", "scope": "Order"}
}

norm_otg = {
    ("Order", "object_interaction", Item): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Package", "object_interaction", Delivery): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Invoice", "object_interaction", Payment): {"status": "typical"},
    ("Order", "object_interaction", Customer): {"status": "unspecified"},
    ("Order", "object_interaction", RiskCase): {"status": "unspecified"},
    ("Order", "object_cobirth", Payment): {"status": "forbidden"}
}

norm_etot = {
    ("Create Order", "Order"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Add Item", "Order"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Add Item", "Item"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Pack Package", "Package"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Dispatch Package", "Package"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Dispatch Package", "Delivery"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Record Payment", "Payment"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Record Payment", "Invoice"): {"status": "mandatory"},
    ("Manual Compliance Review", "RiskCase"): {"status": "unspecified"},
    ("Sensor Heartbeat", "Invoice"): {"status": "forbidden"}
}

# 1. Discover raw object-centric models from the OCEL
discovered_ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
discovered_otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
discovered_etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

# 2. Run raw conformance diagnostics using PM4Py functions
raw_ocdfg_conf = pm4py.conformance_ocdfg(ocel, discovered_ocdfg)
raw_otg_conf = pm4py.conformance_otg(ocel, discovered_otg)
raw_etot_conf = pm4py.conformance_etot(ocel, discovered_etot)

# 3. Add a separate semantic classification layer over raw diagnostics
def classify_deviations(raw_results, norm_spec):
    classified_output = []
    for finding in raw_results:
        # Match finding against external normative metadata
        edge_key = finding.get("edge") or finding.get("transition")
        norm_meta = norm_spec.get(edge_key, {"status": "unspecified"})
        
        status = norm_meta["status"]
        if status == "forbidden" and finding.get("observed_count", 0) > 0:
            classification = "Forbidden Behavior Violation"
        elif status == "mandatory" and not finding.get("compliant", True):
            classification = "Missing Mandatory Behavior"
        elif status == "typical" and not finding.get("compliant", True):
            classification = "Typical Pattern Absence (Legitimate Exception)"
        else:
            classification = "Unregulated / Conforming Behavior"
            
        classified_output.append({
            "finding": finding,
            "norm_status": status,
            "semantic_classification": classification
        })
    return classified_output

semantic_ocdfg_report = classify_deviations(raw_ocdfg_conf, norm_ocdfg)
semantic_otg_report = classify_deviations(raw_otg_conf, norm_otg)
semantic_etot_report = classify_deviations(raw_etot_conf, norm_etot)

print("Semantic conformance processing complete. Preserved raw PM4Py outputs with appended policy classification.")
```

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1.  **Handling of Unapplied Cash:**
    *   *Operational risk:* Cash payments received without matching invoices are flagged as missing mandatory ET–OT edges ($E8$), generating false-positive compliance violations during audits.
    *   *Proposed clarification:* Explicitly permit payment events lacking an invoice reference provided they carry an `unapplied_cash` attribute tag.
    *   *Status:* typical.
2.  **Intervening Manual Reviews:**
    *   *Operational risk:* Orders undergoing compliance reviews fail rigid directly-follows checks ($N1$), obscuring legitimate operational exceptions.
    *   *Proposed clarification:* Define $N1$ as an eventually-follows relationship where intermediate review states (`Manual Compliance Review`) are explicitly permitted.
    *   *Status:* mandatory (with relaxed adjacency).
3.  **Delivery Reference Attributes vs. Object Links:**
    *   *Operational risk:* Packages shipped with carrier references stored as attributes rather than formal object relations trigger false-positive $T2$ violations.
    *   *Proposed clarification:* Allow delivery carrier references in event attributes to serve as a temporary proxy for structural delivery objects during transit.
    *   *Status:* typical.
4.  **Lifecycle Termination (Codeath):**
    *   *Operational risk:* Simultaneous termination of package and delivery lifecycles ($object\_codeath$) is unmentioned, causing confusion when automated cleanups occur.
    *   *Proposed clarification:* Explicitly state that joint object codeath events are permitted and do not require separate termination timestamps.
    *   *Status:* unspecified.
5.  **Financial Write-Offs and Open Credit Terms:**
    *   *Operational risk:* Invoices remaining open or written off ($N5$) are misclassified as missing payments if credit terms exceed standard observation windows.
    *   *Proposed clarification:* Recognize write-off events and approved open credit terms as legitimate terminating states for invoices.
    *   *Status:* typical.
6.  **Telemetry and Heartbeat Ingestion:**
    *   *Operational risk:* Monitoring streams linked to financial objects via ID collision ($E10$) corrupt audit trails.
    *   *Proposed clarification:* Forbid any monitoring, sensor, or system heartbeat event from linking to financial object types.
    *   *Status:* forbidden.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Our conclusions are robust regarding the detection of explicitly forbidden events, such as the sensor heartbeat-invoice collision ($E10$) and the order-cancellation dispatch sequence ($N3$), which violate closed-world prohibitions. However, interpretations of missing mandatory behavior—such as unvalidated orders ($N1$) and unlinked payments ($E8$)—depend heavily on the norm’s incompleteness and the level of abstraction, as unrecorded intermediate review steps and unflagged unapplied cash can mask true compliance. Conclusions regarding open credit terms and write-offs rely on suspected data-quality and metadata gaps rather than direct behavioral proof. Ultimately, graph summaries alone cannot establish operational intent, subjective business justification, or the physical reality behind system events; full event-payload inspection and qualitative audit are required to resolve these ambiguities.
