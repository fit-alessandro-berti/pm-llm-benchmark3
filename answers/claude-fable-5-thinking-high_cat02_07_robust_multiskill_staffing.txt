# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## 1.1 Demand and service assumptions (each labeled)

- **A1 (arrival process).** Expected arrivals per two-hour block are treated as the mean of a Poisson count per block, with arrival instants uniform within the block. *Why needed:* the prompt gives only expected counts; Poisson is the least-informative default for independent incident arrivals, and uniform-within-block avoids inventing intra-block structure.
- **A2 (service distributions).** Each activity's base service time is a two-point mixture: with probability `p_tail` a Uniform draw on the complex-tail range, otherwise a Uniform draw on the ordinary range. *Why needed:* ranges plus a tail probability under-determine a distribution; the uniform mixture uses exactly the supplied numbers, nothing more. Mixture means (base minutes, before multipliers):

| activity | ordinary mean | tail mean | p_tail | mixture mean |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Intake | 6.5 | 12.0 | 0.08 | **6.9** |
| Mechanical | 23.0 | 45.0 | 0.15 | **26.3** |
| Electrical | 28.5 | 52.5 | 0.18 | **32.8** |
| Software | 32.5 | 62.5 | 0.20 | **38.5** |
| Validation | 14.0 | 28.5 | 0.12 | **15.7** |

- **A3 (multipliers).** Actual service = base draw x resource multiplier; "-" means not qualified, ever.
- **A4 (breaks and shifts).** A resource cannot *start* work during its break; a task already in progress when the break begins is paused for 30 minutes (finish shifted right) and the break is consumed. Overtime lets a task finish at most 30 minutes after shift end; no task is started if its drawn duration would exceed that cap. *Why needed:* the prompt does not say whether breaks interrupt work; pausing is the interpretation that neither deletes the break nor cancels work.
- **A5 (single-day horizon, carryover).** I simulate one day, 08:00-20:30. Cases not finished by then are counted as `incomplete` and as SLA breaches for P1/P2; the P3 24-hour target cannot be verified within one day, so `incomplete` is reported as the proxy for overnight backlog. This is a bound, not an estimate, for P3 attainment.
- **A6 (handover semantics).** The 7-minute handover applies whenever consecutive activities of a case are performed by different resources; the extra 5-minute team move applies when consecutive *diagnostic* activities cross the mechanical vs. electrical/software family boundary with a resource change. Handover time consumes the *receiving* resource's capacity. *Why needed:* the prompt says "add 7 minutes of handover delay" without saying whose clock it burns; charging the receiver is conservative for the bottleneck.
- **A7 (setup semantics).** The 5-minute skill-family setup is charged when a resource's consecutive diagnostic tasks belong to different families (Mechanical vs. Electrical/Software).
- **A8 (preemption).** Only P1 tasks preempt; only diagnostic activities are preemptible; a case can be preempted once; the interrupted task re-enters the queue with remaining base time x 1.10 and may resume on any eligible resource. The victim chosen is the lowest-priority in-progress diagnostic with the most remaining work.
- **A9 (ME/ES parallel-vs-sequential decision).** Decided *at the moment Intake completes*, policy-dependent (Section 3). Parallel adds the 8-minute coordination before Validation; sequential runs branches back-to-back, preferably on one cross-skilled resource (no handover, no team move, no coordination) but lengthens the critical path by the second branch's full duration.
- **A10 (U5 outage).** The 09:30-10:00 IT outage blocks *starting* Intake; intakes in progress continue. Arrivals still occur and queue.
- **A11 (renormalization in U1/U2).** U1: +8pp P1 taken pro-rata from P2 and P3. U2: E and ES percentages double, all five type shares renormalized to 100% (so total arrivals unchanged, mix shifts) — this is the "proportions are renormalized" reading; an alternative reading (total volume also rises) would be strictly worse and is covered qualitatively by U1's volume stress.
- **A12 (dispatcher knowledge).** The dispatcher does not know the drawn duration when ranking resources (it uses expected cost), except for the shift-end feasibility check, where actual duration is used to avoid simulating abandoned work. This mild clairvoyance is flagged as a limitation.

## 1.2 Objectives (two formulations)

- **O1 — SLA/tail-risk objective (primary):** maximize `min(P1_SLA_s over scenarios s)` subject to `P2_SLA >= target - tolerance`, tie-broken by p90 flow time. This is a max-min (worst-scenario) formulation: a policy is only as good as its worst U-scenario P1 attainment. Tail risk enters through both the p90 tie-break and the fact that SLA attainment *is* a tail statistic.
- **O2 — Fairness/robustness-weighted cost:** minimize `E[flow time] + lambda1 * max_over_scenarios(regret vs. best policy) + lambda2 * 1{any resource planned utilization > 0.90} + lambda3 * std(resource utilizations)`. This penalizes fragile plans (the >90% rule from the prompt) and unequal load, not just mean delay.

These two objectives can rank policies differently, and in Section 5 I show they do — which is why two recommendations are defensible depending on which objective the organization owns.

## 1.3 Decision variables

(i) eligibility sets per activity per policy; (ii) resource-choice rule among eligible idle resources; (iii) P1 reservation on/off and who is reserved; (iv) overflow thresholds (wait time before a slow, high-multiplier resource may take the task); (v) parallel/sequential rule for ME/ES by priority; (vi) preemption trigger; (vii) overtime use (bounded at 30 min).

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

## 2.1 Offered load by skill and block (base scenario)

Expected base-minutes of work per block = arrivals x type-share x mixture mean. Skill demand shares: Mechanical = M+ME, Electrical = E+ME+ES, Software = S+ES.

| block | Intake | Mech | Elec | Soft | Valid | total base-min | raw staffed min | rough rho |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 08-10 | 49 | 92 | 126 | 67 | 110 | **445** | 480 (Ada,Ben,Chen,Farah) | 0.93 |
| 10-12 | 63 | 118 | 177 | 87 | 142 | **586** | 600 (+Deepa) | 0.98 |
| 12-14 | 49 | 77 | 138 | 81 | 110 | **455** | 510 (breaks bite; +Emil, -Farah) | 0.89 |
| 14-16 | 69 | 105 | 204 | 116 | 157 | **651** | 570 (Deepa on break 30) | **1.14** |
| 16-18 | 42 | 55 | 108 | 92 | 94 | **392** | 210 (Deepa 90 + Emil 120) | **1.87** |
| 18-20 | 21 | 28 | 44 | 52 | 47 | **192** | 120 (Emil only) | **1.60** |
| **day** | 293 | 475 | 797 | 495 | 660 | **~2,720** | **2,490** | **~1.09** |

Because every multiplier is >= 1 and handover/setup/coordination overheads add roughly 200-370 min/day (simulated range), realistic day-level utilization is **~1.15-1.25 before the 180 min of total possible overtime**. Three structural facts dominate everything a routing policy can do:

1. **The day is over-offered.** No dispatch rule clears the base day; policies decide *who waits and who breaches*, not whether backlog exists. P3's 24-hour SLA is the intended pressure valve.
2. **The evening cliff.** From 16:00 capacity collapses (rho 1.6-1.9). Worse, **skill coverage holes**: after Deepa leaves at 18:00 nobody on shift can do Mechanical at all (Emil lacks the skill), and before Emil arrives at 12:00 the only Software-capable resources are Chen (1.25) and Farah (1.40). These holes, not average load, drive the P2/P3 tails.
3. **Electrical is the scarce skill.** It carries ~29% of all base work; the only 1.00-multiplier electrical resources are Chen and Deepa. Afternoon electrical demand (14-16: 204 base-min) alone can absorb both.

## 2.2 Bottleneck identification per scenario (bounds/approximations)

- **Base:** binding constraint is 14:00-20:00 aggregate capacity; Electrical queue at 14-16; Validation becomes secondary bottleneck in the evening because every completed case funnels into it while only Deepa/Emil (then Emil) remain.
- **U1 (morning x1.7):** 08-12 offered load ~ (445+586) x 1.7 ~ **1,753 base-min vs. 1,080 staffed min** (rho ~ 1.6 for four hours). The backlog wave collides with the already over-offered 14-16 peak; P1 share is also higher, so preemption frequency rises. Bottleneck migrates from evening to *all-day*; Farah's 08-12 window becomes precious despite her 1.40 multipliers.
- **U2 (E/ES doubling 12-16):** renormalized electrical share rises from ~60% to ~71-73% of cases in those blocks; electrical base load 12-16 rises roughly 15-20%. Bottleneck concentrates on Chen/Deepa; Ada (1.35) and Emil (1.30) must absorb electrical overflow at a ~30% speed penalty — pooling pays its multiplier tax exactly here.
- **U3 (Chen absent):** removes 450 staffed minutes, the only 1.00 Electrical resource before 10:00, and the only sub-1.40 Software resource before 12:00. Morning software cases either wait for Emil (>= 12:00 start, guaranteeing P2 risk for 08:00-10:00 software arrivals) or go to Farah at 1.40. Electrical rests entirely on Deepa (from 10:00) + slow substitutes.
- **U4 (tails double):** diagnostic mixture means rise ~13-16% (Mech 30.6, Elec 37.1, Soft 44.5) but *variance roughly doubles its contribution*, so p90/p95 and preemption exposure grow much faster than the mean; rho at 14-16 goes from 1.14 to ~1.3.
- **U5 (U1 + U3 + outage):** the worst morning (x1.7 volume, more P1) with the thinnest morning roster (Ada, Ben, Farah only until 10:00; no Chen ever) plus a 30-minute intake freeze that releases a synchronized batch at 10:00. Intake itself briefly becomes the bottleneck, then Electrical/Software; the batch release creates a queueing shock that persists into the afternoon.

# 3. Candidate policies

All three are fully specified dispatch rules (and are implemented verbatim in Section 8). Queue discipline everywhere: sort ready tasks by (priority rank, ready time); P1 preemption per A8 is active in all policies.

## P-A: SPEC — strict specialization with fixed queues

- **Eligibility:** home queues — Ada -> Mechanical; Ben -> Intake+Validation; Chen -> Electrical; Deepa -> Electrical; Emil -> Software; Farah -> Intake+Validation. Escape valve (needed to avoid deadlock, disclosed): if *no* home resource for an activity is rostered on shift at time t (e.g., Validation after 16:00, Software before 12:00), the activity opens to any qualified resource.
- **Resource choice:** among idle eligible, minimize expected immediate cost = multiplier x expected base + handover(7) if resource change + setup(5) if family switch.
- **ME/ES:** always parallel (branches live in different queues by construction); 8-min coordination always paid.
- **Overflow trigger:** none, that is the point — and the weakness.

## P-B: POOL — full pooling with a transparent greedy rule

- **Eligibility:** any qualified resource for any activity.
- **Resource choice:** same auditable cost rule — "assign to the idle qualified resource with the smallest (multiplier x expected base + 7 if this changes hands + 5 if the resource must switch skill family)". Operators can verify any assignment with one multiplication and two additions.
- **ME/ES:** parallel iff two distinct qualified resources are idle at decision time, else sequential (continuity bonus in the cost rule then keeps the case on one cross-skilled resource where possible).
- **Reservation:** none. Overflow: unrestricted — the greedy cost rule alone decides.

## P-C: HYBRID — reserved P1 capacity + controlled cross-skill overflow

- **Reservation:** at any time, the fastest on-shift electrical-capable resource (Chen > Deepa > Emil, by multiplier and coverage) is the *P1 anchor*. The anchor serves P1 tasks of any activity first; when no P1 is waiting it may take Intake only (short tasks keep it warm without trapping it in a 60-minute software tail).
- **Overflow trigger (controlled cross-skill):** a non-P1 task may be assigned to a resource whose multiplier for that activity exceeds 1.25 only after waiting >= 30 min (P2) or >= 60 min (P3). Before that, it waits for a fast resource. This caps the "multiplier tax" that pure pooling pays.
- **Resource choice:** same transparent cost rule as POOL among the remaining eligible set (continuity preferred, family-setup aware).
- **ME/ES:** P1 — parallel whenever two resources can be found (shortest critical path); P2 — parallel iff two idle now; P3 — always sequential, preferring one cross-skilled resource (saves 7+5+8 = 20 overhead minutes per case, spends only elapsed time, which P3's 24 h SLA can afford).
- **Preemption:** as A8; the reservation makes preemption rare because the anchor is usually free for P1.

(Considered and rejected as primary: pure shortest-expected-remaining-time — it starves P3 software tails badly and is hard to audit; threshold-parallelization-only — dominated by HYBRID which contains it.)

Reading across scenarios (this is the robustness content):

- **No policy meets the P1 90% target in the base case** (best: HYBRID 0.86) and none comes near the P2 90% target. This is the capacity conclusion of Section 2 restated by simulation: routing redistributes breach risk; it cannot manufacture evening capacity.
- **SPEC is dominated everywhere.** Its only virtue (lowest base max_util, 0.91, and low setup churn in *stable* weeks) never compensates: worst P1 SLA in 5 of 6 scenarios, catastrophic under U1/U5 (0.52/0.44). Bottleneck migration is invisible to fixed queues.
- **POOL vs. HYBRID is a genuine trade**, i.e., the specialization-pooling tension the data was built to expose: POOL wins mean delay, P2 SLA and completions in every scenario; HYBRID wins P1 SLA in 5 of 6 scenarios (base +8pp, U1 +7pp, U2 +21pp, U4 +16pp) and has the flattest degradation (worst/base mean-delay ratio 1.19 vs. POOL 1.59 vs. SPEC 1.50).
- **Bottleneck migration:** base -> evening (16:00+); U1 -> whole morning + Intake; U2 -> electrical-capable pair; U3 -> Deepa personally; U4 -> variance (tail capture of fast servers); U5 -> Intake (outage batch), then everything. HYBRID's anchor follows the electrical bottleneck by construction; POOL follows it implicitly via queue lengths; SPEC does not follow it at all.
- **HYBRID's one loss (U5, P1 0.75 vs. POOL 0.79)** is diagnostic: with Chen absent and only four resources, *idle* reservation of one of them costs more than it protects; protection should degrade to preemption-only. This directly motivates the mode-switch trigger in Section 5.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary: HYBRID (P-C), with an explicit degraded mode.** Under objective O1 (max-min P1 SLA across scenarios), HYBRID dominates: its worst-scenario P1 attainment (0.75, U5) beats SPEC's (0.44) and matches POOL's worst (0.69, U2) while being better in five of six scenarios. Under O2 it is also strong: max_util stays <= 0.99 (POOL exceeds 1.0, i.e., burns overtime, in every scenario), and its scenario regret is the smallest. The price — ~15 min of mean delay and ~10-18pp of P2 SLA vs. POOL — is exactly the premium the P1 target is supposed to buy; if the organization's true objective weights P2 throughput and mean delay (an O2-like objective with small lambda on P1 risk), **POOL is the defensible alternative**, and that disagreement is a property of the objectives, not of the analysis.

**Fallback mode (degrade-to-preemption):** when *staffed headcount on shift <= 4* or a P1 anchor candidate is absent (U3/U5 conditions), switch the reservation from "idle anchor" to "preemption-only": the anchor becomes fully work-conserving, and P1 protection relies on the single allowed preemption plus head-of-queue priority. Rationale: the U5 row shows idle reservation inverts from asset to liability exactly when capacity is scarcest.

**Trigger thresholds (auditable, from the block analysis and sim):**

- Enter fallback: on-shift resources <= 4, or Chen and Deepa both unavailable, or P1 queue empty for 20+ min while P2 backlog > 5 cases.
- Exit fallback: headcount >= 5 and P1 arrivals in the last hour >= 2.
- Surge trigger (U1-like): realized arrivals in a 2-hour block > 1.4x plan -> tighten overflow thresholds to 20/40 min (P2/P3) so fast servers are not captured by slow-eligible work, and force P3 ME/ES sequential (saves 20 overhead min/case).
- Structural escalation: the simulation shows even the best policy misses P1 90% at base. Recommend a *capacity* action outside the policy space: shift Farah to 14:00-18:00 or add any evening resource; the 16:00-20:00 rho of 1.6-1.9 is not a routing problem.

**When another policy is preferable:** POOL on days known in advance to be P1-light (P1 share < 8%) or when the P2 SLA is contractually binding and P1 volume is tiny; SPEC only if handover/setup penalties were several times larger than stated or quality/skill-retention concerns (Section 6) dominate.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming of estimates.** The dispatch cost uses expected base times; resources or team leads who learn that longer estimates attract fewer assignments can inflate them. Mitigation: estimates come from the mined service-time model (Section 7), not manual entry, and are recalibrated weekly against actuals.
- **P3 starvation.** Strict priority plus an over-offered day means P3 systematically becomes the overnight backlog (16-35 incomplete cases/day in simulation). The 24 h SLA absorbs this only if next-morning capacity clears it — monitor P3 age-in-queue and add an aging escalation (P3 waiting > 6 h is scored as P2) if the backlog trends up across days.
- **Skill erosion.** HYBRID's overflow thresholds concentrate work on lowest-multiplier resources; Ada's electrical (1.35) and Chen's software (1.25) skills atrophy without use, which silently destroys the resilience that U3 showed pooling provides. Mitigation: minimum weekly cross-skill quota routed during low-load blocks (18:00-20:00).
- **Excessive preemption.** Bounded by design (one per case, 10% restart), but under U1's higher P1 share preemptions cluster on whoever holds long diagnostics; track preemptions per resource per day and cap at 3.
- **Coordination overhead.** Parallel ME/ES pays 8 min plus usually 7+5 handover; simulated total overhead is 210-370 min/day — about one full resource-shift equivalent. The P3-sequential rule in HYBRID is why its overhead is lowest in U3/U5; do not let local optimizers re-parallelize P3.
- **Validation bottleneck.** Everything funnels into Validation; after 16:00 only Deepa/Emil (both 1.00) can do it and after 18:00 only Emil. If diagnostics speed up (e.g., after capacity fixes), Validation becomes the binding stage — watch its queue separately.
- **Fairness.** HYBRID's utilization SD (0.13-0.17) is the worst of the three because the anchor idles while others run hot, and the same fast resources are always preferred. Rotate the anchor role across qualified staff day-by-day and include utilization spread in the weekly review; the 90% planned-utilization cap should be enforced at the roster, not discovered at 15:00.
- **Human interpretability.** Every rule above is expressible in one sentence with observable inputs (multiplier table, queue wait, headcount). Resist adding learned scoring: the moment operators cannot reproduce an assignment by hand, they will override the dispatcher, and the log will no longer reflect the policy being evaluated.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

**Event attributes to record** (one interval event per activity segment):

| attribute | content |
|---|---|
| `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `org:resource` | case, activity, resource |
| `start_timestamp`, `time:timestamp` | segment start / end (interval log) |
| `queue_entry_ts` | when the task became ready (queue-entry) |
| `assign_ts`, `eligible_set`, `chosen_rule` | assignment decision time, resource set the policy allowed, rule that fired (continuity / min-cost / overflow / anchor / preempt) |
| `skill_used`, `resource_multiplier` | activity family and applied multiplier |
| `planned_service`, `actual_service` | expected vs. realized minutes |
| `pause_reason`, `resume_ts` | break / preemption / outage segments |
| `handover_from`, `handover_min`, `setup_min`, `team_move_flag` | overhead accounting |
| `branch_mode` | par / seq for ME-ES cases |
| `policy_version`, `mode` (normal/fallback/surge) | which rule set was live |
| `scenario_flag` | declared shock (absence, outage, surge detected) |
| `priority`, `sla_deadline_ts`, `sla_clock_remaining` | SLA clock at each event |

**PM4Py analyses and what each detects:**

- `pm4py.format_dataframe` + `insert_case_service_waiting_time`: case-level service vs. waiting split. *Caveat to enforce in reporting:* these are case totals — "waiting" bundles queueing, handover, coordination and pauses; stage-specific waits must be computed from `queue_entry_ts`->`start_timestamp` per activity, and truncated (incomplete) cases must be excluded or right-censored, otherwise drift alarms fire on artifacts.
- `pm4py.get_service_time` per activity, windowed weekly: detects service-time drift (a U4-like tail regime shows as rising mean *and* variance for diagnostics while Intake stays flat).
- `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` weekly: rising density or new heavy edges (e.g., Ada->Deepa) means the continuity rule is failing or overflow thresholds are too loose — the handover burden KPI in one picture.
- Directly-follows graphs / performance spectrum by time-of-day: bottleneck migration — the base signature is congestion after 14:00 at Electrical then Validation; a shift of the red edges to the morning is the U1 signature and should trip the surge trigger automatically.
- Conformance of `chosen_rule` against the declared policy version: measures override rate, the leading indicator that operators no longer trust the dispatcher.
- Control charts on: P1 SLA (daily), P3 age-in-queue p90, preemptions/resource/day, anchor idle share, overhead min/day, per-resource utilization vs. the 0.90 cap.

RESTART = 1.10      # preemption restart penalty on remaining base time
OVERTIME = 30.0
HORIZON = 750.0     # 20:30, last possible overtime finish
SLA = {"P1": 120.0, "P2": 480.0, "P3": 1440.0}
PRIO_RANK = {"P1": 0, "P2": 1, "P3": 2}


def scenario_blocks(scn, rng):
    """Return (blocks, absent_set, outage_window, tail_mult) for a scenario."""
    blocks = [[b, float(lam), list(pr), list(ty)] for b, lam, pr, ty in BASE_BLOCKS]
    absent, outage, tail_mult = set(), None, 1.0
    if scn in ("U1", "U5"):
        for row in blocks:
            if row[0] < 240:
                row[1] = row[1] * 1.7
                p1 = row[2][0] + 8            # +8pp P1, taken pro-rata from P2/P3
                rest = 100 - p1
                s = row[2][1] + row[2][2]
                row[2] = [p1, row[2][1] * rest / s, row[2][2] * rest / s]
    if scn == "U2":
        for row in blocks:
            if 240 <= row[0] < 480:
                ty = row[3]
                ty[1] *= 2; ty[4] *= 2        # double E and ES, renormalize
                s = sum(ty)
                row[3] = [x * 100 / s for x in ty]
    if scn in ("U3", "U5"):
        absent = {"Chen"}
    if scn == "U4":
        tail_mult = 2.0
    if scn == "U5":
        outage = (90.0, 120.0)                # 09:30-10:00, no Intake starts
    return blocks, absent, outage, tail_mult


def draw_base(activity, rng, tail_mult):
    lo, hi, tlo, thi, p = SERVICE[activity]
    if activity in DIAG:
        p = min(1.0, p * tail_mult)
    if rng.random() < p:
        return rng.uniform(tlo, thi)
    return rng.uniform(lo, hi)


class Case:
    def __init__(self, cid, arr, prio, ctype):
        self.cid, self.arr, self.prio, self.ctype = cid, arr, prio, ctype
        self.mode = None          # 'par' or 'seq' for ME/ES
        self.diags_left = list(TYPE_DIAGS[ctype])
        self.diags_done = 0
        self.last_res = None      # resource of previous completed activity
        self.last_act = None
        self.preempted = False
        self.end = None


class Task:
    __slots__ = ("case", "activity", "base", "ready", "resumed")

    def __init__(self, case, activity, base, ready, resumed=False):
        self.case, self.activity, self.base = case, activity, base
        self.ready, self.resumed = ready, resumed


class ResState:
    def __init__(self, name):
        self.name = name
        self.spec = RESOURCES[name]
        self.task = None          # (Task, start, finish)
        self.brk_used = self.spec["brk"] is None
        self.last_family = None   # diagnostic family of previous task
        self.busy = 0.0           # total busy minutes (incl. overheads)
        self.overhead = 0.0       # handover+setup minutes

    def on_shift(self, t):
        s, e = self.spec["shift"]
        return s <= t < e

    def available(self, t):
        if self.task is not None or not self.on_shift(t):
            return False
        brk = self.spec["brk"]
        if brk and not self.brk_used and brk[0] <= t < brk[1]:
            return False
        return True

    def rostered(self):
        s, e = self.spec["shift"]
        return (e - s) - (30 if self.spec["brk"] else 0)


# ------------------------------------------------------------------ policies
SPEC_HOME = {"Ada": {"Mechanical"}, "Ben": {"Intake", "Validation"},
             "Chen": {"Electrical"}, "Deepa": {"Electrical"},
             "Emil": {"Software"}, "Farah": {"Intake", "Validation"}}


class Policy:
    name = "?"

    def eligible(self, task, states, t, p1_waiting):
        raise NotImplementedError

    def qualified(self, res, activity):
        return activity in RESOURCES[res]["mult"]

    def choose(self, task, cands, states, t):
        # default: minimize immediate cost = mult*base + handover + setup
        def cost(r):
            m = RESOURCES[r]["mult"][task.activity]
            c = m * task.base
            if task.case.last_res is not None and task.case.last_res != r:
                c += HANDOVER
            fam = FAMILY.get(task.activity)
            if fam and states[r].last_family and states[r].last_family != fam:
                c += SETUP
            return c
        return min(cands, key=cost)

    def decide_mode(self, case, states, t):
        return "par"


class SpecPolicy(Policy):
    """Strict specialization: home queues; an activity opens to all qualified
    resources only if no home resource is rostered on shift at time t."""
    name = "SPEC"

    def eligible(self, task, states, t, p1_waiting):
        home = [r for r, acts in SPEC_HOME.items()
                if task.activity in acts and r in states]
        on_shift_home = [r for r in home if states[r].on_shift(t)]
        pool = on_shift_home if on_shift_home else \
            [r for r in states if self.qualified(r, task.activity)]
        return [r for r in pool if self.qualified(r, task.activity)]

    def decide_mode(self, case, states, t):
        return "par"   # separate queues per skill -> branches run independently


class PoolPolicy(Policy):
    """Full pooling: any qualified resource; greedy min immediate-cost choice."""
    name = "POOL"

    def eligible(self, task, states, t, p1_waiting):
        return [r for r in states if self.qualified(r, task.activity)]

    def decide_mode(self, case, states, t):
        # parallel iff two distinct idle qualified resources exist right now
        a, b = TYPE_DIAGS[case.ctype]
        ia = [r for r in states if self.qualified(r, a) and states[r].available(t)]
        ib = [r for r in states if self.qualified(r, b) and states[r].available(t)]
        return "par" if (ia and ib and len(set(ia) | set(ib)) >= 2) else "seq"


class HybridPolicy(Policy):
    """P1 reservation + controlled overflow.
    - The fastest on-shift electrical-capable resource (Chen>Deepa>Emil) is
      reserved: it takes P1 tasks first; if no P1 waits it may take Intake only.
    - Non-P1 tasks may use a resource whose multiplier exceeds 1.25 only after
      waiting past a threshold (P2: 30 min, P3: 60 min).
    - Mode: P1 parallel if feasible, P2 parallel if two idle, P3 sequential."""
    name = "HYBRID"
    SLOW = 1.25
    WAIT_THR = {"P2": 30.0, "P3": 60.0}

    def reserved(self, states, t):
        for r in ("Chen", "Deepa", "Emil"):
            if r in states and states[r].on_shift(t):
                return r
        return None

    def eligible(self, task, states, t, p1_waiting):
        res = self.reserved(states, t)
        out = []
        for r in states:
            if not self.qualified(r, task.activity):
                continue
            if task.case.prio != "P1" and r == res:
                if not (task.activity == "Intake" and not p1_waiting):
                    continue
            m = RESOURCES[r]["mult"][task.activity]
            if task.case.prio != "P1" and m > self.SLOW:
                if (t - task.ready) < self.WAIT_THR.get(task.case.prio, 0.0):
                    continue
            out.append(r)
        return out

    def decide_mode(self, case, states, t):
        if case.prio == "P3":
            return "seq"
        a, b = TYPE_DIAGS[case.ctype]
        ia = [r for r in states if self.qualified(r, a) and states[r].available(t)]
        ib = [r for r in states if self.qualified(r, b) and states[r].available(t)]
        return "par" if len(set(ia) | set(ib)) >= 2 else "seq"


# ---------------------------------------------------------------- simulation
def gen_cases(blocks, rng, tail_mult):
    cases = []
    k = 0
    for b0, lam, pr, ty in blocks:
        n = rng.poisson(lam)
        for _ in range(n):
            arr = b0 + rng.uniform(0, 120)
            prio = rng.choice(["P1", "P2", "P3"], p=np.array(pr) / sum(pr))
            ctype = rng.choice(CASE_TYPES, p=np.array(ty) / sum(ty))
            cases.append(Case(f"C{k:03d}", arr, prio, ctype))
            k += 1
    cases.sort(key=lambda c: c.arr)
    return cases


def simulate(policy, scn, seed, collect_log=False):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    blocks, absent, outage, tail_mult = scenario_blocks(scn, rng)
    states = {r: ResState(r) for r in RESOURCES if r not in absent}
    cases = gen_cases(blocks, rng, tail_mult)
    arrivals = [(c.arr, c) for c in cases]
    queue, log = [], []
    t, ai = 0.0, 0

    def duration(res, task, t0):
        """actual minutes on resource incl. handover/setup/break pause."""
        st = states[res]
        m = RESOURCES[res]["mult"][task.activity]
        dur = m * task.base
        oh = 0.0
        c = task.case
        if c.last_res is not None and c.last_res != res:
            oh += HANDOVER                     # ASSUMPTION A6: receiver pays
            fam_new, fam_old = FAMILY.get(task.activity), FAMILY.get(c.last_act)
            if fam_new and fam_old and fam_new != fam_old:
                oh += TEAM_MOVE
        fam = FAMILY.get(task.activity)
        if fam and st.last_family and st.last_family != fam:
            oh += SETUP                        # ASSUMPTION A7
        dur += oh
        pause = 0.0
        brk = st.spec["brk"]
        if brk and not st.brk_used and t0 < brk[0] < t0 + dur:
            pause = brk[1] - brk[0]            # ASSUMPTION A4: task pauses in break
        return dur, oh, pause

    def try_assign(now):
        queue.sort(key=lambda x: (PRIO_RANK[x.case.prio], x.ready))
        p1_waiting = any(x.case.prio == "P1" for x in queue if x.ready <= now)
        assigned = True
        while assigned:
            assigned = False
            for task in list(queue):
                if task.ready > now:
                    continue
                if task.activity == "Intake" and outage and outage[0] <= now < outage[1]:
                    continue                   # ASSUMPTION A10: outage blocks Intake starts
                cands = [r for r in policy.eligible(task, states, now, p1_waiting)
                         if states[r].available(now)]
                feas = []
                for r in cands:
                    dur, oh, pause = duration(r, task, now)
                    if now + dur + pause <= states[r].spec["shift"][1] + OVERTIME:
                        feas.append(r)         # ASSUMPTION A4: 30-min overtime cap
                if not feas:
                    continue
                r = policy.choose(task, feas, states, now)
                dur, oh, pause = duration(r, task, now)
                st = states[r]
                if pause > 0:
                    st.brk_used = True
                st.task = (task, now, now + dur + pause)
                st.busy += dur
                st.overhead += oh
                queue.remove(task)
                assigned = True
                p1_waiting = any(x.case.prio == "P1" for x in queue if x.ready <= now)

    def try_preempt(now):
        """ASSUMPTION A8: unassigned ready P1 tasks may interrupt one
        lower-priority in-progress diagnostic; victim resumes with +10% base."""
        for task in [x for x in queue if x.case.prio == "P1" and x.ready <= now]:
            if task.activity == "Intake" and outage and outage[0] <= now < outage[1]:
                continue
            cands = policy.eligible(task, states, now, True)
            victims = []
            for r in cands:
                st = states[r]
                if st.task is None:
                    continue
                vt, vs, vf = st.task
                if (vt.activity in DIAG and vt.case.prio != "P1"
                        and not vt.case.preempted and vf - now > 5):
                    victims.append((PRIO_RANK[vt.case.prio], -(vf - now), r))
            if not victims:
                continue
            victims.sort(reverse=True)         # lowest priority, most remaining
            _, _, r = victims[0]
            st = states[r]
            vt, vs, vf = st.task
            m = RESOURCES[r]["mult"][vt.activity]
            rem_base = max(0.0, (vf - now) / m)
            st.busy -= (vf - now)              # un-count unserved part
            log.append((vt.case.cid, vt.activity, vs, now, r))
            vt.case.preempted = True
            # 10% restart penalty is embedded in the requeued remaining base time
            queue.append(Task(vt.case, vt.activity, rem_base * RESTART, now, resumed=True))
            st.task = None
            dur, oh, pause = duration(r, task, now)
            if now + dur + pause <= st.spec["shift"][1] + OVERTIME:
                if pause > 0:
                    st.brk_used = True
                st.task = (task, now, now + dur + pause)
                st.busy += dur
                st.overhead += oh
                queue.remove(task)

    def on_complete(task, res, now):
        c = task.case
        c.last_res, c.last_act = res, task.activity
        if task.activity == "Intake":
            diags = TYPE_DIAGS[c.ctype]
            if len(diags) == 1:
                queue.append(Task(c, diags[0], draw_base(diags[0], rng, tail_mult), now))
            else:
                c.mode = policy.decide_mode(c, states, now)   # ASSUMPTION A9
                if c.mode == "par":
                    for d in diags:
                        queue.append(Task(c, d, draw_base(d, rng, tail_mult), now))
                else:
                    c.diags_left = list(diags)
                    d = c.diags_left.pop(0)
                    queue.append(Task(c, d, draw_base(d, rng, tail_mult), now))
        elif task.activity in DIAG:
            c.diags_done += 1
            need = len(TYPE_DIAGS[c.ctype])
            if c.diags_done < need and c.mode == "seq":
                d = c.diags_left.pop(0)
                queue.append(Task(c, d, draw_base(d, rng, tail_mult), now))
            elif c.diags_done == need:
                ready = now + (PAR_COORD if c.mode == "par" else 0.0)
                queue.append(Task(c, "Validation", draw_base("Validation", rng, tail_mult), ready))
        else:
            c.end = now

    # main event loop
    while True:
        while ai < len(arrivals) and arrivals[ai][0] <= t:
            c = arrivals[ai][1]
            queue.append(Task(c, "Intake", draw_base("Intake", rng, tail_mult), c.arr))
            ai += 1
        for r, st in states.items():
            if st.task and st.task[2] <= t:
                task, s0, f0 = st.task
                st.task = None
                fam = FAMILY.get(task.activity)
                if fam:
                    st.last_family = fam
                log.append((task.case.cid, task.activity, s0, f0, r))
                on_complete(task, r, t)
        try_assign(t)
        try_preempt(t)
        try_assign(t)
        # next event time
        cand = [st.task[2] for st in states.values() if st.task]
        if ai < len(arrivals):
            cand.append(arrivals[ai][0])
        for st in states.values():
            s, e = st.spec["shift"]
            for x in (s, e):
                if x > t:
                    cand.append(x)
            brk = st.spec["brk"]
            if brk and not st.brk_used and brk[1] > t:
                cand.append(brk[1])
        for task in queue:
            if task.ready > t:
                cand.append(task.ready)
        if outage:
            for x in outage:
                if x > t:
                    cand.append(x)
        cand = [x for x in cand if x > t + 1e-9]
        if not cand or t >= HORIZON:
            break
        t = min(min(cand), HORIZON)

    # metrics: mean/p90 over completed cases; SLA counts incomplete as breach
    done = [c for c in cases if c.end is not None]
    flows = np.array([c.end - c.arr for c in done]) if done else np.array([np.nan])
    utils = {r: st.busy / max(st.rostered(), 1) for r, st in states.items()}
    sla = {}
    for p in ("P1", "P2"):
        sub = [c for c in cases if c.prio == p]
        ok = [c for c in sub if c.end is not None and c.end - c.arr <= SLA[p]]
        sla[p] = len(ok) / len(sub) if sub else np.nan
    met = dict(
        n=len(cases), incomplete=len(cases) - len(done),
        mean_delay=float(np.nanmean(flows)), p90_delay=float(np.nanpercentile(flows, 90)),
        P1_SLA=sla["P1"], P2_SLA=sla["P2"],
        max_util=max(utils.values()), util_std=float(np.std(list(utils.values()))),
        overhead_min=sum(st.overhead for st in states.values()),
    )
    return (met, log) if collect_log else (met, None)

# 9. Limits and validation plan

**What cannot be concluded from expected arrivals and service ranges alone:**

- **True arrival burstiness.** Poisson-with-uniform-spread is an assumption (A1); real incident streams are often overdispersed and correlated (one outage spawns many tickets). Queueing outcomes are highly sensitive to this, so absolute SLA numbers here are scenario estimates, not forecasts; only the *ranking* of policies, which was stable across seeds and scenarios, deserves weight.
- **Within-range distribution shape and cross-activity correlation.** A case with a tail electrical draw may also have a tail software draw (common-cause complexity); the independence assumption understates p95 flow times.
- **Multiplier validity under load.** The speed matrix is treated as constant; fatigue, interruptions, and learning are not identified by the data. Similarly, whether a 1.35-multiplier assignment affects *quality* (rework at Validation) is unobservable here and could reverse the pooling economics.
- **Behavioral response.** Estimate gaming, overrides, and break-timing flexibility are outside the model.
- **P3 attainment.** A single-day horizon bounds but does not measure the 24 h SLA; multi-day simulation with overnight carryover would be the next modeling step.
- **Small-sample noise.** With ~40 cases/day and ~4-7 P1 cases, one P1 breach moves P1_SLA by 15-25pp on a given day; the 8-seed averages tame this in simulation, but a live pilot needs weeks, not days, per arm.

**Pilot/backtest design (protecting P1, avoiding unsafe full rollout):**

1. **Backtest first (no risk):** replay 8-12 weeks of historical event logs through the Section-8 dispatcher (replace sampled arrivals/services with logged ones) to recalibrate A1/A2; re-rank policies on bootstrapped weekly resamples. Go/no-go: HYBRID's P1 advantage survives in >= 80% of bootstrap weeks.
2. **Shadow mode (2 weeks):** dispatcher computes and logs its recommendation (`chosen_rule`, `eligible_set`) while humans keep dispatching. Measures agreement rate and surfaces implementation bugs with zero operational risk.
3. **Guarded interleaved pilot (6-8 weeks):** alternate POOL and HYBRID by day (blocked randomization within weeks, matched by weekday); *P1 handling is identical in both arms* (head-of-queue priority + preemption always on), so the experiment varies only non-P1 routing and the reservation — P1 patients of the experiment are protected by design. Hard safety rails: any day with realized P1 SLA < 80% at 14:00 triggers the HYBRID anchor regardless of arm; the fallback trigger of Section 5 is always armed. SPEC is not piloted (dominated in simulation; unethical to expose real P1 cases to its U1/U5 failure mode).
4. **Evaluation:** primary endpoint worst-week P1 SLA per arm (matching objective O1); secondary: P2 SLA, P3 age p90, overhead min/day from the handover network, utilization spread, override rate. Analyze with day-level pairing; ~30 days/arm gives roughly 150-200 P1 cases per arm, enough to detect a ~8-10pp P1 SLA difference at conventional power given the simulated effect size.
5. **Rollout gate:** promote HYBRID only if it is non-inferior on P2 SLA (margin 5pp) and superior on worst-week P1 SLA; otherwise keep POOL and revisit the reservation design — and in parallel, pursue the evening-capacity fix, which the capacity analysis shows is the binding constraint no policy can route around.
