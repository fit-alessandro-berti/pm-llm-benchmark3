# Uncertainty-Aware Predictive Process Monitoring Study: Early Warning for Credit Default

## 1. Target definition

### Primary Target: Time-to-Default After Account Activation (Survival Framing)

**Unit of analysis:** Individual credit application case.

**Population:** Applications that reach Account Activated status within the observation window (1 Jan 2024 – 31 Dec 2025), observed to either default or survive 90 days post-activation, with outcome maturity as of 15 February 2026.

**Prediction time (as-of time):** The moment immediately after the first *k* events in the case (or after time ** elapses), but strictly before any of: Contract Signed, Account Activated, Rejection, Withdrawal, or Transfer event.

**Horizon:** 90 calendar days after Account Activated event timestamp.

**Estimand:** The probability that a case transitions to a Default state within 90 days of Account Activated, conditional on:
- The observed prefix of events up to prediction time;
- The unobserved fact that the case *will eventually* be activated;
- The operational credit-policy regime (pre- or post-1 July 2025) and system version at prediction time.

**Target variable:** Binary indicator, `default_90d_after_activation`, defined only for cases where:
- Account Activated event is recorded;
- At least 90 calendar days have passed since Account Activated as of data-extraction date (15 Feb 2026), *or* a Default Recorded event occurs within 90 days;
- The outcome label (from external feed or system flag) has matured and is present (with timestamp in `label_available_at`  extraction date).

**Prediction task:** Given a prefix of events from case start through (and including) the last event before activation decision is finalized, predict the binary outcome `default_90d_after_activation` with a calibrated probability estimate and uncertainty quantification.

---

### Alternative Target 1: Competing-Outcome Multi-State Analysis

Instead of conditioning on eventual activation, define a multi-state outcome for *all* 48,000 applications:
- **State A:** Rejected (terminal, no credit exposure  default is undefined);
- **State B:** Withdrawn (terminal, applicant choice  default undefined);
- **State C:** Transferred (right-censored or lost to follow-up under current data);
- **State D:** Still-open at extraction (right-censored);
- **State E:** Activated then default within 90 days (event of interest);
- **State F:** Activated then survived 90 days (censored at 90 days or extraction, whichever first).

**Estimand:** Cumulative incidence function (CIF) of transition to State E by time *t* (90 days post-activation), accounting for competing transitions to A, B, C, D.

This formulation acknowledges that "default rate among rejected cases" is a nonsensical question: rejection *prevents* activation, so default is never observed and the risk is undefined. However, the competing-risk framing can illuminate how selection through the approval process biases estimates of causal default risk.

**Why this alternative is important:** It separates the question "what fraction of *activated* accounts default?" (answered by the primary target) from "what is the overall default risk among all applicants given our current approval process?" (answered here, but interpretation requires caution about policy counterfactuals).

---

### Alternative Target 2: Time-to-Default from Application Start (Full-Cohort Framing with Censoring)

Extend the horizon to 365+ days from Application Started, tracking all transitions. Default is only observed after Activated. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still-open cases are censored at the last observed event.

**Why this matters:** If the business model eventually wants to optimize the entire approval pipeline—not just the post-activation phase—this target reveals which cases are never activated and why, and whether early termination (rejection) could have been predicted from the application prefix alone.

---

### **Justification for Primary Target Selection**

The primary target (time-to-default after activation) is chosen because:

1. **Operationally coherent:** The activation event is a decision boundary in the business process and the moment credit exposure begins. Default is only defined for activated accounts.

2. **Avoids pseudo-labels:** Does not artificially mark rejected or withdrawn cases as "non-default." Those cases have no observed default because they were never activated; the absence of default is by design, not evidence of low risk.

3. **Honest about selection:** Conditioning on activation explicitly acknowledges that the labeled cohort (21,600 cases) is a selected sample of the full application flow. This biases any raw estimate of default rate, but the bias is transparent and can be diagnosed through comparison with alternative targets and sensitivity analyses.

4. **Grounds prediction in observable outcomes:** The 21,600 labeled activated accounts have observed 90-day outcomes (or the outcome maturity window is closed without an outcome, indicating permanent label loss). This permits standard supervised learning with right-censored data.

5. **Aligns with delayed-label uncertainty:** The 7–35 day delay between the 90-day horizon and label availability is standard right censoring; the study design explicitly accounts for it.

6. **Enables process-aware prediction:** Prefixes extracted from the first *k* events or the first ** time units before activation remain interpretable: they capture the applicant and firm behavior during the *decision-making phase*, a critical window for early warning.

### As-Of Rules

- **Prediction time** for each case is defined as the **last event before any activation-decision event** (i.e., the last event in the sequence before the first of: Account Activated, Rejected, Transferred, Withdrawn).
- **Features are extracted** from events with timestamp  prediction time only.
- **Static attributes** (e.g., `case:channel`, `case:region`, `case:requested_amount` at Application Started) are frozen at Application Started.
- **Dynamic attributes** (e.g., `risk_score`, `org:resource`) are valid only if their timestamp  prediction time. If an attribute is updated post-prediction, it is treated as leakage and excluded.
- **Outcome observation** begins at Account Activated timestamp and extends 90 calendar days. Labels are valid only if recorded  data extraction date (15 Feb 2026).

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

### Censoring Types and Mechanisms

| **Censoring Type** | **Count/Rate** | **Mechanism & Implication** |
|---|---|---|
| **Right censoring: 90-day window closure** | 18,900 / 21,600 (87.5%) | Cases activated and survived to 90d post-activation or extraction date reached first. No default event observed; case is censored at min(90 days post-activation, extraction date). This is ordinary independent right censoring if default is missing-completely-at-random (MCAR) conditional on observed prefix. |
| **Right censoring: still-open at extraction** | 5,100 | Cases not yet activated by 15 Feb 2026. Prediction time is their last event, but 90-day outcome is unobserved because activation has not occurred. These are excluded from primary cohort but included in sensitivity analysis with censoring date = extraction date. |
| **Delayed-label censoring** | 3,600 | Activated cases whose outcome feed has not yet matured (label_available_at > 15 Feb 2026). They are excluded from primary labeled cohort. In sensitivity analysis, they can be treated as right-censored at min(90d post-activation, label_available_at – some safe margin) but this introduces assumptions about missing-label mechanism. |
| **Selection through activation** | 26,400 (all non-activated) | Rejection, withdrawal, transfer, and still-open cases are not activated. They are not censored in the classical sense; rather, they are **not part of the risk set** under the observed activation policy. This is selection bias, not censoring. These cases must be analyzed separately (competing-risk analysis) if the goal is to understand the full application pipeline. |
| **Transfer + unlinked follow-up** | ~1,160 | Transferred cases without linkage table entry are lost to follow-up. The outcome (default after activation on partner platform) is unobserved. Mechanism: partner-originated accounts have higher probability of missing outcome feed (scenario text). This is **informative censoring**: missingness is correlated with outcome risk. Sensitivity analysis required. |
| **Partner-originated missing feed** | Unknown fraction of partner-activated accounts | Permanently missing outcome feed. Informative censoring. Biases default-rate estimate downward if partners have lower observed default but higher true default (unobserved). |

### Primary Analysis Strategy: Weighted Kaplan–Meier / Cox PH with MCAR Right Censoring

**Assumptions:**
- Cases with observed default or observed survival to 90d represent the "true" outcome distribution.
- Missing labels (unobserved because feed delay) are MCAR given the prefix.
- Rejected, withdrawn, and not-yet-activated cases are excluded (not censored), defining the estimand as restricted to the activated cohort.

**Procedure:**
1. **Outcome coding:**
   - Event = 1 if `default_90d_after_activation` == True within 90d post-activation.
   - Event = 0 if 90 calendar days post-activation have elapsed without default.
   - Time-to-event = min(90 days, time to default event).

2. **Fit Cox proportional-hazards model** with prefix-derived features and process features. Predictions are cumulative hazard or survival probability at 90 days.

3. **Calibration & uncertainty:** Report Brier score, log-loss, and calibration plots. Use bootstrap to estimate confidence intervals for predicted probabilities.

4. **Subgroup analysis:** Stratify by policy regime (pre/post-1 July 2025), system version (pre/post-15 Oct 2025), and channel/region.

---

### Sensitivity Analysis 1: Competing-Risk Analysis (All 48,000 Applications)

**Goal:** Estimate the cumulative incidence function (CIF) of default among all applications, accounting for competing transitions (reject, withdraw, transfer, still-open).

**Model:** Proportional-subdistribution-hazards (Fine–Gray) for the "Default" state.

**Interpretation:** CIF(default at 90d post-activation | applied)  default rate among activated accounts. CIF is lower because many applicants never activate. However, CIF reveals how the approval process selects applicants and what that selection bias means for aggregate default risk.

---

### Sensitivity Analysis 2: Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting (IPCW)

**Goal:** Account for informative censoring (partner missing feed, delayed labels).

**Procedure:**
1. Fit logistic regression to predict P(label_available = 1 | prefix, case attributes).
2. Inverse-weight each case: w_i = 1 / P(label_available = 1 | X_i).
3. Refit Cox model on weighted sample.

**Assumption:** Outcome missingness is missing-at-random (MAR) given observed prefix. Partially testable by checking whether P(default | label available = 0) can be estimated from partially-observed outcomes (e.g., collection_status field).

---

### Sensitivity Analysis 3: Exclude Partner-Originated Cases

**Goal:** Test sensitivity to permanently missing outcome feeds (partners with elevated missing-feed probability).

**Procedure:** Refit model on subset excluding cases with `source_system` = "partner" or analogous flag.

**Interpretation:** If model performance (AUC, calibration) is stable, missing feed is not severely informative. If performance degrades, partners have different default behavior and the MCAR assumption is violated.

---

### Sensitivity Analysis 4: Include Still-Open as Right-Censored

**Goal:** Maximize labeled sample size by including cases not-yet-activated by extraction date.

**Procedure:** Treat 5,100 still-open cases as right-censored at extraction date, with T = time from Application Started to extraction.

**Assumption:** Cases still-open are missing-completely-at-random in their default outcome conditional on prefix and observed case progression. **Violation risk:** still-open cases may be systematically different (e.g., stuck in document loops, slower processors). If rejection/withdrawal is correlated with observed default characteristics, still-open cases could have unobserved default risk that is unpredictable from prefix.

---

### Testability of Assumptions

| **Assumption** | **Testability** |
|---|---|
| MCAR for right censoring (90-day window closure) | Partially testable: compare case characteristics (risk_score, region, amount) between cases with observed default, observed survival, and missing labels. If MCAR holds, no difference should exist. |
| MCAR for delayed labels (3,600 not-yet-mature) | Partially testable: as above. Also examine lag time between default event and label_available_at; if lag is independent of default status, more evidence for MCAR. |
| Informative censoring (partner missing feed) | Partially testable: compare default rate (among observed outcomes) for partner vs. direct cases. If identical, missing feed is likely MCAR or at least not negatively informative. If partner default is lower, feed missingness may indicate unobserved defaults (informative). |
| Proportional hazards | Fully testable: perform Schoenfeld residual test on Cox model; plot log-log survival by subgroups. |
| No unmeasured confounding in Cox model | Fundamentally untestable; addressed via causal graphs, domain knowledge, and sensitivity analyses. |
| Policy/system regime does not interact with prediction model | Partially testable: fit stratified Cox models; examine treatment-by-regime interactions. If significant, separate models per regime are needed. |

## 4. Prefix extraction

### Fixed-Event-Count Prefixes

**Definition:** For each case, extract all events up to and including the *k*-th event, where *k*  {3, 5, 10, 15, 20}.

**Rationale:** Early warning typically requires prediction from few events. *k=5* or *k=10* is typical; smaller *k* (3) is extreme but tests prediction from very minimal information.

**Algorithm:**
```
for each case:
    events_ordered = sort(events for this case, by timestamp)
    for k in [3, 5, 10, 15, 20]:
        prefix_k = events_ordered[0:k]
        prediction_time_k = timestamp of prefix_k[-1]
        include all events with timestamp <= prediction_time_k
        exclude events with timestamp > prediction_time_k
```

**Handling of short cases:**
- If a case has *n* < *k* events before activation, use all *n* events as the prefix.
- Record *k_actual* = min(*n*, *k*) and analyze by *k_actual* in subgroup analyses.
- Example: if 20% of cases have < 5 events before activation, report *k=5* results with caveat.

---

### Elapsed-Time Prefixes

**Definition:** Extract all events within time interval [Application Started, Application Started + **], where **  {7 days, 14 days, 30 days, 60 days}.

**Rationale:** Some prefixes may naturally separate by calendar duration. *=30 days* might capture the full KYC phase; *=7 days* might indicate urgency.

**Algorithm:**
```
for each case:
    t_start = timestamp of Application Started event
    for  in [7, 14, 30, 60]:
        t_cutoff = t_start +  days
        prefix_ = events with (t_start <= timestamp <= t_cutoff)
        prediction_time_ = max(timestamp) in prefix_
        exclude all events after t_cutoff
```

**Handling of long application cycles:**
- If a case takes 90+ days before activation decision, *=30* days captures only early events.
- Report the ** value and the median number of events captured per **.

---

### Preventing Future-Event Leakage

**Critical rule:** For each prefix (fixed-*k* or elapsed-**), **all feature extraction must use only events and attributes with timestamp  prediction_time**.

**Operationalization:**
```python
# Pseudo-code for leakage prevention
def extract_features_at_time(case, prediction_time):
    # Include only events <= prediction_time
    events_at_time = [e for e in case.events if e.timestamp <= prediction_time]
    
    # Exclude events after prediction_time
    # Specifically exclude: Account Activated, Rejected, Transferred, Withdrawn, 
    # Contract Signed, Default Recorded, Collection Contact
    
    # Extract features from events_at_time only
    # Static features (from Application Started) are valid
    # Dynamic features (risk_score, resource) must be the *last* recorded value
    #   before prediction_time
    
    # Do NOT use:
    # - final_limit (only populated after acceptance)
    # - decision_reason (only after final decision)
    # - collection_status (only after activation)
    # - any event timestamp > prediction_time
    
    return features
```

---

### Handling Repeated Document Loops

**Observation:** The "Request Documents"  "Credit Review" cycle may repeat 0–*n* times.

**Handling:**
- Count the number of times "Request Documents" appears before activation decision.
- Record this as a feature: `num_document_requests`.
- Record the total duration from Application Started to last document-request event.
- Do **not** include the content of requested documents (if available), as this is a form of side information that may leak.
- Rationale: repeated document requests indicate incomplete information or higher scrutiny; this is legitimate feature information available at prediction time.

---

### Handling Activity Renaming (System Migration, 15 Oct 2025)

**Observation:** The 15 Oct 2025 migration renamed several activities and created occasional duplicates.

**Handling:**
1. **Build a mapping table** of old  new activity names (obtain from technical documentation).
2. **Normalize** all activity names to a canonical set:
   ```python
   activity_mapping = {
       "KYC_Check_Old": "KYC_Check",
       "CreditReview_v1": "Credit_Review",
       "DocRequest_legacy": "Request_Documents",
       ...
   }
   df['concept:name'] = df['concept:name'].map(activity_mapping).fillna(df['concept:name'])
   ```
3. **Check for duplicate events:** If the same case has two identical events (same activity, same resource, same timestamp or very close timestamps within 1 minute) on or after 15 Oct 2025, remove one via deduplication logic:
   ```python
   df_dedup = df.sort_values(['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp']) \
              .drop_duplicates(['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp'], keep='first')
   ```
4. **Stratify validation by system version** (pre/post-15 Oct 2025) to check that prefix-derived features are stable across the migration.

#### C. **Resource and Queue Features** (Use with Caution)

| **Feature** | **Definition** | **Extraction Timing** | **Leakage Risk** |
|---|---|---|---|
| `num_distinct_resources` | Count of distinct `org:resource` values (e.g., case officers, teams) | At prediction time | Low: resource identity at time of events is observed. |
| `resource_at_last_event` | The `org:resource` value recorded for the last event in prefix | At prediction time | **MEDIUM**: Resource may be assigned based on risk assessment, causing confounding. |

| `case_officer_prior_default_rate` | Empirical default rate among prior cases handled by the same resource (computed from training set, not test set) | At prediction time; compute from training cases only | **MEDIUM**: Case assignment may be correlated with risk (low-risk to experienced officers, high-risk to junior officers). Separate analysis by resource recommended. |

**Recommendation:** Include `num_distinct_resources` and `resource_at_last_event` with interaction terms in sensitivity analyses. Do **not** use current `resource_experience` without reconstructing historical experience. Do **not** use `case_officer_prior_default_rate` in primary model (it introduces selection bias), but report it in subgroup analysis.

### Feature Matrix Summary

**Features Safe to Use at Prediction Time:**
- All control-flow features (event counts, activity sequences, rework counts)
- All timing features (elapsed time, inter-event duration)
- Static case attributes (channel, region, amount)
- First or early risk_score (from early event, not final)
- Number of distinct resources
- Resource identity at prediction time (with noted confounding)

**Features Requiring Careful Handling:**
- `risk_score` from later events (legitimate if available uniformly, but may encode post-prediction information)
- `resource_experience` (must use historical version, not current)
- Resource-based default rates (use in subgroup analysis only)

**Features to Exclude (Post-Prediction Leakage):**
- `final_limit` (only after acceptance decision)
- `decision_reason` (only after final decision)
- `collection_status` (only after activation)
- Any event timestamp > prediction time
- `label_available_at` (outcome-related metadata)
- `default_90d` outcome variable itself

## 6. Leakage audit

| **Feature or Data Element** | **Leakage/Bias Risk** | **Valid Availability Rule** | **Keep/Repair/Drop** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`final_limit`** | **HIGH leakage**: Only populated after credit decision (Accepted or, sometimes, Rejected with conditional offer). Directly encodes the credit review conclusion, which occurs *after* prediction time. | Do not include in features if prediction time is before final decision. If prediction time is defined as "after final decision," this becomes post-hoc and defeats early warning. | **DROP** | Using `final_limit` as a feature means the model learns to predict outcomes from the final decision itself, not from early process indicators. This is circular and has no predictive value for unseen cases. |
| **`decision_reason`** | **HIGH leakage**: Recorded at or after final decision (Accepted/Rejected/Transferred). Describes *why* the decision was made, which is causal outcome information. | Not available until decision event, which is post-prediction. | **DROP** | Same as `final_limit`: decision_reason encodes the outcome, not a predictor of it. |
| **`collection_status`** | **HIGH leakage**: Updated only after Account Activated. Contains direct signals of default (e.g., "Collection Call 1", "Arrears 30+"). If present, this is nearly-deterministic of default outcome. | Do not include if prediction time is before Account Activated (which it is, by design of primary target). If included post-activation, it becomes a feature of the performance-monitoring system, not early warning. | **DROP from primary model; INCLUDE in post-hoc audit** | Collection_status is useful to validate that `default_90d` label is accurate (cross-check: if default_90d=1, collection_status should show arrears). Do not use for prediction. |
| **`documents_total` (nightly snapshot)** | **MEDIUM leakage**: Counts documents uploaded; the snapshot is "nightly" and may include documents uploaded on the same calendar day *after* the nominal prediction time. | If prediction time is, e.g., 2:00 AM, and documents are uploaded later at 10:00 AM the same day, the nightly snapshot includes future documents. | **REPAIR via clarification**: Define prediction time as end-of-business-day or end of calendar day if using daily-snapshot features. Or exclude `documents_total` and replace with `num_documents_in_prefix_events` (count from document-upload events with timestamp  prediction time). | **RECOMMENDED: DROP `documents_total` snapshot, KEEP document-request event count**: Use only document events (Request_Documents, Document_Submitted) with explicit timestamps, not aggregated counts. |
| **`risk_score` at early events** | **LOW leakage**: Initial risk_score from first KYC check or application submission represents early assessment. | Risk_score from first few events is known early and can be used. | **KEEP** | Early risk_score captures lender's initial assessment and is available at prediction time if prediction time is within first few events. |
| **`risk_score` from later events** | **MEDIUM leakage**: Risk_score is updated during Credit Review based on credit bureau checks, bank statements, and other post-application information. Later scores reflect decision-relevant information gathered during the review phase, not truly exogenous applicant characteristics. | If risk_score is updated at event timestamp *t*, it is valid as a feature only if prediction time is  *t*. More fundamentally, later risk_score values are *correlated with default outcome* because they encode findings made during review. | **REPAIR or CONDITIONAL KEEP**: (1) Use only initial risk_score; (2) if using later scores, fit separate models before and after score update; (3) in sensitivity analysis, drop all risk_score features to test whether model relies on outcome-proxying information. | Risk_score progression can be informative about process thoroughness, but be transparent that later scores are not purely exogenous predictors. |
| **`org:resource` (resource identity)** | **MEDIUM confounding (not leakage)**: Resource assignment may be non-random. High-risk applicants may be routed to experienced officers; or, inexperienced officers may process simpler cases. Using resource as a feature induces confounding: high default rate among cases with junior officers could be reverse-causal (junior officers get harder cases) rather than junior officers being incompetent. | Resource identity at the time of events is observed and available at prediction time. | **KEEP with caution**: Include in model but report stratified results by resource to diagnose confounding. In causal analysis, treat resource as a mediator (case  resource assignment) or confounder (risk  resource assignment), not a direct predictor. | Resource is a legitimate feature for descriptive purposes (who processed the case?) but be explicit about confounding. |
| **`resource_experience` (experience at extraction time)** | **HIGH informative bias**: Experience is recorded at data-extraction time (15 Feb 2026), after all outcomes are known. A resource may have more experience handling defaults because they have processed defaulted cases in the past. Using current experience means the model learns an outcome-dependent attribute. | Resource experience must be reconstructed as *historical* experience at the time of the event (i.e., years of experience up to that event date), not current experience. This requires a separate historical experience table. | **REPAIR**: If using resource experience, obtain or construct a historical snapshot of experience at each event timestamp, not current experience. Or **DROP** current experience from model; use resource identity only. | Do not use current experience without historical reconstruction. Standard practice in observational studies. |
| **`label_available_at` (metadata)** | **MEDIUM leakage**: This timestamp indicates when the outcome label became available. If this timestamp is early and correlated with default (e.g., defaults are confirmed quickly, non-defaults take longer), including it or conditioning on it leaks outcome information. | This is metadata, not a feature. Do not include as a feature. Use only for cohort filtering (inclusion rule: label_available_at  15 Feb 2026). | **DROP from model features; KEEP for cohort definition** | label_available_at is useful for determining which cases have mature outcomes, but it is not a predictor of default. If included, the model may learn that fast label availability predicts default, which is confounding. |
| **Post-prediction events (Contract Signed, Account Activated, Default Recorded)** | **TOTAL LEAKAGE**: These occur after prediction time, by definition. Including any information from these events (e.g., timestamp, resource handling the event, contract terms) means the model uses future information. | Exclude all events with timestamp > prediction time. | **DROP entirely** | The entire point of the prediction design is to predict before these events occur. Including them destroys the predictive framing. |
| **`case:requested_amount`** | **LOW leakage**: Loan amount is requested by the applicant at Application Started and is static. Not updated based on review outcomes. | Valid at prediction time and any time after. | **KEEP** | Requested amount is a fundamental case attribute, determined exogenously, and available at application start. |
| **`case:channel`** | **LOW leakage**: Channel (web, broker, partner) is determined at application submission and does not change. | Valid at prediction time and after. | **KEEP** | Channel is an application attribute and is available at start. |
| **`case:region`** | **LOW leakage**: Applicant region is static. | Valid at prediction time and after. | **KEEP** | Geographic location is static case information. |
| **Interaction: risk_score × resource_experience** | **COMPOUND LEAKAGE**: Even if risk_score and resource_experience are individually managed, their interaction can create subtle leakage. For example, a junior officer with high-risk applicants may result in higher observed defaults, confounding resource and risk. | Only combine features that are both valid at prediction time and are not outcome-proxying. | **USE WITH CAUTION**: Include interactions only after stratified analysis confirms that confounding is not driving the interaction. | Check stratified results (e.g., by resource, by initial risk band) to ensure interactions are robust. |

## 7. Validation design

### Temporal Validation Scheme

**Why Random Split Fails:**
- Random case splits ignore the policy change (1 July 2025), system migration (15 Oct 2025), and label-maturity date (cutoff 15 Feb 2026).
- A random split mixes pre- and post-policy cases in train and test, conflating two different data-generating processes.
- The model trained on mixed data will neither reflect the pre-policy nor the post-policy distribution; it will be a blend that may not generalize to either.

**Temporal Split Strategy:**

| **Cohort** | **Application Start Date** | **Cases** | **Purpose** | **Label Maturity Cutoff** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Train (pre-policy)** | 1 Jan 2024 – 30 Jun 2025 | ~10,800 activated | Fit baseline model on pre-1 July 2025 regime | Cases activated by 30 Nov 2024 have outcome matured by 15 Feb 2026 (90d + buffer). Approximately 8,000–9,000 cases. |
| **Validation (post-policy, early)** | 1 Jul 2025 – 30 Sep 2025 | ~3,600 activated | Test generalization to new policy in first quarter post-change | Cases activated by 30 Nov 2025 have outcome matured. Approximately 2,000–2,500 cases. |
| **Test (post-policy, later)** | 1 Oct 2025 – 31 Dec 2025 | ~3,600 activated | Test generalization post-migration, with late policy | Cases activated by end of Feb 2026 have outcome matured or are near-maturity. Approximately 2,000–2,500 cases. |
| **Held-out (future, not matured)** | Jan 2026 – present | ~1,600 | Reserve for future validation once labels mature | Not used in study; outcomes not yet observed. |

**Note:** Exact counts depend on the activation-date distribution within the 48,000 applications. The 21,600 labeled cases are filtered to this time window.

---

### Addressing Policy Change (1 July 2025)

**Approach 1: Separate Models**
- Fit one Cox model on Train (pre-policy, 1 Jan 2024 – 30 Jun 2025).
- Fit a separate Cox model on Validation + Test (post-policy, 1 Jul 2025 – 31 Dec 2025).
- Compare model coefficients, feature importance, and calibration between regimes.
- Rationale: If credit policy changes, the relationship between features and default may change; using a separate model per regime respects this.

**Approach 2: Single Model with Policy Indicator**
- Fit one Cox model on all 21,600 cases with a binary feature `policy_regime` (pre/post 1 July 2025).
- Include interaction terms: `feature × policy_regime` for key features (risk_score, rework, channel).
- Test interaction significance with likelihood-ratio test.
- Rationale: If interactions are weak, a single model with regime adjustment is simpler and more data-efficient.

**Recommended:** Start with Approach 2 (test for interactions). If interactions are significant (p < 0.05 for any key feature), switch to Approach 1 (separate models per regime).

---

### Addressing System Migration (15 Oct 2025)

**Activity Normalization:**
- Normalize activity names (old  new) as described in Section 4.
- Deduplicate events created by migration.

**Migration-Aware Validation:**
- Exclude cases with prediction time very close to 15 Oct 2025 (e.g., 10 Oct – 20 Oct) from test set to avoid borderline cases affected by transition.
- Or: fit separate models for pre-migration (before 1 Oct 2025) and post-migration (after 31 Oct 2025), and test on hold-out cases from outside the transition period.
- Compare DFG (Directly-Follows Graph) discovery pre and post migration to ensure activity sequence semantics are consistent.

---

### Addressing Label Maturity

**Problem:** The 90-day outcome window closes on different dates for different cases. A case activated on 1 Jan 2026 has its 90-day window close on 1 Apr 2026 (after data-extraction date 15 Feb 2026), so the outcome is unobserved.

**Solution: Stratified Labeling by Outcome Maturity**

For each case in the cohort, compute:
- `outcome_maturity_date` = Account Activated timestamp + 90 days
- `label_available_date` = outcome_maturity_date + 7–35 days (external feed delay)

**Inclusion rule for labeled cohort:** `label_available_date`  15 Feb 2026 (extraction date).

This ensures only cases with truly observed (or sufficiently matured) outcomes are used.

**Sensitivity:** Re-fit model including cases with `label_available_date` up to 28 Feb 2026 (allowing ~2 weeks of delay margin) to test sensitivity to this threshold.

---

### Evaluation Metrics

#### Primary Metrics (Survival/Censoring Setting)

| **Metric** | **Formula / Interpretation** | **Why Appropriate** |
|---|---|---|
| **Concordance Index (C-index)** | Fraction of pairs (i, j) where predicted risk(i) > risk(j) if and only if observed survival time(i) < time(j). Range [0.5, 1.0], with 0.5 = random guessing, 1.0 = perfect discrimination. | Standard for Cox models; accounts for censoring. Interprets predictions as relative risk rather than absolute probability. |
| **Brier Score** | Mean squared error between predicted probability P(default at 90d) and observed outcome (0/1). Lower is better. Range [0, 1]. | Measures accuracy of *absolute* probability estimates, not just ranking. Important for business decisions that depend on probability calibration. |
| **Negative Log-Loss** | – (1/n)  [ y_i log(p_i) + (1 – y_i) log(1 – p_i) ]. Lower is better. | Penalizes confident mis-predictions. Useful for evaluating probabilistic forecast quality. |
| **Calibration curve** | Plot predicted probability vs. observed frequency in bins. Overlay 45° line (perfect calibration). | Reveals whether model probabilities match empirical frequencies. Necessary for decision-making. |
| **Integrated Discrimination Improvement (IDI)** | Average increase in predicted probability for cases with event vs. cases without event, comparing model to baseline. | Measures discrimination power per unit of probability shift. |

#### Secondary Metrics (Subgroup Performance)

| **Metric** | **Stratification** | **Why Needed** |
|---|---|---|
| **AUC and Brier by policy regime** | Pre- and post-1 July 2025 | Detect model degradation under new policy. |
| **AUC and Brier by system version** | Pre- and post-15 Oct 2025 migration | Detect impact of system change on feature reliability. |
| **C-index by channel** | Web, broker, partner, etc. | Check for channel-specific bias. |
| **C-index by region** | By geographic region (if available) | Detect geographic heterogeneity. |
| **Calibration slope by cohort month** | Rolled by month of application start | Monitor drift in prediction distribution. |

---

### Backtesting Scheme

**Objective:** Simulate prospective use of the model after each temporal split.

**Procedure:**

```
For fold in [1, 2, 3]:
    Train set: applications started in [T0, T1]  (e.g., Jan 2024 – Jun 2025)
    Validation set: applications started in [T1+1, T2]  (e.g., Jul 2025 – Sep 2025)
    Test set: applications started in [T2+1, T3]  (e.g., Oct 2025 – Dec 2025)
    
    1. Fit Cox model on Train set.
    2. Make predictions on Validation set (using prefixes only, no post-prediction data).
    3. Evaluate on Validation set: compute C-index, Brier, calibration.
    4. If Validation performance is acceptable (C-index > 0.65, no large calibration drift),
       retrain on Train + Validation combined and evaluate on Test.
    5. If Validation performance degrades, perform root-cause analysis:
       - Check policy change effect (fit separate models pre- and post-1 July 2025).
       - Check system migration effect (re-normalize activities post-15 Oct 2025).
       - Check for distribution shift (compare feature distributions in Train vs. Validation).
    6. Report final Test-set metrics for the post-retraining model.
```

**Early-Warning Use Case:**
- For each case in Validation/Test, compute predicted risk score.
- Flag cases with predicted risk > 90th percentile for enhanced review.
- Audit: among flagged cases, what is the observed default rate? Is it significantly higher than non-flagged?
- Compute positive predictive value (PPV) and false-positive rate (FPR) at different threshold.
- Rationale: Early-warning model should achieve high precision (low FPR) to be actionable; lenders do not want to flag 50% of applications.

---

### Calibration Assessment

**Calibration plot:**
```
For each decile of predicted probability:
    d = 1 to 10
    cases in decile d: {i : (d-1)/10 < pred_prob[i] <= d/10}
    observed_frequency = proportion of actual defaults in decile d
    predicted_prob = mean predicted probability in decile d
    plot (predicted_prob, observed_frequency)
    overlay 45-degree line (perfect calibration)
```

**Interpretation:**
- If observed frequency < predicted prob (curve below line), model is over-predicting (pessimistic).
- If observed frequency > predicted prob (curve above line), model is under-predicting (optimistic).
- Large deviations (>5 percentage points per decile) indicate miscalibration and suggest model retraining or probability adjustment (Platt scaling).

**Calibration in Each Regime:**
- Plot separately for pre-policy and post-policy cases to detect regime-specific miscalibration.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

### What the Study CAN Claim

1. **Predictive association:** The fitted Cox model estimates the *association* between observed prefix (events, attributes, process indicators) and the observed risk of default within 90 days post-activation, among the 21,600 activated cases with mature labels.

2. **Ranking (discrimination):** The model can rank cases by predicted risk. A case with high predicted risk is more likely to default than a case with low predicted risk (as measured by C-index  0.70–0.75 if model is reasonable).

3. **Calibrated probability estimates:** With careful validation, the model can provide calibrated probability estimates, e.g., "cases in the top decile have an estimated 25% default risk." These probabilities can inform decision-making (e.g., require additional documentation, lower loan amounts, or decline).

4. **Process-aware insights:** The model identifies which process indicators (e.g., rework count, time-to-decision, channel) are associated with default. These insights are descriptive of the observed process, not causal of default.

5. **Temporal generalization:** The temporal validation design tests whether the model generalizes across the policy change (1 July 2025) and system migration (15 Oct 2025). If it does, we have evidence that the associations are reasonably stable.

---

### What the Study CANNOT Claim

1. **Causal effects:** The model does not estimate whether reducing `rework_count` from 3 to 1 would reduce default risk. It estimates the *association* between rework and default in the observed data. Confounding, reverse causality, and selection effects undermine causal interpretation. (Example: rework may indicate extra scrutiny of already-risky applicants, so observed reworkdefault association is partly due to selection, not harm from rework itself.)

2. **Counterfactual default risk for rejected applicants:** Rejected applicants were never activated; their potential default risk under activation is unobserved and unidentified. The model trained on activated cases cannot be used to estimate "what would happen if we activated this rejected applicant?" (This requires strong assumptions, e.g., ignorability, which are untestable.)

3. **Universal risk:** The model is specific to the activated cohort under the observed approval policy. If the approval process changes (e.g., lower income thresholds, different KYC requirements), the composition of applicants changes, and the model's predictions on the new population may not generalize.

4. **Fairness properties:** The study does not test whether the model is fair across demographic groups. If `case:region` is correlated with default and is a protected attribute, the model may be discriminatory. Fairness assessment requires separate analysis (disparate-impact tests, fairness constraints during training, etc.).

5. **Optimal decision policy:** Even if the model predicts default risk well, the optimal lending policy (which applicants to approve, what terms to offer) is not determined by the model alone. Policy optimization requires a cost–benefit analysis (e.g., cost of default vs. revenue from successful loans), which is outside the scope of this study.

---

### Three Ways Deployment Could Change the Data-Generating Process

#### 1. **Reject more high-risk applicants (Model-guided tightening)**

**Scenario:** The early-warning model identifies high-risk prefixes. The lender uses the model to reject more applicants before they reach the contract stage.

**Impact on data-generating process:**
- Rejected cases shift to lower-risk profile (because high-risk applicants are now filtered earlier).
- Activated cases shift to higher average quality (adverse selection of remaining applicants is reduced).
- The distribution of prefixes changes: fewer high-risk prefixes reach activation.
- **Future training data will show lower default rate** because the population composition changed, not because default risk inherently decreased.
- **Model performance may degrade:** The model trained on the old (less-filtered) population is applied to the new (more-filtered) population, creating a mismatch.

**Mitigation:**
- Monitor the distribution of predicted-risk scores in new applications over time.
- Retrain the model quarterly on fresh data, allowing it to adapt to the new selection regime.
- Track actual default rates in the new activated cohort to calibrate expectations.

---

#### 2. **Change application workflows based on model insights (Process intervention)**

**Scenario:** The study identifies that cases with 3+ document requests have higher default. The lender implements a new rule: if 2 documents are requested, the case goes to senior review before requesting a 3rd.

**Impact on data-generating process:**
- Document-request sequences change: fewer cases reach 3+ requests.
- The rework feature (`num_document_requests`) distribution changes.
- Cases that would have entered severe rework are now either approved at 2 requests or rejected, changing the composition.
- **Default behavior conditional on prefix may change:** If senior review is more effective at identifying risks, default decreases (good outcome). If senior review is slower and applicants withdraw, approval rates change (behavioral shift).
- **Feature correlation with default may flip:** The model learned that 3+ requests  higher default, but this relationship was partly because 3+ requests indicated thoroughness or applicants were already borderline. With the new process, 3+ requests no longer occur, and the feature becomes missing or collapses to 2.

**Mitigation:**
- A/B test the process change on a holdout population before rolling out.
- Monitor feature distributions and outcome rates in the new cohort.
- Refit the model to the new steady-state process.

---

#### 3. **Loan officers adjust behavior in response to risk scores (Gaming)**

**Scenario:** Loan officers receive predicted-risk scores during credit review. Officers with high predicted-risk cases may:
- (a) Spend more effort on documents/verification, reducing approval probability (selection effect).
- (b) Become more conservative, rejecting cases they otherwise would have approved (conservative bias).
- (c) Become more lenient on cases they want to approve, overriding the model's warnings (overconfidence).

**Impact on data-generating process:**
- The relationship between prefix features and default outcome is no longer exogenous. Officers' *decisions* (to request docs, to approve/reject) are now influenced by the predicted risk score.
- Unobserved confounding is introduced: decisions that were previously driven by features {X1, X2, ...} are now driven by features {X1, X2, ...} *and* the model's prediction, which is a deterministic function of {X1, X2, ...}.
- Cases approved despite high predicted-risk scores may have observed lower default (because the officer successfully identified an exception), but this does not mean the model was wrong; it means the officer provided additional information not captured in the prefix.
- **Model recalibration is necessary** because the population of approved cases is now selected not just by policy but by officer judgment in response to the model.

**Mitigation:**
- Monitor decisions and outcomes stratified by predicted-risk score.
- Log officer overrides and reasons.
- Separately model the "approved despite high risk" subset to identify what additional information officers used.
- Implement periodic retraining with recent decisions and outcomes to adapt to the new regime.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
"""
Uncertainty-aware predictive process monitoring: Early warning for credit default.

This script demonstrates:
1. Data loading and normalization with PM4Py.
2. Cohort construction with explicit inclusion/exclusion rules.
3. Leakage audit and feature validation.
4. Temporal train-validation-test split.
5. Prefix extraction and feature engineering.
6. Output for downstream Cox PH or competing-risk modeling.

Non-goals:
- Do not fit a full survival model here (that's downstream).
- Do not tune hyperparameters (focus on data engineering).
- Do not plot or produce final performance metrics (setup only).

Assumptions:
- `applications_events.csv` is available with the schema described.
- PM4Py 2.4+ is installed.
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
import pm4py
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as xes_converter
from pm4py.statistics.traces.generic import case_statistics
from pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg import algorithm as dfg_discovery
from pm4py.algo.filtering.log import log_start_attributes
import warnings

warnings.filterwarnings('ignore')

# ============================================================================
# 1. CONFIGURATION & DATA LOADING
# ============================================================================

INPUT_FILE = "applications_events.csv"
EXTRACTION_DATE = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15")
POLICY_CHANGE_DATE = pd.Timestamp("2025-07-01")
SYSTEM_MIGRATION_DATE = pd.Timestamp("2025-10-15")

# Temporal cohort boundaries (in days from EXTRACTION_DATE)
OUTCOME_MATURITY_DAYS = 90  # 90 days post-activation + label feed delay
LABEL_MATURITY_BUFFER_DAYS = 35  # max external feed delay

print("[1] Loading event log...")
df = pd.read_csv(INPUT_FILE)

# Parse timestamps
df['time:timestamp'] = pd.to_datetime(df['time:timestamp'])
df['label_available_at'] = pd.to_datetime(df['label_available_at'])
df['data_extract_time'] = pd.to_datetime(df['data_extract_time'])

print(f"  Loaded {len(df)} events for {df['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases.")

# ============================================================================
# 2. DATA CLEANING & NORMALIZATION
# ============================================================================

print("\n[2] Normalizing activity names (system migration 2025-10-15)...")

# Mapping table for activity name normalization (illustrative)
activity_mapping = {
    # Pre-migration old names  canonical names
    "KYC_Check_Old": "KYC_Check",
    "CreditReview_v1": "Credit_Review",
    "DocRequest_legacy": "Request_Documents",
    "DocumentSubmitted_legacy": "Document_Submitted",
    # Post-migration (already canonical, identity mapping)
}

df['concept:name'] = df['concept:name'].map(activity_mapping).fillna(df['concept:name'])

# Deduplicate events: if same case, activity, timestamp (within 1 minute), keep first
df_dedup_cols = ['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp']
df['time:timestamp_rounded'] = df['time:timestamp'].dt.round('1min')
df = df.drop_duplicates(subset=df_dedup_cols, keep='first')
df = df.drop('time:timestamp_rounded', axis=1)

print(f"  After deduplication: {len(df)} events.")

# ============================================================================
# 3. CASE-LEVEL LANDMARK EXTRACTION
# ============================================================================

print("\n[3] Extracting case-level landmarks and censoring info...")

def extract_case_landmarks(group):
    """
    For each case, extract:
    - Application start time
    - Terminal event and its time (Activated, Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, or None)
    - Outcome status (labeled, censored, competing, unmatured)
    """
    case_id = group.name
    events = group.sort_values('time:timestamp').reset_index(drop=True)
    
    # Application started (first event)
    t_start = events.loc[0, 'time:timestamp']
    
    # Find terminal/decision events
    decision_activities = {'Account_Activated', 'Rejected', 'Withdrawn', 'Transferred'}
    decision_event = events[events['concept:name'].isin(decision_activities)]
    
    if len(decision_event) == 0:
        t_decision = None
        decision_type = 'Still_Open'
    else:
        decision_event = decision_event.iloc[0]  # First decision event
        t_decision = decision_event['time:timestamp']
        decision_type = decision_event['concept:name']
    
    # Outcome info (from the last event with a label)
    outcome_rows = group[group['default_90d'].notna()]
    if len(outcome_rows) > 0:
        outcome_row = outcome_rows.iloc[-1]
        default_90d = outcome_row['default_90d']
        label_available_at = outcome_row['label_available_at']
    else:
        default_90d = np.nan
        label_available_at = pd.NaT
    
    return pd.Series({
        'case_id': case_id,
        't_start': t_start,
        't_decision': t_decision,
        'decision_type': decision_type,
        'default_90d': default_90d,
        'label_available_at': label_available_at,
        'num_events': len(events),
    })

case_landmarks = df.groupby('case:concept:name').apply(extract_case_landmarks)
case_landmarks = case_landmarks.reset_index(drop=True)

print(f"  Extracted landmarks for {len(case_landmarks)} cases.")

# ============================================================================
# 4. COHORT CONSTRUCTION: INCLUSION/EXCLUSION
# ============================================================================

print("\n[4] Cohort construction: applying inclusion/exclusion rules...")

cohort_flow = pd.DataFrame({
    'cohort': [
        'Total applications started',
        'Reached Account_Activated',
        'Label matured (label_available_at <= extraction_date)',
        'Default within 90d',
        'No default within 90d (survived)',
        '',
        'Rejected (no activation)',
        'Withdrawn (no activation)',
        'Transferred (no activation)',
        'Still-open at extraction',
        'Label NOT yet matured (excluded)',
    ],
    'count': [
        len(case_landmarks),
        (case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Account_Activated').sum(),
        ((case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Account_Activated') & 
         (case_landmarks['label_available_at'] <= EXTRACTION_DATE)).sum(),
        ((case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Account_Activated') & 
         (case_landmarks['label_available_at'] <= EXTRACTION_DATE) & 
         (case_landmarks['default_90d'] == 1)).sum(),
        ((case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Account_Activated') & 
         (case_landmarks['label_available_at'] <= EXTRACTION_DATE) & 
         (case_landmarks['default_90d'] == 0)).sum(),
        np.nan,
        (case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Rejected').sum(),
        (case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Withdrawn').sum(),
        (case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Transferred').sum(),
        (case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Still_Open').sum(),
        ((case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Account_Activated') & 
         (case_landmarks['label_available_at'] > EXTRACTION_DATE)).sum(),
    ]
})

print("\nCohort flow:")
print(cohort_flow.to_string(index=False))

# Apply inclusion criteria
study_cohort = case_landmarks[
    (case_landmarks['decision_type'] == 'Account_Activated') &
    (case_landmarks['label_available_at'] <= EXTRACTION_DATE)
].copy()

print(f"\n Study cohort (labeled activated cases): {len(study_cohort)} cases")
print(f"  - Defaults: {(study_cohort['default_90d'] == 1).sum()}")
print(f"  - Non-defaults: {(study_cohort['default_90d'] == 0).sum()}")

# ============================================================================
# 5. OUTCOME MATURITY & TIME-TO-EVENT DEFINITION
# ============================================================================

print("\n[5] Defining time-to-event (90-day post-activation horizon)...")

# For each case, compute time-to-event and event indicator
study_cohort['t_activation'] = study_cohort['t_decision']  # activation is the decision
study_cohort['t_horizon'] = study_cohort['t_activation'] + timedelta(days=OUTCOME_MATURITY_DAYS)
study_cohort['t_outcome'] = study_cohort['label_available_at']  # when outcome was finalized

# Time-to-event (in days): 90 days post-activation or time to default, whichever is first
study_cohort['event_indicator'] = (study_cohort['default_90d'] == 1).astype(int)
study_cohort['time_to_event_days'] = (
    study_cohort['t_outcome'] - study_cohort['t_activation']
).dt.days

# Cap at 90 days (horizon)
study_cohort['time_to_event_days'] = study_cohort['time_to_event_days'].clip(upper=OUTCOME_MATURITY_DAYS)

print(f"  Time-to-event summary (days):")
print(f"    Mean: {study_cohort['time_to_event_days'].mean():.1f}")
print(f"    Median: {study_cohort['time_to_event_days'].median():.1f}")
print(f"    Min: {study_cohort['time_to_event_days'].min():.1f}")
print(f"    Max: {study_cohort['time_to_event_days'].max():.1f}")

# ============================================================================
# 6. TEMPORAL COHORT ASSIGNMENT (for validation split)
# ============================================================================

print("\n[6] Assigning temporal cohorts for train/validation/test...")

study_cohort['cohort_period'] = 'unknown'
study_cohort.loc[
    study_cohort['t_start'] < POLICY_CHANGE_DATE, 'cohort_period'
] = 'train_pre_policy'
study_cohort.loc[
    (study_cohort['t_start'] >= POLICY_CHANGE_DATE) &

study_cohort.loc[
    study_cohort['t_start'] >= pd.Timestamp('2025-10-01'), 'cohort_period'
] = 'test_post_migration'

for cohort in ['train_pre_policy', 'val_post_policy', 'test_post_migration']:
    count = (study_cohort['cohort_period'] == cohort).sum()
    default_rate = (

    )

df_pm4py = df[['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'time:timestamp', 'org:resource']].copy()

    df_pm4py,
    case_id='case:concept:name',
    activity_key='concept:name',
    timestamp_key='time:timestamp',

print(f"  Formatted event log: {len(df_pm4py)} events, {df_pm4py['case:concept:name'].nunique()} cases")

# Extract fixed-k prefixes (k=5, k=10)
prefix_features_list = []

for k in [5, 10]:
    print(f"\n  Extracting {k}-event prefixes...")
    
    for case_id in study_cohort['case_id'].unique():
        case_events = df_pm4py[df_pm4py['case:concept:name'] == case_id].sort_values('time:timestamp')
        
        if len(case_events) < k:
            # Case has fewer than k events
            prefix_events = case_events
            k_actual = len(case_events)
        else:
            prefix_events = case_events.iloc[:k]
            k_actual = k
        
        # Prediction time is the timestamp of the last event in the prefix
        t_prediction = prefix_events.iloc[-1]['time:timestamp']
        
        # Extract process features from prefix
        num_events = len(prefix_events)
        num_activities = prefix_events['concept:name'].nunique()
        activity_sequence = tuple(prefix_events['concept:name'].values)
        
        # Count rework (number of returns to Credit_Review)
        num_credit_review = (prefix_events['concept:name'] == 'Credit_Review').sum()
        num_document_requests = (prefix_events['concept:name'] == 'Request_Documents').sum()
        
        # Timing features
        t_first = prefix_events.iloc[0]['time:timestamp']
        elapsed_hours = (t_prediction - t_first).total_seconds() / 3600
        
        # Temporal features
        hour_of_day = t_prediction.hour
        day_of_week = t_prediction.dayofweek
        
        # Output row
        prefix_features_list.append({
            'case_id': case_id,
            f'prefix_k_{k}': True,
            f'k_actual_{k}': k_actual,
            f't_prediction_{k}': t_prediction,

            f'num_activities_{k}': num_activities,
            f'activity_sequence_{k}': str(activity_sequence),
            f'num_credit_review_{k}': num_credit_review,
            f'num_document_requests_{k}': num_document_requests,
            f'elapsed_hours_{k}': elapsed_hours,
            f'hour_of_day_{k}': hour_of_day,
            f'day_of_week_{k}': day_of_week,
```

## Summary

This study design prioritizes **statistical rigor, transparency about selection, and honest target semantics** over benchmark optimization:

1. **Primary Target:** Time-to-default after Account Activated, conditioning on eventual activation, with explicit right censoring and outcome maturity rules.

2. **Cohort:** 21,600 labeled activated cases; 26,400 non-activated cases are excluded (not artificially labeled as non-default) and analyzed separately in sensitivity analyses.

3. **Censoring Strategy:** Right censoring from 90-day horizon and label-feed delay is handled with Kaplan–Meier and Cox PH; informative censoring (partner missing feed) is addressed via IPCW sensitivity analysis.

4. **Leakage Prevention:** Comprehensive audit identifies and excludes post-decision features (`final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`), prevents future-event inclusion via explicit prediction-time cutoffs, and handles system migration and policy changes.

5. **Temporal Validation:** Train-validation-test split respects policy change and system migration; random splitting is explicitly rejected. Stratified calibration and competing-risk analysis ensure robustness.

6. **Honest Limitations:** Model estimates *association*, not causation. Rejected and withdrawn cases are not claimed to be "non-default"; their default risk is undefined. Deployment may change the process; ongoing monitoring is required.

7. **Concrete Implementation:** The Python skeleton demonstrates PM4Py integration, cohort construction, leakage auditing, and output generation for downstream survival modeling.

The design acknowledges uncertainty and trade-offs: it cannot answer every question (e.g., counterfactual default under policy changes), but it answers the question it does answer—**early-warning prediction within the observed activated cohort**—with transparency and rigor.
