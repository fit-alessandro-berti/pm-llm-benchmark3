# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

Internal audit reviewed the procurement-to-payment event log. Overall, the evidence does not establish fraud or collusion. The pattern is most consistent with a mixture of emergency handling, informal specialization, and data-quality/resource-mapping limitations, with a small residual cluster requiring targeted review. The most concerning subgroup is the VendorMaster_Lena/AP_Kim and vendor family V-77x bank-detail/payment cluster: 44 co-cases, 72% vendor concentration, 68% high amount, 83% `manual_upload`, 11 tight loops, and 9 same-person bank-change/payment-approval exceptions. These are control exceptions, not proof of misconduct. Benign explanations include V-77x bank-platform mass maintenance, missing maintenance-ticket linkage, manual migration, and role design; however, the combination of bank changes, payment approvals, high amounts, and manual source system warrants priority validation. Emergency PO-approval patterns involving Buyer_Carla/Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan/Manager_Hugo are also notable, but they align with emergency flags, weekend repairs/medical supplies, and shared-account/mapping issues. Same-person retrospective justifications and Gita shared-account entries are materially affected by batch upload and mapping problems. Recommend: (1) validate V-77x bank-change authorizations, maintenance tickets, beneficiary confirmations, payment approvals, access logs, and `manual_upload` originals; (2) resolve shared-account and Bot_PO/legacy_erp mappings and recompute four-eye exceptions using named users; (3) sample emergency cases for stock-out/work-order evidence, approval delegation, and timely independent retrospective justification. Until these are completed, conclusions should be framed as control exceptions and data-quality limitations, not as accusations.
