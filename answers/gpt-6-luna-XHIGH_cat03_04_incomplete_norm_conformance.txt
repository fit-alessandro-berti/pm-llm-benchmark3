## 1. Conformance semantics

I treat a normative status as a rule about a scoped relation, not as a blanket expectation that every observed graph look like a complete reference graph.

- **Forbidden behavior** is an observed in-scope relation explicitly prohibited by the norm. It is a conformance violation if the event and object identities are trustworthy. If a bad join created the relation, the observed model violates the rule while the underlying business behavior may not have occurred.
- **Missing mandatory behavior** is the absence of an explicitly required relation within its stated scope. First check that the scope applies, the observation window is complete, and the model’s relation means the same thing as the norm. A low count is evidence of a gap, but does not by itself distinguish process failure from missing logging or linkage.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior** is an observed relation that is neither required nor prohibited. Under the open-world norm it is not a violation merely because it is absent from the normative graph.
- **Typical-pattern absence** is a departure from a desirable/common pattern, not a breach. The norm gives no frequency threshold, so counts can motivate investigation but cannot create a threshold after the fact.
- **Likely data-quality error** is a plausible explanation for an apparent relation or omission based on evidence such as a reused identifier. This is a hypothesis to verify, not an automatic excusal.
- **Abstraction mismatch** occurs when the model’s edge semantics are narrower or broader than the business rule, such as direct succession standing in for eventual succession, or an event attribute standing in for an object relationship.
- **Insufficient evidence** applies when counts or graph summaries cannot establish scope, ordering, identity, event completeness, or cause. It calls for trace-level evidence, not a confident compliance verdict.

The norm is closed-world only for explicit prohibitions and mandatory relations in their stated scope. Thus a verified forbidden edge is meaningful, and an in-scope missing mandatory edge is actionable. For typical and unspecified relations, silence in the norm is not a prohibition or obligation. An observed unspecified relation may be legitimate; an unlisted relation remains unregulated unless another rule governs it.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG summarizes directly-follows activity relations for each object type. It can show that two Orders have no observed direct Create Order -> Validate Order edge and that two externally shipped Packages have no observed Pack Package -> Dispatch Package edge. N4 is stated as a mandatory relation for externally shipped Packages, and the supplied denominator places both packages in scope. This is an actionable signal, though the full trace and data completeness still matter.

N1’s wording is different: every new standard Order should **eventually** enter validation, while the summary uses direct succession as an approximation. An intervening review can satisfy the business rule while breaking the direct-follows edge. Therefore 98/100 direct edges are not the same as only 98/100 eventual validations. The three Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review cases make the possibility concrete, but do not establish that the two missing edges are among those three. A trace-level eventuality check is needed.

N2 is typical, so 83 direct Validate -> Close edges are descriptive rather than a threshold. The 72 Validate -> Issue Invoice edges are unregulated by this OC–DFG norm and may coexist with closings or intermediate events. N5 similarly makes Invoice -> Payment typical; 92/110 is not a failure rate under this specification. Four write-offs and 14 invoices without an observed successor fit plausible exceptions or right-censoring. N6 explicitly leaves Reopen -> Close unregulated, so the one observation is not a breach. The two Cancel -> Dispatch edges are different: N3 explicitly forbids that same-Order relation, so verify identities and timing promptly.

### OTG

An OTG summarizes object-to-object relationships and special relations such as cobirth or codeath. It shows required Order–Item and Package–Delivery interactions at weights 1450 and 200. It also shows a typical Invoice–Payment relation at 92. Weights are counts, not necessarily unique objects or proof that every event-level association is correct.

The Order–Customer and Order–RiskCase edges are expressly unspecified, so their presence at weights 100 and 3 is not a deviation. The one Order–Payment cobirth edge is explicitly forbidden. Confirm that the OTG’s “born in the same event” semantics match the source event and object creation records before treating it as a business breach. Seven Package–Delivery codeath edges are unregulated; a shared confirmation event is not a violation simply because no expected codeath edge is listed. In particular, do not infer that codeath is improper from N4 or T2: those govern different relations.

### ET–OT

ET–OT relates event types to object types. It can expose a missing link on a particular class of event even when an object-level relationship exists. The OTG’s 200 Package–Delivery relations and ET–OT’s 198 Dispatch Package–Delivery links can both be true: two dispatch events may lack the mandatory Delivery association while the package and delivery objects are related elsewhere. A carrier reference in an attribute is useful repair evidence but is not equivalent to a typed object relationship unless the data model explicitly defines it as such.

The same scope check applies to Record Payment–Invoice. E8 is mandatory except for events explicitly typed as unapplied cash; no such event is typed in this snapshot. Thus 92 links for 93 events is a mandatory-edge gap as recorded. Finance’s statement makes a legitimate exception plausible, but the event must be classified or linked correctly; an undocumented possibility cannot silently waive the rule. Add Item–Order is short by two links under E2, even though Add Item–Item is complete at 1450. This suggests an event association issue that may orphan item additions.

The other listed mandatory ET–OT links are present in aggregate: Create Order–Order 100, Add Item–Item 1450, Pack Package–Package 200, Dispatch Package–Package 200, and Record Payment–Payment 93. Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase is unspecified under E9; the parallel Customer links are not governed by a listed rule. Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice is explicitly forbidden under E10, with a strong suspected bad-join explanation. Keep the normative finding visible while investigating the likely data defect.

## 4. Mandatory, forbidden, typical, and unregulated behavior

- **Mandatory:** N1 eventual validation for every in-scope new standard Order (the two direct-follows gaps remain unresolved until traces establish eventual validation); N4 Pack -> Dispatch for externally shipped Packages (198/200 observed); T1 Order–Item and T2 Package–Delivery (observed at OTG level); E1, E3, E4, E5, E7 observed; E2 Add Item–Order (2 missing); E6 Dispatch Package–Delivery (2 missing); E8 Record Payment–Invoice (1 missing unless the event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash). Scope exclusions, incomplete traces, or validated identity repairs can change an apparent gap into insufficient evidence or an abstraction/data issue.
- **Forbidden:** N3 Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package (2 observed); T6 Order–Payment cobirth (1 observed); E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice (1 observed). The norm-level prohibition is robust; whether it represents real behavior depends on identities, event semantics, and join quality.
- **Typical:** N2 Validate -> Close (83 observed); N5 Issue Invoice -> Record Payment (92/110, with 4 write-offs and 14 without observed successors); T3 Invoice–Payment interaction (92). Their absence or frequency does not establish nonconformance without an additional threshold or stricter rule.
- **Unregulated:** Create Order -> Manual Compliance Review (3); Validate -> Issue Invoice (72); Reopen -> Close (1); Order–Customer (100); Order–RiskCase (3); Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase (3) and –Customer (3); Package–Delivery codeath (7); Write Off (4) and invoice no-successor observations except as contextual evidence under N5. These can still merit data-quality or operational review, but this norm does not make them violations.

# Separate semantic review ledger: retain references to the raw outputs, and
# apply scope, completeness, identity, and exception checks outside PM4Py.
semantic_review = []
for family, rules in policy_metadata.items():
    for rule_id, metadata in rules.items():
        status = metadata["status"]
        if status == "forbidden":
            decision_rule = "investigate observed prohibited relation and validate identity"
        elif status == "mandatory":
            decision_rule = "check missing relation only inside scope and complete traces"
        elif status == "typical":
            decision_rule = "describe frequency; do not fail conformance on absence alone"
        else:
            decision_rule = "no conformance claim from this norm"
        semantic_review.append({
            "family": family,
            "rule_id": rule_id,
            "metadata": metadata,
            "decision_rule": decision_rule,
            "discovered_model": discovered[family],
            "raw_diagnostic": raw_diagnostics[family],
        })

# evidence, scope decisions, and data-quality findings in the application layer.

The discovery and conformance functions provide model diagnostics, not a complete legal or business interpretation. In particular, the semantic ledger must retain scope and exception metadata and distinguish direct succession from eventual behavior.

## 6. Norm-completeness assessment

1. **N1 says “eventually” but its summary is directly-follows.** Operational risk: valid intermediate events look like failures, or real missing validations are hidden. Clarify whether validation is eventual within a time/window and how to test it. Keep the business obligation **mandatory**; mark direct succession as a diagnostic approximation, not the rule.
2. **N4 does not say whether intermediate package events can intervene.** Risk: false failures or missed stalled shipments. Define the trace semantics and completion point for externally shipped packages. Make eventual Pack-before-Dispatch **mandatory** if that is intended.
3. **N2/N5 have no frequency, time horizon, or censoring rule.** Risk: teams may invent arbitrary failure thresholds. Define aging windows and reporting measures separately; retain the relations as **typical** unless policy owners intend a true requirement.
4. **E8’s unapplied-cash exception has no operational labeling contract.** Risk: legitimate payments appear nonconforming, or invoice-less payments are excused without evidence. Specify a required event type/attribute and later allocation handling; the Invoice link stays **mandatory** except for explicitly typed unapplied cash.
5. **T6 does not define the granularity of “same business event” or “born.”** Risk: batch creation or extraction aggregation creates false cobirth findings. Define event identity and object creation semantics; retain **forbidden** for a true shared business event.
6. **The norm does not define identity confidence or join provenance.** Risk: false forbidden edges (as suspected for E10) can drive incorrect escalation. Require stable keys and provenance for conformance claims; keep sensor-to-financial-object linking **forbidden**.
7. **E2/E6/E8 do not say how to handle alternative identifiers or late relationship resolution.** Risk: carrier references are mistaken for object links, and data repair is inconsistent. Define canonical relationship resolution and an auditable unresolved state; retain the typed relationships as **mandatory** in scope.
8. **N3’s temporal interpretation and downstream dispatch handling are unstated.** Risk: clock skew or delayed event arrival can look like dispatch-after-cancel. Define event-time ordering, same-Order identity, and an operational stop/reversal process; retain the forbidden sequence as **forbidden**.
9. **No rule defines log completeness or right-censoring.** Risk: truncated traces are called missing behavior, especially for validation, dispatch, and payment. Specify the observation window and incomplete-trace marker; retain obligations as **mandatory** but classify incomplete cases as unassessable until closed.
10. **The norm does not define whether aggregate counts are unique events, objects, or traces.** Risk: percentages and thresholds are misread. Specify denominators and deduplication; define monitoring metrics without converting a **typical** pattern into a mandatory minimum by implication.

## 7. Investigation and remediation plan

- **Immediate containment:** Prioritize N3 and T6. For the two Cancel -> Dispatch Orders, identify any shipment still controllable and prevent further dispatch where the event sequence is confirmed. For the Order–Payment cobirth, verify the source event and suspend automated conclusions based on the suspect edge until the shared-event semantics are checked. Separately quarantine the E10 Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice association from finance-facing analytics because the reused-ID join is specifically suspected. These steps target potentially harmful or contaminating records; they do not assume all three are genuine business actions.
- **Data repair:** Rebuild E10 from stable identifiers and retain join lineage. Resolve the two missing Dispatch–Delivery edges using carrier references against authoritative Delivery records. Reconstruct the two Add Item–Order links and the one Payment–Invoice association, or mark the latter as unapplied cash using the required event classification. Do not synthesize a relationship solely to make a graph conform.
- **Process investigation:** Review full traces and source evidence for the two N3 Orders, the T6 cobirth, two N4 package sequences, two E6 dispatch links, two E2 item events, and one E8 payment. Check timestamps, identity, event completeness, and applicable scope. For N1, determine whether the two Orders eventually validate after omitted/intervening events. Review invoice aging and write-offs under finance policy; the 92/110 typical count alone is not an incident.
- **Norm revision:** Resolve the ten ambiguities above, especially direct-vs-eventual semantics, explicit unapplied-cash typing, “same event” definition, and trace completeness. Keep Customer/RiskCase and codeath behavior unregulated here unless policy owners intentionally add a rule. Any change should define scope before producing a conformance threshold.
- **Monitoring:** Report forbidden-edge candidates individually with identity confidence; track in-scope mandatory gaps with denominators and incomplete-trace counts; trend typical relations without pass/fail thresholds. Alert on confirmed N3 and E10 cases, and monitor E2/E6/E8 gap resolution. Keep a separate data-quality queue so likely join failures do not get mistaken for confirmed process behavior.

## 8. Uncertainty statement

Robust conclusions are that N3, T6, and E10 are explicitly forbidden if their modeled identities and event semantics are valid; E2, E6, and E8 show scoped mandatory-link gaps as recorded; and N4 has two apparent mandatory sequence gaps among 200 in-scope packages. N2, N5, T3, N6, Customer/RiskCase links, and codeath do not justify violations under the stated statuses. N1 depends on the distinction between eventual validation and direct succession; E8 depends on whether the payment is truly unapplied and correctly typed. E10 is strongly suspected to be a join error. Graph summaries alone cannot establish event ordering reliability, object identity, trace completeness, whether an exception applies, or the underlying cause of any missing or prohibited edge.
