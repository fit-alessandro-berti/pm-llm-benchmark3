# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

## Assumptions
1. **Demand Distribution**: Expected arrivals are treated as Poisson processes with the given block rates. Priority percentages are assumed to be independent of case type (e.g., 10% P1 of all arrivals in 08:00-10:00 block).
2. **Service-Time Modeling**: Service times are modeled as uniform distributions within the provided ranges. Complex-tail cases occur with the given probabilities, and their service times are drawn from the "complex-tail" range.
3. **Parallelism Decision**: For ME/ES cases, parallel execution is chosen unless the resource is cross-skilled (e.g., Deepa for ME) and the coordination penalty (8 minutes) exceeds the sequential time savings. Sequential execution is assumed if the total sequential time is less than the parallel time plus coordination penalty.
4. **Preemption Rules**: P1 cases preempt lower-priority activities only if the restart penalty (10% of remaining time) is less than the time saved by preemption. Preemption is limited to once per case.
5. **Resource Availability**: Break times are treated as unavailability periods. Overtime is limited to 30 minutes per resource per day.

## Objectives
1. **Tail/SLA Risk Objective**: Minimize the probability of missing P1/P2/P3 SLAs, measured as the fraction of cases exceeding their target completion times. This objective prioritizes resilience against worst-case scenarios.
2. **Fairness/Robustness Objective**: Maximize the minimum resource utilization across all resources while ensuring no single resource exceeds 90% utilization in the base scenario. This objective balances workload distribution and operational stability.

## Decision Variables
- **Resource Assignment**: Which resource handles each activity (Intake, Diagnostic, Validation).
- **Parallel vs. Sequential Execution**: For ME/ES cases, whether to execute branches in parallel or sequentially.
- **Preemption Policy**: When and how P1 cases preempt lower-priority activities.
- **Handover Minimization**: Whether to assign cases to resources that minimize handover/setup times.

# 2. Capacity and offered-load analysis

## Base Scenario
### Offered Load by Skill and Time Block
We approximate the offered load by multiplying expected arrivals by the probability of each case type requiring a skill and the average service time for that skill. For example, in the 08:00-10:00 block:
- Mechanical: 7 arrivals * 30% * (23 minutes) = 4.81 hours
- Electrical: 7 arrivals * 25% * (28.5 minutes) = 4.0 hours
- Software: 7 arrivals * 15% * (32.5 minutes) = 2.84 hours
- Validation: All cases require Validation, so 7 arrivals * (14 minutes) = 0.98 hours

### Resource-Specific Capacity
Using the skill multipliers and shifts, we calculate the available capacity per resource per block. For example, Ada's capacity for Mechanical in 08:00-10:00 is:
- Available time: 8 hours (shift) - 0.5 hours (break) = 7.5 hours
- Capacity: 7.5 hours / 1.00 (Mechanical multiplier) = 7.5 hours

### Bottlenecks
- **Base Scenario**: The Mechanical skill is the most constrained during 08:00-10:00 and 10:00-12:00 blocks due to Farah's high multiplier (1.40) and limited shift (08:00-12:00). Validation is also a potential bottleneck due to high demand and limited cross-skilled resources.
- **Uncertainty Scenarios**:
  - **U1 (Morning Surge)**: Increased arrivals in 08:00-12:00 will exacerbate Mechanical and Validation bottlenecks.
  - **U2 (Electrical/Software Incident)**: Doubled demand for Electrical and ES cases will strain Chen and Emil's capacities.
  - **U3 (Absence)**: Chen's absence will reduce capacity for Electrical and Software skills.
  - **U4 (Long-tail Day)**: Increased complex-tail probabilities will extend service times, worsening bottlenecks.
  - **U5 (Combined Stress)**: The IT outage will reduce Intake capacity, and Chen's absence will further strain Electrical/Software skills.

# 3. Candidate Policies

### Policy 1: Strict Specialization with Fixed Queues
- **Eligibility**: Each resource is assigned to a fixed set of skills (e.g., Ada handles Intake, Mechanical, and Validation; Ben handles Intake and Mechanical).
- **Priority Score**: P1 cases are prioritized over P2 and P3 cases. Within priority levels, shortest expected remaining processing time (SERPT) is used.
- **Reservation**: No reservations; cases are assigned to the next available resource in their fixed queue.
- **Preemption**: P1 cases preempt lower-priority activities if the restart penalty is less than the time saved.
- **Parallelization**: ME/ES cases are executed in parallel unless the resource is cross-skilled.
- **Handover Minimization**: Minimize handover by assigning cases to the same resource for consecutive activities where possible.

### Policy 2: Full Pooling with Transparent Dispatch
- **Eligibility**: Any resource qualified for the activity can handle it.
- **Priority Score**: P1 cases are prioritized. Within priority levels, SERPT is used, but resources are selected to minimize handover/setup times.
- **Reservation**: No reservations; cases are dynamically assigned to the best available resource.
- **Preemption**: P1 cases preempt lower-priority activities if the restart penalty is less than the time saved.
- **Parallelization**: ME/ES cases are executed in parallel unless the resource is cross-skilled and sequential execution is faster.
- **Handover Minimization**: Resources are selected to minimize handover/setup times between activities.

### Policy 3: Hybrid Policy with Reserved P1 Capacity and Controlled Cross-Skill Overflow
- **Eligibility**: P1 cases are assigned to a reserved pool of resources (e.g., Ada, Ben, Chen) for their critical activities. Lower-priority cases can overflow to other resources.
- **Priority Score**: P1 cases are prioritized. Within priority levels, SERPT is used, but resources are selected to minimize handover/setup times.
- **Reservation**: 50% of Mechanical and Electrical resources are reserved for P1 cases during peak hours (08:00-16:00).
- **Preemption**: P1 cases preempt lower-priority activities if the restart penalty is less than the time saved.
- **Parallelization**: ME/ES cases are executed in parallel unless the resource is cross-skilled and sequential execution is faster.
- **Handover Minimization**: Resources are selected to minimize handover/setup times between activities, with priority given to cross-skilled resources for overflow cases.

# 5. Robust Recommendation

**Primary Policy**: Hybrid Policy with Reserved P1 Capacity and Controlled Cross-Skill Overflow
- **Why**: This policy balances the need for P1 SLA compliance (high resilience) with operational fairness and robustness. It reserves capacity for P1 cases during peak hours, reducing the risk of P1 SLA violations, while allowing controlled overflow to other resources to manage workload distribution. The hybrid approach also minimizes handover/setup burdens compared to full pooling, making it more practical for operators to manage.

**Fallback Mode**: Full Pooling with Transparent Dispatch
- **Trigger**: If the hybrid policy's reserved capacity exceeds 90% utilization for more than 2 consecutive hours, switch to full pooling to redistribute the workload dynamically.
- **Cases Where Another Policy is Preferable**: In scenarios with very low demand (e.g., 18:00-20:00 block), strict specialization may be more efficient due to reduced handover/setup overhead.

---

# 6. Secondary Effects and Implementation Risks

- **Gaming of Estimates**: Operators may manipulate priority scores or preemption rules to favor certain cases, leading to suboptimal performance. Mitigation: Implement audit trails and random audits of assignment decisions.
- **Starvation of P3 Work**: P1 and P2 cases may dominate resources, delaying P3 cases. Mitigation: Reserve a fixed percentage of capacity for P3 cases during off-peak hours.
- **Skill Erosion**: Over-reliance on cross-skilled resources may reduce their proficiency in specific skills. Mitigation: Rotate assignments and provide cross-training opportunities.
- **Excessive Preemption**: Frequent preemptions may disrupt workflows and increase restart penalties. Mitigation: Limit preemptions to critical P1 cases and monitor restart rates.
- **Coordination Overhead**: Parallel ME/ES cases may introduce coordination delays. Mitigation: Use sequential execution when coordination penalties exceed time savings.
- **New Bottlenecks at Validation**: High demand for Validation may create bottlenecks. Mitigation: Assign Validation to cross-skilled resources during peak hours.
- **Fairness**: Uneven workload distribution may lead to resource fatigue. Mitigation: Monitor utilization and redistribute workload dynamically.
- **Human Interpretability**: Complex dispatch rules may be difficult for operators to understand. Mitigation: Provide clear guidelines and training on policy rules.
