## 7. Provisional conclusion

The primary explanation for the measured conformance collapse is that the event log became less comparable to the normative model because of three intertwined data-provenance changes, followed by model non-maintenance. The May 31 UI release changed `Classify` to `Risk Screen`; the June 3 gateway change inserted `Queue Routed`; and the June 15 ETL change used warehouse-ingestion time for `Decision` and `Notify`. These changes caused raw alignment to fall from 93% to 32%. Once the label was mapped, `Queue Routed` was removed, and source sequence ordering was restored, June-July fitness recovered to 89%. The remaining August-September drop is best explained by the unmodeled `Supervisor Sign-off` policy change: adding an optional sign-off model improved fitting traces from 66% to 86%, although that candidate model is overpermissive.

A real compliance problem may nevertheless remain. The enhanced-review traces without a recorded `Compliance Check`—25 in June-July and 30 in August-September—are unresolved. They could be true bypasses or missing events. The supervisor memo, `Manual Override` use, and incomplete incident detection mean stable appeal and approval rates cannot rule out noncompliance. My confidence is approximately 80–85% that logging/label/timestamp/model artifacts explain most of the apparent collapse, 55–60% that some missing compliance events represent a genuine control weakness, and 20–25% that widespread employee noncompliance caused the measured drop.

Immediate actions: IT should restore source-system sequence numbers and timestamps, document technical events, and validate warehouse ingestion. Process owners should formally confirm the `Risk Screen`/`Classify` alias and update the model for high-value sign-off. Compliance should audit enhanced-review-without-check cases and stratify sign-off by claim value. What must not be claimed yet: that employees are broadly noncompliant because residual moves exist; that the process is compliant because a control-flow-only optional sign-off model raises fitness; or that unchanged business outcomes prove the absence of a compliance problem.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Model versioning:** Normative process models must be versioned with effective dates tied to SOP/policy releases, and conformance checking must fail or warn when the model predates a known process change.
2. **Event-semantic contracts:** Activity labels and aliases must be governed by an event dictionary; UI label changes must produce explicit alias mappings before conformance is interpreted.
3. **Technical-event policy:** Technical events such as `Queue Routed` must be separated from business activities by lifecycle/layer metadata or excluded by default from business conformance logs with provenance.
4. **Timestamp provenance:** Event logs must carry source event timestamps and source sequence numbers; warehouse-ingestion time must not replace event time for ordering without an explicit, tested transformation.
5. **Data-aware constraints:** Data-dependent rules such as `Supervisor Sign-off` for claims above EUR 50,000 must be represented as data-aware conformance artifacts or case-attribute-stratified checks, not as optional control-flow branches.
6. **Post-release validation:** After any UI, gateway, ETL, or logging change, run a negative control by comparing pre/post normalized DFGs, variants, and fitness on a fixed model, and audit a random sample before interpreting deviations as operational noncompliance.
