# 1. Current-process diagnosis

The process is not simply “fast vs slow.” It is a segment- and risk-mismatch problem. The dominant fast path is attractive on upfront speed/cost, but for high-value and incomplete-evidence cases it shifts cost into complaints, reopenings, reshipments, service credits, and compliance exceptions.

- **Dominant fast path is not a clean win.** The DFG shows 2,550 auto-screen cases go to Fast approval and 2,300 Fast approval cases go to Dispatch replacement. This path is fast and 31% cheaper before rework, but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits. Complaint/reopen is 14.1% for Fast approval cases versus 3.3% for the Repair quote  Customer approval  Repair device path. The performance DFG also shows the long-tail risk: Fast approval  Customer complaint/reopen has mean 72h / median 50h / p90 150h, and Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen has mean 96h / median 70h / p90 180h. The contradiction is therefore explicit: the most frequent path is fast and cheap only if downstream rework is ignored.
- **The contradiction is segment-dependent.** Fast approval is especially bad for high-value devices: Premium high-value has 22% complaint/reopen on fast path vs 5% on reviewed/repair path; Standard high-value has 18% vs 6%. But the slow reviewed path is not universally better. Premium low-risk accessories are 8h on fast path vs 46h on manual path, and manual latency may trigger service credits above 24h. Standard low-risk accessories tolerate 72h if status visibility is good. So redesign must preserve fast flow for clearly low-risk, evidence-complete claims while stopping noncompliant fast approval for high-value/unclear claims.
- **Conformance is materially imperfect and policy-relevant.** Token-based replay has log fitness 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces. Alignment diagnostics show frequent move-on-log deviations: Fast approval before evidence completeness, Customer complaint/reopen after close, and Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices. This directly implicates rule R1 (>€400 requires Manual or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval) and R2 (dispatch only after evidence completeness, except Premium low-risk accessory). R3 is also at risk because reopened claims must pass Manual eligibility before final close or denial, yet the DFG shows 310 Complaint/reopen  Manual eligibility plus 120 Complaint/reopen  Escalation board. Low model precision also shows many low-frequency noncompliant combinations of auto approval, risk review, and repair.
- **Evidence completion is a high-friction loop.** The DFG shows 650 Auto screen  Request missing evidence, 780 Manual eligibility  Request missing evidence, 890 Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility, and 320 Request missing evidence  Claim abandoned. Repeated evidence requests occur in 26% of cases entering that loop. The unclear/missing-evidence segment is 22% of cases with median cycle time 140h and highest abandonment. This is not just waste; it is a customer-experience and compliance problem.
- **Resource queues migrate rather than disappear.** Manual eligibility has 7 reviewers at EUR34/h and queues sharply above 82% utilization. Fraud/risk review has only 2 specialists at EUR52/h and bottlenecks on Mondays/after campaigns. Repair quote delay is mostly waiting for part-price confirmation. Quality check has 2 inspectors who are underused except after repair campaigns. The escalation board has strong customer-satisfaction effect but only one daily 45-minute slot, so it cannot scale.

The defensible conclusion is not “force everything down the slow path.” It is to separate: low-risk, evidence-complete, segment-appropriate fast claims; high-value claims needing mandatory review and parts verification; and unclear-evidence cases needing assisted completion rather than repeated requests or abandonment.

# 2. Redesign A: Segment-aware policy router with evidence-complete fast lane

**Redesigned flow.** Replace the current generic auto-screen with a segment-aware policy router at intake. It uses value band, device risk, defect type, evidence completeness, and customer segment to route cases:

- Premium low-risk accessories with complete or allowed incomplete evidence can use a protected fast lane, because the current fast path is 8h vs 46h manual and service credits rise above 24h; R2 permits the Premium low-risk exception, but the case gets automated post-dispatch evidence follow-up and status visibility.
- Standard low-risk accessories with clean evidence can use fast approval/dispatch only after evidence completeness is confirmed; if evidence is missing, they enter a single evidence-completion step rather than repeated loops.
- High-value devices above EUR400 cannot go to Fast approval before Manual eligibility or Fraud/risk review, enforcing R1. Dispatch is blocked until evidence completeness is confirmed, enforcing R2 for all non-Premium-low-risk cases.
- Complaint/reopen always routes to Manual eligibility before final close or denial, enforcing R3.
- Missing-evidence cases get one consolidated evidence request and then either manual review or assisted follow-up; they should not cycle repeatedly through the current 890 Request missing evidence  Manual eligibility loop.

**Targets.** The most frequent noncompliant fast path, high-value devices, unclear/missing-evidence cases, and Premium low-risk service-credit exposure.

**Mechanism of improvement.** The router prevents noncompliant fast approvals and dispatches while preserving fast flow where speed is valuable and risk is low. Updating auto-screen rules can be deployed in roughly two weeks, but may increase manual-review inflow by 15–35%; the redesign accepts that load only for high-value/unclear cases and offsets it by reducing downstream complaints, reopenings, reshipments, and service credits. Evidence completeness becomes a gate rather than a late surprise.

**Deliberate sacrifice / unresolved.** This increases manual/fraud workload and may slow some high-value and unclear cases. It does not fully solve wrong replacement part selection; that requires the technical pre-dispatch gate in Redesign B. It also risks abandonment if digital evidence collection is used without an assisted channel. Exact Pareto gains are not knowable from the data; this is a compliance-and-rework-reduction move, not a pure cycle-time win.

# 3. Redesign B: High-value pre-dispatch verification and repair-quality closed loop

**Redesigned flow.** Keep the fast lane for low-risk accessories where wrong-replacement cost is about EUR95, but add a mandatory high-value verification lane for high-value devices and high-value repairs. After technical decision or repair quote, a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate verifies part selection, replacement configuration, evidence completeness, and required risk review before dispatch. The gate is staffed by the currently underused Quality check inspectors or a dedicated technician using a checklist. Part-price confirmation is integrated into the repair-quote step instead of being a separate waiting period. High-value repairs go through mandatory quality check and rework-loop closure; low-risk repairs are sample-audited rather than fully gated.

The gate adds 6–18h depending on resource design. That is deliberately accepted for high-value cases because wrong replacement has expected cost about EUR410, Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen is a long-tail pattern linked to incorrect replacement part selection, and rework repair is 12.5% for repaired high-value devices vs 4.0% for low-risk devices. This design also enforces R1 and R2 for high-value dispatch: no Fast approval above EUR400 without Manual or Fraud/risk review, and no dispatch before evidence completeness unless it is the narrow Premium low-risk accessory exception.

**Targets.** High-value devices, high-value replacement dispatch, repaired high-value devices, and the long-tail Dispatch replacement  Customer complaint/reopen pattern.

**Mechanism of improvement.** Catch wrong replacement selection before shipment and catch repair-quality issues before close. This reduces expensive reshipments, high-value rework, and compliance exceptions. Integrating part-price confirmation reduces the Manual eligibility  Repair quote delay, which the temporal profile shows is often too slow for Premium cases.

**Deliberate sacrifice / unresolved.** Adds 6–18h to high-value replacement and may create a Quality check bottleneck because there are only 2 inspectors. It is intentionally not applied to low-risk accessories, so low-risk wrong replacement can still occur, though at lower expected cost. It does not solve unclear-evidence abandonment or Premium low-risk service credits; those remain with Redesign A/C.

# 4. Redesign C: Assisted evidence concierge and Premium exception lane

**Redesigned flow.** Change evidence collection from repeated one-size-fits-all requests into a two-channel evidence concierge. At intake, customers receive a consolidated digital evidence checklist through a portal with automatic status visibility and reminders. Customers with low digital literacy or repeated failed digital attempts are proactively routed to an assisted phone/agent channel before abandonment. The evidence portal is expected to reduce repeated requests by 30–45%, but the assisted channel mitigates the known risk of higher abandonment.

Unclear/missing-evidence cases enter a single-owner concierge queue. After one failed reminder, the case gets assisted outreach instead of another automated request. Premium customers and reopened claims get an exception lane: Premium high-value and Premium low-risk approaching service thresholds receive senior manual eligibility/risk review before policy deadlines; reopened claims always pass Manual eligibility before final close or denial, enforcing R3. The daily escalation board is preserved only for high-value/Premium complex reopenings, not used as a general queue, because it has strong CX effect but poor scalability.

**Targets.** The 22% unclear/missing-evidence segment, Premium customers with strict service expectations, abandoned claims, and reopened/complaint cases.

**Mechanism of improvement.** Reduce repeated evidence requests, shorten the 140h median for unclear evidence, lower abandonment, and improve Premium service outcomes. Status visibility helps Standard low-risk customers tolerate up to 72h where appropriate. The assisted channel protects customers who would otherwise drop out under a purely digital portal. Enforcing R3 reduces compliance risk from complaint/reopen after close.

**Deliberate sacrifice / unresolved.** Higher customer-service and assisted-channel cost; possible overuse of manual eligibility for Premium exceptions; digital portal may still alienate some low-literacy customers if the assisted channel is under-resourced. It does not by itself fix high-value part selection or auto-screen policy defects; those require A and B.

# 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**
- Failure mode 1: Auto-screen rules or segment/defect attributes are wrong, so high-value or unclear claims are misrouted into the fast lane and noncompliant fast approvals persist.
- Failure mode 2: The 15–35% projected increase in manual-review inflow overwhelms the 7-reviewer manual eligibility team above 82% utilization, creating queues and Premium SLA misses.
- Failure mode 3: Evidence-completion redesign uses digital-only channels and increases abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers.
- Likely bottleneck migration: downstream complaint/reopen and fraud review shift upstream to manual eligibility and evidence completion.

**Redesign B**
- Failure mode 1: The pre-dispatch gate adds 6–18h and Premium high-value customers breach service expectations; staff may start skipping or informally bypassing the gate under pressure.
- Failure mode 2: The 2 Quality check inspectors become a hard bottleneck, especially after repair campaigns, causing high-value dispatch and repair queues.
- Failure mode 3: The gate verifies part selection but not missing evidence or policy routing, so it reduces reshipments without correcting the broader fast-path problem.
- Likely bottleneck migration: from downstream reshipments/complaints to Quality check/pre-dispatch verification and repair technician queues.

**Redesign C**
- Failure mode 1: The digital portal increases abandonment for low-digital-literacy customers, and the assisted channel is not staffed to absorb them.
- Failure mode 2: The Premium exception lane and concierge queue consume manual eligibility capacity and starve Standard/unclear cases; or status visibility raises expectations without reducing cycle time.
- Failure mode 3: The daily escalation board remains a choke point if reopened high-value cases are not triaged before reaching it.
- Likely bottleneck migration: to assisted evidence agents, manual eligibility, and senior exception reviewers; also possibly back to the escalation board if it remains a single daily slot.
