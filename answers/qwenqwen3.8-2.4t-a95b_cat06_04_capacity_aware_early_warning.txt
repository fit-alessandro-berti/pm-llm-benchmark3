## 1. Decision rule

I would use a constrained expected-utility rule, not a single `p_now` threshold. The decision must answer: *Is this case stage-eligible, is the alert evidentially actionable, and does the plausible causal benefit exceed the swarm cost, false-alarm burden, queue externality, and reserve option value?*

**Eligibility gates**

1. **Process-stage gate**
   - Swarm is eligible only before `Diagnosis Complete`.
   - If `Diagnosis Complete` has occurred, the swarm is not eligible, even if breach probability is high. Use non-swarm escalation instead.

2. **Alert/evidence gate**
   - First alert: actionable if the risk increase is credible and data quality is acceptable.
   - Repeated alert: actionable only if there is a materially new event or a sustained, validated risk deterioration.
   - Material new event examples: new customer log, new error evidence, stage transition, restored missing live-queue field, new dependency failure, or explicit customer escalation.
   - Minimum evidence change for repeated alerts:
     - new event, or
     - calibrated risk increase of at least about `0.10` from the last reliable prefix, or
     - two consecutive model refreshes with high calibrated risk and an urgent deadline.

3. **Data-quality gate**
   - If a key feature is imputed or a sharp risk jump may be artifactual, the case is “conditional” until validated.
   - If the deadline is imminent and validation cannot be completed, a human senior review may override, but the uncertainty should be explicitly recorded.

4. **Calibration adjustment**
   - `p_now` is observational and not the probability under intervention. It is used for alert credibility and urgency, not directly as the causal effect.
   - SME: add approximately `0.04–0.09` to `p_now` for planning, because recent backtests show underprediction.
   - Strategic Enterprise: subtract approximately `0.05–0.12`, because recent workflow redesign suggests overprediction.
   - Public Sector: no central adjustment, but widen uncertainty because sample size is moderate.
   - Non-Native-Language Support: widen uncertainty substantially and require language-support corroboration; do not automatically deny because of sparse validation.

5. **Utility screen**
   - For each case, compute a direct net-benefit interval:

     `Direct net benefit  ARR × value of avoiding breach  €450 swarm cost  false-alarm burden`

   - Use the supplied absolute-risk-reduction range as an uncertain estimate, not a known causal effect.
   - Treat the listed false-alarm burden conservatively as an operational cost of intervening. If it is only probabilistic in reality, the rule becomes more conservative than necessary.
   - Subtract expected spillover harm. Under the base model:

     `Spillover per slot  6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases × 0.01–0.03 added breach probability × average untreated diagnosis-stage exposure`

     Using an approximate average exposure of €20,000, this is about **€1,200–€3,600 per used slot**, before congestion effects.

6. **Reserve and capacity rule**
   - Keep one slot reserved unless the fourth candidate’s robust net benefit clearly exceeds the reserve option value and spillover cost.
   - Because a regulatory incident may arrive before 15:00, the reserve is retained unless a candidate is both urgent and robustly valuable.

7. **Prioritization logic**
   - Tier 1: stage-eligible, actionable evidence, robust positive net benefit, urgent enough to benefit.
   - Tier 2: positive expected value but material uncertainty, data-quality issue, repeated-alert issue, or weaker net benefit after spillover.
   - Tier 3: ineligible, suppressed, or robust net benefit negative/unclear.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

**Cool-down**

- After an alert that does not lead to intervention, suppress further automatic alerts for the same case for **2 hours** or **four model refreshes**, whichever comes first.
- After a deployed swarm, repeated swarms are normally forbidden for at least **4 hours** and only allowed if a materially new event occurs and the case is still before `Diagnosis Complete`.

**Suppression**

Suppress an alert if all of the following are true:

- No materially new event has occurred.
- The calibrated risk movement is small, for example less than `0.05–0.10`.
- No stage change, data-quality restoration, customer log, or new dependency failure has occurred.
- The case is not facing an immediate deadline that makes human escalation necessary.

**Escalation despite suppression**

Even if suppressed, a case should be escalated to human review if:

- Deadline is under 2 hours.
- Calibrated breach probability is very high, for example above `0.80`.
- The customer is strategically or regulatorily sensitive.
- There is suspicion that the model is missing a true deterioration.

This escalation does not automatically mean a swarm; it means a senior reviewer checks whether the intervention lever is still plausible.

**Re-entry**

A suppressed case re-enters the decision pool if:

- A materially new event occurs.
- The live-queue or data-quality issue is resolved.
- The calibrated risk increase reaches at least about `0.10` from the last reliable prefix.
- The case remains stage-eligible.
- Capacity and spillover constraints allow intervention.

- **I-05**: third alert, no new event, risk only moves from `0.55` to `0.57`. This should be suppressed under the repeated-alert policy. ## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

**Value-based prioritization can starve lower-value cases**

Because I-04 and I-08 have high contractual values, a pure value-weighted rule could repeatedly favor large accounts and neglect SME or Public Sector cases.

Concrete safeguards:

- Use net benefit, not value alone.
- Create a minimum safety floor: if a low-value case is imminent, stage-eligible, and has a positive robust net benefit after spillover, it can access a reserve slot.
- For cases with close priority scores, use urgency, evidence quality, or a weighted lottery rather than value alone.
- Monitor intervention rates by value quartile and segment. If high-value Strategic Enterprise cases receive a disproportionate share of swarms without correspondingly larger benefit, adjust the allocation rule.

**Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty**

I-09 shows the risk: the segment has sparse calibration and uncertain intervention-effect estimates, but the case is urgent and potentially high-benefit.

Concrete safeguards:

- Do not treat sparse data as automatic disqualification.
- Require language-support corroboration, translation support, or a specialist familiar with the customer’s language/context before final deployment.
- Record segment-specific outcomes separately.
- Use wider uncertainty intervals, but allow intervention when the lower-bound or discounted expected benefit is still meaningful.

**Differential calibration across segments**

SME risk is underpredicted, Strategic Enterprise risk is overpredicted, Public Sector is broadly acceptable but moderate-sized, and Non-Native-Language Support is uncertain.

Concrete safeguards:

- Apply segment-specific calibration adjustments before decision-making.
- Maintain separate reliability plots for segment, stage, and language-support status.
- Avoid a global threshold that would systematically disadvantage miscalibrated segments.
- Recalibrate more frequently for segments with recent workflow changes or sparse samples.

**Selective intervention changes future training labels**

If intervened cases avoid breach, the model may later learn that similar cases are low-risk, even though they were low-risk only because of intervention. Conversely, untreated cases may breach more often because of spillover delay, making them appear inherently riskier.

Concrete safeguards:

- Log every intervention decision, eligibility score, alert evidence, and time stamp.
- Include an explicit intervention flag in training data.
- Use causal modeling rather than naive outcome learning.
- Where operationally possible, randomize marginal slot decisions or use stepped-wedge deployment.
- Use inverse-probability weighting or matched controls when estimating model performance and intervention effects.

**Spillover harms may fall systematically on untreated groups**

Swarm slots delay other diagnosis-stage cases. If high-value cases are repeatedly treated, the delay burden may fall on lower-value or less-urgent untreated cases.

Concrete safeguards:

- Include spillover cost in every deployment decision.
- Cap the number of simultaneous swarm slots used when diagnosis-queue congestion is high.
- Monitor queue delay by segment, value tier, and language-support status.
- If a case is deprioritized because of spillover, consider compensating actions: expedited triage, lighter-touch review, or queue priority after the swarm completes.
- Treat sustained spillover harm as a policy failure, not just an externality.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**

Track daily:

- Swarm slots used and reserved.
- Whether reserve was released and why.
- Swarm start/end times.
- Specialist interruption minutes.
- Aggregate delay added to non-intervened diagnosis cases.
- Breach outcomes for intervened and untreated cases.
- Regulatory incident arrivals and response times.

**Predictive monitoring**

Track weekly:

- Calibration by segment, stage, and language-support status.
- Observed versus predicted breach probability.
- Brier score, calibration slope, and reliability gaps.
- Alert volume, suppression rate, override rate, and repeated-alert rate.
- Data-quality failures, especially imputed live-queue fields.

Trigger: if a segment’s observed-predicted gap exceeds about `0.05` for two consecutive weeks, update the segment adjustment and review decision thresholds.

**Causal-effect monitoring**

The supplied ARR ranges should not be treated as known causal effects.

Plan:

- Estimate intervention effects using treated versus matched untreated cases, adjusted for eligibility score, stage, segment, and time pressure.
- Use randomized or quasi-randomized marginal slot decisions where feasible.
- Maintain a Bayesian hierarchical model for ARR by segment, stage, and case type.
- Estimate spillover separately by measuring delay and breach changes among untreated diagnosis-stage cases.
- Review posterior ARR intervals after every 20–30 intervention decisions.

Trigger: if the lower credible bound of swarm benefit becomes near zero or negative for a segment, pause automatic deployment for that segment and require senior review.

**Capacity and spillover monitoring**

Track:

- Average delay per used slot.
- Number of untreated diagnosis-stage cases exposed.
- Congestion level in the diagnosis queue.
- Breach-probability increments caused by delay.

Trigger:

- If average delay exceeds 20 minutes per slot or estimated spillover breach probability exceeds `0.03`, reduce simultaneous swarm usage or increase reserve retention.
- If the diagnosis queue is severely congested, prefer non-swarm interventions or targeted queue relief.

**Fairness monitoring**

Track:

- Intervention rates by segment, value tier, language-support status, and customer size.
- False-alarm burden distribution.
- Breach rates after policy changes.
- Suppressed repeated alerts and whether suppressed cases later breach.
- Complaints or customer disruption reports.

Trigger:

- If a protected or operationally vulnerable segment receives materially fewer interventions despite similar robust expected benefit, audit the rule.
- If lower-value cases show rising breach rates while high-value cases receive disproportionate swarms, adjust priority weights or introduce fairness constraints.

**Policy recalibration and threshold changes**

Change the allocation rule only with evidence:

- If rolling net impact over 20 decisions is positive, calibration is stable, and reserve is rarely needed, consider releasing reserve more often or lowering the robust-benefit threshold.
- If rolling net impact is negative, spillover is high, or adverse scenarios occur frequently, raise the required robust benefit, retain reserve more often, or reduce simultaneous swarms.
- If calibration drifts, update segment probability adjustments before changing intervention thresholds.
- If causal estimates show weaker effects than supplied ranges, shrink ARR priors and rely more on waiting, validation, and non-swarm escalation.
