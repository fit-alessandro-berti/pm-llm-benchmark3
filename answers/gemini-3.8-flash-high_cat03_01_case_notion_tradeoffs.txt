## 1. Analytical assumptions

### Core Concepts
- **Case Notion**: In single-case process mining, a case notion partitions the event universe $\mathcal{E}$ into discrete trace sequences by projecting onto instances of an object type $T \in \mathcal{T}_{obj}$. An event $e$ is bound to case $c \in \mathcal{O}_T$ if $c \in \mathcal{O}(e)$ via Event-to-Object (E2O) links.
- **Convergence**: Arises when an event relates to multiple case objects ($|\mathcal{O}(e) \cap \mathcal{O}_T| > 1$). Flattening replicates $e$ across related traces, inflating activity counts and creating artificial synchronization points.
- **Divergence**: Arises when a case object relates to multiple downstream objects ($1:N$ or $N:M$ cardinalities). Flattening forces concurrent child lifecycles into an interleaved sequence, generating spurious loops, false rework, and artificial trace variants.

### Copied Shared Events: Evidence vs. Duplication
Shared events have a dual analytical status:
1. *Empirical Evidence (Object-Centric View)*: They record real physical synchronization and shared resource usage (e.g., dispatch $e_{16}$ binding packages $P_2, P_3$ from orders $O_1, O_2, O_3$ into truck $D_2$).
2. *Analytical Duplication (Flattened View)*: Replicating $e_{16}$ across $O_1, O_2, O_3$ pseudoreplicates one physical vehicle departure into three distinct dispatches, corrupting throughput times and capacity models.

### Permitted Analytical Transformations
1. *Direct E2O Baseline*: Maps events to cases strictly using explicit E2O relations in the snapshot.
2. *Propagated E2O Transformation (O2O Closure)*: Traverses Object-to-Object links (e.g., propagating order creation $e_{01}$ to items $I_{11}, I_{12}$ via $O_1 \xrightarrow{\text{contains}} I$). While completing truncated lifecycles, propagation severely amplifies downstream convergence duplication.
3. *Fractional Event Weighting*: Replicated events receive analytical weight $w(e) = 1 / |\mathcal{O}(e) \cap \mathcal{O}_T|$, preserving physical event count conservation.

## 3. Concrete distortion analysis

### 1. Cross-Order Packaging: Synchronization Delay Misattribution
- **Affected Flattening**: Order.
- **Specific Identifiers**: Orders $O_2, O_3$; Package $P_3$; Items $I_{21}, I_{31}$; Events $e_{07}$ (`Add Item` 09:20), $e_{08}$ (`Add Item` 09:22), $e_{13}$ (`Pack Package` 10:12).
- **Distortion Mechanism**: The 52-minute gap between $e_{07}$ and $e_{13}$ in $O_2$'s trace is misattributed to internal packing inefficiency. In reality, $O_2$'s item $I_{21}$ was ready at 09:20, but packing was held awaiting $O_3$'s item $I_{31}$ ($e_{08}$) to allow co-packing into $P_3$.
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**. It creates a phantom bottleneck in $O_2$ and conceals cross-order batching synchronization.

### 2. Cross-Order Packaging: Spurious Rework Self-Loops
- **Affected Flattening**: Order.
- **Specific Identifiers**: Order $O_3$; Packages $P_3, P_4$; Items $I_{31}, I_{32}$; Events $e_{13}$ (`Pack Package` 10:12), $e_{14}$ (`Pack Package` 10:20).
- **Distortion Mechanism**: $O_3$'s items split across $P_3$ and $P_4$. Flattening forces both pack events into $O_3$'s sequential trace ($e_{13} \to e_{14}$), creating a `Pack Package` $\to$ `Pack Package` self-loop edge. Algorithms interpret this as operational rework or re-packing, whereas it represents two concurrent, single-pass packing operations on distinct parcels.
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**. It fabricates non-existent rework cycles and inflates perceived process complexity.

### 3. Consolidated Delivery: Fleet Volume Triplication and Sequential Dispatch
- **Affected Flattening**: Order.
- **Specific Identifiers**: Orders $O_1, O_2, O_3$; Delivery $D_2$; Packages $P_2, P_3$; Events $e_{15}$ (`Dispatch` $D_1$ 11:00), $e_{16}$ (`Dispatch` $D_2$ 11:05), $e_{23}$ (`Confirm Delivery` $D_1$ 14:00), $e_{24}$ (`Confirm Delivery` $D_2$ 16:00).
- **Distortion Mechanism**: Truck $D_2$ consolidates packages $P_2$ ($O_1$) and $P_3$ ($O_2, O_3$). Flattening replicates $e_{16}$ and $e_{24}$ across $O_1, O_2, O_3$, registering 3 dispatches and 3 deliveries for $D_2$, overstating trips by 200%. For $O_1$, it creates sequential loops: `Dispatch` $\to$ `Dispatch` ($e_{15} \to e_{16}$) and `Confirm Delivery` $\to$ `Confirm Delivery` ($e_{23} \to e_{24}$).
- **Evaluation**: **Context-Dependent**. Highly **harmful** for carrier fleet capacity planning (hallucinating 3 trips), but **informative** for customer service (reflecting 2 partial deliveries for $O_1$).

### 4. Consolidated Delivery: Micro-Level Independence Fallacy
- **Affected Flattening**: Item.
- **Specific Identifiers**: Items $I_{12}, I_{21}, I_{31}$; Delivery $D_2$; Events $e_{16}$ (`Dispatch` 11:05), $e_{24}$ (`Confirm Delivery` 16:00).
- **Distortion Mechanism**: Items $I_{12}, I_{21}, I_{31}$ each generate separate traces recording transit from $e_{16}$ to $e_{24}$ (4h 55m). A queueing model computes cumulative transit consumption as $3 \times \text{4h 55m} = \text{14h 45m}$, treating them as three independently transported units and masking the shared capacity constraint of truck $D_2$.
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**. It distorts transportation resource modeling and prevents accurate freight utilization analysis.

### 5. Consolidated Invoicing: Administrative Multi-Entity Coupling
- **Affected Flattening**: Order.
- **Specific Identifiers**: Orders $O_2, O_3$; Invoice $V_2$; Event $e_{19}$ (`Issue Invoice` 12:05).
- **Distortion Mechanism**: Invoice $V_2$ consolidates orders $O_2$ and $O_3$ into one commercial document. Order flattening replicates $e_{19}$ into both traces, recording two invoicing events instead of one physical invoice. Moreover, $O_2$'s billing lead time appears driven solely by $O_2$, whereas billing was held pending packing completion of $O_3$ ($e_{14}$ at 10:20).
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**. It distorts accounting transaction metrics and hides cross-order batch billing rules.

### 6. Partial Payments: Variant Proliferation and Interleaved Sequences
- **Affected Flattening**: Order (and Invoice).
- **Specific Identifiers**: Orders $O_2, O_3$; Invoice $V_2$; Payments $M_2, M_3$; Delivery $D_2$; Events $e_{19}$ (`Issue Invoice` 12:05), $e_{22}$ (`Record Payment` 12:40), $e_{24}$ (`Confirm Delivery` 16:00), $e_{25}$ (`Record Payment` 09:00 next day).
- **Distortion Mechanism**: Invoice $V_2$ is settled across two instalments ($M_2, M_3$). In $O_2$ and $O_3$, payment events interleave with delivery confirmation ($e_{24}$):
  $$\text{Dispatch } (e_{16}) \to \text{Issue Invoice } (e_{19}) \to \text{Record Payment } (e_{22}) \to \text{Confirm Delivery } (e_{24}) \to \text{Record Payment } (e_{25})$$
  Because $O_1$ pays in full before delivery ($e_{21} \to e_{23}$), and $O_4$ pays after delivery ($e_{20} \to e_{26} \to e_{27}$), Order flattening generates 3 distinct control-flow variants across 4 orders, introducing bidirectional cycles between `Record Payment` and `Confirm Delivery`.
- **Evaluation**: **Context-Dependent**. Highly **informative** for credit risk monitoring (detecting delivery prior to full settlement), but **harmful** for process standardization analysis.

### 7. Near-Singleton Lifecycle: Total State-Space Collapse
- **Affected Flattening**: Payment.
- **Specific Identifiers**: Payments $M_1, M_2, M_3, M_4$; Events $e_{21}, e_{22}, e_{25}, e_{26}$.
- **Distortion Mechanism**: Flattening on Payment yields 4 traces of length 1 containing only `Record Payment`. The directly-follows relation set is empty ($\emptyset$). The discovered DFG is a single disconnected node with 0 edges and mean throughput time of 0.0 seconds, erasing whether payment occurred 30 minutes post-invoicing ($M_1$) or after a 20-hour delay ($M_3$).
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**. It creates a degenerate process space that destroys all behavioral, temporal, and structural insight.

### 8. Upstream Truncation Trap: False Operational Velocity
- **Affected Flattening**: Package.
- **Specific Identifiers**: Packages $P_1$–$P_4$; Orders $O_1$–$O_4$; Events $e_{01}$–$e_{04}$ (Creation), $e_{11}$–$e_{14}$ (Pack), $e_{15}$–$e_{17}$ (Dispatch), $e_{23}, e_{24}, e_{27}$ (Delivery), $e_{28}$–$e_{31}$ (Close).
- **Distortion Mechanism**: In Package flattening, every parcel exhibits a clean sequence: $\langle \text{Pack} \to \text{Dispatch} \to \text{Confirm Delivery} \rangle$. For $P_1$, throughput time is 4 hours ($e_{11}$ at 10:00 to $e_{23}$ at 14:00). This metric ignores customer $O_1$'s 60-minute wait before packing began ($e_{01}$ at 09:00) and the 19-hour administrative dwell time before order closure ($e_{28}$ at 11:00 next day).
- **Evaluation**: **Harmful**. It creates operational-commercial misalignment, giving operations a false impression of rapid fulfillment while customer lead times are much longer.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

### Recommended Flattened Logs and Safe Business Scopes
1. **Flattened Order Log**: Answers customer lead time from `Create Order` to `Close Order`, order intake rates, and order-to-packaging latency. Unsafe for packaging capacity, vehicle dispatch volumes, or transit times.
2. **Flattened Package Log**: Answers warehouse dwell time from packaging completion to carrier dispatch, and parcel loss rates. Unsafe for customer order fulfillment speed or cross-order batching holds.
3. **Flattened Invoice Log**: Answers Days Sales Outstanding (DSO), instalment frequency, and billing-to-payment collection duration. Unsafe for physical handling efficiency or delivery compliance.

### Questions Strictly Requiring Object-Centric Artifacts
- **Object-Centric DFG (OC-DFG)**: Visualizing synchronized flows of Orders, Packages, and Deliveries at consolidation hubs without duplication—e.g., `Dispatch` binding Packages ($P_2, P_3$) and Orders ($O_1, O_2, O_3$) into truck $D_2$.
- **Object Type Graph (OTG)**: Discovering data topology—verifying that Orders and Packages share an $N:M$ relationship, while Invoices and Payments exhibit a $1:N$ relationship.
- **Event-Type to Object-Type (ET-OT) Graph**: Determining multi-object activity binding—showing `Dispatch` binds four object types ($\text{Order}, \text{Item}, \text{Package}, \text{Delivery}$), whereas `Close Order` binds only $\text{Order}$.
- **Object Interaction Summary**: Auditing batching policies—quantifying orders consolidated per package ($P_3$ bundling $O_2, O_3$) and packages co-loaded per delivery run.
- **Equivalent-Execution Clustering (`pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel`)**: Grouping process executions based on multi-object interaction topology, separating standard fulfillment ($O_1$) from cross-docked fulfillment ($O_2, O_3, O_4$).

### Reconciliation Protocol for Contradictory Findings
1. **Disambiguate Metric Foundations**: Distinguish case-event frequency (occurrences across flattened traces) from event emission frequency (unique events in $\mathcal{E}$). If Order flattening reports 6 `Dispatch` events while the operational log shows 3, flag excess as consolidation pseudoreplication.
2. **Apply Fractional Flow Conservation**: Weight each flattened event by $w(e) = 1 / |\mathcal{O}(e) \cap \mathcal{O}_{\text{case}}|$ to reconcile flattened counts with physical event emissions.
3. **Trace Structural Attribution via OTG**: When an apparent rework loop appears in a flattened DFG (e.g., `Pack Package` $\to$ `Pack Package` in $O_3$), check OTG and E2O links. If consecutive events bind distinct objects ($P_3$ at $e_{13}$ vs $P_4$ at $e_{14}$), reclassify from "rework" to "divergent concurrent packaging".

## 5. Lead-object decision

### Position Selection
**C. Reject a single lead object for this process.**

### Structured Argument
- **Decision Criteria**:
  1. *Cardinality Topology*: Requires a hierarchical tree rooted in the lead object; here, relationships are fundamentally many-to-many ($N:M$) in fulfillment (Orders $\leftrightarrow$ Packages $\leftrightarrow$ Deliveries) and finance (Orders $\leftrightarrow$ Invoices $\leftrightarrow$ Payments).
  2. *Semantic Conservation*: Representation must not invent non-existent activities via duplication or generate spurious rework loops.
  3. *Cross-Functional Scope*: Model must cover sales, warehousing, freight, and finance without sub-process truncation.
- **Strongest Supporting Evidence**:
  - Selecting **Order** corrupts physical logistics: triplicates $e_{16}$ and $e_{24}$, generates false rework loops in $O_1, O_3$, and hides cross-order packaging holds ($P_3$ waiting for $O_2, O_3$).
  - Selecting **Item** erases 54.8% of the log (ordering, billing, payments, closure vanish).
  - Selecting **Package** or **Delivery** truncates ordering and finance ($D$ has only 2 events).
  - Selecting **Invoice** or **Payment** destroys fulfillment visibility entirely.
- **Strongest Counterargument**: Corporate ERP systems and dashboards revolve strictly around the **Customer Order**. Rejecting a lead object increases cognitive load and requires non-standard OCEL tooling.
- **Residual Risk**: Loss of a unified cycle time KPI; reliance on advanced OCEL software.
- **Conditions for Reversal**: Operational overhaul enforcing strict single-order packaging and dedicated shipments (eliminating $N:M$ convergence), or a legal mandate requiring order-level SLA audits with fractional weighting.

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

### Check 1: Event Down-Weighting vs. Raw Replication Sensitivity
- **Implementation**: In Order flattening, assign weights $w(e) = 1 / |\mathcal{O}(e) \cap \mathcal{O}_{\text{Order}}|$: single-order events receive $w=1.0$; dual-order events ($e_{13}, e_{14}, e_{17}, e_{19}, e_{22}, e_{25}, e_{27}$) receive $w=0.5$; triple-order events ($e_{16}, e_{24}$) receive $w=0.333$.
- **Evaluation Criteria**: Compare activity frequencies and mean transition dwell times between unweighted and weighted logs.
- **Expected Outcome**: Total weighted events equal the true 31 physical events. Mean wait time between `Pack Package` and `Dispatch` drops significantly as consolidated runs ($e_{16}, e_{24}$) are correctly de-emphasized.

### Check 2: Direct E2O Relations vs. Propagated O2O Closure Sensitivity
- **Implementation**: Propagate relations across O2O links: $O \to I$ upstream to `Create Order`; $P \to I \to O$ downstream to billing/payment; $O \to P \to D$ across fulfillment.
- **Evaluation Criteria**: Measure changes in trace length distribution, event count, and conformance against an end-to-end model.
- **Expected Outcome**: Trace completeness increases for Item/Package flattening, but convergence explodes: linking $O \to P \to D$ causes truck dispatch $D_2$ to inherit items and orders transitively, increasing event duplication by over 300%.

### Check 3: Lead-Object Permutation and Bottleneck Inversion
- **Implementation**: Systematically permute the lead object across Order, Item, Package, and Delivery, executing automated bottleneck detection for each.
- **Evaluation Criteria**: Compare the rank-ordered top 3 bottlenecks across all 4 flattened logs.
- **Expected Outcome**: The identified bottleneck inverts based on lead-object choice:
  - *Order-centric*: Bottleneck is between `Confirm Delivery` ($e_{24}$) and `Close Order` ($e_{29}, e_{30}$) (19h hold).
  - *Package-centric*: Bottleneck is transit delay between `Dispatch` ($e_{17}$) and `Confirm Delivery` ($e_{27}$) for $P_4$ (22h 45m).
  - *Item-centric*: Bottleneck is packing wait time for cross-order items ($I_{21}, I_{31}$).
  - Confirms bottlenecks are artifacts of case projection rather than objective realities.

### Check 4: OC-DFG Metric Triangulation (Events vs. Unique Objects vs. Total Interactions)
- **Implementation**: On the OC-DFG, evaluate edge metrics connecting `Pack Package` to `Dispatch` across three modes:
  1. *Event Count*: Physical transitions ($e_{11}$–$e_{14} \to e_{15}$–$e_{17} = 3$).
  2. *Unique Object Count*: Unique entities flowing across the edge (4 Packages, 6 Items, 4 Orders).
  3. *Total Object Interaction Count*: Sum of all object-event bindings across the transition ($\sum_{e} |\mathcal{O}(e)|$).
- **Evaluation Criteria**: Compute the ratio of Total Interactions to Event Count across each graph edge.
- **Expected Outcome**: Ratio spikes to $9:1$ at consolidation hubs ($e_{16}, e_{24}$), while remaining $1:1$ or $2:1$ at administrative milestones ($e_{01}, e_{18}$), pinpointing where flattening fails.
