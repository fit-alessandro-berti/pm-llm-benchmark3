## 1. Analytical assumptions

* **Case notion**: A case notion is an analytical projection—not an ontological ground truth—that selects one object type as the trace identifier and extracts a sequence of directly related events (`E2O`) for each object instance of that type. Because this process coordinates customer commitments (`Order`), physical handling units (`Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`), and financial instruments (`Invoice`, `Payment`) across many-to-many boundaries, any single-object case notion privileges one operational clock while slicing or duplicating others.
* **Convergence**: Convergence occurs when a single event is related to multiple objects of the chosen case type, causing flattening to replicate that event across multiple traces. For example, flattening on `Order` copies `e16` (`Dispatch` on `D2`) into `O1`, `O2`, and `O3`, and copies `e19` (`Issue Invoice` on `V2`) into `O2` and `O3`.
* **Divergence**: Divergence occurs when one instance of the chosen case type is associated with multiple independently evolving sub-objects of another type, causing repeated activities, false rework self-loops, or non-causal interleaving within a single flattened trace. For example, within `O1`, independent progression of `I11`/`P1`/`D1` and `I12`/`P2`/`D2` produces repeated `Add Item` (`e05`, `e06`), `Pack Package` (`e11`, `e12`), `Dispatch` (`e15`, `e16`), and `Confirm Delivery` (`e23`, `e24`) events; additionally, concurrent progression of `V1`/`M1` (`e18`, `e21`) interleaves with `D1`/`D2` logistics.
* **Status of copied shared events**: Copied events are **both** valid state-transition evidence and statistical duplication. From a state perspective, `e16` genuinely advances `O1`, `O2`, and `O3`. From a workload, capacity, or throughput perspective, treating those three trace entries as three dispatches overstates physical courier activity by 200%.
* **Permitted analytical transformations**: Baseline analysis strictly uses the supplied `E2O` table without silent `O2O` propagation. Notably, the raw `E2O` log already includes downward `Order` links on all 31 events (`e01`–`e31`), whereas downstream types (`Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`, `Payment`) attach only from creation onward within their respective Logistics or Finance branch. Explicit `O2O` propagation is permitted solely as a controlled sensitivity experiment (Section 7), subject to scope guards because naive graph traversal introduces false links (e.g., propagating `O1 -> contains -> I11` onto `e16` would falsely place `I11` onto `D2`, even though `I11` shipped on `D1` via `e15`).

Conversely, `Package` flattening shows four clean traces (`P1`–`P4`), hiding that `P4`'s 22h 45m delivery (`e17 -> e27`) acts as a joint synchronization bottleneck delaying closure for both `O3` (`e30`) and `O4` (`e31`), even though half of `O3` (`P3`) arrived 18 hours earlier (`e24`).
   * *Assessment*: **Harmful** in either isolated flattening because root-cause attribution of `O3`'s closure delay requires cross-object visibility across `O3`, `P3`, `P4`, and `O4`.

3. **Consolidated delivery triple-counting and transit-time skew (`Order`, `Item`, and `Package` flattenings)**
   * *Citations*: `D2` (`e16` `Dispatch` at 11:05; `e24` `Confirm Delivery` at 16:00; duration 4h 55m), carrying `P2` (`I12` from `O1`) and `P3` (`I21` from `O2`, `I31` from `O3`), alongside `D1` (`e15->e23`, 3h 00m) and `D3` (`e17->e27`, 22h 45m).
   * *Distortion*: True fleet volume is 3 dispatches with mean van transit time of **10h 13m** (`(180 + 295 + 1365) / 3 = 613.3 min`). `Package` flattening counts `D2` twice (`P2`, `P3`), lowering mean transit duration by 13.0% to **8h 54m** (`533.8 min`). `Item` and `Order` flattenings inflate dispatch counts by 100% (6 trace dispatches vs. 3 vans) and weight `D2` 3x and `D3` 2x, shifting apparent mean transit to **10h 33m** (`632.5 min`).
   * *Assessment*: **Context-dependent**: weighted averages validly measure *customer-item wait experience*, but severely distort *carrier SLA and fleet utilization* metrics.

4. **Consolidated invoicing inflating financial volume and masking cross-order settlement holds (`Order` vs. `Invoice` flattenings)**
   * *Citations*: `V2` (`e19` `Issue Invoice` at 12:05, billing `O2` and `O3`), `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` of `D2` at Feb 3 16:00), `e25` (`Record Payment` `M3` at Feb 4 09:00), and `e29` (`Close Order` `O2` at Feb 4 11:05).
   * *Distortion*: `Order` flattening duplicates `e19` across `O2` and `O3` (4 apparent invoices vs. 3 real invoices `V1`–`V3`). Meanwhile, `Invoice` flattening preserves `V2` cleanly (`e19 -> e22 -> e25`) but erases order fulfillment state, hiding why `O2` sat unclosed for **19h 05m** after its sole item (`I21` in `P3`/`D2`) was delivered at `e24`—namely, `O2` was financially coupled to `O3` through `V2` and blocked on `M3` (`e25`).
   * *Assessment*: **Harmful** if operations blames warehouse fulfillment for `O2`'s 26h 03m lead time, or if finance double-counts `V2`'s receivable value across `O2` and `O3`.

5. **Partial payments creating ping-pong causal mirages and ambiguous self-loops (`Order` and `Invoice` flattenings)**
   * *Citations*: `V2` settled via `M2` (`partially_settles`, `e22` at Feb 3 12:40) and `M3` (`completes_settlement`, `e25` at Feb 4 09:00), framing `e24` (`Confirm Delivery` at Feb 3 16:00) on `O2` and `O3`.
   * *Distortion*: In `Invoice` flattening (`V2`), `e22 -> e25` forms a `Record Payment -> Record Payment` self-loop (20h 20m) that strips the `O2O` qualifier distinction between scheduled partial payment and duplicate-payment error. Worse, in `Order` flattening for both `O2` and `O3`, `e24` falls chronologically between `e22` and `e25`, generating the cyclic DFG motif `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery -> Record Payment` (frequency 2x each direction), implying delivery confirmation triggers a second billing charge.
   * *Assessment*: **Informative** in `Invoice` flattening if enriched with `O2O` qualifiers; **actively harmful** in `Order` flattening due to false cross-domain causality.

6. **Singleton lifecycle degeneration (`Payment` flattening)**
   * *Citations*: `M1` (`e21`), `M2` (`e22`), `M3` (`e25`), `M4` (`e26`).
   * *Distortion*: Flattening on `Payment` yields four 1-event traces (`[Record Payment]`), discarding 27/31 events (87.1%). Every case has zero duration, the DFG contains zero internal edges, and `M2` and `M3` appear as independent atomic postings rather than sequential settlements of `V2`.
   * *Assessment*: **Harmful** (analytically vacuous for control-flow or bottleneck discovery).

7. **Near-singleton truncation hiding pre-dispatch batching waits (`Delivery` flattening)**
   * *Citations*: `D1` (`e15->e23`), `D2` (`e16->e24`), `D3` (`e17->e27`), vs. upstream `Pack Package` events `e11` (10:00), `e12` (10:10), `e13` (10:12), `e14` (10:20).
   * *Distortion*: `Delivery` flattening drops 25/31 events, starting each trace at `Dispatch`. It conceals that `P2` waited 55 minutes (`e12` to `e16`) and `P3` waited 53 minutes (`e13` to `e16`) on the loading dock so `D2` could consolidate both parcels into one van.
   * *Assessment*: **Context-dependent**: harmless for external carrier SLA auditing, **harmful** for end-to-end dock-to-door bottleneck analysis.

8. **Complete erasure of `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` via concurrency interleaving (`Order` flattening)**
   * *Citations*: Logistics transitions `e15->e23`, `e16->e24`, `e17->e27` vs. billing/payment events `e18`–`e22` (Feb 3 12:00–12:40) and `e25`–`e26` (Feb 4 09:00–09:30).
   * *Distortion*: Across **all four** orders (`O1`–`O4`), invoicing and payment timestamps fall inside the physical transit window (`O1`: `e16 -> e18 -> e21 -> e23`; `O2`: `e16 -> e19 -> e22 -> e24`; `O3`: `e17 -> e19 -> e22 -> e24` and `e24 -> e25 -> e27`; `O4`: `e17 -> e20 -> e26 -> e27`). Consequently, the `Order` DFG contains **zero** `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` edges and instead fabricates `Dispatch -> Issue Invoice` (4x) and `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery` (5x), creating the illusion that couriers hold shipments until payment is recorded.
   * *Assessment*: **Severely harmful**—a textbook interleaved-concurrency trap in monolithic DFGs.

9. **Multi-item divergence masquerading as four-stage operational rework (`Order` flattening)**
   * *Citations*: `O1` containing `I11` (`P1`, `D1`: `e05`, `e11`, `e15`, `e23`) and `I12` (`P2`, `D2`: `e06`, `e12`, `e16`, `e24`).
   * *Distortion*: `O1`'s flattened trace (`e01 -> e05 -> e06 -> e11 -> e12 -> e15 -> e16 -> e18 -> e21 -> e23 -> e24 -> e28`) exhibits four self-loops (`Add Item`, `Pack Package`, `Dispatch`, `Confirm Delivery`). Standard rework detectors flag `O1` (and `O3`) as suffering pervasive repetitive defects, even though every item (`I11`, `I12`) executed each warehouse step once without error.
   * *Assessment*: **Harmful** for process conformance and six-sigma rework reporting.

## 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

* **Flattened logs worth creating and their safe question boundaries**:
  1. **`Delivery` log** (`D1`–`D3`): Safe for carrier transit SLA distribution (`e15->e23`, `e16->e24`, `e17->e27`) and exact shipment counts (0% duplication).
  2. **`Invoice` log** (`V1`–`V3`): Safe for accounts-receivable collection lead times (`Issue Invoice` to final `Record Payment`: 30m on `V1`, 20h 55m on `V2`, 21h 20m on `V3`) and instalment frequency (0% duplication).
  3. **`Package` log** (`P1`–`P4`): Safe for warehouse dock-dwell times (`Pack Package -> Dispatch`: 53m–60m across `e11`–`e17`), provided transit statistics are not treated as van counts due to `D2` (`e16`, `e24`).
  4. **`Order` log** (`O1`–`O4`): Safe *only* for boundary cycle time (`Create Order` to `Close Order`: 26h 00m–26h 04m across `O1`–`O4`) and declarative existence checks (`discover_log_skeleton`), **never** for DFG causality or activity resource counts.
  5. *Discard* **`Payment`** (degenerate 1-event traces) and **`Item`** (subsumed by OC-DFG while inflating shared warehouse events by 50%).

* **Questions requiring native object-centric artifacts**:
  * **OC-DFG (`discover_ocdfg`)**: Required to untangle concurrent branches so `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` is preserved via `Package`/`Delivery` paths while `Issue Invoice -> Record Payment` runs in parallel via `Invoice`, and to track synchronization barriers before `Close Order` (`e28`–`e31`).
  * **Object-Type Graph (`discover_otg`) & Object Interactions Summary (`ocel_objects_interactions_summary`)**: Required to expose M:N cardinalities (`Order <-> Package`, `Order <-> Invoice`, `Invoice -> Payment`) and identify cross-order coupling hubs (e.g., `D2` bridging `O1`, `O2`, `O3`; `O3` bridging `P3`/`O2` and `P4`/`O4`, linking all four orders into a single connected component).
  * **Event-Type to Object-Type (`discover_etot`)**: Required to verify object participation scope—immediately showing why `Order` participates in all 8 event types whereas `Delivery` and `Invoice` span disjoint 2-activity subsets.
  * **Equivalent-execution clustering (`cluster_equivalent_ocel`)**: Required to classify structural execution archetypes around a chosen anchor object (e.g., showing `O1`–`O4` represent 4 distinct multi-object topologies, whereas `Invoice` clusters `V1` and `V3` together against multi-order/two-payment `V2`).

* **Reconciling contradictions between flattened and object-centric views**:
  1. Anchor **activity/resource frequencies** strictly to raw OCEL event counts (`events = 3` dispatches, not `Order`-flattened `6`).
  2. Treat any flattened DFG edge between disjoint `ET–OT` domains (e.g., `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery`, where `M`/`V` and `P`/`D` never co-occur) as **concurrent interleaving**, not causal precedence, unless validated by a shared synchronization object in the OC-DFG.
  3. Reconcile divergent cycle-time averages by explicitly labeling the unit of aggregation (e.g., *mean hours per van* [`Delivery`] vs. *mean hours per parcel* [`Package`] vs. *mean hours per customer item* [`Item`]).

## 5. Lead-object decision

**Selected Position: B. Use a primary lead object (`Order`) plus explicitly secondary lifecycles (`Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`).**

* **Decision criteria**:
  1. *Boundary completeness*: Ability to bracket end-to-end business value from initiation (`Create Order`) to completion (`Close Order`).
  2. *Cross-branch visibility*: Ability to connect physical fulfillment (`Item`, `Package`, `Delivery`) with financial settlement (`Invoice`, `Payment`).
  3. *Metric fidelity*: Resistance to convergence duplication and divergence interleaving on operational KPIs.

* **Strongest supporting evidence**:
  `Order` (`O1`–`O4`) is the **only** object type present in all 31 events (`e01`–`e31`) and all 8 activity types. Neither `Create Order` (`e01`–`e04`) nor `Close Order` (`e28`–`e31`) touches any other object type, and no other object bridges logistics (`e11`–`e17`, `e23`–`e24`, `e27`) and finance (`e18`–`e22`, `e25`–`e26`). Without `Order` as primary lead anchor, it is impossible to explain customer-level closure readiness—such as why `O3` (`e30`) had to wait for both `M3` (`e25` at 09:00 Feb 4) and `D3` (`e27` at 10:00 Feb 4). Pairing `Order` with mandatory secondary lifecycles (`Package` for warehouse staging, `Delivery` for van transit, `Invoice` for receivables) prevents misreading `Order`'s 35.5% event duplication and interleaved concurrency as operational reality.

* **Strongest counterargument**:
  Because `P3` merges `O2`+`O3`, `P4` merges `O3`+`O4`, `D2` merges `O1`+`O2`+`O3`, and `V2` merges `O2`+`O3`, all four orders (`O1`–`O4`) belong to one tangled interaction component. Elevating `Order` even to "primary" status risks biasing stakeholders toward order-level KPIs when real operational bottlenecks (e.g., `D3`'s 22h 45m transit or `V2`'s split payment `M2`/`M3`) are governed at batch-object level—an argument favoring Position C.

* **Residual risk**:
  Analysts inspecting `Order`-rooted execution clusters (`cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Order")`) may double-count shared batch events (`e13`, `e14`, `e16`, `e17`, `e19`, `e22`, `e24`, `e25`, `e27`) across clusters unless cross-cluster overlap is explicitly audited.

* **Conditions under which this decision would change**:
  * **Shift to Position C (Reject a single lead object)** if warehouse/logistics replenishment or consolidated B2B periodic billing operates autonomously before/without `Order` creation (breaking `Order`'s 100% boundary bracket), or if analysis targets purely internal fleet/warehouse optimization rather than order-to-cash SLA.
  * **Shift to Position A (Single lead object `Order`)** only if business rules enforce strict 1:1:1:1 mapping (`1 Order = 1 Package = 1 Delivery = 1 Invoice = 1 Payment`), eliminating convergence and multi-item divergence.

## 6. PM4Py analysis outline

Because Position B establishes `Order` as primary lead object alongside secondary lifecycles (`Package`, `Delivery`, `Invoice`), the script below uses `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel` on `"Order"` (and contrasts it with `"Invoice"`), while programmatically quantifying duplication inflation, missing causal edges, declarative skeleton constraints, and cross-cluster event sharing.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# 1. Baseline OCEL summary and frequency inspection
print(ocel)

object_Types = ["Order", "Item", "Package", "Delivery", "Invoice", "Payment"]
activity_event_counts = ocel.events["ocel:activity"].value_counts()
object_type_counts = ocel.objects["ocel:type"].value_counts()

e2o_enriched = ocel.relations.merge(
    ocel.objects[["ocel:oid", "ocel:type"]], on="ocel:oid", how="left"
)
e2o_freq_matrix = (
    e2o_enriched.groupby(["ocel:activity", "ocel:type"])
    .agg(
        participating_events=("ocel:eid", "nunique"),
        unique_objects=("ocel:oid", "nunique"),
        total_object_links=("ocel:oid", "count"),
    )
    .unstack(fill_value=0)
)

# 2. Systematic flattening across all six object types & distortion diagnostics
flattened_logs = {}
dfg_models = {}
log_skeletons = {}
flattening_diagnostics = {}

total_raw_events = ocel.events["ocel:eid"].nunique()

for ot in object_Types:
    flat_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, ot)
    flattened_logs[ot] = flat_log

    dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(flat_log)
    dfg_models[ot] = {"dfg": dfg, "start": start_acts, "end": end_acts}

    # Declarative log skeleton captures order invariants unaffected by interleaving
    log_skeletons[ot] = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(flat_log)

    flat_df = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(flat_log)
    participating_eids = e2o_enriched.loc[
        e2o_enriched["ocel:type"] == ot, "ocel:eid"
    ].nunique()
    trace_event_rows = len(flat_df)

    flattening_diagnostics[ot] = {
        "covered_raw_events": participating_eids,
        "dropped_raw_events": total_raw_events - participating_eids,
        "flattened_event_rows": trace_event_rows,
        "duplication_ratio": round(trace_event_rows / max(participating_eids, 1), 3),
        "dispatch_to_confirm_freq": dfg.get(("Dispatch", "Confirm Delivery"), 0),
        "payment_to_confirm_freq": dfg.get(("Record Payment", "Confirm Delivery"), 0),
    }

flattening_audit_df = pd.DataFrame.from_dict(flattening_diagnostics, orient="index")

# Programmatic semantic check: verify concurrency erasure in Order vs. Delivery DFG
assert flattening_diagnostics["Order"]["dispatch_to_confirm_freq"] == 0
assert flattening_diagnostics["Delivery"]["dispatch_to_confirm_freq"] == 3

# 3. Native Object-Centric Discovery (OC-DFG, OTG, ET-OT, Interactions)
ocdfg_model = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg_model = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot_model = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)
interactions_summary_df = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)

# Extract multi-metric OC-DFG counts (events vs. unique objects vs. total objects)
ocdfg_event_edges = {
    ot: {edge: len(eids) for edge, eids in edges.items()}
    for ot, edges in ocdfg_model["edges"]["event_couples"].items()
}
ocdfg_unique_obj_edges = {
    ot: {edge: len(oids) for edge, oids in edges.items()}
    for ot, edges in ocdfg_model["edges"]["unique_objects"].items()
}
ocdfg_total_obj_edges = {
    ot: {edge: len(pairs) for edge, pairs in edges.items()}
    for ot, edges in ocdfg_model["edges"]["total_objects"].items()
}

# 4. Equivalent-execution clustering justified by Position B (Primary: Order; Secondary: Invoice)
order_execution_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Order")
invoice_execution_clusters = pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, "Invoice")

# Audit cross-cluster shared events so lead-object clusters are not treated as disjoint
order_cluster_eids = [
    set(sub_ocel.events["ocel:eid"].unique())
    for cluster_list in order_execution_clusters.values()
    for sub_ocel in cluster_list
]
shared_eids_across_orders = set()
for i in range(len(order_cluster_eids)):
    for j in range(i + 1, len(order_cluster_eids)):
        shared_eids_across_orders |= (order_cluster_eids[i] & order_cluster_eids[j])

cluster_overlap_report = {
    "num_order_archetypes": len(order_execution_clusters),
    "num_invoice_archetypes": len(invoice_execution_clusters),
    "shared_event_count_across_orders": len(shared_eids_across_orders),
    "shared_event_ids": sorted(shared_eids_across_orders),
}
```

## 7. Sensitivity and validation plan

1. **Down-weighting and removing copied shared events**:
   Assign each trace-event instance a fractional weight $w(e, o) = 1 / |\{o' \in \text{type}(o) : (e, o') \in E2O\}|$. In `Order` flattening, `e16` and `e24` receive weight $1/3$ per order (`O1`, `O2`, `O3`), while `e13`, `e14`, `e17`, `e19`, `e22`, `e25`, and `e27` receive weight $1/2$. Summing weights across `O1`–`O4` restores exact true activity counts (e.g., 4.0 `Pack Package`, 3.0 `Dispatch`, 3.0 `Issue Invoice`, 4.0 `Record Payment`, and 31.0 total events instead of 42). Separately, remove shared events (`w < 1`) to test DFG fragility: in `Order` flattening, dropping those 9 shared events severs `O2`, `O3`, and `O4` between `Add Item` (`e07`–`e10`) and `Close Order` (`e29`–`e31`), proving cross-order batching is structurally load-bearing rather than peripheral noise.

2. **Direct `E2O` relations versus explicitly propagated `O2O` variants**:
   Compare baseline `E2O` against two controlled `O2O` propagation schemes:
   * *Bookend upward/downward propagation*: Propagate `Create Order` (`e01`–`e04`) and `Close Order` (`e28`–`e31`) along `Order -> contains -> Item` and `Invoice -> bills -> Order`. This reveals pre-packing item lead times (`e01 -> e11`: 60m for `I11`) and post-settlement closure delays (`e21 -> e28`: 22h 30m idle lag on `O1`/`V1` vs. `e25 -> e29`: 2h 05m on `O2`/`V2`).
   * *Unscoped transitive propagation audit (negative control)*: Propagating all `O1` events to all `O1` items via `contains` attaches `I11` to `e16` (`D2`) and `I12` to `e15` (`D1`), creating false 2-delivery loops per item. This validates our rule that `O2O` propagation must never cross selective batching qualifiers (`packs`, `carries`).

3. **Lead-object rotation sensitivity**:
   Rotate the anchor across `Order`, `Package`, `Delivery`, and `Invoice` and track three stability indicators: (a) *Apparent rework rate*—self-loop transitions drop from 10/38 edges (26.3%) in `Order` to 0% in `Package`/`Delivery` and 1/4 edges (`e22->e25`) in `Invoice`; (b) *Transit SLA*—median and mean `Dispatch -> Confirm Delivery` shifts from undefined (0 direct edges) in `Order` to 4h 55m / 8h 54m in `Package` and 4h 55m / 10h 13m in `Delivery`; and (c) *Archetype diversity*—`cluster_equivalent_ocel` shifts from 4 unique singleton archetypes on `Order` (`O1`–`O4` all structurally distinct) to 2 archetypes on `Invoice` (`{V1, V3}` vs. `{V2}`) and 1 uniform archetype on `Delivery` (`{D1, D2, D3}`).

4. **Comparing `Events`, `Unique Objects`, and `Total Objects` in the OC-DFG**:
   Cross-tabulate all three OC-DFG metrics per activity and edge to separate convergence from divergence without flattening:
   * When **Unique Objects > Events** at an activity, *batch convergence* is occurring: at `Dispatch` (`Events = 3`), `Package` shows `Unique Objects = 4` (`P2` and `P3` merged in `e16`), and `Item` shows `Unique Objects = 6`.
   * When **Total Objects > Unique Objects**, *split divergence or repeated participation* is occurring: at `Dispatch`, `Order` shows `Events = 3`, `Unique Objects = 4` (`O1`–`O4`), and `Total Objects = 6` (`O1` and `O3` each dispatched twice across separate vans); on the `Invoice` self-loop `Record Payment -> Record Payment` (`e22 -> e25`), `Events = 1`, `Unique Invoices = 1` (`V2`), and `Unique Payments` across the node = 4 vs. `Total Invoice links` = 4.

5. **Declarative skeleton versus chronological DFG check**:
   Compare `discover_dfg` against `discover_log_skeleton` on the `Order` log: while the DFG fabricates `Record Payment -> Confirm Delivery` due to timestamp ordering, the log skeleton's `equivalence` constraints cleanly separate the `{Create Order, Issue Invoice, Close Order}` cardinality invariants from variable item/delivery multiplicities, confirming that cross-branch ordering between payment (`e21`, `e22`, `e25`, `e26`) and delivery confirmation (`e23`, `e24`, `e27`) is non-causal concurrency.
