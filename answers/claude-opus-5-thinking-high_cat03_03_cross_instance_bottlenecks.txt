# Cross-Instance Coupling of Six "Independent" Jobs

## 1. Interaction-graph description

**Node types.** `Job`, `Component`, `ComponentLot`, `Machine`, `Tool`, `Batch`, `Shipment`. Two types are missing and their absence is evidence: an *Actor* type (the supervisor who overrode the L7 hold) and a *ToolState* type (RACK-7's cleaning/quarantine condition, which has a lifecycle but is stored as an attribute).

**Edge families, kept strictly separate.** *O2O (declared, strongest):* `requires`, `contains`, `batches`, `consolidates`. *E2O (observed hyperedges):* `Batch Cure`@12:05 ~ {B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7, J1, J2, J3, C1, C2, C3} — nine objects in one event; `Machine Process`@11:45 ~ {J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A}; `Quarantine Lot`@10:20 / `Release Lot`@13:50 ~ {L7, C2, C5, J2, J5}. *Derived (weakest):* `blocks` (J3→J4 via FIX-A), `synchronizes-with` (J1↔J2↔J3 via B1), `waits-for-partner` (J1→J4 via S1), `gates` (L7→J5), `turnaround-couples` (B1→B2 via RACK-7), `serializes` (J5→J6 via CAL-3). None of these is logged; each rests on timestamps plus a feasibility assumption of a single tool instance held exclusively between acquire and release.

**Higher-order motifs (>2 object types).**

- *M1 Synchronization barrier* (Job×3, Batch, Machine, Tool, Component×3): {J1,J2,J3} ⋈ B1 ⋈ OVEN-1 ⋈ RACK-7; release time is `max` over members, not `mean`.
- *M2 Tool relay across batches* (Job, Tool, Machine, Job, Batch): J3 –holds– FIX-A –next held by– J4 –on– CNC-2 –feeds– B2. The two jobs share no batch, lot or shipment.
- *M3 Lot fan-out* (Lot, Component×2, Job×2, Batch×2): L7 ⊃ {C2,C5} → {J2,J5} → {B1,B2}, so one quality decision reaches two batches and two shipments.
- *M4 Consolidation join* (Shipment, Job×2, Batch×2): S1 ⋈ {J1∈B1, J4∈B2} re-couples what batching separated. *M5 Calibration-gated test* (Job×2, Machine, Tool, Shipment): {J5,J6} ⋈ TEST-1 ⋈ CAL-3 → S3, with a tool event that has no Job in scope between two tests.

**Compact edge list** (`[O]` observed, `[I]` inferred, `[C]` conjecture):

```
J1-requires->C1 ... J6-requires->C6 [O]   L7-contains->C2,C5 [O]   L8-contains->C1,C3 [O]
B1-batches->J1,J2,J3 [O]  B2-batches->J4,J5 [O]
S1-consolidates->J1,J4 [O]  S2->J2,J3 [O]  S3->J5,J6 [O]
E:BatchCure@12:05 ~ {B1,OVEN-1,RACK-7,J1,J2,J3,C1,C2,C3} [O]
E:MachineProcess@11:45 ~ {J4,C4,CNC-2,FIX-A} [O]
E:QuarantineLot@10:20 / E:ReleaseLot@13:50 ~ {L7,C2,C5,J2,J5} [O]
J3-holds(09:00-11:40)->FIX-A [O]   J3-blocks->J4 [I]
B1-occupies->RACK-7(->~12:55) [I]  RACK-7-delays->B2 [I]
L7-gates->J5 [I]                   L7-gates->J2 [C: refuted by override]
J5-serializes-before->J6 (CAL-3, 16:35-17:20) [I]
J4-delays->J1 via S1 [I]           J6-delays->J5's dispatch via S3 [I]
```

The decisive structural fact: the derived job–job graph — J1–J2–J3 (B1), J4–J5 (B2), J1–J4 (S1), J2–J3 (S2), J5–J6 (S3), J2–J5 (L7), J3→J4 (FIX-A), J5→J6 (CAL-3), {J1,J2,J3}→{J4,J5} (RACK-7) — is a **single connected component of diameter ≈3**, while the job flattening shows six disconnected traces.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Each figure is *attributable* waiting under a stated counterfactual, not proven causation; the counterfactuals are mutually inconsistent, so the numbers must not be summed.

**(1) B1 synchronization, J1 and J2.** J1: 08:10→12:05 = **3 h 55 min**; J2: 08:25→12:05 = **3 h 40 min**; J3: 10 min; aggregate 7 h 45 min. Had the cure started when the last member was ready (11:55), the total would be 7 h 15 min, so **30 min** is post-quorum loading and **7 h 15 min** is quorum waiting. *Counterfactual:* readiness is real, oven and rack were free from 08:10, and a partial-load cure is permissible. J2's figure is weakest: C2 was under quarantine 10:20–13:50, so J2 was arguably never legitimately ready.

**(2) FIX-A and J4.** Arrival at CNC-2 09:50, fixture released 11:40, processing starts 11:45. Upper bound **1 h 55 min**; tool-window bound **1 h 50 min**; *evidence-tight lower bound* **42 min**, the 10:05–10:47 interval in which CNC-2 was idle *and* FIX-A held. *Counterfactual:* a second qualified fixture exists and CNC-2's busy spans were not J4's own setup.

**(3) B2 — rack, not quorum.** J4 ready 13:10, B2 starts 14:50 = **1 h 40 min**: 13:10–14:35 = **1 h 25 min** with RACK-7 in cleaning and OVEN-1 idle; 5 min awaiting J5; 10 min load. Synchronization explains only ~5–15 min here and tool state ~85% — the same mechanism with opposite dominant causes in B1 and B2.

**(4) S1 consolidation and J1.** Packed 13:30, dispatched 19:25 = **5 h 55 min** dwell, against J4's 15 min. Under S2's 25-min or S3's 35-min turnaround, J1 would have left at 13:55–14:05, saving **5 h 20 – 5 h 30 min**. *Counterfactual:* carrier capacity, cost and cut-off permit a separate dispatch.

**(5) CAL-3 and J6.** Ready 16:05, tested 17:25 = **1 h 20 min**: 30 min with TEST-1 genuinely busy on J5, a capacity conflict rather than a CAL-3 effect; **45 min** of recalibration with TEST-1 idle, purely tool-attributable; 5 min handover. *Counterfactual:* a second unit or recalibration outside the test wave is valid; if calibration is mandatory after every test, the 45 min is irreducible.

**(6) L7 and J5.** Hold 10:20→13:50 = **3 h 30 min**, an upper bound: J5 became batch-ready only 50 min after release.

**(7) Invisible waiting for J1.** The job DFG's median happy path is 0.4+1.1+0.3+1.0+1.1 = **3.9 h**; J1 spans 08:10 to 19:25 = **11 h 15 min**, so at least **7 h 20 min (~65%)** is inter-object waiting the DFG represents nowhere.

**(8) The outlier test is near-vacuous.** For n = 6 the maximum possible standardized deviation is (n−1)/√n = **2.04**, so a 1.8 SD threshold is breachable only by a near-theoretical extreme. Worse, batching and consolidation are *common-mode* delays that inflate the mean, leaving cross-sectional z-scores blind to these mechanisms.

## 4. What flattening misses

**Job.** *Visible:* control flow, the 2-of-6 rework loop, activity medians. *Hidden:* one `Batch Cure` duplicated into three traces, so a shared barrier reads as three independent steps; lot events absent by construction. *Misleading:* "`Pack→Dispatch` takes 1.1 h" when J1 took 5 h 55 min — with n = 6 the median discards the bimodality.

**Machine.** *Visible:* utilization, setup sequences, gaps. *Hidden:* why idleness occurs. CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47 reads as slack but coincides with J4 blocked on FIX-A, and the 73-min maximum gap contradicts OVEN-1's 115-min window 12:55–14:50, so that statistic runs on a filtered event set. *Misleading:* "no machine bottleneck" — true and irrelevant.

**Batch.** *Visible:* formation, cure, unload, turnaround, readiness spread. *Hidden:* why members were late and what happens downstream; with two cases any DFG is meaningless. *Misleading:* "curing takes ~50 min, batching is efficient."

**Tool.** *Visible:* occupancy and turnaround intervals. *Hidden:* which job was blocked, since no `Wait for Tool` exists. *Misleading:* "FIX-A is used only 2 h 40 min, so tooling is not scarce" — an exclusive, non-preemptible resource with unsynchronized demand serializes work at low utilization.

**ComponentLot.** *Visible:* `Quarantine → Release` = 3 h 30 min. *Hidden:* which jobs were gated, and that J2 escaped via an unlogged override, so the lot view over-attributes and the job view under-attributes the same event. *Misleading:* "the hold delayed J2 and J5 by 3 h 30 min each."

**Shipment.** *Visible:* `Create → Load → Dispatch` in 1.1 h. *Hidden:* by explicit scope, the dwell of a packed job awaiting partners. *Misleading:* "outbound logistics is fast."

**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting.** Utilization measures how much of a horizon a *server* is busy, but these are not server-queue delays: they are *simultaneity* constraints. A cure needs job ∧ oven ∧ clean rack ∧ quorum, a dispatch needs both consolidated jobs, a test needs machine ∧ valid calibration, and a conjunction can stay unsatisfied for hours while every conjunct is mostly idle. Batching also cuts machine busy time by design, some idleness is policy-mandated, and upstream coupling appears as starvation rather than queue.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**A — FIX-A relay into a shipment** (Job×3, Tool×2, Machine, Batch, Shipment). *Temporal precedence:* J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4 reaches CNC-2 09:50; CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47; J4 processes from 11:45, is batch-ready 13:10, cures in B2 at 14:50, is packed 19:10; S1 dispatches 19:25 carrying J1, packed at 13:30. *Resource feasibility:* only one FIX-A is evidenced, the `Machine Process` hyperedge shows it is a hard input, and CNC-2 was free for 42 min. *Policy:* no fixture preemption; S1's composition fixed in advance. *Speculation:* that J3's hold caused J4's 1 h 50 min wait; J4 might instead have lacked C4 or an operator. Note the double coupling — J3 delays J1 once as a B1 co-member and again via FIX-A → J4 → S1.

**B — one lot decision, two batches, one shipment** (Lot, Component×2, Job×2, Batch×2, Shipment). *Temporal precedence:* `Quarantine Lot`@10:20 on L7 ⊃ {C2,C5}; B1 cures at 12:05 with C2 inside; `Release Lot`@13:50; J5 ready 14:40; B2 at 14:50; J5 tests 16:10–16:35. *Resource feasibility:* C2 and C5 are distinct parts sharing only a lot identity, so the coupling is informational, not material. *Policy:* lot-level rather than component-level holds, plus override authority in another system. *Speculation:* that the quarantine caused J5's late readiness. Identical evidence, opposite outcomes, because an exception was applied to J2 and not J5 — and that exception is invisible in the OCEL.

**C — a tool delays a job that had already tested** (Job×2, Machine, Tool, Shipment). *Temporal precedence:* J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20 with TEST-1 idle; J6 tests 17:25–17:50; S3 dispatches 35 min after J6's pack. *Resource feasibility:* one CAL-3 gates every `Final Test` regardless of machine capacity. *Policy:* recalibration between tests; S3's consolidation rule. *Speculation:* the 45 min transfers one-for-one to S3's dispatch and so harms **J5**, which had already tested — coupling with no shared batch, no shared lot and no contention at the moment of harm.

These three narratives plausibly form one chain — B1's late cure delays RACK-7's release, which gates B2, J4 and S1 — which is why single-mechanism attribution fails.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

**I1 — Bounded-wait batch rule (synchronization).** Cure when two jobs are ready or 60 min elapse. *Benefit:* up to 3 h for J1/J2. *Harms:* J3-type late arrivals slip to the next batch (+2 h), and more cycles mean more RACK-7 cleanings, so B2 can start later — the fix feeds mechanism #1. *Cost:* energy, minimum-thermal-load limits. *Perverse metric:* "average job waiting" falls while half-empty cures multiply. *Evaluation:* A/B on per-Batch fill rate against per-Job readiness→cure *and* readiness→dispatch, the latter being what matters.

**I2 — Duplicate the gating tools (tools).** A second qualified FIX-A and a second RACK-7, or rack cleaning moved off the critical path. *Benefit:* up to 1 h 50 min for J4 and 1 h 25 min for B2. *Harms:* CNC-2 moves toward saturation and earlier machining pushes jobs into the oven barrier sooner, so waiting migrates from tool to batch; if the rack quarantine is contamination control, compressing it is a quality risk. *Cost:* capital, fixture qualification, part-to-part variation. *Perverse metric:* utilization targets reward tool hoarding, and a "rack turnaround" target invites skipped cleaning. *Evaluation:* count blocked-job intervals (machine-free ∧ tool-held) per Tool object and oven idle attributable to rack state.

**I3 — Dwell-capped shipment policy (shipment).** Dispatch when complete *or* when the oldest packed job has waited 2 h; allow re-consolidation (J1 could have joined S2). *Benefit:* ~5 h 20 min for J1. *Harms:* freight cost, S2's schedule, and J2/J3 if S2 is held to absorb J1. *Perverse metric:* shipment-level on-time dispatch stays green while job dwell explodes, and the cap is gameable by splitting shipments. *Evaluation:* joint distribution of job dwell against shipment fill rate and cost per dispatch.

**I4 — Logging rather than operations (data).** Relate lot events to Jobs via Component→Job; add `Wait for Tool` / `Blocked By` events carrying the blocking object id; ingest the override with actor and reason; log RACK-7 and CAL-3 transitions as Tool events. *Benefit:* no throughput gain, but ranks 1–5 move from conjecture to measurement. *Harms/cost:* noisier DFGs, individual blame. *Perverse metric:* "% of waiting explained" rewards over-eager attribution. *Evaluation:* re-run this decomposition and track the unattributed-wait fraction (now ~65% for J1).

**I5 — Calibration scheduling (tools).** Pre-calibrate before the test wave or add a second unit. *Benefit:* 45–50 min for J6, transferring to S3 and hence J5. *Harms/cost:* per-test validity may be a regulatory requirement; a second unit adds inter-device bias. *Perverse metric:* TEST-1 utilization improves even as calibration integrity degrades. *Evaluation:* Job–Tool interaction counts plus a calibration-drift chart.

Portfolio spillover: I1, I2 and I5 all pull work earlier, so unless I3 accompanies them the saved hours reappear as dwell in front of S1. Component-level lot release (freeing C5 without C2) would help J5 by up to 3 h 30 min but trades against escape risk.

```python
               .groupby(["ocel:type", "ocel:type_2"])
               .agg(interactions=("ocel:oid", "size"),
                    distinct_pairs=("ocel:oid_2", "nunique"))
               .reset_index())

desc_graph    = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(ocel, graph_type="object_descendants")

# co-death of {J1, J4} under S1 is the fingerprint of consolidation waiting.

            "m_unique_objects": len(ocdfg["edges"]["unique_objects"][otype][(a, b)]), # METRIC 2
            "m_total_objects":  len(ocdfg["edges"]["total_objects"][otype][(a, b)]),
        })
edge_metrics = pd.DataFrame(rows)
edge_metrics["convergence"] = edge_metrics["m_total_objects"] / edge_metrics["m_unique_objects"]
# convergence > 1 flags duplication, e.g. Batch Cure fanned out to J1, J2, J3.
pm4py.view_ocdfg(ocdfg, annotation="frequency", act_metric="events")
pm4py.view_ocdfg(ocdfg, annotation="frequency", act_metric="unique_objects")

# ---------- 3. Flattenings, kept explicitly as partial views ---------------
perf = {}
for otype in ["Job", "Machine", "Batch", "Tool", "ComponentLot", "Shipment"]:
    log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, otype)
    perf[otype] = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)   # (dfg, start, end)
# Compare Job['Pack'->'Dispatch'] median with the per-job dwell from step 4:
# divergence proves the flattening hides consolidation waiting.

# ---------- 4. Waiting from OBJECT READINESS, never from machine gaps ------
rel = ocel.relations.copy()
EID, ACT, TS, OID, OTY = (ocel.event_id_column, ocel.event_activity,
                          ocel.event_timestamp, ocel.object_id_column,
                          ocel.object_type_column)

def barrier_wait(sync_activity, member_type, barrier_type):
    """Wait = barrier event time minus each member's own last preceding event.
    Job-side evidence only; machine idle time is never used as a proxy."""
    out = []
    for eid, grp in rel[rel[ACT] == sync_activity].groupby(EID):
        t0 = grp[TS].iloc[0]
        barriers = grp.loc[grp[OTY] == barrier_type, OID].tolist()
        for job in grp.loc[grp[OTY] == member_type, OID]:
            prior = rel[(rel[OID] == job) & (rel[TS] < t0)]
            if len(prior):
                ready = prior[TS].max()
                out.append({"barrier": barriers, "member": job, "ready": ready,
                            "sync": t0, "wait_h": (t0 - ready).total_seconds() / 3600})
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

batch_wait    = barrier_wait("Batch Cure", "Job", "Batch")     # B1: J1 3.92 h, J2 3.67 h
shipment_wait = barrier_wait("Dispatch",   "Job", "Shipment")  # S1: J1 ~5.92 h post-pack

# Tool blocking = machine free AND tool held, from acquire/release events only.
# If those events are missing the value is reported as NaN, never imputed.
tool_events = rel[rel[OTY] == "Tool"].sort_values(TS)

# ---------- 5. Features and coupling graph --------------------------------
feat_job, feat_names = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(
    ocel, "Job", include_obj_con=True, include_intercase=True)
nxg = pm4py.convert_ocel_to_networkx(ocel, variant="ocel_to_nx")
jobs = [n for n, d in nxg.nodes(data=True) if d.get("type") == "Job"]
coupling = nx.Graph(); coupling.add_nodes_from(jobs)
for a in jobs:                                  # jobs sharing any non-Job object
    for b in jobs:
        if a < b and set(nxg.neighbors(a)) & set(nxg.neighbors(b)):
            coupling.add_edge(a, b)
print(list(nx.connected_components(coupling)))  # expect one component of six
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked explanation (tool serialization and the chain built on it):**

1. Evidence that FIX-A exists in several qualified copies, or that CNC-2's 10:47–11:45 occupancy was J4's own setup, collapsing the 42-minute lower bound toward zero.
2. A record showing CNC-2 was reserved 10:05–10:47 for changeover or for work outside this log, making "idle with tool held" a scoping artifact.
3. A specification fixing RACK-7's cleaning duration, reclassifying B2's 1 h 25 min as mandated process time; likewise a standard requiring CAL-3 recalibration after every `Final Test`.
4. Confirmation that J1's dispatch is pinned by S1 regardless of upstream change, so any upstream fix yields zero dispatch benefit and demotes both the tool and batch mechanisms for that job.
5. Readiness timestamps proving to be planning flags; J2's 08:25 already conflicts with the 10:20 quarantine, which would invalidate calculations 1 and 3.
6. History showing B1-style quorum waits are rare one-offs, or a shift calendar with no oven operator before 12:00 — either explains the morning with no coupling at all.

**Fields or event types that would discriminate among the hypotheses:**

1. **Resource capacity attributes:** oven slot count and minimum load, and the number of FIX-A, RACK-7 and CAL-3 instances — this alone separates policy barriers from physical serialization.
2. **Explicit blocking events:** `Wait for Tool`, `Enter Queue`, `Blocked By` carrying the blocking object id, turning inferred holds into observations.
3. **Tool state as Tool-object events:** RACK-7 `Cleaning Start/End`; CAL-3 `Calibration Due/Start/End` with trigger reason.
4. **E2O qualifiers with provenance:** relations labelled `input | output | resource | blocked-by` plus source system, so lot events reach J2 and J5 without over-attributing.
5. **Override events inside the OCEL:** the J2 decision with actor, justification and lot state — the sole reason J2 and J5 diverged.
6. **Lot disposition detail:** quarantine reason code, sampling results, and per-component release decisions.
7. **Shipment planning data:** `Job Assigned to Shipment` events, carrier cut-offs, promised dates and cost per dispatch.
8. **Planned versus actual timestamps** and the cure calendar, separating quorum waiting from fixed-slot scheduling.

Temporal succession has been treated as necessary but not sufficient: FIX-A's release at 11:40 precedes J4's start at 11:45, but only the coincidence of a free machine with a held tool is evidence, and even that assumes FIX-A is unique. Machine capacity ranks last because every idle window falls where a job was waiting — the signature of a constraint elsewhere.
