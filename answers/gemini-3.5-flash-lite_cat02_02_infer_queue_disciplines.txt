# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

## Queue QA
*   **Supporting Cases:** Job A03 arrives at 08:08 with an appointment at 08:20. Even though Job A02 arrived earlier (08:04) and had a longer estimate, A03 is started exactly at its appointment time (08:20). Similarly, Job A06 arrives at 08:29 with a 09:15 appointment and is initiated at 09:20 (within the 5-minute arrival scan/lateness tolerance).
*   **Contradicting Cases / Anomalies:** Job A04 has Priority 1 and arrives at 08:13, but is delayed until 08:58 because Resource A1 was occupied by A02 and A03, and underwent calibration from 08:52 to 08:58. Furthermore, Job A08 (Priority 1, arrival 08:40) waits until 09:41. These highlight non-preemption: high priority jobs do not interrupt ongoing lower-priority work.

## Queue QB
*   **Supporting Cases:** Job B02 (Priority 1) arrives at 09:14 and immediately causes B01 to stop at 09:16 (`interruption_reason`: first stop: urgent arrival), matching preemptive priority mechanics. Job B05 (Priority 1) also cuts in at 10:00.
*   **Contradicting Cases / Anomalies:** Job B07 arrives at 10:08 with an appointment at 10:30. The note states "appointment not honored on time"; B07 starts at 10:56 after B06 completes. This contradiction shows that while priorities and preemptions dominate, appointment scheduling breaks down under heavy queue contention.

## Queue QC
*   **Supporting Cases:** Job C02 arrives at 10:03 and starts immediately at 10:10 (when C1 becomes available) with an `est_min` of 8—the shortest among the initial backlog (C01: 36, C03: 14, C04: 22). Job C06 arrives at 10:25 with `est_min` 6 and is executed immediately at 11:34 following C05.
*   **Contradicting Cases / Anomalies:** Job C01 is the oldest job (arrival 10:00, deadline 13:00) but is executed *last* among the initial backlog (11:40–12:16). This severely violates FIFO in favor of SPT, except that Job C05 (Priority 1, deadline 10:50) is prioritized at 10:54 because its deadline was critically at risk, proving a hybrid deadline/SLA safeguard rule.

## Queue QD
*   **Supporting Cases:** Job D02 (firmware) routes to D-F, D03 (electrical) routes to D-F, and D01/D04 (mechanical) route to D-M. Skill requirements precisely match resource qualifications.
*   **Contradicting Cases / Anomalies:** Job D10 (Priority 1, electrical, est 12) arrives at 11:25 but waits until 12:16 because D-F (the only fully qualified firmware+electrical resource) was unavailable, and D-X was busy with D06 and D07. This is explained by resource availability constraints and skill matching rather than pure priority dispatch.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

## Ambiguous Episode 1: Queue QC at 10:10 (SPT vs. Appointment/Deadline Priority)
*   **Competing Explanations:**
    1.  *Shortest Processing Time (SPT):* Backlog contains C01 (36m), C02 (8m), C03 (14m), C04 (22m). Selecting C02 aligns with minimum expected processing time.
    2.  *Deadline/SLA Urgency:* Backlog contains deadlines at 13:00, 14:00, 12:30, and 14:30. C03 has the tightest deadline (12:30) among non-P1 jobs.
*   **Next-Job Prediction:**
    *   *Under SPT:* C06 (est 6) should be prioritized over C07 (est 18) if lengths dictate.
    *   *Under Deadline Urgency:* C07 (deadline 12:00) should be scheduled before C01 (deadline 13:00).
*   **Discriminated Observable:** The explicit *time-to-deadline slack* ($Deadline - Arrival - est\_min$) versus raw *est_min*. If jobs with longer processing times but tighter slack are chosen over shorter jobs with distant deadlines, Deadline Urgency dominates.

## Ambiguous Episode 2: Queue QA at 08:20 (Appointment vs. SPT/Priority)
*   **Competing Explanations:**
    1.  *Appointment Scheduling:* A03 arrives at 08:08 with an explicit appointment at 08:20 and is executed immediately.
    2.  *SPT / Priority:* A03 has est_min = 10, whereas A02 has est_min = 20. Both are Priority 3. SPT would also select A03.
*   **Next-Job Prediction:**
    *   *Under Appointment Scheduling:* A job with a later arrival and longer duration *must* be scheduled if its appointment window arrives, overriding SPT.
    *   *Under SPT:* Duration strictly governs order regardless of clock time.
*   **Discriminated Observable:** Queue entry timestamp and dispatch decision log showing whether dispatch waited idle for an appointment window to open or pulled a shorter job early. In QA, A03 was held or timed for 08:20 despite resource availability.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

1.  **Delayed Arrival Scans (Data Quality):** Arrival scans can be delayed by up to two minutes. This explains why jobs appearing to arrive simultaneously or out of order in the log actually arrived in correct FIFO sequence. *(Plausibility: High; explicitly noted in prompt).* **Impact:** Softens apparent FIFO violations in QA and QB.
2.  **Resource Calibration and Availability Exceptions:** Planned exceptions (e.g., A1 calibration 08:52–08:58; C1 prior job ending 10:10; D-M available 11:00–12:30) force idle time and block eligible jobs. *(Plausibility: High; explicitly logged).* **Impact:** Accounts for forced waiting times of high-priority jobs (e.g., A04, D10).
3.  **Strategic or Noisy Duration Estimates (`est_min`):** Dispatchers rely on estimates that may be inaccurate. If dispatchers use biased estimates, true SPT policies will appear violated. *(Plausibility: Medium).* **Impact:** Distorts apparent adherence to SPT in Queue QC.
4.  **Undocumented Hybrid Preemption Triggers:** Dispatchers may permit preemption only for Priority 1 jobs *and* when remaining work exceeds threshold durations, or under dispatcher whim (as noted in QB: "dispatcher reassignment"). *(Plausibility: Medium).* **Impact:** Creates hybrid rules that mimic erratic human dispatching rather than pure mathematical models.

# 6. Additional event data needed

To resolve remaining ambiguities across the queues, the following fields should be captured in an enhanced event log:

*   `queue_entry_time`: Exact timestamp when the physical item entered the logical queue buffer (independent of barcode scan time).
*   `dispatch_decision_time`: Timestamp when the resource actively selected the job from the queue.
*   `eligibility_set`: Snapshot array of job IDs present, waiting, and skill-qualified in the queue at the exact `dispatch_decision_time`.
*   `resource_state`: Operational status of the resource (idle, busy, calibrating, on break) at decision time.
*   `appointment_tolerance_window`: Permitted early/late tolerance (in minutes) for scheduled appointments.
*   `current_estimated_remaining_time`: Dynamic remaining work estimate at preemption or interruption points (crucial for QB).
*   `preemption_reason_code`: Standardized taxonomy for work stoppage (e.g., *URGENT_ARRIVAL*, *SKILL_MISMATCH*, *DISPATCHER_OVERRIDE*, *BREAK*).
*   `sla_penalty_weight`: Numeric cost function or penalty associated with missing job deadlines in QC.

# 7. Final synthesis

*   **Most Identifiable Queue:** **Queue QB**. It exhibits clear, textbook preemptive priority behavior. Priority 1 jobs (B02, B05) immediately interrupt ongoing work with explicit interruption reasons, making the operational discipline unmistakable.
*   **Least Identifiable Queue:** **Queue QA**. It sits at an observational crossroads between strict FIFO, appointment scheduling, and non-preemptive priority. Because appointment times frequently align with natural queue progression and 2-minute arrival scan fuzziness masks exact ordering, distinguishing pure appointment scheduling from priority/FIFO hybrids is highly uncertain.
*   **Most Dangerous Inferred Policy:** **Queue QC (Inferred as SPT)**. Inferring a pure Shortest Processing Time (SPT) policy from QC is dangerous because the short jobs that execute first also happen to be the ones with the most endangered deadlines (e.g., C02, C05, C06). Assuming an SPT rule would lead management to ignore critical deadline/SLA constraints, risking severe service level failures when longer jobs with tight deadlines arrive.
