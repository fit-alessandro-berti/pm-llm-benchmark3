## 1. Executive causal assessment

My provisional verdict is **mainly confounded**, with **75/100 confidence** that case mix and operating conditions explain much of Orion's adverse aggregate comparison. This confidence concerns that explanation, not identification of the sign or magnitude of an assignment effect.

Orion's mean cycle time is 21.96 hours versus Nova's 15.24, a crude disadvantage of 6.71 hours. Yet Orion is faster within every recorded complexity stratum: by 2.14 hours for low, 2.21 for medium, and 3.43 for high complexity. Standardizing to the pooled complexity distribution gives 17.14 hours for Orion and 19.65 for Nova. This Simpson's-paradox pattern, together with preferential routing of difficult cases to Orion, is the strongest evidence against blaming Orion from the aggregate mean.

The strongest challenge to a purely confounded explanation is Orion's consistently shorter within-stratum waiting and review durations: a beneficial operational effect is plausible. However, broad complexity labels leave residual risk differences, Orion has just one day case, and only completed cases appear. These limitations also leave a harmful effect possible if selection or unmeasured differences mask it. Appeals provide little discrimination: each group has one.

Moving half of Orion's cases may succeed if Nova has spare qualified capacity and relieves a real Orion queue. It may fail if complexity, night coverage, and mandatory checks dominate elapsed time. It could backfire by overloading Nova or reducing suitable reviewer access. At the historical case counts, transferring seven cases would increase Nova's volume by about 44% (7/16), before accounting for their greater difficulty. Neither crude nor standardized means predict that capacity-changing policy's effect.

Define a total assignment contrast as E[T(Orion) - T(Nova)] for a specified population of cases eligible for both groups, under a specified staffing and routing regime. Allow queueing, reviewer access, repeat review, and subsequent handoffs to respond to assignment. A controlled direct contrast holds selected mediators at the same feasible values; a residual group coefficient after adjusting for service and rework is not automatically that contrast. Mediated effects concern specific paths, such as assignment changing compliance scheduling, then waiting, then completion. Neither natural direct/indirect effects nor their decomposition are identified here.

The proposed policy's estimand is different: the change in cohort completion performance when 50% of future Orion-bound cases are reassigned. Shared queues create interference, so cases remaining at Orion and cases already at Nova may also be affected. The label is simultaneously an assignable treatment, a proxy for routing decisions, and a bundle of operational mechanisms. A meaningful experiment must specify which parts of that bundle change.

## 5. Competing causal explanations and falsification tests

Ranks indicate investigative priority, not estimated probabilities. Multiple explanations can operate together. With only 30 cases, matching is a support diagnostic; it will discard substantial data or yield fragile comparisons. An elaborate propensity model cannot repair absent overlap.

Report uncertainty and the excluded population whenever restricting to overlap; do not generalize a low-complexity pilot to uncertified or unsupported high-risk work.

## 6. Intervention and learning plan

The phases below are proposed designs, not actions already performed. Define service, queue, and independently audited quality safety limits before launch, using the committee's actual obligations and staffing constraints. The 30 cases cannot establish safe numerical limits or an adequate pilot sample size.

| phase | intervention | target mechanism | assignment unit | safeguards | process-mining measurements | causal learning value | stop or rollback rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1: Establish cohort and clocks | Maintain routing while logging every incoming case, route decision/eligibility, transfers, reviewer qualifications, pre-routing backlog, and activity start/end/pause events | Separate baseline selection, actual queueing, service, compliance, and request-response delays | Every arrival; measurement rollout by operating unit | Verify case IDs, clock synchronization, lifecycle semantics, follow-up maturity, and independent correctness audits; preserve mandatory checks | Arrival/assignment timestamps; open inventory and abandonment; queue-entry/start intervals; compliance intervals; first/repeat review; Request Info response intervals; variants and conformance | Defines treatment and post-assignment outcomes; exposes completion selection and usable overlap; establishes variance for precision/power planning | Pause causal comparisons and correct instrumentation if clocks, group histories, or cohort linkage are unreliable. Do not infer success from missing open cases. |
| 2: Queue/compliance capacity trial | Randomize additional certified compliance/queue support across comparable operating blocks while retaining the routing rule; document capacity changes and carryover | Congestion and availability around review/compliance, rather than the resource-group label | Queue or staffing pool by shift block, with queue carryover tracked and suitable washout/follow-up | Required checks remain mandatory; staff meet certification requirements; compare similar demand blocks and monitor neighboring queues | Segment-specific wait distributions, queue work-hours, utilization, backlog, completed-check rate, handoffs, valid closures, audited correctness | Tests whether changing capacity reduces delay at fixed routing; separates capacity mechanisms from mere label association | Withdraw an unsafe allocation for any missed mandatory check, qualification breach, or prespecified backlog/SLA/quality safety-limit breach; provide normal rescue capacity and retain assigned-block analysis. |

Do not advance to 50% on an inconclusive case-level result. |

Primary routing analysis should retain every randomized case and compare assignment groups without adjusting for realized waiting, service, rework, or trace. Estimate time to valid closure from assignment, alongside full-cycle performance. Keep abandoned cases visible as unsuccessful closures/competing outcomes; do not silently censor them as if their remaining time were unrelated to treatment. Censoring methods require defensible assumptions. Use block-aware inference for block trials and prespecified baseline adjustment for precision. Mechanism outcomes are secondary, and quality needs enough mature follow-up and audited cases to assess an operationally meaningful quality margin.

**Final recommendation.** Do not authorize the blanket 50% transfer on these aggregate means. The data provide stronger evidence of adverse case composition than of Orion inefficiency, and their within-complexity advantage points mainly toward waiting mechanisms. Neither observation establishes what Nova would do with a large inflow of Orion's harder cases.

Begin with complete-cohort instrumentation and a targeted trial of queue/compliance capacity. Then enroll a small, qualified Orion-bound stream in balanced randomized routing while preserving required checks, documenting capacity, and auditing quality. Evaluate every assigned case, including open and abandoned cases, and distinguish elapsed waiting from total review effort. Treat appeals as supplementary evidence rather than the sole quality gate.

If reassignment improves post-assignment completion without breaching queue or quality safeguards, test larger diversion fractions across shared operating blocks before approaching 50%. If additional compliance or night capacity explains the benefit, prioritize that mechanism. Any expansion should apply only to case types Nova can safely handle and for which the pilot supports inference; unsupported high-risk routes require their own evidence.

def make_sequence_log(cases):
    """Observed order only: no observed or fabricated timestamps are added."""
    log = EventLog()

    return log

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Residual within-complexity risk:** Orion and Nova may handle different cases even within the three labels. **Most valuable additional field:** the routing engine's pre-assignment case-risk score, recorded before group choice.
2. **Which operational segment creates the delay:** recorded waiting mixes review queues and compliance-related waiting, while service aggregates repeated review. **Most valuable additional field:** an activity-lifecycle timestamp stream identifying queue entry, start, pause/resume, and completion for each activity occurrence.
3. **How missing open/abandoned cases change the comparison:** the completed sample may selectively exclude slow or unsuccessful assignments. **Most valuable design change:** follow a fixed incoming cohort through a common horizon, retaining open and abandoned cases.
4. **Whether faster handling preserves substantive quality:** sparse appeals may miss errors and unequal follow-up. **Most valuable design change:** blinded, independent adjudication-quality audits of a representative sample from every assigned arm, including unresolved cases where assessable.
5. **How a 50% transfer changes shared capacity and spillovers:** a small case-level contrast cannot predict system behavior under substantially different loads. **Most valuable design change:** a randomized routing-saturation experiment across connected operating blocks, including outcomes for incumbent Nova and remaining Orion cases.
