   *Evidence that would support this:* original justification documents with earlier dates, spreadsheet upload metadata, migration logs, `entered_by` values, and evidence that the operational approval occurred before the log entry.

2. **Shared-account or assistant-entry artifact.**  
   The same `org:resource` may hide different actual people. An assistant may enter the justification under a manager’s or approver’s shared account.  
   *Evidence that would support this:* assistant `entered_by` fields, access logs, delegation records, and system audit trails showing who created the record.  
   *Evidence that would refute this:* no assistant attribution, shared credentials, or inability to identify the actual person who entered the justification.

**Pattern E: Vendor Check + Change Vendor Bank Details by same person**

1. **Role design in vendor master team.**  
   VendorMaster staff may be authorized to perform both vendor checks and bank-detail changes. This may not be a prohibited four-eyes violation under the organization’s role design.  
   *Evidence that would support this:* role matrix, segregation-of-duties configuration, vendor master SOPs, and evidence of separate bank validation controls.  
   *Evidence that would refute this:* absence of bank validation, same person also influencing payment approval, or repeated changes for high-risk vendors without verification.

2. **Operational efficiency in low-volume vendor master work.**  
   With VendorMaster_Lena and VendorMaster_Marc handling relatively low volumes, the same person may naturally perform adjacent steps.  
   *Evidence that would support this:* workload records, process documentation, and compensating controls such as periodic vendor master reviews.  
   *Evidence that would refute this:* lack of periodic review, unexplained changes to sensitive fields, or concentration of changes in one vendor family without business rationale.

**Pattern F: Bot_Match  AP_Kim  Bot_Match loops**

1. **Automated exception feedback.**  
   Bot_Match may send exceptions to AP_Kim, who resolves them and returns the case to the bot for reprocessing.  
   *Evidence that would support this:* exception codes, bot logs, reprocessing timestamps, and standard AP exception handling procedures.  
   *Evidence that would refute this:* manual events incorrectly recorded as bot events, or repeated exceptions that are always resolved in favor of one vendor without explanation.

2. **Mapping-table error between bot and human resources.**  
   The `legacy_erp` mapping error can make bot and human resources appear identical. This can create artificial four-eyes or loop patterns.  
   *Evidence that would support this:* source-system mapping tables, bot account definitions, and corrected logs after mapping repair.  
   *Evidence that would refute this:* inability to distinguish bot from human activity after remediation, or human approvals recorded under bot IDs without explanation.

```python
import pandas as pd
```

```python
def four_eyes_violations(df, act1, act2, actor_col="effective_actor"):
    first = df[df["concept:name"] == act1][
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", actor_col]
    ].rename(columns={"time:timestamp": "ts_first", actor_col: "actor_first"})

    second = df[df["concept:name"] == act2][
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", actor_col]
    ].rename(columns={"time:timestamp": "ts_second", actor_col: "actor_second"})

    merged = first.merge(second, on="case:concept:name", how="inner")

    # Same human actor, correct order, exclude obvious bots
    violations = merged[
        (merged["ts_first"] <= merged["ts_second"]) &
        (merged["actor_first"] == merged["actor_second"]) &
        (~merged["actor_first"].str.startswith("Bot_"))
    ]

    return violations

po_violations = four_eyes_violations(

)

)
```

```python
def retrospective_justification_violations(df, holidays=None):
    overrides = df[df["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"][
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "effective_actor"]
    ].rename(columns={
        "time:timestamp": "override_ts",
        "effective_actor": "override_actor"
    })

    justifications = df[df["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"][
        ["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "effective_actor"]
    ].rename(columns={
        "time:timestamp": "retro_ts",
        "effective_actor": "retro_actor"
    })

    merged = overrides.merge(justifications, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

    # Keep only justifications occurring after the override
    merged = merged[
        merged["retro_ts"].isna() |
        (merged["retro_ts"] >= merged["override_ts"])
    ]

    # For each override, keep the first subsequent justification
    merged = (
        merged
        .sort_values(["case:concept:name", "override_ts", "retro_ts"])
        .groupby(["case:concept:name", "override_ts"])
        .head(1)
        .reset_index(drop=True)
    )

    # Working-day calculation; add organization holidays if available
    merged["working_days_late"] = np.busday_count(
        merged["override_ts"].dt.date.values,
        merged["retro_ts"].dt.date.values,
        holidays=holidays
    )

    merged["missing_justification"] = merged["retro_ts"].isna()
    merged["late_justification"] = merged["working_days_late"] > 3
    merged["same_person_justification"] = (
        merged["override_actor"] == merged["retro_actor"]
    )

    merged["violation"] = (
        merged["missing_justification"] |
        merged["late_justification"] |
        merged["same_person_justification"]
    )

    return merged[merged["violation"]]

retro_violations = retrospective_justification_violations(df)
```

For shared accounts, repeat the check using `entered_by` only, excluding cases where `org:resource` is shared but `entered_by` identifies a different person.

- No independent bank verification for V-77x bank-detail changes.

- Payments were released shortly after a bank-detail change to the new account without vendor confirmation.

- Manual_upload batches lack change tickets, maker-checker records, or upload approval metadata.
- Multiple V-77x payments are just below approval thresholds, suggesting possible splitting.
- Retrospective justifications have identical timestamps, no underlying documents, and were created only after quarter close.
- Shared account `Approver_Gita` has no reliable `entered_by` trail, preventing identification of the actual approver.
- Emergency flags are present but emergency requests, override reasons, or goods/service receipts are missing.
- Goods receipt or invoice match evidence is absent for high-value emergency cases.
- Access logs show bank-detail changes or payment approvals from unusual devices, locations, or outside expected working patterns without explanation.
- Bot/human mapping errors cannot be corrected, making it impossible to distinguish automated from human approval.
- Duplicate payments, credit notes, vendor complaints, or payment reversals are concentrated in the V-77x cluster.

**Evidence that would reduce concern**

- Valid mass-maintenance tickets explain the V-77x bank-detail changes, including bulk-upload IDs and approval records.
- Independent bank verification exists for each V-77x change, such as vendor callback confirmation, bank letter, or second-person validation.
- `entered_by` and `approved_by` show different individuals for bank changes and payment approvals, even if `org:resource` suggests a close pair.
- Emergency cases have complete documentation: emergency request, override reason, on-call authority, retrospective justification by a different person, and goods/service receipt.
- Retrospective justifications are supported by underlying documents with dates showing that the operational decision occurred before the batch upload.
- The shared-account issue is explained by delegation and assistant entry, with reliable assistant-level audit trails.
- The `legacy_erp` mapping error is confirmed and corrected, removing false four-eyes violations involving Bot_PO.
- Vendor family V-77x payments match invoices, goods receipts, and contracts, with no duplicate payment findings.
- Amounts are within delegated limits and do not cluster just below thresholds.
- Routine buyer/approver pairs show normal variant behavior, no unusual vendor concentration, and no increase in high-risk manual exceptions.
- Periodic vendor master reviews and access reviews show no unauthorized changes.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

During the review, the team identified several control exceptions in the procurement-to-payment log. The most concerning subset involves vendor family V-77x, where Change Vendor Bank Details and Payment Approval events were performed by the same or closely linked resources, often through manual_upload and with short elapsed times. This pattern does not establish wrongdoing, but it weakens segregation of duties and creates a higher exposure to payment redirection. It should be reviewed first.

A second set of exceptions relates to emergency handling. Buyer_Carla with Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan with Manager_Hugo show rapid approvals, override activity, and subcontracting-style loops. Many of these cases have emergency_flag=True, medical or facilities context, and low vendor concentration. The pattern may reflect legitimate urgent operations and informal specialization, but it also indicates that normal approval controls were bypassed or compressed.

The log contains material data-quality limitations. Some retrospective justifications were batch-migrated with identical timestamps, a shared approver account obscures individual attribution, and a legacy mapping error can make bot and human resources appear identical. These issues reduce the reliability of resource-level conclusions until underlying audit trails are reconciled.

The recommended next step is targeted testing, not an accusation-based investigation. Audit should sample V-77x bank-change payments, verify independent bank validation, inspect entered_by/approved_by fields, source-system upload batches, vendor confirmations, and payment limits. For emergency cases, audit should verify override reasons, on-call authority, retrospective justifications by a different person within three working days, and supporting goods or service evidence. If exceptions persist after data-quality remediation, control design and monitoring should be strengthened.
