## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

**Feasibility precedes valuation.** Rules 6, 7, 9, 10, 11 are hard constraints, not penalty terms. They partition the action space; expected-value comparison happens only inside the conformant set. This makes A and B *lexically dominated*, not merely low-scoring (§4).

**Symmetric, monetizable quantities compared in expectation.** Deadline value (€20–35k) enters as probability-weighted: e.g., PLAN's expected SLA capture  0.5–0.75 × €20–35k  €10–26k, against P901 spillover  0.12–0.28 × L(P901)  €3–10k if P901 is comparably sized. These are commensurable; ranges, not points, drive the ranking.

**Asymmetric regulatory harm is treated as a heavy-tailed constraint proxy,** not a line item: €100k–500k plus non-monetary consequences (supervisory scrutiny, licensing, individual accountability, reputational capital that cannot be repurchased). Any option whose tail includes a regime breach is stochastically dominated for any decision-maker with even mild prudence — the trade "€30k sure gain vs. 5% × €500k + tail" is not a trade.

**False rejection vs. prolonged hold vs. deadline loss.** All three are reversible-or-compensable in principle, unlike unlawful execution. Because approval remains plausible (0.55–0.75) and 90 minutes remain, premature foreclosure (F) spends option value the process explicitly grants. The control architecture's entire purpose is to place the irreversible decision with an independent human; the planner should preserve that decision point, not preempt it.

**Observational vs. interventional.** SLA-breach 0.48–0.64 and approval 0.55–0.75 are observational. Selecting S2 *intervenes*: P884's covariates change (fresh reviewer, accelerated queue) and P901's distribution shifts — the numbers do not transport unchanged. The ranking therefore leans on structural timing arithmetic and conformance logic more than on point forecasts.
