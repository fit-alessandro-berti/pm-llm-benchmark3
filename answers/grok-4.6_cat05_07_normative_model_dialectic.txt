## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated (established by the materials without needing a further policy choice)**

- M0 is a control-flow skeleton: `Register_Supplier` → concurrent `{Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review}` → XOR(`Manual_Procurement_Approval` → `Activate_Supplier`, `Reject_Supplier`) → `Notify_Requester` → `Close_Case`. It does not encode risk, country, group membership, amounts, dates, identity, or four-eyes.
- CS-9 (N1) is the only controlled, formally approved standing standard. It requires sanctions and quality before activation, manual procurement approval before activation, and distinct humans for requester and manual approver. It is silent on reactivation and ambiguous on whether rejected pre-screen leads are M0 cases.
- PD-3 (N2) is a time-boxed steering-committee decision (2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31), not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. It allows `Auto_Approve` for a LOW-risk DE supplier already in an approved corporate group; requires sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation; allows `Quality_Review` up to two business days after provisional activation. A Compliance observer contemporaneously called it a “monitored exception,” then later denied having approved a weakening of CS-9.
- FD-7 (N3) is a written emergency directive with automatic expiry (2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59). It permits provisional activation before quality and manual approval, still requires sanctions before activation, caps spend at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, and requires daily monitoring plus quality within two business days.
- From 2026-07-01, notification largely left the onboarding log (N4): about 70% mirrored; missingness rose from 2% to 29%. Stable `notification_id` links are stronger than mutable text fields.
- `Request_More_Info` is used in practice, regarded as desirable by Compliance, and is absent from M0 (N5). No permanent auto-approval amendment exists.
- SUP-701 is a complete, high-risk, four-eyes-respecting M0 execution. SUP-703 is an ineligible high-risk “pilot” activation with a EUR 180,000 first purchase. SUP-708 activated a high-risk supplier before sanctions with no overlay. Internal Audit flagged SUP-703 and SUP-708 as potentially severe.
- Aggregate: 312 June–July `Auto_Approve` cases (289 appear PD-3-eligible, 14 clearly ineligible, 9 missing eligibility attributes); 47 `Request_More_Info` cases with no activation before requested evidence arrived; six first-week-of-August post-pilot `Auto_Approve` cases.

**Disputed (turns on authority, scope, identity, or logging)**

- Whether PD-3 lawfully displaced CS-9’s manual-approval rule even for eligible suppliers, given the later Compliance statement and the instrument’s form (minutes/email, not a controlled amendment).
- Whether “the flood still factually existed” on 2026-07-13 can substitute for a written FD-7 extension (SUP-705).
- Whether anticipated permanence of the pilot is an authority source (SUP-714 and the six August cases).
- Whether M0/CS-9 govern rejected pre-screen leads (SUP-709) and reactivations with a still-valid quality certificate (SUP-710).
- Whether a CRM row is proof of `Notify_Requester` when the onboarding log is empty: stable `notification_id` (SUP-702) versus a mutable text link entered two days later (SUP-706).
- Whether `QA_Clearance` is the same activity as `Quality_Review` before the mapping was approved (SUP-713).
- Whether alias-collapsed identity (SUP-712) and an undisclosed spousal/same-line relationship (SUP-715) are in-scope onboarding noncompliance or separate HR/COI findings.
- Whether extra `Register_Supplier` rows with the same source transaction ID are a second business event (SUP-711).

**Unproved**

- That automated master-data validation actually ran before PD-3 activations (required by PD-3, absent from the alphabet).
- That CS-9’s four-eyes rule was systematically tested; only SUP-712 is evidenced as an alias collision.
- That spouse-in-line is prohibited, as opposed to disclosure-required (SUP-715).
- That SUP-706’s CRM text refers to the same case notification at the required process point.
- That post-expiry operators knew FD-7/PD-3 had lapsed and chose to ignore them, as opposed to treating them as still-operative BAU.

**Burden of proof**

- **To declare NONCOMPLIANT:** the case must be in the scope of the governing norm; the observed behavior must violate a requirement that was effective at the event timestamps; and the deviation must not be explained by (i) a written overlay whose eligibility predicates and residual controls are met on contemporaneous evidence, or (ii) a demonstrated log/model artifact. Where identity, linkage, or eligibility attributes are missing, the finding is not noncompliance by default — it is indeterminate — unless the missing field is itself a required control (e.g., eligibility snapshot for an exception path). Informal practice, desirability, and “the emergency still felt real” do not carry this burden.
- **To accept a JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION:** there must be written authority with a defined population, residual controls, and time window; contemporaneous evidence that the case was inside that population; and evidence that residual controls actually ran (sanctions-before-activate; quality within two business days; spend cap and monitoring for FD-7). A later recantation of PD-3 is a governance defect in how the exception was authorized; it does not, by itself, convert every in-window eligible execution into operator misconduct. Conversely, expiry is not a technicality: after the window, the standing rule (CS-9) revives unless a new writing exists.

Authority rank used below: CS-9 (standing) > written time-boxed overlay (FD-7 clearer than PD-3) > contemporaneous monitored-exception reliance > informal continuation, anticipated amendment, or de-facto emergency (not authority).

## 2. Prosecution Brief

CS-9 remains the only controlled standard. M0 is the organization’s declared control-flow expression of that standard. Neither PD-3 nor shop-floor habit repealed the rule that every activation is preceded by sanctions, quality, and a human procurement approval performed by someone other than the requester. A steering-committee email and a later-disowned “monitored exception” are not a controlled amendment. Treating them as if they were is exactly how standing controls die.

The formal point is sharpest on **expiry and unauthorized use of exception paths**. PD-3 ended 2026-07-31. SUP-714 is a LOW-risk, otherwise-eligible auto-approval on 2026-08-02. Six further August cases exist. Operations “believed permanent approval was imminent.” That is normalization of deviance, not a policy. FD-7 ended 2026-07-12 23:59. SUP-705 is the FD-7 pattern on 2026-07-13: activate, then quality and manual approval. No written extension. A directive that expires automatically is designed to stop this move. If “the flood still existed” were enough, every emergency control would be optional the morning after.

Even *inside* the pilot window the exception was abused. Of 312 June–July `Auto_Approve` cases, **14 are clearly ineligible** and 9 lack the attributes that would make the exception auditable. SUP-703 is the exhibit: HIGH-risk new foreign supplier on 2026-06-20; operator selected “pilot” despite ineligibility; first purchase EUR 180,000 — nine times the emergency spend cap that even flood conditions would not have allowed without retrospective approval. Internal Audit was right to call it potentially severe. An exception regime that cannot keep high-risk foreign spend off the auto path is not a monitored exception; it is an unmonitored hole.

SUP-708 is worse as a control failure, and no overlay saves it. HIGH-risk, no pilot, no emergency: `Quality_Review` then `Activate_Supplier` **40 minutes before** `Sanctions_Check`, with manual approval only after the fact. CS-9, PD-3, and FD-7 all require sanctions before activation. “No transactions occurred before clearance” is luck, not a control. The control is the gate. The gate was open.

M0’s silence does not hide the non-control-flow breaches that CS-9 and adjacent policy already state. SUP-712 fits M0’s token game and still violates the only resource rule CS-9 bothers to write: requester and manual approver must be different humans. Alias resolution is how that rule is attacked, and how it must be tested. SUP-715 is the same family of integrity failure: spouses in one reporting line, disclosure required, none recorded. An approval chain that cannot see identity or conflict is not a four-eyes control.

The volume argument — “M0’s high deviation rate is obsolescence” — fails once the deviations are typed. The RMI loop (SUP-707) and CRM missingness (N4, SUP-706) are real model/data defects, and a fair prosecutor concedes them (below). They do not explain SUP-703, SUP-708, the 14 ineligible auto-approvals, SUP-705, SUP-714, or SUP-712. Those are not “the model forgot a loop.” They are people taking an exception path they did not have.

Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy. That requested certificates arrived before activation in all 47 RMI cases is good operational hygiene and still not a CS-9 rewrite. That SUP-702’s quality landed the next business day does not enact PD-3 into CS-9. That flood recovery continued after 12 July does not extend FD-7. That Operations wants a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch is a change request. N5 says no permanent amendment exists. Until one does, CS-9’s manual-approval rule is the rule.

**Conceded defects (so the charge is not over-pled).** M0 cannot express `Request_More_Info`, so scoring SUP-707 as a control-flow crime is a model error. M0 does not represent CS-9’s resource rule, so a pure PM4Py alignment check will miss SUP-712 and will not speak to SUP-715. Rejected-lead and reactivation scope are genuinely underspecified (SUP-709, SUP-710). After 1 July, absence of `Notify_Requester` in the onboarding log is not, without CRM triage, proof of a skipped duty (N4; contrast SUP-702’s stable id with SUP-706’s mutable late text). SUP-711 is a migration duplicate, not a double onboarding. SUP-713 is vocabulary drift with later-approved equivalence. PD-3-eligible in-window cases such as SUP-702, and in-window FD-7 cases such as SUP-704, are not the same offense as SUP-703. The prosecutor’s claim is not that every M0 mismatch is guilt. It is that the organization ran exception machinery past its legal edges, on ineligible and high-risk work, and is now asking the model’s incompleteness to cover those edges.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is being used as if it were the process. It is a partial order over seven labels that already contradicted authorized overlays, agreed work, and the system of record before this review started. Measuring June–August operations against M0 and calling the residuals “noncompliance” is a category error.

**Concurrency and the standard route.** M0’s one deep insight is right: sanctions and quality are unordered with respect to each other. SUP-701 and the repaired reading of SUP-711, SUP-712’s token sequence, and SUP-715’s token sequence all confirm that the happy path is real. Failures that matter are almost never “quality before sanctions” among the checks. They are activation-before-sanctions (SUP-708 — a true incident) or activation-before-quality under a written overlay that M0 cannot see (SUP-702, SUP-704). A model that cannot distinguish those will indict the flood directive and the eligible pilot together with the one case Audit actually feared.

**Missing loop.** Forty-seven cases carry `Request_More_Info`; none activated before the evidence arrived. SUP-707 is the type case: two info cycles, then quality, manual approval, activation, notify, close. Compliance agrees the behavior is desirable. CS-9 does not prohibit it. M0 has no redo and therefore cannot accept a legal, safer trace. That is a model defect, not a control breach. Trace deviation ≠ misconduct.

**Authorized overlays M0 pretends do not exist.** PD-3 is a recorded steering-committee decision with a defined population, residual controls, and a dated window. SUP-702 is inside that window, LOW-risk DE, approved group, complete eligibility fields, sanctions before activation, quality the next business day, CRM notification with a **stable** `notification_id`. Calling this noncompliant because the activity is named `Auto_Approve` rather than `Manual_Procurement_Approval` is to privilege a stale POWL label over the decision the organization actually took. FD-7 is even cleaner: SUP-704 is a critical pump on 2026-07-11, sanctions first, EUR 18,000 under the cap, daily monitoring, quality and retrospective approval the next day. M0 will mark it deviant. The directive will mark it compliant-with-overlay. The directive is the better source for those 72 hours.

The Compliance observer’s later remark is not a secret second veto of every eligible June case. It is evidence that overlay *governance* was sloppy — which the defense admits. Sloppy authorization of a monitored exception is a finding against the control framework, not proof that 289 apparently eligible executions were rogue.

**Data, not process.** N4 moved notification to CRM on 2026-07-01; missingness jumped from 2% to 29%. A model that requires `Notify_Requester` in the onboarding log will manufacture a compliance crisis out of an integration gap. SUP-702’s stable id is sufficient business evidence of notification. SUP-706 is the cautionary opposite (mutable text, entered two days later) and should be *indeterminate*, not guilty. SUP-711’s second `Register_Supplier` shares a source transaction ID: it is a migration echo. SUP-713’s `QA_Clearance` has approved semantic equivalence and audit-equivalent content; the mapping lagged the UI by a week. Scoring label drift as a skipped quality review is forensic malpractice.

**Scope.** CS-9 itself says rejected leads may use an abbreviated pre-screen and does not say those leads are M0 cases. SUP-709 is `Register → Sanctions → Reject → Notify → Close`. The process owner calls it intake; the model owner wants M0 to start only after pre-screen acceptance; the scope document is ambiguous. That is a MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT. Forcing a quality review onto a sanctions-failed lead would be theater. SUP-710 is a reactivation: no `Register_Supplier`, no new `Quality_Review`, prior certification still valid, CS-9 silent. Applying a new-onboarding POWL to a reopen is incorrect scope, not a missing-quality incident.

**What the defense will not excuse.** SUP-703 is not a model problem. Eligibility was knowable; the operator selected pilot anyway; EUR 180,000 on a high-risk new foreign supplier is real risk. SUP-708 is not a logging problem. Activation before sanctions violates every source, including the overlays. Informal continuation after written expiry (SUP-705, SUP-714) is a genuine limit of the “practice is ahead of the model” story: practice may be ahead, but it is not a source of law. Alias SoD failure (SUP-712) is not cured by M0’s silence; CS-9 already states the rule. The 14 ineligible auto-approvals and the six August cases are not explained by CRM or RMI. The defense’s claim is that M0-vs-log deviation rates are dominated by obsolescence, missing loops, overlays, scope holes, and collection loss — and that those defects must be repaired before anyone uses “percent conforming to M0” as a compliance KPI. It is not a claim that the two Audit-severe cases, or expired-path continuation, are innocent.

**System-level judgment**

Operational compliance is **materially incomplete, not globally collapsed**. A minority of cases are true control failures: ineligible or post-expiry exception-path use (14 ineligible of 312 June–July auto-approvals; six early-August auto-approvals including SUP-714; SUP-705), one sanctions-after-activate incident (SUP-708), one high-risk ineligible auto-activation with large first spend (SUP-703), and at least one CS-9 four-eyes breach (SUP-712). Those are enough to reject a “green” operations opinion.

M0 is **not valid as the exclusive normative control-flow model** for this window. It is obsolete relative to two written overlays, incomplete relative to an agreed RMI cycle and CS-9’s own resource rule, incorrectly scoped for rejects and reactivations, and paired after 1 July with a notification log that is no longer the system of record. Using raw M0 fitness as a compliance rate will mostly measure those defects.

The sound position is therefore dual: **enforce CS-9 and the residual-control core (sanctions before activate; no ineligible exception path; no expired overlay; four-eyes on identity, not labels); do not enforce M0-as-replay against RMI, eligible in-window overlays, CRM notification, synonyms, or out-of-scope reopens.**

**Where each side is rejected**

Rejected from the prosecution: the attempt to treat every M0 mismatch as noncompliance; the attempt to drag SUP-702 and SUP-704 into the same bin as SUP-703; the attempt to convict SUP-706/711/713 on collection and vocabulary artifacts; the attempt to criminalize SUP-707. PD-3’s weak form and later recantation are a **governance** finding. They do not, on this record, make eligible in-window operators noncompliant.

Rejected from the defense: the attempt to let desirability, “permanence was imminent,” or “the flood still existed” mint authority after a writing has died (SUP-705, SUP-714, August cohort); the attempt to dissolve SUP-703 and the 14 ineligible auto-approvals into “M0 is obsolete”; the attempt to mitigate SUP-708 with the absence of pre-clearance spend; the attempt to treat SUP-712 as a mere model gap when CS-9 already states the rule. Informal practice may reveal what the next standard should say. It does not decide this period’s compliance.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Layer the norm. Do not stuff amounts, marriages, and gazette dates into a partial order.

**A. Revised control-flow (POWL)**  
A single family of routes after an optional register:

- Standard: concurrent sanctions ∥ quality, then XOR(manual → activate, reject).
- Low-risk auto: sanctions → `Auto_Approve` → activate → quality (quality deliberately after activate).
- Emergency/provisional: sanctions → activate → concurrent quality ∥ retrospective manual approval.
- Abbreviated reject: sanctions → reject (quality skipped).
- Reactivation (approximation): sanctions → manual → activate (quality skipped only if a data rule says the certificate is valid).
- `Request_More_Info` as a LOOP of 0..n cycles that may interleave with the check phase of the standard route and must complete before activation on every route.
- `Notify_Requester` then `Close_Case` on every terminal path, including reject.

POWL owns only **order, choice, concurrency, and a loop approximation**. It must not pretend to know that a case is LOW-risk DE, that today is 11 July, or that two user ids are one human.

**B. Data-aware eligibility / amount / time rules (pandas or equivalent case-frame checks; optionally DECLARE/log-skeleton for purely temporal fragments)**

- Auto route legal iff `risk==LOW` ∧ `country==DE` ∧ `approved_group==true` ∧ `decision_ts ∈ [2026-06-01, 2026-07-31]` ∧ a live overlay version still points at PD-3 (or a successor). Snapshot these predicates at decision time; do not recompute them later from mutable master data.
- Emergency route legal iff `emergency_flag` tied to a live directive id ∧ `activate_ts` inside that directive’s interval ∧ `Sanctions_Check` ≺ `Activate_Supplier` ∧ `initial_spend ≤ 20000` until `retrospective_approval_ts` ∧ quality within two business days ∧ daily monitoring rows exist.
- Standard route: `{Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review}` ≺ `Activate_Supplier` and `Manual_Procurement_Approval` ≺ `Activate_Supplier`.
- Universal DECLARE-style backbone (all routes): `Sanctions_Check` ≺ `Activate_Supplier`; `Notify_Requester` ≺ `Close_Case`; no activate while an RMI cycle is open.
- Reactivation: `prior_case_id` present ∧ `quality_certificate_valid_at_activate`.
- Post-1 July notify: accept CRM send if `notification_id` is a stable immutable key; reject mutable free-text links as proof.

**C. Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (identity graph + organizational controls; not POWL)**

- CS-9: `requester_person_id ≠ manual_approver_person_id` after alias/delegation resolution.
- Delegation allowed only with an immutable delegation record covering the timestamp.
- COI: disclose (and, where local policy requires, recuse) for household, spousal, and same-reporting-line pairs among requester / quality reviewer / manual approver. Absence of a prohibition is not absence of a disclosure duty (SUP-715).

**D. Policy-overlay governance (organizational control + versioned rule table)**

- Overlays (pilots, emergencies) are first-class objects: `overlay_id`, `parent_standard` (CS-9), `effective_from/to`, `population predicates`, `residual controls`, `approver set`.
- A pilot that weakens CS-9 requires Compliance **and** the standard owner on the version record. Minutes plus a later “I did not approve weakening” (N2) would fail this gate — that is the correct place to record the PD-3 defect.
- Emergencies auto-expire. Continuation requires a new version, not a verbal “still flooding.”
- Expired overlays leave traces that must still carry the `overlay_id` they *claimed*, so post-expiry use (SUP-705, SUP-714) is mechanically detectable.

**E. Explicit scope**

- **New onboarding:** `Register_Supplier` present; full CS-9 unless a live overlay applies.
- **Rejected leads / pre-screen:** in scope of an intake variant; quality not required if the case never leaves the reject path after sanctions (SUP-709). Out of scope of “activation controls.”
- **Reactivation:** in scope of a reopen variant; register optional; quality skip only with a valid certificate (SUP-710).
- **Withdrawals:** silent/explicit withdraw before activate goes to notify → close without activate; not modeled as reject-of-supplier unless a reject decision was taken.
- **Log repair before replay:** collapse same-`source_transaction_id` duplicates (SUP-711); map approved synonyms (`QA_Clearance` → `Quality_Review`) with the mapping’s own effective date (SUP-713).

**Placement summary**

| Concern | Home |
|---|---|
| Route shape, concurrency, RMI loop, notify-before-close | POWL |
| Always-sanctions-before-activate; no activate with open RMI; notify-before-close as a checkable constraint | DECLARE / log skeleton (redundant with POWL, useful on dirty logs) |
| Risk, country, group, amounts, overlay windows, 2-BD quality, certificate validity, notification_id | pandas / case-frame |
| Alias SoD, delegation, COI disclosure | Identity graph + org control |
| Who may publish a pilot or emergency, and when it dies | Overlay register (org + data) |
| Reject vs reactivate vs new vs withdraw | Scope rules (documented population), then a POWL XOR only as an approximation |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

**POWL limitation (stated, not hidden).** A single POWL cannot enforce PD-3/FD-7 predicates, the EUR 20,000 cap, two-business-day quality, person-level four-eyes, COI, or overlay effective dates. It also cannot *bind* “RMI must be closed before activate” as a data condition; the LOOP only makes repetition possible. Reactivation’s skipped quality is an XOR branch that is **unsafe** unless a pandas gate blocks the branch when the certificate is invalid. Duplicate register and `QA_Clearance` are log-repair concerns, not nodes. Distinct `Transition` objects share labels across XOR arms because a POWL node cannot sit in two exclusive parents.

Approximation chosen: (i) optional register via silent skip; (ii) `LOOP(τ, Request_More_Info)` concurrent with standard checks and ordered before activation on auto/emergency/reactivation; (iii) four exclusive evaluation routes plus abbreviated reject; (iv) notify ≺ close on the outer order.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def activity(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def rmi_star():
    """(Request_More_Info)*   — LOOP(tau, RMI) = tau (RMI tau)* """
    return OperatorPOWL(
        operator=Operator.LOOP,
        children=[SilentTransition(), activity("Request_More_Info")],
    )


# --- entry: new registration or silent skip (reactivation / already-registered) ---
entry = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[activity("Register_Supplier"), SilentTransition()],
)

# --- standard route: RMI interleavable with concurrent checks, then XOR(approve, reject) ---
s_std = activity("Sanctions_Check")
q_std = activity("Quality_Review")
checks_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_std, q_std])  # concurrent

man_std = activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
act_std = activity("Activate_Supplier")
approve_std = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[man_std, act_std])
approve_std.order.add_edge(man_std, act_std)

decision_std = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[approve_std, activity("Reject_Supplier")],
)

standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rmi_star(), checks_std, decision_std])
standard_route.order.add_edge(checks_std, decision_std)
standard_route.order.add_edge(standard_route.nodes[0], decision_std)
# no edge between RMI* and checks => SUP-707-style interleaving is legal

# --- low-risk auto route: sanctions before auto/activate; quality after provisional activate ---
s_auto = activity("Sanctions_Check")
auto = activity("Auto_Approve")
act_auto = activity("Activate_Supplier")
q_auto = activity("Quality_Review")
rmi_auto = rmi_star()
auto_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rmi_auto, s_auto, auto, act_auto, q_auto])
auto_route.order.add_edge(rmi_auto, auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(s_auto, auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(auto, act_auto)
auto_route.order.add_edge(act_auto, q_auto)

# --- emergency / provisional: sanctions then activate, then quality || retrospective manual ---
s_em = activity("Sanctions_Check")
act_em = activity("Activate_Supplier")
q_em = activity("Quality_Review")
man_em = activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
rmi_em = rmi_star()
post_em = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[q_em, man_em])  # concurrent residual controls
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rmi_em, s_em, act_em, post_em])
emergency_route.order.add_edge(rmi_em, act_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(s_em, act_em)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(act_em, post_em)

# --- abbreviated reject (failed pre-screen / intake): sanctions then reject, quality not required ---
s_rej = activity("Sanctions_Check")
rej_early = activity("Reject_Supplier")
rmi_rej = rmi_star()
early_reject = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rmi_rej, s_rej, rej_early])
early_reject.order.add_edge(rmi_rej, rej_early)
early_reject.order.add_edge(s_rej, rej_early)

# --- reactivation approximation: sanctions + manual, quality skipped (UNSAFE without data gate) ---
s_re = activity("Sanctions_Check")
man_re = activity("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
act_re = activity("Activate_Supplier")
rmi_re = rmi_star()
reactivation_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[rmi_re, s_re, man_re, act_re])
reactivation_route.order.add_edge(rmi_re, man_re)
reactivation_route.order.add_edge(s_re, man_re)
reactivation_route.order.add_edge(man_re, act_re)

routes = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_route,
        auto_route,
        emergency_route,
        early_reject,
        reactivation_route,
    ],
)

notify = activity("Notify_Requester")
close = activity("Close_Case")

M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[entry, routes, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(entry, routes)
M1.order.add_edge(routes, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# M1 is the control-flow revision. Eligibility, amounts, windows, SoD, and COI
# are enforced by the overlay table and case-frame checks below, not by POWL.
```

**Event / data-collection proposal**

Every onboarding (and CRM) event should carry, immutably at write time:

- `event_id`, `case_id`, `activity` (canonical name), `activity_raw` (UI name), `source_transaction_id` (for dedupe), `record_ts` (store time, immutable), `effective_ts` (business time; separate, also immutable once written).
- `notification_id` as a surrogate key issued by CRM; forbid mutable text `case_link` as the join (fixes N4 / SUP-706). Mirror failures must raise a completeness incident, not a silent skip.
- `rule_version_id` / `overlay_id` claimed at the decision event (`Manual_Procurement_Approval`, `Auto_Approve`, `Reject_Supplier`, emergency activate). Snapshot `risk`, `country`, `approved_group`, `supplier_type`, `prior_case_id`, `certificate_id`, `certificate_valid_to` into the decision payload so later master-data edits cannot rewrite eligibility (fixes the 9 attribute-less auto-approvals).
- `actor_user_id`, `actor_person_id` (resolved), `actor_alias_set`, `delegation_id` + `delegation_valid_interval` (fixes SUP-712).
- `requester_person_id`, `quality_reviewer_person_id`, `approver_person_id`, plus a `coi_disclosure_id` when any pair is household/same-line (fixes SUP-715 as a testable duty).
- Emergency/pilot: `overlay_id`, `authority_instrument_uri`, `spend_at_activate`, `monitoring_date` rows.
- Label map table: `raw_label → canonical_label`, `map_approved_ts`, `map_approver` (SUP-713). Replay uses the map only when `effective_ts ≥ map_approved_ts` **or** an audit equivalence record exists; otherwise keep the raw label and classify as vocabulary-indeterminate, not as a skipped quality review.

Conformance scoring then has three outputs per case, never one: (1) M1 control-flow class, (2) overlay/data-rule class, (3) resource/COI class. SUP-702 scores deviant-to-M0, justified on (2), clean on (3). SUP-712 scores fit-to-M1, fail on (3). That split is the point of the revision.
