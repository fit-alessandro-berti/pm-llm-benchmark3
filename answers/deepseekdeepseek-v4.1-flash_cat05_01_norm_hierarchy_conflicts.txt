## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts treated as given:** source texts, effective dates/windows, amounts, supplier status, recorded events/timestamps, and the ERP-outage evidence note. The July 2026 contamination event, Aachen site, ECD-2 window, and roles are facts for this assessment.

**Interpretations used:**  
- GPIP-4 is a board-approved global control floor for integrity, segregation, Compliance, Finance/controller, budget-owner, thresholds, and CRO exceptions. It is not automatically displaced by a local SOP or an incident email.  
- ECD-2 is a valid operational incident directive for local workflow timing and supplier-onboarding issues **only within its stated scope and time window**. It does not expressly waive GPIP-4 A1, A2, A3, or A7.  
- The Aachen SOP can be stricter than GPIP-4 and can define local evidence, but it cannot silently weaken GPIP-4.  
- The CFO email is not controlled policy. It may carry weight as a Finance-control interpretation, but it cannot clearly waive board-level GPIP-4 controls, Compliance, or segregation of duties.  
- Emergency necessity is assessed narrowly: imminent safety harm or production stoppage for SOP B4; “critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event” for ECD-2.  
- Later approvals mitigate but do not automatically cure missing pre-commitment approvals under GPIP-4 A6.  
- ERP timestamps are not conclusive when better contemporaneous evidence exists, especially around the outage note and where corroborating call/PDF/acknowledgment evidence exists.  
- The party claiming an exception or emergency authority bears the burden to prove scope, timing, authority, and recording. Missing evidence weakens the exception claim but does not itself prove a policy breach.

**Assumptions:** Incident Command members are not automatically independent budget owners; IC authorization is operational unless separately designated. The CFO email is not a CRO A7 exception. Backup-line stock with three days of coverage is not presumptively “required within the next 24 hours” for ECD-2 scope.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

- **P-505 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or COMPLIANT.** If the CFO email is treated as a valid controlled Finance interpretation, and the two IC authorizers are treated as satisfying the independent budget-owner/operational approval requirement, then ECD C1/C3 plus the pre-18:00 evidence would justify the purchase. The premise change is that CFO D1 can waive or reinterpret GPIP A2 and that IC authorization is more than operational. This is credible because the CFO owns Finance controls and the timing evidence favors a pre-expiry commit, but weak because the email is not controlled, D4 limits it, and IC members are not designated budget owners.

- **P-507 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** If CFO D1 validly waives Finance/budget-owner approval for ECD purchases EUR 150,000, then P-507 satisfies ECD authorization, Compliance, and recording. The premise change is that A2 can be satisfied by joint IC authorization rather than a separate budget owner and Finance Controller. This is credible because P-507 obtained Compliance, used an unapproved supplier only within ECD, and recorded the authorization by signed PDF within four hours. It is weak because A2 is board policy, A7 reserves exceptions to the CRO, and the CFO email is not controlled.

- **P-503 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or COMPLIANT.** If ECD scope includes backup-line “critical contingency” components and the COO/IC directive can override GPIP A3 for >EUR 100,000, then P-503 might be justified except for SoD concerns. The premise change is a broad reading of “critical” and an A7-equivalent status for ECD. This is credible because Operations labeled the pumps critical and the site was in a contamination event. It is weak because Internal Audit’s narrower 24-hour reading is more consistent with “required to contain,” the stock lasted three days, and C5 does not mention Compliance.

- **P-501 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** If the Plant Manager was functionally absent or SHIFT-1 had implied emergency delegation, B4 would authorize the pre-PO commitment. The premise change is that “absent” and “actively delegated” can be practical rather than formal. This is credible given the four-hour stoppage prediction and later Plant/Finance approvals. It is weak because the evidence says the Plant Manager was reachable and did not formally delegate SHIFT-1.

- **P-502 — JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** If ECD-2 is treated as an A7-equivalent exception for new-supplier Compliance and CFO D1 validly covers Finance/budget approval, then P-502 would be justified. ## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Replacement hierarchy/exception clause:**  
“GPIP-4 is the mandatory control floor. Aachen SOPs may be stricter but not weaker. An incident directive signed by the COO and Incident Commander may modify only local SOP timing and approved-supplier onboarding for named critical lots within its written scope and window. It does not waive GPIP-4 segregation, Compliance, Finance Controller, independent budget-owner, or CRO-exception requirements. Only the Chief Risk Officer may issue a written, scoped, time-limited GPIP exception.”

“Thresholds during any emergency: EUR 50,000 requires independent budget-owner and Finance Controller approval before supplier commitment; >EUR 100,000 or any new/unapproved supplier requires Compliance clearance before commitment; >EUR 150,000 additionally requires Legal clearance. Joint Incident Command authorization is operational and does not satisfy these roles unless the CFO and CRO jointly designate it in writing for a named amount and scope.”

“New suppliers may be used under an incident directive only if Compliance clearance is obtained before commitment or the CRO issues a written retrospective-clearance exception with a 24-hour Compliance review and supplier suspension if unqualified. Verbal IC authorization must be recorded within four hours; if systems fail, a signed PDF, phone recording, access log, or supplier acknowledgment may be primary evidence, and ERP timestamps are not conclusive.”

“ECD authority expires automatically at the written end time. No verbal extension is valid. Extensions must be written by the original signatories and, for any GPIP-4 control, by the CRO, with a maximum 72-hour extension. Emergency channels may not be used for cafeteria, non-production, routine stock, or backup-line contingency purchases absent documented 24-hour necessity.”

“Safeguard against emergency normalization: cumulative emergency commitments above a defined threshold, repeated use of the same supplier/role, or any post-expiry purchase triggers mandatory CRO and Internal Audit review within five working days. Repeated emergency use without CRO ratification automatically escalates to the board audit committee.”
