# 1. Data audit and estimands

## Queueing quantities and units
- **Arrival intensity ()**: Cases per hour during opening hours (07:00-19:00, 12 hours)
- **Completion intensity ()**: Cases completed per hour during opening hours
- **Service time**: Active handling time (minutes) excluding waiting, reported as median and p90
- **Work-in-progress (WIP)**: Mean number of cases in system from 15-minute snapshots
- **Capacity**: Staff FTE available concurrently (1 FTE = 1 person working full time during opening hours)
- **Flow time**: Total time from arrival to completion (not directly observed)
- **Utilization ()**: /( × staff_fte) - capacity pressure metric

## Opening-hours convention
- 12-hour operational window (07:00-19:00)
- No service occurs outside these hours, but cases can carry over overnight
- Daily aggregates are for opening hours only

## Limitations of aggregates
1. **Temporal aggregation**: Daily totals mask intraday patterns (e.g., peak hours)
2. **Service time distribution**: Only median and p90 provided - mean service time unknown
3. **Backlog state**: Opening WIP not provided - affects daily dynamics
4. **Case mix**: Complex variant percentage may reflect coding changes rather than true process changes
5. **Staffing intervention**: Exact timing of temporary staffing change unknown (began 2026-04-29)
6. **Outage impact**: Only duration provided (180 minutes on 2026-04-15), not exact timing or recovery pattern
7. **Carry-over**: Cases completed may have arrived on previous days

## Threats to naive inference
1. **Simpson's paradox**: Pooled averages may reverse when segmented (e.g., weekday vs weekend)
2. **Non-stationarity**: Multiple change points (outage, staffing intervention, case mix shift)
3. **Autocorrelation**: Daily WIP likely serially dependent due to carry-over backlog
4. **Overdispersion**: Arrival and completion counts likely more variable than Poisson
5. **Boundary effects**: Little's Law requires careful window selection due to opening/closing boundaries
6. **Measurement error**: Complex variant percentage may reflect coding changes rather than true process changes
7. **Confounding**: Staffing changes coincide with case mix changes

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

| Window/Regime | Boundary Stability | Throughput Proxy | WIP Comparability | Carry-over Risk | Stationarity | Implied Flow Time | Verdict |
|---------------|--------------------|------------------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|-------------------|---------|
| Full dataset | Poor (weekday/weekend mix) | Arrivals (biased by carry-over) | Poor (different scales) | High | No | 3.6 days | Not defensible |
| Baseline Weekdays (Regime 1) | Good (12-hour window) | Completions (better for steady state) | Good (similar WIP) | Medium (some carry-over) | Approximate | 2.3 hours | Approximate only |
| Outage Day (Regime 2) | Poor (service interruption) | Neither (non-stationary) | Poor (WIP spike) | High | No | 5.3 hours | Not defensible |
| Outage Recovery (Regime 3) | Medium (declining WIP) | Completions (clearing backlog) | Medium (WIP changing) | High | No | 4.8-5.8 hours | Not defensible |
| Pre-Intervention Weekdays (Regime 4) | Good | Completions | Good | Medium | Approximate | 4.1 hours | Approximate only |
| Post-Intervention Weekdays (Regime 5) | Good | Completions | Good | Medium | Approximate | 3.7 hours | Approximate only |
| Weekends (Regime 6) | Good | Completions | Good | Low | Yes | 4.2 hours | Applicable |
| 2026-04-07 (representative) | Good | Completions | Good | Low | Yes | 2.2 hours | Applicable |
| 2026-04-21 (bursty) | Poor (extreme peaks) | Neither | Poor | Medium | No | 3.6 hours | Not defensible |

**Calculation notes**:
- Implied flow time = WIP / throughput (in hours)
- For weekdays: throughput = completions/12 (per opening hour)
- For weekends: throughput = completions/12
- Boundary stability: whether opening/closing WIP is comparable
- Throughput proxy: whether arrivals or completions better represent system throughput
- Carry-over risk: likelihood that cases span multiple days

**Key findings**:
1. Little's Law is **only strictly applicable** to weekends and the representative ordinary weekday (2026-04-07)
2. For other weekdays, it provides **approximate** estimates if we assume:
   - Opening/closing WIP are similar
   - System is near steady state
   - Completions represent throughput better than arrivals
3. **Not defensible** for:
   - Outage and recovery periods
   - Bursty days (2026-04-21)
   - Pooled datasets

# 7. Operational implications

**Staffing recommendations**:
1. **Weekend staffing**: Current 2.5-3.0 FTE appears sufficient (0.85-1.0), but consider:
   - Adding 0.5 FTE on Saturdays to reduce  to 0.8
   - Cross-training to handle complex cases that now appear on weekends
2. **Weekday staffing**:
   - **Pre-10:00 peak**: Add temporary staff (0.5-1.0 FTE) from 08:00-10:00 to handle arrival bursts
   - **Post-mix-change**: Maintain 6.5-7.0 FTE to handle increased complexity
   - **Friday afternoons**: Consider reduced staffing (5.0 FTE) as arrivals decline
3. **Outage recovery**: Maintain elevated staffing (7.0 FTE) for 1-2 days after major outages

**Backlog recovery**:
1. **Outage protocol**: After service interruptions:
   - Immediately increase staffing by 1.0 FTE for 2 days
   - Implement priority lanes for simple cases to clear backlog faster
2. **Weekend carry-over**: Monitor Monday opening WIP - if consistently >25, add 0.5 FTE on Mondays
3. **Complex case handling**: Dedicate 1.0 FTE to complex cases during peak hours to prevent queue buildup

**Peak smoothing**:
1. **Appointment system**: Implement for 08:00-10:00 peak to spread arrivals
2. **Incentives**: Offer discounts for service outside 08:00-10:00 window
3. **Pre-processing**: Move simple case intake to online portal to reduce in-person load

**Data collection improvements**:
1. **Backlog tracking**: Record opening and closing WIP daily
2. **Service time distribution**: Collect full distribution, not just median/p90
3. **Case mix**: Distinguish between coding changes and true process changes in complex_pct
4. **Intraday metrics**: Collect hourly WIP and service time data
5. **Staffing logs**: Record exact timing of staffing changes and interventions

**Risky pooled-average policy**:
**Policy**: "Since pooled completion rate is 7.9/hr and staffing is 5.2 FTE, we can reduce staffing to 5.0 FTE to improve efficiency."

**Risks under segmented view**:
1. **Weekday capacity**: Already operating at 0.95-1.05 pre-intervention - any reduction would increase WIP
2. **Weekend vulnerability**: Weekends operate at 0.85-1.0 with 2.5-3.0 FTE - small reductions would cause delays
3. **Complex case impact**: Post-intervention weekdays handle higher complexity with same staffing - reduction would disproportionately affect these days
4. **Burstiness**: Pooled average masks extreme peaks (e.g., 29.6% of arrivals in one hour on 2026-04-21)
5. **Outage recovery**: Pooled average obscures the need for temporary staffing increases after disruptions

**Better alternative**: Maintain current staffing levels but implement dynamic staffing adjustments based on:
- Day of week
- Complex case percentage
- Recent outage history
- Intraday arrival patterns

# 4. Change-point heuristic (CUSUM for WIP)
def cusum_change_point(series, threshold=1.5):
    n = len(series)
    cusum = np.zeros(n)
    for i in range(1, n):
        cusum[i] = max(0, cusum[i-1] + series[i] - series[:i].mean() - threshold)
    return np.argmax(cusum)
