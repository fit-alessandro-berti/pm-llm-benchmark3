## 2. Process-model consistency checks

To prevent leaked, out-of-order, or artifactual features from entering training or explanations, implement a six-part automated validation suite between raw event ingestion and model explanation generation:

1. **Prefix-time availability check (Temporal Cut & Future-Erasure Invariance)**
   - *Mechanism*: Define $t_0(c) = \min \{ \tau(e) \mid e \in c, \text{act}(e) = \text{Coverage Check Completed} \}$. Construct a strict prefix projection $\pi_{\le t_0}(c)$ retaining only events with $\tau(e) \le t_0(c)$ (excluding subsequent events sharing timestamp $t_0$ via sequence index). Run a **counterfactual future-mutation test**: take historical complete traces, randomly mutate or delete all events and warehouse updates after $t_0$ (including `extraction_date` shifts), and recompute the feature vector $\mathbf{x}(c, t_0)$. Any feature where $\mathbf{x}_{\text{full}} \neq \mathbf{x}_{\text{truncated}}$ automatically fails CI as a leakage defect (catching `total_case_duration_hours`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, and all post-$t_0$ sequence patterns).

2. **Activity-order feasibility check (Normative Alignment vs. DFG Pitfalls)**
   - *Mechanism*: Encode the normative process as a formal Petri net / BPMN model and run **prefix alignments** (rather than relying on Directly-Follows Graphs). Compute fitness and synchronous vs. model/log moves for prefixes and sequence features.
   - *Role and limitations*: A **DFG** merely counts empirical adjacency in total-ordered logs; a frequent DFG edge is *not* a normative rule because DFGs conflate parallel execution (`Fraud Investigation` interleaved with `Documents Received`), collapse multi-instance loops (`Settlement Decision Recorded`), and elevate ingestion sorting bugs (`Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`) into apparent process transitions. Behavioral **footprint matrices** detect coarse order relations ($\to, \leftarrow, \parallel, \#$) quickly, while **token-based or optimal alignments** precisely flag impossible sequences like activity execution after `Claim Closed`.

3. **Same-timestamp ambiguity check (Partial-Order Semantics)**
   - *Mechanism*: Audit event pairs $(e_i, e_j)$ within a case where $\tau(e_i) = \tau(e_j)$ (or $|\tau(e_i) - \tau(e_j)| < \epsilon$ for coarse clock granularity). Require a monotonically increasing source `ingestion_seq_id` to establish strict order $e_i \prec e_j$.
   - *Handling missing indices*: When `ingestion_seq_id` is null (as in Case Z), treat tied events as a **concurrent/uncertain multiset (partial order)** rather than sorting alphabetically or by database row position. Disallow sequence-pattern extractors from firing directed transitions $A \to B$ across unindexed ties, or require conformance resolution against the normative model (which resolves `{Claim Closed, Damage Assessment Completed}` as either `Damage Assessment -> ... -> Claim Closed` with missing intermediate timestamps or flags data corruption).

4. **Complete-trace feature audit (Lineage Metadata & Cardinality Invariants)**
   - *Mechanism*: Tag every feature table column with `valid_from_ts` and `write_event_trigger`. Enforce automated assertions at $t_0$: (a) no feature may depend on terminal case attributes (`close_ts`, `final_decision`, `final_handler_team`); (b) cardinality of prefix variants at $t_0$ must match valid paths ending at the first `Coverage Check Completed` (typically length 2, or length 3 if early parallel triggers exist—never length 9 like Case Y's `REG-COV-DOC-DOC-DMG-DEC-APP-DEC-CLOSE`).

5. **Prefix-marking reachability check (Enabled vs. Future-Possible vs. Impossible at $t_0$)**
   - *Mechanism*: Evaluate the normative Petri net marking $M(t_0)$ immediately after firing `Coverage Check Completed` to classify candidate explanation concepts:
     - **Observed in prefix**: `Claim Registered`, `Coverage Check Completed` (admissible as factual occurrences).
     - **Enabled at $t_0$**: `Documents Requested`, `Damage Assessment Completed` (fast track), and `Fraud Investigation Opened` (if triggered). Explanations *cannot* claim these have already happened at $t_0$, though downstream models may simulate their transition probabilities.
     - **Merely future-possible (not yet enabled at $t_0$)**: `Documents Received`, `Documents Validated`, `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal`, `Claim Closed`. Any $t_0$ feature asserting their realized presence is leaked.
     - **Normatively impossible**: `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` or `Appeal` following `Approve`/`Withdrawn`. Explanations referencing impossible transitions must trigger data-quality alerts rather than business narratives.

6. **Declarative semantic validation for repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` events**
   - *Mechanism*: Coarse activity labels lack state refinement, so pure structural graphs misinterpret legitimate reconsideration loops. Apply **Linear Temporal Logic over finite traces ($\text{LTL}_f$ / Declare constraints)** over attribute-enriched events `Settlement Decision Recorded(outcome)`:
     - *Precedence on Appeal*: $\neg \text{Appeal} \ \mathcal{W} \ \text{Settlement Decision Recorded}(\text{outcome} \in \{\text{Partial}, \text{Deny}\})$ (an appeal cannot occur unless preceded by a Partial or Deny decision).
     - *Conditional Repeatability*: A second occurrence of `Settlement Decision Recorded` is valid **if and only if** at least one `Appeal` occurs strictly between the first and second decision events; otherwise, flag duplicate-event logging or missing appeal capture.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Credible auditing requires separating four distinct evidentiary standards:
- **Predictive validity**: Does the feature generalize out-of-time using only information strictly available at $t_0$?
- **Causal interpretation**: Would intervening on $X$ at $t_0$ change breach probability $P(Y=1 \mid do(X=x))$, holding confounding factors fixed?
- **Operational actionability**: Can claims managers realistically modify $X$ (or route cases based on $X$) at $t_0$ to prevent SLA breaches?
- **Fairness & robustness review**: Does $X$ encode protected/socioeconomic characteristics or unstable historical regimes that create disparate impact or out-of-distribution failure?

Based on these standards, surviving and repairable features fall into five classes (while permanently dropping irredeemable post-$t_0$ outcomes like `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_decision`, and post-$t_0$ sequence patterns):

### 1. Defensible predictive signal
- **Features**: `initial_damage_complexity` (frozen at registration); `initial_reserve` (contingent on proving registration-time immutability); `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (*after* SQL repair to `< t0`).
- **What may be claimed**: These capture intrinsic baseline claim scope and historical claimant filing frequency known at $t_0$, explaining why a case enters step 3 with higher expected processing effort (e.g., higher likelihood of needing multi-round evidence cycles rather than the fast track).
- **What may NOT be claimed**: One cannot claim `prior_claim_count_before_t0` directly *causes* delay or proves fraud, nor is `initial_damage_complexity` operationally mutable at $t_0$ (you cannot lower risk simply by relabeling a complex crash as "Low" complexity, though you can proactively fast-assign specialist adjusters).

### 2. Process-state or congestion signal
- **Features**: `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; `queue_length_at_t0` (repaired to backward-only `[t0 - 5m, t0]`); `prefix_variant_at_t0` (replacing `full_trace_variant_id`).
- **What may be claimed**: `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` measures exact SLA time budget already depleted before step 2 completes; `queue_length_at_t0` reflects contemporaneous system load competing for handler capacity. These support **operationally actionable triage** (e.g., SLA-aware queue prioritization or dynamic load balancing), though confirming the *magnitude* of rebalancing benefit still requires causal/queuing evaluation because high queues may co-occur with catastrophe surges (weather events) that simultaneously increase claim difficulty.
- **What may NOT be claimed**: A short `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` guarantees safe completion regardless of downstream complexity, or reducing local queue count by artificially deferring intake solves systemic bottlenecks.

### 3. Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review
- **Features**: `claimant_region`; `claimant_channel`.
- **What may be claimed**: Historically, claims originating from specific regions or channels (`Broker`) exhibit higher or lower empirical breach rates, likely reflecting indirect structural factors such as local repair-shop capacity, third-party broker document-relay hops, or language translation needs.
- **What may NOT be claimed**: That `claimant_region` or `claimant_channel` autonomously *drives* delay, or that deprioritizing/penalizing regional claimants is legitimate. Because `claimant_region` correlates with socioeconomic status and language, using it to allocate scarce expedited handling or fraud scrutiny risks unfair disparate impact; explanations must trigger fairness audits and encourage replacing coarse geography with direct operational bottlenecks (e.g., `regional_assessor_backlog_at_t0`).

### 4. Drift/calendar proxy
- **Features**: `calendar_week`.
- **What may be claimed**: Within the historical training window, claims arriving in certain calendar weeks experienced elevated breach rates aligned with holiday staffing reductions and a legacy operating-policy transition.
- **What may NOT be claimed**: That "Week $k$ is inherently operationally worse" in future years. Because an operating-policy shift is a one-time regime change rather than an annual cycle, raw `calendar_week` will suffer severe **concept drift** in production. It should be refactored into explicit stationary indicators (`is_public_holiday_window`, `active_staffing_ratio_at_t0`) while training under the current policy regime.

### 5. Unusable until lineage is repaired
- **Features**: `final_handler_team` ($\to$ `assigned_team_at_t0`), `same_day_document_count_snapshot` ($\to$ `doc_count_uploaded_up_to_t0`), `risk_score_current` ($\to$ `risk_score_at_t0`), `last_resource_role` ($\to$ `resource_role_at_t0`), `employee_experience` ($\to$ `handler_tenure_at_t0`), and `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (in its current broken SQL state).
- **What may be claimed**: Nothing in the current model run. Once point-in-time event-sourced lineage is built and the model is retrained, their point-in-time counterparts may serve as valid $t_0$ state/triage descriptors.
- **What may NOT be claimed**: That the ~65% overlap between `final_handler_team` and initial team makes current SHAP values "mostly right." The ~35% reassigned subset disproportionately captures mid-process escalations (e.g., to SIU or complex-loss teams), biasing both the global weight (0.29) and local attributions.
