# 1. Analytical assumptions

### Interpretation of Key Concepts
- **Case notion**: A logical grouping of related events that represent a coherent process or lifecycle for a specific object type (e.g., Order, Package). Identifying a case notion often provides the "perspective" for analyzing the process.
- **Convergence**: When multiple objects or processes merge into a shared lifecycle (e.g., multiple orders are consolidated into one package or invoice), creating ambiguity in attributing events to one "true" case.
- **Divergence**: When a single object or process splits into multiple dependent lifecycles (e.g., one order results in multiple packages, deliveries, or invoices, making it hard to track the original object across branches).

### Duplication and Shared Events
- Shared events (copied across multiple cases during flattening) are treated as **both evidence and duplication**:
  - Evidence of inclusion in convergence patterns or logical splits.
  - Duplication in that the same event inflates trace lengths or introduces distortions when interpreting flattened logs.
- Shared events will contribute meaningful insights only if they are treated analytically, avoiding naive inflation or over-representation.

### Analytical Transformations
- **Relation propagation**: Explicit propagation through object-object relationships (e.g., propagating order-to-invoice through the Order-Bills-Invoice relation) is permissible when reasoning about connections but not directly applied to the provided flattening logic.
- Indirect propagation introduces the risk of over-attribution and will therefore be used sparingly—limited to evaluating "lost/converged" relations in flattened views.

# 3. Concrete distortion analysis

### Cross-Order Packages
1. **Affected flattening**: `Order`
   - **Events/objects**: `e13 (Pack Package)`, `e16 (Dispatch D2)`.
   - **Description**: P3 involves items from `O2` and `O3`, causing convergence in the flattened log when using `Order`. `e16` appears artificially duplicated across all orders.
   - **Effect**: Inflates DFG frequency and creates apparent variants combining order traces.
   - **Context**: Harmful — cross-order packaging cannot be uniquely attributed.

2. **Affected flattening**: `Item`
   - **Events/objects**: `e13 (Pack Package)` with `I21` and `I31`.
   - **Description**: Focus on individual items causes packing-related events to multiply, visually breaking the shared logistics of P3.
   - **Effect**: Distorts supply chain bottleneck analysis.
   - **Context**: Harmful due to item-level granularity.

### Consolidated Deliveries
3. **Affected flattening**: `Delivery`
   - **Events/objects**: `e16 (Dispatch with D2)` for P2 and P3.
   - **Description**: Shared events (`e16`) inflate activity repeat counts for consolidated movement spanning multiple packages and orders.
   - **Effect**: Causes performance bottlenecks to be misattributed to deliveries.
   - **Context**: Harmful — inflates key event duplicates.

### Consolidated Invoicing
4. **Affected flattening**: `Order`
   - **Events/objects**: `e19 (Issue Invoice)` for `V2`.
   - **Description**: Orders `O2` and `O3` are merged under invoice V2, losing independence of their billing/accounting processes.
   - **Effect**: Reduces interpretability of variants; edges in DFG move toward invoice-level convergence.
   - **Context**: Harmful for nuanced order-centric views.

### Partial Payments
5. **Affected flattening**: `Payment`
   - **Events/objects**: `M2` and `M3` completing `V2`.
   - **Description**: Partial and complete payment records create multiple short payment lifecycles.
   - **Effect**: Truncated sequences artificially separate payments from order context.
   - **Context**: Context-dependent; payment priority may justify this focus.

### Singleton Lifecycle
6. **Affected flattening**: `Invoice`
   - **Events/objects**: `e18/e20 (Issue Invoice)` and linked payment events.
   - **Description**: Invoices have limited activities (issue and settle only), creating very short lifecycles.
   - **Effect**: DFG generates trivial loops, overemphasizing simple edge patterns.
   - **Context**: Harmful for lifecycle completeness.

### Merging of Shared Events
7. **Affected flattening**: `Package`
   - **Events/objects**: `e11/e12 (Pack P1/P2)` and `e15/e16 (Dispatch D1/D2)`.
   - **Description**: Package focus inflates event duplication through heavily shared links.
   - **Effect**: DFG overemphasizes packing-dispatch transitions.
   - **Context**: Harmful for broader operational flow analysis.

8. **Delivery Truncation**
   - **Affected flattening**: `Order`
   - **Events**: Post-delivery lifecycle truncation in `e23, e24`.
   - **Description**: Delivery confirmation events misalign order closure events.
   - **Effect**: Misrepresents post-delivery order processing.
   - **Context**: Harmful for high-level task duration metrics.

# 4. Traditional versus object-centric analysis portfolio

### Flattened Logs — Use Cases
- `Order`: High-level order metrics, lifecycle comparisons, process completeness.
- `Delivery`: Logistics optimization.
- `Invoice`: Payment reconciliation and follow-up.

### Object-Centric Analysis
Best for cross-object dynamics, bottleneck identification, and variant boundaries:
- **OC-DFG**: To analyze dispatch-consolidation effects (e.g., D2).
- **OTG**: Item-package-shipment interdependencies.
- **Interaction Summaries**: Payment/invoice contributions to consolidation complexity.

### Reconciling Findings
Contrast trace lengths and bottlenecks between flattened and object-centric logs.

# 5. Lead-object decision

### Decision: **Reject a single lead object for this process**.
- **Decision criteria**: Cross-object dependencies and branching; shared events.
- **Supporting evidence**: Cross-order packages (`P3`), deliveries (`D2`).
- **Counterargument**: Orders provide a simplistic central lifecycle, but at the cost of essential overlaps.
- **Residual risk**: Introducing complexity through OC approaches.
- **Conditions for revision**: If process simplicity is prioritized over accuracy.
