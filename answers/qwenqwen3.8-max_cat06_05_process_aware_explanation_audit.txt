## 2. Process-model consistency checks

I would implement the following as a validation suite run in CI/CD for feature generation, model training, and explanation rendering.

1. **Prefix-time availability contract**
   - Every feature must have metadata: source system, computation logic, event-time predicate, valid-from/valid-to, and whether it depends on complete-trace fields.
   - Automated assertion: `feature_effective_time <= t0`.
   - For snapshots, require snapshot effective time <= `t0` and prove that the snapshot does not aggregate events after `t0`.
   - For slowly changing dimensions such as handler team, resource role, employee experience, and reserve, require bitemporal history: `valid_from <= t0 < valid_to`.
   - Time-travel backtests should re-extract historical cases as if extraction occurred immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`.

2. **Prefix reconstruction with reliable ordering**
   - Build an event store with `case_id`, `activity`, `occurred_at`, `ingested_at`, `source_sequence_id`, and `event_version`.
   - Cut the trace at the first `Coverage Check Completed` event.
   - If events share identical timestamps, use a reliable sequence index. If the sequence index is missing, mark the ordering as ambiguous and suppress any explanation that depends on that order.

3. **Activity-order feasibility against the normative process**
   Use declarative constraints or linear temporal rules, for example:
   - `Claim Registered` precedes `Coverage Check Completed`.
   - After `Coverage Check Completed`, enabled next activities include fast-track `Damage Assessment Completed` or an evidence cycle beginning with `Documents Requested`.
   - `Damage Assessment Completed` precedes the first `Settlement Decision Recorded`.
   - `Appeal` is enabled only after a `Settlement Decision Recorded` with outcome Partial or Deny.
   - A subsequent `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate only after an `Appeal` and a prior Partial/Deny decision.
   - `Claim Closed` follows a final decision or withdrawal.
   - `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` should fail a “not succession” or precedence check unless an explicitly modeled reopen exception exists.

4. **Same-timestamp ambiguity checks**
   - Detect event pairs with identical timestamps.
   - Require a source sequence identifier, transaction ID, or monotonic event number.
   - If order is ambiguous, do not allow sequence-pattern explainers to assign directional contributions.
   - Run sensitivity analysis: if a pattern’s contribution changes under alternative plausible orders, the pattern is not explanation-ready.

5. **Complete-trace feature detection**
   - Static lineage linting should flag features using final fields, closure fields, last-value semantics, full-trace counts, full-trace variants, or flags such as `appeal_seen_anywhere`.
   - Dynamic tests should compare a feature computed from the full trace with the same feature computed from the prefix. Any difference indicates potential leakage.
   - Features such as `total_case_duration_hours`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_handler_team`, and `last_resource_role` should fail this test for a `t0` model.

6. **Explanation activity-state checker**
   For each explanation, classify referenced activities relative to the prefix at `t0`:
   - **Enabled at `t0`**: activities that can occur next, such as `Damage Assessment Completed` or `Documents Requested` after the given prefix.
   - **Future-possible but not yet enabled**: activities that may occur later but require intermediate events, such as `Settlement Decision Recorded`, `Appeal`, or `Claim Closed`.
   - **Impossible at `t0`**: activities that contradict the prefix or normative model, such as an appeal before any decision or closure before a final decision.
   - **Ambiguous**: same-timestamp events without reliable sequence.
   An explanation for a `t0` prediction should not rely on activities that are future-only or impossible at `t0`.

7. **Repeated Settlement Decision validation**
   - The coarse label `Settlement Decision Recorded` may legitimately appear twice when an appeal triggers reconsideration.
   - Validate that a second decision is preceded by a Partial or Deny decision and an Appeal, with reliable ordering.
   - If multiple decisions occur without an appeal, or with identical timestamps and no sequence index, treat the trace as noisy or ambiguous.
   - For the `t0` model, even a legitimate repeated decision is future information and must not appear in features or explanations.

8. **Role and limits of DFG, footprint, prefix, and alignment checks**
   - **DFG checks** are useful for discovering frequent transitions, but a frequent edge is not automatically a normative rule. For example, a frequent `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` edge may be caused by same-timestamp misordering, not by a real process.
   - **Footprint checks** compare observed activity relations with allowed relations. They can detect impossible orderings but may miss timing leakage if the footprint is computed from full traces.
   - **Prefix checks** ensure that only events up to `t0` are used. They are necessary but not sufficient, because prefix events can still be ambiguously ordered.
   - **Alignment checks** replay traces against the normative model and measure fitness. They help identify impossible or exceptional paths, but they depend on the correctness and completeness of the normative model.
   - **Declarative checks** encode business rules such as precedence and response. They are powerful for semantic validation but require careful domain review to avoid treating legitimate exceptions as errors.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Different standards apply:

- **Predictive validity**: the feature is available at `t0`, reliably measured, and statistically associated with breach risk.
- **Causal interpretation**: requires evidence that changing the feature or its underlying mechanism would change breach risk, typically through experimentation, causal inference, or strong domain identification assumptions. SHAP importance alone is not causal evidence.
- **Operational actionability**: requires not only predictive or causal evidence, but also a feasible, safe, and effective intervention.
- **Fairness review**: even a predictive feature may be restricted or transformed if it proxies protected attributes, socioeconomic status, or unequal access to process resources.

| Class | Examples after repair | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, repaired `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, verified `reserve_at_t0`, event-level documents received before `t0` | These features are admissible and can contribute to breach probability. They can support prioritization or risk stratification. | They do not prove causation. They should not be presented as guaranteed reasons for breach or as sole basis for adverse action without validation. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `queue_length_at_t0`, workload or staffing at `t0`, fraud investigation already opened before `t0`, evidence-cycle state if actually present in prefix | These describe the operational state at prediction time. They can explain why the environment is statistically more likely to delay. | They should not be claimed to “cause” breach unless supported by causal analysis. Operational interventions such as reducing queue length need separate evidence. |
| Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review | `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, possibly reserve or repair-network correlates if not directly measured | These may remain in a governed predictive model if they improve validity and pass fairness, proxy, and robustness review. They can be described as statistical associations, not inherent claimant properties. | They must not be described as causal delay drivers or used as operational reasons without fairness review. They should not justify differential treatment absent governance and oversight. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week`, holiday indicators, policy-change periods | These may indicate period-specific risk due to holidays, staffing, or policy changes. They can support capacity planning if validated. | They should not be interpreted as stable causal effects of weeks. They require revalidation after policy or calendar changes. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, current `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, current `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `final_decision`, future or ambiguous sequence patterns | These may be investigated for data quality or future model stages, but they should not contribute to the current `t0` prediction or explanation. | They must not be used as early-warning explanations. They must not be presented as causal or actionable. They require removal, repair, or replacement with prefix-safe equivalents. |
