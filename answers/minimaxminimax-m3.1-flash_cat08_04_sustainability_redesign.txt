## 3. Intervention options

*(Twelve options; each is tied to a path/segment, a mechanism, a service/quality/resilience effect, and the monitoring evidence required. S1, S5, S7, S8, S9 and S10 are the required minimum; S2–S4, S6, S11, S12 complete the set.)*

**S1 — Green shipping recommender (enabling control)**
*Target:* all shipping events (6,660 ground, 1,420 air, 850 truck, 620 van).
*Mechanism:* a rule that maps `promised_lead_time_hours`, route reliability (empirical ontime distribution per `route_mode`), `case_type`, `customer_type`, weight/fragility to a recommended route: ground by default; consolidated truck when slack 12–24h; air only when slack < ~12h or Critical; dedicated van only with a Critical justification.
*Effect:* converts the 22% SP1 violation in StandardService into compliant routing; service is protected because the rule is slackconstrained, not "greenfirst"; resilience improves by keeping 2 route options flagged for Servicecontract (R1).
*Evidence to monitor:* percase recommended vs actual route, override reason codes, SP1 violation rate, ontime rate of recommendervsnonrecommender orders, routemix shift, added transport kgCO2e from overrides.

**S2 — Dynamic batching with segment waitingtime caps**
*Target:* `Plan production  Batch wait  CNC production` (3,020 cases) and `CNC  Heat treatment` (1,820).
*Mechanism:* release a batch when `batch_size  12` **or** when the segment time cap is reached (e.g. 8h Servicecontract MTO, 24h Regular MTO), then a small residual batch; caps CNC_A energy/item near the 1.3–1.8 kWh band rather than the 2.8 kWh of batch 1–3.
*Effect:* energy down 10–25% for MTO; cycle time up but bounded by the cap; heattreat cases waiting for a batch carry 1.7× late risk, so heattreat batch fill must be protected separately; obsolescence is the main cost (6% of batch items when forecast is wrong)  only batch where demand is forecastvalidated or ordercommitted.
*Evidence to monitor:* batch_size histogram, batch_wait p50/p90 vs cap, energy/item vs batch curve, heattreat wait  late rate, % batch items unshipped at 14 days, inventory value/space.

**S3 — Rebalance MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A when slack allows**
*Target:* MTORegular (22%) and MTOService (12%) currently using the CNC_Binspectionrework path (5% of all orders).
*Mechanism:* CNC_A has 9% vs 18% rework on MTO, so switching removes a large share of the 470 rework loops; CNC_A also supports the heattreat batching curve.
*Effect:* energy down (32  16–24 kWh/order on affected cases) and scrap down sharply (1.8  ~0.7–0.9 kg); but CNC_A is slower in this flow (CNC_B path 160h vs CNC_A path 118h), so cycle time and late risk rise on cases without slack  apply only where duedate slack  ~48h; resilience risk is *load concentration* on CNC_A  cap CNC_A share (e.g. 60–65% of MTO volume) and keep CNC_B as the fallback (the machinelevel analogue of R1).
*Evidence to monitor:* CNC_A/CNC_B volume mix, CNC_A queue length, firstpass yield by machine, rework loops per inspection exit, heattreat queue, ontime rate of migrated cases.

**S4 — Replace air with priority pick + ground for StandardService where slack allows**
*Target:* Standardstock Servicecontract (16%; 1,920 orders), the 5% "stock  standard pack  air" path (18.5 kgCO2e/order) and the 22% SP1 violations.
*Mechanism:* the log states these orders are shipped by air even when the promise date still allows ground + priority picking.
*Effect:* transport down sharply on affected orders (18.5  3.6–7.8 kgCO2e); cycle time up by up to ~24h (air saves ~24h) — acceptable only where `promised_lead_time_hours` slack  that; service quality risk shifts from "fast but expensive" to "onpromise but slower"  monitor percustomer ontime, and use priority picking to hold the promise rather than paying for air.
*Resilience:* improves (ground network is more redundant than a single air lane).
*Evidence to monitor:* air shipment count in this segment, prioritypick handling time, ontime rate vs baseline 5%, transport kgCO2e/order, and any compensatory air shipments.

**S5 — Twicedaily consolidated truck departures for nonurgent stock orders**
*Target:* Standardstock Regular (39%) and StandardService orders with slack, currently ground parcel (6,660 shipments).
*Mechanism:* 35–55% kgCO2e reduction vs individual ground parcels, at a 12–24h consolidation wait.
*Effect:* transport down materially; cycle time up 12–24h, so it must be gated on slack (and on the 31h median / 62h p90 of StandardRegular — p90 means roughly onetenth of the segment will not tolerate the wait); keep a daily fallback departure so carrier/disruption resilience is not reduced to two chances per day; possible displacement: customers request air to recover the wait (see §5).
*Evidence to monitor:* truck shipment share, consolidation wait actual vs planned, ontime rate of truck orders, partialload rate, kgCO2e/order, frequency of "recovery air" shipments after truck delay.

**S6 — Packaging–route compatibility check before shipment**
*Target:* `Package standard/reinforced  Rework packaging` (620 events, ~5.2% of orders).
*Mechanism:* a preshipment check that packaging type, part geometry and `route_mode` are compatible (the log attributes rework packaging to incompatibility) and that Q1 (>8 kg reinforced) is respected; correct packaging choice once, at source.
*Effect:* package material and reworkhandling energy down; cycle time slightly down for affected cases (no repack/second handling); damagereturn risk down (reinforced cuts returns 40% for heavy MTO)  net carbon benefit, provided the check does not push all MTO to reinforced (which doubles material).
*Evidence to monitor:* packagingrework events per 100 shipments, package_material_kg/item, Q1 conformance, damage returns by packaging type and route, share of cases flagged/overridden by the check.

**S7 — Inspection quality gate for highrework machine/part combinations**
*Target:* `inspection_cell` exits into `Rework production` (470 loops  back to CNC 390), concentrated in CNC_B and heavy/awkward geometries.
*Mechanism:* a structured firstarticle/atrisk check plus a slowdown rule (no rushed inspection) for the flagged combinations; the log states rushed inspections miss defects that later cause packaging rework or customer returns.
*Effect:* rework loops and scrap down (each avoided loop avoids a reCNC pass of roughly 8–16 kWh plus scrap); inspection queue time up, but reworkloop queue relieved  net cycle time roughly neutral; late risk up only if inspection capacity is not increased; resilience up (less dependence on rushed decisions).
*Evidence to monitor:* firstpass yield by machine/geometry, rework loops per 100 inspection exits, inspection queue p50/p90, customer return rate, scrap kg/order, and inspection capacity utilisation.

**S8 — Replace some dedicated vans with shared emergency courier lanes**
*Target:* 620 dedicatedvan shipments (52 kgCO2e/order; 50,000 kgCO2e 40% of total transport emissions) — only the *precautious* subset (see S9).
*Mechanism:* consolidate emergency movements of different customers onto shared lanes to fill the van that "often returns empty" (no load-factor attribute exists today  create it).
*Effect:* transport down (plausible 15–40% on affected legs, low confidence until load factors are measured); cycle time up 2–8h; **not** applicable to true production downtime (violates the intent of SP2/R1) — dedicated van capacity must remain as standby; resilience risk is real (single courier dependency)  dualsource the lane.
*Evidence to monitor:* van load factor, emptyreturn km/kgCO2e, kgCO2e per Critical order, timetopickup on shared lanes, share of truedowntime cases still on dedicated van, disruptionscenario coverage.

**S9 — Split Criticaldowntime into true downtime and precautionary urgent**
*Target:* the 11% Critical segment (1,320 orders, 38 kgCO2e/order) and its two logged variants: emergency production + dedicated van (8%, 14h, 3% late, 52 kgCO2e) and the stock/ground exception (3%, 23h, 9% late, 1.2 kWh, 4.8 kgCO2e).
*Mechanism:* a triage at order intake that asks "is the line down now?" If yes  keep the protected express route (SP2 justification event, dedicated van/shared emergency lane). If no (precautionary)  offer a scheduled option or a ground/consolidated route with a guaranteed-by date, since the log shows a lowemission Critical variant already exists and is underused.
*Effect:* the main structural lever on transport emissions (41% of all transport emissions from 11% of orders) with limited service cost *if classification is accurate*; misclassification is the key risk (a mistriaged true downtime becomes a late delivery on a stopped line)  require customer/engineer confirmation and an SLA-protected fast lane; resilience improved because scarce express capacity is freed for genuine emergencies.
*Evidence to monitor:* true vs precautionary classification accuracy, ontime rate by class, kgCO2e/order per class, share of precautionary cases accepting the lowercarbon option, SP2 justification completeness, emergencyproduction share.

**S10 — Carbonaware promisedate negotiation for Regular madetoorder**
*Target:* MTORegular (22%; 1,440  2,640 orders; 18% late, 94h median, 180h p90).
*Mechanism:* at quote time offer two options — (a) current promise via expedited production/air (high emissions), (b) a later date aligned to a batch release + ground/consolidated route (lower emissions, ~+6–24h); the customer chooses.
*Effect:* on accepted (b) orders, energy falls (batched CNC_A) and transport falls (ground ~3.6–5.8 vs air 23); late rate should fall because the promise is now set against a feasible schedule (18%  target <10%); risk is acceptance rate and *demand displacement* — customers who reject may upgrade to a service contract or cancel (410 cancels + 60 backorder cancels already exist); also fairness/price issues and possible complaint spillover into servicecontract terms.
*Evidence to monitor:**offer acceptance rate by customer, emissions per accepted order, late rate before/after, cancellation rate, order loss/upgrade rate, effect on batch fill rates, and complaint volume.

**S11 — Change availabilitycheck rules to reduce partial shipments**
*Target:* `Availability check  Pick from stock  Partial shipment  Backorder wait` (1,010 partials, 7% path share, 79h median, 24% late, 7.2 kgCO2e vs 3.6 for single ground shipment).
*Mechanism:* an atomic (allornothing) or crossline availability check that confirms full order availability before committing, plus pooled stock visibility; fewer split shipments means fewer second legs, less packaging and less customer contact.
*Effect:* transport down (second leg eliminated on affected orders), cycle time down sharply for that path (79h  ~34–45h), late rate down (24%  ~8%); risk: stricter atomicity lengthens order release and may push some orders to partial/cancel, and more inventory must be held (carrying cost, storage energy, obsolescence)  pair with demandbased stocking; resilience: tighter commitment reduces the ability to ship "something now" during disruption  keep an explicit, logged exception for Critical/Servicecontract cases.
*Evidence to monitor:**partialshipment rate, backorderwait duration, secondshipment count and kgCO2e, cancellation rate, ontime rate of formerly partial orders, inventory turns/obsolescence.

**S12 — Sustainability exception log for SP1/SP2/Q1 overrides**
*Target:* all overrides (currently 22% SP1 violations in StandardService; 31% of van cases without justification; Q1 violations).
*Mechanism:* every override records case, rule, justification, approver, carbon delta, service consequence — turning the exception into a learning and governance object.
*Effect:* no direct emissions reduction; it is what makes S1/S4/S5/S8/S9 auditable, prevents silent policy erosion, and creates the dataset needed to reestimate the lowprecision model (exception cases are exactly the emergency/partial combinations where the model is weak).
*Evidence to monitor:* override rate per rule, justification completeness, carbon delta of overrides, and periodic review outcomes.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

1. **Batching  obsolescence rebound.** CNC_A energy/item falls 2.8  1.3 kWh at batch 12–20, but batch wait rises 4h  38h and **6% of batchproduced items are not shipped within 14 days** when the forecast is wrong. Unsold batches become inventory (storage energy, space) and eventual disposal (outside the current boundary) — a real carbon debit that is invisible in `material_scrap_kg`.
2. **Batching  heattreat service displacement.** The lowest heattreat energy/item occurs above batch 10, but heattreat batchwaiters have **1.7× latedelivery risk**. Energy savings in the heattreat furnace are directly converted into lateness and, in the worst case, into emergency production (760 cases) whose dedicatedvan legs carry ~52 kgCO2e/order — an order of magnitude worse than the energy saved.
3. **Machine rebalance  queue and concentration risk.** Shifting MTO from CNC_B to CNC_A cuts rework (18%  9%) and scrap (1.8  ~0.9 kg), but concentrates load on CNC_A and its downstream heattreat/inspection cells. The log explicitly identifies `inspection_cell` queues as the origin of rework loops (rushed inspections); adding load without capacity converts quality gains into more rushed inspections and returns — plus a singlemachine dependency.
4. **Transport consolidation  recoveryexpedite rebound.** Truck consolidation cuts kgCO2e 35–55% but adds a 12–24h wait; a missed consolidation window typically triggers an air recovery shipment (4–6× the emissions) plus a second packaging/handling event, so the net transport saving can be negative. Likewise, ground substitution (S4) lengthens transit ~24h and can trigger returns with reverse legs that outweigh the original saving.
5. **Packaging  returns displacement.** Reinforced packaging cuts damage returns 40% but **doubles package material**; standardising packaging for cost/carbon reasons raises damage returns, and the carbon of reverse logistics plus scrapped damaged parts (not in the boundary) may exceed the packaging carbon saved. Q1 overrides (lightweight packaging for >8 kg parts) sit exactly on this tradeoff.
6. **Qualitygate  capacity displacement.** A stricter inspection gate reduces rework but consumes inspection capacity; if inspection capacity is not added, queue time rises and the resulting lateness is again converted into air/van expediting — the "green" gate becomes the cause of the worstemission path.
7. **Availabilitycheck tightening  inventory and rigidity.** Fewer partial shipments (S11) mean more stock must be held to guarantee atomic availability: higher inventory carrying cost, warehouse space/energy, and obsolescence on wrong SKUs. It also reduces the ability to ship *something now* during disruption, which pushes demand toward emergency production and express couriers — exactly the paths with the highest emissions.
8. **Critical triage  misclassification.** S9 shifts part of the Critical segment to a loweremission, slower option. If a "true downtime" case is triaged as precautionary, the environmental gain is dwarfed by the service consequence (line down, contractual penalties, emergency production, dedicated van). If triage is conservative and everything stays precautionaryunsafe, the intervention simply fails to decarbonise.
9. **Customerbehaviour displacement.** Promisedate negotiation (S10) and slower "green" promises may push Regular MTO customers to upgrade to service contracts, split orders, or cancel (410 + 60 delaycancellations already observed); contractlevel "green" promises may also be accepted while consumption shifts elsewhere. Demandside displacement can erase the calculated benefit.
10. **Emissions displacement across the boundary.** Batching MTO to stock shifts production and inventory risk upstream/downstream; the supplier's production carbon for the 6% obsolescent units is not counted in the distributor's `energy_kwh`, so the true net effect is overstated unless the boundary is extended.
11. **Modelprecision displacement.** Acting on a lowprecision discovered model (emergency and partial paths combine in many ways) can mistarget interventions: e.g., treating a consolidationinduced delay as a routing problem, or reading a lowemission Critical variant as evidence that express lanes are generally unnecessary. Intervention credibility must not be built on the weakest segment of the log.
12. **Emissionmetric displacement.** If only `transport_kgco2e` and `energy_kwh` are tracked, the easy wins (route mix) get optimised while material waste (`material_scrap_kg`, obsolescence, package material) and rework loops — which are the true marginal hotspots for MTO — are ignored. Metrics must include returns and obsolescence or the organisation will buy transport savings with scrap and inventory.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

**Recommended redesign package (phased, with a shared decision layer).**

*Phase 0 — Make the problem measurable and governable (weeks).* Fix the event schema at lineitem level: add `promised_lead_time_hours` per line, `batch_release_reason`, `route_recommendation` + `override_reason`, `van_load_factor`, `packaging_rework_reason`, `return_event` (with reverseleg kgCO2e), `obsolescence_flag` (batch item not shipped within 14 days), `disruption_flag`, and `critical_class` (true downtime vs precautionary). Stand up **S12** (exception log) and conformance dashboards for SP1/SP2/Q1 from day one, because 22% SP1 violations, 31% missing SP2 justifications and Q1linked rework loops are the cheapest, fastest reductions available (they are policy compliance, not new investment).

*Phase 1 — Lowregret, serviceneutral or serviceimproving changes.* Launch **S1** as the single routing decision point, embedding **S4** (StdService air  priority pick + ground where slack  ~24h), **S5** (twicedaily truck for Regular stock with slack  12–24h plus a daily fallback), **S8** (shared emergency lanes, *precautious only*), and **S9** (Critical triage with a protected fast lane). Add **S6** (packaging–route/Q1 compatibility check at pack time) and **S7** (inspection gate for CNC_B and heavy/awkward geometries, with inspection capacity added so the gate is not queuenegative). Add **S11** (atomic/crossline availability check) with a logged exception for Critical and Servicecontract cases so resilience is not traded away.

*Phase 2 — Structural production changes.* **S2** dynamic batching with hard segment waiting caps (release on `batch_size  12` **or** cap reached), restricted to forecastvalidated or ordercommitted demand, with heattreat fill protected by a separate minimumlot rule; **S3** CNC_B  CNC_A migration only for cases with  ~48h duedate slack and with CNC_A volume capped ( ~60–65% of MTO) to preserve machinelevel fallback; **S10** carbonaware promisedate negotiation for MTORegular, optin, with acceptance and cancellation measured.

*Why this package balances the objectives.*
- **Environmental:** it attacks the actual hotspots rather than the visible ones — transport is 41% concentrated in Critical/downtime and express lanes, energy 87% in MTO, scrap 26% in the CNC_B rework path. The package moves each of these three independently, which reduces the chance of one objective crowding out another.
- **Service:** every environmental lever is *slackgated* (promised lead time, batch wait cap, duedate slack, triage class). Route changes are taken from cases that can afford them, so weighted late delivery should stay flat or improve (StdService airground and MTORegular promise dates are the only two places where a small local lateness increase is accepted, and both are monitored separately).
- **Quality:** the package does not buy carbon with damage: reinforced packaging is kept where Q1 applies, packaging is made routecompatible instead of blanketreinforced, and the inspection gate attacks the 470 rework loops at their origin (the rushedinspection mechanism the log identifies) rather than paying for them downstream.
- **Resilience:** no option removes a capability. Dedicatedvan capacity is retained as standby for true downtime (SP2 justification becomes explicit), at least two route options remain flagged for every Servicecontract case (R1), CNC_B stays as fallback for CNC_A, and consolidation retains a daily fallback. The residual accepted risks are documented and monitored, not assumed away.
- **Antirebound by design:** batching is paired with obsolescence tracking and caps; consolidation is paired with a fallback departure and recoveryexpedite monitoring; inventory tightening (S11) is paired with demandbased stocking; triage (S9) is paired with SLAprotected fast lanes; customerfacing changes (S10) are optin with acceptance tracking.

**Explicitly not recommended:** blanket removal of air or dedicated vans; switching all MTO to CNC_A; eliminating partial shipments at the cost of atomicavailability failures for Critical orders; or any batching rule without a waitingtime cap and an obsolescence guardrail. The lowestemission option is never chosen where it clearly breaks the service, quality or resilience requirements — in each of those cases the constraint is treated as a hard boundary and the decarbonisation is delivered on the subpopulation that has slack.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**A. Instrumentation (prerequisite, Phase 0)**
Reissue the event schema at orderline granularity with the new attributes listed in §6. Reconcile the DFG conservation gaps (packaging in/out 130 and 550 events; `Confirm delivery`+cancels = 11,730 vs 12,000) before any baseline is frozen; publish a dataquality note with every model release. Remine the log after implementation to replace the lowprecision discovered model, using the exception log's labelled cases (emergency, partial, disruption combinations) as a supervised ground truth.

**B. Baselines and comparison design**
Freeze a 3month preintervention baseline per segment (case_type × customer_type) with the §2 indicators, including seasonal disruption weeks. Implement interventions as a steppedwedge / phased rollout across the 12,000order population with a matched control group of comparable segments (e.g. StandardRegular not yet eligible for truck consolidation) to separate intervention effects from demand mix. Use the log's own paths (rather than averages) for comparison: "stock  standard pack  ground", "MTO  batch wait  CNC_A  heat  inspection  reinforced  ground", "MTO  CNC_B  inspection  rework  ground", "Critical  emergency  reinforced  dedicated van", "stock  partial  ground + backorder" are the natural A/B strata. Segmentlevel service monitoring must be weekly, not pooled, because pooled averages hide the MTORegular (18% late) and partialshipment (24% late) tails.

**C. Conformance monitoring (SP1 / SP2 / Q1)**
- **SP1:** current violation 22% of StandardService cases. Target 5% within 8 weeks and 2% steady state, with the exception log capturing *why* (no slack available, customerrequested, route reliability failure). Monthly review of each violation by route and promisedate band; any case where ground/truck was feasible at >85% probability but not used is a process defect, not an exception.
- **SP2:** 31% of dedicatedvan cases currently lack a justification event. Target 0% missing justification within 4 weeks (a datacapture fix, not a behaviour change). Every van case must carry `critical_class = true_downtime` plus an engineer/approver signoff; monthly check that truedowntime share of van cases does not fall below the level justified by linestoppage data.
- **Q1:** violations currently correlate with return/rework loops. Target 2% of >8 kg MTO shipments without either reinforced packaging or an `inspection_certified_low_damage` event; link each override to a subsequent return event and review quarterly.
- Dashboard ownership: operations owns SP1, quality owns Q1, logistics/engineering owns SP2; the exception log is reviewed weekly by a named sustainabilityoperations owner, with escalation after 2 consecutive weeks of breach.

**D. Guardrail metrics and rollback triggers**
Rollback (pause the affected intervention, restore prior rule) if any of: weighted late delivery rises >1 pp over 2 consecutive weeks in the affected segment; MTORegular late rate worsens; Servicecontract late rate exceeds its baseline (5%/13%/4%); heattreatwaiter late rate rises toward 1.7× baseline without a compensating improvement; CNC_A queue p90 breaches the cap or CNC_A share exceeds the concentration limit; obsolescence of batchproduced items exceeds baseline 6% +2 pp; R1 alternativeroute coverage for Servicecontract falls below 100% in the simulated disruption scenario; emergencyproduction share rises (a sign that batching/consolidation is pushing cases into the worstemission path); packagingrework events per 100 shipments do not fall after S6/S7.

**E. Carbon and lifecycle accounting discipline**
Freeze a single emissionfactor set and document whether `transport_kgco2e` is welltowheel; add the missing flows (empty van returns, reverse legs for returns, packaging disposal, obsolescent stock) so batching and packaging are evaluated on a likeforlike basis; report emissions **per order delivered on time** alongside perorder emissions so that service and carbon are never traded silently; publish a quarterly sustainabilityoperations statement per segment covering energy, transport, materials, rework, obsolescence, service, quality and resilience together.

**F. Continuous improvement loop**
Monthly: refresh indicators; compare against the §4 planning ranges and replace them with measured effect sizes and confidence intervals; rerun the processmining model on the enriched log (now with route reliability, slack, load factors and truedowntime labels) to retune the recommender thresholds, batch caps and triage rules. Quarterly: review the exception log for patterns (which overrides are productive, which are silent policy erosion), audit the carbon accounting assumptions, and retest the resilience scenarios (carrier outage, machine breakdown, surge demand) to confirm that the redesign's environmental gains survive a disruption — because the log shows disruption periods are exactly where precision is lowest and where the temptation to emit more is highest.
