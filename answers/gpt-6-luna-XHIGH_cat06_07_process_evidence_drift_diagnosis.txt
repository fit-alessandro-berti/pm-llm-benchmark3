## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January–March:** This is the stable reference period: metrics are strong, labels are fully mature, unknown activity is below 0.3%, and variant shares are nearly constant. V3 already has the highest breach rate, so its later deterioration should be compared with its own baseline rather than only with the overall rate.
- **April:** This is primarily a schema and pipeline incident, not clean evidence of underlying concept drift. The renamed diagnosis activity caused wrong or missing prediction prefixes; duplicated and UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY events, missing queue values, and the corrupted cached prefix feature provide direct mechanisms for the metric collapse. The observed 0.19 breach rate is mature, but the predictions and feature rows are not trustworthy. The 18% unmapped/other variants reinforce that this month is not comparable without reconstruction.
- **May:** The mapping patch largely restored the event label and metrics improved, but residual unknown activities and missingness remain, and April's cached count was not completely recomputed. May's 0.78 AUROC and 0.050 ECE are better than April but still worse than baseline. Treat May as a pipeline-recovery month until an end-to-end recomputation confirms it.
- **June:** This is a real routing and mix change: V2 rose to 52%, while V3's share fell to 18%. The remote edge duration stayed stable and V2's matured breach rate improved to 0.09, consistent with the stated policy and not evidence of general process deterioration. Aggregate metrics can shift because the mix and base rate changed; June is fully mature and useful for segment-specific evaluation, but it represents a new operating regime.
- **July:** The supplier issue is visible in the Order Part → Part Received median (65 hours versus 42 in June), and V3's matured breach rate rose to 0.46. This is early evidence of real downstream process deterioration that the post-diagnosis model may not observe at score time. With 98% label maturity, July is informative, subject to a maturity check and the changed mix.
- **August:** The supplier delay became severe (130 hours) and V3's matured breach rate reached 0.64. V3 also expanded to 35%. AUROC and calibration worsened as process deterioration and variant-mix change overlap. The overall observed breach rate is based on 93% mature labels, so report it as provisional; the within-V3 matured evidence is more diagnostic but still needs path and feature conditioning.
- **September:** The parts path remained slow, V3 grew to 42%, and Partner Z introduced a new, more rural population with a higher V3 share. Process drift, population shift, covariate shift, and possible concept drift now overlap. The overall observed 0.31 and Partner Z's observed 0.45 are not final because only 82% of labels are mature and Partner Z matures more slowly. Separate source, variant, and label-age effects before attributing the gap.
- **October:** The process still shows a slow parts edge and high V3 share, but only 55% of labels are mature and the API backlog selectively delays long-path cases. The 0.16 observed rate is therefore not evidence that outcomes improved; it could be strongly downward-biased, though the eventual rate cannot be inferred from this table alone. Predictive metrics computed on these available labels are provisional and potentially selection-biased.

The months represent different failure mechanisms: April is chiefly broken measurement and features; June is a route-mix/policy change with stable remote timing; August–September combine worsening parts performance with mix and population shifts and plausible conditional drift; October is dominated by outcome censoring on top of ongoing process changes.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting.** For each case-start month, plot the fraction labeled by case age (for example, 15/30/45/60/90 days), overall and by variant, source, and eventual resolution-path length. Recompute AUROC, Brier, ECE, and breach rate using labels available at the same fixed age and again on a sufficiently mature cohort. Under censoring, October and Partner Z rates should move materially as later labels arrive, especially for long paths; under real outcome improvement, comparable fixed-vintage and mature estimates should remain lower. Check whether labels are missing at random conditional on path and source; if not, use appropriate censoring adjustment or wait for maturity.
2. **Normalize activity labels, clean duplicates, and recompute.** Rebuild April and May from raw events: map both diagnosis labels to one canonical activity before prefix construction, apply the same documented duplicate rule across sources, restore queue features, and recompute every derived field, including the cached event count and prediction prefix. Compare row-level event order, prefix timestamp, and predictions before and after. If pipeline failure dominates, April's invalid-prefix/missingness rates and anomalous score distribution should largely resolve. If genuine concept drift dominates, poor conditional outcomes or scores will remain after reconstruction. Retain an audit trail of changed/deleted events rather than silently overwriting them.
3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month.** On mature, consistently processed labels, report AUROC and precision/recall at operational thresholds, plus reliability curves, Brier score, and calibration intercept/slope for V1/V2/V3 and Partner Z/other sources. Compare both raw metrics and estimates standardized to a fixed reference mix. If composition shift dominates, standardized metrics and within-segment performance should be near stable even as raw aggregate metrics move. If conditional drift exists, mature within-segment calibration or discrimination should deteriorate, particularly for V3 or within comparable source/path strata.
4. **Compare variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges.** Normalize activity names, then compare monthly case variant counts and transition frequencies alongside edge-duration distributions (median and upper quantiles), especially Order Part → Part Received and diagnosis → Remote Fix. Use both event-calendar and case-start cohorts and record incomplete traces. Under mix shift alone, variant frequencies change while within-variant edge distributions remain stable. Under process drift, relevant edge durations change within V3 (as observed for parts) even after holding variant composition constant; the remote edge should remain stable if June's change is chiefly routing mix.
5. **Test whether the outcome relationship changed conditional on the same path.** On mature cases, compare breach risk across months within exact or carefully grouped variants and stable pre-score features, controlling for source and case mix. Then add process regime indicators and only features available at prediction time. Test interaction terms or month-specific calibration on held-out time periods. Under pure mix shift, conditional risks and residual calibration should be stable. Under concept drift, the same score/features/path will have different mature outcome rates or residual patterns. If adding the supplier state known at diagnosis explains the difference, the model had a missing predictive signal; if only post-diagnosis wait time explains it, that identifies a downstream process mechanism, not a usable feature for this score without a valid at-score estimate.
6. **Partner Z population reweighting / transport check.** Compare distributions and support of available pre-score features for Partner Z and other sources; estimate standardized Partner Z outcomes by reweighting non-Z cases to Z's feature/variant distribution, and evaluate the model on mature Z cases with uncertainty intervals. Under covariate/population shift with a stable conditional relationship, reweighting or transport should largely explain the aggregate gap and calibration should recover in supported regions. Under a source-specific conditional difference, a residual gap remains within common support after adjustment. If support is poor, report that transport is unsupported rather than extrapolating; also assess errors and interventions by relevant customer segments before making fairness claims.
7. **Feature missingness and lineage audit.** For each field, compare availability, value distributions, source system, event timestamp, and transformation version by month. Verify that the scoring event is exactly after the canonical diagnosis event and that no post-score activity leaked into features. Under pipeline failure, April/May abnormalities should align with source/mapping/version and often disappear on a reproducible rebuild. Under real covariate shift, distributions should move consistently in clean data with intact lineage. Missing queue features should be monitored as both a quality defect and a possible source of score degradation.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

1. **Immediate containment:** Mark April predictions and derived features as untrusted, and do not use April for training, calibration, or a performance claim. Until the normalization and prefix rebuild is verified, gate or visibly qualify scores for records with an unrecognized/missing diagnosis event or missing critical queue inputs; use the existing operational fallback for those cases. For current V3 cases, surface the supplier delay as an operational risk and avoid relying on a stale absolute probability for threshold decisions. Do not interpret October's available-label metrics as final.
2. **Short-term recalibration:** Once labels are mature enough and the pipeline is clean, recalibrate only if discrimination remains serviceable within the intended deployment segments while predicted probabilities are systematically off. Evaluate intercept/slope and reliability by variant and source, and validate on a later time block. A single global calibrator can hide opposite errors across V2 and V3; compare a global adjustment with a sufficiently supported hierarchical or segment calibrator. Calibration cannot repair rank loss or missing predictive information.
3. **Feature repair:** Finish the April/May event normalization, consistent deduplication, and complete feature recomputation first. Add or repair robust at-score features for route intent, queue state, supplier/parts availability, rural travel burden, device age, and source when operationally available, with explicit lineage and missingness handling. Do not use realized post-score wait duration as an input to a prediction made immediately after diagnosis; use a contemporaneous estimate or create a separately timed prediction after the information becomes available.
4. **Full retraining:** Retrain after the feature pipeline is stable and the mature-label evaluation shows sustained within-segment conditional deterioration, meaningful ranking loss, or a changed supported population that the old model cannot handle. Include clean examples from the newer regimes and validate by time, variant, and source. Retain a mature pre-change reference set for comparison, but do not let January–March alone represent current supplier and routing conditions.

**Training data:** January–March are the cleanest mature baseline. April is unsafe until fully rebuilt from raw records. May is unsafe pending verification of complete feature recomputation and consistent duplicate treatment. June is fully mature and can be used for the remote-routing regime after pipeline checks. July is nearly mature and can be considered with explicit label-age validation. August and September are provisional and should not be treated as complete training cohorts until their labels mature, with extra care for Partner Z. October is unsafe for outcome training or final evaluation at 55% maturity. A training set can use eligible mature cases from later months with explicit selection/censoring controls, but a naive cutoff would underrepresent slow paths and risky cases.

A variant-specific model is not automatically justified by these aggregate tables. It becomes reasonable if mature data show stable, materially different conditional relationships, enough cases per segment, and the route is known at scoring time. Because the specified score is issued immediately after diagnosis, a model must not condition on the eventual V1/V2/V3 path if that path is selected later. If routing is already known, a route-conditioned or hierarchical model can be validated; otherwise use only route-intent information available at diagnosis. Partner Z should first be handled through representative features, reweighting, calibration and supported subgroup evaluation, rather than a separate source model based solely on immature raw rates.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

- **Predictive performance:** Track AUROC and threshold utility on mature, fixed-vintage cohorts, with confidence intervals and variant/source breakdowns. Keep discrimination distinct from calibration and business utility.
- **Calibration:** Track Brier score, calibration intercept/slope, reliability curves, and ECE on the same eligible cohort. Alert on both aggregate and segment deviations, with minimum sample and maturity requirements.
- **Label maturity:** Track label completeness and delay distributions by case-start month, source, variant, and resolution-path length. Display as-of date and maturity alongside every outcome metric; compare only matched vintages or mature cohorts.
- **Data quality:** Monitor canonical diagnosis-event recognition, duplicate rates, UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY share, missingness, event-order violations, prefix timing, schema/version lineage, and recomputation coverage. Treat a broken scoring prefix as a pipeline incident and gate affected metrics/training.
- **Process variants:** Track case-start variant mix, unmapped variants, route-policy version, and variant transitions. Alert on mix movement even if model performance has not yet changed.
- **Performance-DFG:** Track edge frequency and elapsed-time distributions, especially Order Part → Part Received, as well as diagnosis → Remote Fix. Retain both medians and upper quantiles; case mix and censored/incomplete traces should be shown.
- **Population:** Monitor source/partner shares, rural distance, device age, feature support and missingness, with mature subgroup outcomes. Use overlap checks before transport or reweighting conclusions.
- **Intervention and deployment:** Record score, threshold, action, route decision, override, capacity, and later outcome. Watch for intervention effects and feedback (for example, the model changing routing, which changes later variant mix), and separately monitor business outcomes such as promised-date breach and service capacity.

Alert dependencies should prevent a label-delay alarm from automatically launching retraining. Low maturity marks outcome metrics provisional and starts fixed-vintage follow-up; it does not itself diagnose concept drift. A data-quality alarm invalidates affected model metrics until repaired. A process-edge or variant-mix alarm triggers operational and stratified model review. Retraining evaluation is triggered only when clean-data, sufficiently mature, comparable cohorts show persistent conditional performance deterioration; deployment follows separate validation and approval.

The following sketch assumes a local CSV with `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`, and optionally `source`, `event_id`, and `queue_feature`. It returns nested, auditable process summaries, not model fits. Event-calendar cohorts contain only events occurring in that calendar month (so traces may be partial); case-start cohorts contain all available events for cases whose first event occurred that month. Neither cohort by itself makes outcome labels mature.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
DIAGNOSIS_ALIASES = {
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
}


def variant_counts(frame):
    counts = {}
    for variant, value in pm4py.get_variants(
        frame, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE
    ).items():
        try:
            count = len(value)       # Some PM4Py versions return traces.
        except TypeError:
            count = int(value)       # Others return counts.
        counts[" > ".join(map(str, variant))] = count
    return counts


def one_summary(frame, month, cohort, duplicate_count):
    row = {
        "month": month,
        "cohort": cohort,
        "cases": int(frame[CASE].nunique()),
        "events": int(len(frame)),
        "duplicate_rows_removed_from_month": int(duplicate_count),
        "unknown_activity_share": float(frame[ACT].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean())
        if len(frame) else None,
        "queue_missing_share": float(frame["queue_feature"].isna().mean())
        if len(frame) and "queue_feature" in frame else None,
    }
    if frame.empty:
        row.update({"variant_counts": {}, "dfg": {}, "performance_dfg": {},
                    "part_wait_median_hours": None, "temporal_features": []})
        return row

    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(
        frame, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE
    )
    perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        frame, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE,
        perf_aggregation_key="median",
    )
    part_wait_seconds = perf.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"))
    if hasattr(part_wait_seconds, "total_seconds"):
        part_wait_hours = part_wait_seconds.total_seconds() / 3600
    elif part_wait_seconds is not None:
        part_wait_hours = float(part_wait_seconds) / 3600
    else:
        part_wait_hours = None

    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        frame, grouper_freq="M", activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS,
        case_id_key=CASE,
    )
    row.update({
        "variant_counts": variant_counts(frame),
        "dfg": {f"{a} -> {b}": int(n) for (a, b), n in dfg.items()},
        "performance_dfg": {f"{a} -> {b}": value
                             for (a, b), value in perf.items()},
        "part_wait_median_hours": part_wait_hours,
        "temporal_features": temporal.to_dict(orient="records"),
    })
    return row


def monthly_process_evidence(csv_path):
    raw = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    raw[TS] = pd.to_datetime(raw[TS], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    raw = raw.dropna(subset=[CASE, ACT, TS]).copy()

    # Canonicalize before ordering, prefix/temporal feature extraction, and DFGs.
    raw[ACT] = raw[ACT].replace(DIAGNOSIS_ALIASES)

    # Prefer source-scoped event IDs. Without IDs, remove exact source/case/
    # activity/timestamp duplicates only; audit collisions because same-time
    # repeated activities may be legitimate. Apply the same rule to every source.
    source_key = ["source"] if "source" in raw.columns else []
    if "event_id" in raw.columns:
        duplicate_key = source_key + ["event_id"]
    else:
        duplicate_key = source_key + [CASE, ACT, TS]
    duplicate_mask = raw.duplicated(duplicate_key, keep="first")
    duplicate_month = raw.loc[duplicate_mask, TS].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").value_counts()
    raw = raw.loc[~duplicate_mask].copy()

    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
    )
    case_start = log.groupby(CASE)[TS].min()
    start_month = case_start.dt.strftime("%Y-%m")
    months = pd.period_range(
        log[TS].min().strftime("%Y-%m"), log[TS].max().strftime("%Y-%m"), freq="M"
    )
    summaries = []

    for period in months:
        month = str(period)
        start = pd.Timestamp(month, tz="UTC")
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1) - pd.Timedelta(microseconds=1)

        # Event-calendar view: events in the month, even when their cases began earlier.
        event_cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log, start.isoformat(), end.isoformat(), mode="events",
            timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE,
        )
        summaries.append(one_summary(
            event_cohort, month, "event_calendar", duplicate_month.get(month, 0)
        ))

        # Case-start view: all available events for cases that started in the month.
        started_ids = set(start_month.index[start_month.eq(month)])
        start_cohort = log[log[CASE].isin(started_ids)].copy()
        summaries.append(one_summary(start_cohort, month, "case_start", 0))

    return pd.DataFrame(summaries)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

- **Retraining on immature labels:** October and some September/Partner Z cases are selected toward shorter, faster-resolving cases. A naive retrain could learn that long-path cases are safer, or otherwise distort both prevalence and path relationships. **Mitigation:** wait for maturity or use a justified censoring-aware method; train and compare on fixed-vintage cohorts, with path/source maturity reporting.
- **Calibrating around a pipeline bug:** A calibrator fit to April's inflated risks or malformed prefixes would encode a temporary measurement failure into production scores. **Mitigation:** exclude April until raw-event normalization, duplicate handling, and full feature recomputation are audited; then evaluate calibration on clean, mature data.
- **Ignoring genuine supplier-driven deterioration:** Treating August's poor results as only a metric or label problem could leave V3 cases under-prioritized while the part wait has doubled or tripled and V3 breaches have risen. **Mitigation:** monitor the parts edge, surface supplier/route risk operationally, and evaluate mature V3 outcomes separately while repairing predictive signals available at score time.
- **Attributing Partner Z's raw gap to unfair model behavior without stratification:** Z has a different rural/device mix, more V3 cases, and slower label maturity, so the 0.45 versus 0.27 observed rate is confounded. **Mitigation:** compare mature outcomes within common support, stratify by variant and source, reweight carefully, and measure model errors and interventions across customer segments before drawing a fairness conclusion.
- **Treating June's route mix change as blanket concept drift:** This could trigger needless retraining when V2's share rose but its remote edge stayed stable and its matured breach rate improved. **Mitigation:** standardize aggregate metrics to a reference mix and inspect within-variant metrics before deciding that conditional relationships changed.
- **Using future process information as a feature:** Adding realized Order Part → Part Received duration to a score issued after diagnosis would leak future information and create a model that cannot operate as evaluated. **Mitigation:** enforce timestamp-based feature availability; use only supplier status or expected wait estimates known at score time, or explicitly define a later scoring point.
