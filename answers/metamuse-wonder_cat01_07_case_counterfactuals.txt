## 1. Feasibility model

```text
PARTIAL ORDER (applies conditionally by case)

Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Document Check                    [if no originals required]
Triage < Originals Receipt < Document Check [if originals required]
Identity Check may be concurrent with Document Check [if no originals required]
Identity Check may precede / overlap Originals Receipt and Document Check [if originals required]
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment < Compliance Review < Decision   [if high-risk OR high-value; Compliance after LATEST relevant Assessment]
Assessment < Decision                       [if not high-risk/high-value]
Assessment -> Request More Info -> Document Check(new) -> Assessment(new)
Compliance Review -> Request More Info -> Document Check(new) -> Assessment(new) -> Compliance Review(new/reaffirmed)
Decision < Notify
Escalation Advice / preliminary Compliance screen: no fixed position after Triage,
  may overlap Identity/Document/Assessment, but does NOT satisfy final Compliance Review
```

```text
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS

C1. Originals: Document Check start >= Originals Receipt completion. Identity Check is not blocked by originals.
C2. Concurrency: Identity || Document allowed only when (a) no originals required, or (b) originals already received for the document leg. Resource non-overlap still applies per person.
C3. Final compliance: IF high-risk OR high-value THEN a final Compliance Review by C1 must start after completion of the final Assessment and before Decision. Preliminary work only reduces its service time, by guidance 1-3 h if evidence is reusable.
C4. Rework closure: IF Request More Info is issued THEN no Decision until a new Document Check and a new Assessment complete; high-risk/high-value then also need new/reaffirmed final Compliance Review.
C5. Four-eyes: resource(final Assessment) != resource(final Compliance Review). In these cases A1/A2 vs C1 satisfies this; S1 advice is not Compliance Review.
C6. Resource capacity: for any resource r, service intervals assigned to r must not overlap. Queues in snapshots are wait forecasts at that timestamp, not reserved slots or guarantees; waits evolve with arrivals.
C7. Decision integrity: suppressing a Request More Info loop (K-337) does not waive C3/C5 and does not permit ignoring a known discrepancy; Decision must be approve-with-conditions or reject with the discrepancy recorded.
C8. Timestamp validity: logged concurrency is valid only if start/complete reflect real work intervals, not batch entry; back-dated parallel logs would falsely satisfy C2.
C9. External events: Originals Receipt and supplier responses are exogenous waits (rework collection 8-36 h guidance, and slower over weekends); they are not compressible by internal routing.
```

Explanation of intervention types: K-104 alters *timing/preparation only* — it adds a parallel preliminary escalation/compliance screen and a provisional C1 reservation, but the final Compliance Review still follows the final Assessment, so routing and decision policy are unchanged. K-411 alters *timing and task-creation routing* — it removes an artificial sequential lock so two already-required checks run concurrently; the set of required activities and decision policy are unchanged. K-219 alters *resource routing* — the same Document Check is reassigned D1D2 and started earlier; activity structure is unchanged but service-time and error distributions change. K-337 alters the *allowed decision policy* — it deletes a normatively triggered rework loop (Rule 7) and forces a decision under unresolved evidence, which changes what Decision may legitimately conclude, not merely when it occurs.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

- `Factual mechanism:` The sanctions ambiguity was observable at Triage (Mon 08:50) but traveled invisibly until A1 rediscovered it at Assessment completion (Tue 19:10). S1 advice (Tue 19:20–21:20) only recommended what was already inevitable, and C1 began cold on Wed 13:10 after a 16–20 h compliance queue, spending 4.5 h largely reading evidence from scratch. Total delay is therefore discovery latency + queue wait + cold-start service, in series.
- `Minimal intervention:` Change only Triage behavior: at ~Mon 08:50, emit a preliminary packet to S1 and place a provisional (not guaranteed) C1 reservation targeted after the expected Assessment. Nothing else changes: Identity (I1) and Document (D1, complex) still run Mon–Tue, final Assessment remains A1's, and final Compliance Review remains after it. This is the smallest deviation because no activity is deleted, reordered normatively, or reassigned.
- `Temporal propagation:` S1's preliminary advice can overlap the Mon document queue (D1 wait until 21:40) rather than sitting after Assessment, so the sanctions framing arrives ~1 day earlier at zero critical-path cost if S1 has slack. C1 preliminary reading during Tue can remove 1–3 h from the final review's 3–6 h range. The binding question is whether the provisional slot survives: C1's queue was still 16 h Tue 08:00 and two urgent cases were added Mon 16:00, so the reservation either (a) holds a Wed-morning slot, pulling Decision from Wed 18:00 to roughly Wed late morning, or (b) is bumped by urgent cases, in which case only the 1–3 h service saving remains and Decision lands Wed afternoon. Earlier readiness of the compliance leg does not guarantee earlier Decision because M1 and the final-review ordering still gate it.
- `Process-feasibility check:` Order preserved: Register < Triage < {Identity, Document} < Assessment (A1, Tue evening, assuming unchanged queues) < final Compliance (C1) < Decision < Notify. Four-eyes: A1  C1, satisfied. Preliminary C1/S1 work is explicitly non-substitutive (Rule 10), so no compliance is falsely claimed early. Resource non-overlap requires the provisional slot to be a real reservation against C1's calendar; if C1 does prelim reading Tue, that time must be booked and cannot coincide with other C1 reviews.
- `Best/base/worst case:` Best: packet complete, slot holds, final review 2–3 h Wed early  Decision Wed ~10:00–12:00 (6 to 8 h clock, effectively same-day morning). Base: partial reuse, slot slips a few hours  Decision Wed midday/afternoon (3 to 6 h). Worst: packet misleading or slot bumped by urgent compliance arrivals  final review re-reads everything, Decision unchanged or even later Wed evening, plus another case was displaced by the idle/held reservation.
- `Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` Logs showing C1 preliminary work is rarely reusable (e.g., prelim notes not referenced in final reviews), reservation no-show/slip rates, or displacement data showing urgent cases breaching deadlines on Mon–Tue would reverse the benefit claim. Conversely, timestamps showing S1 was actually idle Mon would strengthen it.

- `Factual mechanism:` The case waited ~16 h for D1 not because D1 is slow — D1 finished in 4 h accurately — but because D1's queue (13 h Mon 08:00, 11 h Mon 16:00) pushed the start to Tue 09:30 despite originals arriving Mon 17:30. Assessment then waited until Tue 20:00 (A2 queue). The factual bottleneck is preferred-resource queueing, partially justified by handwriting risk.
- `Minimal intervention:` Reassign one activity's resource: Document Check D1D2, startable at originals receipt Mon 17:30. Identity path, Assessment (A2), Decision, and the no-compliance status are untouched. No activity is added or removed in the base path.
- `Temporal propagation:` D2's queue was much shorter Mon (2 h at 08:00, 5 h at 16:00), so D2 could plausibly start Mon evening or Tue ~08:00, 1.5–9 h earlier than D1's start. But D2's service on standard documents is 4–8 h vs D1's 3–6 h, and handwritten material plausibly pushes D2 toward or beyond the top of that range while D1's handwriting expertise was the original routing reason. Net document completion ranges from Tue ~04:00 (optimistic, unlikely given working hours) to Tue ~16:00. That earlier completion only converts to earlier Decision if A2 has capacity: A2's queue was 4–7 h across Mon–Tue, so Assessment plausibly moves from Tue 20:00 to Tue afternoon/late evening, and Decision from Wed 08:30 to Tue evening (if M1 available) or stays Wed morning. In the rework branch, a D2 clarification triggers a new Document Check + Assessment (Rule 7 logic), adding external wait 8–36 h and erasing the head start.
- `Process-feasibility check:` Originals Receipt (Mon 17:30) precedes D2's Document Check — satisfied; Identity (I2) already completed Mon 16:20 and in any case may precede originals. No compliance review required (not high-risk/value), so Decision after Assessment is legal. Four-eyes rule is inapplicable (no compliance review). D2 non-overlap must be checked against D2's actual Mon-evening commitments, which the snapshot forecasts but does not guarantee.

Base: D2 starts Tue morning, 6–8 h service, Assessment Tue evening, Decision Wed 08:30 unchanged-to-slightly-earlier  0 to 8 h, with benefit mostly as earlier readiness. Worst: D2 requests clarification on handwriting, external response + re-check + re-assessment  Decision Thu or later (+24 h or more vs factual) and possible quality downgrade on first pass.

### K-337

- `Factual mechanism:` Elapsed time is dominated not by internal service (each Document Check/Assessment is 2.5–6 h) but by two external supplier waits: Wed 11:30Thu 08:00 and Fri 10:30Mon 09:00 (weekend-amplified). Each Assessment legitimately found supplier inconsistencies, triggering Rule 7 rework. The second loop consumed FriTue to resolve one remaining inconsistency, after which compliance and a clean approve followed.
- `Minimal intervention:` Delete exactly the second loop: after Assessment 2 (Fri 10:30), no Request More Info 2, no Document Check 3, no Assessment 3. Add nothing except an explicit treatment of the discrepancy inside the (still mandatory) Compliance Review and Decision. This is minimal in path length but maximal in kind: it changes the decision policy, because the evidence state at decision time is now "one inconsistency unresolved" rather than "evidence accepted."
- `Temporal propagation:` Removing the loop removes the FriMon external wait and Mon–Tue internal re-checks, so the case becomes compliance-ready Fri 10:30 instead of Tue 10:30. However, final completion now depends on C1's Fri/Mon availability, which is not in the snapshots for that week (nearest readings: C1 queue 5 h Thu, 8 h Wed, clearing trend). If C1 takes it Fri afternoon or Mon, Decision is Mon–Tue vs factual Wed — saving roughly 1.5–3 working days, less than the naive 4-day subtraction because weekend and C1 queue intervene. Note the first rework loop is retained, so supplier response behavior before Fri is unchanged.
- `Process-feasibility check:` High-risk + high-value, so final Compliance Review (C1) after the latest Assessment (Assessment 2, A1) and before Decision is mandatory and retained; A1  C1 satisfies four-eyes. Rule 7 is being deliberately overridden by the intervention — feasible only as a policy exception where C1 documents the discrepancy, its materiality assessment, and either conditions (e.g., payment hold pending corrected schedule) or rejection rationale. A silent approve identical to the factual one would be non-compliant with the intervention's own terms and indefensible. Notify after Decision, retained.
- `Best/base/worst case:` Best: discrepancy is immaterial/typographical; C1 reviews Fri, M1 approves with enforceable conditions Mon  2 to 3 working days, no loss. Base: approve-with-conditions or reject late Mon/Tue  similar time saving, but outcome changed from clean approve; conditions require monitoring work not in the factual path. Worst: the inconsistency concealed a real supplier error; unconditional-feeling approval leads to loss/appeal, or rejection triggers appeal and re-application whose total work exceeds the saved loop — system time worsens even though this case closed faster.
- `Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` Materiality data on the remaining inconsistency (size of totals discrepancy relative to exposure), supplier error/appeal history, and enforceability of conditions. If such discrepancies historically predict loss, the "saving" reverses sign; if they are almost always clerical, the case for suppression strengthens but remains a quality-risk trade, not a pure time win.

### K-411

- `Factual mechanism:` Nothing about the work required sequence: digital standard documents, no originals. A legacy lock created only the identity task, so D2 sat idle-capable until identity finished Tue 13:20, document check ran Tue 15:00–19:30, and the case then missed Tuesday assessment capacity, starting Wed 09:30. Delay = artificial serialization + a queue-phase effect (arriving at Assessment after Tue capacity was gone).
- `Minimal intervention:` At Triage, create both tasks and permit concurrent ownership with real-time timestamping. Same resources (I2, D2, A2), same activities, same decision policy — only a system/routing constraint is relaxed. This is the purest timing intervention of the four.
- `Temporal propagation:` Identity: I2 queue ~2–3 h Tue morning, service 1.5–3.5 h  done Tue ~12:30–14:00 (factual 13:20). Document in parallel: D2 queue 3–4 h (Tue snapshot), service ~4.5 h as factual  done Tue ~16:00–19:00, i.e., both checks complete Tue afternoon rather than document alone ending 19:30. The critical uncertainty is Assessment: A2's queue was 5–6 h Tue and grew by Wed in the factual run. If A2 can absorb the case Tue evening, Assessment finishes Tue night and Decision could be Tue evening/Wed early (6 to 20 h). If not, Assessment still starts Wed 09:30 and the entire parallel gain is absorbed by the queue — earlier readiness, identical completion. Expected saving is therefore bimodal, not a smooth subtraction.
- `Process-feasibility check:` No originals required, so Identity || Document is expressly allowed (Rule 3). Both complete before Assessment; no compliance review needed (not high-risk/value); Decision < Notify retained. Feasibility hinges on C8: the workaround must log true start/complete times. If staff batch-enter timestamps later, apparent concurrency is fabricated and the audit trail — not the process — is what changed. Resource non-overlap is per-person (I2 and D2 are different people), so genuine parallelism violates no capacity rule.
- `Best/base/worst case:` Best: both checks done Tue ~15:00, A2 picks up Tue evening, Decision Wed ~09:00 or Tue late  5 to 20 h. Base: checks done Tue afternoon, Assessment Wed morning as factual, Decision Wed late morning  2 to 4 h from a slightly earlier queue position. Worst: workaround mis-logged  audit exception, re-verification demanded, case slower than factual and trust in all parallel timestamps damaged.
- `Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:` A2's actual Tue-evening capacity/calendar and workaround audit logs. If assessment was in fact saturated all Tue–Wed, the honest conclusion is "no cycle-time benefit, only earlier queue entry," reversing any claimed saving; clean real-time logs with A2 slack would confirm the best case.

Rolling any of these out to 100 similar cases in one week changes the analysis fundamentally, because the single-case derivations hold queues approximately fixed, and at scale the interventions move the queues themselves. K-104's mechanism is the most fragile at scale: C1 is a single fully certified reviewer, so 100 provisional reservations cannot all be honored; reservations become a priority-inversion lottery in which held-but-unused slots (whenever Assessment slips) destroy the very capacity they were meant to secure, and preliminary reading competes directly with final reviews for other urgent high-risk cases. The bottleneck does not move — it hardens. K-219 at scale is a genuine load-balancing idea, since D1 carries the longest queue (11–13 h) and moving standard work to D2 could equalize waits; but D2 is slower per case and, on difficult handwriting, more error-prone, so total document hours rise even as D1's queue shrinks, and each additional clarification generates a full Document-plus-Assessment rework loop with an 8–36 h external wait. Whether system throughput improves depends on the D2 first-pass yield, an empirical quantity the factual cases do not provide. K-337 at scale frees real D1/A1 capacity and eliminates many weekend-amplified external waits — note that its factual second loop lost FriMon largely to the calendar, so suppression disproportionately "saves" weekends rather than work — but it does so by converting unresolved discrepancies into conditional approvals, rejections, appeals, and monitoring obligations that land on C1, M1, and downstream remediation, none of which appear in the original cycle-time ledger. K-411 at scale is the most benign in resource terms because it adds no service hours, yet precisely for that reason its benefit is capped by the next bottleneck: 100 cases arriving at Assessment hours earlier will saturate A1/A2 (already 4–12 h queues), converting verification savings into assessment waiting, with gains only for cases that cross daily capacity cut-offs. Finally, timestamp integrity becomes systemic under K-411: if the workaround rewards apparent concurrency, batch-entered logs will manufacture false parallelism at scale, corrupting the very event data needed to detect these queue effects.

## 7. Final synthesis

K-411's parallelization appears most robust: it changes no decision policy, deletes no required activity, uses resources already assigned, and is expressly permitted for no-originals cases. Its downside is bounded — if the Assessment queue is saturated the benefit collapses toward zero rather than turning harmful — provided timestamp discipline is enforced. K-104 is a reasonable second, since preliminary compliance work plausibly saves 1–3 h and earlier sanctions framing has intrinsic value, but it leans on the scarcest resource in the system.

The most dangerous intervention despite its speed is K-337. Suppressing the second rework loop produces the largest nominal saving, yet it does so by forcing a decision with a known unresolved discrepancy in a high-risk, high-value case. The saved days are visible immediately; the costs arrive as losses, appeals, unenforceable conditions, or a weakened compliance file, and a decision once issued cannot be retracted when the discrepancy materializes.

The local counterfactual least transportable to system-wide policy is K-104. Its single-case derivation assumes C1's queue is exogenous, but at scale the intervention is itself a claim on that single-server queue: universal provisional reservations cannot all be honored, idle held slots destroy capacity, and preliminary reading displaces final reviews for other urgent cases. K-337 is a close second, because precedent effects change assessor behavior beyond the treated cases.

The most valuable additional data are, first, resource-level event logs distinguishing true work intervals from batch-entered timestamps; second, time-varying queue and calendar data for C1 and the assessment pool, including reservation slip and idle time; third, D2's first-pass yield and service times on handwritten documents; and fourth, materiality and outcome follow-up on suppressed discrepancies — appeals, condition compliance, and realized losses — without which K-337's trade-off cannot even be measured.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. `structural` — The normative partial order in Section 1, including conditional compliance and rework-closure rules, correctly describes the real process for all four cases; no case has undocumented mandatory steps.
2. `temporal` — Snapshot queue figures are treated as uncertain wait forecasts at their timestamps, not as guaranteed waits at the (different) times counterfactual cases would arrive.
3. `temporal` — Working-hours calendars (evenings, weekends) interrupt internal service and amplify external waits, as in K-337's FriMon supplier delay; exact shift patterns are assumed similar to those implied by the factual logs.
4. `resource` — C1 remains the only fully certified compliance reviewer throughout the counterfactual windows, and S1's advisory capacity can overlap other checks without displacing them materially at single-case scale.
5. `resource` — Service-time ranges in the guidance are broadly applicable to these cases, with D1's advantage concentrated in complex and difficult-handwriting documents and D2's disadvantage likewise concentrated there.
6. `behavioral` — Staff comply with the interventions as specified: provisional slots are released when stale, the K-411 workaround is logged in real time, and assessors in K-337 record discrepancies explicitly rather than silently approving.
7. `quality` — D2's clarification/rework probability on handwritten standard documents is higher than D1's, and the remaining K-337 supplier inconsistency is usually clerical but occasionally material; both distributions are unknown and drive the sign of their interventions.
8. `interference` — In single-case counterfactuals, other cases' arrivals and routing are held approximately fixed, so target-case queue forecasts remain usable; this assumption is explicitly abandoned in the 100-case analysis, where queue displacement and bottleneck migration dominate.
