

## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

To propose repairs for this OCEL dataset, I establish a hierarchy of constraints grounded in OCEL 2.0 semantics and typical logistics process knowledge. These rules act as filters to distinguish likely repairs from artifacts.

**Hard Invariant Rules:**
*   **E2O Cardinality for Creation:** The `Create Order` event must link to exactly one Order object. This is structural; violating it breaks the event-object ontology.
*   **Object Existence:** A relation cannot reference an object ID not present in the Objects table.
*   **Timestamp Monotonicity:** While not absolute due to system clocks, downstream lifecycle activities (e.g., `Confirm Delivery`) generally occur after upstream ones (e.g., `Create Order`).

**Strong Default Rules:**
*   **Pack Item Completeness:** A `Pack Item` event normally relates to both an Item and a Package. If only the Item is listed (as in ev08), it is likely a scanner data drop, not a semantic change.
*   **Dispatch Package Linkage:** A `Dispatch Package` event should link a Package to a Delivery object. Absence suggests a missing relation rather than a missing delivery.
*   **Invoice Scope:** An invoice typically covers all open orders for a customer within a billing cycle, though this is defeasible.

**Weak Heuristic Rules:**
*   **Amount Matching:** Invoice totals or payment amounts matching the sum of order values suggest a billing relationship. This is evidence, not proof, because fees, discounts, or consolidated billing could alter sums.
*   **Route Hint Alignment:** A package `route_hint` matching a delivery `route` supports a carrier relation but does not guarantee it if multiple shipments share routes.
*   **External Reference Prefixing:** Identifiers like `N-420` in events matching `N-420` in deliveries are strong identifiers but require verification against other events (e.g., ev10).

**Evidence Model:**
I combine evidence by weighting structural constraints highest, then direct identifier matches, then semantic alignment (routes, customers, amounts). For example, ev10 lists `N-421?` for PK-8, matching DEL-Y's carrier ref `N-421`. Combined with ev09 (PK-7 to DEL-X), this forms a symmetric dispatch pattern. Conversely, arithmetic equality between INV-44 (230) and ORD-A+ORD-B (130+100) is treated as weak heuristic evidence; the invoice might include tax or exclude shipping. Therefore, repairs based on identifiers (ev10, ev11) receive higher confidence than those based on totals (INV-44, PAY-2). I avoid circular reasoning by not using an inferred O2O relation to confirm an E2O relation.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: Conservative/Minimum-Change Repair**
This bundle prioritizes structural integrity and explicit evidence. It avoids inferring financial relations from arithmetic sums.

*   **ev08 and PK-8:** Adds an O2O relation `PK-8 packs IT-B1` to satisfy the scanner outage rule. This is high confidence because ev07 already established PK-8 handling IT-A2.
*   **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Adds E2O `ev10 relates to DEL-Y`. The identifier match is near-exact (N-421? vs N-421).
*   **ev11 and deliveries:** Leaves `Confirm Delivery` linked only to `ORD-A`. This acknowledges portal data loss without forcing a carrier link.
*   **INV-44 and ORD-B:** No O2O relation is added. We assume ORD-B might be on a separate invoice or consolidated differently.
*   **PAY-2 and INV-44:** No relation added. PAY-2 remains unapplied cash.
*   **Lifecycle Impact:** ORD-A forms a complete lifecycle (Create  Add Item  Pack  Dispatch  Confirm  Bill  Pay). ORD-B has a partial lifecycle (Create  Add Item  Pack) but ends before billing/pay. This preserves data reality but creates orphan financial objects (PAY-2).

**Bundle B: Plausible Alternative Interpretation**
This bundle reconstructs a fully connected business process, assuming the data represents one consolidated shipment cycle for C17.

*   **ev08 and PK-8:** Same as Bundle A (Add O2O relation).
*   **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Same as Bundle A (Add E2O relation).
*   **ev11 and deliveries:** Links `Confirm Delivery` to `DEL-X` and `ORD-A` via the customer match. We assume the customer acknowledged the delivery manually.
*   **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Adds O2O `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. The total 230 equals 130+100 exactly. We treat this as strong accounting evidence despite the risk of separate billing.
*   **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Adds E2O `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. We assume sequential payments cleared the full invoice.
*   **Lifecycle Impact:** All objects are integrated. ORD-A and ORD-B are co-billed. This yields a "healthy" lifecycle for analysis but risks modeling a specific business rule (consolidated billing) that might not be universal. If false, process mining may identify incorrect billing paths.

**Bundle C: Hybrid Financial Repair (Optional)**
A middle ground where PAY-2 links to ORD-B directly as a partial prepayment, avoiding the consolidated invoice assumption. This leaves INV-44 strictly on ORD-A (as observed) and treats PAY-2 as a separate settlement for ORD-B.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

In Bundle A (Conservative), ORD-A is fully realized with 7 object links and 4 distinct lifecycle stages (Order, Item, Delivery, Invoice/Payment). ORD-B, however, is truncated: it links to Items and Packaging but terminates before financial settlement. This creates an orphan Payment (PAY-2) and an orphan Invoice relationship (INV-44 technically settles PAY-1 but ORD-B remains open). There are no circular relations; the flow remains strictly directed from Order to Payment. The `contains` relations maintain a 1-to-many (Order to Items) structure, while `packs` maintains many-to-one (Items to Package) consistent with standard packing logic.

In Bundle B (Inclusive), ORD-B completes its lifecycle by linking to the Invoice and indirectly to Payments. This eliminates orphans but introduces potential cardinality anomalies: INV-44 bills two orders directly (Many-to-Many), which is permissible in OCEL but alters downstream event logic if the process expects 1-to-1 billing.

Crucially, I avoid circular reasoning by not assuming the invoice links to ORD-B solely because Payments match. Instead, I verify the O2O link via semantic aggregation rules. For example, adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` is not supported by an E2O relation between an invoice event and ORD-B; it relies purely on O2O inference. I do not treat the subsequent E2O `PAY-2` link as independent confirmation of the `ORD-B` link. The audit flags `ev08` as having a missing E2O package link originally; adding the O2O `packs` relation (p02) is structurally sound but bypasses explicit E2O evidence. This gap is noted as "provisional" status to maintain auditability.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

Unrepaired logs show fragmented object chains. The OTG (Object Type Graph) will display disconnected components (Order-Item-Package vs. Invoice-Payment).

**OC-DFG:**
*   **Unrepaired:** `Pack Item` -> `Dispatch Package` appears disconnected from `Confirm Delivery` because ev08 lacks Package link.
*   **Bundle A:** The path `Order` -> `Pack Item` -> `Dispatch Package` becomes valid. The `Confirm Delivery` node branches only from `ORD-A`, reducing path diversity.
*   **Bundle B:** `Confirm Delivery` may aggregate to `INV-44` and `ORD-B` if the portal event is traced to the invoice process, increasing path complexity.

**OTG:**
*   **Unrepaired:** `Package` types are under-represented.
*   **Bundle A:** PK-7 and PK-8 both connect to Del-P, increasing `Package` connectivity.
*   **Bundle B:** `INV-44` gains an edge to `ORD-B`, changing the billing topology from 1-to-1 to 1-to-Many.

**Flattenings:**
*   **Order Flattening:** In Bundle A, ORD-B appears as an incomplete case (missing financial events). In Bundle B, both orders appear fully complete.
*   **Invoice Flattening:** In Bundle A, INV-44 represents only ORD-A. In Bundle B, it aggregates two orders. This affects KPIs like "Invoice Cycle Time" (from create to bill) where ORD-B's creation time might skew averages differently than in A.
*   **Event Frequencies:** Activity counts remain constant. However, "Unique Objects" per activity change. For `Issue Invoice`, unique object count increases from 1 (ORD-A) to 2 (ORD-A + ORD-B) in Bundle B.
*   **Equivalent-Execution Clustering:** In Bundle A, `Create Order` for ORD-A and ORD-B behave identically. In Bundle B, downstream clustering shows ORD-A leading to Invoice, whereas ORD-B does not. This might flag ORD-B as "exception" behavior unless repaired.

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1.  **Forcing Financial Summation:** Using the exact match of Invoice Total (230) to sum of Orders (130+100) as absolute proof may ignore taxes, shipping fees, or discounts not recorded. This creates false billing trails for future analysis.
2.  **Carrier Identity Assumption:** Linking ev10 solely to DEL-Y based on prefix similarity ignores potential carrier routing changes. If the system reused carrier ID prefixes, this could create a phantom delivery link.
3.  **Assumption of Single-Invoice Logic:** In Bundle B, forcing both ORD-A and ORD-B onto INV-44 assumes they were billed together. If the business processes orders individually, this introduces incorrect process paths in the OTG.
4.  **Scanner Outage Misinterpretation:** Linking IT-B1 to PK-8 based on ev07 pattern presumes ev08 was purely a scanner failure. If IT-B1 was meant to be packed separately (e.g., fragile item), this repair creates a false package composition.
5.  **Unapplied Payment Misclassification:** Linking PAY-2 to INV-44 without explicit reference assumes it settled the balance. If PAY-2 was a prepayment or unrelated transaction, the financial trace will show incorrect clearance, affecting audit accuracy.
6.  **Circular Confirmation Bias:** Inferring O2O `bills` and E2O `relates` to confirm each other (e.g., because they are linked, they must be correct) creates a loop where errors are validated by their own existence. Always check for independent timestamps or IDs.
7.  **Event Timestamp Rounding:** Relying on "same timestamp" for dispatch events (ev09, ev10) as proof of relationship ignores that bulk dispatches occur in batches without individual tracking, potentially hiding multiple packages in one record.

By maintaining uncertainty (provisional status, unresolved flags), we ensure downstream tools do not treat speculative links as ground truth for automated decisions.
