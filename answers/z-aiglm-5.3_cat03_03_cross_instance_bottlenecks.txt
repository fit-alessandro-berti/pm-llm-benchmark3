## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

**C1 — J1 synchronization wait in B1.** Ready 08:10, cure 12:05  **3 h 55 m (235 min)**. *Counterfactual:* cure could start at quorum 2 (08:25) or per-piece  avoidable  220–225 min. *Caveat:* assumes readiness timestamps are valid and no unlogged oven/rack constraint before 12:05; under a full-batch policy the wait is jointly caused by J3's later readiness (11:55) — the *barrier* is the mechanism; the *skew's source* is upstream. A pre-booked 12:05 oven slot would produce identical timestamps, so this is attribution, not proof.

**C2 — J2 synchronization wait in B1.** 12:05  08:25 = **3 h 40 m (220 min)**, same counterfactual. Sub-calculation: had J2 obeyed the L7 quarantine (release 13:50), B1 could not start before 13:50; the supervisor override therefore "saved" J2 (and J1, J3)  105 min at the cost of curing quarantined-lot material.

**C3 — J4 blocked by FIX-A.** J4 at CNC-2 09:50; CNC-2 idle  10:05; process starts 11:45, i.e., FIX-A release (11:40) + 5 min. Attributable delay  11:45  10:05 = **100 min** (range 58–115 min: lower bound if CNC-2 was legitimately busy 10:47–11:45, upper if counted from J4's 09:50 arrival). *Counterfactual:* a second FIX-A or earlier release, assuming J4 was otherwise ready. *Caveat:* no `Wait for Tool` event exists; the 5-minute offset is circumstantial, and J4's delay could reflect kitting, program, or operator absence.

**C4 — J4 waiting for B2.** Ready 13:10, start 14:50  **100 min = 85 (RACK-7 quarantine to 14:35) + 5 (J5 readiness to 14:40, itself plausibly gated by the 13:50 lot release) + 10 (scheduling slack)**. *Counterfactual:* rack clean at 12:55 and partial batching allowed  start  13:10. OVEN-1 was idle throughout, so capacity was never binding.

**C5 — J1 delayed by S1 consolidation.** Packed 13:30, dispatched 19:25  **5 h 55 m (355 min)**; against S2/S3-normal lags (25–35 min), avoidable  320 min if shipped solo. *Decomposition with binding-constraint logic:* B2's start was set by max(RACK-7 = 14:35, J5 = 14:40), **not** by J4's readiness — so C3's 100 min was *absorbed* by the batch barrier (J4 would simply have waited 200 min for the batch instead of 100 min at the machine) and did **not** propagate to J1. Treating J4's post-batch flow (14:50  19:10 pack) as fixed and additive, a rack-clean-at-12:55 + no-quarantine counterfactual gives B2  13:15, J4 pack  17:35, dispatch  17:50  J1 waits  260 min. Roughly 95–110 min of J1's wait traces to rack/lot conditions; the remaining ~4 h is the consolidation *pairing* of an early job with a structurally later one.

**C6 — J6 delayed by CAL-3.** Ready 16:05; slot free after J5 at 16:35; actual start 17:25  **50 min** attributable to the 16:35–17:20 recalibration (+5 min restart). *Counterfactual:* recalibration done before 16:00 or on a second CAL-3. *Caveat:* if recalibration was drift-triggered by J5's test, the coupling is J5J6 via tool state, and deferral is not free.

**C7 — measurement artifact.** OVEN-1 idle 12:55–14:50 (115 min) vs. a reported longest machine-event gap of 73 min:  42 min of idle-for-rack time is hidden, likely because cleaning is logged as machine activity. Utilization/availability from the Machine flattening is unreliable.

**Alternative ranking, defended:** if ranked by *propagation to completion time* rather than waiting magnitude, lot-release + rack quarantine rise to the top (they bound B2's start and hence J1's 355-min wait), and FIX-A falls (its 100 min was absorbed by the batch barrier, C5). Batch synchronization remains rank 1 as the *structural* mechanism in both rankings because it both creates waiting (B1) and determines what propagates (B2).

## 4. What flattening misses

| Viewpoint | Makes visible | Hides or duplicates | Misleading conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job | Per-job flow, rework loop (2/6) | Lot events (extraction filter); tool waits (no events); duplicates one `Batch Cure` into 3 job cases | "No job exceeds 1.8 SD of the mean" — every job is differently coupled-delayed, inflating SD and erasing outliers; median PackDispatch (1.1 h) hides J1's 5.9 h |
| Machine | Utilization, sequences, gaps | *Why* machines idle; 42 min of OVEN-1 idle (C7); tool/rack gating | "OVEN-1 at 44% is not a constraint" — it was available yet blocked by rack policy; CNC-2's 48-min max gap hides a ~100-min tool block |
| Batch | B1/B2 lifecycle, oven+rack use | Member readiness skew; merges 3 jobs' events into one case | "Batch cycle time  50 min" while members waited up to 3 h 55 m |
| Tool | FIX-A hold 2 h 40 m; rack links | Who was blocked (no waiting events); CAL-3 recal impact on J6; RACK-7 cleaning is a status, not events | "Tools are fine — only 15 interactions" |
| ComponentLot | Quarantine 10:2013:50 | J2's override (separate system) — would wrongly show J2 halted; link to rack cleaning | "Quarantine delayed J2 and J5 equally" |
| Shipment | CreateLoadDispatch (fast) | Pre-load packed-waiting time of early members | "Dispatch adds only 0.3 h" — J1 waited 355 min packed |

**Why low utilization coexists with long waiting:** utilization is a property of *machines*; these delays are properties of *edges* — synchronization points (batch quorum, tool/rack state windows, lot status, shipment partner readiness). Machines idle because gating resources are unavailable; jobs idle because fan-in structures (B1, B2, S1) force them to the pace of their slowest member or latest resource. Per-object views average over time and see none of the cross-object gating, so all flattenings look "broadly normal."

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**Narrative A — the fixture/rack/lot cascade to S1 (Tool, Job, Machine, Component, Batch, Shipment).**
- *Precedence:* FIX-A acquired 09:00 (J3)  J4 at CNC-2 09:50  CNC-2 idle 10:05–10:47  FIX-A released 11:40  J4's `Machine Process`@11:45 {J4, C4, CNC-2, FIX-A}  J4 batch-ready 13:10  RACK-7 quarantine to 14:35  J5 ready 14:40  B2 starts 14:50 on OVEN-1  J4 packed 19:10  S1 dispatches 19:25  J1 (packed 13:30) waits 355 min.
- *Resource feasibility:* CNC-2 demonstrably free in the window; FIX-A single-unit, held by J3; OVEN-1 free 12:55–14:50 while RACK-7 was not.
- *Policy constraints:* full-batch rule (B2 waited for J5), rack quarantine rule, S1 consolidation rule.
- *Causal speculation:* whether B1's 12:05 start was gated by J3's fixture-bound readiness (11:55 = release + 15 min) — compatible but unproven; and, critically, C5's binding-constraint analysis *weakens* the tempting claim that FIX-A delayed J1: the batch barrier absorbed J4's 100 min. Temporal succession here is not propagation.

**Narrative B — lot quarantine with override (ComponentLot, Component, Job, Batch, Tool).**
- *Precedence:* `Quarantine Lot`@10:20 {L7, C2, C5, J2, J5}  B1 cures 12:05 containing C2 (quarantined lot)  oven idle from 12:55  RACK-7 "cleaning/quarantine" until 14:35  `Release Lot`@13:50  J5 ready 14:40  B2 14:50.
- *Feasibility:* J2's B1 entry at 12:05 precedes release, so the override must be real; the rack was unavailable exactly when the oven was free.
- *Policy:* quarantine rule + supervisor override; plausibly a clean-after-quarantined-material rule.
- *Speculation:* that rack cleaning 12:55–14:35 *was caused by* L7 material in B1 (timing compatible: unload  12:55, release 13:50, rack free 14:35) and that J5's 14:40 readiness was gated by the 13:50 release (50-min gap). Neither is recorded.

**Narrative C — shared tool state at final test (Job, Machine, Tool, Shipment).**
- *Precedence:* J5 ready 16:00, J6 ready 16:05  J5 tests 16:10–16:35 {TEST-1, CAL-3}  CAL-3 recalibrated 16:35–17:20  TEST-1 idle 16:35–17:25  J6 tests 17:25–17:50  S3 dispatches 35 min after last pack.
- *Feasibility:* TEST-1 idle while J6 was ready; every `Final Test` requires CAL-3  only CAL-3 can explain the block.
- *Policy:* recalibration policy and J5-first sequencing.
- *Speculation:* recalibration triggered by J5's result (making J5 the upstream cause of J6's 50 min) versus scheduled maintenance (making it policy); deferring recalibration has quality risk either way.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

1. **Rolling batch quorum / partial batches** (reduce synchronization). *Benefit:* J1/J2 save up to ~3.5 h; urgent jobs can bypass. *Helped:* J1, J2, future early members. *Harmed:* late members (a J3-like job would miss B1 and wait for the next rack slot — potentially hours). *Cost/risk:* more oven runs  more rack changeovers  more cleaning, worsening the rack bottleneck. *Perverse metric:* oven utilization (rewards full batches). *OC evaluation:* distribution of Job.readyBatch.start conditioned on batch size and member skew, with rack-cleaning frequency as a covariate.
2. **Second FIX-A / second CAL-3 or fixture-aware scheduling and pre-16:00 recalibration** (tools). *Benefit:* J4 100 min start-time; J6 50 min. *Helped:* J4, J6, any job queued behind a fixture or recal. *Harmed:* none operationally. *Cost:* capex, extra calibration load. *Perverse metric:* tool utilization (a low-utilized second fixture looks wasteful). *OC evaluation:* overlap of tool-hold intervals with other jobs' machine-wait windows (Tool×Job×Machine co-occurrence), pre/post.
3. **Rack pool or validated fast clean** (tools/rack). *Benefit:* B2  13:10; J4 pack ~1.6 h earlier; J1 ~95 min (C5). *Helped:* J4, J5, J1. *Harmed:* none directly. *Cost/risk:* compliance risk if cleaning is shortened. *Perverse metric:* cleaning turnaround time (incentivizes skipping validation). *OC evaluation:* rack-state timeline joined to batch starts; count batch starts blocked by rack vs. by members.
4. **Dynamic consolidation (ship-alone threshold, e.g., >2 h packed-wait)** (shipment policy). *Benefit:* J1 ~5 h. *Helped:* early-ready jobs. *Harmed:* freight cost; possibly none for J4. *Risk:* partial shipments to one customer. *Perverse metric:* freight cost per shipment (drives bigger, slower consolidations). *OC evaluation:* per-member packeddispatch inside the Shipment fan-in (J1 and J4 separately), never shipment-level CreateDispatch.
5. **Logging overhaul** (improve logging, not operations): adopt the alternative integration pipeline so lot events relate to J2/J5; add `Wait for Tool` / `Wait for Batch` / `Wait for Lot` / `Wait for Partner` activities; integrate the override system; log rack cleaning as events with reason codes. *Benefit:* visibility; the quarantined C2 in B1 would have been detectable. *Helped:* analysts, compliance. *Harmed:* none physically; event volume grows. *Perverse metric:* event count or "log completeness %" (rewards noise). *OC evaluation:* E2O coverage per object type; verify QuarantineLot appears in J2/J5 cases after re-flattening; measure detection latency of lot gating.
6. **Lot-release governance** (formalize or gate overrides). *Benefit:* compliance. *Helped:* quality assurance. *Harmed:* J2-like jobs gain ~1 h 45 m — the delay the override had hidden now surfaces (a genuine spillover). *Perverse metric:* "quarantine breaches " (may incentivize not quarantining at all). *OC evaluation:* consistency check that Lot.release  Batch.start for all batch members; track the delay shift from compliance to cycle time.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

**Observations that would weaken the top-ranked (batch synchronization) explanation:**
1. An oven booking record showing 12:05 was a pre-scheduled slot, not a wait for J3.
2. Quality-hold or first-article-inspection records on J1/J2 between 08:25 and 11:30, invalidating their "ready" timestamps.
3. Evidence OVEN-1 or RACK-7 ran an unlogged product until ~12:00 (earlier start impossible).
4. A second FIX-A unit or fixture-transfer log showing FIX-A was free 10:05–11:40 (kills C3's attribution).
5. MES records that J4's 11:45 start awaited missing C4 kitting, a CNC program, or an operator.
6. A cure recipe requiring a full 3-job load (partial batching physically infeasible; the barrier is a process requirement, not a policy choice).
7. Override paperwork showing B1@12:05 was timed to a customer promise unrelated to J3's readiness.
8. J5 machining records showing it would finish 14:40 regardless of the 13:50 release (lot gate not binding).

**Additional fields/event types that would discriminate among hypotheses:** planned vs. actual start times and due dates per job/batch; explicit `Wait for Tool/Batch/Lot/Partner` events carrying the blocking resource ID; tool-state logs (second fixtures; CAL-3 recalibration trigger — drift alarm vs. schedule); rack cleaning reason codes with start/end and the triggering batch or lot; integrated lot-release and override records linked to Jobs; shipment policy parameters (cut-offs, consolidation thresholds, carrier windows); operator/shift assignments; per-station queue-length snapshots; and lot links for C4 and C6, whose absence currently makes J4/J6 lot-coupling unobservable.
