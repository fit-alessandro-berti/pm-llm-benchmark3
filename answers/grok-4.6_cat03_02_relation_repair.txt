## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

The log is treated as an incomplete but mostly trustworthy OCEL 2.0 excerpt. Observed E2O and O2O rows are kept. No events or objects are invented. A repair is a proposed *addition* or an explicit *non-addition*. Arithmetic, prefixes, and lifecycle completeness are clues, not proofs.

**Hard invariants**
- Identifiers in any added relation must already exist.
- Do not delete or reverse observed relations. Qualifiers are directional (`DEL-X carries PK-7` is not `PK-7 carries DEL-X`).
- `Create Order` has exactly one Order (already satisfied by ev01/ev02).
- `Record Payment` relates to exactly one Payment (ev13 already has PAY-1; ev14 already has PAY-2).
- `Add Item` already has the expected Order+Item pairs; those rows are not rewritten.
- A proposed edge that contradicts an observed identifier more specific than a wildcard is not accepted as a unique fact.

**Strong defaults**
- `Pack Item` relates to one Item and one Package. An item-only row is allowed as a scanner outage and may be reconciled later; it is not automatically left forever incomplete if a package is independently indicated.
- `Dispatch Package` relates to one Package and one Delivery, unless the same event clearly names several packages.
- `Issue Invoice` relates to one Invoice and one or more Orders.
- `Record Payment` normally also relates to one Invoice; unapplied cash remains legal.
- When an E2O pair realises a named business link that the O2O table already uses for the same types (`packs`, `carries`, `bills`, `settles`), a matching O2O row is the default *if and only if* the E2O link is itself independently evidenced. The O2O copy is not a second observation.

**Weak heuristics**
- Scanner and carrier prefixes (`PK8-2`, `N-421?`, `N-42*`), `order_hint` / `route_hint`, exact `bank_ref` copies, same-minute timestamps, shared customer `C17`, and shared route `NORTH`.
- Amount patterns: `declared_value(ORD-A)+declared_value(ORD-B)=130+100=230=INV-44.total`; `PAY-1=100` and `PAY-2=130` partition that total and also match the two order values individually.
- Lifecycle order: Create → Add Item → Pack → Dispatch → Confirm → Issue Invoice → Record Payment. Time order in the log already respects this; it supports *compatibility*, not identity of objects.
- An unused object of the expected type (DEL-Y next to a delivery-less dispatch; PAY-2 next to an invoice-less payment) raises prior probability but does not close the case.

**Evidence against a repair**
- The literal `?` on ev10 `external_ref=N-421?` and the literal `*` on ev11 `external_ref=N-42*`.
- Domain rule 5: Confirm Delivery may bind only a customer/order when the portal drops the carrier id. ev11 already has `ORD-A` and `portal_customer=C17`.
- Domain rule 6–7: fee/tax/discount and unapplied cash. Equality of 230, or of 100+130, is not proof.
- A mixed package (items from ORD-A and ORD-B in PK-8) and a split order (ORD-A items on two deliveries) are legal but unusual; they count against over-confident chaining.
- Completing every object lifecycle is not a goal. Orphans may be correct.

**How signals are combined.** Identifier similarity is used first for *local* event–object cardinality (which package was scanned; which delivery number was written). Time is used as a tie-break and as a feasibility filter (pack before dispatch; payments after the invoice). Customer and route are background compatibility: everything already sits in `C17` / `NORTH`, so they almost never discriminate. Amounts and `declared_value` are used only inside the financial subgraph and only as *provisional* support. Existing O2O structure is used as a *schema* (which qualifier to write) rather than as extra votes. If an E2O edge is assumed and an O2O edge is then derived from it, the pair is one hypothesis, not two independent confirmations.

Uncertainty is left in place whenever two stories remain compatible with the same rows. That is the point of the bundles below.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A — conservative / minimum-change (selected).** Add R01, R02, R04, and provisionally R05. Do not add R07–R13. Reject R03, R06, R14–R17.

- **ev08 and PK-8.** Treat ev08 as the documented scanner outage for IT-B1, reconciled to PK-8 by `PK8-2`. PK-8 then packs IT-A2 and IT-B1 (two orders, one box). That is unusual, but inventing a third package is forbidden, and PK-7 is identifier-incompatible.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y.** Bind ev10 to DEL-Y only. ev09 already consumed DEL-X. Do not treat the pair as a bulk dispatch of DEL-X.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects.** Leave both DEL-X and DEL-Y off ev11. The portal row is already legal with only ORD-A. `N-42*` is compatible with X, Y, both, or neither; assigning one uniquely is stronger than the token.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B.** Leave unlinked beyond the observed `bills ORD-A`. The 230 total is compatible with a two-order invoice *or* with fees on ORD-A alone. Existing `PAY-1 settles INV-44` for amount 100 already shows that the financial subgraph is not a clean 130-against-130 story, so adding ORD-B would be explaining an anomaly by another anomaly.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44.** Leave PAY-2 as recorded cash with no invoice (unapplied). Do not attach it to an order.

Lifecycles under A: ORD-A is create–add–confirm–invoice; its items are packed and dispatched, one via DEL-X and one via DEL-Y. ORD-B is create–add–pack (via the mixed package) and then silent — no confirm, no invoice. DEL-X has dispatch and no confirm. DEL-Y has dispatch and no confirm. INV-44 has one order and one settlement of 100 against a total of 230 (partial pay or fee residue). PAY-2 is an orphan payment object with a legitimate event. That incompleteness is retained, not repaired away.

**Bundle B — joint C17 commercial shipment.** Start from A, then add R10–R13 and *both* R07 and R08 (not R09).

- **ev08 / PK-8.** Same mixed package as A. The mix is now *motivated*: one customer, one invoice, two orders staged into two northbound packages.
- **ev10 / DEL-X / DEL-Y.** Same as A: PK-7→DEL-X, PK-8→DEL-Y.
- **ev11.** Read `N-42*` as a truncated portal acknowledgement of *both* north carrier refs, not as a unique choice of X or of Y. Alternatives are not weaker individually — each single-delivery reading fits the wildcard — but they are weaker *together with* the split-shipment already accepted in A, because ORD-A then has goods on both deliveries. Portal-only (Bundle A) remains coherent; B simply takes the other legal reading of rule 5 (“several deliveries acknowledged together”). ORD-B is still *not* added to ev11: the observed object list names only ORD-A, and a joint invoice does not imply a joint delivery confirmation.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B.** One invoice covering both C17 orders. 230 is treated as *supporting* evidence, not a proof. ev12 gains ORD-B; O2O gains `INV-44 bills ORD-B`.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44.** The two payments are complementary cash against that invoice (100+130=230). ev14 gains INV-44; O2O gains `PAY-2 settles INV-44`. The fact that the two amounts also equal the two `declared_value`s is noted as a *competing* story (earmarked per-order cash) and is why confidence stays in the mid-0.5s.

Lifecycles under B: ORD-A as in A plus a shared invoice; ORD-B gains invoice (and packing) but still lacks confirm; both deliveries gain Confirm Delivery from the same event; the invoice is fully settled. Coherent, but every extra edge after A is a single commercial hypothesis, not extra observations.

**Bundle C — do not close DEL-Y.** Accept only R01/R02. Leave ev10 without a delivery (the `?` wins). Leave R05–R13 off. DEL-Y remains an unused master-data object (a booked north slot that this excerpt never attached). Financial and confirm subgraphs stay as observed. This is the “honor the uncertainty tokens” bundle: it yields a *worse* Delivery lifecycle than A and is useful mainly as a sensitivity case, not as a preferred model.

Do not treat B’s extra edges as isolated. If one accepts the combined invoice but refuses both delivery confirms, ORD-A is invoiced after a confirm that still does not mention the second package’s carrier. If one accepts both confirms but refuses the invoice link, two deliveries are acknowledged for a customer whose second order never appears on the invoice. Each bundle is a package of commitments.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order.** ORD-A already has a plausible backbone: create, two adds, later confirm, then invoice. Pack and dispatch never touch the order in E2O, so the order lifecycle *correctly* omits those activities unless one over-repairs. ORD-B stops after create and one add, plus a pack that is visible only through IT-B1/PK-8. Bundle A leaves that truncation in place. Bundle B adds Issue Invoice to ORD-B and still leaves Confirm off ORD-B. Neither selected repair forces ORD-B through a full quote-to-cash path.

Independent E2O+O2O support for orders: `ORD-A contains IT-A*` is backed by ev03/ev04 *and* O2O. `INV-44 bills ORD-A` is backed by ev12 *and* O2O. The proposed `bills ORD-B` would *not* be independently backed; it would be one hypothesis written twice.

**Item.** IT-A1 and IT-A2 are add→pack with matching `packs` O2O. IT-B1 is add→item-only pack until R01. After R01/R02 the item lifecycle is complete. Cardinality: each item has one order (`contains`) and one package (`packs`). No item is shared.

**Package.** PK-7: one pack event, one item, one dispatch, one carrier. Clean 1:1. PK-8: two pack events (ev07, ev08) and two items if R01 is accepted — a 1:2 `packs` that is the main suspicious many-relation in A. It is supported by two E2O pack rows plus two O2O rows, but the second O2O is derived, so the independent core is “two pack events naming the same scanner family.” Dispatch cardinality stays 1 package : 1 delivery under A/B.

**Delivery.** DEL-X: dispatch only under A; optionally confirm under B. DEL-Y: orphan in the unrepaired log; dispatch-only under A; dispatch+confirm under B. Bundle C leaves DEL-Y an object without events — allowed. No delivery is given two packages in the selected repair. Confirm is the dangerous many-relation: one event to two deliveries is legal under rule 5 but must not be treated as observed.

**Invoice.** INV-44 has Issue Invoice and one Record Payment in A (partial relative to total=230). B adds a second order and a second payment. The 1:2 bills relation in B is commercially normal and evidentially weak. Do not read the existing `PAY-1 settles INV-44` as independent confirmation that ORD-B belongs on the invoice; PAY-1 is equally compatible with a partial payment on an ORD-A-only invoice.

**Payment.** PAY-1 is complete (event + invoice + `settles`). PAY-2 has an event and no invoice in A — a permitted unapplied-cash lifecycle, not an error. B completes it. Attaching PAY-2 to an order would be a type-level cardinality violation of the payment default.

**Orphans (selected Bundle A).** Event orphans: none; every event already has at least one object. Object orphans in the strong sense: none after R04 (DEL-Y gains ev10). Soft orphans: ORD-B (no post-pack order events), PAY-2 (no invoice), both deliveries (no confirm). Those are retained.

**Circular reasoning to avoid.** (1) Assume ev08→PK-8, write `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, then claim the O2O “confirms” the E2O. (2) Assume ev10→DEL-Y and R01, walk ORD-A→IT-A2→PK-8→DEL-Y, then treat that walk as independent proof that ev11 must include DEL-Y. (3) Assume INV-44 covers both orders because 230 matches, then treat PAY-2=130 as independent confirmation of the same invoice. In each case there is one story, written on two tables.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log.** Order OC-DFG: `Create Order → Add Item → Confirm Delivery → Issue Invoice` on ORD-A only; ORD-B is `Create Order → Add Item` and stops. Item OC-DFG: `Add Item → Pack Item` for all three items; IT-B1 then dies. Package: `Pack Item → Dispatch Package` for PK-7 and PK-8. Delivery: a single `Dispatch Package` node on DEL-X; DEL-Y is absent from every event-derived graph. Invoice: `Issue Invoice → Record Payment`. Payment: two singleton `Record Payment` objects, only one joined to the invoice. OTG: Order–Item, Package–Item, Delivery–Package (X–7 only), Invoice–Order (A only), Payment–Invoice (PAY-1 only). ET–OT: Confirm Delivery–Order only; Dispatch Package–Package and (once) Delivery; Record Payment–Payment and (once) Invoice; Pack Item–Item and (twice) Package. Flattened Order cluster: two inequivalent traces. Flattened Delivery: one trace `{Dispatch Package}`.

**Bundle A.** OC-DFG Package side gains a second incoming pack on PK-8 (`Pack Item` loops or a parallel pack before dispatch). Delivery OC-DFG *gains an object* DEL-Y with the same `{Dispatch Package}` fragment as DEL-X; the type-level DFG does not gain a new activity, but object-count frequencies of Dispatch Package on Delivery go from 1 to 2. OTG adds `PK-8 packs IT-B1` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`. That single OTG increment is large: Item–Package becomes many-to-one for PK-8, and the Delivery type is no longer a singleton. ET–OT: Pack Item–Package becomes 3/3 instead of 2/3; Dispatch Package–Delivery becomes 2/2. Flattened Order: **unchanged** (pack/dispatch still not on orders) — so equivalent-execution clustering around Order still splits `{A: create, add, add, confirm, invoice}` vs `{B: create, add}`. Flattened Package: PK-8 now has two Pack Item events then Dispatch; PK-7 stays one pack. Flattened Delivery: two parallel one-step cases, still no Confirm. Flattened Invoice/Payment: unchanged. Activity frequencies: event counts do not change (no new events). Unique-object counts: Delivery objects with Dispatch rise; Package objects with two Pack events appear. Total object involvements: Pack Item’s Package-involvement rises from 2 to 3.

Hidden fragment that A *creates*: a cross-order package. Any object-centric directly-follows view that hops Item→Package→Item will show IT-A2 and IT-B1 meeting. That hop does not exist in the unrepaired OTG. A also *duplicates* the dispatch fragment across two deliveries, which can look like two cases of the same subprocess rather than one bulk wave.

**Bundle B.** On top of A: Order OC-DFG for ORD-B becomes `Create Order → Add Item → Issue Invoice` (still no Confirm, and still no Pack on the order). ORD-A keeps Confirm before Invoice. Equivalent-execution clustering **does not collapse A and B into one class**; they remain distinct, but closer (shared invoice activity). If an analyst had wrongly expected B to “complete” ORD-B, this is the disappointment to document. Delivery flattening gains `Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery` on *both* X and Y, from the **same** ev11 timestamp — a classic OC-DFG inflation: one event, two objects, two copies of the same edge. Invoice flattening: `Issue Invoice → Record Payment → Record Payment` (or two parallel pays). Payment flattening: both payments now sit on the invoice OTG. ET–OT: Confirm Delivery–Delivery appears (new type pair); Issue Invoice–Order degree 1→2; Record Payment–Invoice degree 1→2. OTG adds `bills ORD-B` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`.

Compared with the unrepaired log, B can manufacture a clean cash-application story and a clean two-leg north dispatch that the wildcard tokens do not actually certify. Compared with A, the OC-DFG does not add packing on orders, so analyses that flatten only on Order will *still miss* the mixed package — a flattening blind spot common to A and B.

**Frequencies vs objects.** `Pack Item` remains 3 events. Unique items packed: 3 in all versions. Unique packages touched: 2. Total package involvements: 2 unrepaired, 3 in A/B. `Dispatch Package`: 2 events, 2 packages always; unique deliveries 1 unrepaired / 2 in A/B. `Confirm Delivery`: 1 event, 1 order always in A; 1 event, 1 order, 2 deliveries in B — event frequency 1, object-level delivery frequency 0 or 2. `Issue Invoice`: 1 event; orders involved 1 (A) or 2 (B). `Record Payment`: 2 events, 2 payments; invoices involved 1 or 1-with-two-pays.

**Edges that may appear, disappear, or duplicate.** Appear: OTG `packs(PK-8,IT-B1)`, `carries(DEL-Y,PK-8)`; optional `bills(INV-44,ORD-B)`, `settles(PAY-2,INV-44)`; OC-DFG `Dispatch → Confirm` on Delivery (B only); ET–OT Confirm–Delivery (B). Disappear: none of the observed edges. Duplicate: `Dispatch Package` as a delivery-level case (A/B); `Confirm Delivery` copied onto two deliveries (B); `Issue Invoice` copied onto two orders (B); `Record Payment` sequenced twice on one invoice (B). The unrepaired log’s apparent isolation of PAY-2 and DEL-Y disappears in A/B for DEL-Y and only in B for PAY-2 — that disappearance can be a modeling error if they were truly unused.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python

import pm4py
from copy import deepcopy

# --- snapshot originals (do not mutate the input frames in place) ---
events_orig = events.copy()
objects_orig = objects.copy()
relations_orig = relations.copy()
o2o_orig = o2o.copy()

# --- Bundle A additions only ---
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        {"ocel:eid": "ev08", "ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "package"},
        {"ocel:eid": "ev10", "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:qualifier": "delivery"},

o2o_add = pd.DataFrame(
    [
        {"ocel:oid": "PK-8", "ocel:oid_2": "IT-B1", "ocel:qualifier": "packs"},
        {"ocel:oid": "DEL-Y", "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8", "ocel:qualifier": "carries"},
    ]
)

# align to whatever qualifier/column set the incoming frames already use
for col in relations_orig.columns:
    if col not in e2o_add.columns:
        e2o_add[col] = pd.NA
for col in o2o_orig.columns:
    if col not in o2o_add.columns:
        o2o_add[col] = pd.NA
e2o_add = e2o_add[relations_orig.columns]
o2o_add = o2o_add[o2o_orig.columns]

relations = pd.concat([relations_orig, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
o2o = pd.concat([o2o_orig, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)

# --- referential integrity ---
eid_ok = set(events_orig["ocel:eid"])
oid_ok = set(objects_orig["ocel:oid"])
assert relations["ocel:eid"].isin(eid_ok).all(), "E2O references unknown event"
assert relations["ocel:oid"].isin(oid_ok).all(), "E2O references unknown object"
assert o2o["ocel:oid"].isin(oid_ok).all() and o2o["ocel:oid_2"].isin(oid_ok).all()

# --- duplicate checks (ignore a possible ocel:timestamp copy on relations) ---
e2o_key = ["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
o2o_key = ["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
assert not relations.duplicated(e2o_key).any(), "duplicate E2O"
assert not o2o.duplicated(o2o_key).any(), "duplicate O2O"

# --- construct OCEL (VERSION-DEPENDENT) ---
# pm4py>=2.7 typically: OCEL(events=..., objects=..., relations=..., o2o=...)
# older builds may reject the o2o= kwarg; then construct without it and
# assign ocel.o2o = o2o afterwards. Do not call pm4py.ocel_o2o_enrichment
# to "prove" business semantics — it only materialises structural candidates.
from pm4py.objects.ocel.obj import OCEL  # constructor signature varies by version

try:
    ocel = OCEL(
        events=events_orig,
        objects=objects_orig,
        relations=relations,
        o2o=o2o,
    )
except TypeError:
    ocel = OCEL(
        events=events_orig,
        objects=objects_orig,
        relations=relations,
    )
    ocel.o2o = o2o

# --- discoveries requested by the benchmark ---
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. **Forcing ev11 onto exactly one of DEL-X or DEL-Y** turns a portal wildcard into a precise carrier acknowledgement. The OC-DFG then shows `Dispatch → Confirm` on one delivery and a truncated dispatch on the other, which looks like a failed second leg. The log does not say that.

2. **Attaching ORD-B to ev11 because the customer matches** creates a false joint-confirm trace. ORD-B may never have left the warehouse. Equivalent-execution clustering would pull ORD-B toward ORD-A on the strength of a portal field that only named ORD-A.

3. **Treating 130+100=230 as proof that INV-44 bills ORD-B** writes a two-order invoice into every Order flattening and into the OTG. If 230 includes tax or a surcharge on ORD-A alone, analyses will invent a cross-order billing subprocess and will merge two commercial cases.

4. **Closing PAY-2 onto INV-44 to “complete cash application”** produces `Issue Invoice → Record Payment → Record Payment` and erases a real unapplied-cash variant. Finance-oriented conformance then reports a 100% settled invoice that may still be open, and it hides a payment object whose only legal fact is ev14.

5. **Adding Order objects onto Pack Item or Dispatch Package** so that the Order flattening “looks complete” injects `Add Item → Pack Item → Dispatch Package` into the Order OC-DFG. Those activities are item/package/delivery work. The resulting order model is a chimera: every order appears to be packed even when only an item was scanned, and frequencies of Pack Item explode from the object side.

6. **Collapsing ev10 into DEL-X as a bulk dispatch** (or writing `DEL-X carries PK-8`) deletes DEL-Y from the process and fuses two carrier refs. Delivery-level throughput, route parallelism, and any SLA computed per `carrier_ref` become wrong. The simultaneous 10:00 timestamps are compatible with two vans, not only with one.

7. **Packing IT-B1 into PK-7 against `PK8-2`** manufactures a glass+cable package that the scanner trail contradicts and conceals the mixed PK-8 box. Downstream, GLASS-1 appears to travel with CABLE-3, which will pollute any sku- or fragility-aware performance view.

8. **Deriving O2O from a guessed E2O and then feeding `ocel_o2o_enrichment` (or the derived O2O) back as confirmation** is a closed loop. It will happily emit `contains`/`packs`/`carries`/`bills`/`settles` for every reachable pair and present a dense OTG as if the business had recorded it.

The conservative repair therefore stops at the two local cardinality holes that identifiers almost close (ev08–PK-8, ev10–DEL-Y) plus their schema projections, and it leaves confirm, billing scope, and the second payment explicitly unresolved.
