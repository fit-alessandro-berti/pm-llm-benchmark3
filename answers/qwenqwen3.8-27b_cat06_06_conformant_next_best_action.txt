## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The ranking treats conformance as a hard constraint, not a utility term to be traded away. Within conformant or conditionally conformant actions, the comparison is between deadline loss, false rejection/hold, regulatory exposure, and resource spillovers.

- **Deadline benefit of valid execution**: €20k–€35k avoided operational loss.
- **Customer harm from false rejection or prolonged hold**: €15k–€80k.
- **Regulatory/compliance harm from unlawful execution**: €100k–€500k plus non-monetary consequences.

The regulatory harm range is asymmetric. Its lower bound, €100k, already exceeds the upper bound of customer harm, €80k, and the upper bound of operational benefit, €35k. Therefore, any action with a material probability of unlawful execution is dominated by a conformant path that either obtains the required second-officer review or rejects/holds with adequate documentation. Actions A and B are not merely risky; they are currently invalid. A favorable forecast of approval or deadline benefit does not create a valid approval path.

The prediction that ultimate approval after a conformant review is 0.55–0.75 is important. It means a conformant review has positive option value: it can capture the €20k–€35k execution benefit in a majority of cases while avoiding regulatory harm if the fuzzy match is real. Rejecting now without review may be reasonable only if the documented rejection threshold is met or if no conformant review can be obtained before the deadline.

Action C is operationally attractive because it gives the highest chance of a valid P-884 decision before the 90-minute deadline. Its earliest valid execution is around t50. However, it interrupts P-901 for ~45 minutes and increases P-901’s breach probability by 0.12–0.28. If P-901 is high-value, the expected spillover can be large. The expected spillover is:

> 0.12–0.28 × P-901 breach loss.

If P-901’s breach loss is comparable to P-884’s customer harm range, the spillover is already material. If P-901 is materially more valuable, the spillover may outweigh P-884’s deadline benefit. Therefore C is strong but not automatically first.

Action D, especially in parallel with waiting for S4, is preferable when S4 can be used soon because it avoids the P-901 interruption while still gathering additional sanctions information. The alternate screening call may reduce uncertainty, clarify the fuzzy match, or support a rejection/SAR decision. But it does not replace the mandatory second-officer review while risk 70 or an unresolved fuzzy match remains.

Action F is a fast risk-control action. It is better than E when the documented rejection threshold is satisfied because it ends the hold and avoids prolonged customer harm. If the threshold is not satisfied, F is risky and may be nonconformant or vulnerable.

Action E is conformant but almost guarantees the current deadline will be missed. Its value is informational, not operational speed. It is better than an unsupported rejection only when the rejection threshold is not met and the case can be safely held.

Action G has low expected value as a primary action because the exception is not currently enabled. It may consume 20–60+ minutes without producing a valid override. It should not delay a conformant second review. It becomes relevant only if a genuine incident exists and both exception conditions are actually satisfied.

Finally, the predictions are observational. Interventions such as interrupting P-901, running alternate screening, or seeking an override may change approval probability, review duration, and resource availability. The ranking therefore uses ranges and avoids treating the approval probability as a fixed point estimate.
