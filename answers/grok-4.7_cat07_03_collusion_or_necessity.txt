# 2. Benign alternative explanations

### Buyer_Carla → Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan → Manager_Hugo (fast approvals and return loops)

1. Informal specialization plus a designed rush channel. Carla’s main family is emergency medical supplies, Dan’s is facilities repairs, Gita is the delegated urgent approver, and Hugo handles emergency overrides. Medians of 11 and 14 minutes can be the operating norm for that work.
   - Support: category and `emergency_flag` line up with those roles; a written SLA or roster assigns these pairs; non-emergency cases for the same pairs are near the 2.1 h median; loop activity is Negotiate Terms or a comment return, not bank details.
   - Refute: a material subset has `emergency_flag=False`, no incident or work-order id, and high amount band; the same pairs also pass payment or bank changes; Gita’s shared account hides a different person with no delegation record.

2. Genuine emergency handling, with the fast approval permitted and the later justification still outstanding or delayed (late-justification shares are 18% and 21%).
   - Support: clinical, on-call, or facilities tickets open before Create Purchase Request; Retrospective Justification exists, is written by someone else, and is late only because of weekends or a documented backlog.
   - Refute: no upstream ticket; justification missing; justification resource equals override resource after the shared-account correction; goods or services not received.

### Create Purchase Order + Approve Purchase Order four-eyes exceptions (42 cases)

1. Authorized emergency exception, not a segregation failure, when Emergency Override is present and a different person justifies within 3 working days.
   - Support: `emergency_flag=True`, override event present, `entered_by` on the justification differs from the approver, business-day gap ≤ 3, and the narrative matches the purchase category.
   - Refute: justification absent, late, or same person after identity cleansing; flag set with no override.

2. `legacy_erp` mapping makes Bot_PO and the human approver look like one `org:resource`.
   - Support: `source_system="legacy_erp"`, `approved_by` differs from the collapsed `org:resource`, and the 42 shrink sharply when the resource field is rebuilt from `entered_by` / `approved_by`.
   - Refute: `entered_by` and `approved_by` are the same human on a non-emergency, non-legacy case.

### VendorMaster_Lena → AP_Kim, vendor family V-77x

1. Legitimate mass maintenance after a banking-platform change, joined poorly to procurement cases. Several bank-detail edits were expected; the maintenance-ticket link is missing in the event log.
   - Support: one change ticket lists the V-77x accounts; new account numbers match the vendor’s mandated platform; a second vendor-master or treasury reviewer distinct from Lena and AP_Kim signed the ticket; the same new accounts appear on later ordinary payments; the 9 four-eyes cases disappear once the maintenance user is distinguished from the payment approver.
   - Refute: no ticket; beneficiary account differs from the migration standard; account used for few payments and then changed again (relevant to the 11-case return loop); the human who changed the account also released payment in the access log; pattern repeats on families with no platform change.

2. AP_Kim’s higher-risk-supplier queue produces short, legitimate payment-approval times after a vendor-master update, without a second person having edited the bank data.
   - Support: Kim’s other high-risk approvals are similarly fast; `entered_by` on the bank change is a migration user or VendorMaster_Marc; invoice match and goods receipt are complete; amount and payee match the contract.
   - Refute: Kim or Lena is the authenticated user on both events; match failed or was overridden; payee does not match the vendor record that treasury confirmed.

### Same-person Emergency Override and Retrospective Justification (17 cases)

1. Quarter-close migration copied a single user and a single timestamp onto 12 historical justifications (`source_system="quarter_close_upload"`).
   - Support: the spreadsheet has a real author different from Hugo or Gita, original dates within 3 working days, and the identical minute appears only as the load time.
   - Refute: the spreadsheet also shows the same person authored override and justification, or the justification text is duplicated boilerplate dated after the deadline.

2. Batch certification by the duty manager after a weekend surge, recorded late but based on earlier independent review notes.
   - Support: on-call notes, ticket logs, or email show a different reviewer within 3 working days; the event log only stores the person who later typed the batch.
   - Refute: no earlier note; `entered_by` equals the override resource in the source system as well as in the log.

### Subcontracting loops elsewhere, including Bot_Match → AP_Kim → Bot_Match

1. Ordinary rework: approver comments, buyer edits the PO, approver completes the case. The Aline / Elena and Boris / Felix loops (33 and 30) are the reference pattern.
   - Support: a comment or attribute change sits between the two outer events; both teams show similar rates; cycle time stays near the routine median.
   - Refute: the middle event is Change Vendor Bank Details, Emergency Override, or Payment Approval, especially on high amount bands.

2. Bot exception feedback for higher-risk suppliers, which is AP_Kim’s assigned work (210 handovers, median 6 min; 52 loops).
   - Support: match-exception reason codes explain the bounce; Iris and Jonas show the same bot loop on their own queues at similar rates per exception.
   - Refute: Kim clears match failures that Iris and Jonas cannot, immediately before payment to a newly changed account.

### Same person on Vendor Check and Change Vendor Bank Details (31 cases)

1. Role design in a two-person vendor-master team: Lena’s main family is bank changes, Marc’s is vendor checks, and each covers the other.
   - Support: policy allows one vendor-master role to do both; cases occur during the other person’s absence; a second control binds payment approval to someone outside vendor master.
   - Refute: policy requires segregation; the 31 cases cluster on V-77x and on the same cases as the 9 payment-approval exceptions.

2. Sequential steps of one onboarding case, misread as a four-eyes rule that was never adopted for this pair.
   - Support: control matrix has no such rule; Marc → AP_Iris (41 cases, median 1.8 h) shows the ordinary check-to-payment path with low manual share.
   - Refute: internal policy does require a second vendor-master user, and waivers are missing.

def case_ids(log):
    return {trace.attributes["concept:name"] for trace in log}

def four_eyes_violations(log, act1, act2, resource_key):
    """PM4Py's filter keeps cases that RESPECT four-eyes (different resources).
    Violations are cases that contain both activities and fail that filter.
    """
    both = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        log, "concept:name", [act1, act2], level="case", retain=True
    )
    # Drop cases that do not actually contain each activity.
    kept = []
    for trace in both:
        acts = {e["concept:name"] for e in trace}
        if act1 in acts and act2 in acts:
            kept.append(trace)
    both = pm4py.filtering.filter_log if False else kept  # both is an EventLog-like list
    compliant = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        log,
        act1,
        act2,
        activity_key="concept:name",
        resource_key=resource_key,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    bad_ids = case_ids(log)  # placeholder replaced below
    compliant_ids = case_ids(compliant)
    # Rebuild both-activity ids explicitly
    both_ids = set()
    viol_ids = set()
    for trace in log:
        cid = trace.attributes["concept:name"]
        res1, res2 = set(), set()
        for e in trace:
            if e["concept:name"] == act1:
                res1.add(e.get(resource_key))
            elif e["concept:name"] == act2:
                res2.add(e.get(resource_key))
        if res1 and res2:
            both_ids.add(cid)
            if res1 & res2:
                viol_ids.add(cid)
    return viol_ids, both_ids - viol_ids

po_viol, po_ok = four_eyes_violations(

)
bank_viol, bank_ok = four_eyes_violations(
    log, "Change Vendor Bank Details", "Payment Approval", "resource_for_sod"
)

# Normative check (pseudocode): business days, different human, case-specific text.
def justification_defects(df):
    rows = []
    for cid, g in df.sort_values("time:timestamp").groupby("case:concept:name"):
        ov = g[g["concept:name"] == "Emergency Override"]
        ju = g[g["concept:name"] == "Retrospective Justification"]
        if ov.empty:
            continue
        t0 = ov["time:timestamp"].iloc[0]
        actor = ov["resource_for_sod"].iloc[0]
        if ju.empty:
            rows.append((cid, "missing", actor, None))
            continue
        t1 = ju["time:timestamp"].iloc[0]
        justifier = ju["resource_for_sod"].iloc[0]
        # Count Mon–Fri between the two timestamps; do not use raw 72 hours.
        bdays = len(pd.bdate_range(t0.normalize(), t1.normalize())) - 1
        same = actor == justifier
        batch = (
            ju["source_system"].iloc[0] == "quarter_close_upload"
        )
        if same or bdays > 3:
            rows.append((cid, "same" if same else "late", actor, justifier, bdays, batch))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

# 5. Evidence that would materially change the assessment

### Would increase concern

- Access logs show one human authenticated both Change Vendor Bank Details and Payment Approval on a V-77x case, and `entered_by` / `approved_by` agree after the mapping correction.
- The account paid is not the account on the vendor’s platform-migration standard, or it was used for a small number of payments and then changed again, with no treasury ticket.
- Payment or Release Payment occurred despite a failed invoice match, missing goods receipt, or a payee that does not match the contract, especially in the 16-case manual fast set.
- The same bank-change-to-payment pattern appears for other vendor families that had no platform change, or V-77x concentration remains after the mass-maintenance batch is removed.
- Retrospective justification text is identical across unrelated cases, timestamped before the override, or still same-person in the source spreadsheet and the identity log.
- For Carla / Gita or Dan / Hugo, `emergency_flag=False`, there is no prior incident or work order, the amount band is high, and the shared account cannot be tied to a delegated approver.
- Assistant names in `entered_by` have no delegation, and those users both entered and approved high-amount non-emergency POs.
- The 11-case Lena → AP_Kim → Lena loop corresponds to a bank change, a payment, and a second bank change, rather than a field correction with a ticket.

### Would reduce concern

- A migration ticket enumerates the V-77x bank changes, a reviewer other than Lena and AP_Kim approved it, and every paid account matches that ticket.
- Rebuilding the resource from `entered_by` and `approved_by` removes the 9 bank/payment same-person hits and the legacy_erp portion of the 42 PO hits.
- The quarter-close spreadsheet shows a different author and a business date within 3 working days for the 12 batched justifications; the shared minute is the load time.
- Written roster and SLA assign Gita to urgent medical approvals and Hugo to weekend facilities overrides, and the fast cases carry a work-order time earlier than the PO.
- Subcontracting loops sit on comment or quote revisions, with no bank-detail or payment event between the outer activities.
- Kim’s Bot_Match speed matches the exception queue used by other AP resources once rates are scaled by exception count.
- Policy explicitly allows one vendor-master resource to perform both Vendor Check and Change Vendor Bank Details, and payment approval stays outside that team.

# 6. Cautious audit-report wording

This review covered procurement-to-payment pairings, segregation-of-duties exceptions, approval timing, subcontracting loops, and case attributes for the last quarter. The evidence is more consistent with a mixture of informal specialization, emergency handling, and data-quality limits than with a single explanation. It does not establish collusion or fraud. A smaller set of control exceptions still needs testing.

Buyer_Aline with Approver_Elena and Buyer_Boris with Approver_Felix account for most joint work. Medians near 2.5 hours, low emergency rates, and dispersed vendors fit ordinary team routing. Frequent co-working is an expected result of that routing and is insufficient to indicate a control failure. Faster paths for Buyer_Carla with Approver_Gita and Buyer_Dan with Manager_Hugo match assigned rush roles in medical supplies and facilities repairs. Emergency flags cover about 79 to 88 percent of those cases, so speed is an expected feature of the rush channel. Same-resource purchase-order approval remains acceptable only if a different person justifies it within three working days. Some justifications are late.
