## 2. Raw-versus-Normalized Interpretation
The **raw fitness collapse in March (0.920.55)** reflects a **semantic/logging drift**: the system migration renamed `Validate Eligibility` to `Eligibility Screen` and introduced duplicated `Open Case` events. Mapping these labels (e.g., treating `Eligibility Screen` as `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicates) **recovered fitness to 0.90**, proving the drift was **provenance-related**, not behavioral. However, this does **not** imply the *business process* was unchanged—only that the **event labels** were reconciled. Duplicated technical events (e.g., `Open Case`) and missing resource IDs **distort conformance checks** by introducing spurious paths, but they do not explain later performance drift (e.g., rework spikes in May).

Post-May, the **gap between raw (0.48–0.55) and mapped (0.86–0.89) fitness persists**, indicating **real behavioral/performance drift** (e.g., supplier-induced rework, queueing). The mapped fitness cannot mask **control-flow changes** (e.g., increased `Request Info` loops) or **service-time degradation** (e.g., queueing delays). Thus, while March’s drift was primarily **logging**, later drift is **multifactorial**.

    mapped_fitness = pm4py.conformance_token_based_replay(df_mapped)

## 9. Provisional Attribution
The strongest conclusions are:
1. **March 2026 drift** is **primarily logging/semantic** (95% confidence), driven by the system migration (label renaming, duplicated `Open Case` events, missing resource IDs). The fitness recovery after mapping confirms this.
2. **May 2026 drift** is **supplier-driven performance drift** (90% confidence), with Supplier Z’s incomplete documents causing a **12% spike in rework loops** and a **5-hour cycle-time increase**. The non-random subset of Supplier Z cases (higher baseline complexity) amplifies the effect.
3. **June–July 2026 drift** is **staffing-driven queueing** (85% confidence), with `median_cycle_h` increasing by **12.7 hours** and `missing_resource_pct` peaking at 6.2%. The priority rule in July **redistributes delay** (high-complexity cases wait less, standard cases wait more), but the aggregate data obscures this.
4. **October 2026 drift** is **seasonal population drift** (95% confidence), with `high_complexity_pct` spiking to 41% and `cycle_h` peaking at 47.8 hours. The interaction with Supplier Z’s backlog explains the persistence of rework.

**Plausible but less certain**:
- The **December 2026 improvements** are likely due to **interventions (overtime + document-quality campaign)**, but seasonality may also play a role (80% confidence).
- **Persistent behavioral adaptation** (e.g., assessors learning to handle incomplete docs) is plausible but cannot be isolated from other mechanisms.

**Mediation/Interaction**:
- The **priority rule (July) mediates the staffing cuts**, reducing queueing for high-complexity cases but worsening it for standard cases. This interaction is **not fully observable in monthly aggregates**.
- **Supplier Z’s non-random subset** confounds case-mix and supplier effects. Without case-level data, we cannot fully disentangle them.

**Would revise if**:
- Case-level data showed Supplier Z cases were **not** inherently more complex (challenging the population drift hypothesis).
- December improvements persisted beyond the intervention period (suggesting behavioral adaptation).
- Staffing cuts were **not** concentrated in high-complexity teams (weakening the queueing hypothesis).

## 10. Monitoring Decision Rules

1. **IF** `raw_model_fitness` drops >15% from baseline **AND** `mapped_model_fitness` recovers to >0.90, **THEN** diagnose **logging/semantic drift** (e.g., label renaming or duplicated events), **BECAUSE** the fitness gap is due to provenance issues, not behavioral changes.

2. **IF** `supplier_z_pct` spikes >10% **AND** `Assess->RequestInfo` rate increases >15%, **THEN** investigate **supplier disruption**, **BECAUSE** incomplete documents from Supplier Z drive rework loops.

3. **IF** `median_cycle_h` increases >20% **AND** `missing_resource_pct` rises >3%, **THEN** flag **staffing/organizational drift**, **BECAUSE** resource shortages cause queueing delays.

4. **IF** `high_complexity_pct` spikes >15% during renewal season **AND** `Assess->SeniorAssessment` rate increases, **THEN** confirm **seasonal population drift**, **BECAUSE** annual renewals historically increase complexity.

5. **IF** `Assess` waiting time (p90) increases >25% for standard cases **BUT** decreases for high-complexity cases, **THEN** diagnose **priority rule redistribution**, **BECAUSE** the rule prioritizes high-complexity cases in the queue.

6. **IF** `rework_variant_pct` remains elevated (>20%) despite interventions, **THEN** suspect **persistent behavioral adaptation** or **unresolved supplier issues**, **BECAUSE** assessors may be compensating for incomplete documents.

7. **IF** `missing_resource_pct` is concentrated in migrated activities (e.g., `Eligibility Screen`), **THEN** diagnose **system migration artifacts**, **BECAUSE** resource IDs were lost during the March migration.
