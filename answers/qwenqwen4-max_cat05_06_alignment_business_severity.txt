# 1. Materiality Framework

## 1.1 Two independent judgements, never one number

Every case receives **two separate assessments** that must never be collapsed:

- **Technical deviation (from the supplied alignment):** what edit distance the model needed — synchronous fit, log-only move (`L`), model-only move (`M`), silent move (`T`). This is a statement about *model/log correspondence*, measured in edits under a uniform cost of 1.
- **Business severity (analyst judgement, 1–5):** the consequence for the control objective if the interpretation is true, given money, timing, reversibility, harm, recurrence and model scope.

A third, orthogonal dimension is **confidence**: how likely the interpretation is to be true given the evidence. Severity answers "how bad if true"; confidence answers "how sure are we it is true". A severity-5 case with medium confidence (CL-602: did an off-system fraud screen happen?) demands *evidence preservation and investigation*, not a conclusion of misconduct. A severity-1 case with high confidence (CL-603 retry) demands *automated cleanup*, not investigation.

## 1.2 Severity axes

| Axis | Question | Why it changes materiality |
|---|---|---|
| Control objective | Which control failed: authorization/limits, fraud prevention, segregation of duties, loss-assessment integrity, payment integrity, customer communication, or only record-keeping? | Authorization and fraud controls protect money; record-keeping protects provability. Both matter, unequally. |
| Amount / exposure | Euros actually released or at risk; is the amount inside delegated authority? | €480,000 (CL-602) vs €800 (CL-605) with identical or lower edit cost. |
| Reversibility | Can funds be recalled? Was the beneficiary verified? Is the payee established or newly changed? | Irreversible disbursement to a freshly changed IBAN (CL-610) is worse than a recoverable over-payment. |
| Timing | Was the control performed *before* the irreversible act? Was notification inside 24h? | CL-604: payment 10:03, approval 10:20 — order in real time, not order in the log, is decisive (P3). |
| Customer / regulatory harm | Was the claimant misled, left uninformed, or paid into a possibly diverted account? Are AML/consumer-protection/reporting duties engaged? | Determines whether this is an internal hygiene item or a reportable control breach. |
| Recurrence | Isolated event or a monthly pattern (74 retries, 9 adjuster gaps, 4 auto-approval violations)? | Systemic patterns justify preventive redesign; isolated events justify case handling. |
| Evidence reliability | Immutable, independently corroborated record vs mutable text link vs verbal assertion. | CL-606 (signed, dated external report) is stronger evidence than CL-608 (mutable CRM text field), even at equal cost 1. |
| Model scope (P6) | Is the trace even in scope of M0 (payable claims)? | Out-of-scope traces generate *phantom* obligations (CL-607, CL-612) that must not become operational findings. |

## 1.3 Severity scale used in this report

| Sev | Meaning | Anchor cases |
|---|---|---|
| **5** | Irreversible funds movement with a mandatory fraud/authorization control defeated, or payment executed before approval; possible diversion; potentially reportable. | CL-602, CL-604, CL-610 |
| **4** | Funds released under an approval authority that policy does not grant (data-aware failure), no fraud indicator yet; recovery uncertain; systemic. | CL-609 |
| **3** | A key control was probably performed but compliance cannot be reliably reconstructed on a high-exposure file (broken/mutable evidence linkage). | CL-608; CL-606 *if* document linkage is unstable |
| **2** | Control objective likely met but recorded outside the model/system, or naming/semantic mismatch; process and data hygiene. | CL-606, CL-611 |
| **1** | Technically deviating but business-conformant: telemetry, retry duplicates, silent routing moves, out-of-scope terminal outcomes. | CL-601, CL-603, CL-605, CL-607, CL-612 |

**Escalation/de-escalation is explicit, not implicit.** Each row in §2 states the condition that would move it (e.g. CL-608  2 once an immutable, corroborated notification link is retained; CL-606  3 if no stable document-to-case ID; CL-612  2 only if no closure acknowledgement exists anywhere).

## 1.4 Confidence rubric (four separable components)

1. **Alignment fidelity** — high by instruction (alignments are authoritative diagnostics) but *not unique*: another optimal alignment may place blame differently. Move placement never determines accountability.
2. **Attribute reliability** — amount, risk score, bank-change recency as given; may themselves be wrong or stale (tested in §4).
3. **Occurrence inference** — a missing event is *absence of evidence*, never proof of absence. `M(Fraud Screen)`  "no screen happened"; `M(Assess Loss)`  "no assessment happened".
4. **Causal attribution** — whether the likely cause (rule-engine gap, connector retry, label drift, scope gap) is established or merely plausible.

Reported confidence is the weakest link of components 2–4 for the *business* claim being made.

## 1.5 Priority rule (judgement, not arithmetic)

Priority  severity × time-criticality (reversibility window) × recurrence, **modulated** by confidence:

- High severity + high confidence  **contain now** (recall, freeze, gate).
- High severity + low/medium confidence  **preserve evidence and investigate now**, with precautionary containment; do not downgrade severity because evidence is thin, and do not accuse.
- Low severity + high confidence + high recurrence  **automate the fix** (filtering, dedup, mapping, model repair).
- **Separate track:** model/scope/data defects get their own priority even when individual case severity is 1 (CL-607/CL-612), because they corrupt the entire conformance signal.

## 1.6 Sample-level materiality fact

Total supplied raw cost = **20**. Cases rated severity  2 carry **15 of 20 cost units (75%)**. The three severity-5 cases carry **4 of 20 cost units (20%)** but **€574,500 of the €986,700 paid value (58%)**. Raw cost and business risk are, in this sample, close to inversely informative.

# 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

Ground rule applied throughout: **a missing event is not proof that an activity did not occur.** Each hypothesis set below states what would discriminate, and how the result would change severity or ownership.

### 4.1 CL-602 — missing Fraud Screen on €480k with a 1-day-old bank change
- **H1:** Screen never performed (control bypass under time pressure).
- **H2:** Screen performed in a separate fraud tool/case number, never integrated.
- **H3:** Screen performed verbally by an authorized fraud officer (evidence failure, not decision failure).
- **H4:** Event existed but was lost in ingestion/ETL.
**Discriminators:** search the fraud tool/vendor portal by claim ID, IBAN and claimant; SIU officer roster, calendar and call records for the approval window; approver's UI audit trail (was a screen result displayed?); ETL completeness reconciliation (source row counts vs ingested events for that day); check whether *other* claims that day carry fraud events (isolated vs systemic).
**Effect:** H2/H4  severity stays 5 for exposure but reclassifies to integration/logging remediation; H1  conduct and AML escalation; H3  severity 4–5 with mandatory artefact gating. Until resolved, treat as "authorization not evidenced", not "fraud occurred".

### 4.2 CL-604 — payment 10:03, approval 10:20
- **H1:** Payment genuinely released before approval (no release gate).
- **H2:** Approval was given verbally before 10:03 but recorded late/back-entered.
- **H3:** Clock skew between core and bank timestamps an apparent inversion.
- **H4:** Two payments exist (an erroneous early one and a correct later one) — the alignment's `M(Pay Claim)` would then be coincidentally right.
**Discriminators:** bank statement / ISO 20022 settlement records (count, amounts, acceptance timestamps); payment-instruction release log and release user/token; core audit trail distinguishing *record-created* vs *effective* timestamps; NTP/clock-offset evidence for both systems; reconciliation of instructions to settlements.
**Effect:** H3 could dissolve the deviation (verify **before** alleging a breach); H2 remains a P3 breach (approval must be recorded before release) but changes intent and remediation; H4 changes financial exposure and requires immediate recovery. **Do not** read `M(Pay Claim)` as evidence of a second disbursement.

### 4.3 CL-606 / PAT-5 — external-adjuster assessment absent from core
- **H1:** Assessment performed externally; pure logging gap.
- **H2:** The signed report belongs to a **different** claim (mis-linkage — acute for the 2 cases without stable IDs).

- **H1:** Pure synonym; one activity, two labels.
- **H2:** `Assess Damage` is a **narrower sub-step**; the full `Assess Loss` procedure is not evidenced (granularity mismatch).
- **H3:** The mapping was changed retroactively to make cases appear conformant.
**Discriminators:** SOP/procedure definitions and UI field specifications for both labels; mapping-table version history (author, timestamp, before/after); re-run conformance with the **as-of** mapping; sample other `Assess Damage` cases for assessment content (survey scope, reserve setting, report sign-off).
**Effect:** H1  severity 2, mapping versioning; H2  severity 3–4 on a €62k HIGH-risk file; H3  data-integrity investigation with compliance involvement.

### 4.7 CL-607 / CL-612 / PAT-7 — denial and withdrawal traces
- **H1:** M0's scope excludes non-payable outcomes; the cases are conformant to the *real* business process (model defect).
- **H2:** Intake should route denials/withdrawals to a separate case type (namespace/data defect).
- **H3:** A subset are genuinely **premature terminations of payable claims** (real deviation).
**Discriminators:** claim lifecycle status fields and reason codes; the coverage pre-screen decision record; existence of any reserve, invoice or payment instruction; volumes of denial/withdrawal by channel; a **written scope decision** signed by process owner and model owner.
**Effect:** H1/H2  model repair or documented exclusion, no operational findings; H3 for any subset  those cases re-enter triage at severity 3–5. Until the ruling exists, publish these as "scope disputed", never as "missing payment".

### 4.8 PAT-1 — four fully synchronous auto-approvals violating P1/P2
- **H1:** Engine thresholds stale relative to the authority matrix.
- **H2:** Risk score / bank-change flag not present in the decision payload at decision time.
- **H3:** Staff deliberately used Auto Approve as a shortcut (override behaviour).
- **H4:** Attribute data wrong (risk mis-scored, bank change mis-dated) — the decisions may have been lawful on the true data.
**Discriminators:** decision-engine rule version history and stored input payloads; authority-matrix sign-off dates; actor identity on `Auto Approve` events (system service vs human user); offline re-scoring of the four claims; bank-change audit table.
**Effect:** H1/H2  preventive engine and data-contract fix; H3  conduct and training with detective monitoring; H4  data-quality remediation and re-evaluation of severity per case.

### 4.9 CL-603 / PAT-4 — duplicate notification with identical message ID
- **H1:** Transport retry, single actual delivery (noise).
- **H2:** Idempotency failed downstream — the claimant received two messages.
- **H3:** Two genuinely different notifications wrongly share an ID (ID-generation defect).
**Discriminators:** provider delivery receipts and provider-side message IDs; claimant portal inbox/message count; payload hash equality; notification template/type fields.
**Effect:** H1  filter/dedup only; H2  minor customer-harm fix plus connector idempotency; H3  correlation-key defect, which also undermines any evidence built on message IDs (relevant to CL-608).

# 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

**Formally:** the supplied cost is ` edits`, with every visible log-only and model-only move priced at 1 and silent moves at 0. It is therefore an **edit-count distance between a trace and a data-blind, time-blind, scope-limited control-flow model**. It contains no term for amount, reversibility, elapsed time, evidence strength, recurrence, or customer/regulatory harm. Business risk is a function of exactly those absent terms. The two quantities are not merely differently scaled — they measure different objects.

**Empirically, in this sample:** severity  2 cases hold **15/20 (75%)** of raw cost; the three severity-5 cases hold **4/20 (20%)** while carrying **58% of paid value (€574,500 / €986,700)**. Cost-ranked triage would place CL-607 and CL-612 (cost 4, no money at risk) at the top and CL-610 (cost 0, probable diversion) at the bottom — an inversion, not a noisy correlation.

**1. Cost-function design (CL-601 vs CL-602).** Three harmless workbench events cost **3**; a missing mandatory fraud screen on a €480,000 HIGH-risk claim with a one-day-old bank change costs **1**. Uniform weighting treats "event not in the model's vocabulary" and "mandatory control absent" as the same currency. Worse, log-moves and model-moves are conflated: an *extra* harmless event and a *missing* critical event are priced identically. Re-weighting can improve triage signal, but it also changes which alignment is optimal — so weights must be an overlay on the authoritative alignments, never a silent re-derivation, and never a substitute for severity judgement.

**2. Scope (CL-607, CL-612).** Both cost 4, the joint maximum, and in both cases `M(Pay Claim)` is a **phantom obligation** manufactured by a model that has no denial or withdrawal terminal state. No payment was intended (CL-607) or owed (CL-612). Cost here measures *model inexpressiveness*, and if consumed naively it generates absurd findings ("claim not paid") and depresses measured fitness for a process that behaved correctly. P6 is explicitly disputed; a cost number cannot adjudicate a scope dispute.

**3. Hidden moves (CL-605).** `T(tau,c=0)` is a silent routing transition from the X-choice. It is **fitting**: no activity was skipped, nothing is missing, and it must never be reported as a deviation. This is the one place where the cost function is exactly right — and it demonstrates the asymmetry: cost 0 correctly means "fits" here, while cost 0 means "fits but unlawful" in CL-609/CL-610. Fitness and compliance are different predicates.

**4. Data conditions (CL-610, CL-609).** The tree offers `X(->(Fraud Screen, Manual Approve), Auto Approve)` with **no guards**. P1's €5,000 / LOW-risk / 7-day bank-change conditions and P2's mandatory-screen triggers live entirely outside the model. CL-610 (€4,500, bank changed one hour before payment) is fully synchronous at cost **0** and is a severity-5 possible diversion; CL-609 (€18,000, MEDIUM) is fully synchronous at cost **0** and is a severity-4 authority breach. Control-flow conformance is structurally incapable of seeing these; only data-aware/temporal conformance can.

**5. Activity granularity and labels (CL-611, and CL-606 across systems).** `L(Assess Damage) + M(Assess Loss)` costs 2 for what is very probably **one activity under two names**, with every control performed. The identical cost 2 could also arise from a genuinely missing assessment plus one unrelated extra event. Cost cannot distinguish lexical variance from substantive omission, nor sub-step granularity from a missing procedure. It also cannot distinguish "not done" from "done in another system" — the central ambiguity of CL-606.

**6. Duplicated telemetry (CL-601, CL-603, PAT-4).** CL-603's extra notification is the *same message ID* re-sent by one connector; 74 such cases exist in the month. Under uniform weighting that is ~74 cost units of pure transport noise — enough to dominate any cost-ranked backlog and consume the triage capacity that CL-602/CL-604/CL-610 need. Per P5, these are not business activities at all (subject to the caveat that telemetry must first be checked for genuine control signals, e.g. segregation-of-duties violations revealed by workbench user IDs).

**7. Missing-but-external evidence (CL-606 vs CL-608).** Both cost 1 for a single model-only move. CL-606 has a **signed, dated adjuster report** that precedes screening and approval — strong corroboration, severity 2. CL-608 has a portal message at +3h whose only case link is a **mutable text field** — weak attribution on a €250,000 file, severity 3. Identical edit distance; materially different provability. Cost has no dimension for evidence reliability, and a missing event is never proof that an activity did not occur.

**8. Irreversibility and timing (CL-604, CL-610).** CL-604 costs 3 — the same as harmless CL-601 — yet the bank accepted the payment 17 minutes **before** approval, and no fraud screen exists on a €90,000 claim. The model has no clock, so P3 is detectable only from timestamps outside the alignment. Additionally, one of CL-604's three cost units (`M(Pay Claim)`) is an **alignment artefact**: there is exactly one disbursement, and reading the model move as a second payment would fabricate financial exposure. CL-610's irreversibility (funds already at a freshly changed IBAN) is likewise invisible at cost 0.

**9. Non-uniqueness of optimal alignments.** Several optimal alignments can distribute the same cost differently (e.g. in CL-604 the `M(Fraud Screen)` may sit before or after the `L(Pay Claim)`). The supplied alignments are authoritative diagnostics here, but **move placement must never determine accountability**: an artefact of tie-breaking is not evidence about who did what.

**Conclusion / operating rule.** Use raw alignment cost for what it measures: model fitness, model-repair prioritization, and logging-vocabulary hygiene. Use a documented, separately maintained severity + confidence judgement for risk, and always publish both columns side by side (as §2 and the code in §7 do). Any attempt to make cost "be" risk — even with clever weights — reintroduces the same category error, because amount × reversibility × timing × evidence strength × recurrence is not an edit distance.
