# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

- **Upstream data quality dependencies:** Committee packaging (A8) and Contract generation (A9) are highly dependent on clean, normalized data from classification (A2) and financial extraction (A6). If A6 performs poorly on low-quality PDFs, A8 will suffer from a garbage-in/garbage-out effect, directly compromising the committee decision.
- **Legal/compliance controls:** Loan activation (A11) absolutely requires foolproof upstream controls. Currently, KYC clearance is bypassed for provisional signing in 6.5% of cases. Therefore, A11 cannot be automated until strict system gates enforce KYC clearance *before* signing (A10) and ensure contract terms match decisions (A9). Furthermore, A8 must be built to enforce the four-eyes principle between analysis and packaging to prevent the 5.8% violation rate seen in high-exposure cases.
- **Human escalation capacity:** The organization only has 3 FTE Compliance specialists. If front-end volume is accelerated (via A1), these specialists will be completely overwhelmed, worsening the already severe 31.0h wait time for entity mismatch. Similarly, automating A5 (document requests) without A6 (normalization) will overwhelm the 9 FTE Credit analysts.
- **External valuation dependency:** Committee packaging (A8) waits on external collateral valuation in 2350 cases. Since this external wait is 58.0h and required in 71% of equipment finance cases, automating internal steps will not remove this fundamental synchronization point.
- **Core-banking integration constraints:** IT can only deeply integrate two modules in the first six months. This restricts combining A1 (registration), A9 (contract generation), and A11 (activation) simultaneously, necessitating a phased choice between front-office throughput and back-office control.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

**Quick-win Portfolio (A1, A2, A5, A10)**
- *Optimizes:* High-visibility, direct labor reduction in high-frequency tasks. Automating A1 directly addresses the 11,880 registration cases, and A10 modernizes customer interactions. It is politically attractive and yields immediate front-office time savings.
- *Sacrifices:* Process-level flow and compliance. It ignores the true bottlenecks (Compliance and Credit Analysis wait times) and simply pushes work faster into blocked queues. Crucially, it fails to meet the compliance mandate requiring at least one module to strengthen controls in the first year.

**Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio (A4, A5, A6, A9)**
- *Optimizes:* End-to-end cycle time by targeting the most severe delays and rework loops identified by process mining: the 31.0h entity mismatch wait (A4), the 20.0h normalization wait (A6), and the 52% missing-document repeat rate (A5). It includes A9 to directly address the 8.5% contract remediation loops and satisfy the compliance mandate.
- *Sacrifices:* The vanity metric of automating the highest frequency activity (A1). It requires bypassing the politically attractive back-office automation in favor of more complex, middle-office cognitive automation.

**Compliance-first Portfolio (A3, A8, A9, A12)**
- *Optimizes:* Regulatory adherence, auditability, and risk reduction. It targets the 5.8% four-eyes violation via A8, enforces contract consistency via A9, controls exceptions via A12, and systematizes KYC hits via A3.
- *Sacrifices:* Speed, cycle-time reduction, and customer experience. Manual waiting times in the credit and compliance phases will remain high, and the high rate of missing-document loops will continue to frustrate Relationship Managers.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

**Quick-win Portfolio**
- *Cycle time / Waiting time:* The 1.5h wait at registration drops, but the massive 31.0h mismatch and 20.0h normalization waits will *increase* as cases pile up faster upstream. E2E cycle time barely improves.
- *Rework:* Missing document rework (52%) drops via A5, but provisional signing control violations (6.5%) may worsen if E-sign (A10) operates without hard KYC gates.
- *Handovers:* Reduces manual handovers from customers to portal clerks.
- *Resource demand:* Lowers utilization of the 12 FTE back-office (which is already <65% after 15:00) while starving them of work and overloading the constrained 3 FTE Compliance and 9 FTE Credit teams.
- *Control violations:* Fails to fix the 5.8% four-eyes violation and the 8.5% contract error rate.

**Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio**
- *Cycle time / Waiting time:* Dramatic end-to-end reduction. Directly collapses the 31.0h wait (A4), 20.0h wait (A6), and 8.0h wait (A5).
- *Rework:* The 52% missing-document loop is mitigated by A5, and the 8.5% contract remediation loop (14% for credit lines) is eliminated by A9.
- *Handovers:* Formalizes the messy handover from Relationship Managers (who trigger instructions poorly) to the portal (A5), and eases the handover to the 2 senior compliance specialists (A4).
- *Resource demand:* Perfectly balances shifted workloads. A5 increases document inflow, but A6 absorbs the impact, protecting the 9 FTE Credit analysts from being crushed. A4 protects the 3 FTE Compliance team.
- *Control violations:* A9 inherently fixes the contract-decision mismatch, strengthening legal controls.

**Compliance-first Portfolio**
- *Cycle time / Waiting time:* Minor improvements in committee decision (A8) and contract prep (A9), but the core 31.0h mismatch and 20.0h normalization waits remain completely untouched.
- *Rework:* Nearly eliminates the 8.5% contract loop (A9).
- *Handovers:* Formalizes exception handovers via the remediation workbench (A12).
- *Resource demand:* Shifts workload toward A12 remediation tasks, heavily utilizing legal and compliance ops.
- *Control violations:* Enforces the four-eyes principle (A8) and ensures perfectly auditable contract terms (A9).

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **False Positives/Negatives:** Models like the KYC pre-classifier (A3) or Financial extraction (A6) risk severe false negatives or poor extraction. *Compensating control:* Mandatory random QA sampling of 5% of automated KYC clearances; dynamic confidence thresholds for A6 that route low-confidence OCR to human analysts.
- **Auditability:** Automated logic can obscure why a decision was made. *Compensating control:* System architecture must preserve an immutable audit trail logging the model's confidence score, specific data inputs, and any human overrides for all automated steps (especially A9).
- **Model Drift:** Extraction (A6) and classification (A2) degrade as document formats change over time. *Compensating control:* Implement monthly performance dashboards tracking error/rework rates to trigger model retraining.
- **Exception Handling Overload:** Shifting exceptions to humans (A4) can crash small teams. *Compensating control:* Capacity monitoring; if the queue for the 3 FTE compliance specialists exceeds a 24-hour SLA, the system throttles upstream automated intake.
- **Four-eyes Compliance:** System automation might inadvertently allow a single user to run end-to-end tasks. *Compensating control:* Strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) must be hardcoded to physically block the same UserID from completing both Credit Analysis and Committee Packaging for High-exposure SME cases.

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

*Constraint check: Max 4 modules in year 1; max 2 deep core integrations in H1.*

**Phase 1 (Months 1-6): Foundation & Controls**
- *Module 1: Financial-statement extraction (A6).* Requires minimal deep core-banking integration (mostly OCR/document processing). Safely deployed first to clear the 20.0h analyst wait time.
- *Module 2: Contract generation w/ control rules (A9).* Deep IT Integration #1. Solves the 8.5% contract rework loop and satisfies the Compliance mandate to strengthen controls immediately.
- *Monitoring metrics:* Analyst processing time per case; % of contracts requiring manual remediation.
- *Gate criteria for Phase 2:* A6 extraction accuracy > 85%; A9 successfully generating > 90% of standard contracts with zero control violations.

**Phase 2 (Months 7-12): Synchronization Accelerators**
- *Module 3: Entity-mismatch assistant (A4).* A workflow overlay that avoids deep core banking constraints. Targets the worst 31.0h wait time without risking core system stability.
- *Module 4: Missing-doc portal nudging (A5).* Deep IT Integration #2 (CRM/Portal). Deployed in H2 because A6 is now stable enough to handle the increased velocity of incoming documents without flooding the credit analysts.
- *Monitoring metrics:* Median waiting time for entity mismatch; % of document requests requiring a second loop.
- *Gate criteria for scaling:* Entity mismatch wait time drops below 15 hours; missing-document repeat rate drops below 25%.

# 7. Final recommendation

I recommend the **Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio (A4, A5, A6, A9)**.

*Defensible Trade-off Argument:*
Senior management's instinct to automate the highest-frequency activity (A1: Register application, 11,880 cases) relies on an illusion of efficiency. The process-mining evidence reveals that frequency is highly misleading here: the Back-office resource pool (12 FTE) handling registration is not the systemic constraint, as their utilization drops below 65% after 15:00. Automating A1 would merely accelerate cases into the actual process bottlenecks—the 3 FTE Compliance team (blocked by a 31.0h wait) and the 9 FTE Credit Analysts (blocked by a 20.0h wait).

By sacrificing the "vanity metric" of front-office automation, this portfolio tackles the true synchronization delays. A4 unclogs the severe compliance bottleneck. A5 and A6 act as a necessary pairing: A5 speeds up document retrieval, and A6 ensures the Credit Analysts aren't crushed by that increased inflow, directly managing the shift in resource demand. Finally, A9 ensures we meet the strict Compliance mandate to strengthen controls in the first year, eliminating the downstream contract rework loop without automating broken processes. This approach respects process dependencies, balances resource workloads, and delivers genuine end-to-end cycle time reduction.
