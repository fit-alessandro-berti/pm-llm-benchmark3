## 1. Disruption timeline

- **T0 (Day -30 to Day -1, baseline)**  
  - **Operational state**: Normal digitally enforced order-to-delivery flow.  
  - **Dominant variants (DFG)**: `Receive order -> Validate authorization -> Inventory reservation -> Pick -> Pack -> Quality/release check -> Dispatch -> Proof of delivery -> Invoice close`, with bounded exceptions/loops (`Compliance hold`, `Backorder wait`, `Repack/recheck`, cancellations).  
  - **Risk posture (metrics + conformance)**: Low and controlled. Median cycle time: Hospital 8h, Pharmacy 23h, Bulk 36h; traceability exceptions 0.8%; cold-chain temp docs missing 0.4%; controlled dual-control missing 0.2%. Normative fitness 0.96; 91% fitting traces.

- **T1 (Day 0 morning, disruption onset)**  
  - **Operational state**: Warehouse management system unavailable after cyber incident; digital checks and scanning become impossible/unreliable.  
  - **Dominant variants**: Transition to manual improvisation (printing snapshots, phone/spreadsheet coordination) and a shift from system-enforced controls to human-enforced controls.  
  - **Risk posture**: Rapidly rising—controls become dependent on training, documentation discipline, and capacity of compliance/pharmacy staff.

- **T2 (Day 0 afternoon to Day 2, manual workaround phase)**  
  - **Operational state**: Manual workarounds dominate (printed inventory snapshot, phone confirmations, spreadsheet pick lists, manual courier calls).  
  - **Dominant variants (DFG)**:
    - `Receive order -> Emergency triage -> (Manual authorization check | Skip system authorization check | Hospital-critical fast lane) -> Manual inventory snapshot check -> Manual pick list -> Manual pack -> (Temporary release control?) -> Dispatch without scan -> Phone proof of delivery -> Spreadsheet invoice hold -> END`
    - `Defer noncritical order -> Backlog queue -> (Manual pick list | Cancel order)`
  - **Risk posture (metrics + conformance)**: High operational and compliance risk. Traceability exceptions jump to 9.5% (vs 0.8% baseline); cold-chain temp documentation missing 4.8% (vs 0.4%); controlled dual-control missing 2.9% (vs 0.2%); rework/correction 12.5% (vs 3.5%); cancellations 7.8% (vs 2.1%). Normative fitness drops to 0.71 with only 44% fitting traces; move-on-log/model deviations include skipped authorization checks, dispatch without scan, phone PoD, missing release/scan/PoD/temperature records.  
  - **Service impact (segmented)**: Hospital-critical service was protected (fill rate 91%; median 10h vs 8h baseline), but Pharmacy-standard degraded sharply (median 54h; fill rate 61%), and Wholesale-bulk worsened (median 96h).  
  - **Temporal signature of hidden work**: Variability in `Dispatch without scan -> Phone proof of delivery` delay and the emergence of invoice holds indicate “evidence debt” forming alongside throughput preservation.

- **T3 (Day 3 to Day 6, partial recovery)**  
  - **Operational state**: Order intake returns, but inventory synchronization and scanning remain unreliable—hybrid process with partial system checks and continued manual evidence capture/reconstruction.  
  - **Dominant variants**: Mixed: some returns to baseline steps, but persistent missing/late evidence and manual reconciliation to bridge system gaps.  
  - **Risk posture (metrics + conformance)**: Improving but still elevated. Traceability exceptions 5.4%; cold-chain missing 2.3%; controlled dual-control missing 1.4%; rework 9.0%; cancellations 5.2%. Fitness 0.82; 59% fitting traces.

- **T4 (Day 7 to Day 21, stabilization with recovery tail)**  
  - **Operational state**: “Mostly normal” operations return, but backlog, reconciliation, and audit remediation persist (explicitly noted).  
  - **Dominant variants (DFG)**: Baseline flow resumes plus explicit recovery/control-tail activities:
    - `Receive order -> Backlog prioritization -> Validate authorization`
    - `Inventory reservation -> Reconcile manual reservation -> Pick`
    - `Dispatch -> Proof-of-delivery remediation -> Invoice close`
    - `Invoice close -> Audit remediation -> END` (2,300 cases)
  - **Risk posture (metrics + conformance)**: Moderated but not baseline. Traceability exceptions 2.2% (still ~3× baseline); cold-chain missing 0.9%; controlled dual-control missing 0.5%; rework 5.8%; cancellations 3.0%. Fitness 0.90; 76% fitting traces. Median cycle time returns for hospitals (8h), but pharmacies (28h) and bulk (58h) remain slower, consistent with backlog and reconciliation load.  
  - **Key recovery insight**: Operational cycle times can look “recovered” while compliance closure lags—`Invoice close -> Audit remediation` represents a long, risk-bearing tail that must be managed as part of recovery, not ignored.

## 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this incident, **resilience** is the ability to maintain critical service (especially hospitals) **while preserving the intent of key controls and producing auditable evidence**, even if the control is executed in a different form during disruption (manual substitute for electronic checks).

**Concealed noncompliance** is throughput preservation achieved by **dropping or deferring controls/evidence**, creating “compliance debt” that surfaces later as a long audit remediation tail (or, worse, as untraceable recall/diversion/patient-safety exposure). The stabilization DFG makes this visible: even as core operations looked normal, **2,300 cases** flowed from `Invoice close -> Audit remediation`, meaning “operationally closed” was not “regulatorily closed.”

- **True resilience (control-preserving adaptations)**:
  - **B (Manual authorization check)** and **F (Temporary release control)**: they preserve control intent under WMS failure, and can be made robust with standardized evidence packets.
  - **J (Backlog prioritization)** and **K (Reconcile manual reservation)**: they convert chaotic work into ordered recovery, provided rules and staffing are explicit.
  - **E (Manual pick list)** is resilient only when paired with strong evidence capture (lot/batch, timestamps, sign-offs); otherwise it becomes traceability debt.

- **Risky shortcuts / concealed noncompliance**:
  - **C (Skip system authorization check)**: creates an authorization gap that is hard to validate after the fact and directly undermines conformance (T2: 44% fitting traces).
  - **G (Dispatch without scan)**: breaks chain-of-custody and is explicitly the largest traceability gap; it shifts risk into downstream reconstruction.
  - **H (Phone proof of delivery)** becomes concealed noncompliance when used as primary evidence for controlled/recall-sensitive shipments without identity and anti-tamper controls.

- **Ambiguous and risk-dependent**:
  - **A (Hospital-critical fast lane)** is resilient if it is “priority + minimum controls,” not “priority therefore bypass.”
  - **D (Manual inventory snapshot check)** is resilient early but becomes risky as staleness grows; it needs time bounds and physical verification for high-risk SKUs.
  - **I (Defer noncritical order)** is resilient capacity protection if fairness guardrails prevent the observed pharmacy degradation (61% fill; 7.8% cancellations).

## 4. Recovery process model

The redesigned model is a **risk-based Disruption Mode** with explicit triggers, allowed variants, minimum controls, and a visible reconciliation/audit tail.

### 4.1 Explicit trigger conditions (mode switching)

Declare Disruption Mode when any holds:

- **WMS unavailable** > 15 minutes or confirmed containment disables it (T1)
- **Scan success rate** < 95% over 30 minutes or scan-sync failures repeat (T3-like)
- **Inventory sync lag** > 30 minutes or mismatch vs physical count exceeds threshold for high-risk SKUs
- **Conformance sentinel**: surge in missing release/PoD/temperature/authorization events beyond baseline tolerance

Declaration requires Ops Incident Lead + Compliance Lead sign-off, timestamp, and a “mode ticket” referenced on all manual evidence packets.

### 4.2 Triage rules (customer × product risk)

Classify orders by:

- **Customer**: Hospital-critical > Pharmacy-standard > Wholesale-bulk  
- **Product risk**: Controlled-substance > Cold-chain > Recall-sensitive > Ambient

Use a transparent priority score (documented) and add a **fairness guardrail** (e.g., minimum daily capacity or maximum backlog age for Pharmacy-standard) to avoid the T2 pattern where hospital service was protected but pharmacies collapsed.

### 4.3 Minimum controls by risk (what may never be skipped)

Define “minimum control sets” that must be satisfied **before dispatch**:

- **All orders**: authorization evidence (system or offline) + pick/pack identity and timestamps + dispatch handoff evidence (scan or substitute chain-of-custody)
- **Cold-chain**: temperature-control record (logger ID, start/stop, acceptance range) + packaging validation/exposure time limit
- **Controlled-substance**: dual-control sign-offs at pick, pack, dispatch + tamper-evident seal numbers + courier ID verification
- **Recall-sensitive**: lot/batch capture + segregation confirmation

Prohibited variants are explicit:

- No “skip authorization” beyond an approved low-risk whitelist with offline evidence.
- No “dispatch without handoff evidence” for Controlled-substance or Cold-chain.

### 4.4 Manual evidence capture (designed, not improvised)

Introduce a standardized **Disruption Evidence Packet** per order:

- Pre-printed **unique packet ID (barcode)** attached to pick list + shipping label
- Offline capture (mobile offline app) or paper forms later scanned:
  - authorization source + staff ID + timestamp
  - inventory verification method (snapshot time; physical bin check for high-risk)
  - pick details (SKU, lot/batch, qty; witness for controlled)
  - pack + seal numbers
  - release checklist + pharmacist sign-off (mandatory for high-risk)
  - dispatch handoff (courier ID, signature/scan)
  - PoD evidence type (signature/OTP/photo; phone only as backup with call metadata)
- Explicit “unsynced packets” backlog once systems return.

### 4.5 Operational flow (BPMN-style bullets)

- START
- Receive order
- Triage (priority score + fairness guardrail)
- Authorization:
  - if system up: Validate authorization
  - else: Manual authorization check
  - if cannot confirm: Compliance hold
- Inventory reservation:
  - manual reservation ledger + snapshot check
  - high-risk SKUs: physical verification/cycle count
- Pick (manual pick list; lot/batch capture; dual-control if controlled)
- Pack (seal + label + evidence packet)
- Release:
  - mandatory for Controlled-substance/Cold-chain/Recall-sensitive
  - sampling allowed for low-risk Ambient with documented rationale
- Dispatch:
  - offline scan queue OR chain-of-custody form (no “dispatch without evidence”)
- Proof of delivery:
  - preferred: signature/OTP + photo + timestamp
  - phone only as backup for low-risk; capture call metadata
- Invoice:
  - if minimum control set complete: Invoice close
  - else: Invoice hold (visible evidence backlog)
- END (operational close)

### 4.6 Backlog prioritization (post-recovery)

When systems partially return:

- Apply **Backlog prioritization (J)** using the same risk matrix and fairness constraints
- Separate:
  - **Operational backlog** (not yet picked/shipped)
  - **Evidence backlog** (shipped but missing minimum controls)
- Publish daily backlog dashboards by segment and product risk.

### 4.7 Reconciliation and audit-remediation (make the tail visible, risk-based)

Create two explicit recovery queues:

- **Reconcile manual reservation queue (K)**: match manual ledger to system inventory; resolve mismatches with SLAs by risk (e.g., controlled/cold chain within 24–48h).
- **Audit remediation queue (L)**: risk-score by missing evidence type (authorization/dispatch/PoD/temp/dual-control). High-risk missing evidence triggers management escalation (customer notification, regulatory reporting, potential retrieval/recall), not silent back-office cleanup.

Define exit criteria for “back to normal”: stable WMS + scan success > 99% + inventory sync lag below threshold + evidence/audit backlog age below agreed limits.

## 5. Resilience indicators

### Leading indicators (during incident)

- **Throughput and service protection**
  - Hospital-critical fill rate and median/p90 cycle time (T2: fill 91%, p90 21h)
  - Pharmacy/bulk cycle time tails and fill (T2: pharmacy fill 61%, p90 118h)
- **Traceability and control completeness (near-real-time)**
  - Traceability exceptions rate (baseline 0.8% vs T2 9.5%)
  - Cold-chain temperature documentation completeness (baseline 0.4% missing vs T2 4.8%)
  - Controlled-substance dual-control completeness (baseline 0.2% missing vs T2 2.9%)
  - % orders with dispatch handoff evidence (scan/chain-of-custody)
- **Process conformance signals**
  - Fitness and % fitting traces trend (0.96/91% baseline → 0.71/44% T2 → 0.90/76% stabilization)
  - Spike in move-on-log/model deviations (missing release check/PoD/temp, dispatch without scan, skip authorization)
- **Backlog health**
  - Operational backlog count and backlog-age p95 (noted as exceeding baseline during T2)
  - Evidence backlog count (orders shipped but missing minimum evidence)
- **Rework and correction pressure**
  - Rework/correction rate (3.5% baseline → 12.5% T2)
  - Compliance staff utilization and queue length (manual checks as bottleneck)
- **Fairness**
  - Fill/cancellation rates by customer group; maximum delay per segment; disparity ratio (hospital vs pharmacy)

### Lagging indicators (after incident)

- **Compliance closure time** and size/age of the audit tail (`Invoice close -> Audit remediation` volume and duration)
- **Residual exception rates** during stabilization (traceability 2.2%; cold-chain missing 0.9%; dual-control missing 0.5%)
- **Audit outcomes**: number/severity of findings by evidence type
- **Financial corrections**: credits/write-offs/disputes due to PoD or authorization gaps
- **Customer outcomes**: cancellations (7.8% T2), complaints, SLA penalties per segment
- **Safety/regulatory outcomes**: diversion investigations, cold-chain excursions, recall execution speed (time to locate/confirm affected lots)

## 7. Management trade-off statement

Management should **accept** explicitly slower fulfillment and/or controlled deferral for low-criticality orders (especially bulk) and fairness-balanced throttling for pharmacies **if** triage rules are transparent and minimum controls remain intact, because this protects hospital supply without creating irrecoverable compliance gaps. Management should **not accept** any “throughput-first” workaround that drops or defers non-substitutable controls—especially **authorization evidence, cold-chain temperature records, controlled-substance dual-control, and dispatch chain-of-custody**—because the stabilization `Invoice close -> Audit remediation` tail shows that after-the-fact remediation is not equivalent to real-time compliance. Evidence that would change this recommendation would be a validated offline evidence system demonstrating near-baseline control completeness (exception rates approaching baseline tolerances) and recovery without a large, aging audit-remediation tail.
