## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated Facts (undisputed evidence):**
1. **Normative Sources:**
   - M0 and CS-9 (2025-01-01) require: (a) concurrent *Sanctions_Check* and *Quality_Review* before activation, (b) *Manual_Procurement_Approval* before *Activate_Supplier*, (c) exclusive choice between approval and rejection, and (d) *Notify_Requester* before *Close_Case*.
   - CS-9 mandates distinct requester/approver roles (not modeled in M0).
   - PD-3 (2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31) permitted *Auto_Approve* for LOW-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups, with *Quality_Review* post-activation.
   - FD-7 (2026-07-10 to 2026-07-12) allowed provisional activation before *Quality_Review* and manual approval for flood relief, with spend caps and retrospective approval.
   - PD-3 and FD-7 were not formally incorporated into CS-9 or M0.

2. **Operational Data:**
   - 312 cases used *Auto_Approve* (289 eligible, 14 ineligible, 9 indeterminate).
   - 47 cases included *Request_More_Info* loops (not in M0).
   - Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29% post-CRM migration (70% mirrored with stable IDs).
   - SUP-703 (HIGH-risk ineligible) and SUP-708 (activation before *Sanctions_Check*) were flagged by Internal Audit.
   - SUP-705 continued FD-7 practices post-expiry without written extension.
   - SUP-712 violated the distinct-roles rule (same human via aliases).
   - SUP-715 involved undisclosed spousal relationship (conflict-of-interest policy).

3. **Model/Data Defects:**
   - M0 lacks: (a) *Request_More_Info* loops, (b) *Auto_Approve* branch, (c) emergency/provisional routes, (d) resource rules, (e) reactivation scope, (f) rejected-lead scope.
   - CRM notification links are mutable for older cases.
   - *Quality_Review* was renamed to *QA_Clearance* (SUP-713) without model update.

**Disputed Propositions:**
| Proposition | Prosecution Position | Defense Position | Burden of Proof |
|-------------|----------------------|------------------|------------------|
| **Authority of PD-3/FD-7** | PD-3/FD-7 are unauthorized exceptions; CS-9 remains binding. | PD-3/FD-7 were approved by steering committee and monitored; CS-9 is outdated. | Prosecution must show CS-9’s formal authority supersedes informal directives. Defense must show PD-3/FD-7 were *de facto* policy. |
| **Scope of M0** | M0 governs all supplier onboarding post-pre-screen. | M0 only applies to accepted leads; rejected leads (SUP-709) and reactivations (SUP-710) are out of scope. | Defense must provide unambiguous scope documentation. Prosecution may rely on M0’s silence as implicit inclusion. |
| **Materiality of Deviations** | Deviations from M0/CS-9 create compliance risk (e.g., SUP-703, SUP-708). | Most deviations reflect model obsolescence or logging gaps; business outcomes were achieved safely. | Prosecution must show harm or risk from deviations. Defense must show deviations were justified or harmless. |
| **Notification Sufficiency** | Missing notifications (SUP-706) or mutable links violate CS-9. | CRM evidence with stable IDs is sufficient; M0’s logging requirements are outdated. | Prosecution must show CS-9’s notification rule is substantive. Defense must show CRM evidence is reliable. |
| **Permanent Auto-Approval** | Auto-approval post-PD-3 (SUP-714) is noncompliant. | Operations normalized the pilot; permanent approval was imminent. | Prosecution must show no policy change was approved. Defense must show normalization was reasonable. |

**Burden of Proof:**
- **Noncompliance:** Prosecution must show (1) a clear violation of a binding rule (CS-9 or M0), (2) material risk or harm, and (3) no authorized exception. Deviations from M0 alone are insufficient without CS-9 linkage.
- **Exception/Defense:** Defense must show (1) the deviation was authorized (e.g., PD-3/FD-7), (2) the rule was obsolete/incomplete, or (3) the deviation was harmless and logged adequately. "Desirable outcomes" alone do not justify exceptions.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Core Argument:** The operation is materially noncompliant with CS-9 and M0 due to unauthorized exceptions, normalization of deviations, and high-risk cases. Desirable outcomes do not retroactively amend policy, and informal practices lack governance.

### **1. Unauthorized Exceptions and Normalization of Deviations**
- **PD-3 and FD-7 were temporary and unincorporated:**
  - PD-3 was a 2-month pilot, yet *Auto_Approve* was used in 312 cases (14 ineligible, 9 indeterminate). SUP-714 used *Auto_Approve* post-expiry, showing normalization.
  - FD-7 was a 3-day emergency directive, yet SUP-705 continued its practices post-expiry without written extension. Operations’ claim of a "factual emergency" is unsupported by documentation.
  - **Binding Rule:** CS-9 requires *Manual_Procurement_Approval* before activation. PD-3/FD-7 were not formally incorporated, so CS-9 remains binding.

- **High-Risk Cases:**
  - SUP-703: HIGH-risk ineligible supplier used *Auto_Approve*, leading to a EUR 180,000 purchase. This violates CS-9’s approval requirement and PD-3’s eligibility criteria.
  - SUP-708: Activation occurred 40 minutes before *Sanctions_Check*. No emergency or pilot applied; this is a clear violation of CS-9’s control-flow.
  - **Material Risk:** These cases demonstrate systemic risk from informal exceptions. Internal Audit flagged both as severe.

### **2. Model Defects Do Not Excuse Noncompliance**
- **M0’s silence does not authorize deviations:**
  - M0 lacks *Request_More_Info* loops, but this does not permit SUP-708’s activation before *Sanctions_Check*. The control-flow violation is unambiguous.
  - M0 does not model resource rules, but SUP-712’s same-human approval violates CS-9’s distinct-roles requirement. The rule exists; the model’s omission does not excuse noncompliance.
  - **Burden:** Prosecution need not prove M0 is perfect—only that CS-9’s binding rules were violated.

- **Notification Failures:**
  - SUP-706 lacks a notification event in the onboarding log, and the CRM link is mutable. CS-9 requires *Notify_Requester* before *Close_Case*; missing or unreliable evidence violates this.
  - Post-CRM migration, 29% of notifications are missing. This systemic gap undermines compliance monitoring.

### **3. Desirable Outcomes  Compliance**
- **SUP-707’s *Request_More_Info* loops are desirable but unauthorized:**
  - The behavior is not in M0 or CS-9. While useful, it cannot be retroactively justified without policy amendment.
- **Auto-approval normalization:**
  - Operations’ belief that permanent approval was "imminent" (SUP-714) is not a policy change. Without formal amendment, this remains noncompliant.

### **4. Scope and Authority**
- **Rejected Leads (SUP-709):**
  - M0’s silence on rejected leads does not mean they are out of scope. CS-9 states "every supplier onboarding," which plausibly includes pre-screen failures.
- **Reactivations (SUP-710):**
  - CS-9 has no reactivation procedure, but M0’s *Register_Supplier* event is missing. This suggests the case is out of scope, but prosecution may argue that reactivations are a subset of onboarding.

### **5. Aggregate Evidence of Noncompliance**
- **312 *Auto_Approve* cases:** 14 ineligible, 9 indeterminate, 6 post-pilot. Even if 289 were eligible, the 23 ineligible/indeterminate cases are noncompliant.
- **47 *Request_More_Info* cases:** All are deviations from M0, and none are authorized by CS-9.
- **Notification missingness:** 29% post-migration is a systemic failure.
- **High-risk cases:** SUP-703, SUP-708, and SUP-712 demonstrate material risk.

**Conclusion:** The operation is noncompliant because:
1. CS-9’s binding rules were violated (approval, control-flow, notifications).
2. Temporary exceptions (PD-3/FD-7) were normalized and extended.
3. High-risk cases show systemic risk from informal practices.
4. Desirable deviations (e.g., *Request_More_Info*) lack authorization.

## 3. Defense Brief

**Core Argument:** M0 is obsolete, incomplete, and incorrectly scoped, and the operation’s deviations reflect necessary adaptations to business reality. The prosecution overstates risk and ignores governance of exceptions.

### **1. M0 is Obsolete and Incomplete**
- **Control-Flow Gaps:**
  - M0 cannot express *Request_More_Info* loops (47 cases, e.g., SUP-707), which are desirable and safe (no activation before evidence). The absence of a loop is a model defect, not noncompliance.
  - M0 lacks *Auto_Approve* and emergency routes, despite PD-3/FD-7 being approved and monitored. The model’s silence does not invalidate these practices.

- **Resource and Scope Defects:**
  - M0 does not model CS-9’s distinct-roles rule (violated in SUP-712). The rule exists, but the model’s omission makes compliance monitoring impossible.
  - M0 does not define scope for rejected leads (SUP-709) or reactivations (SUP-710). CS-9’s "every supplier onboarding" is ambiguous; operations treats these as out of scope.

- **Label Equivalence:**
  - SUP-713 renamed *Quality_Review* to *QA_Clearance* with equivalent content. M0’s rigidity forces false deviations.

### **2. PD-3 and FD-7 Were Authorized Exceptions**
- **PD-3:**
  - Approved by steering committee, monitored by Compliance, and limited to LOW-risk DE suppliers. 289 of 312 *Auto_Approve* cases were eligible; the 14 ineligible cases (e.g., SUP-703) were operator errors, not systemic noncompliance.
  - **Governance:** The Compliance observer’s note ("pilot may proceed as a monitored exception") shows the exception was authorized, even if not formally incorporated.

- **FD-7:**
  - A written emergency directive with spend caps and retrospective approval. SUP-704 complied fully; SUP-705’s post-expiry continuation was reasonable given the ongoing flood recovery.
  - **Materiality:** No transactions exceeded EUR 20,000, and retrospective approvals were completed. The risk was managed.

### **3. Data Quality and Notification Sufficiency**
- **CRM Migration:**
  - 70% of notifications are mirrored with stable IDs. SUP-706’s mutable link is a logging gap, not a substantive violation. The notification was sent (CRM evidence), and the rule’s purpose (transparency) was achieved.
  - **Prosecution’s Standard:** Requiring onboarding-log notifications is outdated. CRM evidence is more reliable and auditable.

- **Event Duplication (SUP-711):**
  - The duplicate *Register_Supplier* event is a data-quality issue, not a control-flow violation. The case otherwise complied with M0/CS-9.

### **4. Desirable Deviations Reflect Business Reality**
- **Request_More_Info Loops:**
  - 47 cases used loops to obtain missing evidence before activation. This is safer than rejecting suppliers outright and is universally regarded as desirable. M0’s inability to express this is a model defect.
- **Auto-Approval Normalization:**
  - Operations’ use of *Auto_Approve* post-PD-3 (SUP-714) reflects the pilot’s success. Permanent approval was imminent; the deviation was temporary and low-risk.

### **5. High-Risk Cases Are Isolated Errors**
- **SUP-703 (HIGH-risk ineligible):**
  - Operator error, not systemic noncompliance. The case was flagged and corrected; no transactions occurred before *Quality_Review*.
- **SUP-708 (activation before *Sanctions_Check*):**
  - No transactions occurred before clearance. The deviation was procedural, not substantive. The supplier was later approved, showing no material risk.
- **SUP-712 (same-human approval):**
  - The distinct-roles rule is not modeled in M0, making compliance monitoring impossible. The case otherwise complied with control-flow.

### **6. Aggregate Evidence of Model Obsolescence**
- **312 *Auto_Approve* cases:** 93% eligible or indeterminate. The 7% ineligible cases are operator errors, not systemic noncompliance.
- **47 *Request_More_Info* cases:** All achieved business outcomes safely. M0’s inability to express this is a defect.
- **Notification missingness:** 70% of missing notifications have stable CRM IDs. The rule’s purpose (transparency) was achieved.
- **High-risk cases:** SUP-703 and SUP-708 were isolated errors with no material harm.

**Conclusion:** M0 is obsolete because:
1. It cannot express desirable behaviors (*Request_More_Info*, *Auto_Approve*).
2. It omits resource rules (distinct roles) and scope (rejected leads, reactivations).
3. PD-3/FD-7 were authorized and monitored exceptions, not noncompliance.
4. Data-quality issues (notifications, duplicates) are logging gaps, not substantive violations.
5. High-risk cases are isolated errors, not systemic risk.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

### **Layered Architecture**
| Layer | Purpose | Implementation |
|-------|---------|----------------|
| **Control-Flow (POWL)** | Core process structure. | Revised POWL model (see Section 7). |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator

# Transitions
register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
manual_approval = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
provisional_activate = Transition(label="Provisional_Activate_Supplier")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

# Concurrent checks (sanctions and quality)
checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])

# Request_More_Info loop (approximated as optional loop)
request_loop = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[
        request_more_info,
        StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[checks])  # Loop back to checks
    ]
)

# Standard manual-approval branch
manual_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[manual_approval, activate])
manual_branch.order.add_edge(manual_approval, activate)

# Auto-approval branch
auto_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[auto_approve, activate])
auto_branch.order.add_edge(auto_approve, activate)

# Emergency/provisional branch
emergency_branch = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[provisional_activate])

# Decision: XOR between manual, auto, emergency, and reject
decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[manual_branch, auto_branch, emergency_branch, reject]
)

# Main model with optional Request_More_Info loop
M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, checks, request_loop, decision, notify, close])
M1.order.add_edge(register, checks)
M1.order.add_edge(checks, request_loop)
M1.order.add_edge(request_loop, decision)
M1.order.add_edge(decision, notify)
M1.order.add_edge(notify, close)

# Policy-overlay table (normalized)
policy_overlays = [
    {
        "overlay_id": "PD-3",
        "name": "Low-Risk Auto-Approval Pilot",
        "effective_start": "2026-06-01",
        "effective_end": "2026-07-31",
        "eligibility_rule": "risk == 'LOW' and country == 'DE' and corporate_group in approved_groups",
        "control_flow_deviation": "auto_approve instead of manual_approval; quality_review post-activation",
        "spend_limit": None,
        "authority": "Steering committee",
        "monitoring": "Compliance observer; monthly review"
    },
    {
        "overlay_id": "FD-7",
        "name": "Flood Emergency Directive",
        "effective_start": "2026-07-10",
        "effective_end": "2026-07-12",
        "eligibility_rule": "critical_relief_supplier == True",
        "control_flow_deviation": "provisional_activate before quality_review and manual_approval",
        "spend_limit": 20000,
        "authority": "Emergency committee",
        "monitoring": "Daily transaction monitoring"
    }
]

# Event/data-collection proposal
event_schema = {
    "case_id": str,
    "event_id": str,
    "activity": str,
    "timestamp": "datetime64[ns]",
    "resource": str,
    "risk": str,  # "LOW", "MEDIUM", "HIGH"
    "country": str,
    "corporate_group": str,
    "critical_relief_supplier": bool,
    "spend_amount": float,
    "notification_id": str,  # Stable CRM ID
    "rule_version": str,  # e.g., "CS-9", "PD-3"
    "overlay_id": str,  # e.g., "PD-3", "FD-7"
    "effective_timestamp": "datetime64[ns]",  # When the event was valid
    "record_timestamp": "datetime64[ns]",  # When the event was logged (immutable)
    "actor_aliases": list,  # List of aliases for resource
    "delegation_evidence": str  # Link to delegation record
}

# Limitations:
# 1. POWL cannot enforce data-aware rules (e.g., risk == "LOW"). These must be checked separately.
# 2. The Request_More_Info loop is approximated as a LOOP operator, which may not capture all real-world variations.
# 3. Resource rules (e.g., distinct roles) are not modeled in POWL and require DECLARE constraints.
```

### **Explanation of Model Choices:**
1. **Concurrency:** *Sanctions_Check* and *Quality_Review* remain concurrent (as in M0).
2. **Auto-Approval:** Added *Auto_Approve* branch for low-risk suppliers.
3. **Emergency Route:** Added *Provisional_Activate_Supplier* for FD-7.
4. **Request_More_Info:** Approximated as a LOOP operator. In practice, this may require DECLARE constraints for more complex loops.
5. **Notification:** Retained *Notify_Requester* before *Close_Case*, but the data schema ensures stable CRM IDs.
6. **Policy Overlays:** The governance table separates temporary exceptions from the core model.
7. **Data Schema:** Includes stable IDs, rule versions, and immutable timestamps to address logging gaps.
