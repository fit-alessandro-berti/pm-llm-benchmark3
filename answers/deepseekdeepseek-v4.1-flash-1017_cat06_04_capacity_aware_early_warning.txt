## 1. Decision rule

**Deployable rule:** A case is swarm-eligible only if it is in `Triage` or `Diagnosis Started`, has not already received a swarm for the same event, and has enough remaining time for the 45-minute swarm to precede `Diagnosis Complete`. A repeated alert without a material new event never creates a new intervention opportunity.

For each eligible case, compute a risk-adjusted marginal net score:

`Score_i  _i^robust × V_i  €450  B_i  S_i  uncertainty/ congestion penalty`

where:
- `_i^robust` uses the effect interval, not just the midpoint. For scarce slots, require positive base-case net and acceptable adverse-case downside; if the lower-bound effect produces material harm, the case is a backup rather than an immediate deployment.
- `V_i` is contractual/economic value of avoiding breach.
- `B_i` is false-alarm/customer disruption burden.
- `S_i` is expected spillover harm per slot. Operationally approximate it as `6 × p_spill × average queue value` plus congestion-delay equivalent. For today, use a base marginal spillover penalty of about €2.4k per slot, with a high-congestion penalty of €3.6k+.
- `_i^robust` and `p_now` are separate: `p_now` is an observational alert signal, not the causal effect of the swarm.

**Calibration adjustment before ranking/alerting:**
- **Public Sector:** use `p_now` and a moderate uncertainty band; no systematic adjustment, but keep sample-size caution.
- **Strategic Enterprise:** adjust downward by about `0.05–0.12` (midpoint `0.085`) because recent backtests show overprediction after workflow redesign.
- **SME:** adjust upward by about `0.04–0.09` (midpoint `0.065`) because recent backtests show underprediction.
- **Non-Native-Language Support:** do not apply an aggressive point adjustment; widen intervals, prefer conservative effect estimates, and require human review for high-stakes deployment. Do not automatically exclude solely because validation is sparse.
- **Imputed or missing live-queue features:** widen uncertainty and, where possible, verify live data before spending a slot. A sharp model jump caused by imputation is not sufficient evidence by itself.

**Alert persistence and repeated alerts:**
- First actionable alert: score normally.
- Repeated unchanged alert: suppress for at least 60 minutes; no new notification and no slot.
- Re-open only if a material evidence change occurs: new customer log, resolved missing data, stage change, regulatory/customer escalation, or a decision-relevant calibrated probability shift of roughly `0.10+`.
- A re-opened case does not automatically get a swarm; it re-enters the priority queue.
- Repeated swarms on the same case are forbidden unless a materially new event has occurred and a duty manager approves under conservative net-benefit assumptions.

**Prioritization:** rank by risk-adjusted net score, reliability/calibration quality, stage eligibility, non-monetary obligations, and fairness constraints. Keep one slot in reserve when the marginal fourth slot is small or adverse-case downside is large. If queue congestion is high, raise the spillover penalty, cap deployments, and stop using marginal slots.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

- **Cool-down:** after any alert, especially a non-deployed one, suppress repeat notifications for at least 60 minutes unless a material new event occurs. Continued model refresh alone is not a new event.
- **Suppression:** unchanged repeated alerts do not consume capacity. They remain visible for monitoring but do not re-enter the slot queue.
- **Escalation:** a material new event reopens scoring: new customer diagnostic log, resolved missing data, new regulatory flag, customer escalation, stage change, or a calibrated probability shift large enough to change the tier.
- **Re-entry:** re-entered cases are re-ranked from scratch. They do not inherit priority from prior alerts. Repeated swarms require a materially new event before Diagnosis Complete plus senior approval and a conservative positive net score.
- **I-05 vs I-07:**  
  - `I-05` is a third alert with no new event. It should be **suppressed** despite moderate raw risk and SME underprediction. Repeated refresh is not new evidence.  
  - `I-07` is also a third alert, but a new customer log is a genuine evidence change. It is therefore **re-opened and re-scored**, not automatically deployed. Its new log may improve diagnosis, but its absolute value is only €5k and its midpoint net before spillover is small; after spillover it is below the slot threshold. Thus `I-07` remains low priority unless no better use exists and congestion is very low.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases:** high-value cases like I-04 and I-08 naturally dominate. Safeguard: monitor allocation by value decile and set a rolling minimum for high-effect/lower-value cases, e.g. at least 10–15% of deployed slots over a week if their conservative net is positive. Do not let repeated suppression of low-value cases become permanent exclusion.
- **Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support:** I-09 has sparse validation and uncertain effect. Safeguard: do not automatically downgrade sparse segments; use interval-based scoring, include high-effect sparse-segment cases when downside is acceptable, and audit post-swarm outcomes by segment. Require manual review if sample uncertainty is the main reason for exclusion.
- **Differential calibration across segments:** use segment-specific calibration adjustments and report reliability curves by segment. Strategic Enterprise should not be over-ranked due to overprediction; SME should not be under-ranked due to underprediction. Raw `p_now` should not be used as a cross-segment threshold.
- **Selective intervention changing future training labels:** intervened cases may have artificially lower breach rates, corrupting future observational labels. Safeguard: log swarm use as a treatment variable, include it in the data warehouse, and estimate effects with propensity weighting, doubly robust methods, or randomized encouragement near the decision threshold. Do not train future breach models as if intervened outcomes were natural.
- **Spillover harms falling on untreated groups:** queue delays and added breach risk may disproportionately affect cases not selected, often lower-priority or already-congested queues. Safeguard: include spillover penalties in slot scoring, cap swarms per interval, schedule during lower-congestion windows, monitor breach and delay rates of untreated diagnosis-stage cases, and add temporary queue capacity or priority remediation if spillover harm concentrates.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

- **Operational monitoring:** slot use, reserve use, time from alert to swarm, stage at swarm, compliance with pre-Diagnosis-Complete cutoff, repeated-swarm violations, queue delay minutes per slot, customer complaints, and false-alarm burden.
- **Predictive monitoring:** calibration curves, Brier score, log loss, and reliability by segment, value decile, and language-support group. Track `p_prev` vs `p_now`, missing-data rates, imputation frequency, and sudden probability jumps caused by data-quality issues.
- **Causal-effect monitoring:** estimate intervention effects using randomized slot availability, threshold-based encouragement, or propensity-matched comparison where randomization is infeasible. Update effect estimates by segment, stage, congestion level, and value tier. Flag cases where observed benefit is near zero despite predicted benefit.
- **Capacity and spillover monitoring:** track queue length, specialist workload, delay minutes per used slot, and breach rates among untreated diagnosis-stage cases. Compute marginal net per additional slot and stop using slots when marginal spillover exceeds expected direct benefit.
- **Fairness monitoring:** report intervention rates, breach outcomes, suppression rates, and spillover burden by segment, value decile, and language-support group. Trigger review if one group’s selection or outcome gap exceeds a pre-specified threshold, e.g. a persistent 10-percentage-point allocation gap not explained by stage/effect.
- **Change triggers:** recalibrate or revise the policy if segment calibration is off by more than about `0.05` for two consecutive review periods; if the lower confidence bound of marginal net becomes negative; if estimated swarm effects fall below the supplied lower ranges; if adverse spillover exceeds expectations; or if fairness auditing shows systematic exclusion. Review live queue daily, calibration weekly, and intervention-effect/fairness policy monthly, with an emergency stop if severe adverse spillover or repeated harm is detected.
