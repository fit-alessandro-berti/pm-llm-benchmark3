### 1. Current-process diagnosis

The current warranty process exhibits a classic **efficiency-quality trap** driven by a non-segmented "fast path" that optimizes local cycle time and direct labor cost while externalizing costs to downstream rework, compliance breaches, and customer dissatisfaction. The contradiction is stark: the most frequent path appears highly efficient in the Frequency DFG but is economically and operationally fragile.

**Key Problems & Artifacts:**

1.  **Fast-Path Cost Illusion:** The Frequency DFG shows `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` handles ~46% of volume (2,300/5,000 cases). While `Fast approval` cases are 31% cheaper pre-rework, the **Rework statistics** reveal they are only 9% cheaper post-rework due to high complaint rates (14.1% vs. 3.3% for the repair path). The **Performance DFG** confirms this drag: `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` has a mean of 72 hours, creating long-tail cycle time distortion that masks the true cost of the fast path.
2.  **Compliance & Risk Violations:** **Conformance findings** indicate log fitness of 0.91 but only 68% fitting traces. Alignment diagnostics show frequent deviations against **Normative Rule R1** (High-value >€400 requires Manual/Fraud review) and **R2** (Replacement requires evidence completeness). The **Frequency DFG** shows `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` (2,550 cases) likely bypasses risk review for high-value devices, violating R1. This exposes the organization to high compliance risk and financial loss, particularly for `Standard high-value device` claims where the cost of wrong replacement is EUR 410.
3.  **Customer Experience Segmentation Failure:** **Customer segment outcomes** show `Premium high-value device` complaints at 22% on the fast path versus 5% on the reviewed path. The fast path fails Premium expectations by dispatching incorrect replacements or skipping necessary risk checks. Conversely, `Unclear defect / missing evidence` cases suffer a median cycle time of 140 hours and high abandonment (320 abandonments from `Request missing evidence`), indicating the evidence loop is broken.
4.  **Resource Bottlenecks & Queue Dynamics:** **Resource information** highlights that `Manual eligibility review` has 7 reviewers with queue growth sharply above 82% utilization. The current manual inflow (1,400 cases) is stable, but the **Performance DFG** shows `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` takes 34 hours mean, suggesting the manual queue is already impacted by evidence loops. `Fraud/risk review` (2 specialists) is a bottleneck, and `Escalation board` is unscalable (one daily slot).
5.  **Evidence Loop Inefficiency:** **Rework statistics** show 26% of cases entering `Request missing evidence` loop at least twice. The **Performance DFG** edge `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` (mean 34h) is a major cycle-time contributor, driving abandonment and delaying resolution for the 22% of cases with unclear defects.

**Synthesis:** The process prioritizes throughput over outcome quality. The fast path saves ~EUR 30 per case in direct costs but risks EUR 410 in wrong replacements for high-value devices and incurs heavy service credits/complaint handling. The slow path is expensive but yields superior quality (3.3% complaints) and compliance. The redesign must segment risk, enforce compliance gates without exploding manual queues, and fix the evidence abandonment loop.

### 2. Redesign A: Risk-Segmented Routing with Targeted Pre-Dispatch Gates

**Redesigned Process Flow:**
`Intake`  `Smart Segment/Risk Router` 
*   **Path P1 (Premium Low-Risk):** `Auto Screen`  `Fast Approval`  `Dispatch`  `Close`. (Retains R2 exception).
*   **Path P2 (Standard Low-Risk):** `Auto Screen`  `Fast Approval`  `Pre-Dispatch Quality Gate`  `Dispatch`  `Close`.
*   **Path P3 (High-Value Devices):** `Auto Screen`  `Fraud/Risk Review` (if risk flags) OR `Manual Eligibility`  `Decision`  `Dispatch/Repair`.
*   **Path P4 (Unclear Evidence):** `Evidence Portal`  `Auto Completion Check`  `Router`.

**Targets:**
*   Enforces **R1** and **R2** by segmenting High-Value claims away from the blind `Fast approval` path.
*   Targets `Dispatch replacement -> Customer complaint/reopen` (260 cases) by catching wrong part selection via the pre-dispatch gate.
*   Leverages the **Auto-screen update** intervention to implement segment-aware rules.

**Mechanism of Improvement:**
*   **Compliance & Quality:** The router forces High-Value claims into Manual/Fraud review, eliminating R1/R2 violations. The **Pre-Dispatch Gate** (lightweight quality check) intercepts wrong replacement selections before shipping. Given the **Performance DFG** shows `Dispatch -> Complaint` mean is 96 hours, catching errors at dispatch saves the long-tail complaint cycle and the EUR 410/95 replacement error costs.
*   **Cost:** Reduces net cost by eliminating high-value wrong replacements and reducing complaint volume. The gate adds 6-18 hours but avoids the 72-hour complaint loop.
*   **Speed:** Low-risk paths remain fast. High-value speed is sacrificed for compliance, which is acceptable given the high risk.

**Sacrifices / Unresolved:**
*   **Manual Queue Pressure:** Updating auto-screen rules may increase manual inflow by 15-35% (**Resource information**). Current manual inflow is 1,400 cases; a 35% increase pushes inflow to ~1,890 cases. With 7 reviewers and sharp queue growth >82% utilization, this risks destabilizing the manual review queue. This redesign deliberately accepts this risk to gain compliance, requiring close monitoring of reviewer utilization.
*   **Cycle Time for High-Value:** High-value median cycle time increases from 20h (fast path) to ~96h (reviewed path), which may pressure `Premium high-value` CX, though complaints drop from 22% to 5%.

### 3. Redesign B: Standard High-Value Repair-First with Parallel Evidence

**Redesigned Process Flow:**
`Intake`  `Auto Screen` 
*   **Standard High-Value:** `Repair Quote`  `Customer Approval`  `Repair Device`  `Quality Check`  `Close`.
*   **Premium High-Value:** `Manual Eligibility`  `Dispatch` (with strict evidence check).
*   **Low-Risk:** `Fast Approval`  `Dispatch`.

**Targets:**
*   Shifts `Standard high-value device` (21% share, 1,050 cases) from the risky replacement path to the high-quality repair path.
*   Exploits the **Rework statistics** showing the repair path has only 3.3% complaints vs. 14.1% for fast approval.
*   Targets the high cost of wrong replacement (EUR 410) for standard customers who are less sensitive to replacement vs. repair than Premium customers.

**Mechanism of Improvement:**
*   **Quality & Cost:** By routing Standard High-Value to `Repair Quote -> Repair`, the process leverages the `Repair device -> Quality check` loop which ensures part correctness and functional validation. This eliminates the EUR 410 wrong-replacement risk and drastically reduces complaints. The **Performance DFG** shows `Repair device -> Quality check` is efficient (mean 9h), and `Quality check -> Rework` is only 45 cases vs. 260 dispatch complaints.
*   **Compliance:** Repair path inherently satisfies evidence requirements via `Repair Quote` and `Customer Approval`, reducing R2 violations.
*   **Customer Experience:** Standard customers tolerate longer cycle times if visibility is good (**Segment outcomes**). The repair path provides status updates during quote and repair, improving perceived service.

**Sacrifices / Unresolved:**
*   **Repair Capacity Bottleneck:** **Resource information** shows only 3 repair technicians. Current repair volume is ~390 cases (`Customer approval -> Repair device`). Shifting 1,050 Standard High-Value cases to repair could triple repair load, creating a severe bottleneck. This redesign risks displacing the bottleneck to repair technicians.
*   **Cycle Time:** Median cycle time for Standard High-Value increases from 28h to 120h. While Standard customers tolerate this, it requires robust communication.
*   **Premium High-Value:** This redesign keeps Premium High-Value on dispatch but forces Manual review. It does not solve the Premium speed expectation vs. compliance tension fully; Premium High-Value may still face 96h cycle times.

### 4. Redesign C: Evidence-First Asynchronous Loop with Abandonment Recovery

**Redesigned Process Flow:**
`Intake`  `Evidence Completeness Check` 
*   **Incomplete:** `Evidence Portal`  `Customer Upload`  `Auto Validation`  `Auto Screen`  `Fast/Manual`.
*   **Abandonment Risk:** `Recovery Bot` (SMS/Email nudge with direct upload link)  `Portal`.
*   **Complete:** `Auto Screen`  `Fast Approval`  `Dispatch`.

**Targets:**
*   Addresses the `Unclear defect / missing evidence` segment (22% share, 1,100 cases) which has 140h median cycle time and high abandonment.
*   Targets the `Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` loop (mean 34h) and the 26% double-request rate.
*   Uses the **Evidence portal** intervention to reduce repeated requests by 30-45%.

**Mechanism of Improvement:**
*   **Cycle Time & Abandonment:** The portal allows customers to upload evidence asynchronously, breaking the synchronous `Request -> Manual` loop. Reducing double requests by 30-45% cuts the 34-hour mean delay significantly. The **Performance DFG** shows `Request missing evidence -> Claim abandoned` is 320 cases; the portal with recovery bots aims to convert these abandonments into completions, increasing revenue recovery and reducing re-intake costs.
*   **Resource Relief:** By resolving evidence issues via auto-validation in the portal, inflow to `Manual eligibility review` decreases, alleviating the 82% utilization risk. This is crucial if Redesign A is also considered.
*   **Quality:** Better evidence quality at intake improves `Auto eligibility screen` accuracy, potentially reducing false fast approvals.

**Sacrifices / Unresolved:**
*   **Digital Literacy Risk:** The **Resource information** notes the portal "may increase abandonment for customers with low digital literacy." This redesign risks worsening CX for a subset of customers who struggle with the portal.
*   **Compliance:** This redesign focuses on evidence and does not directly enforce R1/R2 for High-Value devices. It must be combined with routing changes (like Redesign A) to address compliance. Standing alone, it leaves High-Value compliance risks open.
*   **Implementation Complexity:** Deploying a portal and recovery bot is higher complexity than rule updates.

### 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

*   **Failure Mode 1:** *Manual Queue Explosion.* If auto-screen update increases manual inflow by 35%, utilization may breach 82%, causing queue times to grow sharply (**Resource information**). This could degrade cycle time for all manual cases and increase abandonment.
*   **Failure Mode 2:** *Pre-Dispatch Gate Bottleneck.* If the gate requires manual inspection for many cases, it may create a new bottleneck before dispatch, delaying low-risk claims and negating speed benefits.
*   **Bottleneck Migration:** From `Fraud/risk review` to `Manual eligibility review` as risk-flagged cases are routed there.

**Redesign B:**
*   **Failure Mode 1:** *Repair Capacity Breach.* Shifting Standard High-Value to repair triples load on 3 technicians. Queue times for repair could explode, delaying all repairs and causing customer complaints about repair delays.
*   **Failure Mode 2:** *Customer Rejection.* Standard customers may reject repair quotes if they prefer replacement, leading to `Customer approval -> Claim abandoned` increases.
*   **Bottleneck Migration:** From `Manual eligibility` to `Repair technicians` and `Repair quote` (part-price confirmation delay).

**Redesign C:**
*   **Failure Mode 1:** *Abandonment Spike.* Customers with low digital literacy may abandon claims upon encountering the portal, increasing abandonment rate beyond current 390 cases.
*   **Failure Mode 2:** *Portal Validation Errors.* Auto-validation may reject valid evidence or accept insufficient evidence, causing downstream rework or compliance issues.
*   **Bottleneck Migration:** From `Request missing evidence` loop to `Portal support` or `Abandonment recovery` workload.

---

### 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**Staged Experiment Plan:**

1.  **Pilot 1: Redesign A (Auto-Screen Update) on Standard High-Value Subset.**
    *   *Rationale:* Low complexity, addresses high compliance risk and wrong-replacement cost. Standard High-Value is less sensitive to speed than Premium.
    *   *Scope:* Route 10% of Standard High-Value claims through updated auto-screen to Manual/Fraud review.
    *   *Metrics:* Manual queue utilization, Complaint rate for High-Value, Wrong replacement cost, Cycle time for Standard High-Value.
    *   *Stop/Go Thresholds:* Go if Manual utilization < 80% and Complaint rate drops > 50%. Stop if Manual queue wait time exceeds 24h or utilization > 85%.
    *   *Learning:* Validates if manual capacity can absorb increased inflow and quantifies compliance/cost savings. Checks if improvements are real by monitoring end-to-end complaint rates, not just local cycle times.

2.  **Pilot 2: Redesign C (Evidence Portal) on Unclear Evidence Segment.**
    *   *Rationale:* High impact on abandonment and cycle time for 22% of cases. Independent of routing changes.
    *   *Scope:* Deploy portal for all `Unclear defect` claims. Implement recovery bot.
    *   *Metrics:* Abandonment rate, Double evidence request rate, Cycle time for evidence cases, Digital literacy abandonment proxy (e.g., portal drop-off).
    *   *Stop/Go Thresholds:* Go if Abandonment rate drops > 20% and Double requests drop > 30%. Stop if Portal abandonment exceeds 15% of evidence cases.
    *   *Learning:* Tests portal effectiveness and digital literacy risk. Monitors if reduced evidence loops relieve manual queue (synergy with A).

3.  **Pilot 3: Redesign B (Repair-First) on Standard High-Value (Post-A Validation).**
    *   *Rationale:* Only proceed if Redesign A confirms manual capacity is sufficient or if Redesign C reduces manual load. Repair-First is high risk on capacity.
    *   *Scope:* Route Standard High-Value to Repair Quote. Monitor repair queue.
    *   *Metrics:* Repair queue length, Repair cycle time, Customer approval rate, Complaint rate.
    *   *Stop/Go Thresholds:* Go if Repair queue wait < 48h and Complaint rate < 5%. Stop if Repair queue exceeds capacity or Approval rate drops < 80%.
    *   *Learning:* Validates repair capacity and customer acceptance. Ensures quality gains do not come at cost of repair delays.

**Sequencing Logic:**
*   Start with A to fix compliance and test manual capacity elasticity.
*   Run C in parallel or immediately after to address evidence bottlenecks and potentially reduce manual load, creating headroom for A.
*   Consider B only if A and C succeed and repair capacity can be scaled or if Standard High-Value volume is manageable. B is the riskiest due to capacity constraints.

**Detecting Shifted Improvements:**
*   Monitor **end-to-end cycle time** and **complaint rate** for each segment, not just local edge times.
*   Track **downstream rework** (e.g., if A reduces fast approvals, does manual review introduce errors? Manual path has lower complaints, so likely safe).
*   Check **abandonment rates** across the process to ensure interventions are not pushing customers out.
*   Use **process mining** on pilot logs to detect new deviations or bottlenecks (e.g., new queue formation at repair or portal).
