# 1. Fairness dimensions

Procedural fairness is not equal approval rates. It is whether applicants with the same legitimate needs receive the same process, and applicants with different legitimate needs receive appropriate support without unjustified extra burden, delay, or risk of error.

**Outcome parity — raw, descriptive only.** Raw approval is 78% for Group A versus 63% B, 69% C, 58% D. Median cycle time is 8.1 days A versus 13.7 B, 15.9 C, 12.1 D. These gaps cannot by themselves establish discrimination, because eligibility, risk, documentation, and needs differ. They are a starting signal, not a conclusion.

**Adjusted outcome disparity.** After the preliminary adjustment for risk_band, documentation, channel, household size, prior cases, region and month, residual approval gaps remain: B -5.5 percentage points, C -2.2, D -3.1 versus A. Residual cycle-time gaps also remain: B +2.6 days, C +4.1, D +0.9. This is more informative than raw parity, but still not proof of discrimination: the model may omit true eligibility factors, and may over-adjust for biased covariates such as risk_score_band or documentation_completeness.

**Procedural burden.** How many steps, handovers, repeat requests and reviews a case must endure. Group B and C are far less likely to follow the fast V1 path (42% A vs 19% B, 22% C) and more likely to follow longer V2/V4/V5 paths. Adjusted probability of Supervisor Review is +8.0 pp for B and +5.5 pp for C, and of Request Missing Documents +9.5 pp for B and +4.2 pp for C. Burden is a fairness issue even if the final decision is correct.

**Timeliness, including the tail.** Median gaps understate tail risk. At the 90th percentile, gaps versus A are +12.8 days B, +17.4 C, +8.1 D, compared with median gaps of +5.6, +7.8 and +4.0. Bottleneck queues tend to harm the slowest cases most.

**Consistency of routing.** Whether the same documented need triggers the same next activity across groups. Higher risk requiring Fraud Review, disability accommodation requiring Accommodation Review, and language need requiring Language Support Contact are legitimate routing rules. Inconsistent addition of Supervisor Review or repeated documentation requests beyond those rules is not.

**Quality and accessibility of support.** Language Support Contact and Accommodation Review are rights-preserving. Fairness requires not just their existence, but timely, integrated, competent access. If using a right predictably adds 3.8-4.7 days of waiting and fragmented handovers, the right carries a procedural penalty.

**Appeal and rework burden.** Appeal rates are 6% A, 13% B, 10% C, 16% D; reopen/rework rates are 11%, 22%, 24%, 20%. Adjusted appeal gaps are +4.4 pp B, +1.1 C, +5.7 D. Higher appeals may reflect applicant propensity, but may also signal poorer initial explanation, inaccessible notification, or lower decision quality.

**Resource-allocation fairness.** Resources should be allocated in proportion to need and complexity, not concentrated so that minority-language or accommodation cases depend on a handful of overloaded staff. Concentration creates queueing, key-person risk, and unequal service even under formally equal rules.

# 2. Adjusted comparison strategy

The strategy must distinguish four concepts:

- **Disparity:** an observed difference in outcome, path, wait or resource.
- **Legitimate routing:** a difference explained by a policy-required step tied to a pre-existing, valid need.
- **Discrimination / differential treatment:** a difference for otherwise comparable cases that is not explained by legitimate need or valid policy, and that persists after auditing biased measures.
- **Uncertainty:** differences that could be explained by unmeasured eligibility, measurement error, or model choice.

### Estimands and adjustment sets

Estimate several quantities, not one model:

1. **Total disparity:** raw differences by group.
2. **Disparity adjusted for clearly pre-treatment factors:** region, month, household_size_band, channel, language_preference, disability_accommodation_requested, prior_case_count with caveat.
3. **Disparity additionally adjusted for risk_score_band and documentation_completeness:** interpret cautiously, because both may be biased mediators rather than neutral confounders.
4. **Path-specific disparities:** differences conditional on entering a step.

Do not condition on mediators such as Supervisor Review, Request Missing Documents, or waiting time when estimating total disparity; that would adjust away the very burden under investigation. Estimate mediation separately.

### Matching and stratification

Use exact or coarsened matching / stratification within cells defined by region x month x channel x risk_band x documentation x household band x prior-case band, and separately within need strata: language support needed yes/no, accommodation requested yes/no, high risk yes/no. Check overlap and discard or flag cells with no comparable A cases. Report both matched differences and regression differences, with cluster-robust uncertainty by caseworker and region. Matching on language_preference or accommodation_requested does not explain away group differences, but shows whether, among people with the same recorded need, groups are routed and timed differently.

### Path and variant comparison

Compare variant distributions overall and within case-mix strata. More importantly, compare transition probabilities step-by-step: given Completeness Check, probability of Request Missing Documents; given Risk Screening, probability of Fraud Review; given a support step, probability and time to Eligibility Assessment. A group difference in overall variant share may be legitimate composition; a difference in transition probability given the same prior state and need is more concerning.

### Resource-assignment comparison

For each activity, compare resource pools by group conditional on region, activity type and workload: number of distinct resources, concentration, experience proxies, proportion assigned to bilingual or accommodation specialists, handover count, and whether the same resource retains ownership. Use handover and working-together networks to quantify general -> specialist -> general ping-pong versus single-owner handling.

### Waiting-time decomposition

Decompose total cycle time into service time and waiting time between consecutive events, using service/waiting insertion. For each transition, separate: (a) composition effect — groups enter slower steps more often; and (b) rate effect — groups wait longer at the same step. An Oaxaca-Blinder / Kitagawa decomposition of the adjusted cycle-time gap into these two parts identifies whether delay comes from legitimate extra steps, slow queues at those steps, or both.

### Sensitivity analysis for biased covariates

Run sequential models: unadjusted; +region/month/household; +channel/prior cases; +documentation; +risk. If a gap disappears only after adding risk or documentation, that is not exculpatory, because those variables may encode language barriers or historical bias. Add Rosenbaum-style bounds / E-values for unmeasured confounding, alternative definitions of documentation completeness, and models excluding high-risk cases. Also test whether risk_score_band predicts Fraud Review and denial equally across groups, and whether documentation incompleteness predicts rework equally.

### Subgroup checks

Estimate group x region and group x channel interactions. The scenario flags two regions with supervisor backlog and more handovers for B and C. Pooling over regions can hide Simpson's paradox. Suppress small cells, adjust for multiple comparisons, and inspect intersectional subgroups, e.g. B in-person versus B online, C with dual-role supervisor-specialists.

# 3. Path and resource disparities

**Most important disparities:**

1. **Fast-path access.** V1 (Submit -> Verify -> Completeness -> Eligibility -> Decision -> Notify -> Activate, median 5.8 days) covers 42% of A but only 19% B, 22% C, 18% D. V2, which adds Request Missing Documents, covers 24% A vs 34% B, 29% C, 26% D, median 14.9 days. Part of this reflects genuinely higher incomplete documentation: 46% B, 36% C, 33% D vs 21% A. But adjusted Request Docs probability remains +9.5 pp for B, suggesting either unmeasured documentation difficulty caused by language barriers, or differential strictness at Completeness Check.

2. **Rights-preserving support steps as bottlenecks.** V4 Language Support path is 24% of B vs 1% A, median 19.2 days. V5 Accommodation + Supervisor path is 31% of C vs 2% A, median 21.6 days. The direct waits are large: Language Support -> Eligibility 3.8 days, Accommodation Review -> Eligibility 4.7 days, versus Decision -> Notification 0.5-0.8 days. These steps are legitimate in purpose, but their timeliness is concerning because service-level targets do not include them. Note also that recorded need (57% B need language support, 62% C request accommodation) exceeds the top-variant shares (24% and 31%), so support delivery, recording, or exact-variant capture needs audit rather than assuming all needs are met in those variants.

3. **Supervisor Review escalation.** V3 Fraud/Supervisor is 28% of D vs 9% of A, consistent with D's 51% high risk and 49% high prior cases. Adjusted supervisor probability for D is only +2.0 pp, so D routing is largely explainable by measured risk. For B (+8.0 pp) and C (+5.5 pp), adjusted escalation is larger and is not directly mandated by the stated rules, except that C's accommodation protocol may link to supervisor check. B's escalation warrants investigation for defensive review under language/documentation uncertainty or regional backlog.

4. **Timeliness at specific transitions.** Submit -> Identity Verification: 1.4 days B vs 0.8 A. Completeness -> Request Docs: 1.6 B vs 0.4 A, a fourfold difference. Supervisor -> Decision: 1.9 B, 1.6 C vs 1.0 A. Risk -> Fraud is similar across groups (2.0-2.8), suggesting risk routing is not the main source of unequal delay. The burden is concentrated in support, documentation and supervisor queues.

5. **Resource concentration and handovers.** Group A is spread across 52 caseworkers. Group B depends disproportionately on 7 bilingual caseworkers and 3 overloaded regional officers; Group C on 5 accommodation specialists, two of whom also do supervisor review. In two regions B shows general -> bilingual -> general handovers and C shows general -> accommodation specialist -> supervisor handovers. This creates queueing, loss of ownership, repeated explanation by applicants, and a conflict when the same specialist is also the supervisor reviewer. This is procedural unfairness in capacity design even if individual staff act in good faith.

6. **Appeal burden, especially D and B.** Adjusted appeal probability is highest for D (+5.7 pp) and B (+4.4 pp). For D, whose routing and cycle time are mostly explained by risk, the appeal gap points more to decision explanation, fraud-review communication, or initial decision quality than to volume of routing. For B, appeals may reflect both documentation/language issues and inaccessible decisions.

**Summary judgment by group:** D's extra steps are most plausibly largely justified by measured risk, but its appeal gap is concerning. C's burden is primarily timeliness and handover burden from accommodation processing, with a small adjusted approval gap. B shows the strongest combined concern: largest adjusted approval gap, large adjusted documentation/supervisor escalation, long support waits, and high appeals.

# 4. Plausible mechanisms

**Legitimate mechanisms**

1. **Policy-mandated complexity routing.** High risk -> Fraud Review, accommodation request -> Accommodation Review, language need -> Language Support Contact. This legitimately pushes D toward V3, C toward V5, B toward V4, increasing steps and time.

2. **Genuine case complexity.** Higher incomplete documentation, in-person channel, prior cases and household complexity genuinely require more checks and rework, especially for B and D.

3. **Specialisation economies.** A small pool of bilingual or accommodation specialists may improve quality and consistency, at the cost of queueing if capacity is not scaled to demand.

**Concerning or mixed mechanisms**

4. **Under-resourced rights-preserving steps.** No timeliness threshold for language/accommodation steps, only 7 bilingual and 5 accommodation staff, and generalist capacity not backfilled, produce predictable queues. The right exists formally but is rationed by waiting time.

5. **Language-documentation loop.** Language barriers cause incomplete documentation; incompleteness is then recorded as an applicant deficiency, triggering Request Missing Documents, Supervisor Review, delays and appeals. Adjusting for documentation_completeness would then hide a mechanism of disadvantage.

6. **Defensive or discretionary escalation.** Caseworkers uncertain about translated documents or accommodation evidence may add Supervisor or Fraud Review beyond policy, differentially for B and C. This would appear as adjusted escalation without a corresponding policy rule.

7. **Fragmented ownership and dual-role conflict.** General -> specialist -> general/supervisor handovers lose context, require applicants to re-explain needs, and create queues when specialists also supervise. Regional backlog amplifies this for B and C concentrated in those regions.

8. **Historical bias in risk and prior-case measures.** If risk_score_band or prior_case_count reflects past unequal enforcement or access, legitimate-seeming Fraud Review routing for D may compound earlier disadvantage. Similarly, decision notices in majority language or inaccessible formats may generate appeals, especially for B and D.

Each mechanism has testable implications: e.g. mechanism 5 predicts documentation gaps shrink after Language Support Contact; mechanism 6 predicts supervisor escalation varies by caseworker; mechanism 4 predicts waits rise with specialist queue length and fall when capacity increases.

# 5. Limitations of available attributes

**Measurement bias.** risk_score_band may encode historical disparities and is not a neutral measure of current eligibility. documentation_completeness is measured after language and channel barriers have operated, so it is partly an outcome of the process, not only an input. applicant_group is a coarse proxy for language, disability, ethnicity, migration status and their intersections.

**Missing applicant needs.** The log does not capture interpreter quality, preferred notification format, digital access, literacy, health urgency, caring responsibilities, complexity of accommodation requested, or whether support was actually received as requested. Two cases with the same flags may have very different needs.

**Unobserved resource workload.** Workload is observed only at daily granularity. Queue length at assignment, case complexity per resource, leave, experience, language proficiency and informal mentoring are not in org:resource. Comparing raw resource assignment without workload will mistake capacity scarcity for individual bias, or vice versa.

**Appeal quality.** appeal_flag records presence, not merit, success, timeliness, or reason. case_outcome records approval, not correctness, explanation quality, dignity, or timeliness of notification. A process can have equal approvals and unequal procedural quality.

**Historical and selection bias.** Prior_case_count may reflect prior denials or difficulty accessing the system rather than need. Non-applicants, abandoned applications and cases with missing events are invisible, so group differences among applicants may be selected. Region and month adjustment cannot fix unmeasured eligibility differences.

Together these limitations mean adjusted gaps are suggestive, not dispositive, and adjusting for risk/documentation can either clarify or conceal unfairness depending on their causal status.

# 6. Mitigation options and side effects

| Option | Fairness gain | Side effects / trade-offs and guardrails |
|---|---|---|
| **Adding capacity for language support and accommodation review** | Reduces the penalty attached to exercising a right; lowers 3.8-4.7 day waits and tail delays. | Hiring lag; training and quality risk if scaled too fast; cost. Guardrail: fund backfill for generalists, measure quality and applicant-reported accessibility, not only speed. Do not remove support steps to save time. |
| **Service-level targets for support steps** | Makes currently invisible delays managed; creates accountability for Language Support -> Eligibility and Accommodation -> Eligibility. | May incentivise rushed, low-quality support or gaming of start/stop timestamps; may shift queue to the next step. Guardrail: pair timeliness targets with quality audits and applicant experience measures, monitor the full tail not just the median. |
| **Routing redesign to reduce handovers** | Single accountable owner, warm handoffs, parallel rather than sequential completeness + language support, embedding bilingual/accommodation input early. Reduces general -> specialist -> general ping-pong. | Parallel review can increase coordination cost and error risk; single-owner models may overload specialists. Guardrail: pilot in the two problem regions, track handover count, rework and decision quality. |
| **Decision-explanation improvements** | Translated, plain-language, accessible-format notices; clear reasons for Request Docs, Fraud Review and denial; named contact. May reduce appeals/rework for B and D and improve dignity. | Initial appeals may rise as applicants better understand how to appeal; translation errors if unreviewed. Guardrail: professional translation review, user testing with B and C applicants, monitor appeal success not just appeal volume. |
| **Monitoring adjusted disparities** | Routine dashboard of adjusted approval, cycle time, transitions, waits, handovers and appeals by group, region and channel, with uncertainty intervals. Detects drift early. | Risk of gaming, quota thinking, or over-interpreting small cells; may pressure staff to equalise approvals superficially. Guardrail: monitor procedural metrics alongside outcomes, audit samples, avoid automatic sanctions based on one metric. |
| **Auditing risk_score_band and documentation_completeness** | Tests whether risk predicts fraud/eligibility equally across groups, whether documentation scoring is applied consistently after language support, and whether prior-case count is a valid need measure. | Audits may reveal the score has predictive value with disparate impact, forcing a trade-off between fraud detection and equity; recalibration may change routing and require revalidation. Guardrail: independent audit, publish validation results, consider second review for high-impact fraud routing rather than acting on score alone. |

# 7. PM4Py analysis plan

The code below is pseudocode; PM4Py signatures vary by version, so adapt argument names. The logic, not exact API version, is the point.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

raw = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv", parse_dates=["time:timestamp"])
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

groups = ["Group A", "Group B", "Group C", "Group D"]
logs_by_group = {}
for g in groups:
    logs_by_group[g] = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        log, "applicant_group", [g]
    )

# Variant analysis overall and within case-mix strata
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
for g in groups:
    gv = pm4py.get_variants(logs_by_group[g])
    print(g, gv)

# Example: compare only language-support-needed cases across groups
need_lang = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log, "language_preference", ["minority-language"]
)
print(pm4py.get_variants(need_lang))

# Case and path performance filtering for sensitivity analyses
fast_cases = pm4py.filter_case_performance(
    logs_by_group["Group B"], min_perf=0, max_perf=730
)
print(pm4py.get_all_case_durations(fast_cases))
print(pm4py.get_all_case_durations(logs_by_group["Group B"]))

# Directly-follows and performance DFGs per group, normalised by case count
for g in groups:
    dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(logs_by_group[g])
    perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(
        logs_by_group[g], timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )
    print(g, dfg)
    print(g, perf_dfg)

# Compare specific transitions, e.g. Completeness Check -> Request Missing Documents
# by inspecting edge counts and median performance times in each perf_dfg,
# and by filtering to cases that reach Completeness Check.

# Service and waiting time decomposition
log_sw = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)
for activity in ["Eligibility Assessment", "Decision Draft", "Supervisor Review"]:
    print(activity, pm4py.get_service_time(log_sw, activity))

for g in groups:
    sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log_sw, "applicant_group", [g])
    print(g, pm4py.get_all_case_durations(sub))

# Path performance for longer vs fast variants
long_variant = pm4py.filter_variants(log, variants=[variant for variant, count in variants.items() if count > 0])
print(pm4py.filter_paths_performance(long_variant))

# Handover, working-together and resource similarity networks
handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
abss = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log, abss=abss)
print(handover)
print(working_together)
print(roles)

# Repeat handover analysis per group and per region to locate
# general -> bilingual -> general and general -> specialist -> supervisor patterns
for region in sorted(raw["region"].unique()):
    sub = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(log, "region", [region])
    print(region, pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(sub))

# Declarative / skeleton checks for policy conformance
skeleton = pm4py.discover_log_skeleton(log)
declare = pm4py.discover_declare(log)
print(skeleton)
print(declare)

# Targeted constraints to test per group:
# Language Support Contact eventually followed by Eligibility Assessment
# for minority-language cases; Accommodation Review before Eligibility
# for accommodation-requested cases; Fraud Review only after Risk Screening
# for high risk_band cases. Compare constraint violation rates by group.
```

Interpretation guide: a fair process should show similar DFG transition probabilities and similar performance times for the same transition after stratifying by need; handover networks should not show systematic specialist ping-pong for one group; skeleton/declare checks should show support steps reliably precede Eligibility Assessment when needed, without extra Supervisor Review loops. Deviations concentrated in specific regions, resources, or transitions localise the mechanism.

# 8. Bottom-line assessment

The evidence does not establish intentional discrimination, and raw approval differences alone do not prove it. Nor can disparities be dismissed as legitimate solely because case mix differs.

What the evidence does support, with moderate confidence, is a procedural-fairness concern for Groups B and C: after adjusting for measured case mix, they still experience longer cycle times, more documentation requests and supervisor escalation, reliance on scarce specialist capacity, more handovers, and longer waits at rights-preserving support steps. Much of the extra routing is legitimate in purpose — language and accommodation support are required — but its current resourcing and sequencing impose a disproportionate timeliness and burden penalty, especially in the tail and in two regions.

For Group D, routing volume is largely consistent with higher measured risk and prior cases, but the large adjusted appeal gap and the unvalidated status of risk_score_band leave open whether fraud routing and decisions are applied and explained fairly.

The appropriate conclusion is therefore **indeterminate on discrimination, but suggestive of procedural unfairness in burden, timeliness and resource allocation**, particularly for language and accommodation support. Closing the gap requires auditing risk and documentation measures, measuring support quality and workload, and resourcing support steps so that exercising a right does not predictably make the process slower and more burdensome.
