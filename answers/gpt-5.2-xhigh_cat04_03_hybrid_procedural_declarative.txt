# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material assumptions (explicit)

- **Case start/end**: `Receive Onboarding Request` is the first event when the case is complete; `Close Onboarding` marks the end of an onboarding case. Traces without `Close Onboarding` are treated as **incomplete** (warn, not auto-fail).
- **Accepted vs rejected cases**: an **accepted** case is identified by the occurrence of `Activate Vendor`; a **rejected** case is identified by `Reject Vendor`. (R1 demands they never co-occur.)
- **Route identification**: “low-risk route” is inferred by the presence of `Manager Approval`; “high-risk route” is inferred by `Enhanced Due Diligence` and `Compliance Approval`. (If those are missing, route classification becomes data-quality dependent and is handled in the external validation layer.)
- **Clarification cycles**: a clarification cycle is the pair `Request Clarification` → `Receive Clarification`. Several cycles are allowed. A response may be executed by an external actor (e.g., resource “Vendor”); we treat that as a normal resource value.
- **Placement of clarification in the procedural backbone**: for a block-structured procedural model, clarification cycles are modeled **before** `Classify Risk`. This is a **conservative approximation**; if the real process allows clarifications after classification, that behavior is delegated to DECLARE + external checks (and would require a richer procedural model than a compact process tree).
- **Sanctions escalation**: `Sanctions Escalation` is interpreted as an exception that can occur after screening results are known (and is therefore modeled after `Classify Risk` in the high-risk branch). `Obtain Legal Opinion` is treated as part of the escalation handling and is assumed to occur at most once.
- **Bank-detail changes**: `Change Bank Details` is modeled as a post-activation safeguard loop that happens **between** `Activate Vendor` and `Release First Payment`. Post-payment maintenance is out-of-scope for the onboarding backbone and should be modeled as a separate “Vendor Maintenance” process if needed.
- **Event attributes available**: the event log is assumed to provide at least `concept:name` and `time:timestamp`; resource checks require `org:resource`. Emergency authorization in R6 requires an **external** case attribute/registry because the event log lacks it.

## R5/R6 conflict: at least two coherent resolutions

- **Resolution A — Legal-first (strict)**:
  - Interpret R5 as authoritative: once `Sanctions Escalation` happens, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must occur and must **precede** `Activate Vendor` in all accepted cases.
  - Any activation before the opinion is a **hard violation** (fail).
  - **Compliance risk**: low (least chance of activating a sanctioned vendor), but may block legitimate emergency onboarding.

- **Resolution B — Emergency carve-out (ops)**:
  - Accept R6 as a permitted exception: after `Sanctions Escalation`, `Activate Vendor` may occur before `Obtain Legal Opinion` **only if** a joint emergency authorization exists **outside** the event log, and the opinion arrives within **24 hours** after the emergency activation.
  - Because the event log has no emergency flag/authorization, cases that rely on this exception are **policy-dependent** unless an external authorization record is joined.
  - **Compliance risk**: medium/high if the external authorization source is missing, weakly governed, or not tamper-evident; the exception can be abused to bypass legal review.

**Chosen for the implemented hybrid model**: **Resolution A (Legal-first strict)** for the procedural backbone and the authoritative validation logic. Resolution B is documented as a supported alternative that requires additional external data.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Procedural (authoritative) + DECLARE + Log Skeleton | Procedural: XOR between reject vs accepted branches; DECLARE: `noncoexistence`(`Reject Vendor`,`Activate Vendor`); Log skeleton: `never_together` pair | Mutual exclusion is core control-flow and easy to double-check declaratively | Redundant enforcement; still valuable for noisy logs (e.g., T8) |
| R2 | Resource/data-aware validation layer (authoritative) + Procedural | External check: if `Activate Vendor` occurs, exactly one of (`Manager Approval`,`Compliance Approval`) occurs; if `Reject Vendor` occurs, neither approval occurs | Conditional “accepted vs rejected” logic is not cleanly expressible in activity-only DECLARE without attributes | Procedural backbone enforces the common case, but external check catches out-of-model mixes |
| R3 | DECLARE (authoritative for pairing) + external state check | DECLARE: `altresponse`(`Request Clarification`,`Receive Clarification`) for per-request pairing; external check ensures no final outcome while a request is outstanding | Alternate-response matches “no next request before response” well | Pure DECLARE cannot express “before final outcome” conditional without over-constraining; external check completes it |
| R4 | DECLARE (authoritative) + Log Skeleton | DECLARE: `response`(`Sanctions Escalation`,`Obtain Legal Opinion`); Log skeleton: `always_after`(`Sanctions Escalation`,`Obtain Legal Opinion`) | “If escalation then opinion eventually” is a canonical response constraint | Response is vacuously satisfied when escalation absent; does not ensure escalation happens when needed (needs data) |
| R5 | Resource/data-aware validation layer (authoritative) + Procedural (strict variant) | Strict mode: if `Sanctions Escalation` and `Activate Vendor` occur, require timestamp(`Obtain Legal Opinion`) < timestamp(`Activate Vendor`) | Conditional and conflict-laden; must be explicit and auditable | Cannot be “silently” combined with R6; needs explicit policy choice |
| R6 | Resource/data-aware validation layer (authoritative in ops variant) | Emergency mode: allow activation before opinion only if external `case:joint_emergency_authorization==True` AND opinion arrives within 24h of activation; otherwise policy-dependent/fail | Requires external data absent from the log; must live in a joined validation layer | With missing authorization attribute, conformance is inherently policy-dependent |
| R7 | DECLARE (authoritative) + Log Skeleton | DECLARE: `precedence`(`Run Sanctions Screening`,`Sanctions Escalation`); Log skeleton: `always_before`(`Sanctions Escalation`,`Run Sanctions Screening`) | “Escalation only after screening” is a precedence constraint | Log skeleton provides a cheap relational cross-check; still only order-based |
| R8 | DECLARE (soft) + Log Skeleton (soft) | DECLARE: `noncoexistence`(`Enhanced Due Diligence`,`Manager Approval`) and `noncoexistence`(`Manager Approval`,`Compliance Approval`) with lower confidence; Log skeleton: same pairs in `never_together` interpreted as warnings | “Should not co-occur” fits declarative “anti-coexistence”; easy to flag as soft | Declarative engines report deviations uniformly; we must post-process as warnings based on confidence/policy |
| R9 | Resource-aware validation layer (authoritative) | Four-eyes checks using `org:resource`: `Verify Bank Account` resource ≠ applicable approval resource; `Capture Master Data` resource ≠ `Activate Vendor` resource | Requires event attributes; activity-only formalisms cannot express SoD | Missing `org:resource` makes the check not evaluable (warning/data gap) |
| R10 | Log Skeleton (authoritative) + Procedural | Log skeleton `activ_freq`: `Activate Vendor`,`Reject Vendor`,`Release First Payment` ∈ {0,1}; Procedural backbone also implies at-most-once | Log skeleton frequency constraints are compact and executable | Frequency caps for looped activities must be approximated with finite sets (documented) |

| R12 | Temporal profile (authoritative for timing warnings) | `temporal_profile` dict with given mean/std; conformance via `zeta=2.0` producing warnings | Time expectations are best treated as performance norms, not control-flow | Timestamp noise, batching, weekends, and missing events can create false warnings; must not hard-reject |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## 3.1 Compact formal description (process tree)

Notation: `->` sequence, `+` parallel, `X` exclusive choice, `*` loop, `τ` silent.

Implemented strict-backbone (Resolution A):

`->(`
`Receive Onboarding Request,`
`+(Capture Master Data, Run Sanctions Screening, Verify Bank Account),`
`X(τ, *(->(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification), τ)),`
`Classify Risk,`
`X(`
`  ->(Reject Vendor, Close Onboarding),`
`  ->(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor, X(τ, *(->(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor, Verify Bank Account), τ)), Release First Payment, Close Onboarding),`
`  ->(+(->(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval), X(τ, ->(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion))), Activate Vendor, X(τ, *(->(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor, Verify Bank Account), τ)), Release First Payment, Close Onboarding)`
`)`
`)`

Key consequence: if the sanctions-handling branch is taken (i.e., `Sanctions Escalation` occurs), then `Obtain Legal Opinion` must complete **before** `Activate Vendor` (strict R5 interpretation).

## 3.2 Self-contained PM4Py code (`procedural_model`)

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator

def leaf(label: str) -> ProcessTree:
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def tau() -> ProcessTree:
    return ProcessTree(label=None)

def _pt(operator: Operator, *children: ProcessTree) -> ProcessTree:
    n = ProcessTree(operator=operator, children=list(children))
    for c in n.children:
        c.parent = n
    return n

def seq(*children: ProcessTree) -> ProcessTree:
    return _pt(Operator.SEQUENCE, *children)

def xor(*children: ProcessTree) -> ProcessTree:
    return _pt(Operator.XOR, *children)

def par(*children: ProcessTree) -> ProcessTree:
    return _pt(Operator.PARALLEL, *children)

def loop(do: ProcessTree, redo: ProcessTree) -> ProcessTree:
    # PM4Py loop has exactly 2 children: do, redo (exit is implicit tau)
    return _pt(Operator.LOOP, do, redo)

def opt(subtree: ProcessTree) -> ProcessTree:
    return xor(tau(), subtree)

# Concurrent checks after receiving the request
checks = par(
    leaf("Capture Master Data"),
    leaf("Run Sanctions Screening"),
    leaf("Verify Bank Account"),
)

# Zero-or-more clarification cycles BEFORE risk classification (block-structured approximation)
clar_cycle = seq(leaf("Request Clarification"), leaf("Receive Clarification"))
clarifications = opt(loop(clar_cycle, tau()))

# Optional post-activation bank-change safeguard loop (between activation and first payment)
bank_cycle = seq(
    leaf("Change Bank Details"),
    leaf("Suspend Vendor"),
    leaf("Verify Bank Account"),
)
bank_safeguards = opt(loop(bank_cycle, tau()))

# Strict sanctions handling (Resolution A): if escalation occurs, legal opinion must complete before activation
sanctions_handling = opt(seq(leaf("Sanctions Escalation"), leaf("Obtain Legal Opinion")))

reject_branch = seq(leaf("Reject Vendor"), leaf("Close Onboarding"))

low_risk_accept = seq(
    leaf("Manager Approval"),
    leaf("Activate Vendor"),
    bank_safeguards,
    leaf("Release First Payment"),
    leaf("Close Onboarding"),
)

high_risk_accept = seq(
    par(
        seq(leaf("Enhanced Due Diligence"), leaf("Compliance Approval")),
        sanctions_handling,
    ),
    leaf("Activate Vendor"),
    bank_safeguards,
    leaf("Release First Payment"),
    leaf("Close Onboarding"),
)

outcome = xor(reject_branch, low_risk_accept, high_risk_accept)

procedural_model = seq(
    leaf("Receive Onboarding Request"),
    checks,
    clarifications,
    leaf("Classify Risk"),
    outcome,
)

# Optional: convert to accepting Petri net if needed
# from pm4py.objects.conversion.process_tree import converter as pt_converter
# net, initial_marking, final_marking = pt_converter.apply(procedural_model)
```

## 3.3 Explanation of modeling choices

- **Concurrency**: `Capture Master Data`, `Run Sanctions Screening`, and `Verify Bank Account` are in `PARALLEL`, matching the “may proceed concurrently” statement.
- **Clarification loop**: modeled as optional `LOOP(Request Clarification; Receive Clarification)` **before** `Classify Risk`. This enforces that a request is not left unanswered before classification in the procedural layer (useful for T5), but it is an approximation if clarifications can occur after classification in reality.
- **Choices / primary outcomes**: XOR among (reject) vs (low-risk accept) vs (high-risk accept).
- **Terminal outcomes**: `Close Onboarding` ends both the rejection path and the accepted path after `Release First Payment`.
- **Sanctions escalation & legal opinion**: included only in the high-risk branch as an optional parallel component that must finish before `Activate Vendor` (strict R5). This makes the procedural backbone explicitly incompatible with an “activation before opinion” emergency path (R6), which is handled as an alternative policy in the external layer.
- **Post-activation behavior**: `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor` / (re-)`Verify Bank Account` are included as an optional loop between `Activate Vendor` and `Release First Payment`, capturing R11’s “suspend then re-verify before payment” intent for onboarding-time changes.

### Overlap/disagreement with other layers (and pair orientation)

- **Orientation (as implemented in PM4Py classic)**:
  - `always_after` expects a pair `(A,B)` to be present in the trace’s **after-relations** (some occurrence of `A` before some occurrence of `B`). It behaves like an inexpensive “A implies B occurs later” check, but it is **existential**, not per-occurrence.
  - `always_before` expects `(A,B)` in the trace’s **before-relations** (some occurrence of `B` before some occurrence of `A`). It behaves like “A requires some B earlier”.
- **Overlap**:
  - R1 is duplicated (`never_together`) to catch noisy logs even if the procedural backbone is correct.
  - Classification prerequisites appear in procedural, DECLARE precedence, and skeleton `always_before/always_after` for robustness.
- **Limitations**:
  - Clarification pairing in skeleton (`always_after(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)`) is weaker than DECLARE `altresponse`: it can miss per-request issues when multiple cycles exist.
  - Frequency sets for loopable activities (`Request/Receive Clarification`, `Verify Bank Account`, `Change Bank Details`, `Suspend Vendor`) are **finite approximations** (blind spot: traces with >4 clarification cycles would be flagged even if allowed).

```python
zeta = 2.0
temporal_profile = {
```

Temporal deviations are treated as **warnings** (not control-flow violations) because:

- they describe **performance expectations** (service levels, typical latency), not logical necessity;
- timestamps are susceptible to batching, time-zone issues, weekends/holidays, and missing events;
- enforcement as hard control-flow would create false negatives and encourage “gaming the clock”.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

## Required attributes and missing-data handling

- **Always required**: `concept:name` (activity label).
- **Ordering/time checks**: `time:timestamp`. If missing, return `not evaluated` (warning) for checks that depend on time.
- **Segregation-of-duties (R9)**: `org:resource`. If missing for either side of a four-eyes comparison, return `not evaluated` (warning) for that comparison.
- **Emergency authorization (R6)**: an external case attribute (e.g., `case:joint_emergency_authorization`) or join to an authorization register. If missing, the emergency carve-out verdict is `policy-dependent`.

## Executable-style validation logic (Python-like pseudocode)

```python
from datetime import timedelta

FINAL_OUTCOMES = {"Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"}

def _events(case_df, act):
    return case_df[case_df["concept:name"] == act].sort_values("time:timestamp")

def _first_ts(case_df, act):
    ev = _events(case_df, act)
    return None if ev.empty else ev.iloc[0]["time:timestamp"]

def _first_res(case_df, act):
    ev = _events(case_df, act)
    if ev.empty or "org:resource" not in ev.columns:
        return None
    return ev.iloc[0]["org:resource"]

def check_R1_terminal_mutex(case_df):
    a = not _events(case_df, "Activate Vendor").empty
    r = not _events(case_df, "Reject Vendor").empty
    return "fail" if (a and r) else "pass"

def check_R2_approval_exclusive_by_outcome(case_df):
    activated = not _events(case_df, "Activate Vendor").empty
    rejected = not _events(case_df, "Reject Vendor").empty
    man = not _events(case_df, "Manager Approval").empty
    comp = not _events(case_df, "Compliance Approval").empty

    if rejected:
        return "fail" if (man or comp) else "pass"
    if activated:
        return "fail" if (man == comp) else "pass"   # exactly one must be true
    return "warning"  # incomplete/indeterminate outcome

def check_R3_clarification_cycles(case_df):
    # Enforces: each Request Clarification is answered before next request and before any final outcome.
    pending = 0
    for _, e in case_df.sort_values("time:timestamp").iterrows():
        a = e["concept:name"]
        if a == "Request Clarification":
            if pending > 0:
                return "fail"  # request before previous response
            pending = 1
        elif a == "Receive Clarification":
            pending = max(0, pending - 1)
        elif a in FINAL_OUTCOMES:
            if pending > 0:
                return "fail"  # final outcome while clarification outstanding
    return "fail" if pending > 0 else "pass"

def check_R9_segregation_of_duties(case_df):
    # Verify Bank Account resource != applicable approval resource (if any)
    # Capture Master Data resource != Activate Vendor resource (if activation occurs)
    if "org:resource" not in case_df.columns:
        return "not evaluated"

    # Determine applicable approval for accepted cases
    activated = not _events(case_df, "Activate Vendor").empty
    man_res = _first_res(case_df, "Manager Approval")
    comp_res = _first_res(case_df, "Compliance Approval")
    appr_res = man_res if man_res is not None else comp_res

    # Compare approval with ALL resources that performed Verify Bank Account (conservative)
    if appr_res is not None:
        verify_res = set(_events(case_df, "Verify Bank Account")["org:resource"].dropna().tolist())
        if appr_res in verify_res:
            return "fail"

    if activated:
        cap_res = _first_res(case_df, "Capture Master Data")
        act_res = _first_res(case_df, "Activate Vendor")
        if cap_res is None or act_res is None:
            return "not evaluated"
        if cap_res == act_res:
            return "fail"

    return "pass"

def check_R5_R6_sanctions_activation(case_df, policy_mode, case_attrs=None):
    # policy_mode: "strict" (R5) or "emergency" (R6 carve-out)
    case_attrs = case_attrs or {}

    esc_t = _first_ts(case_df, "Sanctions Escalation")
    if esc_t is None:
        return "pass"

    op_t = _first_ts(case_df, "Obtain Legal Opinion")
    if op_t is None:
        return "fail"  # violates R4 regardless of R5/R6

    act_t = _first_ts(case_df, "Activate Vendor")
    if act_t is None:
        return "pass"  # rejected or incomplete; R5/R6 about activation

    if op_t <= act_t:
        return "pass"

    # activation before opinion
    if policy_mode == "strict":
        return "fail"

    # emergency carve-out requires external authorization + 24h deadline after activation
    auth = case_attrs.get("case:joint_emergency_authorization", None)
    if auth is None:
        return "policy-dependent"
    if auth is False:
        return "fail"
    return "pass" if (op_t - act_t) <= timedelta(hours=24) else "fail"

def check_R11_bank_change_safeguards(case_df):
    # For each Change Bank Details after activation (and before any Release First Payment),
    # require a later Suspend Vendor and a later Verify Bank Account before that payment.
    act_t = _first_ts(case_df, "Activate Vendor")
    if act_t is None:
        return "pass"  # R11 scoped to post-activation
    pay_t = _first_ts(case_df, "Release First Payment")

    changes = _events(case_df, "Change Bank Details")
    if changes.empty:
        return "pass"

    for _, ch in changes.iterrows():
        if ch["time:timestamp"] < act_t:
            return "fail"  # bank change before activation is out-of-scope/invalid in this policy

        # Suspension must follow the change
        susp = _events(case_df, "Suspend Vendor")
        susp_after = susp[susp["time:timestamp"] > ch["time:timestamp"]]
        if susp_after.empty:
            return "fail"

        # Re-verify must occur after change; if payment exists after the change, verify must be before payment
        ver = _events(case_df, "Verify Bank Account")
        ver_after = ver[ver["time:timestamp"] > ch["time:timestamp"]]
        if ver_after.empty:
            return "fail"
        if pay_t is not None and pay_t > ch["time:timestamp"]:
            if ver_after[ver_after["time:timestamp"] < pay_t].empty:
                return "fail"

    return "pass"
```

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

- **Duplicated enforcement across layers**:
  - R1 is enforced procedurally (XOR), declaratively (`noncoexistence`), and via skeleton (`never_together`), and can also be rechecked in the external layer. This redundancy is intentional to catch both modeling errors and log noise.
  - “Checks before classification” are enforced in procedural, DECLARE `precedence`, and skeleton `always_before/always_after`.

- **Constraints that become inconsistent under loops or post-activation changes**:
  - If clarifications can happen after `Classify Risk` in reality, the block-structured procedural placement (before classification) will **over-reject** such traces even if DECLARE would accept them.
  - R11 depends on whether there is a “later payment” after a bank change; a pure `response(Change Bank Details, Verify Bank Account)` can **over-constrain** cases where payment never occurs (or change occurs after payment).

- **Vacuity in declarative rules**:
  - `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` is vacuously satisfied when `Request Clarification` never occurs; it does not say whether clarification should have been requested.
  - `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is vacuous when escalation does not occur; it cannot detect “missed escalations” without data attributes indicating screening outcomes.

- **Missing case attributes / unobservable conditions**:
  - R6 requires emergency joint authorization that is explicitly absent from the log. Without an external record, conformance is **not decidable** from activities alone.
  - R2’s “accepted case” condition is only inferable from `Activate Vendor`; if logs are incomplete (no activation recorded) the rule becomes ambiguous.

- **Timestamp-only limitations**:
  - Temporal-profile warnings ignore calendars, business hours, and workload regimes; a “late” legal opinion could be acceptable if the clock includes weekends or waiting time outside process control.
  - If events are logged with coarse granularity, “order” constraints can be brittle under same-timestamp ties.

- **Support/confidence metadata is not normative truth**:
  - In this answer, `support`/`confidence` are policy annotations, not evidence. In real deployments, they must not be treated as “the law”; they should be traceable to governance decisions, versioned, and auditable.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

- **Canonical procedural representation**: use the process tree as the design artifact and compile it to an accepting Petri net for execution/conformance where needed (PM4Py `process_tree` → Petri net conversion).
- **Authoritative policy layer**: treat the **resource/data-aware validation layer** as authoritative for:
  - conditional rules (R2, R3 “before final outcome”, R11 payment-conditioned logic),
  - segregation of duties (R9),
  - and the sanctions-policy conflict (R5 vs R6), which requires explicit policy selection plus external authorization data.
- **Order of checks (recommended pipeline)**:
  - schema/time sanity (required attributes present, timestamps monotone) → resource/data checks (R9, R5/R6, R11) → procedural conformance → DECLARE conformance → log skeleton conformance → temporal profile warnings.
- **Surfacing contradictory findings**:
  - Present a single case report that separates **hard violations** (e.g., R1, R9, strict R5), **warnings** (R8 soft noncoexistence, R12 timing), and **policy-dependent** outcomes (R6 without external authorization).
  - When layers disagree (e.g., T6 passes temporal but fails strict policy), display the earliest decisive violation with the exact rule, then list downstream issues as secondary diagnostics.
