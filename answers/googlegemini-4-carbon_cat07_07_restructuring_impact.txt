# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

Relying on global mean or median cycle time (currently ~6.8–8.6d) can actively mislead management because ~55–60% of cases are simple/standard cases that bypass scarce specialists, dominating central-tendency statistics. Seven critical risks can worsen even if average cycle time stays flat or improves:

1. **Tail latency explosion (bimodal distribution shift):** Fast-tracking 60% of routine cases via automated/standardized flows can shave 0.5d off the mean while the top 10–15% of cases sitting in saturated `Legal/Risk` (91–93% baseline utilization + 33% new routing) and downsized `Supervisor Approval` queues jump from 18d to >30d. Mean cycle time improves; SLA breaches surge.
2. **Compound queueing on high-complexity cases:** Under the new rules, a high-complexity case with a risk flag and medium/high value must traverse *three sequential centralized queues* (`Technical Shared Service`  `Legal/Risk Shared Queue`  `Shared Approval Queue`  centralized `Quality Check`), plus pull-queue return delays at each step. Average metrics dilute these multi-gate delays across thousands of single-touch cases.
3. **Local knowledge loss and hidden customer burden:** When central specialists lack regional context (`local_expertise_flag == True`), they may close their own service ticket quickly (`get_service_time` looks fast) by issuing generic conditions or rejecting files back to local staff. The resulting rework, repeat customer document requests, and drops in customer satisfaction (currently 4.3 in North vs. 3.8 in East) are invisible in task-level duration averages—and if frustrated customers abandon applications early, closed-case cycle times artificially *shorten*.
4. **Distributional and segment fairness regressions:** East’s volume (520/mo) improving by 1.5d while North (820/mo) and South (760/mo) degrade by 0.3d yields a near-zero net average change, yet two-thirds of customers get worse service. Even more critically, minority `customer_segment` groups requiring language accommodation or non-standard regional documentation absorb disproportionate handover loops and SLA misses while majority standard segments speed up.
5. **Local learning decay and long-term capability hollowing:** Routing 100% of `case_complexity == "high"` cases out of local teams immediately speeds up local caseworkers’ average processing time (composition effect: they only touch easy cases!). Over 6–12 months, however, junior caseworkers never develop technical judgment (`Local Technical Assessment` becomes a pass-through), eliminating the internal talent pipeline to replace `N_Tech`, `S_Tech`, or `E_Tech` and driving long-term escalation inflation.
6. **Accountability dilution and queue cherry-picking:** In shared pull queues (`Shared Approval` and post-specialist advice queues), workers optimized on throughput metrics naturally pull crisp, well-documented cases first. Ambiguous cases sit untouched (`waiting_time` balloons on hard variants) and bounce across multiple handlers (`reassignment_count` rises), eroding end-to-end ownership without moving median wait time.
7. **Brittle exception handling and loss of surge resilience:** Pushing specialist utilization +15% (from ~73% to ~85–88%) and cutting 1 of 3 supervisors eliminates slack capacity. In normal weeks, averages look lean and efficient; during regulatory changes, seasonal spikes, or a single specialist/supervisor illness, the system lacks buffer capacity and enters nonlinear backlog failure, especially for out-of-policy exceptions that previously relied on rapid local supervisor+tech huddles.

# 5. Pilot design

Rather than an irreversible org-wide reorganization and immediate elimination of a supervisor post, test the operating mechanisms via a **controlled 10-week phased overflow/hybrid pilot**.

* **Pilot scope (Hybrid "Overflow + Standardized Note" Shared Pool):**
  * **Do not** eliminate the 3rd local supervisor position or dump more untriaged cases onto `Legal/Risk` (91–93% utilized) on Day 1.
  * Standardize the cross-team case-note schema across all three teams first (2-week prep).
  * Pool **40% of `N_Tech` and `S_Tech` capacity alongside `E_Tech`** into a virtual Technical Shared Service handling: (a) East’s high-complexity overflow, and (b) randomized 50% of high-complexity non-local-flag cases from North and South.
  * Enforce **return-to-sender affinity** (`Specialist  originating local caseworker`, falling back to team pull only if the caseworker is out >24h) rather than an anonymous all-teams pull queue.
  * Pilot the **Shared Approval Queue** on medium-value cases for two days/week (or 50% randomized medium-value cases) *with all 3 supervisors retained* to empirically measure cross-team review service/wait times before any headcount reduction.
* **Comparison group (Difference-in-Differences + Within-Team Randomized Split):**
  * **Control arm:** Remaining 50% of North/South high-complexity cases stay in the embedded local specialist loop (`N_Tech` / `S_Tech`), and cases with `local_expertise_flag == True` are stratified (half routed locally vs. half routed to cross-location pooled tech with standardized notes) to directly test whether standardized notes cure the 18% cross-team rework penalty.
* **Pre/post observation window:**
  * **Pre-pilot baseline:** 12 weeks of historical event logs (needed to capture full 90th-percentile tails of 16.4–22.8d without right-censoring bias).
  * **Ramp-up / stabilization:** 2 weeks (monitored for leading indicators, excluded from steady-state comparison).
  * **Active evaluation window:** 8 weeks of case cohorts, plus a **4-week run-out window** so all pilot-cohort cases reach `Close` without survival/right-censoring bias toward fast cases.
* **Mandatory segmentation matrix:**
  * Stratify every metric across: `team` (`North`, `South`, `East`) × `case_complexity` (`low`, `medium`, `high`) × `required_specialty` (`none`, `tech`, `legal`, `risk`, `security`) × `customer_segment` × `local_expertise_flag` (`True`, `False`).
* **Guardrail metrics (hard boundaries monitored weekly):**
  * `Legal/Risk` median post-handover wait must not exceed **12.0h** (baseline 9.6h) or p90 > **24.0h**.
  * Cross-location technical referral rework rate must stay **10.5%** (vs. 9% local baseline and 18% historical cross-team rate).
  * North and South p90 cycle time must not degrade by **>10%** (>18.0d North, >20.0d South) or SLA miss rate by **>+1.5 percentage points**.
  * `reassignment_count` per case must not rise by **>15%**, and `A  B  A` subcontracting return rate must remain **80%**.
* **Staff & qualitative feedback collection:**
  * Short weekly pulse micro-surveys for `*_Case*`, `*_Tech`, `Legal/Risk`, and `*_Supervisor` rating: (1) incoming case-note completeness (1–5), (2) clarity of ownership on returned cases, and (3) frequency of unlogged/informal coaching requests lost.
  * Bi-weekly structured case-audit calibration reviewing 15 reworked cases (`rework_flag == True`) across locations to classify root causes (missing regional context vs. poor intake notes vs. policy ambiguity).
* **Rollback or adjustment triggers:**
  * **Immediate adjustment trigger (within 48h):** If `Legal/Risk` or `Supervisor Approval` queue WIP grows monotonically for 5 consecutive business days, freeze expanded routing rules and restore local triage pre-screening.
  * **Partial rollback trigger:** If rework on `local_expertise_flag == True` cases routed to out-of-region specialists exceeds **13%** after week 4, carve out regionally flagged segments to remain permanently with local embedded/affinity specialists.
  * **Full pilot halt:** If overall SLA miss rate rises >2.5 percentage points in North/South without a larger offsetting reduction in East, or if high-complexity p90 cycle time rises >20%.

# 6. Recommendation

**Recommendation: Modify the design and pilot first—do NOT proceed with the full restructuring as proposed.**

### Rationale for Modification before Piloting
While management correctly diagnoses an imbalance in specialist utilization (`E_Tech` at 82% vs. `N_Tech`/`S_Tech` at 71%/68%) and inconsistent cross-team cover, three elements of the current proposal contradict basic queueing mechanics and empirical baseline signals in the log:
1. **Do not add +33% volume (9  12/100) and difficult Quality Check onto Legal (93%) and Risk (91%) without adding capacity or automated pre-triage.** At >90% utilization, adding demand without reducing service time guarantees severe central queue gridlock.
2. **Do not eliminate a local supervisor position upfront while increasing approval volume by 17% (42  49/100).** Cutting supervisor capacity by 33% under rising routing load creates a second artificial central bottleneck and strips away local escalation accountability.
3. **Replace anonymous pull-queue returns with "originating-caseworker return" (`A  B  A`) and keep medium/coached technical work partially local.** Blind queue pulling breaks case continuity, and routing 100% of hard technical work away from local teams risks converting North and South (currently high-performing at 6.8d/7.4d and 4.3/4.1 CSAT) into deskilled clerical intake hubs while importing the 18% cross-team rework penalty.

### Conditional Go/No-Go Path
* **Proceed to a scaled hybrid model** (virtual cross-team tech overflow pool + standardized case notes + affinity return routing) *only if* the 10-week pilot proves that standardized notes bring cross-location technical rework down from **18% to 10%**, East’s p90 cycle time drops below **19d**, and North/South p90 and `local_expertise_flag` customer outcomes remain within guardrail tolerances.
* **Defer any supervisor headcount reduction** until post-pilot `insert_case_service_waiting_time` data confirms that pooled approval utilization remains safely below **78–80%** even during peak arrival weeks and PTO coverage.

### What Management Must NOT Infer from Early Averages
During the first 2–6 weeks of piloting or rollout, management must explicitly guard against four statistical traps:
1. **Right-censoring illusion (Weeks 1–3):** Cases completed in the first three weeks are overwhelmingly fast, low-complexity cases; complex cases stuck in new shared queues have not closed yet, making early average cycle time look artificially fast.
2. **Local average speed-up via composition shift:** Local caseworkers’ average handling time will drop simply because all `high` complexity cases were removed from their denominator—this reflects workload sorting, not productivity gain.
3. **Pooled mean masking regional/segment divergence:** An East improvement offsetting North/South degradation—or fast standard cases masking delays for language/regionally accommodated customers—can leave global mean cycle time unchanged while SLA misses and customer dissatisfaction climb.
4. **Honeymoon backlog absorption:** High specialist utilization (~85–90%) can look productive in calm weeks before the first arrival surge or sick leave triggers nonlinear queue runaway.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The Python sketch below demonstrates how to evaluate pre- vs. post-restructuring (or pilot vs. control) event logs across all required organizational, performance, variant, and fairness dimensions using `pm4py`.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load & format Pre and Post event logs
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def prepare_log(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = df.copy()
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)
    # Standardize core XES columns for PM4Py
    log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    # Enrich each event with waiting_time and service_time (requires start/complete or consecutive timestamps)
    log = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)
    return log

df_pre = prepare_log(pd.read_csv("event_log_pre.csv"))
df_post = prepare_log(pd.read_csv("event_log_post.csv"))

# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Organizational Mining: Handover, Working-Together, Subcontracting, Roles
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
def analyze_org_networks(log: pd.DataFrame, label: str) -> dict:
    # Directed resource-to-resource handovers (org:resource -> org:resource)
    how_net = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, resource_key="org:resource")
    
    # Joint case participation (captures tight local team cliques vs diffuse star pools)
    wt_net = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log, resource_key="org:resource")
    
    # Subcontracting (A -> B -> A): directly measures if embedded/affinity loop survives pull-queues!
    sub_net = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log, resource_key="org:resource")
    
    # Resource-activity profile similarity (tracks deskilling / polarization between Case* and Tech)
    res_sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
        log, resource_key="org:resource", activity_key="concept:name"
    )
    
    # Organizational role discovery (clusters of activities performed by similar resource groups)
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
        log, resource_key="org:resource", activity_key="concept:name"
    )
    
    print(f"=== {label} Org Summary ===")

def analyze_flow_and_queues(log: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    # Frequency DFG & Performance DFG (mean, median, p90 edge durations)
    dfg_freq, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    perf_dfg_med, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log, perf_aggregation_key="median")
    perf_dfg_all, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log, perf_aggregation_key="all")
    
    # Activity-level service time summary
    svc_times = pm4py.get_service_time(log)
    
    # Waiting vs Service time breakdown at bottleneck gates
    target_gates = ["Specialist Review", "Supervisor Approval", "Quality Check"]
    gate_df = log[log["concept:name"].isin(target_gates)]
    queue_summary = (
        gate_df.groupby("concept:name")
        .agg(
            events=("case:concept:name", "count"),
            median_wait_h=("@@waiting_time", lambda s: np.nanmedian(s) / 3600.0),
            p90_wait_h=("@@waiting_time", lambda s: np.nanpercentile(s, 90) / 3600.0),
            median_svc_h=("@@service_time", lambda s: np.nanmedian(s) / 3600.0),
        )
    )
    
    # Variants & clarification ping-pong detection
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    # Filter to inspect top 10 mainstream variants vs long-tail exception variants
    top_variants_log = pm4py.filter_variants(log, list(variants.keys())[:10])
    
    # Extract case-level temporal & WIP workload features for regression / queue diagnostics
    temporal_features = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log)
    
    return {
        "dfg_freq": dfg_freq,
        "perf_dfg_med": perf_dfg_med,
        "perf_dfg_all": perf_dfg_all,
        "service_times": svc_times,
        "queue_summary": queue_summary,
        "variant_count": len(variants),
        "mainstream_case_share": top_variants_log["case:concept:name"].nunique()
        / log["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "temporal_features": temporal_features,
    }

# 4. Rework, Tail Cycle Time, SLA Misses & Stratified Fairness Comparison
# -------------------------------------------------------------------------
SLA_LIMIT_DAYS = 15.0  # Example contractual SLA threshold

def compute_cohort_kpis(log: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    if log.empty:
        return {}
    
    # Activity-specific rework counts (e.g., repeated Specialist Review or Local Customer Contact)
    rework_by_act = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log)
    
    # Case-level aggregation for cycle-time distribution, rework, reassignments & SLA misses
    case_stats = (
        log.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        .groupby("case:concept:name")
        .agg(
            start_ts=("time:timestamp", "first"),
            end_ts=("time:timestamp", "last"),
            rework_flag=("rework_flag", "max"),
            max_reassignments=("reassignment_count", "max"),
            outcome=("outcome", "last"),
        )
    )
    case_stats["cycle_days"] = (
        case_stats["end_ts"] - case_stats["start_ts"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 86400.0
    
    return {
        "n_cases": len(case_stats),
        "median_days": round(case_stats["cycle_days"].median(), 2),
        "p90_days": round(np.percentile(case_stats["cycle_days"], 90), 2),
        "p95_days": round(np.percentile(case_stats["cycle_days"], 95), 2),
        "sla_miss_pct": round((case_stats["cycle_days"] > SLA_LIMIT_DAYS).mean() * 100, 2),
        "rework_case_pct": round(case_stats["rework_flag"].astype(bool).mean() * 100, 2),
        "mean_reassignments": round(case_stats["max_reassignments"].mean(), 2),
        "specialist_repeat_cases": rework_by_act.get("Specialist Review", 0),
    }

def compare_segments(pre_log: pd.DataFrame, post_log: pd.DataFrame, attr: str, values: list):
    rows = []
    for val in values:
        # Filter trace cohorts using PM4Py attribute filtering (case-level scope)
        sub_pre = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            pre_log, attribute_key=attr, values=[val], level="case", retain=True
        )
        sub_post = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
            post_log, attribute_key=attr, values=[val], level="case", retain=True
        )
        kpi_pre = compute_cohort_kpis(sub_pre)
        kpi_post = compute_cohort_kpis(sub_post)
        rows.append({
            "dimension": attr,
            "segment": val,
            "pre_med_d": kpi_pre.get("median_days"),
            "post_med_d": kpi_post.get("median_days"),
            "pre_p90_d": kpi_pre.get("p90_days"),
            "post_p90_d": kpi_post.get("p90_days"),
            "pre_rework_%": kpi_pre.get("rework_case_pct"),
            "post_rework_%": kpi_post.get("rework_case_pct"),
            "pre_sla_miss_%": kpi_pre.get("sla_miss_pct"),
            "post_sla_miss_%": kpi_post.get("sla_miss_pct"),
            "post_reassign": kpi_post.get("mean_reassignments"),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)
```
