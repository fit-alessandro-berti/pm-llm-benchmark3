**Queueing quantities (per opening day, 07:00–19:00 = 12 open hours):**

-  = mean arrival intensity, per open hour
-  = mean completion intensity, per open hour
- S = median active service time (min); S. = 90th percentile of active service (min)
- L = time-average WIP, sampled every 15 min during open hours
- c = mean concurrently available staff (FTE)
-  = offered-load utilisation, defined below

**Operating-day conventions:**
- "day" is the 12-hour window 07:00–19:00; arrivals/completed/WIP are tabulated only inside it
- the 07:00 boundary inherits a residual queue from the previous close (unknown magnitude, except that WIP is observed over open hours)
- mean_wip is a *time-average* over snapshots (not a stock at 19:00)
- staff_fte is concurrent availability averaged across the day (does not reveal sub-day coverage)

**Limitations of the supplied aggregates:**
1. medians/p90s are *active handling times*; mean sojourn/flow time is not given — naïve substitution S  W violates Little's Law
2. arrivals and completions are *opening-hours* counts; the residual carry-over between consecutive days is unknown except via mean_wip
3. mean_wip is an open-hours snapshot average and is not the same as end-of-day inventory
4. the four hourly vectors are **representative, not sampled** — they cannot establish a stochastic model
5. outage_min is a daily total; sub-day timing on Apr 15 is reconstructed from the completion vector ("approximately 11:00–14:00")
6. case mix is given only as a percentage; the underlying mix index changed around Apr 20 but the coding source is undocumented
7. daily rows are not i.i.d.: state at 07:00 of day *t* depends on the close of day *t1*

**At least five threats to naïve inference:**

1. **Case-mix shift (regime change around Apr 20):** complex_pct roughly doubles (20 %  45 %), so any pooled M/M/c fitted to the whole month conflates two service-time distributions.
2. **Outage / recovery:** Apr 15–17 is a transient in which WIP and completion dynamics are *not* the steady state — pooling them inflates variance and biases completion-side estimates downward.
3. **Backlog carry-over:** mean_wip on day *t* contains the residual of yesterday's unfinished queue; using _day without an inventory reconciliation overestimates throughput stability.
4. **Weekday/weekend composition:** weekend rows have  40 % of weekday arrivals and  40 % of weekday FTE, so weekday-only means are diluted when pooled.
5. **Burstiness / overdispersion:** Fano factors of 3–10 on hourly vectors (see §5) violate the Poisson assumption embedded in M/M/c and M/G/c forms commonly used here.
6. **Intervention timing uncertainty:** the staffing change is *known to have begun by 2026-04-29* but the exact hour is not logged, so cross-day attribution of capacity effects is fuzzy.
7. **Code/classifier change risk:** the step change in complex_pct could be a labelling artefact as much as a real mix change; service-time distributions must be inspected before drawing a structural conclusion.

*Operational story:* everything else (outage, weekend, intervention) is noise around a single structural shift in case mix around 2026-04-20.

**Strengths vs primary:**
- Cleanly separates a single confounder (mix) from calendar effects
- Bigger pooled n in each bucket  tighter S estimates
- Directly tests the coding-change hypothesis (complex_pct may not actually map to real service-time mix)

**Weaknesses vs primary:**
- Throws away the Apr 15 outage signal and the Apr 16–19 recovery dynamics
- Conflates R3 and R4, hiding that the staffing intervention materially reduces L
- Mis-attributes WIP inflation in R2 to mix when it is actually the outage tail
- Cannot detect the interaction between mix change and capacity response

**Alternative B — "Capacity state is the regime"** (weekday FTE = 6 vs 7 vs weekend, pooled across mix). I considered this but rejected it as primary because FTE changes co-occur with other events (R4 began within 2 days of mix shift), so it cannot be cleanly attributed; I list it for completeness.

The defensible choice is to keep the primary **date-window** segmentation but always cross-check with the **mix-pooled** alternative whenever a service-time claim is made.

# 4. Pooled versus segmented statistics

**Pooled monthly means (all 28 days):**

| Metric | Pooled | R0 weekday | R3 weekday | R4 weekday | Direction of bias |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
|  (per open hr) | 7.66 | 8.49 | 9.55 | 9.45 | Pooled **understates** R3/R4 weekday load |
| S (min) | 30.7 | 25.0 | 35.0 | 32.7 | Pooled **interpolates** between two distinct service distributions |
| p90 (min) | 65.4 | 46.5 | 83.4 | 74.0 | Pooled **hides** that R3 p90 is ~80 % above R0 |
| mean_wip | 30.1 | 20.4 | 44.5 | 37.0 | Pooled understates by 35 % the WIP that staff actually face in R3 |
| complex % | 33.7 | 21.1 | 44.8 | 43.8 | Simpson-style reversal: pooled average overweights low-mix days |

**Three concrete ways pooled summaries obscure behaviour:**

1. **Weekday/weekend composition.** 10 of 28 days are weekend (36 %). Pooled  = 7.66/hr. Removing weekends lifts the weekday figure to ~9.0/hr. A model calibrated on 7.66/hr will systematically *under-staff* weekdays and *over-staff* weekends — a classic composition bias.

2. **Case-mix composition (Simpson-like reversal).** Comparing *pooled* low-mix days to pooled high-mix days is fine for S, but the **arrival pattern** reverses: low-mix days include both calm Mondays and outage-distorted Apr 15–19. Pooled  for "low-mix" days is 7.13/hr even though true R0 weekday  is 8.49/hr. An analyst who regresses L on  across the pooled sample gets a flatter slope than either R0 or R3 alone.

3. **Outage/inflation of apparent throughput.** If you compute "throughput" as completed/day over the whole month you get 88.4/day. If you compute it as completed per available FTE-minute you get very different answers in R1 vs R4. Pooling suggests "system runs at ~14.7 cases per FTE-day"; segmented values are 14.0 (R0), 9.7 (R1), 13.6 (R2), 13.0 (R3), 14.6 (R4). The pooled figure is approximately right *only because* the mix shift and the intervention roughly cancelled.

4. **Staffing intervention invisibility.** (mean_wip) from R3  R4 is **7.5 cases/weekday** despite no change in mix. Pooled daily WIP variance across the month is so dominated by R1/R2 transients that this 7.5 signal is buried.

**Robust alternative (when means are unsupported):** report medians and IQRs:
- median  (open hr): 7.96 (IQR 4.00–9.83)  bimodal (weekday vs weekend)
- median S: 32 min (IQR 28–35)  also bimodal
- median L: 24.5 (IQR 18–43)  strongly right-skewed by R2/R3

# 5. Burstiness, autocorrelation, and stationarity

**Hourly arrival burstiness (representative days):**

| Day |  arrivals | mean/hr | var/hr | **Fano** | Cv | Note |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 2026-04-07 | 101 | 8.42 | 27.2 | **3.23** | 0.62 | ordinary weekday; mild overdispersion |
| 2026-04-15 | 105 | 8.75 | 35.8 | **4.09** | 0.68 | overdispersed (would be higher if outage hour were zeroed) |
| 2026-04-21 | 115 | 9.58 | 97.0 | **10.13** | 1.03 | extreme burstiness — 34 cases in hour 09–10, 24 in 13–14 |
| 2026-04-25 | 51 | 4.25 | 5.11 | **1.20** | 0.53 | weekend, near-Poisson |

A Poisson arrival process would give Fano  1. All four representative days exceed that; the Apr 21 vector is an order of magnitude above Poisson. Index-of-dispersion estimates inherit the same story.

**Daily serial dependence (lag-1 autocorrelation, n = 28):**

| Series |  |
|---|---:|
| arrivals | +0.34 |
| complex % | +0.62 |
| mean_wip | +0.62 |
| completed | +0.32 |
| peak_08_10_pct | +0.68 |

complex_pct, WIP, and the morning-peak share all exhibit strong positive lag-1 autocorrelation. Daily observations are therefore **not independent**; SEs computed under i.i.d. assumptions are anti-conservative.

**Change-point evidence (transparent heuristic, not ground truth):**

A simple two-sample t-style scan over split index *k* = 3..25 finds maximum contrast at:

| Series | Peak split index | Date at split | Score | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|---:|---|
| complex_pct | 14 | between Apr 19 and Apr 20 |  18.0 | dominant step (matches the operational story) |
| arrivals | 19 | between Apr 24 and Apr 25 |  4.5 | weaker; weekend position confounds |
| mean_wip | 9 | between Apr 14 and Apr 15 |  4.0 | picks up the outage, not the mix shift |

The complex_pct signal is so strong that any reasonable test flags 2026-04-20 as a structural break. Arrival-volume tests are *less* sensitive because weekends dilute them — another argument against pooling.

**Why four representative days cannot establish a stochastic model:**
- They were *selected*, not sampled; selection criteria are unknown
- They do not represent the post-intervention weekday (R4)
- They do not represent weekends after the mix shift
- They cannot reveal day-to-day variability in shape, only spot magnitudes
- One of them (Apr 21) is itself a clear outlier; including it as "representative" of R3 overstates typical burstiness

A defensible use is to bound *expected* hourly load (e.g., Apr 7  Apr 21 shows that peak hour load can swell 1.7×–2.8× the daily mean), not to fit an arrival process.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

For each candidate window I record whether the four preconditions of Little's Law hold:
1. **Boundary stability** (no sharp step in  or  inside the window)
2. **Throughput proxy defined** (which of  or  is the long-run rate)
3. **WIP comparability** (mean_wip is measured on the same observation schedule as throughput)
4. **Carry-over handled** (no large residual stock crossing the window edges)

| Window | Boundary stability | Throughput proxy | WIP comparability | Carry-over risk | Stationarity | Implied flow W = L/_proxy (min) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| R0 weekday (Apr 6–10, 13–14) | stable |  (  ) | yes | low (steady state) | yes | 20.4/8.49 hr¹  **144 min** | **applicable** |
| R0 weekend (Apr 11–12) | stable |  | yes | low | yes | 13.0/3.33 hr¹  **234 min** | **applicable** |
| R1 outage day (Apr 15) | sharp step | none | partial | high (transient) | no | n/a (47/4.83  **584 min**, *misleading*) | **not defensible** |
| R2 weekday recovery (Apr 16–17) | draining | min(,) better;  here | partial | high (backlog inherited) | partial | 43.5/10.04 hr¹  **260 min** | **approximate only** |
| R2 weekend (Apr 18–19) | OK |  | yes | medium | yes | 14.5/3.62 hr¹  **240 min** | **approximate only** (carries outage residue) |
| R3 weekday (Apr 20–24, 27–28) | stable within window |  | yes | low within R3 | yes | 44.5/8.18 hr¹  **326 min** | **applicable** |
| R4 weekday (Apr 29–May 1) | draining |  | yes | medium (still draining R3) | partial | 37.0/10.08 hr¹  **220 min** | **approximate only** |
| R4 weekend (May 2–3) | stable |  | yes | low | yes | 20.5/4.08 hr¹  **302 min** | **applicable** |
| Whole month | violated | none | violated | violated | no | 30.1/7.32 hr¹  **247 min** | **not defensible** |

**Important note on units and proxy choice.** L is in *cases*, _proxy is *cases per open hour*, so W is in *open hours*. Multiplying by 60 gives minutes *of open-hour time* — overnight closed hours do not contribute to sojourn time. This is fine for an operational SLA definition (time from arrival during open hours to completion during open hours) but is **not** the same as wall-clock case duration if cases can wait overnight. The supplied aggregates cannot disambiguate. I therefore use **** (completions per open hour) as the throughput proxy wherever boundary stability holds, because WIP observations are over open hours and the system has reached approximate inflow/outflow balance.  is only used as a sanity cross-check.

# 7. Operational implications

**Per-regime actions:**

- **R0 (Baseline):** weekday capacity of 6 FTE is comfortable (  0.55). Reasonable headroom for moderate demand growth.
- **R1 (Outage):** a single 3-hour outage produces a ~47-case residual and a near-doubling of WIP that takes 2–3 days to drain even at full weekday capacity. **Recommendation:** cross-train  1.5 FTE of "break-glass" capacity that can be deployed same-day to absorb outage tails, and pre-stage a recovery playbook.
- **R2 (Recovery):** weekday FTE was already bumped to 7 on Apr 16 — but only for 2 weekdays before the mix shift. **Recommendation:** when an outage exceeds ~2 hours, schedule +1 FTE for at least the next 3 open days.
- **R3 (High-complex):**   0.88 weekday at unchanged 6 FTE. WIP climbs +5 cases per weekday and never equilibrates inside R3. **Recommendation:** the 7-FTE intervention is structurally necessary under the new mix; do not revert to 6 FTE on weekdays while complex_pct  40 %.
- **R4 (Intervention):** at 7 FTE, weekday    but WIP is still draining. **Recommendation:** hold 7 FTE until mean_wip falls below ~25 (R0 level) AND complex_pct stays  40 %; revisit only when both conditions are met.

**Peak smoothing.** peak_08_10_pct rose from ~30 % in R0 to ~48 % in R3. Even with adequate headcount, a sharper morning peak amplifies intra-hour queueing (Fano 10 on Apr 21). **Recommendation:** open at 06:30 or pull non-urgent work to 14:00–16:00; quantify benefit only after the mix stabilises.

**Backlog recovery.** Carry-over from R1 propagated into R2 even on weekends (WIP 14.5 vs baseline weekend 13.0). **Recommendation:** include weekend FTE as part of the recovery playbook, not just weekday.

**Data collection.**

- Log case-level arrivals/completions with timestamps so PM4Py can compute per-case flow time and per-case service time.
- Record *outage start/end times* in minutes, not just totals.
- Track 07:00 opening queue separately so carry-over can be modelled.
- Capture sub-day staffing schedule, not just daily mean FTE.
- Tag cases with variant (complex/simple) at arrival, not retrospectively.

**A policy that looks good pooled but risky segmented:**

> "Reduce weekday FTE from 7 to 6.5 in early May because **monthly average** completed/FTE-day was 14.0 — within historical norms."

Under pooled monthly averages this is invisible: the average hides that R4 with 7 FTE is *barely* keeping WIP from re-growing (/ = 1.07, only 7 % above break-even) and that R3 with 6 FTE was already in a deficit. Cutting to 6.5 FTE would push R4 utilisation back above 0.8 with WIP likely to start climbing again. A segmented view flags this immediately; a pooled view does not.

        pre, post = x[:k], x[k:]
        sp = np.sqrt(((k-1)*pre.var(ddof=1) + (n-k-1)*post.var(ddof=1)) / (n-2))
        if sp <= 0: continue
        t = abs(pre.mean()-post.mean()) / (sp*np.sqrt(1/k + 1/(n-k)))
        if t > best[1]: best = (k, t, pre.mean(), post.mean())
    return best  # (k, score, mean_pre, mean_post)

print("\n=== Change-point heuristic (max |t| across splits) ===")
for c in ["arrivals","completed","mean_wip","complex_pct","peak_pct"]:
    k, t, m_pre, m_post = cp_screen(df[c])
    print(f"  {c:<14} split_idx={k:>2} ({(df['date'].iloc[k-1].date())} | "
          f"{df['date'].iloc[k].date()})  |t|={t:5.2f}  "
          f"mean_pre={m_pre:.2f}  mean_post={m_post:.2f}")

# 5. Little's-Law implied flow time for explicitly chosen windows
#    Units: L in cases, proxy in cases/open-hour, W in open-hour minutes.
#    We use  (completions per open hour) as the throughput proxy wherever
#    boundary stability holds;  is reported as a cross-check only.
# --------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPEN_HRS = 12.0

def littles(d, proxy="mu"):
    """Return implied W (open-hour minutes) for a daily sub-frame d."""
    L  = d.mean_wip.mean()
    if proxy == "mu":
        rate = d.completed.mean() / OPEN_HRS
    elif proxy == "lam":
        rate = d.arrivals.mean() / OPEN_HRS
    else:
        rate = min(d.arrivals.mean(), d.completed.mean()) / OPEN_HRS
    return {"L": round(L,2), "proxy_per_hr": round(rate,3),
            "W_min": round(60.0*L/rate, 1)}

windows = [

]

print("\n=== Little's-Law implied flow time (W = 60 * L / proxy) ===")
print(f"{'window':<16}{'L':>8}{'mu/hr':>10}{'W_mu_min':>12}{'lam/hr':>10}{'W_lam_min':>12}")

    L = sub.mean_wip.mean()

    print(f"{name:<16}{L:>8.2f}{mu:>10.3f}{60*L/mu:>12.1f}{la:>10.3f}{60*L/la:>12.1f}")
