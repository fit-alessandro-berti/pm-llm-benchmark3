## 1. Materiality Framework

Use the following ordinal scale for **business severity**, not for alignment cost:

1. **Minimal:** No identified business-control failure; harmless technical noise or a fitting path.
2. **Low:** Documentation, linkage, or model-scope issue with limited demonstrated harm; resolve or monitor.
3. **Moderate:** Confirmed policy breach or material evidence gap without the strongest indicators of imminent loss; investigate and correct.
4. **High:** Serious control or evidence failure with substantial exposure or a known fraud-risk indicator; act promptly.
5. **Critical:** Payment released before a required control or a potentially absent fraud control on a particularly exposed claim; urgent containment and investigation.

Assess the control objective first: prevention of unauthorized payment and fraud is generally more material than a cosmetic log mismatch. Consider the amount potentially exposed, but do not equate the claim amount with actual loss; reversibility and recovery prospects; whether a control happened before payment; actual or potential customer and regulatory harm; recurrence and common-cause exposure; reliability and independence of evidence; and whether the case belongs in the model's intended scope. A repeated low-severity issue can become a high-priority portfolio issue. No regulatory breach or realized loss is established solely by these alignments.

**Confidence** describes how strongly the available evidence supports the specific conclusion, not how serious the consequence would be. A high-severity suspected control failure can have only medium confidence when evidence is missing; a high-confidence label or retry issue can have low severity. A synchronous move establishes a match to the supplied log and model, not that the activity was effective or that all policy conditions were met. `M` indicates no matching log event in the alignment, not proof the real-world activity did not occur. `L` indicates an unmatched recorded event, not necessarily an improper business action. The supplied alignments are taken as authoritative diagnostics.

## 4. Causal Hypotheses and Evidence Tests

- **CL-602:** A verbal fraud check may have been performed but not documented; alternatively the check was superficial or omitted. Obtain contemporaneous fraud-system records, call records or notes, analyst identity, bank-change verification, and the applicable screening standard. A later assertion alone cannot establish what happened before payment.
- **CL-604:** Approval may truly have followed release, or it may have happened earlier and been entered late; the screen may have been omitted or recorded elsewhere. Compare immutable authorization and workflow audit trails, bank instruction and acceptance timestamps, clock synchronization, user actions, and fraud-system evidence. Establish whether bank acceptance corresponds to an irrevocable release. The model's missing later payment is not a second payment.
- **CL-603 and the 74 retry cases:** Duplicate log entries may be retry attempts for one message, or some may represent multiple customer-visible deliveries. Correlate message ID, attempt ID, connector retries, delivery receipts and payloads; test whether distinct legitimate messages ever reuse IDs before deduplicating.
- **CL-606 and external-adjuster cases:** A valid external assessment may be complete but absent from the core event stream, or an attached document may be unrelated, late, or incomplete. Verify immutable claim identifiers, report contents, signature validity and timestamps against the event sequence; inspect the two unstable links individually.
- **CL-608:** The portal message may be the required timely notification, or the mutable link may point to an unrelated or subsequently changed record. Examine immutable CRM audit history, message content, recipient, case identifier, delivery status and retention history. A message created at +3 hours alone does not prove receipt or durable linkage.
- **CL-609 and CL-610:** The decision engine may lack eligibility guards, receive stale data, permit an override, or use an incorrect policy version. Reconstruct decision inputs and timestamps, rule version, bank-change history, overrides, and payment authorization; independently search for any external controls. Compare the four known policy-violating cases with the full eligible population.
- **CL-611:** `Assess Damage` may be the historical synonym for `Assess Loss`, or a narrower activity that does not satisfy assessment requirements. Review the historical UI and procedure, payloads, actor, underlying report and time-versioned mapping. A mapping added later is supporting evidence, not proof of historical semantic equivalence.
- **CL-607 and CL-612:** These cases may be correctly excluded from the payable model, or intake scope may intentionally include them and require explicit exit branches. Reconcile the model charter with process owners and actual closure and notification rules; verify no subsequent payment or reopening. Reopened cases need separate scope and lifecycle treatment.
- **CL-601 and CL-605:** UI events may be harmless instrumentation, while a particular telemetry event could disclose a meaningful override; the silent transition may simply route the model. Inspect event schemas and audit semantics before filtering, and verify Petri-net routing and CL-605's bank-change history.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

An alignment cost is the sum of moves chosen under a model and cost function. Here every visible log or model move costs one, irrespective of claim value, control criticality, timing, reversibility, actual loss, and evidence quality. It describes the effort to reconcile a trace with the tree, not the probability or magnitude of harm. Changing weights could improve triage for specified objectives but would encode judgments and still could not prove whether an unlogged control occurred.

CL-601's cost 3 is driven by UI activity at a finer granularity than the business model; CL-603's cost 1 counts a retry of the same message, not necessarily a second business notification. CL-605's hidden routing transition is a fitting, zero-cost model move, not an absent real-world task; zero cost also cannot verify unstated data conditions. CL-609 and CL-610 both cost zero because the tree permits the auto branch without testing amount, risk, or bank changes, although the business overlay prohibits those decisions. Conversely, CL-606's model move flags a missing core assessment event while external evidence may establish the assessment, subject to linkage; CL-611's two moves may be one activity with a historical label mismatch. CL-608's missing core notification can coexist with a timely portal message, though mutable linkage limits confidence.

Scope matters: CL-607 and CL-612 cost 4 because the payable model forces work after denial or withdrawal, not because payment or approval was necessarily required. CL-602 costs only 1 despite a potentially missing fraud control on EUR 480,000; CL-604 costs 3 but includes a bank-accepted payment before recorded approval, which may be difficult to reverse. Its model-only later payment is a repair in the alignment, not another transaction. Severity therefore requires policy, timing, evidence, exposure and recovery analysis alongside the alignment.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

The following standalone example requires `pm4py` and `pandas`. It creates synthetic event timestamps solely to order the sample traces; they are not evidence of actual business times or notification deadlines. The diagnostic results are illustrative: PM4Py's cost scale and formatting can differ, and alternative optimal alignments can differ from the supplied authoritative notation. The supplied move summaries and unit raw costs below are kept separately from PM4Py's generated output and from the narrative severity judgments.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

TREE = "-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), 'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)
net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# Actual recorded business/telemetry events only: do not insert model-only moves.
traces = {
    "CL-601": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Open Workbench", "Assess Loss", "Autosave", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant", "Close Workbench"],
    "CL-602": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-603": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-604": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Pay Claim", "Manual Approve", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-605": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-606": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-607": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Deny Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-608": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim"],
    "CL-609": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-610": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-611": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Damage", "Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-612": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss", "Withdraw Claim"],
}
rows = []
for case_id, activities in traces.items():
    for position, activity in enumerate(activities):
        rows.append({
            "case_id": case_id,
            "activity": activity,
            "timestamp": pd.Timestamp("2025-01-01", tz="UTC") + pd.Timedelta(minutes=position),
        })
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    pd.DataFrame(rows), case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
)
log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(events)
diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(log, net, initial_marking, final_marking)
print("PM4Py alignments (illustrative; supplied alignments remain authoritative):")
for index, diagnostic in enumerate(diagnostics):
    print(index, diagnostic)

# Supplied unit-cost move summaries. S moves are summarized by the trace above;
# T denotes the zero-cost hidden move. Labels preserve the given diagnostics.
summaries = [
    ("CL-601", 1200, "LOW",    3, 1, "high",        "P1 eligible; P4 timing unknown",             [("L", "Open Workbench"), ("L", "Autosave"), ("L", "Close Workbench")]),
    ("CL-602", 480000, "HIGH", 1, 5, "medium",      "P2 fraud evidence missing",                  [("M", "Fraud Screen")]),
    ("CL-603", 3200, "LOW",    1, 1, "high",        "P1 eligible; retry; P4 timing unknown",      [("L", "Notify Claimant")]),
    ("CL-604", 90000, "MEDIUM", 3, 5, "high/medium", "P2/P3 payment before recorded approval",      [("L", "Pay Claim"), ("M", "Fraud Screen"), ("M", "Pay Claim")]),
    ("CL-605", 800, "LOW",     0, 1, "high/conditional", "P1 bank history unknown",                [("T", "tau")]),
    ("CL-606", 75000, "HIGH",  1, 2, "medium-high", "P2 controls logged; external assessment",    [("M", "Assess Loss")]),
    ("CL-607", None, "UNKNOWN", 4, 2, "high/medium", "denial; payable scope disputed",             [("L", "Deny Claim"), ("M", "Assess Loss"), ("M", "Auto Approve"), ("M", "Pay Claim")]),
    ("CL-608", 250000, "HIGH", 1, 2, "medium",      "P4 portal message; mutable link",             [("M", "Notify Claimant")]),
    ("CL-609", 18000, "MEDIUM", 0, 3, "high",       "P1 auto approval violation",                  []),
    ("CL-610", 4500, "LOW",    0, 4, "high/medium", "P1/P2 bank-change violation",                []),
    ("CL-611", 62000, "HIGH",  2, 2, "medium",      "P2 controls logged; label mapping uncertain", [("L", "Assess Damage"), ("M", "Assess Loss")]),
    ("CL-612", 2000, "LOW",    4, 2, "high/medium", "withdrawal; payable scope disputed",         [("L", "Withdraw Claim"), ("M", "Auto Approve"), ("M", "Pay Claim"), ("M", "Notify Claimant")]),
]
columns = ["case", "amount_eur", "risk_level", "raw_cost", "business_severity", "confidence", "policy_overlay", "moves"]
cases = pd.DataFrame(summaries, columns=columns)
cases["amount_eur"] = pd.to_numeric(cases["amount_eur"], errors="coerce")

# Each row is one supplied non-synchronous move; T is excluded from visible cost.
move_rows = [
    {"case": row.case, "move_type": kind, "activity": activity,
     "unit_cost": 0 if kind == "T" else 1}
    for row in cases.itertuples(index=False)
    for kind, activity in row.moves
]
moves = pd.DataFrame(move_rows)
move_totals = moves.groupby("case")["unit_cost"].sum().reindex(cases["case"], fill_value=0)
assert move_totals.to_numpy().tolist() == cases["raw_cost"].tolist()

print("\nSupplied move patterns, with sample exposure and policy context:")
joined = moves.merge(cases.drop(columns="moves"), on="case", how="left")
patterns = joined.groupby(["move_type", "activity"], dropna=False).agg(
    move_count=("case", "size"),
    cases=("case", "nunique"),
    sum_raw_cost_across_cases=("raw_cost", "sum"),
    known_amount_eur=("amount_eur", lambda s: s.sum(min_count=1)),
    risk_levels=("risk_level", lambda s: ", ".join(sorted(set(s)))),
    policy_flags=("policy_overlay", lambda s: "; ".join(sorted(set(s)))),
).reset_index()
print(patterns.to_string(index=False))
print("Pattern totals overlap: a case can contribute to multiple patterns; amounts are not losses.")

print("\nCase-level aggregation by risk and policy-overlay flag:")
risk_summary = cases.groupby(["risk_level", "policy_overlay"], dropna=False).agg(
    case_count=("case", "size"),
    total_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
    known_amount_eur=("amount_eur", lambda s: s.sum(min_count=1)),
    missing_amounts=("amount_eur", lambda s: s.isna().sum()),
).reset_index()
print(risk_summary.to_string(index=False))

# Explicit judgment and confidence are retained as evidence; no formula produces severity.
evidence = cases[["case", "amount_eur", "risk_level", "raw_cost", "policy_overlay", "business_severity", "confidence"]].copy()
evidence["supplied_move_summary"] = cases["moves"].map(
    lambda ms: ", ".join(f"{kind}({activity})" for kind, activity in ms) if ms else "all visible moves synchronous"
)
evidence = evidence.sort_values(["business_severity", "case"], ascending=[False, True])
print("\nRisk-prioritization evidence table (severity is a human judgment, not a formula):")
print(evidence.to_string(index=False))
print("\nAdditional monthly evidence: 74 retry cases; 9 external-adjuster gaps, 2 without stable IDs; 4 auto-policy violations. Overlap is unspecified.")
```
