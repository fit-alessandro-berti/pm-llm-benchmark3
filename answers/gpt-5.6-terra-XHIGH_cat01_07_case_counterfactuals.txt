## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Register  Triage
Triage  Identity Check
Triage  Document Check_1                                      [no originals required]
Triage  Originals Receipt  Document Check_1                  [originals required]

{Identity Check, Document Check_1}  Assessment_1

(Assessment_j or Compliance Review_j)  Request More Info_j
Request More Info_j  Document Check_(j+1)  Assessment_(j+1)

Assessment_last  Compliance Review_final  Decision  Notify  [high-risk or high-value]
Assessment_last  Decision  Notify                            [otherwise]

Triage  Escalation Advice                                     [optional]
Triage  Preliminary Compliance Screen                         [optional]
```

```text
- Register is the first activity and Triage is second.
- Identity Check and the applicable Document Check must both be complete before an Assessment.
- With no originals requirement, Identity Check and Document Check may overlap.
- With an originals requirement, Originals Receipt must precede each Document Check using those originals.
- A high-risk or high-value case requires a final Compliance Review after the latest Assessment and before Decision.
- A preliminary compliance screen, even if performed by C1, is not the final Compliance Review.
- A Request More Info creates a new Document Check and a new Assessment; high-risk/high-value cases then require a new or reaffirmed final Compliance Review.
- The resource completing the final Assessment cannot complete the final Compliance Review.
- C1, S1, and all operational resources may have only one active service interval at a time.
- A reserved compliance slot either consumes capacity or must be explicitly released; it is not free capacity.
- Queue estimates describe anticipated waits and may be changed by priority arrivals, cancellations, or other cases' routing.
- Real-time timestamps must reflect task claim, service start, and completion, rather than later batch entry.
```

K-104 and K-411 primarily alter task timing and readiness, although K-104 also changes C1 priority through a reservation. K-219 changes document-resource routing from D1 to D2. K-337 changes the allowable decision policy: it replaces evidence resolution through a second loop with an explicit conditional-approval or rejection decision using unresolved evidence.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

**Factual mechanism:** D1 completed the complex-document check at Tue 05:40, but A1 did not start until Tue 15:10. The sanctions ambiguity was then rediscovered, causing S1 advice at Tue 19:20 and a C1 review only from Wed 13:10 to 17:40. Thus, the late discovery converted what could have been preparation during the document and assessment waits into a serial compliance-reading block.

**Minimal intervention:** At Mon 08:50, Triage sends S1 a limited sanctions packet and requests a provisional C1 window. S1 may identify the relevant counterparties or evidence gaps while I1 and D1 work. C1 may perform a short, explicitly preliminary pre-read if capacity is reserved, but neither that pre-read nor S1 advice substitutes for the final C1 review after A1’s final assessment.

**Temporal propagation:** Identity and document completion need not change. In a favorable base schedule, C1 reads the packet before A1 begins, then uses a protected slot shortly after A1 completes at Tue 19:10. If preparation saves roughly one to three hours of final evidence reading, final C1 work could finish late Tuesday rather than late Wednesday. The time saving comes from converting part of C1’s reading from post-assessment work to pre-assessment work and from avoiding the ordinary C1 queue, not from shortening D1 or A1 service.

**Process-feasibility check:** The case has no originals requirement, so I1 and D1 may remain parallel. A1 remains the final assessor and C1 remains the final reviewer, satisfying four-eyes separation. The final C1 review begins only after A1 completes. If either S1 or C1 identifies a material unresolved issue, a Request More Info remains permitted and would require the mandated new document check, assessment, and compliance review.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is an early S1 review, useful C1 pre-read, an intact Tue evening final C1 slot, and a reachable M1, producing about 18–22 hours of improvement. A base case saves about 8–18 hours because M1 may not sign until Wednesday morning. In the worst case, the packet is too incomplete to save final review time, the slot is preempted by an urgent matter, or a newly found issue creates rework; this case may finish unchanged or later.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The conclusion weakens if C1’s preliminary and final effort together exceed the factual 4.5-hour review, if protected slots are routinely preempted, or if historical packets rarely contain sufficient sanctions evidence before assessment. It also reverses systemically if the displaced urgent compliance cases have greater risk or priority than K-104.

### K-219

**Factual mechanism:** The originals arrived Mon 17:30, but D1’s accumulated work caused service to begin Tue 09:30. D1 then completed an accurate four-hour review, so the factual path avoided rework. The remaining delay included the A2 queue from Tue 13:30 to 20:00 and the overnight wait for M1’s decision batch.

**Minimal intervention:** After Originals Receipt, the task joins D2’s queue rather than D1’s. No eligibility rule, assessment rule, or decision rule changes. A D2-discovered unreadable field should be recorded as a document defect awaiting correction, followed by a new D2 document check and A2 assessment; it should not be silently passed through.

**Temporal propagation:** With a Mon 16:00 D2 queue forecast of five hours, D2 could plausibly start late Monday and finish around early Tuesday despite being slower than D1. That may make the case assessment-ready several hours before the factual Tue 13:30 document completion. However, A2 had a six-hour forecast queue on Tuesday morning and M1 may still batch decisions on Wednesday morning. Consequently, an earlier D2 completion can be absorbed in the assessment or manager queue rather than translated one-for-one into earlier notification.

**Process-feasibility check:** Originals Receipt still precedes D2’s document review, and both I2’s identity check and D2’s document check precede A2’s assessment. The case is neither high-risk nor high-value, so no final compliance review is required. If a corrected original is needed before assessment, the repeat document check and subsequent assessment preserve the required evidence sequence.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case is D2 service beginning late Monday, ending before Tuesday’s assessment capacity opens, followed by a same-day A2 and M1 decision; this can save roughly 12–18 hours. The base case is a smaller or zero gain because A2 or M1 remains the limiting queue. Worst case is a clarification request followed by an 8–36-hour external response, a repeat D2 review, and another A2 wait, producing a one-to-three-day delay and a possible changed decision.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The routing benefit disappears if D2’s actual late-Monday queue is comparable to D1’s next available slot, if D2’s handwritten-document defect rate is materially higher than D1’s, or if M1’s decision timing is fixed regardless of assessment completion. Evidence that D1’s handwriting accuracy prevents costly post-decision corrections would favor retaining D1.

### K-337

**Factual mechanism:** The factual case did not merely wait in internal queues. The second supplier confirmation request began Fri 10:30 and returned only Mon 09:00 because of weekend delay. That response triggered another D1 check and A1 assessment before the mandatory C1 review. The long elapsed time therefore reflects external response latency, rework service, and re-entry into D1, A1, C1, and M1 queues.

**Minimal intervention:** Immediately after Assessment 2, A1 records the unresolved inconsistency and does not issue Request More Info 2. C1 then performs a final review of the available evidence and explicitly assesses the discrepancy. M1 must either reject or approve with stated conditions, monitoring, limits, indemnities, or other permitted controls; no actor may treat the discrepancy as resolved without evidence.

**Temporal propagation:** The case becomes C1-ready on Fri 10:30 instead of only after Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 on Tuesday. This avoids the external weekend wait and removes D1 and A1 service, but it does not guarantee a Friday decision. C1 may already be committed to other high-risk work, may take longer because the discrepancy is unresolved, and M1 may not decide until Monday or Tuesday. The saved external time can therefore collapse into a compliance or decision queue.

**Process-feasibility check:** The first Request More Info loop remains valid: it is followed by Document Check 2 and Assessment 2. Because no second Request More Info occurs, a third document check and assessment are not required. C1 reviews after the latest assessment, and C1—not A1—performs the final compliance review. The altered branch is feasible only because the decision explicitly accepts conditions or rejects on the existing evidentiary record.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case has C1 and M1 capacity on Friday and saves three to four calendar days. A base case completes on Monday or Tuesday and saves roughly one to three days. Worst case has C1 and M1 capacity only when they would have been available factually, yielding little elapsed-time improvement while still changing the decision policy. Conditional approval, rejection, or a materially more restrictive approval is plausible.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The intervention should not be used if regulation, policy, or contract requires the missing confirmation before any approval, even conditional approval. It is also unattractive if historical data show that this discrepancy type predicts loss or fraud, or if C1 routinely rejects such cases and thereby creates more appeal and exception workload than the avoided loop would have created.

### K-411

**Factual mechanism:** The case lock made document ownership unavailable until I2 completed at Tue 13:20. D2 therefore began only at Tue 15:00 and completed at 19:30. Although the verification work itself was standard, serial task creation delayed assessment readiness, after which the expanded A2 queue carried the case into Wednesday.

**Minimal intervention:** At Triage, the system creates separate identity and document tasks. A controlled lock workaround assigns I2 and D2 independent, auditable ownership scopes, with server-generated claim, start, pause, completion, and lock-release events. The intervention does not give either resource permission to work another assigned service interval simultaneously.

**Temporal propagation:** If D2 joins the Tuesday queue at 09:35, a plausible service start is around noon rather than 15:00. D2 could then work roughly Tue 12:00–16:30, overlapping I2’s Tue 11:30–13:20 interval and making both checks complete around 16:30 rather than 19:30. The case enters the A2 queue earlier, but the Tuesday assessment backlog may still keep A2 service on Wednesday. Earlier readiness only improves final completion if it changes the case’s position in the assessment and M1 queues.

**Process-feasibility check:** The case has no originals requirement, so I2 and D2 may work concurrently. Assessment cannot start until both real-time completion events exist. A2 remains the sole assessor, and no compliance review is needed for this low-risk, low-value case. The workaround is feasible only if simultaneous ownership cannot create conflicting document state, duplicate notifications, or retrospective timestamp entry.

**Best/base/worst case:** Best case produces genuine overlap, an earlier A2 slot, and an earlier M1 decision, saving roughly 6–12 hours. The base case saves zero to four hours because A2 remains congested. The worst case is no timing gain after queue absorption, plus a correction or rollback if lock behavior produces an integrity exception. The substantive decision should remain approval unless the workaround exposes a genuine data discrepancy.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** The conclusion fails if measured D2 start times do not advance after early task creation, if A2 operates strict batch priorities that ignore early readiness, or if the workaround creates any meaningful rate of conflicting edits or audit failures. It is also weakened if later batch timestamps, rather than actual service events, were responsible for the apparent factual seriality.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 early escalation/preparation | Earlier S1 work; C1 pre-read plus reserved final slot, with only partial offset from shorter final review. | Late-stage prepared-case compliance work, if pre-reading genuinely substitutes for final reading. | C1 reservation queue and possibly S1 advisory queue. | Urgent C1 cases without reservations; S1 advisory clients. | Ambiguous: target-case improvement can be purchased by priority displacement. | Near-full C1 utilization makes reservations and missed/cancelled slots disproportionately harmful. |
| K-219 D2 routing | D1 demand falls; D2 demand rises by one longer and more variable handwritten-document review, with possible repeat work. | D1 queue, especially for D1’s complex corporate cases. | D2 queue; A2 queue if clarification creates re-assessment. | D1 complex-document cases may improve; D2 standard cases may wait longer. | Conditional positive if D2 has slack and rework remains rare. | A small increase in clarification probability creates external-delay tails and repeat internal service. |
| K-337 suppress second loop | Avoids Document Check 3, Assessment 3, and external collection; may require longer C1 exception review. | D1, A1, and external-follow-up workload. | Earlier C1/M1 demand; exception, appeal, and remediation queues. | Other D1/A1 cases benefit; high-risk C1 cases and appeals cases may be harmed. | Operationally positive but quality/compliance effect is uncertain or negative. | Weekend timing and the probability of adverse decisions dominate elapsed-time savings. |
| K-411 parallel verification | Similar total I2/D2 service, but document demand arrives earlier and assessment-ready arrivals are advanced. | Lock-induced verification delay and some target-case waiting. | A2 assessment queue; transient early-day D2 queue. | A2 cases and D2 cases arriving in the same period. | Usually modestly positive locally; may be neutral system-wide if A2 is saturated. | A burst of earlier completions can move the bottleneck to A2 without increasing throughput. |

At 100 similar cases in one week, these are interacting queueing policies, not 100 independent savings. K-104 is the clearest capacity hazard. Every provisional C1 reservation reserves an appointment before the final assessment exists; a cancellation or incomplete packet leaves C1 fragmented, while a kept reservation displaces urgent non-target reviews. Preliminary reading offsets this only if it reduces final C1 service by at least the pre-read time and reservation slack. Otherwise C1, already the sole certified reviewer, becomes the bottleneck.

K-219 transfers roughly one D1 check to D2 per case. It can free D1 for complex corporate work, where D1 has the strongest comparative advantage, but 100 handwritten cases can tip D2 into a longer queue. A modest increase in clarification probability has nonlinear effects: external responses of 8–36 hours generate repeat D2 checks and assessments, creating both additional load and irregular arrivals.

K-337 avoids third-pass D1/A1 work and often avoids a weekend response, so document and assessment queues may improve. It also moves unresolved high-risk files earlier into C1, M1, exception management, appeals, or loss handling. If decisions batch on weekdays, saved external time may not become same-week completion.

K-411 changes arrival timing more than verification volume. A hundred earlier-complete verification pairs can form a burst at A2; an already congested assessment queue then absorbs the apparent gain and delays ordinary cases. D2 may also see an earlier daily peak. Real-time event, lock, and workstation timestamps are needed to distinguish true I2/D2 overlap from two tasks entered concurrently after completion. With all four policies together, C1 and assessment gates, rather than documents, are likely bottlenecks.

## 7. Final synthesis

Controlled parallel verification in K-411 is the most robust intervention, provided the lock workaround is transactionally safe and its real-time audit trail is enforced. It preserves eligibility, the two verification prerequisites, resource separation, decision authority, and the substantive decision rule. Its expected speed-up is modest because A2 can absorb earlier readiness, but a modest, reversible improvement with direct observability is preferable to a large unmeasured claim. K-219 is a reasonable second pilot when D2 has demonstrable spare capacity and the handwriting cohort is tightly limited; its benefit comes from bypassing D1’s wait, not from assuming D2 is intrinsically faster.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite potentially being the fastest. Suppressing the second loop eliminates a slow external and weekend dependency, but it substitutes managerial risk acceptance or rejection for evidence resolution. Even with an explicit conditional decision and final C1 review, it can alter losses, appeals, and compliance defensibility. It should be treated as an exception-policy experiment, not a lean-process improvement.

The single-case K-104 result is least transportable to a system policy. A protected C1 slot looks valuable for one case because it moves that case ahead of an otherwise invisible queue. Repeating that choice for many cases turns the sole certified reviewer into a reservation bottleneck and can delay more urgent files. K-411 also needs system-level monitoring, but it does not consume a new scarce review service.

Most valuable additional data are actual resource calendars; queue-entry, priority, reservation, cancellation, and preemption events; C1 preliminary and final effort separately; assessment queue behavior; lock-acquisition and workstation timestamps; document-defect reasons; supplier-response distributions by weekday and weekend; and post-decision conditions, appeals, losses, and audit findings. These data distinguish less work from displaced work or merely earlier event recording.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — The stated normative prerequisites apply consistently to all cases, including repeat document and assessment work after a formal Request More Info.

2. **temporal** — Snapshot queue estimates are informative about likely waiting order but do not determine exact staff availability, shift boundaries, or completion times.

3. **resource** — I1, I2, D1, D2, A1, A2, S1, C1, and M1 retain approximately their factual availability except where an intervention explicitly reserves or advances work.

4. **behavioral** — A provisional C1 reservation can be honored, released, or preempted through an identifiable scheduling policy rather than being an informal promise.

5. **quality** — D2’s extra clarification propensity for handwritten documents is detectable through document-defect and recheck events rather than silently appearing as correct work.

6. **quality** — K-337’s unresolved discrepancy can legally be addressed by rejection or an explicit conditional approval, rather than requiring further evidence in every circumstance.

7. **interference** — A weekly rollout changes queue composition and priority displacement, especially at C1 and A2, even when individual resource service times remain unchanged.

8. **structural** — The K-411 lock workaround can create separate identity and document ownership without corrupting shared case state or allowing retrospective timestamp fabrication.
