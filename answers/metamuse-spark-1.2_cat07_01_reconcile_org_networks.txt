# 1. Network-by-network interpretation
- **handover-of-work network** *(pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network)*: Measures directed succession: A completes activity, B is next resource to work on same case. Does not require collaboration.
  - *Implies for Mira_Coordinator*: She is a dominant hub. Out-degree 0.82, in-degree 0.51, betweenness 0.77 are far above TeamLeads and adjusters. Top edges: Bot_Rules -> Mira (920), Mira -> 4 adjusters (total ~1250), Mira -> specialists (390), specialists/Temps -> Mira (520). This is consistent with a **routing/dispatch function** downstream of automation. It does **not** imply she does most of the work (only 960 events / 905 cases vs Bot_Rules 4750), nor that she co-works cases for long, nor that handover is value-adding vs. rework loop. High betweenness here reflects position on exception paths, not necessarily overall volume. Cannot infer formality of role or decision quality from edge frequency alone; thresholding and lifecycle:transition handling matter.
- **working-together network** *(pm4py.discover_working_together_network)*: Measures undirected co-occurrence: two resources appear on the same case at any point, normalized. Measures shared case membership, not direct interaction.
  - *Implies for Mira_Coordinator*: Peripheral despite handover centrality: weighted degree 0.16 vs Alice 0.46, Carla 0.49. Pairwise co-case rates: Mira-Carla 8%, Mira-Alice 5%, Mira-Bruno 4% vs Alice-Carla 27%, Bruno-Diego 26%. This does **not** imply she is uninvolved or unimportant. It implies her involvement is **transient, short, and non-overlapping**: a few minutes of routing/assignment/reopen per case, then she disappears for several events (as observed). Working-together penalizes brief touches; a coordinator who touches many cases briefly will have low co-occurrence density because partners do not overlap repeatedly on the same cases. It also does **not** imply poor collaboration, just a different temporal footprint than Team North/South dyads who co-own a case from Assess to Approve. Cannot distinguish intentional triage from queue-cleaning without duration/content analysis.
- **subcontracting network** *(pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network, n=2)*: Measures A -> B -> A within 2 steps: A hands off to B and soon resumes work on same case. Captures delegation-with-return, supervision, or exception loop.
  - *Implies for Mira_Coordinator*: Very high subcontracting betweenness 0.69 vs 0.19-0.21 for TeamLeads. Patterns: Mira->Omar->Mira 126, Mira->Priya->Mira 98. This indicates she **initiates specialist work and retakes control** to reassign or reopen, not just fire-and-forget. For Alice/Bruno, subcontracting is Carla/D Jefe -> Approve loop (internal approval cycle). For Mira, it is cross-boundary: exception desk -> specialist -> exception desk. Does **not** imply hierarchy or subcontracting in contractual sense, nor that she supervises content. Could be formal exception management, broker pattern, or artifact of logging Assign Specialist as separate event. Must not be read as productivity.
- **performance/time statistics** *(pm4py.get_service_time, pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time)*:
  - *Implies*: Mira median service 4.2 min (short) but avg case cycle-time contribution high variance. Her cases have exception_flag 0.48 vs 0.21-0.33 for others, 0.62-0.71 for specialists. She touches only 22% of claims but 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints. Absence natural experiment: 6-day absence leaves low-value claims median unchanged, but exception_flag=True 90th percentile rises 8.4 -> 13.9 days with queue accumulation. This suggests **conditional bottleneck**: not system-wide, but critical path for complex/exception segment. Service time low + waiting high implies queuing rather than processing is risk. Does **not** imply she is slow; indeed she is fast per event. Vendor_Verify (2.1h) and Vendor_Pay (1.3h) drive waiting-time component; Bot_Ingest/Bot_Rules drive minimal cycle time. Her high variance suggests heterogeneity of exception cases, not uniform inefficiency.
- **role-discovery or activity-profile similarity** *(pm4py.discover_organizational_roles, pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity)*:
  - *Implies*: Activity distribution distinct: Mira alone combines Request Missing Information + Reopen + Escalate Complaint + Assign Specialist, not clustered with any adjuster (Assess/Negotiate), TeamLead (Triage/Approve), specialist (Medical/Legal), Temp (Validate/Close), or bot. Similarity network would place her as singleton or outlier, not Team North/South. This supports a **specialist coordinator / exception desk role** rather than adjuster or lead. Does **not** imply formal job description matches observed behavior; role mining discovers behavioral clusters, not authority. Vendors likewise cluster separately (single activity). Temp_1/2 similarity high on Validate Coverage but low working-together (3%) indicating parallel, non-collaborative capacity pool.

**Reconciliation of contradiction**: High handover/subcontracting + low working-together is classic **broker / dispatcher signature**, not contradiction. She creates many brief, directed handovers across team boundaries and returns after subcontracted work, but does not stay on case. TeamLeads/adjusters create fewer handovers but stay co-present longer, inflating working-together. Normalized SNA amplifies Mira's centrality on exception subgraph which is small in case count but central topologically between Bot_Rules and all human specialists/adjusters.

# 2. Competing role hypotheses

### H2.1 Formal coordinator / exception desk (as designed)
- **Mechanism**: Bot_Rules automatically routes ~19-31% exception-prone claims (Fraud Screen, missing data) to a human exception desk. Mira performs triage, requests missing information, assigns to Medical/Legal or reassigns to adjusters, and handles Reopen/Complaint formally mandated steps.
- **Supporting**: Bot_Rules->Mira 920 edge is second largest handover; 48% exception rate vs process average; concentration on Reopen/Complaint (64%/71%); return edges from specialists; short service time consistent with routing decision; operational manager view.
- **Limiting / Contradicting**: Formal desk should have documented rules, substitution, and appear in working-together with TeamLeads (5%/4% suggests isolation); Temp->Mira escalations suggest informal overflow handling; no evidence of SLA or decision log reviewed; process owner calls her "queue cleaner" not formal lead.
- **Predictions if true**: Filtering log to `exception_flag=True` with `pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values` should increase Mira betweenness further; variant analysis `pm4py.filter_variants` should show Mira in variants containing Request Missing Information, Escalate, Reopen; activity-based similarity should keep her singleton across time windows; replacement by any trained coordinator should restore exception 90th percentile quickly.

### H2.2 Hidden bottleneck / key-person dependency (single point of failure)
- **Mechanism**: Over time, discretionary routing decisions and tacit knowledge accumulate in one person because automation cannot resolve ambiguous cases and no backup is staffed. Queues for exception cases are effectively single-server.
- **Supporting**: Handover betweenness 0.77, subcontracting betweenness 0.69 far above others; absence experiment shows queue accumulation and +65% rise in p90 for exception cases without affecting normal claims; median service short but cycle contribution high variance implies waiting on her; Hospital of Vendor_Verify/Pay not affected during absence (isolating her).
- **Limiting**: Bottleneck is **segmented**, not systemic (78% of claims, all low-value claims, unaffected); low working-together degree suggests low collaborative load, so capacity may be about decision latency not volume; could be understaffing of role rather than personal dependency.
- **Predictions**: `pm4py.get_service_time` and waiting-time by resource should show waiting before Mira events spikes during high load; `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` computed rolling weekly (time-filtered) should show increasing Mira centrality over 12 weeks; filtered log to period of absence should show longer time between Bot_Rules and next human event and shift of edges Bot_Rules->Alice/Bruno but not to specialists; temporary cross-training would reduce waiting without changing quality metrics.

### H2.3 Organizational boundary spanner / triage broker (informal knowledge hub)
- **Mechanism**: Mira spans structural holes between three silos: automated triage (Bot_Rules), geographic teams (North/South), and specialists (Medical/Legal) + external vendors. She knows "where hard cases should go" without formal authority, matching adjusters' description.
- **Supporting**: Connects to every human cluster (Team North, Team South, both specialists, both temps) while teams themselves are clustered internally (Alice-Carla-Elise, Bruno-Diego-Farid 19-27% co-occurrence). Low working-together but high handovers is textbook broker. Subcontracting return pattern indicates she remains accountable for cross-boundary referral.
- **Limiting**: Broker role may lack formal approval authority (Approve Settlement stays with Alice/Bruno), so cannot close cases alone; peripheral working-together with TeamLeads contradicts sponsor-like broker who would co-decide; could be conflated with data artifact of Assign Specialist event being logged under her.
- **Predictions**: Network analysis `pm4py.discover_network_analysis` with Burt's constraint or `pm4py.save_vis_sna` should show Mira lowest constraint / highest brokerage; activity-profile similarity should place her equidistant from multiple roles; filtering by `region` or `case_type` should show she is the only resource with cross-region handovers; surveying reassignment correctness (rework rate after Mira assignment vs TeamLead assignment) would favor broker if lower rework.

### H2.4 Additional: Data artifact / logging artifact hypothesis
- **Mechanism**: Mira's centrality inflated because routing/assignment actions that other resources do implicitly (e.g., system assignment) are explicitly logged as Mira events (Assign Specialist, Reopen), and because Bot_Rules auto-updates create self-loop-like patterns counted as handover.
- **Supporting**: Short median service (4.2 min) suggests system-assisted clicks; Bot_Rules->Mira->Bot_Rules subcontract 62 cases of Fraud Screen updates suggests system logging loop; Temp->Mira handovers may be escalation button.
- **Limiting**: Absence impact and specialist return pattern not explainable by pure logging; Vendors' waiting times independent.
- **Predictions**: Checking `lifecycle:transition` (complete vs assign vs start) and `concept:name` granularity would collapse some handovers if filtered to only `complete`; replaying with `pm4py.format_dataframe` and keeping only human complete events should sharply drop Mira centrality if artifact.

### H2.5 Additional: Quality gatekeeper / compliance control hypothesis
- **Mechanism**: Mira inserted as pre-specialist check to bundle information and prevent specialist overload / control discretion, as compliance reviewer fears opposite (uncontrolled discretion).
- **Supporting**: Mira median exception 48% and specialist cases 62-71% pass through her; she precedes both specialists.
- **Limiting**: Gatekeeper should have high in-degree from adjusters, but in-degree is mainly Bot_Rules and Temps/Specialists; no approval authority logged.
- **Predictions**: Cases with Mira touch should have lower downstream defect/reopen rate if gatekeeping effective; filtering to `exception_flag=True` and comparing specialist activity count with vs without Mira involvement would test.

# 3. Risk assessment
- **Bottleneck risk**: **High but bounded.** For routine claims, no bottleneck (absence no effect, bots/vendors handle). For exception/complaint/reopen segment (~22% of cases, ~48% exception rate), queueing risk is high: p90 +5.5 days during absence, waiting time accumulates. Not automatic condemnation; central routing can be efficient if staffed.
- **Coordination risk**: **Moderate.** Many cross-team handovers (Mira->Carla/Diego/Elise/Farid ~1220) create coordination overhead: mean 16-19 min handover gaps plus specialist 42-51 min. If Mira absent, fallback Bot_Rules->Alice/Bruno does not route to specialists effectively (no evidence they did during absence), leading to pile-up. No evidence of duplicate work.
- **Key-person risk**: **High.** Betweenness 0.77/0.69 with no observed backup achieving same routing. Absence experiment demonstrates single-server dependency. Human risk (absence, turnover) translates directly to SLA breach for regulated complaints. Calibrated: does not imply irreplaceability, only lack of demonstrated redundancy in 12-week log.
- **Compliance/control risk**: **Moderate, uncertain, not evidence of wrongdoing.** Concentrated discretion (detect problem + select next specialist) violates segregation-of-duties principle in a regulated process, but no log evidence of biased selection or bypass of Approve Settlement (still via Alice/Bruno -> Vendor_Pay). Risk is **control design**: undocumented decision logic, no four-eyes on assignment, potential for inconsistent triage. Requires audit of decision criteria, not accusation.
- **Workload fairness risk**: **Moderate.** Mira handles 960 events with high variance and complaints (emotionally demanding) but adjusters handle ~700-1100 events of longer service (17-24 min). TeamLeads handle ~740-780 approvals. Temps handle 480-510 low-exception (13-14%) routine validations, rarely co-work. Risk is not overload of Mira by volume but emotional/complexity load and uneven exception burden between senior adjusters (Carla/Diego high subcontract involvement) vs juniors. Also risk that Temps are isolated from learning.
- **Data-quality risk**: **Material for interpretation.** Automated resources (Bot_Ingest 4 sec, Bot_Rules 7 sec) and vendors (2.1h, 1.3h) have very different time granularity; mixing without lifecycle filtering can inflate handover counts. If Assign Specialist is logged as Mira completing an activity vs. system event, centrality is logging choice. Vendor_Verify/Verify and Vendor_Pay/Payment have high waiting component; need to distinguish service vs waiting time via `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`. Missing resource anonymization or batch logging could suppress working-together rates. No direct evidence of error, but conclusions must be hedged.

# 5. Recommendation
- **Change immediately (low-regret, no need for more evidence):**
  1. Document and externalize triage rules: codify Mira's decision criteria for Medical/Legal assignment and missing-info requests in a shared playbook and Bot_Rules configuration; add reason code attribute to Assign Specialist events.
  2. Create coverage: cross-train at least one backup (e.g., one TeamLead deputy) and ensure system allows Bot_Rules to route exception cases to backup queue when Mira unavailable; set alert when exception queue waits > 2h mean.
  3. Preserve segregation: require that selection of specialist be logged with distinct detector vs assigner if possible (e.g., Bot_Rules flags, Mira assigns), and ensure Approve Settlement remains with Alice/Bruno per existing handover pattern; do not give Mira payment release authority (already via Vendor_Pay).
  4. Do not ignore automation/vendors: keep Bot_Ingest/Bot_Rules for routine routing and keep Vendor_Verify/Pay integration; do not re-insource verification to reduce Mira load.

- **Monitor (establish metrics/dashboards, review monthly):**
  1. Exception queue health: waiting time before first human after Bot_Rules, p50/p90 cycle time split by exception_flag, queue length for Mira vs TeamLeads, rework rate after Mira assignment.
  2. Network drift: weekly handover betweenness and working-together degree for Mira and TeamLeads; proportion of cases without Mira touch that still reach specialist correctly.
  3. Fairness and burnout: case complexity-weighted workload (exception share * service time) across Carla/Diego/Elise/Farid and Temps, complaint handling distribution.

- **Do not change until more evidence is available:**
  1. Do not remove or reassign Mira_Coordinator. She handles cross-boundary exception flow that bots/vendors/TeamLeads do not currently replace (evidenced by absence pile-up). Removal without replacing coordination with explicit backup + automated rules would worsen specialist overload and formal escalations, as operations manager warns.
  2. Do not restructure teams (e.g., merging Team North/South) or automate Medical/Legal triage solely on centrality metrics; require variant and waiting-time analyses and audit of decision quality first.
  3. Do not institute punitive control on Mira based on compliance worry alone; conduct targeted file review of 30-50 exception cases for assignment consistency before redesigning segregation.

# 6. Short executive summary
Mira_Coordinator is central to how hard claims move, but not to everyday claims. She touches only 22% of cases, yet 64% of reopens and 71% of complaints, mostly after automated triage. She quickly routes work to adjusters or medical/legal specialists and retakes the case afterwards. That creates many handovers but little long-term co-working, which is why she looks highly central in handover maps and peripheral in teamwork maps - a classic broker pattern. When she was absent, routine claims were unaffected but complex claims queued sharply, with the worst 10% taking 5.5 days longer. This indicates a bounded but real key-person and bottleneck risk, plus a compliance risk because one person both spots issues and chooses the next expert without visible backup or documented rules. Immediate action should document her decision rules and train a backup, while monitoring exception queues and workload fairness. Removing her without replacing that cross-team routing would increase delays and specialist overload.
