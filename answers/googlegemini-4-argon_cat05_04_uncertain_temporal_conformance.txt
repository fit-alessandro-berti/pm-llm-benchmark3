## 1. Normalization Assumptions

### 1.1 Mathematical Model of True-Time Intervals
Let $T_{\text{disp}}$ be the displayed timestamp in local time, and let $\Delta_{\text{TZ}}$ be the deterministic time-zone offset converting local wall-clock time to UTC ($T_{\text{UTC\_disp}} = T_{\text{disp}} - \Delta_{\text{TZ}}$).

Per the problem instructions, **timestamp-resolution uncertainty is applied before clock correction**, and $\text{clock\_offset} = t_{\text{recorded}} - t_{\text{true}} \in [a, b]$.
1. **Resolution Step**: A displayed time $T_{\text{UTC\_disp}}$ represents a recorded clock interval $[t_{\text{rec}}, t_{\text{rec}}] = [T_{\text{UTC\_disp}} + r, T_{\text{UTC\_disp}} + r]$.
2. **Clock-Offset Step**: Because $t_{\text{true}} = t_{\text{recorded}} - \text{offset}$, subtracting the interval $[a, b]$ flips the bounds:
   $$t_{\text{true}} \in [t_{\text{rec}} - b,\; t_{\text{rec}} - a] = [T_{\text{UTC\_disp}} + r - b,\; T_{\text{UTC\_disp}} + r - a]$$

For continuous real-time interval arithmetic, floor-to-minute semantics $[0\text{ s}, 59.999\dots\text{ s}]$ are treated as the closed topological closure $[0\text{ s}, 60\text{ s}]$ (width $60\text{ s}$), noting that in strict floating-point representations the upper boundary is open or differs by $1\text{ ms}$. Both exact continuous bounds (`:00` at the top of the next minute) and discrete millisecond bounds (`:59.999`) yield identical classification states across all cases; we write closed intervals to the second for arithmetic clarity while noting the open upper bound where relevant.

### 1.2 Specific Case & Semantic Assumptions
* **Duration Arithmetic**: For an ordered activity pair $(A, B)$ with true-time intervals $I_A = [A, A]$ and $I_B = [B, B]$, the feasible duration interval is $D_{AB} = [B - A,\; B - A]$. If an event has a union of disjoint intervals $I = \bigcup_k I^{(k)}$, durations are computed component-wise across all valid combinations and preserved as a disjoint union unless contiguous/overlapping.
* **SLA vs. Temporal Profile Semantics**:
  * **Hard SLAs** ($S_1 \le 900\text{ s}$, $S_2 \le 2700\text{ s}$, $S_3 \le 129600\text{ s}$) are upper-bound legal/operational thresholds $[0, S_k]$ (assuming causal non-negativity). A case is a `CERTAIN_PASS` iff $D^+ \le S_k$, `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` iff $D^- > S_k$, and `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` iff $D^- \le S_k < D^+$.
  * **Temporal Profiles** are two-sided statistical bands $[\mu - \zeta\sigma,\; \mu + \zeta\sigma]$. `CERTAIN_PASS` iff $[D^-, D^+] \subseteq [\mu - \zeta\sigma, \mu + \zeta\sigma]$. `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` iff $[D^-, D^+] \cap [\mu - \zeta\sigma, \mu + \zeta\sigma] = \emptyset$. `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` iff they partially overlap.
* **T-404 (Missing `Investigation_Start`, Signed Work Record)**: `Investigation_Complete` occurred on HUB at `10:35:00` $\implies [10:34:58.5, 10:35:01.5]$. The signed work record bounds the actual duration $d_{\text{inv}} \in [1080\text{ s}, 1500\text{ s}]$ ($[18\text{ m}, 25\text{ m}]$). Because $t_{\text{start}} = t_{\text{complete}} - d_{\text{inv}}$, we subtract the duration interval from the completion interval:
  $$I_{\text{start}} = [10:34:58.5 - 25\text{ m},\; 10:35:01.5 - 18\text{ m}] = [\text{10:09:58.5},\; \text{10:17:01.5}].$$
* **T-405 (DST Fold & Ingestion Order)**: On `2025-10-26`, Europe/Berlin transitions from CEST (`UTC+2`, `fold=0`) to CET (`UTC+1`, `fold=1`) at `03:00` local time, repeating the `02:00–02:59` hour. Without fold metadata, a local time $t_{\text{loc}} \in [02:00, 02:59]$ maps to a disjoint union of two UTC intervals separated by $3600\text{ s}$. However, the ingestion sequence strictly orders `Alert_Received` (`02:50`) $<$ `Investigation_Start` (`02:05`) $<$ `Containment_Complete` (`02:42`).
  * Because `02:05` is earlier on the wall clock than `02:50` by $45\text{ minutes}$ (far exceeding the maximum $3\text{-minute}$ clock-uncertainty window of LEGACY-BER), `Alert_Received` **must** have occurred in `fold=0` (`CEST`, `UTC+2`) and `Investigation_Start` **must** have occurred in `fold=1` (`CET`, `UTC+1`).
  * Because `Containment_Complete` (`02:42`) follows `Investigation_Start` (`02:05` in `fold=1`), it must also be in `fold=1` (`CET`, `UTC+1`). Thus, ingestion sequence uniquely resolves the DST fold ambiguity for all three events! (We also note what would happen without ingestion order in Section 2).
* **T-406 (Mobile Entry Delay Semantics)**: The user-entered timestamp `13:14` on `MOBILE-LON` may have been entered up to $5\text{ minutes}$ ($300\text{ s}$) after the actual start of the investigation. We distinguish two interpretive models:
  * **Primary Model (Occurrence-Time Shift via Late Logging)**: The user clicked "Start Investigation" in the mobile app up to $5\text{ minutes}$ after actually beginning work, so the app recorded the current mobile device clock at the moment of data entry ($t_{\text{entry}} = t_{\text{occurrence}} + d_{\text{delay}}$, where $d_{\text{delay}} \in [0, 300\text{ s}]$). Thus $t_{\text{occurrence}} = t_{\text{entry}} - d_{\text{delay}}$, which shifts the lower bound of true occurrence time earlier by an additional $300\text{ s}$: net shift $[-630\text{ s}, +330\text{ s}]$ ($[-10.5\text{ m}, +5.5\text{ m}]$).
  * **Secondary Model (Record-Time Only / Pure Memory Transcription)**: The user typed `13:14` from memory $5\text{ minutes}$ later, meaning the delay only affected when the database row was committed, not the displayed value `13:14` (net shift $[-330\text{ s}, +330\text{ s}]$). We evaluate both and show that in **both** models, T-406 results in `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` for S1 and S2, though the interval widths differ.
* **T-407 (Missing `Alert_Received` & Causal Constraint)**: `Alert_Sent` is logged at `14:00:00` HUB ($[13:59:58.5, 14:00:01.5]$). Network delivery latency is $d_{\text{net}} \in [0, 1200\text{ s}]$. We evaluate two distinct semantic stances:
  * **Model A (Strict Evidential / Uncoupled)**: Without a receipt acknowledgment, `Alert_Received` is unverified; or if assumed delivered via network bounds alone, $I_{\text{rec}} = [13:59:58.5, 14:20:01.5]$. Because `Investigation_Start` is $[14:16:58.5, 14:17:01.5]$, pure network bounds allow $D_{\text{S1}} \in [-183\text{ s}, +1023\text{ s}]$.
  * **Model B (Causal Truncation)**: Applying the domain constraint $t_{\text{Alert\_Received}} \le t_{\text{Investigation\_Start}}$ truncates the upper bound of `Alert_Received` at `14:17:01.5`, yielding $I_{\text{rec}} = [13:59:58.5, 14:17:01.5]$ and bounding $D_{\text{S1}} \ge 0\text{ s}$, i.e., $D_{\text{S1}} \in [0\text{ s}, 1023\text{ s}]$. We report both variants explicitly.
* **T-409 (Missing NTP Diagnostics / Weak Engineering Estimate)**: For `OPS-BER` at `18:04 CEST`, normal NTP bounds $[-90\text{ s}, +120\text{ s}]$ are absent; only a weak engineering estimate $[-720\text{ s}, +720\text{ s}]$ ($[-12\text{ m}, +12\text{ m}]$) exists. Combining floor-to-minute $[0, 60\text{ s}]$ with $[-720\text{ s}, +720\text{ s}]$ gives a shift of $[-720\text{ s}, +780\text{ s}]$ ($[-12\text{ m}, +13\text{ m}]$).

# 2. INTERVAL NORMALIZATION ENGINE (WITH DST FOLD & INFERENCE SUPPORT)

def parse_local_to_utc(dt_str: str, tz_name: str, fold: int = 0) -> datetime:
    fmt = "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S" if len(dt_str.split(":")) == 3 else "%Y-%m-%d %H:%M"
    naive = datetime.strptime(dt_str, fmt)
    local_dt = naive.replace(tzinfo=ZoneInfo(tz_name), fold=fold)
    return local_dt.astimezone(timezone.utc)


def compute_base_interval(dt_str: str, sys_name: str, fold: int = 0, custom_offset=None, extra_shift=(0.0, 0.0)):
    meta = SYSTEM_META[sys_name]
    utc_disp = parse_local_to_utc(dt_str, meta["tz"], fold=fold)
    r_min, r_max = meta["res"]
    a, b = custom_offset if custom_offset is not None else meta["offset"]
    e_min, e_max = extra_shift
    # true_time = recorded_time - clock_offset => [utc_disp + r_min - b + e_min, utc_disp + r_max - a + e_max]
    low = utc_disp + timedelta(seconds=(r_min - b + e_min))
    high = utc_disp + timedelta(seconds=(r_max - a + e_max))
    return (low, high)


def normalize_case_events(raw_df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict:
    """
    Returns dict: case_id -> activity -> list of (utc_low, utc_high) tuples (representing union of intervals).
    """
    normalized = {}
    for case_id, group in raw_df.groupby("case_id", sort=False):
        normalized[case_id] = {}
        rows = group.sort_values("seq_order").to_dict("records")

        if case_id == "T-405":
            # Represent both DST folds first, then prune via ingestion sequence order
            cand = {}
            for r in rows:
                act = r["activity"]
                cand[act] = [
                    (0, compute_base_interval(r["recorded"], r["system"], fold=0)),
                    (1, compute_base_interval(r["recorded"], r["system"], fold=1)),
                ]
            # Prune fold combinations (f_alert, f_inv, f_cont) where intervals cannot satisfy Alert <= Inv <= Cont
            valid_combos = []
            for f1, i1 in cand["Alert_Received"]:
                for f2, i2 in cand["Investigation_Start"]:
                    for f3, i3 in cand["Containment_Complete"]:
                        if i1[0] <= i2[1] and i2[0] <= i3[1]:
                            valid_combos.append((i1, i2, i3))
            normalized[case_id]["Alert_Received"] = sorted(list({c[0] for c in valid_combos}))
            normalized[case_id]["Investigation_Start"] = sorted(list({c[1] for c in valid_combos}))
            normalized[case_id]["Containment_Complete"] = sorted(list({c[2] for c in valid_combos}))
            continue

        for r in rows:
            act, rec, sys_name, notes = r["activity"], r["recorded"], r["system"], r["notes"]
            if notes == "infer_start_18_25m":
                # Keep Investigation_Complete and infer Investigation_Start = Complete - [18m, 25m]
                comp_iv = compute_base_interval(rec, sys_name)
                normalized[case_id][act] = [comp_iv]
                start_iv = (comp_iv[0] - timedelta(minutes=25), comp_iv[1] - timedelta(minutes=18))
                normalized[case_id]["Investigation_Start"] = [start_iv]
            elif notes == "late_entry_5m":
                # Primary model: user entry delay [0, 300s] means occurrence was up to 300s earlier
                iv = compute_base_interval(rec, sys_name, extra_shift=(-300.0, 0.0))
                normalized[case_id][act] = [iv]
            elif notes == "net_latency_0_20m":
                sent_iv = compute_base_interval(rec, sys_name)
                normalized[case_id][act] = [sent_iv]
                # Proxy Alert_Received with [0, 20m] network delivery latency
                rec_iv = (sent_iv[0], sent_iv[1] + timedelta(minutes=20))
                normalized[case_id]["Alert_Received"] = [rec_iv]
            elif notes == "weak_ntp_12m":
                iv = compute_base_interval(rec, sys_name, custom_offset=(-720.0, 720.0))
                normalized[case_id][act] = [iv]
            else:
                iv = compute_base_interval(rec, sys_name)
                normalized[case_id][act] = [iv]

        # Apply causal cap for T-407 (Alert_Received <= Investigation_Start) and T-409 (Alert_Received <= Investigation_Start)
        if case_id == "T-407":
            a_iv = normalized[case_id]["Alert_Received"][0]
            i_iv = normalized[case_id]["Investigation_Start"][0]
            normalized[case_id]["Alert_Received"] = [(a_iv[0], min(a_iv[1], i_iv[1]))]
        if case_id == "T-409":
            a_iv = normalized[case_id]["Alert_Received"][0]
            i_iv = normalized[case_id]["Investigation_Start"][0]
            normalized[case_id]["Investigation_Start"] = [(max(i_iv[0], a_iv[0]), i_iv[1])]

    return normalized


# ==============================================================================
# 3. DURATION & CONFORMANCE CLASSIFICATION FUNCTIONS
# ==============================================================================
def duration_intervals(ivs_a, ivs_b):
    """Computes list of feasible duration intervals (in seconds) between two interval unions."""
    res = []
    for a_low, a_high in ivs_a:
        for b_low, b_high in ivs_b:
            d_min = (b_low - a_high).total_seconds()
            d_max = (b_high - a_low).total_seconds()
            res.append((d_min, d_max))
    return res


def classify_interval(d_ivs, low_bound, high_bound):
    all_pass = True
    all_viol = True
    for d_min, d_max in d_ivs:
        if d_min >= low_bound and d_max <= high_bound:
            all_viol = False
        elif d_max < low_bound or d_min > high_bound:
            all_pass = False
        else:
            all_pass = False
            all_viol = False
    if all_pass:
        return "CERTAIN_PASS"
    if all_viol:
        return "CERTAIN_VIOLATION"
    return "POSSIBLE_VIOLATION"

for zeta in [1, 2, 3]:
    pm4py_flagged = set()
    if PM4PY_AVAILABLE:
        # Note on PM4Py return shape: conformance_temporal_profile returns a list of lists of violating
        # tuples (one list per trace). Each tuple has shape: (act1, act2, duration_sec, z_score).
        conf_res = pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(

    diffs = []
    for cid, acts in norm_events.items():

                        f"  [zeta={zeta}] {cid} ({act_a[:5]}->{act_b[:5]}): PM4Py={mid_class:18s} vs Interval={iv_class} (d in [{d_ivs[0][0]:.1f}s, {d_ivs[0][1]:.1f}s])"
                    )
    print(f"Discrepancies at zeta={zeta} ({len(diffs)} cases where midpoint hides uncertainty):")
    for d in diffs:
        print(d)

### Key Diagnostic Insight from Midpoint vs. Interval Comparison
When collapsing intervals to midpoints, `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` evaluates only a single point duration $d_{\text{mid}}$. Consequently:
1. **False Reassurance (`MIDPOINT_PASS` vs. `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`)**: At $\zeta=2$, midpoint approximation marks **T-401 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=855\text{ s}$)**, **T-403 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=930\text{ s}$)**, **T-404 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=810\text{ s}$)**, **T-405 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=900\text{ s}$)**, **T-406 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=690\text{ s}$)**, **T-407 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=511.5\text{ s}$)**, and **T-409 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=510.75\text{ s}$)** as completely conformant (`MIDPOINT_PASS`), masking the fact that every single one of those cases has feasible durations exceeding $\mu + 2\sigma = 960\text{ s}$ (or falling below $\mu - 2\sigma = 240\text{ s}$).
2. **Over-confident Violation (`MIDPOINT_VIOLATION` vs. `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`)**: At $\zeta=2$, midpoint approximation flags **T-402 (`Alert->Inv`, $d_{\text{mid}}=1095\text{ s}$)** as a definite violation, overlooking the $1.5\text{ s}$ feasible overlap $[958.5, 960.0]\text{ s}$ where the case passes.
3. **Boundary Blindness**: In **T-408 (`Inv->Cont`)**, the midpoint duration is exactly $3300.0\text{ s} = \mu + 2\sigma$, so PM4Py passes it at $\zeta=2$, whereas HUB's $\pm 1.5\text{ s}$ uncertainty yields $[3297.0, 3303.0]\text{ s}$ (`POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`).

## 7. Governance Recommendation

To prevent timestamp ambiguity, clock skew, lifecycle conflation, and DST artifacts from undermining legal SLA enforcement and statistical process mining, incident-response logging architecture should adopt the following seven controls:

1. **Mandatory UTC Storage & ISO-8601 Dual-Timestamp Schema**:
   * All event stores must persist timestamps in **UTC** (`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.ffffffZ`) or integer epoch nanoseconds/microseconds.
   * Every event record must separate three distinct temporal attributes:
     * `occurrence_time_utc`: Best estimate of when the business activity occurred;
     * `recorded_clock_raw`: Exact literal string/value produced by the source device (preserving original time zone and resolution for forensic audit);
     * `ingested_time_utc`: High-trust central ingestion timestamp assigned by `HUB` upon receipt.
2. **Resolution Metadata & Prohibition of Pre-Storage Truncation**:
   * Systems like `OPS-BER`, `VENDOR-NY`, and `LEGACY-BER` must stop truncating timestamps to the minute before persistence. Where legacy or third-party sources only emit coarse timestamps, the ingestion pipeline must tag each event with explicit resolution metadata (`resolution_unit: "1m"`, `rounding_mode: "FLOOR" | "NEAREST"`).
3. **Inline Clock-Offset Telemetry & Heartbeat Cross-Calibration**:
   * Every log collector agent should append current NTP/PTP synchronization telemetry (`ntp_offset_ms`, `root_dispersion_ms`, `stratum`, `sync_status`) to event batches.
   * If NTP telemetry is unavailable (as in `T-409`) or untrusted (`VENDOR-NY`, `MOBILE-LON`), the `HUB` ingestion gateway must compute an empirical upper bound on clock offset by comparing the source's `recorded_clock_raw` against `HUB`'s `ingested_time_utc` (since causal network latency $\ge 0$ guarantees $t_{\text{true\_occurrence}} \le t_{\text{HUB\_ingested}}$).
4. **Explicit Lifecycle Semantics (`Start` vs. `Complete` vs. `Sent` vs. `Received`)**:
   * Enforce XES standard lifecycle transitions (`lifecycle:transition = start | complete`). `SENSOR` events (`T-401`) and work records (`T-404`) must never silently substitute completion events for start events.
   * For asynchronous messaging (`T-407`), require two-phase logging: `Alert_Sent` (sender), `Alert_Delivered` (transport ACK), and `Alert_Acknowledged` (human/system read), with explicit SLA definitions specifying which event starts the legal timer.
5. **DST Fold Disambiguation (`PEP 495` / IANA Compliance)**:
   * Any legacy system unable to emit UTC directly (`LEGACY-BER` in `T-405`) must log the numeric UTC offset (`+02:00` vs. `+01:00`) or boolean `dst_fold` (`0` vs. `1`) alongside local wall-clock time, coupled with a strictly monotonic sequence number (`seq_id`) per case.
6. **Causal Provenance Identifiers (`event_id` & `parent_event_id`)**:
   * Require Lamport timestamps or vector clocks / parent-child causal IDs (`parent_event_id`) linking `Investigation_Start` to the specific `Alert_Received` event that triggered it. This makes causal ordering constraints ($t_{\text{Alert\_Received}} \le t_{\text{Investigation\_Start}}$) a verified structural property of the log rather than an analyst assumption.
7. **Enforcement Policy for Uncertain Intervals (Legal SLAs vs. Statistical Monitoring)**:
   * **Contractual / Punitive SLA Enforcement (`Burden of Proof` Rule)**: An SLA penalty or compliance breach may only be enforced on a **`CERTAIN_VIOLATION`** ($D^- > \text{SLA}$), where every feasible true-time realization violates the threshold.
   * **Operational Quality & Telemetry Alerting (`Precautionary` Rule)**: Any **`POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`** ($D^- \le \text{SLA} < D^+$) or **`INDETERMINATE`** finding must trigger an automated **Data Quality & Observability Defect** against the logging infrastructure owner (e.g., fixing `OPS-BER` NTP or `VENDOR-NY` sync), while flagging the incident for manual supervisory review without automatic contractual penalty.
