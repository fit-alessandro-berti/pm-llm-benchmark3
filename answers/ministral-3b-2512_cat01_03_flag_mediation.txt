- **Assignment:** Randomly assign 30% of flagged cases to protected queue (e.g., `reviewer=M2` reserved for 2h/day).
- **Guardrail:** Cap protected queue at 5 cases/hour.

- **Data:** Need timestamps for manual review start/end and reviewer assignment.

### **8. Reversal conditions**
1. If `risk_score` perfectly predicts underlying risk, suppressing the flag (P1) would have no effect.
2. If reviewers handle flagged cases in a shared queue without capacity constraints, P3’s protected queue may not improve cycle times.
3. If manual review decisions improve outcomes (e.g., flagged cases are harder to resolve), P2 (removing review) could worsen quality.
4. If timestamps are local and daylight saving affects triage times, reversing time zones could invert observed delays.
5. If reviewers rotate assignments, P3’s protected queue may not be sustainable.
6. If unobserved case severity correlates with `complexity`, reversing complexity thresholds could invert observed delays.

---

### **9. Final synthesis**
**Observed:** Flagged cases complete triage faster but experience longer downstream delays due to mandatory manual review and escalation queues. High-complexity cases show proportional delays, and queue interference exacerbates cycle times.

**Plausible mechanism:** The flag triggers a **policy bundle** (priority triage + mandatory review + escalation), where downstream delays are mediated by shared queues and reviewer capacity. The triage effect is direct, but the cycle-time effect is mediated through manual review and escalation.

**Unidentified:** Whether the flag’s risk score is a proxy for unobserved severity, whether reviewer fatigue or resource allocation biases outcomes, and whether the observed delays are causal or due to structural queue constraints.

**Preferred intervention:** **P3 (protected queue for flagged cases)** balances cycle-time reduction with quality compliance, assuming reviewers can handle the load. A pilot with capacity guardrails would test this empirically.
