# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Management
**From:** Process Optimization Team
**Date:** October 26, 2023
**Subject:** Strategic Decision on Fiber Installation Redesign R

**Recommendation:** Do not scale Redesign R nationally. Instead, adopt a **Targeted Hybrid (Option E)**: deploy coordinator ownership only for low-risk/simple segments, suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation, and run targeted measurement before wider scale.

**The Trade-off:**
Redesign R improves speed for standard cases but degrades reliability for complex cases. The median cycle time dropped from 14.2 to 10.8 days, but the p95 duration worsened from 45 to 62 days. While visible costs fell (EUR 184 to EUR 161), estimated costs including complaints rose (EUR 198 to EUR 207). The process-mining model indicates high fitness (0.94) but critically low precision (0.51), suggesting the redesign allows non-compliant paths (e.g., billing before activation) that threaten customer trust and compliance.

**Rationale:**
1.  **Segmentation:** Simple houses benefit significantly (median -6 days), but apartments and old buildings suffer (median +1 day, complaints +7%). The "skip survey" rule failed for complex addresses.
2.  **Resilience:** Coordinator utilization is 93%. Single points of failure increase stall risk when coordinators are absent.
3.  **Hidden Costs:** Rework (manual activation 7%  15%) and complaints (6.1%  9.8%) indicate that short-term speed gains are offset by downstream friction.
4.  **Compliance:** Alignment diagnostics show frequent instances of billing before activation and missing permit checks for sensitive buildings.

**Next Steps:**
1.  **Pause:** National rollout and survey-skipping rules.
2.  **Deploy:** Coordinator ownership for simple/low-risk orders only.
3.  **Measure:** Validate risk rules and capture full downstream costs.
4.  **Threshold:** Rollout only if p95 duration reverts to baseline or complaint rate drops below 7% in a controlled experiment.

**Conclusion:** We must prioritize customer reliability and compliance over median speed. A targeted approach allows us to keep the efficiency gains while mitigating the tail-risk exposure.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

*   **High fitness but low precision:** The process-mining model "fits" the data well because it captures the new simplified path (fitness 0.94). However, it has low precision (0.51) because it allows too many divergent paths (e.g., billing before activation) that violate standard process logic, indicating the redesign introduces ambiguity rather than just speed.
*   **Improved median but worse tail:** The redesign optimizes the "easy" path (simple houses), reducing median time. However, it removes safety nets (surveys, permits) for complex cases, causing them to stall or fail, inflating the p95 and p90 durations.
*   **Fewer handovers but lower resilience:** Consolidating cases under one coordinator reduces administrative handovers (12% vs 34% with 3+ handovers). However, this creates a bottleneck; when coordinators are absent, cases stall because context is not shared, reducing system resilience.
*   **Lower visible cost but higher rework:** Direct installation costs dropped (EUR 184  EUR 161), but the rise in manual activation and complaints suggests downstream costs (revisits, complaint handling) are increasing, pushing the estimated total cost higher (EUR 207).
*   **Simple houses vs. Apartments:** The redesign relies on a risk-prediction rule. For simple houses, the rule is accurate, and efficiency gains are realized. For apartments/old buildings, the rule is inaccurate (incorrectly skipping surveys), leading to missed permits and activation failures.

# 4. Decision alternatives

*   **A. Scale Redesign R nationally:** High risk. Ignores tail degradation, compliance breaches, and segment-specific failures. Likely to increase downstream costs and customer complaints.
*   **B. Roll back to baseline:** Conservative. Preserves stability but forfeits efficiency gains for the 34% of simple houses that benefit significantly.
*   **C. Scale coordinator-ownership only:** Moderate risk. Improves accountability but does not address the flawed survey-skipping logic or activation timing which drive the rework.
*   **D. Run controlled regional experiment:** Necessary but slow. Provides data but delays value capture. Best used to validate specific risk rules.
*   **E. Hybrid (Targeted deployment):** **Recommended.** Captures simple-house gains while containing risk. Allows measurement of risk rules before scaling.

# 6. Final recommendation

**Immediate Action:** Implement **Option E (Targeted Hybrid)**. Deploy the coordinator model strictly for simple, low-risk houses. Suspend the survey-skipping rule and post-billing activation for all other cases.

**What to Pause:** National rollout of Redesign R. Freeze the "skip survey" predictive rule pending M6 validation.

**What to Scale:** Coordinator ownership for the 34% simple-house segment where median time improved and complaints dropped.

**What to Measure:** Prioritize **M6** (Validate building-risk predictive rule) and **M3** (Structured reason codes for manual activation/complaints).

**Threshold for Broader Rollout:** Only expand to medium-risk segments if the p95 cycle time remains within 5% of baseline (45 days) and the complaint rate drops below 7% in the next 8 weeks of targeted measurement.
