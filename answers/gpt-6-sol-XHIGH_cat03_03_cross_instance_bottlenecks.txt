## 1. Interaction-graph description

The relevant graph is a typed, time-stamped network, not six disjoint Job traces. Nodes are Jobs, Components, ComponentLots, Batches, Machines, Tools, and Shipments; event nodes can be retained to distinguish one shared event from several copied case events. Directed business edges include `requires`, `contains`, `batches`, and `consolidates`. Event participation adds time-specific `uses`, `holds`, `cures-on`, `tests-with`, `quarantines`, and `releases` links. Direction on a lot–job interaction is a modeling convention, not proof that one object caused the other's delay.

Compact adjacency list (parentheses give qualifiers):

- J1→C1, J2→C2, J3→C3, J4→C4, J5→C5, J6→C6 (`requires`); L7→{C2,C5}, L8→{C1,C3} (`contains`).
- B1→{J1,J2,J3}, B2→{J4,J5} (`batches`); both B1 and B2→{OVEN-1,RACK-7} (`cure uses`).
- S1→{J1,J4}, S2→{J2,J3}, S3→{J5,J6} (`consolidates`).
- J3→FIX-A (`holds`, 09:00–11:40); J4→{CNC-2,FIX-A} (`Machine Process`, 11:45); J5 and J6→{TEST-1,CAL-3} (`Final Test`, 16:10–16:35 and 17:25–17:50).
- The 10:20 `Quarantine Lot` and 13:50 `Release Lot` event nodes→{L7,C2,C5,J2,J5} (`participates` in the supplied object-centric account). The original Job extraction omits those events; another integration pipeline would attach them to J2 and J5.

Three important higher-order motifs are: (1) {J1,J2,J3, their Components, B1, OVEN-1, RACK-7}, where one cure start synchronizes three otherwise separate jobs; (2) {J3,FIX-A,CNC-2,J4,C4}, where a fixture hold can block J4 despite CNC-2 capacity; and (3) {L7,C2/J2,C5/J5,B1/B2}, where a lot policy couples jobs in different batches and J2's override breaks the apparent common gate. Further motifs join {J5,J6,CAL-3,TEST-1,S3} and {J1,J4,S1,B1/B2}. The O2O rows, shared event memberships, acquire/release records, readiness stamps, and timestamps are direct observations as supplied. Edges such as “J4 waits for FIX-A,” “J1 waits for J4,” or “L7 release causes J5's start” are inferred dependencies requiring counterfactual checks. The absence of a `Wait for Tool` event is not evidence of no wait.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

Every figure below is elapsed clock time under a stated counterfactual, not an identified causal effect. Readiness quality, feasible recipes, schedules, and priorities are unverified.

1. **B1, J1:** 08:10→12:05 is **3 h 55 min**. If J1 truly could be cured independently immediately on readiness with a compatible oven and rack, that is its potential synchronization-related wait. If the only avoidable part is waiting for the last partner, J3 becomes ready at 11:55, giving **3 h 45 min** of partner-wait plus **10 min** before the batch starts.
2. **B1, J2:** 08:25→12:05 is **3 h 40 min** under the same independent-cure counterfactual. Relative to J3's 11:55 readiness, **3 h 30 min** precedes the last partner and **10 min** follows. J1 and J2 together accumulate **7 h 35 min of job-wait**; that sum is not 7 h 35 min of oven time saved. J2's L7 override and any recipe restrictions could invalidate the independent-start scenario.
3. **B2, J4 and RACK-7:** J4's 13:10→14:50 wait is **1 h 40 min**. If B2 requires J5, 13:10→14:40 (**1 h 30 min**) is partner synchronization and 14:40→14:50 (**10 min**) is additional start slack. Under a separate counterfactual in which both jobs were ready and oven capacity was available at 12:55, the rack's cleaning/quarantine through 14:35 could make up to **1 h 40 min** of the 12:55→14:50 oven-idle interval infeasible for rack-dependent curing. These are overlapping explanations, not additive delays.
4. **FIX-A, J4:** Arrival at CNC-2 at 09:50→process start 11:45 is **1 h 55 min**. Assuming J4 was otherwise eligible, CNC-2 could process it, and FIX-A was required throughout, J3's hold until 11:40 makes **1 h 50 min** a plausible fixture-related upper portion, with **5 min** for handoff/setup. The recorded 10:05→10:47 CNC-2 idle spell is **42 min** within that interval; it supports a mismatch between available machine and unavailable fixture but does not prove the whole wait was fixture-caused.
5. **S1, J1:** Pack at 13:30→dispatch at 19:25 is **5 h 55 min**. Assuming J1 could ship alone at pack completion, this is potential consolidation-related dwell. J4 packs at 19:10, so **5 h 40 min** elapses before the last partner is packed and **15 min** from that point to dispatch. A realistic independent-shipment counterfactual also needs a carrier cutoff and loading time; the full 5 h 55 min may not be removable.
6. **CAL-3, J6:** Ready at 16:05→test start at 17:25 is **1 h 20 min**. J5 occupies the tester through 16:35. If a five-minute handoff were normal and a calibrated spare were available, J6 could start at 16:40; the 16:40→17:25 difference is **45 min**, equal to the 16:35→17:20 recalibration interval shifted by the handoff. The full TEST-1 idle interval is **50 min** (16:35→17:25), but five minutes may be routine handoff. Tester availability, priority, and the necessity of recalibration remain to be checked.
7. **L7:** 10:20 quarantine→13:50 release is **3 h 30 min** of policy exposure. If both jobs were otherwise ready and neither had an override, that is a maximum window in which a hold could affect them. J2 did proceed through B1 before release under an override, and J5's actual readiness relative to the hold is not given; **3 h 30 min is not an observed delay** for either job.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job:** Shows Release→Stage→Process→Inspect→Pack→Dispatch and rework, including each job's elapsed transitions. It hides the identities and readiness of batch mates, tools, lots, and shipment partners; a shared cure can be copied into three traces, and the specified extraction drops L7 events. The normal-looking DFG and lack of a 1.8-standard-deviation outlier misleadingly suggest independent, routine flow.
- **Machine:** Shows setups, cures/tests, recorded idle and utilization. It hides whether a machine is *usable* when FIX-A, RACK-7, or CAL-3 is held, and whether jobs are ready. Repeated shared events can be mistaken for separate work if joined poorly. “OVEN-1 is 44% utilized, so curing cannot constrain jobs” is invalid.
- **Batch:** Shows B1/B2 membership, a shared start, and potentially earliest/latest readiness after joining Jobs. It hides upstream lot/tool causes of late arrivals and downstream shipment waits; copying job activities into a batch trace can multiply apparent work. A ten-minute last-ready-to-start time would misleadingly imply B1 caused little waiting for J1 and J2.
- **Tool:** Shows FIX-A holds, RACK-7 quarantine, and CAL-3 recalibration if these states are logged. It hides batch/lot policy and the economic cost of the jobs blocked; one cure event may be duplicated across tool views. Low tool-event frequency would misleadingly imply low tool impact.
- **ComponentLot:** Shows L7's quarantine/release and its shared C2/C5 membership. It hides J2's true route unless the separate override is integrated, and may duplicate a lot event into both job traces under another pipeline. A lot DFG that sends both jobs through “release before processing” would be false for J2.
- **Shipment:** Shows three Create→Load→Dispatch traces and short Load→Dispatch times. It hides each member's pack-to-load dwell; a shared dispatch can be duplicated into both job traces. The 0.3 h median Load→Dispatch misleadingly suggests S1 imposed little delay on J1.

Low machine utilization and long job waiting coexist because readiness, tools, batch composition, lot policy, and shipment policy determine *when* capacity is usable. Utilization averages also erase short demand peaks and do not measure queues. An idle machine can be the wrong feasible resource for the waiting job.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**B1 to S1:** Temporal precedence: J1/C1 is ready at 08:10; J2/C2 at 08:25; J3/C3 at 11:55; one B1–OVEN-1–RACK-7 cure starts at 12:05. J1 packs at 13:30 and S1 dispatches with J4 at 19:25. Resource feasibility: one shared cure event does not establish three independent oven slots; RACK-7 availability before 12:05 is unknown. Policy: B1 batching and S1 consolidation link J1 to J3 and later to J4; J2 uses an L7 override before 13:50. Causal speculation: splitting B1 might advance J1, while S1 could still hold its dispatch until J4. Thus improving oven-side flow need not improve J1's delivery time.

**Fixture to later shipment:** Temporal precedence: J3 holds FIX-A from 09:00–11:40; J4/C4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50, starts at 11:45, is ready for B2 at 13:10, packs at 19:10, and leaves in S1 at 19:25. Resource feasibility: J4's process uses both CNC-2 and FIX-A; the 10:05–10:47 machine idle period shows capacity but not complete process eligibility. Policy: B2 requires its partner J5 and RACK-7. Causal speculation: a second fixture could advance J4's CNC step, but B2 could remain gated by J5 and rack cleaning, and S1's departure may follow a carrier schedule anyway.

**Lot, batch, test, shipment:** Temporal precedence: L7 containing C5 (and C2) is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50; J5 is B2-ready at 14:40; B2 cures using OVEN-1/RACK-7 at 14:50. J5 tests with TEST-1/CAL-3 at 16:10–16:35, CAL-3 is recalibrated until 17:20, and J6 tests at 17:25–17:50; J5 and J6 share S3. Resource feasibility: lot release and rack status constrain different stages, while CAL-3 is required even when TEST-1 is idle. Policy: L7 hold, B2 composition, calibration protocol, and S3 consolidation are distinct gates. Causal speculation: faster lot release might move J5 earlier but could increase B2's wait for J4; faster J6 testing might change S3 departure only if J6 is its last packed member. Pack times for S3 are missing, so that final link is unproven.

## 6. Intervention portfolio and spillovers

Evaluate interventions on the *same connected object subgraphs*, not solely the treated job. A useful design is a staggered pilot or matched shift comparison, stratified by recipe, workload, staffing, carrier schedule, and priority; link readiness, resource states, pack times, and dispatch for all incident Jobs and objects.

| Intervention and target | Expected benefit; helped | Potentially harmed; new cost or risk | Perverse metric | Object-centric evaluation design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Allow smaller B1-like batches or a maximum early-member wait; batch synchronization | Earlier cure for J1/J2-like early members | Late partners such as J3 may lose a slot; extra oven cycles, energy, setup, or recipe violations | Average batch fill, which rewards holding early jobs | Compare ready→cure and ready→dispatch for *all* batch members and adjacent batches, plus cycles, quality, and energy |
| Add or pre-stage FIX-A; fixture contention | Earlier feasible CNC start for J4-like jobs | J3-like incumbents may lose priority; purchase, maintenance, and setup cost | FIX-A utilization, which rewards continuous holding | Record requests, grants, releases, CNC states, and downstream B2/S1 times; compare blocked minutes and displacement |
| Add RACK-7 capacity or shorten validated cleaning/quarantine; rack gate | More feasible oven starts, especially B2-like cases | Other batches may compete for OVEN-1; contamination/quality risk and rack cost | OVEN-1 utilization, which can encourage curing before quality clearance | Link rack state, batch readiness, oven starts, quality results, and waits of both current and following batches |
| Make L7 hold decisions and supervisor overrides explicit, with component-level release where safe; lot policy | Less unnecessary delay for J5-like unaffected components | Quality exposure for J2/J5 or rework for downstream batches; inspection effort | Quarantine duration, which rewards premature release | Compare held-component eligibility, approved overrides, defect/rework rates, and wait across every Job and Component in each lot |
| Permit early-ready jobs to use separate shipment slots or a departure cutoff; consolidation | Reduce J1-like packed dwell | Partner J4 may face higher freight cost or less favorable service; more loads and emissions | Packed-to-dispatch time alone, which rewards costly split shipments | Measure per-job packed dwell, delivery time, freight, emissions, and carrier-slot effects across S1 and other shipments |
| Pre-calibrate a spare CAL-3 or schedule recalibration before test peaks; calibration gate | Earlier J6-like tests | J5-like jobs may face calibration queue; spare cost and test-quality risk | TEST-1 uptime, which can suppress necessary calibration | Join tool-validity windows, requests, test starts, failures, and S3 departure; monitor displaced tests |
| Log ready-to-process, tool requests/grants, rack state, lot links, overrides, and pack completion consistently; observability | Better attribution and safer intervention choice for all Jobs | Extra operator entry and integration burden; false timestamps from backfilling | Logging completeness alone, which rewards meaningless events | Audit source timestamps against scans, compare relation coverage across pipelines, and recompute waits under both linkage rules |

## 7. PM4Py analysis outline

The code treats object-centric frequency in two ways: event–object incidences and distinct events/objects. It retains the shared event IDs so that one B1 cure is not silently counted as three oven cures. API arguments and returned graph types can vary by PM4Py version; inspect them before drawing process conclusions.

```python
import pm4py
import pandas as pd

# Structural views; preserve the returned graph objects for inspection/plotting.
interaction_summary = pm4py.ocel_objects_interactions_summary(ocel)
interaction_graph = pm4py.discover_objects_graph(
    ocel, graph_type="object_interaction"
)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)       # object-type interactions
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)     # event-to-object-type relations
ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)   # object-centric directly-follows graph

# Compare frequencies without treating a multi-object event as several events.
rel = ocel.relations[["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:type"]].drop_duplicates()
evt = ocel.events[["ocel:eid", "ocel:activity"]].drop_duplicates()
links = rel.merge(evt, on="ocel:eid", validate="many_to_one")
frequency = (links.groupby(["ocel:type", "ocel:activity"])
             .agg(event_object_links=("ocel:eid", "size"),
                  distinct_events=("ocel:eid", "nunique"),
                  distinct_objects=("ocel:oid", "nunique"))
             .reset_index())
print(frequency.sort_values(["ocel:type", "ocel:activity"]))
print(interaction_summary)

# The conventional view is useful for comparison, with its omissions explicit.
job_log = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, "Job")
job_performance = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(job_log)
job_features = pm4py.extract_ocel_features(ocel, "Job")
print(job_performance)
```

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken **batch synchronization as the top explanation** include: (1) audit scans showing J1's 08:10 or J2's 08:25 “ready” stamp was provisional; (2) a required prior operation for either job completing near 12:05; (3) recipe or regulation proving B1 could not be split or started before J3; (4) OVEN-1 or RACK-7 demonstrably unavailable until near 12:05 for independent reasons; (5) a carrier or downstream gate that would leave end-to-end completion unchanged after earlier curing; (6) many comparable batches with staggered readiness but negligible early-member dwell; (7) J1/J2 deliberately deprioritized for a documented reason independent of B1; or (8) event-clock misalignment that collapses the apparent readiness gap. Evidence that B2's start is explained entirely by validated rack cleaning would also reduce the claimed recurrence of *partner* synchronization, though not the broader rack-gated mechanism.

Discriminating fields/events should include: verified job/component readiness and the source of each timestamp; batch recipe, capacity, minimum-load rule, planned versus actual membership, and planned start; resource-request, queue-entry, grant, acquire, and release for CNC-2/FIX-A and TEST-1/CAL-3; machine state and staffed production calendars; RACK-7 cleaning/quarantine start, clearance, and validation; L7 component-level hold reasons, effective release times, and signed overrides from the supervisor system; dispatch eligibility, pack completion, carrier cutoff, planned departure, and load assignment for each shipment member; priority changes and rework/quality outcomes; and stable event IDs with relation provenance across both integration pipelines. These data would test feasibility and spillovers, rather than merely adding more directly-follows edges.
