# 1. Policy Interpretation and Conflict Resolution

## Material assumptions

1. **Case start and end.** Every complete case starts with `Receive Onboarding Request` and ends with `Close Onboarding`.
2. **One primary outcome.** After `Classify Risk`, a case is either:
   - rejected through `Reject Vendor`; or
   - accepted through exactly one approval route.
3. **Accepted routes.**
   - Low risk: `Manager Approval`  `Activate Vendor`.
   - High risk: `Enhanced Due Diligence`  `Compliance Approval`  `Activate Vendor`.
4. **Clarification placement.** Clarification cycles may occur after receipt and before `Classify Risk`. This is a procedural restriction chosen for the backbone. R3 itself only requires every request to be answered before another request and before a final outcome.
5. **Clarification pairing.** `Request Clarification` and `Receive Clarification` form non-overlapping cycles; multiple cycles are permitted.
6. **Sanctions escalation placement.** Escalation can occur after `Run Sanctions Screening` and before the primary outcome. The procedural model places an optional escalation/legal-opinion subprocess after `Classify Risk`, which is more restrictive than R7.
7. **Completed traces.** Eventual-response obligations are evaluated on completed cases. An unanswered request or escalation at closure is a violation, not merely a pending obligation.
8. **Applicable approval for R9.** It is `Manager Approval` on the low-risk route and `Compliance Approval` on the high-risk route.
9. **Resource comparison.** Equality is exact equality of the event-level resource identifier.
10. **R11 scope.** A post-activation `Change Bank Details` creates obligations to:
    - later perform `Suspend Vendor`; and
    - perform a new `Verify Bank Account` before any later `Release First Payment`.
11. **R11 does not require a later payment.** If no later payment occurs, the re-verification-before-payment condition is vacuous, but suspension remains mandatory.
12. **Temporal-profile units.** Hours are converted to seconds. The temporal profile is advisory; values outside `mean ± 2` generate warnings.
13. **Timestamp order.** Equal timestamps require a stable event-order/index attribute; otherwise strict ordering cannot be established reliably.
14. **R2 wording.** “Exactly one” is authoritative for accepted cases. Rejected cases must have neither approval.
15. **R8 wording.** “Should not co-occur” is treated as normative noncoexistence, not merely advisory.
16. **Post-activation changes.** They are optional exceptional behavior. They are represented in the accepting Petri net, but R11’s conditional re-verification semantics remain authoritative in DECLARE/external validation.

## R5/R6 conflict resolutions

### Resolution A — legal-first, conservative

Treat R5 as the governing rule: if `Sanctions Escalation` occurs, `Obtain Legal Opinion` must precede `Activate Vendor`. Because the log lacks the emergency flag and joint-authorization evidence needed by R6, no logged case can prove that it qualifies for the exception.

- Emergency activation before the opinion therefore fails.
- R6 may still be checked as an external operational observation, but it cannot override R5 without evidence.
- This is the resolution implemented below.

**Compliance risk:** false positives are possible. A genuinely authorized emergency case will fail because the evidence needed to establish the exception is absent from the event log.

### Resolution B — emergency exception with external evidence

Interpret R6 as an explicit exception to R5:

- normally, legal opinion must precede activation;
- activation may precede opinion only if `emergency_authorized == True`, joint authorization is evidenced, and the opinion arrives within 24 hours after activation.

This cannot be encoded exactly in an activity-only ProcessTree, Petri net, DECLARE model, or Log Skeleton because it depends on non-event attributes and elapsed time.

**Compliance risk:** if the exception is accepted without reliable authorization evidence, unauthorized early activation may be legitimized. If the evidence is maintained in a separate system, correlation and audit-integrity failures can also produce wrong verdicts.

### Chosen resolution

The implemented control-flow and DECLARE models use **Resolution A**. The resource/data layer also reports the hypothetical result under Resolution B so that policy owners can see the conflict rather than having it silently merged.

# 2. Requirement-to-Formalism Mapping

| Requirement | Primary formalism | Exact encoding | Why this formalism | Redundancy or limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Procedural model | Exclusive rejection versus acceptance branches; DECLARE `noncoexistence(Reject Vendor, Activate Vendor)` | Primary outcomes are central control flow | Repeated in DECLARE and Log Skeleton for defense in depth |
| R2 | Procedural model | Rejection branch has no approval; accepted branch chooses exactly one of manager or compliance route | Route-dependent cardinality is clearest procedurally | DECLARE noncoexistence plus precedence rules help, but pure DECLARE does not conveniently express “exactly one iff accepted” as one rule; external aggregate validation is authoritative for the conditional count |
| R3 | Procedural model and DECLARE | Loop of `Request Clarification`  `Receive Clarification`; `altresponse(Request, Receive)` and `precedence(Request, Receive)`; external check that no final outcome occurs while a request is open | Combines repetition with non-overlapping response obligations | `altresponse` prevents another request before response but does not itself forbid a final outcome before response; procedural placement and external state check close that gap |
| R4 | DECLARE | `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Eventual obligation is a natural response constraint | Procedural escalation branch also includes the opinion under chosen legal-first policy |
| R5 | DECLARE under Resolution A | `precedence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` plus `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`; external exact check: if escalation exists, an opinion must occur after escalation and before activation | Ordering obligation is declarative | The two precedence rules alone can be satisfied by an unrelated earlier opinion; external correlated check is authoritative |
| R6 | Resource/data-aware validation | Under exception policy: verify `emergency_authorized`, two distinct authorizers or approval evidence, and opinion no more than 24 hours after early activation | Depends on missing authorization data and time | Not exactly expressible in activity-only formalisms; chosen Resolution A does not permit the exception |
| R7 | DECLARE | `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Direct historical prerequisite | Procedural model is more restrictive by placing escalation after classification |
| R8 | DECLARE | `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Manager Approval)` and `noncoexistence(Compliance Approval, Manager Approval)` | Case-level mutual exclusion is declarative | The route choice already enforces both; Log Skeleton repeats them |
| R9 | Resource-aware validation | Compare resources for `Verify Bank Account` versus applicable approval, and `Capture Master Data` versus `Activate Vendor` | Activity-only models do not carry performer identity | `filter_four_eyes_principle` may assist, but custom logic is needed for route-dependent approval and repeated verification |
| R10 | DECLARE / Log Skeleton | `absence` for each activity, interpreted as at most one; Log Skeleton `activ_freq` sets `{0,1}` | Unary occurrence bounds fit both formalisms | Procedural model also limits terminal events; PM4Py template semantics/version should be verified |
| R11 | DECLARE plus external validation | `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` and `response(Change Bank Details, Verify Bank Account)`; external order check ensures a new verification lies between each change and any later payment | Mixed eventual and interval-sensitive obligations | Simple response does not prove verification is before payment; external ordered-event check is authoritative |
| R12 | Temporal profile | Three `(mean_seconds, standard_deviation_seconds)` pairs with `zeta=2.0` | Designed for statistical elapsed-time deviations | A temporal warning is not a control-flow rejection; timestamps alone do not explain delays |

# 3. Procedural Backbone

## Compact formal description

Let:

- \(A =\) `Capture Master Data`
- \(S =\) `Run Sanctions Screening`
- \(B =\) `Verify Bank Account`
- \(Q =\) `Request Clarification`
- \(C =\) `Receive Clarification`

The main language is approximately:

\[
Receive \cdot (A \parallel S \parallel B) \cdot (Q \cdot C)^*
\cdot Classify
\cdot EscalationOption
\cdot (Reject \oplus LowRisk \oplus HighRisk)
\cdot Close
\]

where:

- `EscalationOption` is either silent or  
  `Sanctions Escalation`  `Obtain Legal Opinion`;
- `LowRisk` is  
  `Manager Approval`  `Activate Vendor`  optional post-activation maintenance  `Release First Payment`;
- `HighRisk` is  
  `Enhanced Due Diligence`  `Compliance Approval`  `Activate Vendor`  optional post-activation maintenance  `Release First Payment`;
- post-activation maintenance is either absent or  
  `Change Bank Details`  `Suspend Vendor`  `Verify Bank Account`.

An accepting Petri net is used because explicit places make the concurrency, joins, loops, and route choices auditable.

## PM4Py Python

```python
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.obj import PetriNet, Marking
from pm4py.objects.petri_net.utils import petri_utils

p_after_reject = place("p_after_reject")
p_after_payment = place("p_after_payment")

# Visible transitions
t_receive = trans("t_receive", "Receive Onboarding Request")
t_capture = trans("t_capture", "Capture Master Data")

t_bank_initial = trans("t_bank_initial", "Verify Bank Account")

t_request = trans("t_request", "Request Clarification")
t_receive_clarification = trans(
    "t_receive_clarification", "Receive Clarification"
)
t_classify = trans("t_classify", "Classify Risk")

t_escalate = trans("t_escalate", "Sanctions Escalation")

t_reject = trans("t_reject", "Reject Vendor")
t_manager = trans("t_manager", "Manager Approval")
t_edd = trans("t_edd", "Enhanced Due Diligence")
t_compliance = trans("t_compliance", "Compliance Approval")

# Duplicate transitions may share the same visible activity label.
t_activate_low = trans("t_activate_low", "Activate Vendor")

t_change = trans("t_change", "Change Bank Details")

t_bank_reverify = trans("t_bank_reverify", "Verify Bank Account")

# Silent transitions
tau_split = trans("tau_parallel_split", None)
tau_join = trans("tau_parallel_join", None)
tau_no_escalation = trans("tau_no_escalation", None)
tau_no_change = trans("tau_no_change", None)
tau_reject_to_close = trans("tau_reject_to_close", None)
tau_payment_to_close = trans("tau_payment_to_close", None)

# Start and parallel checks
arc(p_start, t_receive)
arc(t_receive, p_after_receive)

arc(p_after_receive, tau_split)
arc(tau_split, p_capture_ready)
arc(tau_split, p_screen_ready)
arc(tau_split, p_bank_ready)

arc(p_capture_ready, t_capture)
arc(t_capture, p_capture_done)

arc(p_screen_ready, t_screen)
arc(t_screen, p_screen_done)

arc(p_bank_ready, t_bank_initial)
arc(t_bank_initial, p_bank_done)

arc(p_capture_done, tau_join)
arc(p_screen_done, tau_join)
arc(p_bank_done, tau_join)
arc(tau_join, p_preclass)

# Zero or more clarification request/response cycles before classification
arc(p_preclass, t_request)
arc(t_request, p_wait_clarification)
arc(p_wait_clarification, t_receive_clarification)
arc(t_receive_clarification, p_preclass)

arc(p_preclass, t_classify)
arc(t_classify, p_after_classify)

# Optional escalation under conservative legal-first policy
arc(p_after_classify, tau_no_escalation)
arc(tau_no_escalation, p_route)

arc(p_after_classify, t_escalate)
arc(t_escalate, p_after_escalation)
arc(p_after_escalation, t_legal)
arc(t_legal, p_route)

# Primary outcome: reject, low-risk accept, or high-risk accept
arc(p_route, t_reject)
arc(t_reject, p_after_reject)

arc(p_route, t_manager)
arc(t_manager, p_low_approved)
arc(p_low_approved, t_activate_low)
arc(t_activate_low, p_active)

arc(p_route, t_edd)
arc(t_edd, p_after_edd)
arc(p_after_edd, t_compliance)
arc(t_compliance, p_high_approved)
arc(p_high_approved, t_activate_high)
arc(t_activate_high, p_active)

# Optional single post-activation bank-change remediation cycle
arc(p_active, tau_no_change)
arc(tau_no_change, p_payment_ready)

arc(p_active, t_change)
arc(t_change, p_after_change)
arc(p_after_change, t_suspend)
arc(t_suspend, p_after_suspend)
arc(p_after_suspend, t_bank_reverify)
arc(t_bank_reverify, p_payment_ready)

arc(p_payment_ready, t_release)
arc(t_release, p_after_payment)

# Both outcomes converge on close
arc(p_after_reject, tau_reject_to_close)
arc(tau_reject_to_close, p_before_close)

arc(p_after_payment, tau_payment_to_close)
arc(tau_payment_to_close, p_before_close)

arc(p_before_close, t_close)
arc(t_close, p_end)

initial_marking = Marking()
initial_marking[p_start] = 1

final_marking = Marking()
final_marking[p_end] = 1

# Accepting Petri net tuple used by PM4Py conformance functions.
procedural_model = (net, initial_marking, final_marking)
```

## Modeling choices

- **Concurrency:** the three initial checks are split into three branches and synchronized by an AND-join before classification.
- **Clarifications:** the request/receive loop permits zero or more complete cycles. An unanswered request cannot reach classification or closure.
- **Choice:** rejection, low-risk acceptance, and high-risk acceptance are mutually exclusive because they consume the same route token.
- **Escalation:** the conservative model requires the opinion immediately after escalation and before route execution. This is stricter than R4/R5, which allow intervening activities.
- **Terminal outcomes:** rejection reaches close without activation or payment. Acceptance requires activation and first payment before close.
- **Post-activation behavior:** one optional bank-change remediation cycle is included. Repeated changes are deliberately excluded from the procedural backbone to keep it compact. If repeated changes are operationally valid, the net should be extended with a controlled loop, while maintaining the at-most-once payment rule.
- **Approximation:** the net requires suspension and re-verification immediately in that procedural order after a represented bank change. R11 only requires eventual suspension and verification before later payment, so the net is intentionally conservative.

## Rule semantics

| Template and rule | Direction and meaning |
|---|---|
| `init(Receive Onboarding Request)` | The first event must be `Receive Onboarding Request`. |
| `existence(Close Onboarding)` | A completed trace must contain at least one close event. |
| `precedence(A, B)` for each initial check and classification | If `Classify Risk` occurs, the named check must have occurred earlier. The pair is oriented prerequisite  constrained event. |
| `precedence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | A receive cannot occur unless a request occurred earlier. It does not require every request to be answered. |
| `response(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Every request must have a later receive. It does not prohibit another request in between. |
| `altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` | Every request must be followed by a receive before another request occurs. It is weaker than `chainresponse`: intervening non-request activities are allowed. |
| `precedence(Run Sanctions Screening, Sanctions Escalation)` | Every escalation requires a prior screening. |
| `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Every escalation creates an eventual opinion obligation. |
| `precedence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` | Every opinion must have a prior escalation. Together with response this approximates succession. |
| `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` | Under Resolution A, any activation in a case containing this pattern requires a prior opinion. The external layer checks exact correlation to escalation. |
| Approval precedences | Activation requires an earlier listed approval, but these separate rules are too strong if interpreted simultaneously. In deployment they should be treated as route-specific diagnostics, while the procedural/external conditional route check is authoritative. |
| `precedence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval)` | Compliance approval requires prior EDD. |
| `response(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Activation creates an eventual payment obligation. |
| `precedence(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` | Payment requires prior activation. Together these approximate succession. |
| Change responses | A bank change requires later suspension and later re-verification. They do not alone enforce re-verification before payment. |
| `noncoexistence(A, B)` | The two activities may not both occur anywhere in one case, regardless of order. Unlike `nonsuccession`, it forbids both orderings. |
| `absence(A)` | Intended deployment meaning: activity occurs at most once. |

### Important route-specific limitation

Encoding both:

- `precedence(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)`, and
- `precedence(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)`

as ordinary global DECLARE rules would require **both** approvals before activation, contradicting R2. Therefore these two rules must not both be globally authoritative.

For an executable PM4Py model, the safe dictionary is the following adjusted form:

```python
# Remove globally conflicting route-specific activation precedences.
declare_model["precedence"].pop(("Manager Approval", "Activate Vendor"))
declare_model["precedence"].pop(("Compliance Approval", "Activate Vendor"))

# R2 is enforced authoritatively by the procedural route and external
# conditional-count validation. Other DECLARE rules remain executable.
```

Thus, the final assigned `declare_model` avoids silently imposing an incompatible conjunction.

# 5. Log-Skeleton Model

```python
log_skeleton = {
    "equivalence": {
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
    },
    "always_before": {
        ("Capture Master Data", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Verify Bank Account", "Classify Risk"),
        ("Run Sanctions Screening", "Sanctions Escalation"),
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
    },
    "always_after": {
        ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"),
        ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"),
        ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"),
        ("Change Bank Details", "Suspend Vendor"),
        ("Change Bank Details", "Verify Bank Account"),
    },
    "never_together": {
        ("Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"),
        ("Enhanced Due Diligence", "Manager Approval"),
        ("Compliance Approval", "Manager Approval"),
    },
    "activ_freq": {
        "Receive Onboarding Request": {1},
        "Capture Master Data": {1},
        "Run Sanctions Screening": {1},
        "Classify Risk": {1},
        "Activate Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Reject Vendor": {0, 1},
        "Release First Payment": {0, 1},
        "Close Onboarding": {1},
    },
    "directly_follows": set(),
}
```

## Orientation and interpretation

- `always_before(A, B)` means that when `B` occurs, `A` must occur earlier.
- `always_after(A, B)` means that when `A` occurs, `B` must occur later.
- `never_together(A, B)` is symmetric in intended meaning even though represented as an ordered Python tuple.
- `equivalence(A, B)` means equal occurrence counts. It is defensible for clarification because requests and receives are one-to-one in completed cases.
- `activ_freq` gives allowed occurrence-count sets.
- `directly_follows` is empty because none of the stated requirements demands immediate adjacency. Adding direct-follow constraints would overconstrain legal interleavings.

## Overlap and limitations

- R1, R4, R7, R8, and R10 intentionally overlap with DECLARE or the procedural model.
- `equivalence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` checks count equality but not correct ordering. `always_after` adds direction; it still does not express “before the next request” as directly as DECLARE `altresponse`.
- The initial `Verify Bank Account` has no `{1}` frequency restriction because R11 permits another verification after a bank change.
- No `always_before(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is included globally because it would require an opinion for every activation. The conditional R5 relationship is handled by DECLARE plus external validation.
- Route-specific approval conditions are not put into the skeleton because global pairwise relations cannot naturally express “manager XOR compliance depending on route.”

# 6. Temporal Profile

```python
HOUR = 3600

temporal_profile = {
    ("Request Clarification", "Receive Clarification"): (
        48 * HOUR,
        24 * HOUR,
    ),
    ("Sanctions Escalation", "Obtain Legal Opinion"): (
        12 * HOUR,
        6 * HOUR,
    ),
    ("Activate Vendor", "Release First Payment"): (
        72 * HOUR,
        36 * HOUR,
    ),
}

zeta = 2.0
```

The warning bands are:

| Pair | Mean | Standard deviation | `zeta=2` warning band |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Request  Receive Clarification | 48 h | 24 h | 0–96 h |
| Escalation  Legal Opinion | 12 h | 6 h | 0–24 h |
| Activation  First Payment | 72 h | 36 h | 0–144 h |

Temporal deviations are warnings because R12 explicitly says they should be flagged but should not automatically reject a case. A delay may reflect an approved hold, calendar effects, data quality problems, or an exceptional business circumstance. Negative elapsed time remains an ordering/data-quality failure rather than an ordinary temporal-profile warning.

# 7. Resource- and Data-Aware Checks

## Required attributes

At minimum:

- case identifier, such as `case:concept:name`;
- activity label, such as `concept:name`;
- timestamp, such as `time:timestamp`;
- performer, such as `org:resource`;
- stable event order for tied timestamps, such as `event_index`.

For Resolution B additionally:

- `emergency_authorized` or case-level equivalent;
- authorization evidence, ideally two distinct authorizer identifiers;
- optionally authorization timestamp and authorization-system reference.

## Executable-style validation

```python
from collections import Counter

FINAL_OUTCOMES = {"Reject Vendor", "Activate Vendor"}

def ordered_events(case_df):
    required = {"concept:name", "time:timestamp"}
    missing = required - set(case_df.columns)
    if missing:
        return None, [f"not evaluated: missing {sorted(missing)}"]

    sort_cols = ["time:timestamp"]
    if "event_index" in case_df.columns:
        sort_cols.append("event_index")
    elif case_df["time:timestamp"].duplicated().any():
        return None, [
            "not evaluated: tied timestamps require stable event_index"
        ]

    return case_df.sort_values(sort_cols).reset_index(drop=True), []


def validate_case(case_df, conflict_resolution="legal_first"):
    events, findings = ordered_events(case_df)
    if events is None:
        return findings

    names = events["concept:name"].tolist()
    counts = Counter(names)

    # R2: conditional approval cardinality.
    accepted = "Activate Vendor" in counts
    rejected = "Reject Vendor" in counts
    approval_count = (
        counts["Manager Approval"] + counts["Compliance Approval"]
    )

    if accepted and approval_count != 1:
        findings.append(
            "fail R2: accepted case must contain exactly one applicable approval"
        )
    if rejected and approval_count != 0:
        findings.append(
            "fail R2: rejected case must contain no manager/compliance approval"
        )

    # R3: no nested request; no final outcome while a request is open.
    clarification_open = False
    for activity in names:
        if activity == "Request Clarification":
            if clarification_open:
                findings.append(
                    "fail R3: next clarification request occurred before response"
                )
            clarification_open = True
        elif activity == "Receive Clarification":
            if not clarification_open:
                findings.append(
                    "fail R3: clarification received without an open request"
                )
            clarification_open = False
        elif activity in FINAL_OUTCOMES and clarification_open:
            findings.append(
                "fail R3: final outcome occurred before clarification response"
            )

    if clarification_open:
        findings.append(
            "fail R3: completed case has unanswered clarification request"
        )

    # R5/R6 exact escalation/activation relation.
    escalation_positions = [
        i for i, a in enumerate(names) if a == "Sanctions Escalation"
    ]
    opinion_positions = [
        i for i, a in enumerate(names) if a == "Obtain Legal Opinion"
    ]
    activation_positions = [
        i for i, a in enumerate(names) if a == "Activate Vendor"
    ]

    for esc_i in escalation_positions:
        later_opinions = [i for i in opinion_positions if i > esc_i]
        if not later_opinions:
            findings.append(
                "fail R4: escalation has no later legal opinion"
            )
            continue

        opinion_i = later_opinions[0]
        later_activations = [i for i in activation_positions if i > esc_i]

        if later_activations and later_activations[0] < opinion_i:
            activation_i = later_activations[0]

            if conflict_resolution == "legal_first":
                findings.append(
                    "fail R5: activation preceded post-escalation legal opinion"
                )

            elif conflict_resolution == "emergency_exception":
                required = {
                    "emergency_authorized",
                    "joint_authorizer_1",
                    "joint_authorizer_2",
                }
                missing = required - set(events.columns)
                if missing:
                    findings.append(
                        "policy-dependent R6: emergency exception cannot be "
                        f"proved; missing {sorted(missing)}"
                    )
                else:
                    activation = events.iloc[activation_i]
                    opinion = events.iloc[opinion_i]

                    authorized = bool(
                        activation["emergency_authorized"]
                    )
                    auth1 = activation["joint_authorizer_1"]
                    auth2 = activation["joint_authorizer_2"]
                    joint = (
                        auth1 is not None
                        and auth2 is not None
                        and auth1 != auth2
                    )
                    elapsed = (
                        opinion["time:timestamp"]
                        - activation["time:timestamp"]
                    ).total_seconds()

                    if not authorized or not joint:
                        findings.append(
                            "fail R6: early activation lacks valid joint authorization"
                        )
                    elif elapsed < 0 or elapsed > 24 * 3600:
                        findings.append(
                            "fail R6: legal opinion not within 24 hours of "
                            "emergency activation"
                        )
            else:
                findings.append("not evaluated: unknown conflict resolution")

    # R9: segregation of duties.
    if "org:resource" not in events.columns:
        findings.append(
            "not evaluated R9: missing org:resource"
        )
    elif events["org:resource"].isna().any():
        findings.append(
            "not evaluated R9: one or more relevant resources are missing"
        )
    else:
        verification_resources = set(
            events.loc[
                events["concept:name"] == "Verify Bank Account",
                "org:resource",
            ]
        )

        if counts["Manager Approval"] > 0:
            approval_resources = set(
                events.loc[
                    events["concept:name"] == "Manager Approval",
                    "org:resource",
                ]
            )
        else:
            approval_resources = set(
                events.loc[
                    events["concept:name"] == "Compliance Approval",
                    "org:resource",
                ]
            )

        if verification_resources & approval_resources:
            findings.append(
                "fail R9: bank verification and applicable approval share resource"
            )

        capture_resources = set(
            events.loc[
                events["concept:name"] == "Capture Master Data",
                "org:resource",
            ]
        )
        activation_resources = set(
            events.loc[
                events["concept:name"] == "Activate Vendor",
                "org:resource",
            ]
        )

        if capture_resources & activation_resources:
            findings.append(
                "fail R9: master-data capture and activation share resource"
            )

    # R11: every bank change after activation must be followed by suspension;
    # any later payment needs a verification strictly after the change and
    # strictly before that payment.
    first_activation = next(
        (i for i, a in enumerate(names) if a == "Activate Vendor"),
        None,
    )

    if first_activation is not None:
        for change_i in [
            i for i, a in enumerate(names)
            if a == "Change Bank Details" and i > first_activation
        ]:
            later_suspension = any(
                i > change_i and names[i] == "Suspend Vendor"
                for i in range(len(names))
            )
            if not later_suspension:
                findings.append(
                    "fail R11: no suspension after post-activation bank change"
                )

            later_payments = [
                i for i, a in enumerate(names)
                if a == "Release First Payment" and i > change_i
            ]
            for payment_i in later_payments:
                verified_between = any(
                    names[i] == "Verify Bank Account"
                    for i in range(change_i + 1, payment_i)
                )
                if not verified_between:
                    findings.append(
                        "fail R11: later payment lacks intervening bank verification"
                    )

    return findings
```

## Missing-attribute policy

- Missing resource on a relevant event: R9 is **not evaluated**, not passed.
- Missing emergency authorization evidence under Resolution B: exception is **unproved**. The result is `policy-dependent`; a fail-closed implementation may classify it as failure.
- Missing timestamp: temporal and interval-order checks are **not evaluated**.
- Tied timestamps without stable event order: strict before/after checks are **not evaluated**.
- Missing case identifier: events cannot be safely grouped, so all case-level checks are **not evaluated**.

# 9. Contradictions, Redundancies, and Blind Spots

## Duplicated enforcement

Several constraints are intentionally repeated:

- R1 appears in the Petri net, DECLARE, and Log Skeleton.
- R3 appears in the clarification loop, DECLARE response/altresponse, skeleton count/order constraints, and external state validation.
- R4 appears in the procedural escalation branch, DECLARE response, and skeleton `always_after`.
- R8 appears in procedural branch choice, DECLARE, and skeleton `never_together`.
- R10 appears in route structure, DECLARE cardinality, and skeleton frequencies.

This improves diagnostics, but duplicated layers can disagree if one is changed without versioning all others. Each requirement therefore needs a named authoritative layer.

## Loop and post-activation inconsistencies

- `equivalence(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` is sound only for completed cases. It is inappropriate for live, incomplete cases with a pending request.
- A simple `response(Change Bank Details, Verify Bank Account)` does not ensure that the verification occurs before payment.
- If multiple bank changes are allowed, one later verification might satisfy several simple DECLARE responses even when policy expects each change to be independently re-verified.
- A global “exactly one verification” rule would contradict R11, which explicitly permits another verification.
- The procedural model allows only one post-activation change cycle; R11 itself does not impose that bound.

## Vacuity

Declarative implications pass vacuously when their trigger does not occur:

- `response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` says nothing in a case without escalation.
- `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` says nothing without a bank change.
- `precedence(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` says nothing if payment never occurs.
- `response(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` is needed to require payment after activation.
- Pairwise approval rules alone cannot establish the route-dependent “exactly one iff accepted” requirement.

Vacuity is normal DECLARE semantics, but dashboards should distinguish “satisfied with activation” from “vacuously satisfied.”

## Conditional precedence limitations

R5 is not simply global `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`, because that would require an opinion before every activation, including cases with no escalation. Splitting it into response and precedence constraints can still be fooled by an unrelated earlier opinion. The external ordered-event check is therefore authoritative.

Similarly, globally encoding both approval precedences for activation would accidentally require both approvals. That directly contradicts R2 and demonstrates why conditional routes should remain procedural or data-aware.

## Missing case attributes

The supplied log does not include:

- emergency status;
- joint authorization identities;
- authorization timestamp;
- risk classification value;
- explicit accepted/rejected case status;
- role or organizational-unit metadata.

The route is inferred from visible activities. Emergency authorization cannot be inferred responsibly from activation-before-opinion.

## Timestamp-only limitations

Timestamps show recorded event time, not necessarily:

- when work actually started or completed;
- whether a delay occurred during weekends or approved holds;
- whether clocks and time zones are synchronized;
- whether events were entered retrospectively;
- whether equal timestamps have a meaningful order.

A 24-hour emergency requirement may mean calendar hours or business hours; the scenario states hours, so the implementation assumes elapsed calendar hours.

## Temporal matching ambiguity under repetition

Temporal-profile conformance must define which occurrence pair is measured when activities repeat. For clarification loops, pairing each request with the next corresponding receive is appropriate. A naive all-pairs implementation can report misleading deviations. R11’s repeated verification creates similar matching issues.

## Support and confidence metadata

`support=1.0` and `confidence=1.0` are model metadata required by the requested dictionary shape. They must not be mistaken for empirical proof that every historical trace complied or for a legal statement that the policy is unquestionably correct. Normative status comes from approved policy governance, not mined confidence values.

## Model-library semantics

PM4Py template naming and cardinality details can vary by version. In particular, `absence` must be regression-tested to confirm that it means the intended at-most-once condition. Dictionary syntax being accepted does not prove semantic correctness.

# 10. Deployment Recommendation

1. **Canonical procedural representation:** use the accepting Petri net as the canonical stable workflow model. Store it with explicit policy/version metadata, including that it embodies the conservative legal-first resolution.
2. **Authoritative policy layer:**
   - procedural net for stable route, concurrency, and terminal structure;
   - DECLARE for cross-cutting event-order and eventuality obligations;
   - external validation for R2’s conditional cardinality, R3’s open-request state, exact R5/R6 handling, R9, and R11 interval semantics;
   - temporal profile for advisory timing signals only.
3. **Recommended execution order:**
   1. schema, case-ID, timestamp, and event-order validation;
   2. procedural token-replay or alignment;
   3. authoritative external conditional/resource/data checks;
   4. DECLARE conformance;
   5. Log Skeleton conformance as a structural cross-check;
   6. temporal-profile warnings.
4. **Conflict handling:** never collapse contradictory findings into a single unexplained score. Report:
   - requirement identifier;
   - governing policy version;
   - authoritative layer;
   - observed event positions and attributes;
   - whether the result is fail, warning, not evaluated, or policy-dependent;
   - whether another layer disagreed because it was an approximation.
5. **R5/R6 governance:** either:
   - retain the legal-first model and fail closed when authorization evidence is absent; or
   - formally approve an emergency-exception policy, add mandatory authorization attributes, and deploy a separate exception-aware procedural/policy model.
6. **Incomplete cases:** evaluate response obligations as pending rather than failed until closure or a defined SLA deadline. The T1–T8 verdicts assume completed traces.
7. **Change control:** regression-test every model revision against these eight traces plus dedicated traces for repeated clarifications, repeated bank changes, missing resources, tied timestamps, unauthorized emergencies, and opinions exactly at the 24-hour boundary.
