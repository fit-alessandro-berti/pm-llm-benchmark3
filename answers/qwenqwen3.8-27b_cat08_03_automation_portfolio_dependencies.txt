- A11 is deliberately excluded because it should not operate until upstream controls are demonstrably reliable.

## 7. Final recommendation

I recommend the **bottleneck / synchronization portfolio: A5, A6, A4, A9**.

This portfolio is preferable to a pure high-frequency strategy because the highest-frequency activity, **Register application**, has high volume but relatively low process leverage: 8 min service time, 1.5h waiting time, and 4% rework. Automating A1 would produce a visible labor saving and a politically attractive quick win, but it would also push more cases into downstream classification, KYC, document review, and credit-analysis constraints. Frequency alone is therefore misleading.

The process-mining evidence points to lower-frequency but high-impact synchronization and control points:

- **Resolve entity mismatch** is only 1,750 cases, but has 31h waiting time, 38% rework, blocks KYC and signing, and is on the critical path in 82% of cases where present.  
- **Missing-document loops** involve 3,300 requests and a 52% repeated loop, driving customer-response delay and rework in financial-document review.  
- **Financial-statement normalization** is 5,200 cases with 75 min service time, 20h waiting time, and 16% rework, and it directly affects credit-analysis quality.  
- **Contract preparation** is 7,600 cases with 22h waiting time, 12% rework, and material control risk because contract terms must match committee decision and KYC clearance must precede signing.

The recommended portfolio balances four needs:

1. **A5** reduces missing-document loops and improves input quality for A6.  
2. **A6** reduces analyst time and improves normalized financial statements before credit analysis and committee packaging.  
3. **A4** relieves the constrained 3-FTE compliance-specialist pool and reduces a low-frequency but high-impact blocking activity.  
4. **A9** provides the required control-strengthening module by enforcing KYC-before-signing, contract-term match, and auditability.

This portfolio also accounts for changed resource demand. It relieves credit analysts through A6, but only if normalization is completed before handover and workload leveling is managed. It relieves compliance specialists through A4, but only if escalation capacity and false-negative controls are in place. It relieves legal ops through A9, but only if the legal rule library is current and upstream decision data is reliable.

A1 should not be the first primary automation in a four-module first year. It is a valid quick win and can be considered in the second wave, or as a lightweight non-core enhancement if it does not displace A5, A6, A4, or A9. The stronger first-year argument is to automate the activities that create synchronization delay, rework, and control exposure, while preserving human accountability for exceptions and compliance decisions.
