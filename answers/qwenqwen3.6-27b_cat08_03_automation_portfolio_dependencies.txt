- **Gate criteria:** Contract remediation <5%; 100% audit trail coverage; zero provisional-signing breaches; RM friction score <2/5

**Phase 2 (Months 5–8): Bottleneck Attack**
- **Modules:** A4, A6
- **Integration approach:** Lightweight APIs/batch syncs (deep integration limit already used)
- **Metrics:** Entity mismatch wait time, normalization accuracy, credit analyst throughput, compliance specialist exception queue length
- **Gate criteria:** Entity wait <10h; normalization accuracy >90%; credit analyst utilization <85%; compliance exception SLA met >95%

*Monitoring framework:* Weekly process-mining dashboards tracking DFG edge volumes, waiting time percentiles, and exception routing. Monthly control compliance reviews. Quarterly model performance audits.

# 7. Final recommendation

**Recommended portfolio:** Bottleneck/Synchronization Portfolio (adapted as A4, A6, A8, A12)

**Rationale & trade-off argument:**
Senior management’s instinct to automate the highest-frequency activity (A1: auto-registration) is understandable but process-misleading. Frequency alone ignores waiting time, synchronization delays, and critical-path impact. Process-mining evidence shows that `Resolve entity mismatch` (1750 cases, 31h wait, 82% critical-path presence) and `Committee packaging` (26h wait, multi-input synchronization) dominate end-to-end performance more than high-frequency front-end tasks. Automating A1 first would simply accelerate cases into existing KYC, credit, and committee bottlenecks, increasing downstream queue pressure and exception handling without meaningful cycle-time improvement.

The recommended portfolio prioritizes A4, A6, and A8 to attack the actual performance drivers: long waits, synchronization points, and artifact reconciliation. A12 is included to satisfy the compliance mandate, ensuring that control remediation, four-eyes segregation, and auditability are strengthened from day one. This combination delivers the highest process-level ROI by:
- Collapsing the 31h entity mismatch wait and unblocking the `KYC -> Contract preparation` path
- Reducing the 20h normalization wait and feeding cleaner data into credit analysis
- Reconciling multi-source artifacts before committee packaging, cutting 26h idle time
- Enforcing control gates and audit trails without relying on manual workarounds

**Trade-offs accepted:** Lower immediate FTE savings than a high-frequency portfolio; higher initial change management for compliance and credit teams; dependency on exception-routing discipline. These are justified by the structural impact on cycle time, risk reduction, and sustainable capacity utilization. A1 and A10 can be safely deployed in Year 2 once upstream controls and data quality are stabilized, ensuring automation amplifies process efficiency rather than merely shifting work downstream.
