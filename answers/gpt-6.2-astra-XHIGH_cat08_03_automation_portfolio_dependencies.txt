# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

- **Intake and parallel work.** A1 supplies a unique case record. A2 could confirm the route that determines A5's document list, A7's applicability and the high-exposure reviewer requirement. Existing staff can confirm that route without either module. Classification directly precedes KYC 7,250 times and financial review 3,940 times. KYC and financial review can overlap despite sequential logging. Use work-start, completion and clearance timestamps to reconstruct both branches.
- **Financial and identity branches.** A5 improves the inputs to A6. A6 supplies verified figures to the analyst, who retains credit judgment. On the identity branch, A3 can prioritize hits and A4 can assemble conflicting records for a specialist. Each can operate with today's manual counterpart. A suggestion from either module cannot constitute KYC clearance. Assign an owner, backup and deadline for each escalation, including cases requiring one of the two senior specialists.
- **Valuation and the committee join.** Credit analysis directly precedes 2,400 valuation requests. For eligible cases, A7 can identify the need and check valuer availability during analysis. Dispatch early only when product policy, required consent and authority to incur the cost permit it. Otherwise reserve capacity or improve dispatch at the existing release point. A8 can prepare a draft while work continues. Pack release waits for cleared KYC, normalized statements, credit notes and an accepted valuation where required. The last required artifact and the next eligible sitting determine the benefit of speeding up a branch.
- **From decision to activation.** A9 needs approved decision data and legal rules. A10 needs the released contract and a fresh clearance check at signing, including for assisted or paper routes. A12 can direct discrepancies back to the responsible owner for correction and reapproval. A11 would require positive evidence of every applicable gate and a check against prior activation. In high-exposure SME cases, distinct humans must complete credit analysis and committee packaging, even if A8 prepares the pack.
- **Integration capacity.** In the recommended portfolio the two deep core connections in months 1–6 go to A4 for case-linked evidence and clearance status, and A6 for approved financials in the credit file. A5 uses the existing portal and staff-confirmed receipt status. A7 starts after month six. A4's evidence workflow and the existing compliance sign-off establish the first-wave control improvement. Existing staff enforce release checks wherever a channel lacks an automated gate.

# 4. Expected process-level effects

**Quick win.** Registration occupies position two in 98% of cases, with 11,880 receive-to-register transitions. A1 can remove touch time and some of the 1.5-hour median registration queue. Back-office utilization already falls below 65% after 15:00, so releasing effort there may do little for morning queues or overall cycle time. A5 should reduce RM-to-customer-to-review handovers. A9 should reduce the 650 contract-preparation-to-remediation and 510 return transitions, particularly for Credit-line renewal, with a 14% contract-remediation rate. A10 can reduce reminder loops for consenting customers. The customer still controls when they sign. Legal ops may gain month-end drafting capacity while analysts and activation staff receive work sooner. Decision matching and KYC-gated signing target two violations, while high-exposure four-eyes checks remain manual.

**Bottleneck and synchronization.** Mismatch resolution has a 31-hour median wait and is critical in 82% of cases where it occurs. There are 1,750 KYC-to-mismatch transitions and 1,160 returns to KYC. A4 should reduce repeated evidence handovers and specialist queue age. Each clearance advances activation only to the extent that another branch is ready. A5 targets the 3,300 review-to-request transitions and 2,800 returns to review. A6 protects the nine analysts receiving that cleaner work at the 5,000 normalization-to-credit-analysis transitions. Analyst demand shifts toward validation, poor scans and judgment-heavy high-exposure files. A7 targets the 58-hour external wait and the 1,980 external-wait-to-packaging transitions. Timing matters most in Equipment finance, where 71% require valuation, versus 18% in Working-capital. A report received before the pack cutoff could save a weekly sitting. Earlier reports that sit waiting for credit notes save no committee time. Expect fewer identity and document loops, with remaining peaks at committee and in the four-person legal team. A4 improves clearance evidence. Manual signing, four-eyes and contract checks remain necessary to reduce violations elsewhere.

**Compliance first.** A3 triage and A12 evidence tracking can shorten time spent reconstructing a case, but holding questionable cases can initially increase waiting and recorded exceptions. A8 can reduce RM–analyst collation handovers over 3,900 pack-to-decision transitions. An independent completer still needs time before the high-exposure cutoff. A9 should reduce redrafting and legal rework, especially for renewals, but a weekly committee, missing documents and external reports continue to govern cycle time. Demand moves from pack assembly and drafting to specialist escalation, policy decisions and independent review at month end. Compare confirmed violations with the observed 5.8% high-exposure four-eyes violation rate, 6.5% provisional-signing rate and 8.5% overall contract-remediation rate. Track prevented attempts separately from completed violations so improved detection is visible.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **False decisions and drift.** Never merge a plausible duplicate or clear a disputed identity on similarity alone. Route ambiguous matches, adverse hits and uncertain exposure classifications to authorized reviewers. Sample apparently clean results against independently reviewed evidence to detect false negatives. Measure false positives and reviewer minutes as well. Monitor by segment, document format and model version. Revert an affected rule or model to manual handling if performance deteriorates.
- **Financial and valuation errors.** Retain page-level source references for extracted fields, reconcile totals, currency and periods, and have an analyst approve the financials before use. Queue unreadable documents for assisted handling. Use approved valuers, check conflicts and report quality, and route disputed or late valuations to a named collateral owner. Track cancellations and revaluations alongside speed.
- **Release and segregation of duties.** Hold signing on missing, expired or reopened clearance in every channel. Reconcile the contract against the current approved terms and withdraw superseded drafts. Record the two human completer IDs on high-exposure credit analysis and packaging, block a same-person completion, and roster an independent backup at month end. A service account cannot stand in for the second reviewer. Keep activation under operations sign-off.
- **Auditability and exceptions.** Record case ID, source evidence, model or rule version, suggestion, confidence where applicable, human override and reason, final decision, actor and time. Restrict access to customer data and preserve the decision history. Give every held case a queue, owner, escalation deadline and safe manual path. Reconcile duplicate or delayed messages before retrying. Compliance owns clearance, credit owns the assessment and legal owns contract rules and their effective dates.
- **Customer and staffing effects.** Have RMs test product-specific instructions with customers. Combine outstanding requests, stop nudges on receipt, cap reminders and provide assisted submission. Monitor complaints and abandonment. Use afternoon back-office capacity to check document receipt and prepare evidence. Keep specialist adjudication and independent credit review with qualified staff.

Use phased, comparable cohorts by product, exposure, channel and calendar period. Track median and 90th-percentile time from receipt to activation, cases activated, open-case age and withdrawals together. Measure branch waiting separately from service time and customer response time. Do not add activity medians across concurrent branches. At each gate, review repeat handovers, analyst and specialist hours per case, pack readiness by cutoff, legal month-end backlog and control outcomes. Include provisional signing and subsequent remediation separately, given the reported 6.5% provisional signing and 1.1% requiring remediation. Set operational tolerances with the responsible owners before the pilot. Missing clearance, missing required independent approval or untraceable decisions block release.

# 7. Final recommendation

Fund **A4, A5, A6 and A7**, with **A4 first**. A4 addresses a documented critical-path delay and gives compliance an auditable identity decision. A5 and A6 belong together because a complete file arriving faster creates value only if analysts can process it. A7 earns its place through the concentration of valuation delays in Equipment finance and the opportunity to meet a committee cutoff. Its expansion depends on accepted reports arriving earlier.

Receipt is the most frequent event and is already digital in 72% of cases. Registration is the attractive high-volume automation candidate, but saving eight minutes there still delivers a case into the same specialist and analyst queues. Defer A1. The material cost of this choice is leaving contract remediation, especially on renewals, to legal staff this year. Keep decision-to-contract reconciliation in force and use measured residual delays and control failures to decide whether A9 should lead the next investment.
