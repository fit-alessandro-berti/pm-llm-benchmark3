# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Observations (given in the prompt, not assumed):** control flow `Intake  diagnostic(s)  Validation`; service-time ranges and tail probabilities; skill/speed matrix; shifts and breaks; demand blocks with mixes; handover/setup/coordination rules; preemption rule (P1 only, once per case, 10% penalty on remaining active service); SLA targets; 30-min overtime cap; >90% planned utilization = unfair/fragile.

**Assumptions (each needed because the prompt gives ranges, not distributions):**

- **A1 (service distribution).** Within a regime, service time ~ Uniform(lo, hi); the regime (ordinary vs complex-tail) is a Bernoulli draw with the given probability. Uniform is the maximum-entropy choice given only bounds. A triangular or beta shape would lower the variance ~10–20%; the capacity conclusions below are driven by means, so they are robust to this choice; tail (p95) conclusions are not, and are flagged as such.
- **A2 (arrivals).** Arrivals are Poisson per block with the given expected counts, uniform within the block. A deterministic-fluid alternative gives the same utilization numbers but hides queueing variance; the DES in §8 uses Poisson.
- **A3 (preemption semantics).** "Restart penalty equal to 10% of remaining active service time" is implemented as *resume with remaining time inflated by 10%* (work done is not lost). The harsher reading (full restart + 10%) would make preemption 5–10× more expensive and would push the recommendation toward fewer, later preemptions; this is a sensitivity, not a reversal of the ranking.
- **A4 (handover/setup occupancy).** Handover (7 min), team-change (5 min), and setup (5 min) delays are modeled as resource-occupied pre-service delay. Treating them as case-side waiting would lower measured utilization by ~3–6 pp but not change rankings.
- **A5 (ME/ES mode decision).** Parallel mode runs both branches on two qualified resources concurrently; Validation becomes ready at max(branch ends) + 8 min coordination. Sequential runs MechanicalElectrical (ME) and ElectricalSoftware (ES). The mean critical-path saving from parallelizing is  min(branch means)  8  **18 min (ME)** and **25 min (ES)**, at the cost of simultaneously holding two resources. Default economics: parallelize P1 ME/ES always (see SLA math in §2); parallelize P2 only if both resources are free; sequential for P3 under load.
- **A6 (SLA clock and horizon).** Elapsed time starts at arrival; the day ends at 20:30 (20:00 + 30-min overtime cap). Cases unfinished at the horizon are counted as SLA breaches (conservative; in reality they spill to the next day and would breach P2's 8 h anyway).
- **A7 (breaks).** A resource who is busy at break start finishes the current activity, then takes the break (no mid-activity break interruption).
- **A8 (U4 scope).** Only *diagnostic* tail probabilities double (Intake/Validation are not diagnostic activities).

**Objective formulations (decision is which policy  to adopt):**

- **O1 — SLA/tail risk (primary):**
 `J1() = _s w_s · [0.60·SLA_P1(s) + 0.25·SLA_P2(s) + 0.15·(1  P90_delay(s)/480)]`, with scenario weights w = (base 0.30, U1–U5 0.14 each). Equivalently: minimize CVaR. of case delay across scenarios. This objective penalizes policies that look good on the base mean but fail under U2/U3/U5.
- **O2 — Fairness/robustness (constraint form):**
 minimize `max_{s,r} util_{r,s}` subject to `SLA_P1(s)  0.90 s` and a penalty `·(max_r util  mean_r util)` per scenario, with the hard operational rule that planned utilization > 90% for any individual resource is unfair/fragile.

**Decision variables:** per-activity eligibility sets; queue discipline (priority/FIFO/SERPT); parallel-vs-sequential trigger; overflow wait-thresholds _P1, _P2, _P3; reservation windows (who is protected for what, when); preemption victim-selection rule.

# 3. Candidate policies

All policies share: SLA clock from arrival; P1 preemption allowed on diagnostic activities, once per case, 10% penalty on remaining (A3); overtime 30 min; handover/team/setup rules as given.

**Policy A — Strict specialization, fixed queues.**
- *Eligibility:* Intake {Ada, Deepa, Farah}; Mechanical {Ada, Ben}; Electrical {Chen, Deepa}; Software {Emil, Chen}; Validation {Ben, Deepa, Emil}. No cross-skill overflow, ever.
- *Dispatch:* strict priority (P1P2P3), FIFO within class; among eligible free resources take the lowest multiplier (fastest specialist).
- *Parallelization:* ME/ES parallel only if both families' specialists are free at diagnostic start; else sequential.
- *Preemption:* P1 preempts the lowest-priority, longest-remaining eligible busy specialist.
- *Handover minimization:* structural (same small team), no explicit rule.

**Policy B — Full pooling, transparent dispatch.**
- *Eligibility:* any qualified resource for every activity.
- *Dispatch score:* for waiting case c and candidate r: `expected completion = now + handover/setup delay(c,r) + remaining_base × mult_r`; serve cases in (priority, ready-time, arrival) order, assign the (case, resource) pair minimizing the score. Fully auditable: the score is arithmetic on visible quantities.
- *Parallelization:* ME/ES parallel whenever one qualified resource is free for each branch.
- *Preemption:* as shared rule.
- *Handover minimization:* implicit via the score (a same-resource continuation has +0 delay and wins ties).

**Policy C — Hybrid: reserved P1 capacity + controlled overflow (recommended).**
- *Eligibility:* primary = Policy A's specialist sets; secondary = all other qualified resources, unlocked per case when its wait exceeds ** = (P1: 10, P2: 45, P3: 150) minutes**. P1 cases may always use the full qualified set.
- *Reservations:* from 14:00, Emil is withheld from non-Software work while a Software task or a waiting P1 diagnostic exists; Chen is Electrical-first (he may take Software only if no Electrical task waits).
- *Parallelization:* P1 ME/ES always attempts parallel first; P2 parallel if both primaries free; P3 sequential (conserves the 8-min coordination and dual-resource hold).
- *Preemption:* shared rule, with an audit guard: preempt only if victim remaining  20 min (below that, 0.1·r + handover  cost exceeds typical benefit).
- *Handover minimization:* score of B used *within* the eligible set, so same-resource continuations are preferred when eligible.

**Policy D — SERPT with fairness guard (variant, described not simulated).** Dispatch the waiting case with the smallest expected remaining processing (remaining/mult); guard: a resource above 85% utilization may not take new work while another qualified resource is below 70% (fairness), P1 exempt. Expected behavior: best base-case mean, worst fairness of tail resources, no P1 reservation  weaker under U2/U3 than C. Included to show the frontier, not as a recommendation.

# 5. Robust recommendation

**Primary policy: C (hybrid reservation + controlled overflow)** with the parameters of §3 ( = 10/45/150 min; Emil afternoon reservation; Chen Electrical-first; P1 ME/ES always attempt parallel; preemption guard r  20 min).

Why it dominates under **O1**: across U1–U5 it holds the highest or tied-highest P1 SLA band in every scenario (§4), because its three mechanisms target the actual failure modes: (i) parallel-first P1 ME/ES removes the ~108-min sequential mean that sits at the 120-min target (F4); (ii) the Emil reservation protects the F1 single-point resource for exactly the work only he can do; (iii) overflow triggers convert C into B locally, exactly when queues form, without paying B's full-time handover overhead. Under **O2**, C is slightly worse than B on base fairness but is the only policy that approaches the 90% utilization rule in the base case while keeping the P1 floor; A violates the fairness rule structurally (Emil >100% planned 16–20).

**Fallback mode: switch C  B-style full pooling** (keep Emil's Software reservation) when any of:
- Chen is absent or unplugged (U3-type): C's specialist reservations presuppose Chen's presence; with him gone, pooling across Ada/Deepa/Emil/Farah flows better.
- Forecast Electrical+Software load for 12:00–16:00 > **1.4× base** (U2-type): the specialist pair is then >125% loaded; overflow should be proactive, not wait-triggered.
- Rolling 2-hour P1 queue wait > **15 min**.

**Escalation triggers (structural, outside routing):** Emil utilization >95% for two consecutive days, or 3-day rolling P1 SLA <88%  change staffing, not routing: extend Software-qualified coverage past 16:00 (e.g., shift Chen to 12:00–20:00, or cross-train Ben for Software). This is the single highest-leverage fix (F1); no routing policy removes that constraint.

**When another policy is preferable:** A — if demand is persistently  capacity, the organization requires single-owner accountability, and measured handover costs exceed the modeled 7+5+5 min. B — if fairness/skill-development dominates the objective and P1 share falls below ~5% (reservation overhead not worth it).

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Gaming of estimates:** dispatch scores use remaining-time estimates and tail probabilities; teams can inflate tail-probability claims or understate remaining work to attract/avoid assignments. Mitigation: scores computed centrally from logged actuals; quarterly recalibration; audit trail of estimate vs actual per activity (§7 attributes).
- **P3 starvation:** preemption + reservations both tax P3. The _P3 = 150-min overflow trigger is the protection, but under U2/U3 P3 waits will grow 30–80%. If P3 SLA matters politically, add a daily P3 quota slot in the 16–18 block (the cheapest idle-capacity window is Farah's morning, not the evening).
- **Skill erosion:** A-style specialization erodes cross-skills; C's overflow keeps secondary skills warm at low dose (~15–25% of assignments). Full pooling (B) maximizes practice but at 1.30–1.40 multipliers produces slow, error-prone work — a quality risk not modeled here.
- **Excessive preemption:** with the 10% penalty + handover, preempting a task with <20 min remaining is net-negative (0.1r + 7  9–12 min cost vs small P1 gain). Cascades (P1 preempting P2, then another P1 arriving) are capped by the once-per-case rule, but monitor preemption counts/day; >3/day signals chronic P1 capacity shortfall, not a dispatch problem.
- **Coordination overhead:** parallel ME/ES costs 8 min + a dual-resource hold; under U2 congestion, indiscriminate parallelization *reduces* throughput. C restricts parallel to P1 (always) and P2 (both free) for this reason.
- **New bottleneck at Validation:** Validation is 661 min/day (28%) but every case converges there; if diagnostic cycle time drops 25–30% (policy success!), Validation plus its handovers becomes the next constraint, peaking 14–16. Watch it in §7 telemetry.
- **Fairness:** Emil is structurally overloaded (F1) and Farah underused (~20 h/week, all at 1.4 speed). Utilization-based fairness must be reported per resource per day, not as a team average.
- **Interpretability:** every rule in C is an if-then a shift lead can audit ("P1 waited 10 min  secondary pool opens; Emil reserved for Software after 14:00 while Software queue non-empty"). Avoid opaque composite scores in the dispatch UI; show the two or three terms of the score.

**PM4Py analyses mapped to questions:**
- *Handover burden & organizational structure:* `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` per policy version — cross-team (MECHELSW) edge weights quantify the 5-min team-change exposure; compare density A vs B vs C.
- *Service-time changes / drift:* `pm4py.get_service_time` per activity per week; sliding-window means of actual service vs planned; variant drift via `pm4py.stats` windowed variant comparison. A rising Electrical mean with stable mix signals U2-type demand shift or skill erosion.
- *Case-level totals:* `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` — note the **label semantics**: `case:service_time` sums all activity durations of a case; `case:waiting_time` sums inter-event gaps (queue + handover + coordination). They are *not* per-stage queue times; stage-specific waits must be computed from `start_timestamp  queue_entry_ts` (as in §8's `stage_waits`).
- *Bottleneck migration:* `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` / performance spectrum filtered by 2-h blocks — waiting bands moving from Software (base evenings) to Electrical (U2 middays) make migration visible; per-block stage utilization from the log confirms §2's table.
- *Conformance:* alignments against the declared model (Intake  allowed diagnostic sets  Validation) detect workarounds (Validation started before a parallel branch finishes, skipped handovers, sequential execution of cases flagged parallel).

    return blocks, service, drop_chen, outage

def simulate(policy, scen, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    blocks, service, drop_chen, outage = scenario_tables(scen)
    resources = [r for r in MULT if not (drop_chen and r == 'Chen')]
    R = {r: dict(task=None, busy_until=None, last_fam=None, busy_min=0.0,
                 n_hand=0, n_team=0, n_setup=0) for r in resources}
    cases, log = [], []
    nid = [0]

    def draw(act):
        s = service[act]
        lo, hi = s['tail'] if rng.random() < s['p'] else s['lo']
        return float(rng.uniform(lo, hi))

    def qualified(act):
        return [r for r in resources if act in MULT[r]]

    def avail(r, t):
        if R[r]['task'] is not None:
            return False
        br = BREAK[r]
        if br and br[0] <= t < br[1]:
            return False
        return t < SHIFT[r][1] + OVERTIME_CAP   # ASSUMPTION: OT budget gates new starts

    def new_case(t, pr, ct):
        nid[0] += 1
        c = dict(id='C%03d' % nid[0], arrival=t, prio=int(pr), ctype=ct, tasks=[],
                 last_res=None, last_fam=None, preempted=False, par=False,
                 pending_second=None, completed=False, comp=None)
        b = draw('Intake')
        c['tasks'].append(dict(act='Intake', base=b, rem=b, ready=t, state='wait',
                               start=None, end=None, res=None))
        cases.append(c)

    def delay_parts(c, k, r):
        hand = team = setup = 0
        if c['last_res'] is not None and c['last_res'] != r:
            hand = HANDOVER_MIN
        fam = TEAM.get(k['act'])
        if fam and c['last_fam'] and fam != c['last_fam'] and c['last_res'] != r:
            team = TEAM_MIN
        if fam and R[r]['last_fam'] and R[r]['last_fam'] != fam:
            setup = SETUP_MIN
        return hand + team + setup, hand, team, setup

    def start_task(c, k, r, t):
        d, h, tm, su = delay_parts(c, k, r)
        dur = int(np.ceil(k['rem'] * MULT[r][k['act']]))
        k.update(state='run', res=r, start=t + d, end=t + d + dur)
        R[r].update(task=(c, k), busy_until=k['end'])
        R[r]['busy_min'] += d + dur   # ASSUMPTION: handover/setup occupy the resource
        R[r]['n_hand'] += (h > 0); R[r]['n_team'] += (tm > 0); R[r]['n_setup'] += (su > 0)

    def mk_diag(c, act, t):
        b = draw(act)
        c['tasks'].append(dict(act=act, base=b, rem=b, ready=t, state='wait',
                               start=None, end=None, res=None))

    def create_diag(c, t):
        need = DIAG[c['ctype']]
        if len(need) == 1:
            mk_diag(c, need[0], t); return
        if policy == 'A':
            par = all(any(rr in HOME[a] and avail(rr, t) for rr in qualified(a)) for a in need)
        elif policy == 'B':
            par = all(any(avail(rr, t) for rr in qualified(a)) for a in need)
        else:
            if c['prio'] == 1:   # C: P1 always attempts parallel
                par = all(any(avail(rr, t) for rr in qualified(a)) for a in need)
            else:
                par = all(any(rr in HOME[a] and avail(rr, t) for rr in qualified(a))
                          for a in need)
        c['par'] = par
        if par:
            for a in need:
                mk_diag(c, a, t)
        else:                # ASSUMPTION: sequential order ME = M->E, ES = E->S
            mk_diag(c, need[0], t)
            c['pending_second'] = need[1]

    def finish(c, k, t):
        log.append(dict(case=c['id'], act=k['act'], start=k['start'], end=t,
                        res=k['res'], prio=c['prio'], ctype=c['ctype'], preempted=0))
        R[k['res']]['task'] = None; R[k['res']]['busy_until'] = None
        fam = TEAM.get(k['act'])
        if fam:
            R[k['res']]['last_fam'] = fam; c['last_fam'] = fam
        c['last_res'] = k['res']
        k['state'] = 'done'
        if k['act'] == 'Intake':
            create_diag(c, t)
        elif k['act'] in DIAG_ACTS:
            if c['pending_second']:
                nxt = c['pending_second']; c['pending_second'] = None
                mk_diag(c, nxt, t)
            need = DIAG[c['ctype']]
            if all(any(x['act'] == a and x['state'] == 'done' for x in c['tasks'])
                   for a in need):
                dend = max(x['end'] for x in c['tasks'] if x['act'] in need)
                vb = draw('Validation')
                c['tasks'].append(dict(act='Validation', base=vb, rem=vb,
                                       ready=dend + (PAR_COORD if c['par'] else 0),
                                       state='wait', start=None, end=None, res=None))
        else:                # Validation finished -> case complete
            c['completed'] = True; c['comp'] = k['end']

    def waiting(t):
        wl = []
        for c in cases:
            for k in c['tasks']:
                if k['state'] == 'wait' and k['ready'] <= t:
                    if k['act'] == 'Intake' and outage and outage[0] <= t < outage[1]:
                        continue
                    wl.append((c['prio'], k['ready'], c['arrival'], c, k))
        wl.sort(key=lambda x: (x[0], x[1], x[2]))
        return wl

    def eligible(c, k, t):
        act = k['act']; q = qualified(act)
        if policy == 'A':
            return [r for r in q if r in HOME[act]]
        if policy == 'B':
            return list(q)
        # Policy C
        if c['prio'] == 1 or (t - k['ready']) > OVERFLOW_TRIG[c['prio']]:
            return list(q)
        prim = [r for r in q if r in HOME[act]]
        elec_wait = any(x['state'] == 'wait' and x['act'] == 'Electrical'
                        for cc in cases for x in cc['tasks'])
        soft_wait = any(x['state'] == 'wait' and x['act'] == 'Software'
                        for cc in cases for x in cc['tasks'])
        p1_wait = any(cc['prio'] == 1 and any(x['state'] == 'wait' and x['act'] in DIAG_ACTS
                                              for x in cc['tasks']) for cc in cases)
        keep = []
        for r in prim:
            if r == 'Emil' and act != 'Software' and t >= EMIL_RESERVE_FROM \
                    and (soft_wait or p1_wait):
                continue                      # afternoon Emil reservation
            if r == 'Chen' and act == 'Software' and elec_wait:
                continue                      # Chen electrical-first
            keep.append(r)
        return keep if keep else prim

    def choose(c, k, cands, t):
        if policy == 'A':
            return min(cands, key=lambda r: (MULT[r][k['act']], r))
        return min(cands, key=lambda r: t + delay_parts(c, k, r)[0]
                   + k['rem'] * MULT[r][k['act']])

    def dispatch(t):
        for prio, rdy, arr, c, k in waiting(t):
            if k['state'] != 'wait':
                continue
            cands = [r for r in eligible(c, k, t) if avail(r, t)]
            if cands:
                start_task(c, k, choose(c, k, cands, t), t)

    def try_preempt(t):
        for c in cases:
            if c['prio'] != 1:
                continue
            for k in c['tasks']:
                if not (k['state'] == 'wait' and k['ready'] <= t and k['act'] in DIAG_ACTS):
                    continue
                q = qualified(k['act'])
                if any(avail(r, t) for r in q):
                    continue
                vict = []
                for r in q:
                    if R[r]['task']:
                        vc, vk = R[r]['task']
                        if vk['act'] in DIAG_ACTS and vc['prio'] > 1 and not vc['preempted']:
                            rem = (vk['end'] - t) / MULT[r][vk['act']]
                            if rem > 0:
                                vict.append((vc['prio'], rem, r, vc, vk))
                if vict:
                    # preempt the LEAST important victim: lowest priority, longest remaining
                    _, rem, r, vc, vk = min(vict, key=lambda x: (x[0], -x[1]))
                    log.append(dict(case=vc['id'], act=vk['act'], start=vk['start'],
                                    end=t, res=r, prio=vc['prio'], ctype=vc['ctype'],
                                    preempted=1))
                    R[r]['busy_min'] -= (vk['end'] - t)
                    vk['rem'] = rem * (1 + PREEMPT_PENALTY)  # 10% restart penalty (A3)
                    vk.update(state='wait', ready=t, start=None, end=None, res=None)
                    vc['preempted'] = True                   # at most once per case
                    R[r]['task'] = None; R[r]['busy_until'] = None

    arr = []
    for b in blocks:
        for _ in range(rng.poisson(b['lam'])):
            tt = float(rng.uniform(b['t0'], b['t1']))
            pr = int(rng.choice([1, 2, 3], p=[b['p1'], b['p2'], 1 - b['p1'] - b['p2']]))
            ks = list(b['types']); w = np.array([b['types'][x] for x in ks]); w = w / w.sum()
            arr.append((tt, pr, ks[int(rng.choice(len(ks), p=w))]))
    arr.sort(key=lambda x: x[0])

    ai = 0
    for t in range(480, HORIZON):
        while ai < len(arr) and arr[ai][0] <= t:
            new_case(arr[ai][0], arr[ai][1], arr[ai][2]); ai += 1
        for r in resources:
            if R[r]['task'] and t >= R[r]['busy_until']:
                c, k = R[r]['task']
                finish(c, k, t)
        try_preempt(t)
        dispatch(t)
    return cases, R, log, resources

def metrics(cases, R, log, resources):
    done = [c for c in cases if c['completed']]
    delays = np.array([c['comp'] - c['arrival'] for c in done], float)
    def sla(p, T):
        cs = [c for c in cases if c['prio'] == p]
        if not cs:
            return float('nan')
        return float(np.mean([(c['completed'] and c['comp'] - c['arrival'] <= T) for c in cs]))
    util = {r: R[r]['busy_min'] / ((SHIFT[r][1] - SHIFT[r][0]) - (30 if BREAK[r] else 0))
            for r in resources}
    us = np.array(list(util.values()))
    return dict(mean_delay=float(np.mean(delays)) if len(delays) else float('nan'),
                p90_delay=float(np.percentile(delays, 90)) if len(delays) else float('nan'),
                P1_SLA=sla(1, SLA_MIN[1]), P2_SLA=sla(2, SLA_MIN[2]),
                umax=float(us.max()), ufair=float(us.max() - us.mean()),
                hand=int(sum(R[r]['n_hand'] for r in resources)),
                team=int(sum(R[r]['n_team'] for r in resources)),
                setup=int(sum(R[r]['n_setup'] for r in resources)),
                preemptions=int(sum(row['preempted'] for row in log)),
                unfinished=len(cases) - len(done))

def run_all(n_seeds=8):
    rows, keep = [], {}
    for pol in ('A', 'B', 'C'):
        for sc in ('base', 'U1', 'U2', 'U3', 'U4', 'U5'):
            ms = []
            for sd in range(n_seeds):
                cases, R, log, resources = simulate(pol, sc, sd)
                ms.append(metrics(cases, R, log, resources))
                if sd == 0 and sc == 'base':
                    keep[pol] = (cases, log)
            df = pd.DataFrame(ms)
            row = {'policy': pol, 'scenario': sc}
            for col in ('mean_delay', 'p90_delay', 'P1_SLA', 'P2_SLA', 'umax', 'ufair',
                        'hand', 'team', 'setup', 'preemptions', 'unfinished'):
                row[col] = '%.2f±%.2f' % (df[col].mean(), df[col].std())
            rows.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(rows), keep

def offered_load_table():
    mean = {a: (1 - SERVICE[a]['p']) * np.mean(SERVICE[a]['lo'])
            + SERVICE[a]['p'] * np.mean(SERVICE[a]['tail']) for a in SERVICE}
    rows = []
    for b in BLOCKS:
        n, ty = b['lam'], b['types']
        rows.append(dict(block='%02d-%02d' % (b['t0'] // 60, b['t1'] // 60),
                         mech=(ty['M'] + ty['ME']) * n * mean['Mechanical'],
                         elec=(ty['E'] + ty['ME'] + ty['ES']) * n * mean['Electrical'],
                         soft=(ty['S'] + ty['ES']) * n * mean['Software'],
                         intake=n * mean['Intake'], validation=n * mean['Validation']))
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

def daily_capacity():
    out = {}
    for act in SERVICE:
        tot = 0.0
        for r, m in MULT.items():
            if act in m:
                br = 30 if BREAK[r] else 0
                tot += (SHIFT[r][1] - SHIFT[r][0] - br) / m[act]
        out[act] = round(tot, 1)
    return out

def stage_waits(cases):
    # stage-specific queue wait = task start - ready (queue-entry) time.
    # This is deliberately separate from PM4Py case:waiting_time, which is a
    # case-level sum of inter-event gaps and NOT a per-stage queue metric.
    rows = [dict(act=k['act'], wait=k['start'] - k['ready'])
            for c in cases for k in c['tasks'] if k['start'] is not None]
    df = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    return df.groupby('act')['wait'].mean().round(1).to_dict() if len(df) else {}

def to_event_log(log):
    ev = pd.DataFrame(log)
    base = pd.Timestamp('2024-01-15')
    ev['start_timestamp'] = base + pd.to_timedelta(ev['start'], unit='m')
    ev['time:timestamp'] = base + pd.to_timedelta(ev['end'], unit='m')
    ev['case:concept:name'] = ev['case']; ev['concept:name'] = ev['act']
    ev['org:resource'] = ev['res']
    return ev[['case:concept:name', 'concept:name', 'start_timestamp', 'time:timestamp',
               'org:resource', 'prio', 'ctype', 'preempted']] \
        .sort_values(['case:concept:name', 'start_timestamp']).reset_index(drop=True)

if __name__ == '__main__':
    print('== Base offered load (mean base-minutes per 2h block, multiplier 1.0) ==')

Omitted mechanisms (identified, per the scaffold contract): no multi-day backlog carryover (cases unfinished at 20:30 are censored as breaches); no rework/loopbacks, no-shows, or quality failures; breaks never interrupt an active activity (A7); handover/setup modeled as resource-occupied (A4); preemption uses resume-with-10% semantics (A3); utilization denominators are nominal shift minus break, so overtime can push utilization above 100%.

# 9. Limits and validation plan

**What cannot be concluded from expected arrivals and service ranges alone:**
- True variability structure: Poisson arrivals and Uniform service are assumptions; overdispersed arrivals or correlated surge periods would materially worsen tail delays beyond the bands in §4.
- Distribution shape within ranges and tail dependence (a bad day may correlate Mechanical, Electrical, and Software tails); U4's uniform-tail doubling is a proxy, not evidence.
- Multi-day dynamics: next-day carryover, morning backlog burn-down, and weekly patterns are invisible in a single-day horizon; P2's 8-hour target spans shifts, so cross-day staffing matters.
- Human factors: learning/erosion rates, fatigue under overtime, error/rework rates under cross-skill overflow (a 1.35–1.40 multiplier may understate the true cost if quality fails), and real preemption behavior (does the interrupted engineer actually resume cleanly?).
- Behavioral responses to the policy itself (gaming, queue hiding), which only observation can reveal.

**Pilot / backtest design (protecting P1, no unsafe full rollout):**
1. **Weeks 1–2 — shadow mode:** run C's dispatcher in advisory-only mode against the current process; log `eligible_resource_set`, `dispatch_score`, and the counterfactual assignment; validate the §2 load model against actual stage waits (calibration gate: predicted vs observed mean stage wait within ±20%).
2. **Weeks 3–6 — switchback A/B:** alternate C-rules and current/B-rules by day (swap schedules in weeks 3–4 and 5–6 to control day-of-week), **with P1 cases always routed under C rules regardless of arm** (safety floor). Primary endpoints: P2/P3 delay and SLA, handover burden, per-resource utilization; secondary: preemption counts, setup counts.
3. **Guardrails and stop rules:** abort to the safer arm if rolling 3-day P1 SLA < 88%, any resource exceeds 30-min overtime, or P3 median wait doubles. Power note: ~42 cases/day (~5.5 P1) means P1 SLA differences of 15–20 pp need ~3–4 weeks; P2/P3 endpoints (n36/day) resolve ~10 pp differences in ~2 weeks.
4. **Backtest before go-live:** replay 4–8 weeks of historical logs through the §8 simulator with empirically fitted arrival and service distributions; accept C only if it does not lose to the incumbent on any scenario-weighted objective (O1/O2) and wins on the U2/U3-style stress replays.
5. **Structural decision separate from the pilot:** the Software 16:00–20:00 coverage gap (F1) should be costed and decided on staffing grounds now — every routing policy evaluated here inherits it as a hard ceiling on evening P1 and P2 performance.
