## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Partial-order relations for any case:

Register < Triage
IdentityCheck < Assessment
DocumentCheck < Assessment
Decision < Notify

If originals are required:
    OriginalsReceipt < DocumentCheck

If case is high-risk or high-value:
    Assessment < ComplianceReview < Decision

If RequestMoreInfo occurs:
    RequestMoreInfo < new DocumentCheck < new Assessment
    and, for high-risk/high-value cases,
    new Assessment < (new or explicitly reaffirmed ComplianceReview) < Decision
```

```text
Conditional and declarative constraints:

- Register must be first; Triage immediately follows.
- Identity Check and Document Check may be concurrent only when originals are not required.
- If originals are required, Originals Receipt must precede Document Check; Identity Check may still proceed earlier.
- Compliance Review is mandatory for high-risk/high-value cases after the latest relevant Assessment and before Decision.
- Four-eyes rule: final Assessment and final Compliance Review must be performed by different people.
- After Request More Info, a new Document Check and a new Assessment are required before Decision; high-risk/high-value cases then need a new or explicitly reaffirmed Compliance Review.
- A preliminary compliance screen may occur earlier but does not replace final Compliance Review.
- A resource cannot perform two service intervals simultaneously.
- S1 advisory work may overlap with other checks.
- Queue lengths are forecasts, not deterministic guarantees.
```

Of the four interventions:

- **K-104** changes timing and scheduling: it adds preliminary S1/compliance preparation and a C1 reservation, but does not change routing or the allowed decision policy.
- **K-219** changes routing: the Document Check task is moved from D1 to D2.
- **K-337** changes the allowed decision policy: it forbids the second Request More Info loop and permits conditional approval or rejection on incomplete evidence.
- **K-411** changes routing and timing: it creates both verification tasks at Triage and allows true concurrency, with a logging requirement.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

`Factual mechanism:`
The case was delayed mainly by the D1 queue and later by the C1 queue. Document Check did not start until Mon21:40. Assessment did not start until Tue15:10, and A1 then found the sanctions ambiguity that Triage had noticed. The separate escalation advice took two hours, and C1 had to reconstruct the file from raw documents, producing a 4.5-hour final review.

`Minimal intervention:`
At Triage, the system sends a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and provisionally reserves a C1 slot. No task order changes. The preliminary packet is an information and scheduling change, not a replacement for final compliance.

`Temporal propagation:`
S1 can prepare the packet while document and identity checks are still in the queue. By the time Assessment starts, the file already has a structured escalation summary. Assessment still cannot start earlier because D1 and A1 queues are unchanged. The first real saving is after Assessment: the S1 update is shorter, and C1’s final review can use the prepared packet. Whether the final C1 start moves earlier depends on whether the reservation actually protects C1 capacity; if not, only the service duration shrinks.

`Process-feasibility check:`
Register precedes Triage; no originals are required, so Identity and Document may be concurrent. Both complete before Assessment. Final Compliance follows Assessment and precedes Decision. A1 performs Assessment and C1 performs final Compliance, satisfying the four-eyes rule. S1 advisory work may overlap with other checks.

`Best/base/worst case:`
Best: C1 slot is honored at Wed11:30 and final review takes 2h, giving a decision by Wed14:00 and a cycle-time saving of about 4.5h. Base: C1 starts at the factual time, Wed13:10, but review takes 3h instead of 4.5h, saving about 2h. Worst: the early packet is incomplete, C1 must reread materials, and the reservation delays another urgent case; local cycle time is flat or slightly worse.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`
If S1’s packet is stale or incomplete, C1’s final review may be no shorter. If the C1 reservation is implemented by preempting another urgent compliance case, the system-wide effect is negative even if this case improves. If C1 is so heavily loaded that the reservation cannot be honored, the saving disappears.

### K-219

`Factual mechanism:`
Originals arrived Mon17:30, but the case was routed to D1, whose queue was long. Document Check therefore did not start until Tue09:30. D1 completed it in 4h. Assessment then waited for A2 until Tue20:00.

`Minimal intervention:`
When originals arrive, the Document Check is assigned to D2 instead of D1. No other changes are made.

`Temporal propagation:`
D2’s queue at Mon17:30 is shorter than D1’s queue, so D2 can start the document task earlier. D2 may be slower, but an earlier start can still produce earlier completion. Earlier Document completion makes Assessment eligible earlier, but A2’s queue still controls the actual Assessment start. D2 is also more likely to request clarification on difficult handwriting; if that happens, the external response time of 8–36h can erase the queue advantage and make the intervention slower than the factual path.

`Process-feasibility check:`
Originals Receipt precedes Document Check, satisfying rule 4. Identity Check may complete before originals arrive. The case is not high-risk or high-value, so no final compliance review is required. A2 is appropriate for a standard case.

`Best/base/worst case:`
Best: D2 starts Mon19:30, completes by Tue02:30, A2 starts Tue13:00, and decision/notify occur Tue16:00–16:10, saving about 16h. Base: D2 starts around Tue00:00, completes Tue06:00, A2 starts Tue15:00, and decision/notify occur Tue18:00, saving about 15h. Worst: D2 requests clarification, external response takes a day or more, and the rework loop leaves the case later than the factual Wednesday decision.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`
If D2’s queue at 17:30 is not actually shorter, or if the handwritten documents require clarification, the intervention can lengthen the case. If A2’s queue is already saturated, the earlier document completion will not translate into earlier decision.

### K-337

`Factual mechanism:`
After Assessment1, Request More Info 1 led to Document2 and Assessment2. Assessment2 still found one inconsistency and issued Request More Info 2. That request crossed a weekend and was followed by Document3 and Assessment3 before the final compliance review.

`Minimal intervention:`
After Assessment2, the second Request More Info loop is prohibited. The decision is made on the available evidence, either as conditional approval or rejection. This is a decision-policy change, not merely a time-saving change.

`Temporal propagation:`
Removing Request More Info 2 removes the roughly 70-hour external wait and the subsequent Document3 and Assessment3. The next mandatory step is final compliance after Assessment2. The remaining uncertainty is C1 availability on Friday. If C1 can review the case on Friday, decision and notify can occur the same day. If not, the case waits until Monday. The unresolved discrepancy does not disappear; it moves into the decision, conditions, or rejection.

`Process-feasibility check:`
The first Request More Info loop was satisfied by Document2 and Assessment2. No new Request More Info is issued after Assessment2, so no new Document Check or Assessment is required. As a high-risk/high-value case, it still needs final Compliance after Assessment2 and before Decision. A1 performs final Assessment and C1 performs final Compliance, preserving four-eyes.

`Best/base/worst case:`
Best: C1 is available Friday12:30 and completes by 15:30, with decision/notify Friday16:00–16:10; cycle time falls to about 100h, saving about 4.8 days. Base: C1 starts Monday10:00 and decision/notify occur Monday13:30; cycle time is about 170h, saving about 2 days. Worst: the unresolved discrepancy is material; conditional approval leads to an appeal, loss, or compliance challenge, and the apparent time saving is offset by post-decision costs.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`
If the remaining inconsistency is decision-relevant, suppressing the loop creates a high-risk decision. If the evidence is sufficient for a well-specified conditional approval, the intervention is defensible. The conclusion is sensitive to whether “available evidence” is actually adequate.

### K-411

`Factual mechanism:`
Triage created only the identity task because of a legacy system limitation. The case lock prevented the document task from being created until identity completed. This forced I2 and D2 to work sequentially, and Document Check did not start until Tue15:00. Assessment then waited for A2 until Wed09:30.

`Minimal intervention:`
At Triage, create both the identity and document tasks. Use a controlled workaround to break the case lock so I2 and D2 can work concurrently. Require real-time timestamps for both tasks.

`Temporal propagation:`
The critical path changes from the sum of identity and document durations to the maximum of the two durations. I2 and D2 are different resources, so concurrency is physically feasible. D2 may not start at exactly Tue11:30 if its queue has backlog, but it can start well before the factual Tue15:00. Once both tasks complete, Assessment becomes eligible. The A2 queue is the next potential bottleneck; if it is already long, the end-to-end gain is smaller than the verification-span gain.

`Process-feasibility check:`
Originals are not required, so Identity Check and Document Check may be concurrent. Both complete before Assessment. No compliance review is needed because the case is low-risk and low-value. I2 and D2 are distinct resources and do not overlap in service.

`Best/base/worst case:`
Best: Identity completes Tue13:30 and Document completes Tue16:30; A2 starts Tue19:00, with decision/notify Tue21:00–21:10, saving about 17h. Base: Document completes Tue17:00 and A2 starts Wed08:00; decision/notify occur Wed11:30–11:40, saving about 3h. Worst: the workaround logs false concurrency or the A2 queue grows, leaving the total cycle unchanged or worse.

`Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:`
If A2 is the true bottleneck, the intervention will not reduce end-to-end cycle time. If timestamps are unreliable, the apparent parallelism is not real, and the process may fail an audit or hide a missing service.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 | Adds S1 preliminary work; reserves C1 time; C1 final duration may fall | Target case’s C1 wait, if reservation is honored | C1 queue for non-target urgent cases; S1 advisory backlog | Other cases needing C1 or S1; urgent compliance cases | Ambiguous; negative if C1 is already saturated | C1 reservation is zero-sum; at high utilization, small reservations create nonlinear waiting |
| K-219 | Shifts document work from D1 to D2; D2 service is slower and rework-prone | D1 queue | D2 queue; document queue if clarification is needed | Cases waiting for D2; downstream assessment cases | Positive if D1 was the bottleneck; negative if D2 becomes the bottleneck | D2 clarification probability creates a feedback loop; D2 backlog can grow nonlinearly |
| K-337 | Removes one Document Check, one Assessment, and one external Request More Info | D1 and A1 queues in aggregate; fewer external-response waits | Post-decision monitoring, appeals, or conditional-approval follow-ups outside the recorded process | Cases downstream in appeals/compliance-enforcement, not immediate queues | Positive for throughput; negative for quality/risk | Suppressed rework is converted into unobserved post-decision risk |
| K-411 | No total service change; identity and document tasks overlap, so completions arrive in a narrower window | Target case’s verification span; possibly D2/identity wait for that case | Assessment queue burst at the moment of clustered completions | Other cases in assessment queue; D2 queue if document workload clusters | Positive if assessment queue has slack; neutral/negative if assessment is the bottleneck | Queue arrivals become more bursty; waiting time grows superlinearly with arrival variability |

Scaling a single-case fix to 100 cases changes the story. Under K-104, every treated case reserves a C1 slot. Since C1 is one person, 100 reservations do not create 100 new hours; they relabel the same backlog. Early S1 packets can shorten each final review, but if total C1 work is not reduced, non-target urgent cases are displaced. At high utilization, even one additional urgent case can create nonlinear waiting; C1 shifts from a queue to a priority-search problem. K-219 shifts document demand from D1 to D2. D1’s queue shrinks, but D2 receives 100 additional standard-handwritten files. D2 is slower and has a higher clarification probability. Each clarification adds external waiting and rework, and D2 can become the new bottleneck while D1 becomes idle. K-337 removes second Request More Info loops and saves about 100 weekend waits, but it also produces conditional approvals or rejections. These outcomes may generate appeals, compliance challenges, post-decision corrections, and monitoring work outside the recorded process. What looked like eliminated rework is transferred to later stages. K-411 clusters completions: 100 parallel verifications deliver 100 assessment-ready files in a narrow arrival window. The assessment queue—already forecast at 5–7h—will grow transiently. If A1/A2 cannot absorb the burst, end-to-end cycle time improves less than verification time. Real-time timestamps are essential; without them, the system can claim concurrency that never existed, invalidating the pilot. Queue displacement and bottleneck migration are therefore the main evaluation criteria, not case-level cycle time.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Eligibility | No-original, digital, standard cases with both verification tasks; exclude cases already in lock failure | High-risk/high-value cases without originals required; not already past the C1 queue |
| Assignment rule | Stratified randomization at Triage by queue load; workaround enabled only for treatment arm | Block randomization by C1 queue load; provisional C1 reservation only for treatment arm |
| Sample/support logic | 30–50 cases per arm over 2 weeks; monitor queue depth and service times | 15–25 cases per arm over 2–3 weeks to limit C1 displacement |
| Primary outcome | Time from Triage to assessment eligibility and to notify; true overlap of timestamps | Time from registration to decision/notify; final C1 review duration |
| Process-path outcome | No skipped identity/document checks; both tasks before Assessment; no duplicate tasks | S1 packet before Assessment; C1 review after Assessment; four-eyes and mandatory order preserved |
| Quality outcome | Audit findings, decision consistency, document errors | Approval/condition/rejection mix; appeal/adverse outcomes; compliance completeness |
| Queue-spillover outcome | Non-target D2/assessment waits; peak assessment arrivals | Non-target C1 wait; urgent compliance case delay; S1 backlog |
| Logging validation | Compare real-time event log with manual stamps; reject overlapping false records | Audit C1 reservation ledger; verify preliminary packet timestamp; ensure final C1 occurs after latest Assessment |
| Stopping rule | Stop if false concurrency exceeds 5% or spillover threshold is crossed; continue if verification span improves | Stop if non-target C1 waits increase significantly or final review duration is not reduced; continue if net benefit is positive |

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411. It changes no policy, adds no scarce resource, and can be audited through real-time timestamps. Its weakness is that the A2/A1 queue absorbs part of the gain, but the intervention itself is easy to reverse and does not create compliance risk.

The most dangerous intervention, despite its speed, is K-337. It can remove many days, but suppressing the second request-for-information changes the decision policy and leaves a known discrepancy unresolved. The resulting conditional approval or rejection can shift loss, appeal, and compliance risk outside the observed process, making the measured speed partly illusory.

The least transportable local counterfactual is K-104’s C1 reservation. In one case, a reserved slot looks harmless; with 100 cases, C1 is a singleton and every reservation is zero-sum. What is a timing improvement locally becomes a priority displacement and a bottleneck migration. K-337 is also nontransportable because a few suppressed rework loops may be tolerable, but suppressing them systematically changes the risk profile of the whole portfolio.

The most valuable additional data are actual queue-wait distributions at the moment of arrival, not only snapshot point estimates; C1 reservation and preemption behavior; D2 clarification probabilities by document type; assessment queue interarrival and service distributions; and post-decision outcomes such as conditions, appeals, and rework. Real-time timestamps are especially important for K-411 to distinguish genuine concurrency from batch backdating. Without these data, a local improvement cannot be safely scaled.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. Structural: The normative process order and four-eyes rule are invariant, and no undocumented approval override is allowed.
2. Temporal: Work can continue outside normal business hours, and queue forecasts are estimates, not deterministic commitments.
3. Resource: C1 is the only fully certified final compliance reviewer, and S1 advisory work does not contend with C1.
4. Resource: D1 is faster on complex corporate documents, while D2 is slower and more generalist; standard handwritten documents are within D2’s scope but carry higher clarification risk.
5. Temporal: External supplier responses take 8–36 hours and do not usually occur on weekends, which explains the weekend delay in K-337.
6. Behavioral: The preliminary S1 packet is complete enough to reduce final C1 review time without causing a second read.
7. Quality: Decisions made with unresolved evidence may be conditioned or rejected, and such decisions carry higher appeal/loss risk than confirmed approval.
8. Interference: Queue interactions are linear only at low load; at high utilization, sudden reductions or increases create nonlinear waiting-time effects.
