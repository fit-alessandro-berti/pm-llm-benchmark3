## 2. Counterfactual continuations

2. **C, S2 now:** Evidence Validated → renewed R17 Analyst Review → independent S2 review → approve and execute within the deadline if the evidence supports it and timing permits; otherwise reject or hold/report. The 0.55–0.75 ultimate-approval prediction is a starting forecast, not the probability of approval after assigning S2. The main extra branch is P-901 breaching because S2 was interrupted.
3. **E, more evidence:** Evidence Validated → Evidence Requested → Evidence Received → Evidence Validated → Analyst Review again → independent second review if the trigger remains → approve/execute later, reject, or hold/report. New evidence may resolve the match or reveal a real hit; arrival in 6–24 hours almost certainly misses this deadline. 4. **F, conditional rejection:** Evidence Validated → renewed Analyst Review and threshold check → documented Rejected if the applicable standard is met. If the evidence does not support rejection, the continuation is hold and independent review, not a speculative rejection. The uncertainty is whether a 0.46-confidence unresolved match and the other case facts meet that standard, and whether any local rejection review requirement applies.
5. **G, exception request:** Request director authorization and a genuine incident identifier while the ordinary review proceeds. If both are obtained, recorded, and the authorization actually covers this payment and proposed deviation, a scoped exception decision may follow. If either fails or arrives too late, continue independent review or hold; no payment execution follows merely from filing a report or asking for an override. The main uncertainties are director availability, incident validity, scope, and lost time.

## 3. Benefit and harm reasoning

The direct deadline upside is bounded by the stated €20,000–€35,000 and requires both approval and execution within 90 minutes. Avoiding a false rejection or prolonged hold may protect €15,000–€80,000, but the probability and duration of that harm are unknown. These values may describe the same customer loss and must not be double counted. Conversely, executing without a valid path creates plausible €100,000–€500,000 regulatory exposure and non-monetary sanctions, reputational, and control-integrity harm. This asymmetry makes A and B dominated as admissible choices even if a narrow financial forecast favors their speed. An appropriate rejection or justified hold does not incur the same *false*-decision harm, although it may still miss the deadline.

The 0.48–0.64 breach forecast describes normal processing, and 0.55–0.75 approval describes observed conformant cases. Neither is a causal estimate for D, C, screening, S2 interruption, rejection, or override. There is no defensible point expected value without intervention effects, the monetary consequence of P-901's breach, the probability of a real sanctions hit, and the applicable decision thresholds. The fuzzy-match confidence of 0.46 is not itself a 46% probability that payment is lawful. The 12-minute-old score should be checked against the required freshness standard and recalculated if necessary; it cannot simply be assumed to have fallen below 70.

## 4. Conformance constraints

The evidence cycle in the observed prefix ends at Evidence Validated, so the prescribed return to Analyst Review is still due. R17 can perform that renewed first-analyst review; S2 or S4 must independently perform any required second review. The current score of 72 independently meets the at-least-70 trigger, and the fuzzy match is independently unresolved. Either trigger alone requires a second officer before Approve or Payment Executed. A compliant approval must then be recorded before execution.

- **A:** Violates the second-review and approval-path requirements now. Favorable deadline economics do not waive a mandatory control.
- **B:** R17 and the second officer must be different people. Self-certification does not create an independent review; favorable forecasts cannot make it valid, and a purported certification could damage the audit record.
- **C:** Valid if S2 is eligible, independent of R17, actually reviews the evidence, and the approval path is completed before execution.
- **D:** Alternate screening supplies evidence, not authorization. R17 must reassess the validated record; the independent review remains required under current triggers, even if the alternate vendor calls the match false.
- **E:** The repeat evidence cycle is allowed; once evidence is validated, return to Analyst Review and apply the second-review trigger again.
- **F:** Requires a documented rejection standard actually met by the evidence, a renewed analyst decision after the evidence cycle, and any review authority required for rejection. The facts given do not prove the threshold. Unresolved uncertainty alone is not automatically proof of a rejectable hit.
- **G:** The exception is not enabled until a Compliance Director actually authorizes it and a valid, justified incident identifier is recorded. Its scope must cover the proposed deviation; a pending request is insufficient. A Suspicious Activity Report may accompany a hold, but does not authorize execution and must not be disclosed to the customer.

## 5. Resource spillovers

S2 offers the best stated chance of finishing P-884 inside 90 minutes: 35–75 minutes for review plus 5–15 minutes for the final record/execution, with any renewed R17 review time added. Yet taking S2 from P-901 for 45 minutes raises that case's breach probability by 0.12–0.28. P-901 is also high value and has only 70 minutes; its monetary harm is unspecified, so the increment cannot be converted to euros or ignored. If S2's work lasts more than 45 minutes, the given spillover estimate may understate the impact. Triage P-901's remaining work and available cover before diverting S2, then document the two-case priority decision.

S4 avoids that displacement. If S4 arrives at 35 minutes, even the shortest stated review and completion take P-884 to about minute 75, leaving only 15 minutes of slack; with S4 in hand and R17's renewed review done, the S4 branch of D ranks above C. If S4 arrives at 65 minutes, the minimum is about minute 105, so S4 cannot meet P-884's deadline on the stated durations. In that state, C rises above an S4-only D plan if P-901's assessed spillover is acceptable; otherwise accept a documented P-884 hold rather than break a control. Obtain a firm ETA immediately: waiting until minute 65 to *then* request S2 defeats the contingency.

An alternate screening call can clarify a consequential sanctions ambiguity during the S4 wait or S2 review. It consumes one of only three expedited calls and does not replace the independent review under current conditions. Spend it when its result can inform the decision in time; if the queue has a more urgent use or its result would arrive after the decision, omit it and proceed with review. Likewise, do not let the 20–60-minute director response or creation of an incident identifier block the ordinary review path. A genuine exception is a contingency, not a promised shortcut.

## 6. Final recommendation and confidence

**Primary:** Start R17's renewed review and fresh risk/match check now. Run the alternate screen in parallel if its result can arrive before the officer's decision, and immediately secure a firm S4 ETA plus a P-901 impact assessment. Use S4 if an approximately 35-minute arrival and credible completion within 90 minutes are confirmed. **Fallback:** If neither officer can provide a valid timely decision, hold and obtain genuinely needed evidence rather than execute or self-certify. Reject only if the documented rejection standard is met. Confidence is **moderate in the conformance ordering and low in the precise S2-versus-S4 timing choice**, because intervention effects and P-901's loss magnitude are unknown. A firm S4 availability estimate, an alternate-screening result, or a fresh score/match determination—whichever arrives first—should trigger immediate reranking; a confirmed sanctions hit or adequate rejection evidence should trigger a new decision at once.
