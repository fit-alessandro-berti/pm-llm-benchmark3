## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

### Stipulated facts

1. **M0’s control-flow semantics are clear.** For a case covered by M0:
   - `Register_Supplier` precedes both `Sanctions_Check` and `Quality_Review`.
   - The two checks are concurrent in the partial-order sense: neither is required to occur before the other, but both must complete before the XOR decision.
   - The approved branch requires `Manual_Procurement_Approval` before `Activate_Supplier`.
   - The rejection branch has `Reject_Supplier`.
   - `Notify_Requester` precedes `Close_Case`.
   - Consequently, M0 requires `Quality_Review` before rejection as well as before activation, even though CS-9 expressly states the quality requirement only in relation to activation.

2. **CS-9 is the strongest stated baseline authority.** It is controlled, formally approved, and effective from 2025-01-01. It requires:
   - sanctions and quality checks before activation;
   - manual procurement approval before activation; and
   - requester/manual-approver separation of duties.

3. **M0 is incomplete even if retained as a baseline.** It does not represent:
   - the requester/approver identity rule;
   - conflicts of interest;
   - pilot eligibility;
   - emergency spend caps, monitoring, or retrospective deadlines;
   - CRM notification correlation;
   - reactivation;
   - withdrawal;
   - label mappings; or
   - `Request_More_Info` cycles.

4. **PD-3 was a documented temporary pilot decision, not a formal amendment to CS-9 or M0.** Its stated effective period was 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. It permitted a specified auto-approval route only for low-risk DE suppliers in approved corporate groups, with sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation and quality within two business days after provisional activation.

5. **FD-7 was a written emergency directive with a fixed expiry.** It allowed limited provisional activation from 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12 for critical flood-relief suppliers, subject to sanctions-before-activation, spending limits, daily monitoring, and retrospective controls.

6. **The CRM migration created a known data-quality problem.** After 2026-07-01, only about 70% of notifications were mirrored into the onboarding log. A stable `notification_id` is stronger evidence than a mutable free-text case link.

7. **Several observed facts are directly corroborated.**
   - SUP-701 is a clean standard-route case.
   - SUP-703 is high-risk, foreign, and plainly outside PD-3’s stated eligibility.
   - SUP-704 occurred during FD-7’s stated window and has evidence of the stated safeguards.
   - SUP-705 occurred after FD-7’s expiry.
   - SUP-708 was activated 40 minutes before sanctions clearance.
   - SUP-712’s requester and approver resolve to the same human through aliases.
   - SUP-711 has duplicate registration records with the same source transaction ID.
   - SUP-715 has no recorded conflict-of-interest disclosure.

### Disputed or unproved propositions

| Proposition | Status |
|---|---|
| PD-3 had delegated authority to derogate from CS-9 for individual pilot cases | Unproved. It was a steering-committee decision, but the record supplied does not establish the committee’s authority to waive a controlled compliance standard. The Compliance observer’s “monitored exception” comment supports narrow operational tolerance, but her later statement cuts against treating PD-3 as a general approval to weaken CS-9. |
| FD-7 had sufficient emergency authority | Strongly supported as an issued directive, and no contrary evidence is supplied. Its scope and expiry remain binding. |

| SUP-709 was in M0’s scope | Disputed. CS-9 permits abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads but does not define whether those leads are onboarding cases governed by M0. |

### Burden-of-proof approach

A defensible review should use two related but different standards.

**To declare confirmed noncompliance:**
1. Establish that a rule applied to the case at the relevant time.
2. Establish the relevant event order, actor identity, or data condition from reliable evidence.
3. Establish that no valid exception, alternative scope rule, or data correction applies.
4. Use the balance of credible evidence, while treating immutable timestamps, source transaction IDs, and independent system evidence as stronger than manually editable text.

A raw M0 trace deviation is therefore **prima facie technical nonconformance**, not automatically business misconduct.

**To accept a justified exception:**
1. There must be a contemporaneous written rule or exception.
2. The approving authority must have authority to derogate from the baseline.
3. The case must satisfy the rule’s date, scope, risk, geography, and other predicates.
4. Required safeguards and evidence must be recorded.
5. The rule/version and authority reference must be linked to the case.

The operation bears the practical burden of retaining exception evidence. An informal belief that a pilot was becoming permanent, a continuing factual emergency after expiry, or a later mapping approval does not retroactively create authority.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

The strongest prosecution case is that CS-9 remains the controlling baseline and that M0, while incomplete, correctly captures its core sequencing for ordinary accepted onboarding cases. The evidence shows not merely harmless trace variance, but repeated bypass of preventive controls, weak exception governance, and normalization of temporary practices.

### Formal authority and expiry matter

CS-9 is controlled and formally approved. PD-3 was not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. A steering-committee decision may potentially authorize a narrowly governed pilot exception, but that authority is not established merely because operations followed it. The Compliance observer’s statement that the pilot “may proceed as a monitored exception” is not the same as approving a permanent weakening of CS-9; her later clarification expressly denies such approval.

FD-7 is stronger as an emergency directive, but it expired automatically. SUP-705 occurred on 2026-07-13. The continuation of flood recovery is a factual circumstance, not an extension of authority. Emergency powers are valuable precisely because they are bounded. Treating the continued emergency as self-extending defeats the expiry control.

### Substantive core-control breaches

- **SUP-703** is serious noncompliance. It used `Auto_Approve` for a high-risk new foreign supplier, outside PD-3 eligibility. Quality occurred after activation, contrary to CS-9. The EUR 180,000 first purchase does not itself violate PD-3’s terms, but it demonstrates why eligibility controls matter. There is no emergency basis.

- **SUP-708** is a serious breach of a preventive sanctions control. Activation preceded sanctions clearance by 40 minutes, and manual approval also followed activation. The absence of transactions before clearance reduces realized harm, but does not cure the control failure. An activated supplier can create contractual, reputational, and sanctions exposure even before a purchase posts.

- **SUP-705** copied the emergency route after FD-7 expired. The EUR 12,000 amount and next-business-day retrospective controls mitigate consequence but do not supply legal authority.

- **SUP-714** used the pilot route after PD-3 expired. A belief that a permanent amendment was imminent is not an approved amendment. The six post-pilot auto-approval cases in the first August week show a concerning tendency to normalize a temporary exception.

- **SUP-712** fits M0 control-flow but violates CS-9’s explicit maker-checker requirement because the requester and manual approver are the same human through aliases. This demonstrates why trace-only conformance is insufficient.

- **SUP-715** fits M0 but raises a separate policy breach: no conflict-of-interest disclosure was recorded for a spouse/same-reporting-line relationship. The prosecution should not claim that the relationship is automatically prohibited, because no such rule is stated. It should claim the narrower and better-supported failure to disclose.

### Aggregates point to governance weakness

The aggregate evidence does not prove an overall breach rate, but it supports a material governance concern:

- Of 312 June/July auto-approval cases, 14 are clearly PD-3-ineligible and 9 lack eligibility attributes. Even if some could theoretically fall under another authorized overlay, they are not proven compliant.
- Six auto-approval cases occurred after the pilot expired.
- `Request_More_Info` appears in 47 cases. It may be desirable, but repeated use without a controlled model or procedure means that an informal process has become operationally significant.
- Notification missingness rising from 2% to 29% after migration is not proof that notifications stopped. It is, however, evidence that the organization allowed a mandatory procedural control to become poorly observable.

### Deviations hidden by M0 are not exculpatory

M0 does not detect the most important failures in SUP-712 and SUP-715. It also cannot assess pilot eligibility, emergency scope, spend caps, monitoring, actor delegation, or whether a CRM notification belongs to the correct case. The fact that M0 is incomplete is a reason to strengthen the conformance framework, not a reason to disregard CS-9.

### Desirable behavior does not amend policy

SUP-707’s information requests are sensible and may have prevented bad approvals. That does not authorize operations to create an unmanaged loop. The appropriate response is to model and govern the loop, not to treat every desirable practice as self-validating.

### Prosecution concessions

The prosecution should concede several genuine defects:

- M0 overstates the requirement for rejected leads by forcing `Quality_Review` before all rejections.
- M0 lacks valid temporary pilot and emergency routes.
- SUP-704 is materially distinguishable from SUP-705 because it fits FD-7’s time window and safeguards.
- SUP-706 cannot be confidently called a business notification failure from the supplied log alone.
- SUP-711 is likely a duplicate-data problem, not two actual registrations.
- SUP-713 may represent label drift rather than a missed quality control.
- SUP-710 may be a reactivation-scope gap rather than a fresh-onboarding violation.

Those concessions do not remove the confirmed high-risk breaches in SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and likely SUP-715.

## 3. Defense Brief

The strongest defense case is not that every deviation is acceptable. It is that M0 is an inadequate sole measure of operational compliance because it models only one standard route, treats all cases as one lifecycle, omits known overlays and evidence channels, and confuses label/trace mismatch with misconduct.

### M0 is a baseline workflow, not a complete compliance architecture

M0 correctly expresses a standard full-onboarding route, but it does not express:

- temporary pilot approval;
- emergency provisional activation;
- optional information-gathering cycles;
- lead pre-screen rejection;
- reactivation;
- notification evidence held in CRM;
- actor identity and delegation;
- conflict-of-interest disclosure;
- versioned activity mappings.

A model that cannot represent a permitted exception will necessarily report that exception as a deviation. That is a model limitation, not proof that the underlying conduct was prohibited.

### Concurrency must be interpreted correctly

M0 does not require `Sanctions_Check` to precede `Quality_Review`, or vice versa. It allows both orders and true concurrency. Therefore, ordinary traces such as SUP-701 are conformant, and a reviewer should not invent an ordering violation simply because sanctions appears before quality in the event list.

The real M0 restriction is that both checks complete before the decision node. That restriction is defensible for standard approved cases, but it may be too broad for a rejected lead that never becomes an onboarding case.

### Pilot and emergency overlays explain some apparent deviations

- **SUP-702** occurred during PD-3’s window, involved a low-risk DE supplier in an approved corporate group, and completed quality review on the next business day. CRM has a stable notification ID. The behavior fits the substantive pilot design, even though it does not fit M0. The absence of `Manual_Procurement_Approval` is not inherently misconduct if PD-3 was a valid monitored exception.

- **SUP-704** is an even stronger example. It was a critical pump supplier activated on 2026-07-11, within FD-7’s effective period. Sanctions preceded activation; initial spend was below EUR 20,000; daily monitoring exists; quality and approval followed the next business day. Treating this as ordinary CS-9 noncompliance would disregard the emergency directive’s purpose.

- **SUP-705** is weaker for the defense. Continued flood conditions may explain why operations acted, but cannot establish authority after FD-7 expired. The defense can argue mitigation, not full justification.

### CRM migration creates logging, not necessarily notification, failures

SUP-706 illustrates why missing event-log evidence is not equivalent to nonperformance. The CRM message at +3 hours is evidence that a notification may have occurred. The case link is weak because it was mutable and entered late, so the defense cannot prove conformance. But the prosecution cannot validly prove that no notification occurred either.

The 29% notification missingness after migration strongly indicates collection failure. A conformance program should reconcile CRM delivery records rather than treating a partial mirror as the authoritative source.

### Scope ambiguity affects rejected leads and reactivations

- **SUP-709:** CS-9 expressly acknowledges abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads. It does not say those leads are M0 cases. If the lead failed sanctions pre-screen and was never accepted into onboarding, the absence of quality review is not necessarily a breach. M0’s requirement for quality before rejection is stricter than the stated CS-9 activation requirement.

- **SUP-710:** A supplier closed six months earlier may be a reactivation, not a new onboarding. Prior quality certification remained valid, while fresh sanctions and manual approval occurred. CS-9 contains no reactivation procedure. It is unfair to infer a fresh registration and fresh quality-review requirement solely because M0 has no reactivation branch.

### Label and data defects should not be treated as behavior defects

- **SUP-711:** two register events with the same source transaction ID are a deterministic migration duplicate. The logical business event occurred once.
- **SUP-713:** procedure and UI renamed `Quality_Review` to `QA_Clearance`, and audit material supports equivalent content. A later-approved mapping is a governance weakness, but not evidence that quality control failed.
- **SUP-707:** repeated information requests obtained missing certificates before approval. The behavior enhanced, rather than weakened, due diligence. M0 lacks a loop and therefore falsely treats a beneficial trace enrichment as nonconformance.

### Distinguish trace deviation from business misconduct

The defense should insist on this distinction:

- A trace can fail M0 because of an extra event, an alias, a duplicate, an external system event, a permitted temporary route, or a lifecycle type M0 does not model.
- Business misconduct requires proof that an applicable substantive rule was breached or that a valid exception was absent.

SUP-703 and SUP-708 remain difficult to defend because their deviations directly defeat sanctions, eligibility, and approval safeguards. By contrast, SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, SUP-713, and potentially SUP-709 and SUP-710 are better characterized as model, scope, or data problems.

### Defense concessions

The defense cannot plausibly claim that informal practice has made permanent auto-approval lawful. PD-3 expired. FD-7 expired. Neither was incorporated into CS-9. The defense also cannot rely on incomplete logs to prove controls were performed; it can only prevent unsupported accusations.

It must acknowledge:
- SUP-703 is outside pilot eligibility;
- SUP-708 breached preventive controls;
- SUP-705 and SUP-714 lack an active written overlay;
- SUP-712 violates an explicit identity rule if the alias resolution is accurate; and
- SUP-715 at least lacks required conflict-of-interest evidence.

### System-level judgment on operational compliance

The operation is **materially noncompliant as a compliance system**, even though not every M0 deviation is a substantive breach.

The material conclusion is supported by:
- confirmed high-risk failures in SUP-703 and SUP-708;
- post-expiry exception use in SUP-705 and SUP-714;
- a direct segregation-of-duties failure in SUP-712;
- an apparent disclosure-control failure in SUP-715;
- 14 clearly PD-3-ineligible auto-approval cases among 312 June/July cases;
- 9 more auto-approval cases with missing eligibility attributes; and
- six post-pilot auto-approval cases in the first August week.

The evidence does **not** support a quantified population-wide noncompliance rate because the case set is not demonstrated to be representative and the aggregate data lacks complete denominator, timing, and authority details. It does support a conclusion that exception governance, evidence capture, and policy-version enforcement are materially weak.

### System-level judgment on M0’s validity

M0 remains a reasonable **baseline control-flow model for ordinary accepted onboarding**, but it is not a complete normative architecture.

It is defective as the sole model because it:
- omits valid temporary-route capability;
- cannot represent data-guarded eligibility;
- cannot test resource and conflict rules;
- does not distinguish lead pre-screening from onboarding;
- lacks reactivation and withdrawal lifecycle states;
- lacks information-request loops;
- treats CRM evidence as invisible; and
- lacks activity-code/version mapping.

It is also arguably over-restrictive for rejected leads because it requires quality review before all rejections, while CS-9’s stated quality condition is tied to activation.

### Where the prosecution argument is rejected

The prosecution is rejected to the extent it equates every raw M0 mismatch with misconduct. SUP-704, SUP-707, SUP-711, SUP-713, and potentially SUP-709 and SUP-710 show why that is unsound. Notification missingness after the CRM migration also cannot be treated as proof that notifications were not sent.

### Where the defense argument is rejected

The defense is rejected to the extent it treats favorable operational outcomes, continued emergencies, incomplete logs, or anticipated policy changes as substitutes for authority. PD-3 did not become permanent through repetition. FD-7 did not self-extend. No transactions before sanctions clearance does not cure SUP-708. A desirable information-request practice does not legitimize ungoverned process change.

The correct remedy is therefore neither “enforce M0 unchanged” nor “accept current practice as policy.” It is to retain the standard baseline, formalize permitted overlays, clarify scope, and strengthen data and organizational controls.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The revised architecture should separate structural workflow from eligibility, authority, resource, and evidence rules.

### 1. Revised control-flow layer

The control-flow model should contain distinct structural routes:

1. **Standard accepted onboarding**
   - Register.
   - Sanctions and quality checks may occur in either order or concurrently.
   - Optional repeatable `Request_More_Info` / evidence-receipt cycles may occur before the decision.
   - Manual procurement approval precedes activation.
   - Notification precedes closure.

2. **Temporary low-risk auto-approval route**
   - Register.
   - Sanctions and automated master-data validation precede auto-approval and provisional activation.
   - Quality may follow provisional activation within a governed deadline.
   - This route exists structurally but is executable only when an active policy overlay permits it.

3. **Emergency provisional route**
   - Register.
   - Sanctions precede provisional activation.
   - Quality review, retrospective approval, and monitoring evidence follow activation.
   - Spend limits, calendar deadlines, and daily monitoring are outside POWL.

4. **Lead pre-screen rejection**
   - A separately typed `lead_prescreen` case may have sanctions followed by rejection without quality review.
   - This route must not silently be used for an already accepted onboarding case.

5. **Reactivation**
   - Explicit reactivation assessment.
   - Fresh sanctions.
   - Either a current quality review or documented validation of still-valid prior certification.
   - Manual approval before reactivation, unless a future controlled policy states otherwise.

6. **Withdrawal**
   - A pre-activation withdrawal route with notification and closure.
   - A withdrawal after activation should invoke a separate deactivation/offboarding process, not merely close an onboarding case.

### 2. Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules

These belong primarily in SQL/pandas-style checks or a rules engine, not in POWL.

Examples:

- `standard_manual` route:
  - `Sanctions_Check.status == "passed"` before `Activate_Supplier`;
  - `Quality_Review.status == "passed"` before activation;
  - valid manual approval before activation.

- `pilot_auto` route:
  - `risk_rating == "LOW"`;
  - `country == "DE"`;
  - approved corporate-group membership as of event time;
  - automated master-data validation pass;
  - occurrence within PD-3’s effective window;
  - quality completed no later than two local business days after provisional activation.

- `emergency_provisional` route:
  - activated during directive window;
  - verified critical-relief purpose;
  - sanctions passed before activation;
  - cumulative spend before retrospective approval is at or below EUR 20,000;
  - daily monitoring for each applicable local business date;
  - quality and retrospective approval within two business days.

- Notification:
  - a CRM delivery or send event must be correlated through immutable `notification_id`;
  - `Notify_Requester` must precede `Close_Case`.

- `Request_More_Info`:
  - each request must have a linked evidence receipt, withdrawal, or formally approved expiry;
  - activation cannot occur while unresolved required-evidence items remain.

### 3. Resource and conflict-of-interest rules

These should be implemented through identity resolution, HR/role data, delegation records, and organizational controls.

- Canonical requester person ID must differ from canonical manual approver person ID.
- Alias resolution must be versioned and auditable.
- A delegated approver must have a valid delegation record effective at approval time.
- Conflict-of-interest policy should require:
  - disclosure of defined relationships;
  - a recorded conflict assessment;
  - recusal or independent review where policy requires it.
- The current facts support a disclosure rule for SUP-715; a categorical prohibition on spouses or reporting-line relationships should be added only through an explicit policy amendment.

### 4. Policy-overlay governance

Use a formal policy registry rather than embedding temporary policy in informal process behavior.

Each overlay must identify:
- policy/rule version;
- authority and delegation basis;
- effective start and end timestamps;
- applicable case types and route;
- predicates and exclusions;
- required compensating controls;
- evidence requirements;
- owner and revocation status.

A structural auto-approval branch must not mean auto-approval is permanently permitted. The route is merely a modeled capability. A live policy-overlay record determines whether it may be used for a particular case.

### 5. Scope rules

The revised policy should explicitly define:

| Case type | Proposed scope rule |
|---|---|
| `lead_prescreen` | A lead that fails sanctions before acceptance and before supplier-master creation is not a full onboarding case. It must follow a minimal documented pre-screen process and be notified/closed. |
| `accepted_onboarding` | Full CS-9 baseline applies unless an active, authorized overlay applies. |
| `rejection_after_acceptance` | Define whether quality is required before rejection. At minimum, no activation may occur without sanctions, quality, and the required approval path. |
| `reactivation` | Require reactivation assessment, fresh sanctions, and quality either newly performed or formally validated as current; define material-change and expiry conditions. |
| `withdrawal_pre_activation` | May close without activation; must preserve reason, notification, and evidence status. |
| `withdrawal_post_activation` | Must be routed to deactivation/offboarding controls. |

### Allocation of controls to technologies

| Control type | Best implementation |
|---|---|
| Structural ordering, XOR choices, standard/manual/auto/emergency route shapes, notification-before-close | POWL |
| Response, precedence, absence, and route-specific event-pattern checks | DECLARE/log-skeleton-style constraints |
| Eligibility, dates, business-day deadlines, amounts, cumulative spend, evidence completeness, duplicate IDs, CRM reconciliation | pandas/SQL/rules engine |
| Alias resolution, delegation validity, separation of duties, conflicts, policy approval, emergency authority, training, exception approval | Organizational controls and identity/governance systems |
| Label equivalence and schema evolution | Versioned activity-code registry and mapping governance |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following model deliberately represents permitted structural routes without treating them as permanently authorized. It uses repeated information-request cycles as an optional loop. The loop is an approximation: POWL can represent repeated request/evidence cycles, but it cannot by itself enforce that every outstanding request is resolved, calculate business days, or bind a selected branch to a policy predicate.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def sequence(*nodes):
    """Strict sequence of POWL nodes."""
    po = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for left, right in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        po.order.add_edge(left, right)
    return po


def parallel(*nodes):
    """Concurrent/unordered children in a strict partial order."""
    return StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))


def xor(*nodes):
    return OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=list(nodes))


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# Optional repeatable Request_More_Info mechanism.
#
# LOOP(do, redo) gives one or more executions of do; redo is silent.
# The enclosing XOR with silence allows zero cycles.
# Each cycle requires Request_More_Info before Evidence_Received.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
evidence_received = Transition(label="Evidence_Received")
info_cycle = sequence(request_more_info, evidence_received)

repeat_info_cycles = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[info_cycle, SilentTransition()]
)

optional_info_cycles = xor(
    SilentTransition(),       # no information request
    repeat_info_cycles        # one or more request/evidence cycles
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Standard accepted-onboarding route.
# Sanctions and quality are concurrent. Information cycles may interleave
# with them because there are no ordering edges among the three nodes.
# All must complete before the approval/rejection decision.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
register_standard = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions_standard = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
quality_standard = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

standard_predecision = parallel(
    sanctions_standard,
    quality_standard,
    optional_info_cycles,
)

manual_approval_standard = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
activate_standard = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
standard_approved = sequence(
    manual_approval_standard,
    activate_standard,
)

reject_after_full_review = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
standard_decision = xor(
    standard_approved,
    reject_after_full_review,
)

standard_case = sequence(
    register_standard,
    standard_predecision,
    standard_decision,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Low-risk auto-approval route.
# This is only a structural capability. A policy overlay must prove:
# low risk, DE, approved group, active rule window, and validation pass.
# Quality is modeled as post-provisional-activation in this exception route.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
register_auto = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions_auto = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
master_data_validation = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
auto_preconditions = parallel(
    sanctions_auto,
    master_data_validation,
)

auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
activate_auto = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
quality_after_auto = Transition(label="Quality_Review")

low_risk_auto_case = sequence(
    register_auto,
    auto_preconditions,
    auto_approve,
    activate_auto,
    quality_after_auto,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Emergency/provisional route.
# Monitoring is represented as one abstract evidence event. Daily frequency,
# amount caps, and business-day deadlines are enforced by data checks.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
register_emergency = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions_emergency = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
provisional_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

quality_emergency = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
retrospective_approval = Transition(
    label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"
)
monitoring_evidence = Transition(
    label="Daily_Transaction_Monitoring"
)

emergency_post_activation = parallel(
    quality_emergency,
    retrospective_approval,
    monitoring_evidence,
)

emergency_case = sequence(
    register_emergency,
    sanctions_emergency,
    provisional_activate,
    emergency_post_activation,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Lead pre-screen rejection route.
# This route is only valid when case_type == "lead_prescreen" and the
# controlled scope rule says the lead was never accepted into onboarding.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
register_lead = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
sanctions_lead = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
reject_lead = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")

lead_prescreen_rejection = sequence(
    register_lead,
    sanctions_lead,
    reject_lead,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Withdrawal before activation.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
register_withdrawal = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
withdraw_request = Transition(label="Withdraw_Request")

withdrawal_case = sequence(
    register_withdrawal,
    withdraw_request,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# 6. Reactivation route.
# The route permits either a new Quality_Review or a controlled validation
# that a prior quality certification remains valid. Data rules decide whether
# the latter is allowed for a specific supplier.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
reactivation_assessment = Transition(label="Reactivation_Assessment")
sanctions_reactivation = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")

new_quality_review = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
validate_prior_quality = Transition(label="Validate_Prior_Quality")
reactivation_quality_choice = xor(
    new_quality_review,
    validate_prior_quality,
)

reactivation_checks = parallel(
    sanctions_reactivation,
    reactivation_quality_choice,
)

manual_approval_reactivation = Transition(
    label="Manual_Procurement_Approval"
)
activate_reactivation = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")

reactivation_case = sequence(
    reactivation_assessment,
    reactivation_checks,
    manual_approval_reactivation,
    activate_reactivation,
)

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# All routes converge on notification and closure.
# route_id and policy_rule_version_id in the event data distinguish routes
# that share human-readable labels such as Activate_Supplier.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------
route_choice = xor(
    standard_case,
    low_risk_auto_case,
    emergency_case,
    lead_prescreen_rejection,
    withdrawal_case,
    reactivation_case,
)

notify_requester = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close_case = Transition(label="Close_Case")

M1 = sequence(
    route_choice,
    notify_requester,
    close_case,
)
```

### POWL limitations in this proposal

The model should not be treated as self-sufficient:

- `low_risk_auto_case` and `emergency_case` are structurally possible in M1, but are invalid unless an active overlay authorizes them.
- POWL cannot enforce “low risk,” “DE,” approved-group status, a EUR 20,000 cumulative cap, business-day deadlines, or an expiry date.
- POWL cannot determine whether two aliases belong to the same person.
- The loop enforces a structural `Request_More_Info` then `Evidence_Received` pattern, but unresolved-request counting requires a stateful data check.
- `Daily_Transaction_Monitoring` is represented abstractly. A data rule must verify one monitoring record per required day.
- Shared labels across routes require a `route_id` and `policy_rule_version_id`; labels alone are insufficient for interpretation.

### Normalized policy-overlay proposal

Use normalized relations rather than putting policy meaning in event labels:

- `policy_rule_version(rule_version_id, policy_id, version, status, effective_from, effective_to, owner)`
- `policy_route(rule_version_id, route_code, case_type)`
- `policy_predicate(predicate_id, rule_version_id, attribute_name, operator, value_or_reference)`
- `policy_obligation(obligation_id, rule_version_id, obligation_code, deadline_basis, evidence_type)`
- `policy_authority(authority_id, rule_version_id, authority_type, signer, delegation_reference, approved_at)`
- `case_policy_application(case_id, rule_version_id, authority_id, route_code, eligibility_snapshot_id, applied_at)`
- `eligibility_snapshot(snapshot_id, case_id, snapshot_hash, captured_at, risk, country, group_status, validation_result)`

A review-oriented view of the core policy versions would be:

| Rule/version | Status and effective period | Permitted route | Predicate / preconditions | Required safeguards | Authority treatment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CS9-2025.1 | Active from 2025-01-01 | `standard_manual` | Full accepted onboarding; sanctions and quality before activation; manual approval before activation | Different requester and approver humans | Controlled CS-9 approval |
| PD3-2026.1 | Expired: 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31 | `low_risk_auto` | Low risk, DE, approved corporate group, sanctions pass, automated master-data validation pass | Quality within two business days after provisional activation | Steering record must be linked; waiver delegation status should be explicitly recorded, not assumed |
| FD7-2026.1 | Expired: 2026-07-10 through 2026-07-12 | `emergency_provisional` | Critical flood-relief supplier; sanctions pass before activation | EUR 20,000 cumulative pre-approval cap, daily monitoring, quality and retrospective approval within two business days | FD-7 authority reference required |
| M0-NOTIFY.1 | Active procedural rule | All closure routes | A case has a valid notification obligation | Stable CRM notification evidence before close | Process-owner authority |
| LRA-PERM.0 | Not approved | None | No active permanent low-risk auto-approval rule | N/A | Explicitly unavailable until formally approved |
| LEAD-SCOPE.1 | Proposed; not retroactive until approved | `lead_prescreen_rejection` | Lead has not been accepted into onboarding or supplier-master creation | Sanctions pre-screen, reason, notification, closure | Requires formal scope approval |
| REACTIVATION.1 | Proposed; not retroactive until approved | `reactivation_case` | Prior certification validity and material-change assessment | Fresh sanctions, quality validity evidence, manual approval | Requires formal lifecycle-policy approval |

### Event and data-collection proposal

Every event should carry at least:

| Field group | Required fields |
|---|---|
| Event identity | `event_id`, immutable `source_system`, `source_transaction_id`, `case_id`, object/supplier ID, activity code, display label |
| Time | immutable `occurred_at` or effective timestamp, `recorded_at`, time zone, correction-event reference rather than overwriting history |
| Route/policy | `case_type`, `route_id`, `policy_rule_version_id`, `authority_id`, `exception_reason_code` |
| Eligibility | `eligibility_snapshot_id`, snapshot hash, risk rating, country, corporate-group reference/version, automated validation result and timestamp |
| People | raw actor alias, canonical person ID, alias-resolution version, role, delegation evidence ID, requester ID, approver ID |
| Conflict controls | conflict disclosure ID, relationship assessment ID, recusal/independent-review result where required |
| Emergency controls | emergency directive ID, criticality evidence, cumulative pre-approval spend, monitoring dates, retrospective approval deadline/result |
| Notifications | immutable `notification_id`, CRM message/delivery ID, recipient, send/delivery timestamp, CRM status, correlation confidence |
| Semantics | stable `activity_code`, label-map version, procedure/checklist version, evidence-document IDs |

For notifications, the CRM should generate the stable `notification_id` before send. The onboarding log should store that immutable ID, not a mutable free-text case link. Reconciliation should compare CRM sends/deliveries to onboarding cases daily and create explicit exception records for unmatched items. Historical mutable links should remain preserved as weak evidence, not silently rewritten as certain evidence.

This architecture preserves CS-9 as the baseline, permits narrowly governed deviations where authorized, and prevents the structural model from becoming an accidental substitute for authority, eligibility, evidence, or human-accountability controls.
