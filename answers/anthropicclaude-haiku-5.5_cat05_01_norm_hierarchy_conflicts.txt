## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts (taken from the materials as stated):**
- GPIP-4 is a board-approved, globally effective policy (from 2026-01-01). Its A-clauses are controls, and A7 reserves formal exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer (CRO).
- SOP DE-AAC-PROC-17 Rev 6 is a controlled local procedure (from 2025-03-01). Its B-clauses cover maintenance and production-support purchases at Aachen.
- ECD-2 is jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander (IC). It is effective 2026-07-14 09:00 to 2026-07-18 18:00 CEST, and its stated scope is "critical sterilization-line lots required to contain the current contamination event."
- The CFO email (2026-07-15 07:20) is an uncontrolled communication. It is not in the policy repository.
- The CRO was unavailable and did not ratify ECD-2. No A7 written exception is in the file.
- The ERP outage ran 2026-07-14 08:30–13:00. Recovery-entered events may carry timestamps up to 12 minutes later than the real action. A separate ERP clock skew of about nine minutes is recorded for 2026-07-18.

**Assumptions (my working premises, labelled as such):**
- A1. ECD-2 is an incident directive, not a GPIP-4 exception. The A7 text requires a CRO-issued written exception, and ECD-2 lacks one. The COO's authority to issue operational directives is real, but it does not reach GPIP-4 reserved exceptions.
- A2. Hierarchy for the question at hand: GPIP-4 (board) > ECD-2 and SOP-B for the matters ECD-2 expressly addresses, within scope and window > SOP-B for ordinary workflow > CFO email as a Finance-interpretation instrument only. The CFO email is weighed as an interpretation, not as a waiver.
- A3. I read "ordinary local workflow steps" (SOP B7) narrowly. It reaches sequencing and routing steps (PO timing, approval routing), not GPIP-4 controls and not the approved-supplier register as a governance control. ECD-2 C2 expressly overrides the register for the directive's scope, so I treat that override as valid within scope.
- A4. ECD-2 scope ("critical... required to contain") is read as: the component is needed for containment of the affected sterilization line within 24 hours. The Internal Audit reading is adopted because (a) the ordinary meaning of "contain" is immediacy, (b) an exception to purchasing timing should be read narrowly, and (c) the operations reading ("backup-line components are critical") would expand scope on the basis of a label. This is a judgment and is flagged in Section 5.
- A5. Time of each act is determined by the instrument in force at that time. The CFO email applies only from 2026-07-15 07:20. ECD-2 applies only inside its window. Commitments after 2026-07-18 18:00 are outside ECD-2 unless a valid written, jointly signed extension exists.
- A6. Where the case file lists no event for a required control, I treat the event as not evidenced for this assessment. That is a documentary-evidence standard, not a finding that the event never happened. Each such finding is flagged with reversal conditions.
- A7. "Independent budget owner" (GPIP-4 A2) is a distinct approval from the Finance Controller approval. An IC authorization satisfies it only if the IC members are shown to hold budget authority over the cost centre. The IC charter does not say that, so I do not assume it.

**Decision method:**
1. Establish for each act: amount, supplier status, purpose, time, and the instruments in force at that time.
2. Apply the hierarchy by issue (Section 2), not by a single global ordering.
3. Test whether each required pre-commitment control was evidenced before commitment. Post-commitment approvals are recorded as late, not as cures (GPIP-4 A6).
4. Resolve timestamp conflicts by weight of evidence. External, contemporaneous records (supplier acknowledgments, call recordings, signed PDFs with queue logs) outrank a single system timestamp, particularly where clock skew is documented.
5. Classify. A deviation is NONCOMPLIANT only where a required control is contradicted or unevidenced and no valid override reaches it. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION requires a valid override that covers the deviated control. INDETERMINATE is used where the classification would flip on an unresolved fact.

**Burden:** The entity bears the burden of producing contemporaneous evidence for each pre-commitment control. The assessor does not presume compliance from the existence of an emergency. Emergency necessity mitigates severity and can justify specific sequencing deviations, but it does not supply authority.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

**P-501  COMPLIANT (or JUSTIFIED).** *Premise to change:* "absent" under SOP-B4 means not on the floor and unable to act, not "reachable by phone." *Credibility:* moderate. The SOP does not define absence. A Shift Lead acting on an urgent four-hour stoppage, with the Plant Manager's approval obtained at 15:10, is a plausible reading of the procedure's purpose. *Why it is weaker:* the substitution clause requires both absence and formal delegation, and the file says the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. A reachable-but-unengaged manager is not "absent." *Reversal evidence:* a call log showing the PM was unreachable at 08:38–08:50, or an oral delegation recorded before commitment.

**P-502  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION.** *Premise to change:* ECD-2 C4 ("takes precedence over ... supplier-onboarding requirements") extends to new-supplier Compliance clearance, which is the onboarding step for a new supplier. *Credibility:* weak-to-moderate. It draws support from the directive's broad wording and from the operational need. *Why it is weaker:* GPIP-4 A3 is a board-level control, A4 expressly preserves it under emergency, C5 expressly omits Compliance, and the directive was not a CRO exception. Compliance vetting is a risk function, not a supplier-register step. *Reversal evidence:* a CRO written exception, or a board-level delegation that makes COO/IC directives able to set A3 timing.

**P-503  NONCOMPLIANT is not robust under the CFO email (partial).** *Premise to change:* the CFO's D1 ("IC authorization sufficient for all operational approvals up to EUR 150,000") covers the Finance and Plant steps, and the broad operations reading of "critical" covers backup-line pumps. *Credibility:* moderate for the Finance and Plant steps; weak for A1 and A3. *Why it is weaker for the classification:* even if D1 covered Finance and Plant, the Compliance requirement above EUR 100k remains (D3 bars waiver), and A1 (requester/committer as IC authorizer) is a segregation control not reached by D1 or C5. The classification would move from High to Medium only if the scope and Finance points were both resolved in the entity's favor. *Reversal evidence:* a documented CFO determination that approves the pump as a Finance-substituted approval, plus a CRO exception on A1 and A3. Neither exists in the file.

**P-505  COMPLIANT (timing) / JUSTIFIED (commit).** *Premise to change:* the commitment occurred inside the ECD-2 window and the Finance approval at 19:00 was a routing step that CFO D1 made unnecessary. *Credibility:* the timing premise is strong. The supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is external and contemporaneous and corroborates the call at 17:57 and the ERP 18:06 entry corrected for documented skew. The Finance premise is weaker: D1 does not address the budget-owner limb of A2, and the Finance approval followed commitment. *Why it is weaker overall:* even if the timing is accepted in full, A2's budget-owner limb and B2's Plant approval are unevidenced. The classification would move to INDETERMINATE (not COMPLIANT) if the budget-owner and Plant approvals were found to exist pre-commitment. *Reversal evidence:* a pre-commitment budget-owner approval and a Plant approval record.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

*(Recommendation. Drafted as a replacement clause for insertion into GPIP-4 with a conforming SOP amendment.)*

**Clause X — Authority, Scope, and Exceptions During Incident Directives**

**X.1 Hierarchy.** Clauses A1, A2, A3 and A6 of GPIP-4 are not displaced by any incident directive, operational directive, local procedure, or individual instruction. The only route to a temporary exception is a written exception issued by the CRO under A7, which must state (a) the specific clause(s) waived, (b) the transactions or categories covered, (c) an expiry date and time not exceeding 72 hours, and (d) the compensating controls.

**X.2 Incident directives.** A jointly signed COO and Incident Commander directive may temporarily govern operational sequencing (PO timing), supplier-register admission for listed categories, and the routing of operational approvals, but only where the directive lists those matters expressly. A directive may not govern segregation of duties, pre-commitment Compliance clearance, pre-commitment Finance Controller approval, or budget-owner approval.

**X.3 Named approvals during activation.** For purchases EUR 50,000 during any activation, a standing on-call Finance Controller and a designated independent budget owner must each give electronic approval before commitment. Incident Command authorization is an additional requirement, not a substitute. Approvals may be given by telephone only if logged in a system with a server-side timestamp within 15 minutes.

**X.4 Thresholds.** One threshold table governs all purchases. Interpretive email or memo instruments from executives do not alter thresholds. Any change requires a controlled policy amendment.

**X.5 Segregation.** No person may both request or commit a purchase and serve as an authorizing member of the Incident Command for that purchase.

**X.6 New suppliers.** Any supplier not on the approved register requires Compliance_Clearance before commitment. Activation may set an expedited vetting target of two hours, but not a waiver. If Compliance cannot clear within the target, the purchase may proceed only under a written CRO exception.

**X.7 Retrospective records.** A record created after the event must carry its true creation time and must state the event time it describes. Late records are evidence of the event time only where contemporaneous external evidence (call logs, supplier acknowledgments, signed documents with queue metadata) corroborates it. A late approval never satisfies a pre-commitment requirement (GPIP-4 A6).

**X.8 Expiry.** An activation expires at the stated time automatically. Any extension must be a written instrument, jointly signed by the authorizing officers, recorded before the expiry time, and ratified by the CRO within 24 hours. A verbal extension has no effect. Purchases committed after expiry follow ordinary rules, except that a purchase whose PO and supplier acknowledgment are both dated before expiry may complete its PO within 24 hours.

**X.9 Scope definition.** A component is within an activation's scope only if it is needed to restore or contain the affected line within 24 hours and is recorded as such, with a stated need-by time, at the time of authorization. A "critical" label, by itself, is not sufficient.

**X.10 Emergency normalization safeguards.**
- (a) An emergency designation is valid only for the harm or stoppage criteria in SOP-B4 or the activation scope in X.9. Facilities, catering, and non-sterile-process items are not eligible unless a documented safety harm is shown.
- (b) Each emergency-coded purchase is reported to the CRO within one business day, with a volume cap per activation set by the CRO.
- (c) Every activation is followed by a post-event review within five business days. The review must identify all purchases committed under the activation, their approval evidence, and any deviation.
- (d) Repeated emergency designations by the same requester or approver within a rolling 30 days trigger an internal audit.
- (e) The activation's existence does not, by itself, change any approval requirement.

**X.11 Precedence statement.** Where an incident directive, a local procedure, and this policy conflict, this policy controls except where a valid A7 exception applies. Where this policy is silent, the incident directive controls for the matters it expressly addresses, within its stated scope and window.
