## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Stipulated facts (established by the materials as stated):**

- **CS-9** is effective from 2025-01-01, controlled, and formally approved. It requires Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before activation, and Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier. It requires requester and manual approver to be different humans. It does not represent this resource rule in M0.
- **PD-3** is a steering-committee decision recorded in minutes and a follow-up email. It is not incorporated into CS-9 or M0. Its window is 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31. It permits Auto_Approve for LOW-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group, requires Sanctions_Check and automated master-data validation before activation, and allows Quality_Review up to two business days after provisional activation.
- **FD-7** is effective 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59. It covers critical relief suppliers. It permits provisional activation before Quality_Review and manual approval, requires Sanctions_Check before activation, caps spend at EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, and requires daily monitoring and Quality_Review within two business days. It expired automatically.
- **Notification** moved to a CRM service on 2026-07-01. About 70% of notifications are mirrored into the log. Case links in older records are mutable text fields.
- **Aggregates:** 312 Auto_Approve cases in June–July, of which 289 appear PD-3-eligible, 14 are clearly ineligible, and 9 lack eligibility attributes. 47 cases contain Request_More_Info, and none activated before the requested evidence arrived. Notification missingness rose from 2% to 29%. Six post-pilot cases used Auto_Approve in the first week of August.
- **Calendar facts (computed by me, not stipulated):** 2026-06-15 is a Monday; 2026-06-20 is a Saturday; 2026-07-10 is a Friday; 2026-07-11 is a Saturday; 2026-07-13 is a Monday; 2026-07-14 is a Tuesday; 2026-08-02 is a Sunday. No holiday calendar was supplied, so "two business days" is computed on weekdays only. This matters for SUP-704 and SUP-705.

**Disputed propositions:**

- **D1.** PD-3, a steering-committee decision in minutes and email, is sufficient authority to create a monitored exception to CS-9. *Disputed.* The Compliance observer's "may proceed as a monitored exception" was later disowned as not approving any weakening of CS-9.
- **D2.** FD-7 covers activations after 2026-07-12 23:59 because the emergency "factually" continued. *Unproved and contrary to the text.* Expiry was automatic and no extension exists.
- **D3.** M0 applies to rejected leads. *Disputed.* CS-9 permits an abbreviated pre-screen but does not say whether those leads are M0 cases.
- **D4.** Request_More_Info is a deviation to be corrected. *Disputed.* CS-9 neither requires nor prohibits it. Compliance regards it as desirable, but desirability is not a rule.
- **D5.** Requester and approver in SUP-712 are the same human. *Supported by the case record but dependent on the alias-resolution method, which is not documented.*
- **D6.** SUP-706 was not notified. *Disputed.* A CRM message exists at +3 hours. Its link is mutable and was entered two days later.
- **D7.** QA_Clearance is equivalent to Quality_Review (SUP-713). *Supported on substance by audit documents, but the effective date of the renaming is unclear.*
- **D8.** SUP-702 satisfied the PD-3 pre-activation master-data validation. *Unproved.* No such event appears in the trace.
- **D9.** The high M0 deviation rate is mainly due to obsolescence and incomplete scope. *Unproved.* The 15 cases are a purposive sample, not a random one. The aggregate figures do not compute a deviation rate for M0.
- **D10.** Quality_Review and Sanctions_Check are intended to be concurrent. *Not stated in CS-9.* CS-9 requires both before activation, not any particular order between them. The concurrency is M0's choice.
- **D11.** SUP-715's spousal relationship is governed by the conflict-of-interest policy, and disclosure is absent. *Partly unproved.* A disclosure may exist outside the onboarding log.
- **D12.** Timestamps are accurate and in a consistent time zone. *Unproved.* This matters for SUP-705 (activation near midnight on 12 July) and SUP-708 (40-minute ordering).

**Burden-of-proof approach.**

*Declaring noncompliance* requires three elements to be shown for each deviation:

1. A rule was in force at the event time, with the correct authority level and effective window.
2. The case falls within the rule's scope, using attributes as they stood at the decision time.
3. The event evidence (sequence, timestamps, actor identity) is reliable enough to support the finding.

If element 3 fails, the correct finding is DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE, not NONCOMPLIANT. Findings rated critical or high should be corroborated by at least two independent sources, such as the event log and CRM, or the event log and system configuration.

*Accepting an exception* requires written authority at the appropriate level, a time window covering the activation timestamp, a scope predicate satisfied by an eligibility snapshot taken at decision time (not reconstructed afterwards), evidence of the required compensating controls, and no retrospective widening of scope. Observer remarks, operational narratives, and the desirability of an outcome do not satisfy the burden. The party relying on an exception carries that burden. The party alleging a violation carries the burden of showing the evidence.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

**Formal authority.** CS-9 is the only controlled and formally approved normative source in the materials. M0 is its control-flow projection, and it is the baseline against which conformance is measured. PD-3 and FD-7 are overlays of narrower legal force. PD-3 is a steering-committee decision recorded informally. Its only trace of Compliance involvement is a remark that was later disowned. Even if the steering committee had authority to pilot, its own conditions (LOW risk, DE, approved group, master-data validation before activation, complete eligibility fields) are binding on each case. An operational pilot does not transfer CS-9's approval status to a pilot route.

**Expiry and the post-expiry pattern.**

- **SUP-705** is activated on Monday 2026-07-13, after FD-7 expired at 23:59 on 12 July. Operations concedes that no written extension exists. "The emergency factually existed" is an argument about policy, not an authority. If a factual emergency could extend an expired directive, the directive's expiry clause would be meaningless. The activation also preceded Quality_Review and Manual_Procurement_Approval, which CS-9 requires before activation.
- **SUP-714** is activated on Sunday 2026-08-02, two days after PD-3 expired. The operator believed permanent approval was "imminent." Belief about a pending amendment is not an amendment. Six further post-pilot cases in the first week of August show that the flow has normalized beyond its authority. This is drift, not an isolated exception.

**Hidden deviations.** M0 is a normative model. Any trace that differs from it is a deviation by definition, and the deviations are not confined to "M0 obsolescence." Auto_Approve is absent from M0 and from CS-9's manual-approval rule. It is therefore a deviation from CS-9 unless an overlay applies. The 312 Auto_Approve cases in June–July are 312 CS-9 deviations that rely on overlay authority. Of those, 289 "appear" eligible. Appearance is not evidence that eligibility was established at decision time, and the 9 cases without eligibility attributes cannot be shown to be eligible at all. Even within the pilot, 23 of 312 cases (about 7%) fail or cannot demonstrate eligibility. That is a failure of the control that is supposed to guard the pilot.

**Normalization of exceptions.** The pilot flow was adopted in operations as a default, not as a bounded experiment. Evidence:

- SUP-703: the operator selected "pilot" for an ineligible HIGH-risk foreign supplier with a EUR 180,000 first purchase. The system did not prevent selection of an ineligible route.
- SUP-714: the flow continued after expiry.
- SUP-704 and SUP-705: the FD-7 pattern (activation before approval) was reused after the directive expired.

Normalization means exceptions are no longer exceptional. That undermines the premise that a monitored exception is controlled.

**High-risk cases.**

- **SUP-703** is the most serious. A HIGH-risk foreign supplier was onboarded through an ineligible route, with no manual approval, and received EUR 180,000 in first spend. Internal Audit rated it potentially severe.
- **SUP-708** is a HIGH-risk supplier activated 40 minutes before the sanctions check. CS-9 requires sanctions clearance before activation. Sanctions risk is the category of control that cannot be cured after the fact. The defense that no transactions occurred is a matter of luck of timing, not of control design. The control existed and was bypassed.
- **SUP-712** is a complete self-approval. Requester and approver are the same human, which CS-9 expressly forbids. M0 does not encode the rule, but CS-9 does, so the case is a violation regardless of trace fit.

**Why desirable outcomes do not amend policy.** Compliance agrees that requesting more information is desirable (SUP-707, 47 cases). Operations wants a permanent low-risk auto-approval branch. Both are reasonable proposals. Neither is policy. A desirable outcome does not establish a rule, and the existence of 289 apparently successful pilot cases does not show that the pilot was authorized to vary CS-9. The correct path is formal amendment of CS-9, with approval, versioning, and an effective date. That path exists and has not been used. Circumventing it because the results are good weakens the standard for the next case where the results are not good.

**Concessions.** The prosecution acknowledges the following genuine defects, which do not establish misconduct:

- M0 has no loop and cannot represent SUP-707. This is a model defect.
- M0 has no reactivation route (SUP-710). CS-9 also says nothing about reactivation, which is a scope defect in the standard.
- SUP-709 cannot be scoped without an explicit rule about rejected leads.
- SUP-711 is a migration duplicate with identical source IDs. Deduplication is appropriate.
- SUP-713 is a label change with documented equivalent content.
- The CRM migration has degraded notification evidence. SUP-706 may be a logging failure rather than a missed notification.

These concessions narrow the prosecution. They do not affect the cases in which control-level requirements were breached.

## 3. Defense Brief

**M0 is obsolete as a description of onboarding practice.** M0 was built before the operational realities now in the record. Specifically:

- **Concurrency.** M0 places Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review in a partial order with no ordering between them. That is probably right, but CS-9 does not establish the concurrency. M0 treats it as normative. The concurrency is a modeling choice, and it has never been separately approved.
- **Missing loops.** Request_More_Info is a repeated, productive activity. 47 cases used it, and none activated before the requested evidence arrived. Compliance itself calls it desirable. A model that cannot express the most common pre-decision behavior is incomplete, not wrong about the decision.
- **Rejected leads.** M0 places the rejection decision after both checks. SUP-709 is therefore M0-nonconformant in its own terms. Yet CS-9 explicitly allows an abbreviated pre-screen for rejected leads. The M0 model is incorrectly scoped: it claims authority over a population that CS-9 has partly exempted.
- **Reactivation.** SUP-710 satisfies CS-9's explicit activation rules: sanctions and manual approval occurred, and the prior quality certification remained valid. CS-9 has no reactivation procedure. M0 does not apply to reactivations, and the trace is not an onboarding case.
- **Label equivalence.** SUP-713 uses QA_Clearance, with equivalent content documented in audit records. M0 is a model of activities, not of label strings. Treating a label mismatch as a control deviation confuses vocabulary with substance.
- **CRM migration.** The notification step, which M0 requires before closure, is now collected by a service that mirrors only about 70% of notifications. Missingness rose from 2% to 29%. The Notify_Requester activity is therefore unobservable in roughly a third of cases. M0's conformance verdicts for every case after 1 July rest on degraded evidence.

**Pilot and emergency overlays are legitimate governance responses.** The steering committee and the emergency directive reflected deliberate choices. Within their windows and scopes:

- **SUP-704** matches FD-7 point by point. Sanctions preceded activation, spend was EUR 18,000 against a EUR 20,000 cap, monitoring existed, and quality review and approval occurred within the two-business-day window (activation on Saturday 11 July; approval and quality on Monday 13 July). Under FD-7, the activation is a permitted deviation from M0, not a violation.
- **SUP-702** matches PD-3's eligibility criteria and timing. Quality review occurred next business day, notification was logged at +2 hours with a stable ID, and eligibility fields are complete. It is the best-documented pilot case, and the only real gap is the absence of a logged master-data validation event, which may be an automated step not written to the log.

**Trace deviation is not business misconduct.** Several deviations are benign or positive:

- **SUP-707** adds requests that improved the evidence base before approval. This is a deviation from M0 with no compliance cost.
- **SUP-711** is a duplicate row caused by migration. There is no second registration.
- **SUP-706** may have executed notification correctly; the missing link is a data-quality problem.
- **SUP-708** involved no transactions before sanctions clearance, so the exposure is limited to a 40-minute ordering.

Calling every trace deviation misconduct would discourage the reporting of genuine process improvements and make the compliance function less credible.

**Acknowledged limits of this defense.** The defense does not claim the following:

- That SUP-705 and SUP-714 were authorized. Both were outside their overlay windows, and no extension existed. Operations' belief about an imminent amendment is not authority.
- That SUP-703 was acceptable. It was an ineligible route selected by the operator, and the system permitted it.
- That SUP-712 is harmless. Self-approval is a serious breach regardless of the model's blindness to it.
- That SUP-708 is trivial. Sanctions-after-activation is a control failure with potentially severe consequences if the counterparty had been listed.
- That the informal pilot practice was adequately governed. The fact that eligibility was missing from 9 cases and contradicted in 14 shows that it was not.

### System-level judgment

**Operational compliance: not established, and materially deficient.** The operation is noncompliant in at least five cases, including one critical (SUP-703) and several serious (SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714). The noncompliance is not confined to M0 gaps. It includes breaches of explicit CS-9 requirements (sanctions sequencing, self-approval) and the use of overlays beyond their windows and scopes (SUP-705, SUP-714), as well as a system that let an ineligible route be selected (SUP-703). Operations' claim that the deviation rate mostly reflects obsolescence is contradicted by the record: M0 correctly flags the five NONCOMPLIANT cases, and obsolescence explains only the MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT and some of the DATA_QUALITY cases.

**Model validity: M0 is valid for the standard route's core and invalid as a complete normative model.** The standard route (SUP-701, SUP-711 after deduplication, SUP-712's control-flow fit) is correctly represented. M0 is not valid as a description of onboarding because it omits Request_More_Info, rejected-lead scope, reactivation, the time-boxed overlays, and the resource rule. It is also dependent on a notification source whose completeness fell from 98% to 71% after July 2026. M0 should remain the baseline for standard-route conformance, but it should not be used to adjudicate overlay cases, reactivations, or rejected leads without the scope rules in Section 6.

**Aggregate pilot finding.** Of 312 Auto_Approve cases, 289 appear eligible. That is not sufficient to find the pilot compliant. Eligibility must be shown at decision time, and 23 cases cannot be shown to be eligible. The pilot is therefore best described as partially governed.

### Where I reject each side's argument

**Rejected prosecution arguments:**

- *"A factual emergency extends FD-7."* Rejected. Directive expiry is textual and explicit. Factual conditions justify a new directive, not an unwritten extension.
- *"PD-3 is no authority at all."* Partly rejected. A steering committee can plausibly authorize a monitored pilot. What it cannot do is override CS-9 without amendment, and the pilot's own conditions still bind each case. SUP-702 is a conditionally acceptable exception, not a violation.
- *"Every deviation from M0 is a compliance failure."* Rejected. M0 is a model of a policy, and SUP-707 deviates from M0 without deviating from CS-9.
- *"SUP-706 is noncompliant."* Not established. The absence of a log event does not prove absence of the activity. The correct classification is DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE.

**Rejected defense arguments:**

- *"M0 is obsolete, so the deviations are not violations."* Rejected for the NONCOMPLIANT cases. CS-9's sequencing and resource rules are in force regardless of M0's shape. SUP-708 and SUP-712 breach CS-9 directly.
- *"The emergency existed, so SUP-705 is justified."* Rejected. Factual circumstances do not extend expired authority.
- *"Normalization was benign because outcomes were good."* Rejected. Outcomes do not amend policy. The 14 ineligible cases and the August post-pilot cases show the control is failing even where outcomes are acceptable.
- *"SUP-708 is trivial because no transactions occurred."* Rejected. Sanctions clearance is a precondition control. Its absence is a finding whatever the exposure turned out to be.
- *"SUP-710 is compliant because the activation rules were met."* Not established. The rules CS-9 states were met, but the scope of onboarding rules for reactivation is unsettled, so the case is a scope defect rather than a finding of compliance.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

The architecture separates what must be stated as normative (CS-9 and approved amendments), what represents control-flow (POWL), what represents temporal and sequential invariants (Declare or log-skeleton constraints), what requires data evaluation (pandas or database checks), and what depends on organizational controls (identity, delegation, COI disclosure, authority registers).

**Layer 0: Normative core (CS-9, amended).** The approved document should contain, at minimum:

- Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review before Activate_Supplier, for every activation route.
- Manual_Procurement_Approval before Activate_Supplier, except for routes that are explicitly enumerated and approved.
- Requester  approver, as a resource rule.
- Explicit definitions of onboarding, reactivation, rejection, pre-screen, and withdrawal.
- A policy-overlay mechanism: overlays have IDs, authority, effective windows, scope predicates, required controls, and no automatic extension.

**Layer 1: Control-flow (POWL).** The model in Section 7 represents routes and iteration. It cannot represent amounts, dates, identities, or authority. It is the place for ordering and branching, not for conditions.

**Layer 2: Sequencing invariants (Declare or log-skeleton constraints).** Some constraints are cleaner as declarative rules over the log, checked independently of POWL's route structure:

- `precedence(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` (for all activation variants, including Activate_Provisional).
- `precedence(Manual_Procurement_Approval, Activate_Supplier)` for the standard route.
- `response(Activate_Supplier, Notify_Requester)`, with a time bound handled by data.
- `not-co-occurrence` between Reject_Supplier and Activate_Supplier in the same case.
- `at-most-one(Register_Supplier)` per source transaction ID, after deduplication.

These constraints are useful because they are robust to route-level changes in the POWL and can be checked on historical logs.

**Layer 3: Data-aware eligibility, amount, and time rules (pandas or database).** These are not expressible in POWL:

- PD-3 eligibility: risk_level = LOW, country = DE, group_approved at decision time, MDV before activation, quality within two business days after activation, activation within 2026-06-01 to 2026-07-31.
- FD-7 eligibility: directive window, critical-supplier determination on file, spend  EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval, quality and manual approval within two business days, daily monitoring present.
- Business-day calendar as a versioned reference table.
- Notification completeness: a notification event with a stable notification_id, recorded before closure, with a verified CRM link.
- Time-zone normalization: all timestamps stored in UTC with the source time zone recorded.

**Layer 4: Resource and conflict-of-interest rules (organizational controls plus data).**

- Requester  manual approver, resolved through a time-bounded identity registry.
- Delegation evidence: any approval by a delegate must cite a delegation record with scope, validity, and signatory.
- Reviewer–approver independence: a COI disclosure is required when a relationship (including spousal or reporting-line relationships) exists. The policy must define which relationships are disqualifying and which require disclosure only. The check is organizational plus a data join to the disclosure registry.

**Layer 5: Policy-overlay governance.** Overlays are not hard-coded into the model. Each overlay is a versioned record:

- Overlay ID, type (pilot, emergency, permanent amendment, scope rule), authority reference, signatory level, effective_from and effective_to in UTC.
- Scope predicate version and content hash.
- Required compensating controls.
- Expiry behavior: expired overlays do not apply to events after effective_to. Extension requires a new versioned record with its own authority, effective before the first covered event.
- Post-hoc obligations (retrospective approval, quality review deadlines), with their own deadlines.
- No overlay may be selected by an operator without a system-enforced eligibility check.

**Layer 6: Explicit scope rules.**

- **Rejected leads.** Two scope options. Option A: rejected leads that fail sanctions pre-screen belong to an intake model R1 (Register  Sanctions_Check  Reject_Supplier  Notify_Requester  Close_Case) under CS-9's pre-screen allowance. Option B: they are onboarding cases and M0 applies in full. Option A is recommended because CS-9 already contemplates pre-screen. The decision belongs to the authority that owns CS-9.
- **Reactivations.** A separate model R2 (Sanctions_Check, Manual_Procurement_Approval, Activate_Supplier, Notify_Requester, Close_Case), with a precondition: a valid quality certification at activation time. CS-9 must state this explicitly.
- **Withdrawals.** Withdrawal before activation terminates the case with Notify_Requester and Close_Case. Withdrawal after activation is a deactivation, not a withdrawal, and must be a separate process.
- **Label aliases.** A time-bounded alias table mapping historical labels (QA_Clearance  Quality_Review) with effective dates. Log normalization applies the alias valid at the event's occurrence time.
- **Duplicate registrations.** Deduplicate by source transaction ID during migration; flag distinct IDs for duplicate-supplier review.

### Summary of allocation

| Requirement | Encoding |
|---|---|
| Control-flow routes, iteration, concurrency | POWL |
| Sanctions/approval precedence across routes | Declare or log-skeleton constraints |
| Eligibility, amount caps, time windows, business days | pandas/database checks |
| Notification completeness and linkage | Data checks plus event schema |
| Requester  approver | Identity registry plus data check |
| Delegation and COI disclosure | Organizational controls plus registry join |
| Overlay validity and expiry | Versioned authority registry |
| Rejected leads, reactivations, withdrawals | Separate models plus CS-9 scope clauses |

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### Executable model and overlay check

The code below constructs the revised onboarding model M1, a reactivation model R2, and an overlay-validation function. It reads no external files. The demonstration data are in-memory.

Design notes that the code embeds:

- **Concurrency.** The standard route keeps Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review concurrent, as in M0. Concurrency is a retained modeling choice, not a CS-9 requirement, and the notes flag this.
- **Request_More_Info.** Encoded as a loop with zero or more repetitions via `OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP)` wrapped in an optional XOR. The loop is placed in the standard route only. Its approximation: it is not ordered relative to Quality_Review and is not bounded by evidence.
- **Rejected leads.** Encoded as a pre-screen route, which is valid only if Scope Option A is adopted.
- **Duplicate Sanctions_Check nodes.** Each route has its own sanctions node. The same label appears in several routes. This is intentional: each route must be self-contained for sanctions-before-activation. The overlay check, not the POWL, enforces that the route is authorized.
- **Quality after activation.** Only the pilot route places Quality_Review after activation. POWL cannot bound this to two business days, so that bound is enforced in data.
- **Emergency route.** Activation is labeled Activate_Provisional to distinguish it from standard activation in the log. Timing and spend caps are data checks.
- **Immutable timestamps and actor aliases.** Not representable in POWL. They are event-schema requirements (below).

```python
"""
Revised onboarding model M1 (POWL), reactivation model R2 (POWL),
and an in-memory overlay/data check. No external files are read.

Limitations (stated, not hidden):
- POWL cannot bound times, amounts, risk classes, authorities, or identities.
  These are enforced by check_overlays() on the event/data table.
- Quality_Review after activation is encoded only for the PILOT route.
  The two-business-day bound is a data check.
- Request_More_Info is an approximation: a zero-or-more loop placed before
  manual approval, not ordered relative to Quality_Review, not evidence-bounded.
- The REJECT route assumes Scope Option A (pre-screen for rejected leads).
- Sanctions_Check appears in several routes as distinct nodes so each route is
  self-contained for the sanctions-before-activation rule.
"""
import pandas as pd
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL,
                                    Transition, SilentTransition)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def act(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


def tau():
    return SilentTransition()


def build_onboarding_model():
    """M1: onboarding with REJECT (pre-screen), STD, PILOT, EMERG routes."""
    register = act("Register_Supplier")
    notify = act("Notify_Requester")
    close = act("Close_Case")

    # --- REJECT route (scope option A: pre-screen only, no Quality_Review) ---
    s_r = act("Sanctions_Check")
    reject = act("Reject_Supplier")
    reject_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_r, reject])
    reject_route.order.add_edge(s_r, reject)

    # --- STD route (CS-9). Sanctions and Quality concurrent, as in M0.
    #     Request_More_Info: zero or more repetitions, before manual approval.
    s_s = act("Sanctions_Check")
    q_s = act("Quality_Review")
    man_s = act("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    act_s = act("Activate_Supplier")
    rmi = act("Request_More_Info")
    rmi_loop = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.LOOP, children=[rmi, tau()])  # rmi (tau rmi)*
    rmi_opt = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR, children=[tau(), rmi_loop])  # zero or more
    std_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_s, q_s, rmi_opt, man_s, act_s])
    std_route.order.add_edge(s_s, act_s)
    std_route.order.add_edge(q_s, act_s)
    std_route.order.add_edge(rmi_opt, man_s)
    std_route.order.add_edge(man_s, act_s)

    # --- PILOT route (PD-3 overlay). Auto_Approve replaces manual approval.
    #     Master-data validation and sanctions precede activation;
    #     Quality_Review follows activation (bounded in data).
    s_p = act("Sanctions_Check")
    mdv_p = act("Master_Data_Validation")
    auto_p = act("Auto_Approve")
    act_p = act("Activate_Supplier")
    q_p = act("Quality_Review")
    pilot_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_p, mdv_p, auto_p, act_p, q_p])
    pilot_route.order.add_edge(s_p, act_p)
    pilot_route.order.add_edge(mdv_p, act_p)
    pilot_route.order.add_edge(auto_p, act_p)
    pilot_route.order.add_edge(act_p, q_p)

    # --- EMERG route (FD-7 overlay). Provisional activation precedes
    #     quality and retrospective manual approval (bounded in data).
    s_e = act("Sanctions_Check")
    act_e = act("Activate_Provisional")
    q_e = act("Quality_Review")
    man_e = act("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    emerg_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s_e, act_e, q_e, man_e])
    emerg_route.order.add_edge(s_e, act_e)
    emerg_route.order.add_edge(act_e, q_e)
    emerg_route.order.add_edge(act_e, man_e)

    screen = OperatorPOWL(operator=Operator.XOR,
                          children=[reject_route, std_route, pilot_route, emerg_route])

    m1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, screen, notify, close])
    m1.order.add_edge(register, screen)
    m1.order.add_edge(screen, notify)
    m1.order.add_edge(notify, close)
    return m1


def build_reactivation_model():
    """R2: reactivation (scope rule). Precondition, checked in data: valid
    quality certification at activation time. No Register or Quality here."""
    s = act("Sanctions_Check")
    man = act("Manual_Procurement_Approval")
    act_r = act("Activate_Supplier")
    notify = act("Notify_Requester")
    close = act("Close_Case")
    r2 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[s, man, act_r, notify, close])
    r2.order.add_edge(s, act_r)
    r2.order.add_edge(man, act_r)
    r2.order.add_edge(act_r, notify)
    r2.order.add_edge(notify, close)
    return r2


TS_COLS = ["overlay_from", "overlay_to", "activation_ts", "sanctions_ts", "mdv_ts",
           "manual_ts", "quality_ts", "quality_due_ts", "notification_ts", "record_ts"]


def check_overlays(df: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Data-aware checks for routes, overlays, SoD, COI, notification, and
    record timing. All timestamps are normalized to UTC. Returns PASS,
    EVIDENCE_GAP (missing evidence that may be a logging issue), or FAIL."""
    d = df.copy()
    for c in TS_COLS:
        d[c] = pd.to_datetime(d[c], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    d["group_approved"] = d["group_approved"].fillna(False).astype(bool)
    d["reviewer_related_to_approver"] = (
        d["reviewer_related_to_approver"].fillna(False).astype(bool))

    in_window = (d.activation_ts >= d.overlay_from) & (d.activation_ts <= d.overlay_to)
    pre_act_sanctions = d.sanctions_ts < d.activation_ts

    standard = ((d.route == "STD") & (d.manual_ts < d.activation_ts)
                & (d.quality_ts < d.activation_ts))
    pilot = ((d.route == "PILOT") & (d.overlay_id == "PD-3") & in_window
             & (d.risk_level == "LOW") & (d.country == "DE") & d.group_approved
             & (d.mdv_ts < d.activation_ts) & pre_act_sanctions
             & (d.quality_ts <= d.quality_due_ts))
    emergency = ((d.route == "EMERG") & (d.overlay_id == "FD-7") & in_window
                 & (d.spend_eur <= 20000) & pre_act_sanctions
                 & (d.quality_ts <= d.quality_due_ts)
                 & (d.manual_ts <= d.quality_due_ts))
    authority = standard | pilot | emergency

    rules = pd.DataFrame({
        "authority_ok": authority,
        "sanctions_before_activation": pre_act_sanctions,
        "sod_requester_ne_approver": d.requester_person_id.notna() & (
            d.approver_person_id.isna()
            | (d.requester_person_id != d.approver_person_id)),
        "coi_disclosed_if_related": (~d.reviewer_related_to_approver)
            | d.coi_disclosure_id.notna(),
        "notification_logged": d.notification_id.notna(),
        "no_backdated_record": d.record_ts >= d.activation_ts,
    }).fillna(False).astype(bool)

    d["failed_rules"] = rules.apply(
        lambda r: ";".join(c for c in rules.columns if not r[c]), axis=1)

    gap = (d.sanctions_ts.isna() | d.notification_id.isna()
           | ((d.route == "PILOT") & d.mdv_ts.isna())
           | (d.route.isin(["STD", "PILOT", "EMERG"]) & d.quality_ts.isna()))
    d["overlay_check"] = "FAIL"
    d.loc[d.failed_rules == "", "overlay_check"] = "PASS"
    d.loc[(d.failed_rules != "") & gap, "overlay_check"] = "EVIDENCE_GAP"
    return d
```

### Event and data-collection proposal

Each event in the onboarding log should carry the following fields. The schema is append-only: corrections create new events that reference the superseded event; they never overwrite it.

| Field | Purpose | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| event_id | Unique identifier | Immutable |
| case_id | Case identity | Immutable |
| source_transaction_id | Deduplication key | Unique per registration; duplicates rejected at ingestion |
| activity | Controlled vocabulary value (including Activate_Provisional, Request_More_Info, Auto_Approve, Master_Data_Validation) | Must resolve through the label alias registry |
| occurred_at_utc | Business occurrence time | UTC, with original time zone stored |
| recorded_at_utc | System record time | Immutable, append-only; must be  occurred_at |
| actor_alias | Raw actor identifier | As presented by source system |
| actor_person_id | Resolved human identity | Resolved through identity registry with validity interval and resolution method |
| delegation_id | Delegation evidence, if the actor acts for another | Must cite delegation record with scope and validity |
| rule_version_id | CS-9 or amendment version applied | Required on every decision event |
| overlay_id and authority_ref | Pilot or emergency authority | Required for any overlay route; must match an authority record valid at occurred_at |
| eligibility_snapshot_id | Immutable snapshot of risk_level, country, group status, spend, and eligibility at decision time | Content hash stored; snapshot cannot be re-derived after the decision |
| notification_id | Stable CRM identifier, issued at send time | Immutable; the case link must be an ID, not free text |
| notification_recorded_at | CRM send time | Used to test notification before closure |
| sanctions_result_id | Reference to the sanctions check outcome | Must precede activation |
| quality_review_id | Reference to quality review | Required before activation (CS-9) or within window (PD-3/FD-7) |
| manual_approval_id | Reference to manual approval | Includes approver_person_id and delegation evidence |
| spend_eur and spend_cap_ref | Amount and applicable cap | Required for FD-7 |
| business_calendar_version | Calendar used for business-day computation | Versioned; prevents retrospective recalculation |
| time_zone_source | Source system time zone | Ensures UTC normalization is auditable |

**Migration requirements.** Historical records should be backfilled as follows: deduplicate by source_transaction_id; map historical labels through a dated alias table; reconstruct eligibility snapshots only where the source data are demonstrably contemporaneous, and otherwise mark them as reconstructed; link CRM notifications by verified notification_id only, and flag mutable free-text links as unverified.

**Reporting consequences.** Until notification completeness is restored above a stated threshold, notification-based conformance findings should be reported as EVIDENCE_GAP, not as NONCOMPLIANT. Eligibility-based findings for the PD-3 population should be reported separately for the 289 apparently eligible cases, the 14 ineligible cases, and the 9 with missing attributes. The aggregate "deviation rate" should not be reported as a measure of model obsolescence, because the sample is purposive and the deviations mix control failures with model gaps.
