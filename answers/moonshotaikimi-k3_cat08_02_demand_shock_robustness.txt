## 2. Bottleneck-migration analysis

There is no single bottleneck to "fix"; any throughput gain at station *i* lands on station *i+1* within hours. Priority rules at 95% utilization **redistribute delay rather than reduce it** — only waste removal (repeats, recollections, batch waits, arrival variance) and demand shedding add real capacity.

**S1 — Mix-shift wave.** Chain: Courier intake (88%)  Pre-analytical (96%, queue 9.5h)  Assay (98%, queue 18h)  Technical validation (101%, queue 16h)  **Medical validation (queue 22h)**  Specialist review (queue 36h) for the Complex subset. ## 6. Residual vulnerabilities

1. **The chain remains hot.** Even after waste removal, peak utilization at four consecutive stations stays 90%. A true compound event (S1+S2+S3 simultaneously) exceeds any feasible mitigation — an explicit degradation doctrine is still needed (Routine degrades first, Complex overflows second, Urgent protected last-resort), agreed with clinicians *before* winter.
2. **Priority inflation (redesign-induced).** P3's protected lane creates an incentive to over-label Urgent. If Urgent share exceeds ~30%, the lane protects nothing and Routine starvation worsens; the cap converts the failure into visible demand-shedding, but gaming remains a live risk requiring audit.
3. **Auto-release rollback risk (redesign-induced).** A single safety incident forces reversion to manual validation, instantly re-loading Medical validation (22h peak queue) with no transition plan; conservative rule scope and audit sampling are load-bearing, not optional.
4. **Cross-trained staff contention (redesign-induced).** The flex pool is the only relief for two different bottlenecks (accession vs validation); under S5 both demand it at once. Floors and triggers ration it, but it cannot cover both fully.
5. **Correlated external dependencies.** The same weather that compresses courier arrivals (S4) delays partner-lab transport (P6) and slows recollection round-trips — three mitigations share one failure mode.
6. **Recollection transport is outside internal control.** The 60h peak edge and 47% abandonment are dominated by physician response and courier legs; P1 reduces but cannot eliminate this; Repeat-after-failure p90 stays worst-in-system.
7. **Data blindness.** The log is flattened by order while cases carry multiple tubes; tube-level congestion in Complex panels is invisible, and P3's time-to-breach dispatch depends on timestamp quality the current logging may not fully support. Trigger-based management fails silently if queue data lags.

| Open recollections older than 48h | <15 | 15–30 | >30 (fast-lane courier run) |
| Tomorrow's validator/specialist coverage vs forecast demand | 100% | 80–100% | <80% (S3 policy at 06:00) |
| M2 status: unplanned downtime, calibration due | none | warnings | downtime >4h (S2 policy) |
| Clerk/tech shift lengths | 9h | isolated >9h | systematic >9h (rotation) |

**Lagging outcome metrics (weekly review only — never used for real-time control):** median and p90 cycle time by class vs 18h/72h targets; Urgent penalty breaches; % of cases with any queue >12h (22% normal / 54% peak as anchors); overall rework %; recollection abandonment %; partner-lab TAT adherence; cancellation count (DFG endpoints).

The logic of the split: every leading indicator either forecasts load (mix, arrivals, staffing) or detects self-generated load (repeats, rejections, errors) early enough to act the same day; the lagging metrics confirm whether the policy bundle — not any single station — is holding the tails, which is the actual design goal.
