## 1. Decision rule

- Process stage must be strictly before `Diagnosis Complete`.
- Time to SLA deadline must be  2.0 hours (allows 45-min swarm + buffer for diagnosis acceleration).
- Cases failing either gate are excluded from swarm consideration.

- `p_adj = clip(p_now + offset, 0, 1)`
- SME: +0.065 (mid of 0.04–0.09 underprediction)
- Strategic Enterprise: 0.085 (mid of 0.05–0.12 overprediction)
- Public Sector: 0.00

- First alerts always pass.
- Repeat alerts require either: (a) `p = p_now  p_prev  0.08`, or (b) a materially new event (e.g., new diagnostic log, system change, customer-reported symptom shift).
- If neither condition is met, the alert is suppressed for a 2-hour cooldown to prevent alert fatigue and redundant swarm requests.

**Prioritization metric**
Cases are ranked by a conservative Expected Net Benefit (ENB) score:
`ENB = p_adj × eff_mid × Value  €450  FalseAlarmBurden  UncertaintyPenalty  SpilloverPenalty`
- `eff_mid` = midpoint of supplied risk-reduction range.
- `UncertaintyPenalty` = €300 for imputed data or sparse-segment cases; €0 otherwise.
- `SpilloverPenalty` = estimated expected harm to 6 untreated diagnosis-stage cases per slot (see Section 3 assumptions).
- Cases are selected top-down until capacity is reached, respecting the reserve preference.

**Capacity rule**
- Maximum 3 active slots per cycle; 1 slot held in reserve for unplanned regulatory/critical incidents.
- If ENB  0 for the next candidate, stop selection even if capacity remains.

**Value-based prioritization starving lower-value cases**
- *Risk:* High-contract-value cases (I-04, I-08) dominate slots, leaving SME/Public cases with longer wait times and higher untreated breach rates.
- *Safeguard:* Implement a tiered capacity cap (max 2 slots per cycle for top-value tier). Allocate minimum 1 slot per cycle to SME/Public segments when ENB > €0. Track breach rates by segment, not just absolute € saved.

**Uncertainty for Non-Native-Language Support**
- *Risk:* Sparse validation leads to poorly calibrated `p_now` and unreliable effect ranges, causing either under-intervention or wasted capacity.
- *Safeguard:* Apply a 0.15 probability shrinkage toward segment mean for Non-Native cases. Require human diagnostic review before swarm approval. Log all interventions separately to build a targeted validation cohort.

**Differential calibration across segments**
- *Risk:* Strategic overprediction and SME underprediction create systematic allocation bias if raw `p_now` is used.
- *Safeguard:* Enforce segment-specific calibration offsets in the decision rule (as defined in Section 1). Recalibrate offsets monthly using rolling backtests. Flag cases where `p_adj` crosses segment-specific decision thresholds for manual review.

**Selective intervention changing future training labels**
- *Risk:* Successful swarms prevent breaches, making treated cases appear as “low risk” in hindsight. This biases the model to underpredict risk for similar future cases (label leakage).
- *Safeguard:* Maintain a causal logging schema that records `p_now`, intervention flag, and counterfactual risk estimate. Retrain using inverse probability weighting or targeted missing-data imputation to preserve the observational risk distribution. Never use post-intervention outcomes as raw labels without adjustment.

**Spillover harms systematically falling on untreated groups**
- *Risk:* Queue delays from swarms disproportionately impact cases already in `Diagnosis Started` stage, often lower-value or public sector cases, increasing their breach probability.
- *Safeguard:* Cap concurrent swarms at 2 per queue segment. Include spillover penalty in ENB scoring. Monitor queue wait-time distribution by segment; if untreated delay exceeds 15 min median, pause new swarm allocations until queue drains.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

**Operational monitoring**
- Track slot utilization, reserve slot deployments, specialist overtime, and queue wait-time percentiles (P50, P90) by stage.
- Alert if concurrent swarms > 2 or average diagnosis queue delay > 15 min.

**Predictive monitoring**
- Weekly reliability diagrams (calibration curves) per segment.
- Brier score & log-loss tracked monthly.
- Trigger: If segment calibration error exceeds ±0.05 for two consecutive weeks, update offsets and re-run backtests.

**Causal-effect monitoring**
- Estimate Average Treatment Effect (ATT) on time-to-`Diagnosis Complete` using propensity-score matching against untreated cases with similar `p_now` and stage.
- Track actual breach avoidance rate vs. predicted effect ranges.
- Trigger: If observed ATT falls below 0.05 absolute risk reduction for 3 consecutive cycles, revise effect ranges downward and increase uncertainty penalties.

**Capacity & spillover monitoring**
- Log spillover p for untreated diagnosis cases post-swarm.
- Monitor false-alarm burden realization (actual customer disruption vs. estimated).
- Trigger: If spillover harm consistently exceeds direct benefit in Base scenario, reduce max active slots to 2 or implement queue-throttling rules.

**Fairness & feedback-loop monitoring**
- Monthly audit of intervention rates, breach rates, and queue delays by segment and contract value tier.
- Track label drift: compare pre-intervention `p_now` distribution with post-intervention outcome distribution.
- Trigger: If any segment’s breach rate increases >10% relative to baseline while receiving <15% of slots, activate fairness quota override. If label leakage indicators exceed threshold, switch to causal-aware retraining pipeline.

**Rule adjustment protocol**
- Changes to thresholds, offsets, or capacity caps require: (1) statistical validation against rolling 4-week window, (2) impact simulation across Optimistic/Base/Adverse scenarios, (3) sign-off from operations and risk governance. Policy is treated as a living decision framework, not a static threshold.
