## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

Treating April through October as "the model decaying over time" conflates four fundamentally distinct failure mechanisms that demand opposite engineering responses:

*   **January–March (Stable Baseline):** Volume (3,200–3,410), variant mix (45% V1, 35% V2, 20% V3), part lead time (28–35 h), and calibration (breach 0.18–0.19 vs. predicted 0.18–0.19; AUROC 0.81–0.82; ECE 0.022–0.025) establish a clean, 100%-mature reference regime.
*   **April (Pure Upstream Data-Pipeline Failure, Not Operational Drift):** Observed breach (0.19) and part delivery times (34 h) are virtually unchanged from baseline, and variant-level breach rates move only slightly (likely slightly distorted by 18% of traces falling into `Unmapped/other`). Yet AUROC collapses to 0.67 and mean predicted risk surges to 0.28. **Observed fact:** Unmapped `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` labels broke prefix truncation, duplicated events inflated cached pre-diagnosis event counts, and 14% of queue features went missing. **Hypothesis:** Duplicate events and late prefix cutoffs made normal cases look like heavily reworked cases to the model, inflating predicted probabilities. *Falsification:* If full raw-log normalization and deduplication leave April’s mean predicted risk near 0.28.
*   **May (Incomplete Remediation):** The May 6 mapping patch restores variant visibility (1% unmapped) and lifts AUROC to 0.78, but May 1–5 ran unpatched (2% unknown activity, 3% missing queue), leaving mild overprediction (0.21 vs. 0.18, ECE 0.050). Meanwhile, historical April tables remain tainted because cached event-count features and pre-Apr-20 duplicates were never cleaned.
*   **June (Downstream Process/Routing Policy Shift Without Deterioration in Execution):** On June 1, the remote-routing policy lifts V2 from 34% to 52% and drops V1 from 43% to 30%. Within-variant execution does *not* degrade (V1 breach 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35; remote fix edge stable at ~4 h), and discrimination remains strong (AUROC 0.79). However, because prediction occurs *immediately after diagnosis*—before the downstream `Remote Fix` vs. `Dispatch Technician` step occurs—cases that the legacy model scored assuming a V1 visit were routed to fast V2 resolution. Consequently, overall breach dropped to 0.15 while mean predicted risk remained 0.20 (ECE rose to 0.060). June is **benign downstream control-flow drift** causing upward calibration bias ($P(Y \mid X)$ shifted down), requiring lightweight recalibration rather than emergency feature overhaul.
*   **July–August (Supplier-Driven Process Performance Drift Inducing Severe Concept Drift + Pre-Partner-Z V3 Surge):** Starting July 10, supplier delays push `Order Part -> Part Received` from 42 h (Jun) to 65 h (Jul) and 130 h (Aug), driving V3 breach rates from 0.35 to 0.46 (Jul) and 0.64 (Aug). Simultaneously, V3 share climbs from 22% (Jul) to 35% (Aug) while V2 retreats from 50% to 40% *before* Partner Z arrives. **Hypothesis:** Either harder-to-fix seasonal/hardware cohorts arrived in August, or failed remote-fix candidates were increasingly diverted to parts replacement. Critically, because supplier backorders occur *after* `Initial Diagnosis Completed` and are absent from $X$, mean predicted risk stays stuck at 0.18–0.19 while observed breach jumps to 0.28 in August (even at 93% maturity, which slightly *understates* true August breaches). AUROC plunges to 0.68 because high-V3-propensity cases are severely under-scored relative to their new breach reality.
*   **September (Overlapping Population Shift, Covariate Shift, Unmodeled Risk Factors, and Growing Censoring):** Partner Z onboarding adds ~920 cases (+22% volume), shifting V3 share to 42% overall (58% in Partner Z vs. 36% non-Z) while supplier delays worsen to 148 h. Mean predicted risk rises slightly to 0.21—showing the model’s existing covariates partially detect V3-heavy symptoms—but falls far short of the 0.31 observed breach rate (ECE 0.140, AUROC 0.66). Furthermore, two effects pull in opposite directions: Partner Z brings unmodeled risk factors (rural travel distance, older devices) that raise true $P(Y \mid X)$, while falling label maturity (82%, median delay 42 d) censors slow V3 cases, meaning September’s *true* mature breach rate will exceed 0.31.
*   **October (Informative Right-Censoring Masking Severe Underlying Breach Rates):** Operational stress actually peaks in October (Partner Z at 27%, V3 at 41%, part delivery at 155 h), and mean predicted risk reaches 0.23. Yet observed breach seemingly drops from 0.31 to 0.16! **Observed fact:** Maturity is only 55% (median delay 52 d), and the outcome API backlog disproportionately withholds longer resolution paths. Fast, non-breaching V2 cases (3.8–4.6 h remote fix) close and report quickly, whereas slow, breaching V3 cases (155 h+ part wait) remain open or stuck in the API backlog. Treating October as "overprediction" (0.23 vs. 0.16) would invert reality.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1.  **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
    *   *Procedure:* For fully mature cohorts (Jan–Jun) and partially mature cohorts (Jul–Oct), plot empirical cumulative label arrival $F(\tau)$ at horizons $\tau \in \{14, 21, 30, 45, 60, 90\}$ days from case start, stratified by source and predicted-risk decile. Recompute Jan–Sep breach rates and AUROC artificially truncated at October’s current age distribution ("fixed-vintage backtest"), and apply Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting (IPCW) / Kaplan–Meier breach estimation for Aug–Oct.
    *   *Competing expectations:*
        *   **H1 (Informative right-censoring / API backlog dominates Oct):** Truncating Aug/Sep at a 30-day vintage reproduces an apparent ~0.15–0.17 breach rate (matching Oct’s 0.16), and IPCW projects Oct’s true breach rate at $\ge 0.32$.
        *   **H2 (Genuine operational recovery in Oct):** Even within fast-maturing strata (e.g., cases resolved within $\le 14$ days) and under IPCW adjustment, Oct breach hazards are significantly lower than Aug–Sep.

2.  **Prefix recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**
    *   *Procedure:* Re-ingest raw logs for Mar–Jun; map `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\to$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed`; resolve `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` via source event codes; deduplicate `(case_id, activity, timestamp)` prior to Apr 20; rebuild exact prefixes at the first diagnosis completion; recompute cached `events_before_diagnosis` and queue features; re-score with the frozen baseline model.
    *   *Competing expectations:*
        *   **H1 (Data-pipeline failure explains Apr & early May):** Cleaned April mean predicted risk drops from 0.28 to ~0.19, missing queue drops from 14% to ~1%, unmapped variants drop from 18% to ~0%, and April AUROC rebounds from 0.67 to ~0.80–0.82.
        *   **H2 (Genuine April operational/concept shift):** After full raw-log cleaning and feature backfill, April AUROC remains $< 0.72$ or calibration error stays high.

3.  **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**
    *   *Procedure:* Evaluate reliability diagrams, ECE, Brier skill score, and AUROC across `(Month × Source [Legacy vs. Partner Z])` and—strictly as a *post-hoc diagnostic* on mature cohorts—across realized `(Variant V1/V2/V3)`.
    *   *Competing expectations:*
        *   **H1 (Downstream routing shift in Jun; supplier concept shift in V3 in Jul–Sep):** In June, within-V1 and within-V2 calibration shapes look normal, and global overprediction comes entirely from cases shifting from V1 to V2. In Jul–Sep, severe underprediction is concentrated in cases realizing V3, while V1/V2 calibration drifts minimally (except for Partner Z rural V1 travel effects).
        *   **H2 (Global feature corruption or universal SLA tightening):** Underprediction and AUROC collapse occur uniformly across V1, V2, and V3 and equally across Legacy and Partner Z sources.

4.  **Comparison of process variants and DFG / performance-DFG edges**
    *   *Procedure:* Extract monthly case-start variant distributions and edge-level duration quantiles (p50, p75, p90) across all DFG transitions (`Case Created -> Triage`, `Triage -> Diagnosis`, `Diagnosis -> Order Part`, `Order Part -> Part Received`, `Part Received -> Dispatch`, `Dispatch -> Repair Completed`). Stratify Sep–Oct performance DFGs by Partner Z vs. Legacy.
    *   *Competing expectations:*
        *   **H1 (Supplier bottleneck + Partner Z rural dispatch delay):** Legacy cases show duration blow-up isolated to `Order Part -> Part Received` (42 h $\to$ 130–155 h), whereas Partner Z *additionally* exhibits elevated p50/p90 durations on `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed` (rural travel) and higher transition probability on `Diagnosis -> Order Part`.
        *   **H2 (Front-end triage bottleneck):** Pre-diagnosis edges (`Case Created -> Triage -> Diagnosis`) consumed the SLA budget before prediction even occurred.

5.  **Conditional outcome-relationship tests ($P(Y \mid X, \text{Path})$ and $P(Y \mid X)$ stability)**
    *   *Procedure:* Fit a diagnostic logistic/gradient-boosted model for $Y$ on clean pre-diagnosis features $X$, month indicators, and interactions $X \times \text{Month}$ on mature cohorts (Jan–Aug, plus IPCW-weighted Sep). Repeat conditioning on realized variant $(X, V)$ and further conditioning on `part_wait_hours` $(X, V, T_{\text{part}})$.
    *   *Competing expectations:*
        *   **H1 (Process-mediated concept drift):** Month interaction terms $X \times \text{Aug}$ are highly significant in $P(Y \mid X)$ (proving concept drift at prediction time), shrink substantially when conditioning on realized variant $V$, and largely vanish when additionally conditioning on `Order Part -> Part Received` duration $T_{\text{part}}$.
        *   **H2 (Pure covariate drift):** Month main effects and $X \times \text{Month}$ interactions are statistically indistinguishable from zero in $P(Y \mid X)$ once $X$ is controlled for.

6.  **Population reweighting / transportability checks for Partner Z**
    *   *Procedure:* Train a domain classifier $\hat{e}(X) = P(\text{Source} = \text{Partner Z} \mid X)$ on September’s mature/IPCW-adjusted cases. Reweight Legacy September cases by inverse odds $\frac{\hat{e}(X)}{1 - \hat{e}(X)}$ to match Partner Z’s observed covariate distribution $P_Z(X)$, then compare reweighted Legacy breach rate and calibration against Partner Z’s actuals. Regress residuals on the candidate features `rural_travel_distance` and `device_age`.
    *   *Competing expectations:*
        *   **H1 (Omitted-variable bias / local concept mismatch in Partner Z):** Reweighting Legacy cases on current features $X$ closes only part of the 0.27 $\to$ 0.45 breach gap; residuals correlate strongly with `rural_travel_distance` and `device_age`.
        *   **H2 (Pure observed covariate shift + differential label censoring):** After matching $P(X)$ via propensity weights and adjusting for Partner Z’s slower label maturity, Partner Z and Legacy breach rates and AUROC align within sampling noise.

7.  **Feature missingness and lineage audits**
    *   *Procedure:* Trace end-to-end field lineage from raw source payloads to the feature store for every model input, auditing null rates, default/zero-imputation spikes, duplicate event timestamps per case, and cache hit/staleness rates across Apr 1, Apr 20, May 6, Sep 1 (Partner Z schema onboarding), and Oct 1.
    *   *Competing expectations:*
        *   **H1 (Isolated Apr–May pipeline failure + clean Sep schema integration):** Schema/cache anomalies spike solely in Apr 1–May 6, while Partner Z features populate cleanly for existing $X$ fields (with missingness restricted to unmodeled fields).
        *   **H2 (Hidden Partner Z mapping defect):** Partner Z onboarding silently mapped certain categorical codes or queue metrics to default values, mimicking business shift.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

### Staged Remediation Plan
1.  **Stage 1 — Immediate Containment (Days 1–3):**
    *   **Freeze automated retraining** so October’s 55%-mature, API-backlogged labels cannot poison model weights.
    *   **Fix the Outcome API backlog** and enforce strict schema normalization (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\to$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed`) plus idempotent `(case_id, activity, timestamp)` deduplication in streaming ingestion.
    *   **Operational guardrail:** Append a rule-based operational warning flag alongside model scores whenever diagnosis outputs indicate a parts order on backordered SKUs or Partner Z rural zones, rather than silently shifting a broken global probability threshold.
2.  **Stage 2 — Historical Feature Repair & Short-Term Recalibration (Weeks 1–2):**
    *   Backfill April and May 1–5 prefixes from normalized, deduplicated raw logs and recompute cached `events_before_diagnosis`.
    *   Apply **temporary stratified post-hoc recalibration** (e.g., logistic/Platt offset stratified by `Source  {Legacy, Partner Z}` and `Diagnosis indicates Part Needed  {Yes, No}` if recorded at diagnosis completion) fitted on **August + IPCW-adjusted September** data. *Rationale:* Recalibration alone restores aggregate expected breach rates (fixing the 0.19–0.21 vs. 0.28–0.31 gap), though it cannot recover lost AUROC caused by missing supplier/rural features.
3.  **Stage 3 — Feature Engineering Repair (Weeks 2–4):**
    *   Add **dynamic environment/context features available at diagnosis completion**: rolling 7-day median/p75 `Order Part -> Part Received` duration (overall and by part category/supplier), active part backorder counts, and remote-routing eligibility flags (capturing the June policy explicitly).
    *   Onboard **Partner Z / population covariates** available at case creation/diagnosis: `rural_travel_distance` (estimated from customer-to-depot geography), `device_age`, and `partner_id`.
4.  **Stage 4 — Full Retraining (Once Outcome API Backlog Clears & Sep Hits $\ge 98\%$ Maturity):**
    *   Retrain a full gradient-boosted/tabular prefix model incorporating the repaired historical features and new operational/geographic covariates, applying recency sample weighting or explicit regime features (supplier lead-time state) so baseline mechanics and supplier-stressed mechanics are both learned cleanly.

### Safe vs. Unsafe Months for Training
*   **Jan–Mar & Jun:** **Safe** (clean pipeline, 100% mature; June requires including the remote-eligibility feature or sample weighting so pre-June routing rules do not bias baseline probabilities).
*   **Apr & May 1–5:** **Unsafe as currently stored**; **Safe only after** raw-log deduplication, label normalization, and full cache recomputation are verified by Test 2.
*   **May 6–31, Jul, Aug:** **Safe once Aug reaches $\ge 99\%$ maturity** via outcome API recovery (Jul–Aug are essential for learning how supplier lead-time spikes translate into breaches).
*   **Sep:** **Unsafe for unweighted training right now (82% mature)**; becomes **Safe** once API backlog clearance and elapsed time push maturity to $\ge 98\%$ (or with validated IPCW weights in the interim).
*   **Oct:** **Strictly Unsafe** (55% mature with informative censoring against breaching paths). Exclude from training and standard validation until cohort maturity exceeds 98%.

### Is a Segment-Specific or Variant-Specific Model Justified?
*   **Beware post-prediction leakage on Variants:** The model predicts *immediately after* `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, whereas `V1`, `V2`, and `V3` are defined by *future* events (`Dispatch Technician`, `Remote Fix`, `Order Part`). Conditioning directly on realized `V1/V2/V3` during training or inference is **target/future leakage**.
*   **Recommended architecture (Hierarchical / Multi-Task Prefix Model, Not Disjoint Silos):** Instead of separate realized-variant models, use either:
    1.  The **planned next action** recorded *at* diagnosis completion (if the diagnostic assessment explicitly outputs `recommended_route  {Remote, Standard_Dispatch, Order_Part}`), or
    2.  A **two-stage / multi-task model** at diagnosis completion that predicts $\hat{P}(V = v \mid X)$ and $\hat{P}(\text{Breach} \mid X, \hat{V}=v, Z_{\text{env}})$ using real-time supplier lead times $Z_{\text{env}}$ and geographic features (`rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`).
*   For **Partner Z**, once `rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, and planned-routing/supplier features are added, a **single unified model with source/geography features (plus source-specific calibration layers if needed)** is preferable to an isolated Partner Z model, because Partner Z currently has zero fully mature months of training data.

| **4. Population & Covariate** | Partner/source mix (`partner_id` share), case volume delta, PSI / Wasserstein on clean pre-diagnosis features $X$ and predicted risk $\hat{p}$ | New source $> 5\%$ share OR feature PSI $> 0.20$ |
| **5. Process-Variant (Control-Flow)** | Rolling share of V1, V2, V3, and `Unmapped/other` (tracked on closed cases + censored prefix transitions `Diagnosis -> Order Part / Remote Fix`) | Variant share shift $> 8\text{ pp}$ vs. 30-day baseline, or `Unmapped` $> 2\%$ |
| **6. Performance-DFG (Timing)** | Rolling p50/p90 elapsed time on key DFG edges (`Order Part -> Part Received`, `Diagnosis -> Remote Fix`, `Dispatch -> Repair`) | Edge p50 increases $> 35\%$ or crosses SLA-critical threshold (e.g., part wait $> 60\text{ h}$) |
| **7. Predictive & Calibration** | AUROC, Brier, ECE, observed-to-expected (O/E) breach ratio—computed **only** on fixed-vintage or $\ge 98\%$-mature cohorts (plus IPCW early warning) | Mature/fixed-vintage AUROC drop $> 0.04$ OR ECE $> 0.05$ |

**Alert Dependency & Gating Logic (Preventing False Retraining):**
Alerts evaluate top-down along a strict dependency DAG:
1.  **Gate 1 (Pipeline Gate):** If **Data-Quality Monitor** fires (e.g., April’s 17% `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` or missing queue spike), **suppress covariate, process-variant, calibration, and retraining workflows**; route P1 incident to Data Engineering.
2.  **Gate 2 (Maturity Gate):** If **Label-Maturity / Outcome API Monitor** fires (e.g., October’s 55% maturity / API backlog), **quarantine raw Predictive & Calibration alarms** and **hard-block automated retraining/recalibration**; switch executive reporting to fixed-vintage (e.g., 14-day breach rate) and leading **Performance-DFG / Population** indicators, and page Platform Engineering to clear the API backlog.
3.  **Gate 3 (Root-Cause Triage for Validated Model Degradation):** Only when **Data-Quality is green** AND **Cohort Maturity $\ge 98\%$** (or fixed-vintage controlled) may a **Calibration / AUROC** alarm trigger model remediation:
    *   *Calibration shift ONLY + Intervention/Routing shift (June pattern):* Trigger lightweight post-hoc recalibration review.
    *   *AUROC + Calibration degradation + Performance-DFG or Population shift (Aug–Sep pattern):* Trigger Data Science investigation for feature augmentation (supplier telemetry, partner covariates) and staged retraining.

### Monthly Process-Evidence Extraction Sketch (Python + PM4Py)

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

LABEL_MAP = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
KNOWN_ACTIVITIES = {
    "Case Created", "Triage", "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    "Dispatch Technician", "Repair Completed", "Case Closed",
    "Remote Fix", "Order Part", "Part Received",
}

def extract_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str, months: list[str]) -> pd.DataFrame:
    raw = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)

    # 1. Normalize renamed diagnosis labels BEFORE formatting & feature extraction
    raw["activity_clean"] = raw["activity"].replace(LABEL_MAP).fillna("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")
    raw.loc[~raw["activity_clean"].isin(KNOWN_ACTIVITIES), "activity_clean"] = "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY"

    # 2. Audit & remove exact duplicate events (e.g., pre-20-Apr migration duplicates)
    dup_mask = raw.duplicated(subset=["case_id", "activity_clean", "timestamp"], keep="first")
    dedup = raw.loc[~dup_mask].sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"]).copy()

    # 3. Format for PM4Py
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        dedup, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity_clean", timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    case_starts = log_df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()

    # Build diagnosis prefixes once for temporal prefix auditing
    diag_ts = (
        log_df[log_df["concept:name"] == "Initial Diagnosis Completed"]
        .groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    )
    prefix_df = log_df.loc[
        log_df["case:concept:name"].isin(diag_ts.index)
        & (log_df["time:timestamp"] <= log_df["case:concept:name"].map(diag_ts))
    ]

    records = []
    for m in months:  # e.g., ["2026-01", ..., "2026-10"]
        start_dt = pd.Timestamp(f"{m}-01 00:00:00", tz="UTC")
        end_dt = start_dt + pd.offsets.MonthEnd(1) + pd.Timedelta(hours=23, minutes=59, seconds=59)

        # COHORT A: Event-calendar window — captures current operational congestion in month m,
        # even for cases started in prior months (fragments traces across month boundaries).
        cal_events = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            log_df,
            start_dt.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"),
            end_dt.strftime("%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"),
            mode="events",
        )

        # COHORT B: Case-start cohort — follows full trajectories of cases opened in month m;
        # aligns with cohort predictive metrics, but recent months suffer right-censoring.
        cohort_ids = case_starts[(case_starts >= start_dt) & (case_starts <= end_dt)].index
        cohort_df = log_df[log_df["case:concept:name"].isin(cohort_ids)]

        dfg_counts, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(cohort_df)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cohort_df)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort_df)

        # Extract PM4Py temporal features on diagnosis prefixes for this case-start cohort
        cohort_prefixes = prefix_df[prefix_df["case:concept:name"].isin(cohort_ids)]
        temp_feats = (
            pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(cohort_prefixes)
            if not cohort_prefixes.empty else pd.DataFrame()
        )

        def edge_hours(edge: tuple[str, str]) -> float | None:
            val = perf_dfg.get(edge)
            if val is None:
                return None
            sec = val["median"] if isinstance(val, dict) else float(val)
            return round(sec / 3600.0, 2)

        total_cases = max(len(cohort_ids), 1)
        v3_sig = (
            "Case Created", "Triage", "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
            "Order Part", "Part Received", "Dispatch Technician",
            "Repair Completed", "Case Closed",
        )
        v3_count = variants.get(v3_sig, 0)
        v3_cases = v3_count if isinstance(v3_count, int) else len(v3_count)

        records.append({
            "month": m,
            "cases_started": len(cohort_ids),
            "calendar_events_in_month": len(cal_events),
            "unknown_activity_share": round(
                (cohort_df["concept:name"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(), 4
            ),
            "missing_diagnosis_prefix_share": round(
                1.0 - cohort_ids.isin(diag_ts.index).mean(), 4
            ),
            "v3_complete_share": round(v3_cases / total_cases, 4),
            "dfg_diag_to_order_part_cnt": dfg_counts.get(
                ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Order Part"), 0
            ),
            "part_wait_median_h": edge_hours(("Order Part", "Part Received")),
            "remote_fix_median_h": edge_hours(("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")),
            "prefix_temporal_feature_cols": temp_feats.shape[1],
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(records)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1.  **Retraining or recalibrating downward on October’s immature, censored labels**
    *   *Failure mode:* Naive monitoring reads October’s 0.23 mean predicted risk vs. 0.16 observed breach as severe *overprediction* and triggers automated retraining or downward Platt scaling. Because the outcome API backlog selectively hides slow, breaching V3 cases (155 h part waits), the retrained model slashes risk scores right when actual SLA breaches are exceeding 30%+, blinding dispatchers to urgent escalations.
    *   *Mitigation:* Enforce a hard **Label-Maturity Gate** ($\ge 98\%$ cohort maturity and healthy API lag) before any data enters calibration or training pipelines; use fixed-vintage or IPCW survival estimators for interim monitoring.

2.  **Recalibrating or retraining around April’s schema/pipeline bug**
    *   *Failure mode:* Treating April’s 0.28 predicted risk vs. 0.19 observed breach as covariate/concept drift and fitting a downward calibration offset (or retraining on corrupted April features). Once the May 6 mapping patch restores normal prefixes and queue features, the downward-adjusted model systematically *underpredicts* valid May cases—and keeping uncleaned April records in historical training sets permanently distorts learned weights for `events_before_diagnosis`.
    *   *Mitigation:* Gate model updates behind **Data-Quality & Lineage checks** (`UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, duplicate rates, missing feature rates); remediate upstream schema changes via raw-log normalization and full feature-cache backfills rather than model adjustments.

3.  **Masking supplier-driven concept drift (Jul–Sep) with global probability scaling—or ignoring it as "temporary noise"**
    *   *Failure mode:* Dismissing Jul–Aug underprediction disables proactive SLA interventions during a multi-month supplier crisis; conversely, applying a *global* upward intercept shift raises predicted breach risk indiscriminately across V1 and V2 cases (which still breach at only 0.10–0.19), flooding operations with false alarms on remote-fix cases while still failing to rank V3 backordered cases accurately (AUROC stays stuck at ~0.66–0.68).
    *   *Mitigation:* Pair **Performance-DFG edge monitors** (`Order Part -> Part Received`) with feature engineering so dynamic supplier lead-time/backorder states are fed directly into the model, restoring both discrimination and conditional calibration.

4.  **Misattributing Partner Z’s higher scores/errors to "unfair partner bias" (or blaming Partner Z for all late-summer drift)**
    *   *Failure mode:* Observing Partner Z’s discrepancy (0.45 observed breach vs. 0.27 non-Z, higher predicted scores, or poor calibration) without stratified process and maturity audits leads to two opposite mistakes: either artificially forcing demographic parity (capping Partner Z risk scores so rural customers receive *fewer* proactive escalations despite genuine older-device and travel-distance bottlenecks), or scapegoating Partner Z for the August collapse (even though Partner Z was 0% of August volume!).
    *   *Mitigation:* Conduct **stratified calibration, transportability reweighting, and DFG duration audits** separating Legacy vs. Partner Z at equal label maturity, and incorporate objective operational predictors (`rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, part wait times) rather than arbitrary partner penalties or parity caps.

5.  **Introducing target/future leakage by building naive "variant-specific" models**
    *   *Failure mode:* Noticing that breach rates diverge sharply across V1 (0.19), V2 (0.12), and V3 (0.61), an engineer trains three separate models conditioned on realized variant `V1/V2/V3`. Offline backtests show spectacular AUROC (>0.90), but production fails immediately because `Order Part`, `Remote Fix`, and `Dispatch Technician` have not happened yet when the model fires immediately after `Initial Diagnosis Completed`.
    *   *Mitigation:* Enforce strict **prefix-timestamp lineage assertions** (`feature_timestamp <= prediction_timestamp`) in feature pipelines, and model downstream routing uncertainty explicitly via planned-route outputs at diagnosis or multi-task variant-propensity estimation $\hat{P}(V \mid X)$.
