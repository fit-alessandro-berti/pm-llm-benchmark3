# 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

**Hard prerequisites concern verified business states, not purchasing every upstream module.** A manually validated artifact can satisfy a dependency. An automation recommendation, uploaded document, or completed task flag cannot substitute for clearance or approval.

The main dependencies are:

- **Trusted registration and confirmed classification → correct parallel workstreams.** A1 and A2 can improve this foundation, but existing human checks can provide it. Exposure and product classification determine document requirements, collateral obligations, approval routing, and High-exposure four-eyes rules. Uncertain classifications require review; they must not default to a less restrictive path.
- **A5 → complete-enough documents → A6 → reconciled financial data → human credit analysis → A8.** A5 supports completeness and clearer customer instructions. A6 requires suitable source documents and provides proposed normalized data, which analysts validate. Financial normalization is conditional on the case's requirements; neither all 9,200 reviews nor all 8,900 credit analyses necessarily follow the normalization variant.
- **A3 → unresolved hits/mismatches → A4 → authorized KYC clearance.** A3 and A4 are complementary assistants rather than a compulsory sequence of purchases. A4 can operate with current KYC tooling. Only authorized compliance staff can resolve ambiguous evidence and grant clearance.
- **Confirmed collateral requirement → A7-supported request/routing → acceptable external valuation.** Starting valuation earlier can overlap it with eligible credit/document work, subject to policy, adequate inputs, and customer/cost authorization. Routing automation cannot guarantee external capacity or accepted valuation quality.
- **KYC clearance AND required normalized financials AND required accepted valuation AND reconciled credit notes → A8/committee readiness.** Committee packaging is a synchronization point. “Required” is variant-specific; genuinely inapplicable artifacts need a documented exemption, not a missing-data workaround.
- **Authoritative approval → A9-controlled contract → KYC-cleared, identity/consent-validated signing through A10 → fully controlled activation through A11.** Contract preparation has 7,600 occurrences versus 3,900 committee decisions. This suggests that approval variants or logging coverage need verification; do not require a nonexistent committee record or invent an approval. Every contract must reference its applicable authorized decision.
- **A12 supports and enforces controls across these paths.** It links issues to evidence, accountable owners, independent reviewers, and verified closure conditions. It must prevent prohibited progression through the existing workflow, not merely display warnings.

The directly-follows graph must be interpreted with these dependencies. KYC → Credit analysis (4,200) does not establish that financial review is absent; Classify → KYC (7,250) and Classify → Financial review (3,940) do not establish mutually exclusive paths. Reconstruct actual starts/completions and artifact readiness to recognize concurrency. Where true task timestamps are missing, instrument the pilots instead of inferring readiness from recording order. Similarly, Credit analysis → Committee packaging (2,700) and External valuation wait → Committee packaging (1,980) may represent different observed last predecessors to an AND-join, not independent approvals or disjoint case counts.

Human escalation is a prerequisite for every assistant. Three compliance specialists, with work concentrated on two senior people, cannot absorb unlimited alerts. Reserve review capacity, establish deputies and escalation rules, and use trained back-office staff for permitted evidence collection. Analysts must retain time for A6 validation and judgment-heavy credit analysis. Independent, authorized High-exposure packaging reviewers must be available; a model cannot serve as the second human in a four-eyes rule.

Define deep integration as a module changing authoritative core/workflow states or enforcing controls across them. In the recommended portfolio, reserve the two first-six-month integration slots for **A12 and A6**. Pilot A5 through existing approved communication/document channels and A4 through read-only evidence support with human updates in the existing KYC system. These pilots must not introduce hidden core write integrations; any required new authoritative write interface is scheduled after month six.

# 3. Portfolio alternatives

Each portfolio contains four modules and strengthens controls in its first wave. Supporting policy, staffing, supplier, and scheduling changes are ordinary process changes, not additional automation modules.

| Portfolio | Modules | What it optimizes | What it sacrifices | First-wave controls and integration plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Quick-win** | **A1, A5, A9, A10** | Visible registration effort savings, clearer document intake, faster controlled contract preparation, and easier signing | Does not directly relieve specialist mismatch resolution or analyst normalization; increases downstream inflow and retains valuation/committee delays | Start controlled A1 and A9 releases only after approved input/rule checks; A9 provides first-wave control strengthening. Use first-half deep slots for A1/A9. A5 uses existing channels; add A10 integration after month six |
| **Bottleneck/synchronization — recommended** | **A4, A5, A6, A12** | KYC branch readiness, fewer document loops, analyst capacity, and control-safe convergence toward approval/contract/signing | Defers high-volume registration savings, automated external valuation routing, pack assembly, and e-sign adoption; cannot remove weekly committee cadence or customer/external waiting | A12 is first. A5 and A4 begin bounded pilots; integrate A6 next. First-half deep slots: A12/A6; A4 remains a human-operated sidecar until later |
| **Compliance-first** | **A3, A8, A9, A12** | Auditable KYC triage, independent and complete committee packs, decision/contract consistency, and controlled remediation | Does not directly repair missing-document loops or normalization capacity, and may initially lengthen queues through additional holds/review | A12 and controlled A3 form the first wave and take the first-half deep slots. Pilot A8 read-only; integrate its workflow and A9 after month six, once inputs and segregation controls are reliable |

The quick-win portfolio is defensible for clean, lower-complexity cases with spare downstream capacity. It is not a justification for releasing all 11,880 registration occurrences faster regardless of readiness. The compliance-first portfolio is preferable if management determines that observed control violations demand immediate broader containment before throughput gains. The recommended portfolio balances that containment with relief of shared upstream constraints.


# 4. Expected process-level effects

These are directional expectations to test in pilots, not quantified ROI or promised cycle-time reductions. Evaluate both median and tail performance by applicant, product, collateral requirement, and exception status. Keep open, declined, and withdrawn cases visible alongside activated loans, and distinguish calendar from business time.

## Quick-win: A1, A5, A9, A10

- **Cycle time:** Receive → Register (11,880) becomes quicker, and eligible cases may move faster through Contract preparation → Signing (7,300). A10 can reduce some of the 44h signing wait, but customer availability and consent remain limiting. Cases blocked by entity mismatches, analyst queues, or external valuations see much less improvement.
- **Waiting time:** Targets registration's 1.5h wait, contract preparation's 22h wait, and signing follow-up. It leaves mismatch resolution's 31h, normalization's 20h, credit analysis's 28h, and external valuation's 58h largely intact. Faster registration can increase these queues; restrict release to capacity-supported cohorts.
- **Rework:** A5 targets Financial review → Request missing documents (3,300) → Financial review (2,800). A9 checks approved terms before release, reducing preventable Contract preparation → Remediate control issue (650) → Contract preparation (510) loops. Renewal cases merit particular attention because their contract-remediation rate is 14%.
- **Handovers:** Removes some clerk registration, document-chasing, legal preparation, and RM reminder handoffs. Human exception ownership must remain explicit; duplicate detections, unusual contracts, and failed signing attempts create replacement handovers.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office and legal routine effort falls; RM chasing may fall. The nine analysts and three compliance specialists receive cleaner or faster inflow without direct normalization/mismatch assistance. Legal's four FTE can still face month-end bursts, and operations may face more signing completions. Registration automation does not justify headcount cuts while downstream queues grow.
- **Control violations:** A9 strengthens decision/contract consistency. A10 is enabled only after verified KYC clearance and identity/consent checks; increasing e-sign adoption before this gate would amplify provisional signing. Existing enforceable four-eyes controls must remain in place even though A12 is absent. If these prerequisites cannot be enforced, A9/A10 stay in draft or shadow mode.

## Bottleneck/synchronization: A4, A5, A6, A12

- **Cycle time:** A4 targets a shared blocking condition on the critical path in 82% of affected cases. A5 reduces avoidable document-return cycles, while A6 helps financial readiness and preserves analyst capacity. Benefits depend on which branch finishes last: faster KYC does not shorten a case still waiting for valuation or financial data.
- **Waiting time:** Targets mismatch waiting of 31h and normalization waiting of 20h, and can indirectly reduce financial review's 18h and credit analysis's 28h queues. Earlier branch readiness can reduce Committee packaging's 26h wait and missed weekly cutoffs, without removing the committee's cadence. External valuation's 58h and signing's 44h remain major residual waits.
- **Rework:** A4 prepares clearer evidence for KYC → Mismatch (1,750) → KYC (1,160), reducing avoidable returns while preserving necessary rechecks. A5 improves the missing-document loop; A6 reduces preventable normalization corrections within its supported coverage. A12 moves control discovery before contract release/signing, although recorded holds may initially rise as hidden issues become visible.
- **Handovers:** Shared checklists, evidence bundles, and validated financial artifacts reduce repeated RM/back-office/analyst and KYC/specialist exchanges. A12 assigns one accountable issue owner and makes required specialist review explicit rather than leaving cases circulating.
- **Resource demand:** A6 releases analyst preparation time for validation, credit judgment, and independent committee work. A4 shifts specialists toward ambiguous cases; it may initially expose a backlog. Use back-office afternoon slack, with utilization below 65% after 15:00, for permitted document/evidence preparation and queue clearing. Provide deputies for the two senior specialists. Smoother KYC completion increases legal demand, requiring planned coverage rather than relying on historical underutilization.
- **Control violations:** A12 blocks signing without prior KYC clearance, records legal staff's verified contract-to-approval checks, gates unresolved discrepancies, and enforces distinct human completers for High-exposure credit analysis and packaging. These directly address the 6.5% provisional-signing rate, 1.1% remediation rate, 5.8% High-exposure four-eyes violations, and contract-remediation findings. The workbench supports responsible humans; it does not automatically resolve ambiguous policy or approve exceptions.

## Compliance-first: A3, A8, A9, A12

- **Cycle time:** Some clean cases benefit from organized KYC triage and faster pack/contract preparation. Overall cycle time can initially increase because previously bypassed controls now create holds. Missing documents, 75-minute normalization work, and judgment-heavy High-exposure analysis remain constraints.
- **Waiting time:** A3 can improve the 4h KYC queue, provided additional escalations fit specialist capacity. A8 reduces assembly waiting after its required artifacts arrive; it cannot eliminate missing-artifact waiting or external valuation's 58h. Committee packaging → Committee decision (3,900) still depends on weekly scheduling.
- **Rework:** A8 prevents incomplete or inconsistent packs from reaching the committee, and A9/A12 address the contract-remediation loop, particularly Credit-line renewal. Neither module fixes defective source financials merely by assembling or reproducing them; invalid inputs return to their accountable owner.
- **Handovers:** A3 provides structured hit evidence, A8 provides versioned packs, and A12 provides issue ownership. Additional independent reviews are intentional control handovers, not waste to eliminate. A pack is complete only after the appropriately independent human review.
- **Resource demand:** Compliance may receive more true issues and false-positive alerts; analyst review and missing-document work remain high. Packaging and legal preparation effort can fall, but retained humans must perform independent checks and handle unusual cases. Protect specialist review capacity and month-end legal coverage before increasing throughput.
- **Control violations:** Provides the broadest automated coverage of KYC triage, segregation, pack integrity, and contract consistency. Benefits depend on A3 false-negative control, genuinely independent human review, current rules, and A12 enforcement. More detected violations initially can reflect better detection; distinguish detection from new violations caused by automation.

For all portfolios, measure branch-ready timestamps and the last missing prerequisite at committee/contract joins. Neither aggregate DFG edge counts nor a sum of activity medians identifies the actual critical path. Faster upstream completion can also bunch cases before a weekly meeting, shifting rather than removing waiting.

# 5. Risks and compensating controls

| Risk introduced or amplified | Compensating control |
|---|---|
| **False negatives in KYC/entity matching** permit an unsafe clearance | Restrict assistants to evidence preparation and recommendations; retain authorized clearance. Review uncertain, conflicting, and out-of-scope cases. Validate missed-hit risk by segment against adjudicated cases, with independent testing and uncertainty assessment. Low observed errors in a small pilot are insufficient evidence for broad release |
| **False positives, false duplicate matches, or over-requesting documents** create avoidable work and customer harm | Use confidence bands and human confirmation; do not automatically merge entities or reject applications. Track escalation yield, unnecessary requests, and specialist workload. Adjust thresholds only with control-owner approval |
| **Poor PDF extraction or incorrect normalization** contaminates credit analysis | Check entity, period, currency, units, totals, and source coverage. Link each value to its source page, retain originals, and require analyst validation. Route low-confidence/unsupported documents to a staffed manual path |
| **Wrong product/exposure routing** omits required controls | Confirm uncertain classifications, aggregate relevant exposure, and recheck routing after material changes. Default uncertain cases to review. Retain the applicable collateral and approval requirements in the case record |
| **Incomplete, stale, or inconsistent artifacts** are presented as ready | Maintain versioned prerequisite records; reconcile sources and invalidate downstream artifacts when material upstream data changes. Require explicit accepted valuations and documented exemptions where applicable |
| **Control bypass, stale clearance, or premature signing** | Enforce KYC-before-signing at contract release/signing access, including assisted/manual channels. Revalidate authoritative status when signing and activation occur. Fail closed on unknown state; later remediation cannot retroactively satisfy a pre-signing requirement |
| **Four-eyes violation under month-end pressure** | Compare actual human identities for High-exposure credit-analysis and packaging completion. Prevent self-completion through another account or bot attribution. Require meaningful independent review, named deputies, and audit of rush-period behavior; an automated pack is not the second pair of eyes |
| **Outdated legal rules or approval/contract mismatch** | Assign legal owners, effective dates, version control, and approved regression cases to rule/template changes. Compare key terms with the authoritative approval. Material differences require authorized correction or renewed approval before release |
| **Model/rule drift or new document/customer variants** | Monitor error, override, coverage, and escalation rates by segment and input type. Review significant changes in product/policy/source data; revalidate before widening scope. Retain a versioned rollback and manual processing option |
| **Unmanageable exceptions or silent abandonment** | Define exception categories, accountable queues, aging limits, escalation, staffing, and closure evidence. Measure reopenings and human handling time. Pause expansion when arrivals exceed funded review capacity; automation must never close unresolved cases to improve its completion rate |
| **Missing auditability or opaque overrides** | Preserve case/artifact IDs, source references, model/rule versions, suggestion and confidence, human override with reason, reviewer identity, timestamps, and final authorized action. Protect logs from alteration and reconcile them with core events; apply appropriate access and data-minimization controls |
| **Customer friction and RM resistance** | Co-design product-specific instructions, request a consolidated checklist, suppress duplicate reminders, and offer assisted/non-digital channels. Use one communication owner and measure complaints, abandonment, repeated requests, and RM work, not portal adoption alone |
| **Supplier delays or disputed valuations** | Agree input/acceptance standards, deadlines, escalation, and backup capacity with valuers. Start eligible requests early and monitor late/poor-quality work. Preserve independent acceptance; do not trade valuation quality for speed |
| **Integration failures, retries, and hidden workload transfer** | Reconcile authoritative state, make supported writes idempotent, test partial failures, and route uncertainty to humans. Maintain stop/rollback procedures and funded fallback capacity. Release work at a pace downstream pools can absorb |

Automation changes the distribution of human work: remaining cases are often harder than the routine cases removed. Measure review effort, not just exception percentages or nominal hours saved. Do not transfer compliance decisions to clerks, legal interpretation to a model, or specialist responsibility to an unattended queue.

# 6. Recommended phased rollout

Deploy only **A12, A5, A4, and A6** in year one. Sequence customer/document quality work before scaling normalization, while placing control containment first.

| Phase | Module order and scope | Integration commitment | Gate criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Month 1: baseline and prerequisite repair** | Reconstruct concurrent branches, actual clearance/signing times, approval variants, repeated visits, and month-end failures. Establish policy owners, current checklists, exception ownership, specialist deputies, and independent packaging coverage. Configure existing permitted manual holds while preparing A12 | Size interfaces and confirm that A12 and A6 are the two first-half deep modules. A5/A4 pilots use existing channels or read-only access | No ambiguous ownership of signing/approval/segregation gates; traceable prerequisite states; representative baseline including committee cycles, month end, difficult documents, and exceptions |
| **Months 1–2: A12 first-wave control foundation** | Introduce structured remediation and enforce KYC-before-signing, approval/contract consistency, and High-exposure four-eyes checks. Use humans to resolve findings and policy ambiguity | **Deep integration 1: A12**, within a bounded, enforceable scope; mere dashboard alerts are insufficient | Tested fail-closed behavior and no hard-gate bypass in the pilot; complete audit records; named, staffed remediation queues; no unresolved high-severity control defect |
| **Months 2–3: A5 bounded customer/document pilot** | Pilot co-designed checklists and consolidated nudges in selected Micro/SME product cohorts; support RM and assisted channels. Extend to Equipment finance and renewals after validating their requirements | Existing approved portal/communication/document channels; authoritative case updates remain in the current human workflow | Fewer avoidable repeated requests and review returns; no deterioration in abandonment/complaints; no conflicting requests; analyst inflow stays within capacity |
| **Months 3–4: A4 specialist sidecar pilot** | Shadow-test suggestions, then assist evidence collection for selected mismatch categories. Specialists retain clearance; uncertain/high-exposure cases retain enhanced review | Read-only evidence support with auditable human updates in existing KYC tools; **no third deep integration** | Acceptable independently reviewed identity accuracy; no unsupported clearance; reduced mismatch queue age/avoidable returns without overloading the three specialists or their senior deputies |
| **Months 4–6: A6 controlled production pilot** | Start with supported, good-quality financial statements, preserving originals and requiring analyst validation. Expand only as extraction, reconciliation, and exception handling prove reliable | **Deep integration 2: A6**, publishing approved normalized data with source/version/validation records | Acceptable reconciliation/error results by document type; lower total analyst effort including review; no worsening of credit-analysis queue age or decision quality; reliable manual fallback |
| **Months 7–9: strengthen integration and expand proven coverage** | Deepen A4 workflow integration and A5 status synchronization if needed; expand the same four modules by product/exposure/document type. Keep difficult cases manual until validated | Further integration starts only after month six and within confirmed IT capacity; adds capability to the same four modules | Prior gates remain satisfied through full committee and month-end cycles; downstream workload, exception aging, and legal peaks are controlled |
| **Months 10–12: stabilize and evaluate** | Tune thresholds/checklists, validate changed variants, and review persistent supplier/committee/customer constraints. Prepare the next-year business case, especially for A7 or A9 | Maintain and harden the four deployed modules | Demonstrated end-to-end benefit or documented safety gain, stable audit/control results, no hidden queue transfer, and funded human ownership of remaining work |

Alongside these phases, make eligible collateral requests earlier through the existing manual process, agree valuer deadlines/backup arrangements, and use committee-ready cutoffs with completeness checks. These measures address synchronization without adding A7 as a fifth module. Do not order unnecessary valuations merely to move a timestamp earlier.

Monitor a shared portfolio scorecard:

- **Flow:** application-to-activation median and upper-percentile cycle time; outstanding-case age; throughput, abandonment, and incomplete-case censoring; all stratified by applicant/product/exception variant.
- **Queues and joins:** wait and queue age at mismatch, review, normalization, credit analysis, packaging, legal, and signing; branch-ready times; last missing prerequisite; missed committee cutoffs; external valuation lateness.
- **Rework and customer experience:** repeated document requests, returns to review/KYC, normalization corrections, contract-remediation loops, reminder frequency, complaints, and assisted-channel use.
- **Human demand:** arrival and completion rates, backlog, handling/review time, utilization by time of day, overtime, senior-specialist concentration, analyst validation burden, independent-review availability, and legal month-end peaks.
- **Controls and model quality:** KYC/signing chronology violations, High-exposure same-person completions, approval/contract mismatches, audit completeness, false positives/negatives, overrides, unsupported-input rates, and unresolved issue aging.

Track the supplied baselines with their original denominators: 5.8% four-eyes violations in High-exposure cases, 6.5% provisional signing, 1.1% requiring remediation, and 8.5% contract-remediation loops overall versus 14% in renewals. Distinguish incident detection, attempted blocked violations, and actual completed violations.

Before each expansion, compare total workload with staffed capacity: new review arrivals multiplied by measured handling time, plus reopened work and existing demand. Include peaks, not only average utilization. Set performance/error tolerances with accountable owners before seeing pilot results. Require complete suggestion-to-decision traceability and zero permitted hard-gate bypasses; any actual bypass triggers containment and investigation. Expand only when comparisons with comparable baseline/control cohorts show improved targeted outcomes without material downstream queue, customer, or quality deterioration. No credible exact ROI can be calculated from the supplied table alone.

# 7. Final recommendation

Choose **A4 + A5 + A6 + A12**, with **A12 first**, followed by bounded A5/A4 pilots and validated A6 integration. This portfolio combines control containment, relief of a critical specialist dependency, fewer document loops, and protection of the nine-person analyst pool. It also respects the two-deep-integration limit by reserving first-half slots for A12 and A6.

The trade-off is deliberate: registration savings, automated valuation routing, contract generation, and signing automation wait. A7 ranks behind the selected modules because routing has less control over external waiting than A5/A6 have over document rework and shared analyst effort. External valuation may still dominate Equipment finance, where collateral is required in 71% of cases, and customer/committee waiting remains. Measure those residual critical paths and manage eligible valuation timing and supplier performance now; prioritize A7 next if those waits remain decisive.

Do not automate intake/registration first solely because it is most frequent. Receive application has 12,000 occurrences and is already digital for 72% of cases; A1's 11,880 registration occurrences offer attractive effort savings but address only eight minutes of service and a 1.5h median queue. Faster release can worsen much longer specialist and analyst waits, especially with visible back-office slack after 15:00. A5 offers an early, feasible win with stronger upstream-quality value, while A12 establishes safe progression. Reconsider A1 when downstream capacity and controls can absorb its faster inflow and a measured capacity or critical-path benefit justifies it.
