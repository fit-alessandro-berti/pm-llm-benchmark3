## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation
The March collapse of `raw_model_fitness` (≈0.93→0.55) alongside near-stable `mapped_model_fitness` (≈0.90–0.91) strongly implies that most of the apparent “nonconformance” in raw logs is **semantic/provenance drift**: the same business step is logged under a new label (`Eligibility Screen`) and technical duplicates (`Open Case`) create extra events that violate the old normative model. However, the gap does **not** imply the business process was unchanged: mapping can “explain away” deviations even when the meaning of an activity, its granularity, or its permissible ordering changed (e.g., screening split/merged, or performed with different rules) but still maps to the old label.

Removing duplicated technical events improves conformance and DFG clarity, but duplicates can still bias performance (extra timestamps), variant frequencies, and rework detection if not de-duplicated consistently. Missing resource identifiers primarily threatens **organizational/resource mining** (handover networks, workload, staffing impact inference) and can induce selection bias if missingness correlates with teams, migrated activities, or peak-load periods.

Post-May changes cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone because they persist **after** the label/duplicate normalization: mapped fitness drifts downward (0.91→0.86), rework edges (`Assess→Request Info`) roughly double, fast variants collapse, and cycle-time (especially p90) deteriorates while service time changes only modestly—patterns consistent with real control-flow and queueing changes driven by supplier disruption, capacity loss, priority rules, and seasonal case mix.

## 6. Segmentation plan
| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 (global) | month | broad drift detection and alerting | ≥500 cases/month (satisfied) | detects major regime shifts | overaggregation masks within-class redistribution (priority) |
| L1 (case mix) | month × {complexity: high/standard} | separate population drift from behavioral drift | ≥150 cases/cell/month | within-complexity routing and performance trends | complexity label may drift or be affected by process decisions |
| L2 (supplier) | month × complexity × {supplier: Z / non-Z} | isolate supplier-driven rework from internal changes | ≥80 cases/cell/month; else pool 2 months | supplier-specific loops, doc delays, outcomes | supplier assignment correlated with region/customer type (confounding) |
| L3 (priority regime) | month × complexity × supplier × {priority class} (post-07 only) | diagnose redistribution/fairness effects | ≥60 cases/cell/month; else collapse supplier | waiting-time gaps and tail risk by priority | priority class may be derived from actions (post-treatment) |
| L4 (org) | quarter × team/resource (only where IDs complete) × complexity | staffing/redistribution analysis with robustness to missingness | ≥200 cases/team/quarter with ≥95% resource completeness | handover networks, utilization, bottleneck teams | selection on “complete IDs” can bias if missingness is systematic |

Primary monitoring population: **standard-complexity, non–Supplier-Z cases**, because it is the largest, least directly exposed to the supplier shock, and can act as a quasi-control to separate internal logging/organizational drift from supplier-driven population changes. Parallel dashboards should still track Supplier Z and high-complexity segments for operational action.

## 7. Monitoring architecture
| monitor | statistic/artifact | reference window | alert logic | diagnostic follow-up | likely false-positive source | owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Schema/label drift | count of distinct activity labels; new-label rate; mapping coverage | rolling 2 months | any new label not in allowlist OR mapping coverage <99% | update mapping; run alignment diff raw vs mapped | planned releases | data engineering + process mining |
| Technical duplication | `duplicate_open_case_pct` and per-case duplicate counts | rolling 4 weeks | >P99 of pre-migration baseline or sudden step >2pp | validate de-dup rule; trace-level audit | batch replays/backfills | data engineering |
| Resource completeness | `missing_resource_pct` overall and by activity | rolling 8 weeks | activity-specific thresholds (e.g., migrated activities: +2pp over baseline) | isolate teams/steps; instrument fixes; MNAR check | peak-load omissions | ops analytics + IT |
| Raw vs mapped conformance gap | (mapped fitness − raw fitness) and both series | rolling 3 months + seasonal baseline | gap spike without mapped drop ⇒ semantic drift; mapped drop >0.03 ⇒ real drift | inspect new labels/DFG; alignment cost distribution | mapping change or model mismatch | process mining |
| Control-flow drift (DFG) | L1 distance on normalized relation rates; or full DFG distance (event-level) | rolling 6 months | threshold = 95th percentile of in-regime distances (per segment) | identify edges driving distance (rework/escalation) | omitted-edge artifact in fragments | process mining |
| Variant distribution | JS divergence of variant frequencies; `fast_variant_pct`, `rework_variant_pct` | same-month last year + rolling 3 months | segment-specific divergence threshold; fast/rework shifts >10pp | drill down to top variants and new variants | label normalization differences | process mining + ops |
| Rework loop intensity | `Assess→Request Info` rate; mean loops per case | rolling 8 weeks | +2× baseline within supplier/complexity segment | supplier and doc-quality escalation; SLA review | seasonal doc incompleteness | supplier manager + ops |
| Queue waiting before `Assess` | median and p90 waiting time (derived) by segment | rolling 8 weeks + seasonal baseline | p90 waiting increases >30% while service stable | capacity check; priority calibration; staffing plan | measurement error in wait derivation | workforce management |
| `Assess` service time | median/p90 service time for `Assess` | rolling 8 weeks | activity-specific control chart (not same threshold as waiting) | coaching/tooling; split by assessor | case-mix within `Assess` | ops lead |
| Case mix / escalation | `high_complexity_pct`; `Assess→Senior Assessment` rate | same-month last year + rolling 6 months | exceed seasonal band (e.g., +2σ) OR jump >10/100 | adjust staffing mix; audit semantics of “Senior” | semantic drift in label | policy owner + ops |
| Outcome stability | approve/reject rates; time-to-decision; decision-edge rate per case (censoring proxy) | rolling 6 months | outcome shift beyond segment baseline; decision-edge drop with rising WIP | audit policy/thresholds; check backlog and censoring | cohort definition changes | compliance/policy + ops |
| Supplier exposure | `supplier_z_pct` and performance conditional on supplier | rolling 3 months | Z share or Z-conditional rework > pre-set contractual thresholds | supplier incident response; reroute intake if possible | geographic seasonality | vendor management |

Seasonality: for demand and complexity monitors, compare against **same-month seasonal baselines** (or a model with month-of-year effects) rather than a single rolling average. Multiple testing: use **hierarchical alerting** (global → segment) and control the false discovery rate (e.g., Benjamini–Hochberg across monitors) instead of one universal threshold.

def baseline_relative_changes(baseline_months=("2026-01","2026-02")):
    base = MONTHLY[MONTHLY["month"].isin(baseline_months)].mean(numeric_only=True)
    cols = ["supplier_z_pct","high_complexity_pct","rework_variant_pct","fast_variant_pct",
            "median_cycle_h","p90_cycle_h","raw_model_fitness","mapped_model_fitness",
            "missing_resource_pct","duplicate_open_case_pct"]
    delta = MONTHLY.set_index("month")[cols].subtract(base[cols], axis=1)
    return base, delta

def dfg_l1_distance(ref_month="2026-01"):
    # Use only non-constant columns; omitted edges are ignored -> lower bound distance.
    cols = ["Eligibility_to_CollectDocs","CollectDocs_to_Assess","Assess_to_RequestInfo",
            "RequestInfo_to_CollectDocs","Assess_to_SeniorAssessment","Decision_rate"]
    ref = DFG_RATES.loc[ref_month, cols].astype(float)
    dist_to_ref = DFG_RATES[cols].astype(float).apply(lambda r: float(np.abs(r - ref).sum()), axis=1)
    dist_adj = DFG_RATES[cols].astype(float).diff().abs().sum(axis=1)
    return dist_to_ref, dist_adj

def fitness_recovery():
    out = MONTHLY.copy()
    out["fitness_recovery"] = out["mapped_model_fitness"] - out["raw_model_fitness"]
    return out.set_index("month")["fitness_recovery"]

def simple_change_points(series: pd.Series, k: float = 3.0):
    # Explainable rule: flag months where month-to-month absolute change exceeds k * median(abs(diff)).
    s = series.astype(float)
    diffs = s.diff().abs().dropna()
    scale = float(diffs.median())
    thr = k * scale
    if thr == 0:
        thr = 0.0
    flagged = s.index[(s.diff().abs() > thr).fillna(False)].tolist()
    return thr, flagged

import pm4py

def format_event_df(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_id: str = "case:concept:name",
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
    start_timestamp_key: str = "start_timestamp",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Return a PM4Py-formatted dataframe (does not mutate caller)."""
    out = df.copy()
    out[timestamp_key] = pd.to_datetime(out[timestamp_key], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    if start_timestamp_key in out.columns:
        out[start_timestamp_key] = pd.to_datetime(out[start_timestamp_key], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    out = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        out,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity_key,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_key,
        start_timestamp_key=start_timestamp_key,
    )
    return out

def slice_time_range(

) -> pd.DataFrame:

def discover_variants(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
) -> Dict[Tuple[str, ...], int]:
    """Return variant frequencies (DataFrame input supported)."""
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(
        df, activity_key=activity_key, timestamp_key=timestamp_key, case_id_key=case_id_key

def make_normalized_copy(
    df_raw: pd.DataFrame,
    activity_key: str = "concept:name",
    case_id_key: str = "case:concept:name",
    timestamp_key: str = "time:timestamp",
    activity_map: Optional[Dict[str, str]] = None,
    drop_duplicate_open_case: bool = True,
    open_case_label: str = "Open Case",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Create a normalized copy for conformance/DFG comparison without overwriting the original log.
    - Preserves original activity in '<activity_key>_raw'
    - Applies mapping to the activity_key in the returned copy
    - Optionally removes duplicated technical 'Open Case' events (simple heuristic)
    """
    out = df_raw.copy()
    out[f"{activity_key}_raw"] = out[activity_key]
    if activity_map:
        out[activity_key] = out[activity_key].map(lambda x: activity_map.get(x, x))

    if drop_duplicate_open_case and open_case_label in set(out[activity_key].unique()):
        # Heuristic: within each case, keep the first 'Open Case' event (sorted by timestamp).
        out = out.sort_values([case_id_key, timestamp_key]).reset_index(drop=True)
        is_open = out[activity_key].eq(open_case_label)
        first_open_rank = out[is_open].groupby(case_id_key).cumcount()
        drop_idx = out[is_open].index[first_open_rank > 0]
        out = out.drop(index=drop_idx).reset_index(drop=True)

    return out

## 9. Provisional attribution
**March (high confidence):** the abrupt drop in `raw_model_fitness` with a much smaller change in `mapped_model_fitness`, together with a jump in distinct activity labels, duplicated `Open Case`, and higher missing-resource rates, is best attributed to **logging/semantic and provenance drift caused by the system migration**. This conclusion is strong because normalization directly explains the conformance collapse while leaving the normalized control-flow largely near baseline (small DFG distance in March).

**May (medium–high confidence):** the sharp rise in Supplier Z share (18%→43%) coincides with doubled rework signatures (`Assess→Request Info` and rework variants) and worsening cycle-time. The most plausible mechanism is **supplier-driven document incompleteness** inducing extra loops and longer “Collect Documents” waiting. Attribution is not unique: Supplier Z serves a non-random population, so some of the observed drift is also **population drift** (case mix and regions), not purely behavioral.

**Mid-June to August (medium confidence):** cycle-time (especially p90) deteriorates strongly while `Assess` service time changes only modestly, aligning with the assessor departures and suggesting **queueing/performance drift from reduced capacity**. Clean separation is prevented by mediation: supplier-driven rework increases workload per case, which amplifies queueing under lower capacity.

**July (low–medium confidence):** the priority rule likely **redistributes delay** (benefiting high-complexity at the expense of standard cases) and may increase escalation usage, but aggregates cannot confirm within-class effects; targeted segmentation is required.

**October–November (high confidence for seasonal mix; medium for semantics):** the renewal season produces a large, plausibly recurring **population/case-mix shift** (high complexity +20–25pp) and increased escalation. The jump in `Senior Assessment` could be both real (more complex cases) and partly **semantic drift** (more consistent recording); disentangling requires fixed-complexity comparisons.

**December (medium confidence):** improvements plausibly reflect **overtime + document-quality interventions** plus lower seasonal load; causal certainty is limited because these co-occur.

I would revise this account if event-level segmentation shows rework increases equally for non-Z suppliers (policy change), if queue waiting does not rise after controlling for loop counts (no capacity effect), or if “Senior Assessment” frequency jumps within stable complexity bins (pure semantic shift).

## 10. Monitoring decision rules
1. `IF raw fitness drops sharply (>0.2) while mapped fitness stays within its recent band and new/renamed labels or technical duplicates rise, THEN treat as a semantic/provenance incident and update mapping + de-duplication, BECAUSE the deviation is likely logging drift rather than business-process change.`
2. `IF mapped fitness drops (>0.03) and normalized DFG rework edges (e.g., Assess→Request Info) increase concurrently, THEN open a process-change investigation and supplier/doc-quality review, BECAUSE this pattern indicates true behavioral/control-flow drift.`
3. `IF p90 cycle-time and waiting-before-Assess rise materially while Assess service time is stable, THEN trigger capacity actions (overtime/rebalancing) and queue-discipline diagnostics, BECAUSE the dominant mechanism is queueing/performance drift.`
4. `IF after 2026-07-01 the gap in waiting time between priority and non-priority classes widens beyond its configured target, THEN recalibrate the priority rule and run fairness/throughput trade-off analysis, BECAUSE priority mechanisms redistribute delay across populations.`
5. `IF high_complexity_pct or SeniorAssessment rate exceeds a seasonally matched baseline (same month-of-year) by a large margin, THEN switch to seasonal reference thresholds and pre-allocate senior capacity, BECAUSE the likely driver is recurring seasonal population drift.`
6. `IF missing_resource_pct spikes in specific migrated activities or teams, THEN freeze organizational-mining conclusions and prioritize instrumentation fixes, BECAUSE measurement missingness can masquerade as resource/organizational drift.`
7. `IF interventions reduce cycle-time/rework but approve/reject rates or decision timing shift unexpectedly, THEN audit policy thresholds and escalation semantics, BECAUSE operational changes can mediate or change decision behavior, not just speed.`
