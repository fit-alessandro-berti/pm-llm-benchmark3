## 1. System boundary and assumptions

**Inside the boundary — order-to-close, with rework and follow-up waste**

- **Production:** `Plan production / Batch wait -> CNC production (machine_group=CNC_A/CNC_B) -> Heat treatment -> Inspection -> Rework production -> CNC production`, including `energy_kwh`, `material_scrap_kg`, `batch_id/batch_size`, `rework_flag`, queue and batch-wait time. This matters most for Made-to-order, where average energy is 18.5–20.0 kWh/order versus 0.8–0.9 kWh for Standard-stock.
- **Fulfilment and packaging:** `Pick from stock / Partial shipment / Backorder wait -> Package standard / Package reinforced -> Rework packaging`, including `package_material_kg`, packaging-route incompatibility, and damage-related rework.
- **Transport:** `Ship ground / Ship air / Dedicated van / Consolidated truck -> Confirm delivery`, including `transport_kgco2e`, `route_mode`, `expedite_flag`, empty or low-utilisation van returns where observable, and the second shipment created by partial/backorder paths.
- **Service and resilience outcomes:** cycle time versus `promised_lead_time_hours`, late delivery, cancellation `Cancel due to delay -> END`, rework loops, and availability of an alternative route for Service-contract customers during carrier disruption.
- **Obsolescence follow-up:** batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days. This requires a look-ahead beyond `Close`, because the flattened order log otherwise hides unused production.

**Outside the boundary, or only partially observable**

- Upstream raw-material extraction, purchased-part manufacture, warehouse building energy, carrier fleet manufacture, customer use-phase, and end-of-life treatment beyond recorded `material_scrap_kg` and `package_material_kg`.
- Customer downtime cost and any emissions caused by a machine being down. This is especially important for Critical-downtime: the log shows high transport impact for dedicated vans, but not the avoided downtime consequence.
- Distance, load factor, vehicle fill, empty-return kilometres, and electricity-generation mix. The log gives `transport_kgco2e` and `energy_kwh` per flattened order, not the underlying factors needed for a full lifecycle assessment.
- Allocation of shared batch energy. `batch_id` allows grouping, but the prompt does not state whether `energy_kwh` is metered per item, per batch, or allocated, so per-item comparisons must be validated with `batch_size` and `machine_group`.

**Key assumptions**

- `case_type`, `customer_type`, promised lead time, part geometry/weight, and inspection certification are knowable at Availability check.
- SP1 can be assessed from promised lead time and route reliability; SP2 requires an explicit Critical-downtime justification event; Q1 requires weight and inspection-risk data for Made-to-order parts over 8 kg.
- Carrier disruption is a realistic scenario, so R1 — at least one alternative route for Service-contract customers — is a hard constraint, not a preference.
- No conversion from kWh to kgCO2e, or from scrap kg to lifecycle impact, is attempted because emission factors are not supplied.

The central lifecycle insight is segment-specific: Standard-stock impact is dominated by transport choices; Made-to-order impact is dominated by production energy, scrap, and rework; Critical-downtime impact is dominated by air/van transport, but service failure has a consequence outside the recorded boundary.

## 3. Intervention options

**I1. Green shipping recommender enforcing SP1 (S1 + S12)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Package standard/reinforced -> Ship air` for Standard-stock Service-contract and non-critical MTO where slack remains. SP1 is violated in 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases.  
- **Mechanism:** At shipping, compare promised lead time, route reliability, and segment. Recommend ground or consolidated truck when on-time probability exceeds 85%; require a coded override reason in the sustainability exception log.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Low risk if the 85% threshold is respected. Protects R1 by keeping air available as an exception rather than banning it. Prevents silent SP2-type drift.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Add `recommender_shown`, `recommender_accepted`, `override_reason`; monitor conformance of SP1, air share by segment, `transport_kgco2e/order`, late rate, and repeat air use within the same customer.

**I2. Priority pick + ground for Standard-stock Service-contract (S4)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Stock -> standard pack -> air`, currently 5% of orders, median 17h, 3% late, 18.5 kgCO2e, versus stock-ground at 42%, 34h, 8% late, 3.6 kgCO2e. Standard-stock Service-contract averages 7.8 kgCO2e/order versus 4.2 for Regular.  
- **Mechanism:** Expedite picking and packaging, not the flight. Use ground when promise slack covers the roughly 24h ground-versus-air transport-time difference plus pick time.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Should preserve most service benefit while removing unnecessary air. Risk is concentrated in short-promise orders; those remain air-eligible.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Compare variant migration from air to ground, median/p90 cycle by promised-lead-time band, `pick` duration, late rate for Service-contract Standard-stock, and SP1 violations.

**I3. Dynamic batching with segment-specific wait caps (S2)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Plan production -> Batch wait -> CNC production` and heat-treatment batching. CNC_A energy per item falls with batch size, but median batch wait rises from 4h to 38h; heat-treatment batch waiting carries 1.7x late risk.  
- **Mechanism:** Release batches at the earlier of a target size or a maximum wait cap: tighter caps for Service-contract and short-promise MTO, looser caps for Regular MTO with slack. Do not default to batch size 12–20.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Captures part of the energy saving without converting the 21% late rate on the efficient batch path into cancellations. Protects resilience by avoiding fragile dependence on forecast-perfect batches.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Batch-wait duration, `batch_size`, energy per item, late rate conditional on wait, cancellation after `Batch wait`, and 14-day obsolescence by batch.

**I4. Conditional shift from CNC_B to CNC_A (S3)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `MTO -> batch wait -> CNC_B -> inspection -> rework -> ground`, 5% of orders, median 160h, 32% late, 32.0 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, driven by CNC_B's 18% MTO rework rate versus 9% on CNC_A.  
- **Mechanism:** Route to CNC_A when its queue and due-date slack allow; keep CNC_B as overflow, not default, for suitable part families.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Quality and scrap should improve, but CNC_A queue displacement could delay other MTO work. This is a quality intervention first and an energy intervention second.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Machine-group share, CNC_A queue wait, rework rate by machine and part family, energy and scrap per good item including rework loops, and late rate for displaced CNC_A orders.

**I5. Twice-daily consolidated truck departures for non-urgent stock (S5)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Package standard -> Ship ground` for Regular Standard-stock and other orders with at least 12–24h slack. Consolidated trucks reduce transport kgCO2e by about 35–55% versus individual ground parcels but require consolidation wait.  
- **Mechanism:** Pool eligible parcels into scheduled consolidated departures; exclude Critical and short-promise Service-contract orders.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Moderate cycle-time increase, acceptable only where promise slack is explicit. Improves resilience if multiple regional lanes are retained.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Consolidated share, consolidation wait by segment, `transport_kgco2e/order`, late rate, partial-shipment substitution, and carrier-lane concentration.

**I6. Packaging–route compatibility and Q1 gate before shipment (S6)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Package standard/reinforced -> Rework packaging -> Package reinforced/standard` (620 rework-packaging entries) and Q1 violations for MTO parts over 8 kg.  
- **Mechanism:** At packaging, validate part geometry/weight, route mode, and inspection risk. Use reinforced packaging only where Q1 or inspection risk requires it; where standard packaging is certified low-risk, avoid doubling package material.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Reduces packaging rework, damage, and returns. Over-aggressive downgrading would increase returns and negate savings, so inspection certification is the control.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Packaging-rework rate, Q1 violations, `package_material_kg`, damage/return proxy, and rework-packaging loops by route and part weight.

**I7. Inspection quality gate for high-rework combinations (S7)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Inspection -> Rework production` (470) and `Inspection -> Package reinforced` (3,470), especially CNC_B MTO and parts with rushed inspection.  
- **Mechanism:** Add a targeted gate or second check for high-rework machine/part combinations; slow down inspection where queue pressure predicts missed defects; feed defect codes back to CNC setup.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** May increase inspection time for a subset, but should reduce downstream CNC rework, scrap, packaging rework, and customer returns. Risk is an inspection bottleneck if applied to all parts.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Inspection queue/duration, rework rate by machine/part, first-pass yield, downstream packaging rework, energy and scrap including rework, and late effects.

**I8. Reclassify Critical-downtime and share emergency courier capacity (S9 + S8)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Critical -> emergency production -> reinforced pack -> dedicated van`, 8% of orders, median 14h, 3% late, 52.0 kgCO2e, versus the stock/ground exception at 3%, 23h, 9% late, 4.8 kgCO2e. Critical segment overall averages 38.0 kgCO2e/order.  
- **Mechanism:** Split “true downtime” from “precautionary urgent” at order intake. Reserve dedicated vans for true downtime with SP2 justification; route precautionary urgent orders to shared emergency courier lanes or priority ground.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Protects the highest-consequence cases while removing van use where service risk is lower. Shared lanes must be capacity-managed; R1 requires a retained fallback for Service-contract customers.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** New critical subclass; SP2 justification completeness, currently missing in 31% of van cases; van share, `transport_kgco2e`, late rate by subclass, shared-lane utilisation, and fallback use during disruption.

**I9. Reduce partial shipments through availability-check rules (S11)**  
- **Target path/segment:** `Pick from stock -> Partial shipment` (1,010), `Partial shipment -> Backorder wait` (220), and the partial/backorder path at 7%, median 79h, 24% late, with a second shipment.  
- **Mechanism:** At Availability check, either reserve complete stock, promise a single consolidated ship date, or obtain explicit customer consent for partial shipment. Avoid automatic partial shipment when the remainder will trigger a second high-impact shipment.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Could increase initial wait for some orders but reduce total cycle time, customer contacts, duplicate transport, and cancellations. Must not simply starve Regular customers to protect Service-contract stock.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Partial rate, backorder duration, shipments per order, second-shipment kgCO2e, late and cancellation rates by customer type, and allocation fairness between Regular and Service-contract.

**I10. Carbon-aware promise negotiation for Regular MTO only (S10)**  
- **Target path/segment:** Regular Made-to-order, 22% of orders, median 94h, p90 180h, 18% late, 18.5 kWh, 0.90 kg scrap.  
- **Mechanism:** Offer a transparent later promise with batch/consolidation benefits for customers with genuine slack; explicitly exclude Service-contract and true Critical-downtime orders. Record negotiation as an event.  
- **Service/quality/resilience effect:** Can enable efficient batching and ground/consolidated transport, but if customers later expedite, the benefit reverses. Use sparingly and measure expedite behaviour.  
- **Process-mining evidence:** Offer acceptance, promise changes, `expedite_flag` after negotiation, late/cancel rate, batch size, and transport mode.

## 5. Rebound and displacement risks

**R1. Batching rebound — energy per item improves, service and obsolescence worsen.** Larger batches reduce CNC_A energy per item and heat-treatment energy, but batch wait rises from 4h to 38h, heat-batch waiting carries 1.7x late risk, and 6% of batch-produced items may not ship within 14 days. A policy that maximises batch size will manufacture efficiency into waiting, cancellation, and unused stock.

**R2. Transport displacement — consolidation and ground shifts create expedite demand.** Consolidated trucks save 35–55% versus individual ground parcels but add 12–24h wait; ground adds about 24h versus air. If promises are not adjusted, customers or planners will reintroduce air, dedicated vans, or `expedite_flag` behaviour, erasing the saving and worsening reliability.

**R3. Packaging trade-off — less packaging material can become more returns.** Reinforced packaging doubles package material but cuts damage-related returns by 40% for heavy MTO. Downgrading packaging to standard to save material can increase damage, packaging rework, customer returns, and repeat transport — a classic displacement from Scope of packaging to rework and field failure.

**R4. Rework displacement — fixing inspection or machine choice moves the bottleneck.** Shifting CNC_B work to CNC_A can halve rework rates, but CNC_A becomes the constraint. Adding inspection gates can reduce rework production yet create an inspection queue, delaying `Package reinforced` and increasing late delivery. The 470 inspection-to-rework events should fall without inspection duration and queue becoming the new p90 driver.

**R5. Obsolescence and forecast risk — larger or earlier production becomes waste.** Pre-producing or over-batching to improve energy per item increases the chance that demand forecasts are wrong. The supplied evidence already shows 6% of batch-produced items not shipped within 14 days under forecast error. Every obsolete item carries production energy and scrap with zero service benefit.

**R6. Customer-behaviour rebound — “green” promises trigger expedites, splits, or churn.** Regular MTO customers offered later promises may accept and then expedite, request partial shipments, cancel, or switch suppliers. Service-contract customers may interpret ground substitution as a service downgrade even when the promise is met, increasing contacts and exception pressure.

**R7. Resilience displacement — shared or consolidated capacity concentrates failure.** Replacing dedicated vans with shared courier lanes, or consolidating into fewer truck departures, lowers average emissions but can create a single point of failure during carrier disruption. R1 requires that Service-contract customers retain an alternative route; average-case optimisation must not remove disruption-case redundancy.

**R8. Partial-shipment displacement — completeness for one segment can create incompleteness for another.** Tightening availability rules to avoid partial shipments may protect Service-contract completeness while pushing Regular orders into longer backorders and cancellations. Segment-level fairness must be monitored, not only the overall partial rate.

## 6. Balanced recommendation

Adopt a **guardrailed, segment-specific package**, not a blanket low-carbon rule.

**Tier 1 — no-regret fixes first**

- Implement **I1 + I2 + I12-style exception governance**: green shipping recommender, priority pick, ground substitution for Standard-stock Service-contract where SP1 slack is sufficient, and a mandatory override log. This attacks the clearest waste: SP1 violations in 22% of Standard-stock Service-contract cases and air at 18.5 kgCO2e versus ground at 3.6 kgCO2e, without touching true Critical-downtime.
- Implement **I6 + I7**: packaging-route compatibility checks, Q1 enforcement, and targeted inspection gates for CNC_B/high-rework combinations. These reduce rework packaging and rework production loops — visible in the DFG as 620 packaging-rework entries and 470 inspection-to-rework entries — with limited service risk if applied selectively.
- Introduce the **sustainability exception log** immediately so SP1, SP2, and Q1 overrides become auditable events rather than invisible practice.

**Tier 2 — conditional process redesign**

- Implement **I3 dynamic batching** with segment-specific maximum wait caps. Use batch sizes above 10–12 only where due-date slack, forecast confidence, and obsolescence monitoring support them. This captures some CNC_A and heat-treatment energy benefit while resisting the late-delivery and obsolescence rebound.
- Implement **I4 selectively**: shift suitable MTO families from CNC_B to CNC_A only when CNC_A queue slack exists. Treat CNC_B rework reduction as a quality programme, not a machine ban.
- Implement **I5 consolidated departures** only for non-urgent Regular stock and other orders with verified 12–24h slack.
- Implement **I9 partial-shipment control** with customer consent and single-shipment preference, monitored by customer type to avoid unfair displacement to Regular customers.

**Tier 3 — structural but protected changes**

- Implement **I8 critical reclassification** before reducing van capacity. Dedicated vans remain for true downtime with SP2 justification; precautionary urgent orders move to shared emergency lanes or priority ground. This directly addresses the 31% of van cases missing justification while preserving the 3% late performance on the critical van path.
- Pilot **I10 carbon-aware promises** only for Regular MTO customers with real slack. Do not apply to Service-contract or Critical-downtime.

**Explicitly do not do:** ban air or dedicated vans across all segments; maximise batch size; remove reinforced packaging for heavy MTO; consolidate Critical orders; or use carbon-aware negotiation as a promise-stretching tool for contract customers. Those options would optimise one recorded emission line while violating SP1/SP2/Q1/R1, increasing rework, or creating unacceptable service and resilience risk.

## 7. Validation and governance plan

**Event-log enrichment before launch**

Add events and attributes for every decision that currently is invisible: `recommender_shown/accepted`, `override_reason`, `priority_pick_flag`, `batch_release_reason`, `batch_wait_cap`, `packaging_route_check_result`, `inspection_gate_result`, `critical_subclass`, `shared_lane_id`, `van_justification_id`, `promise_negotiation_offer/acceptance`, and a 14-day `shipped_after_batch` flag for obsolescence. Without these, process mining can describe outcomes but cannot govern the redesign.

**Baseline and variant monitoring**

Use the supplied baselines as the control period: Standard Regular 31h median/7% late, Standard Service-contract 18h/5%, MTO Regular 94h/18%, MTO Service-contract 78h/13%, Critical 4% late. Track variant migration in the DFG: expected movement includes shrinkage of `Stock -> standard pack -> air` (5%), shrinkage of `MTO -> CNC_B -> rework` (5%), growth of consolidated truck beyond 830 cases only in eligible segments, and reduced `Partial shipment -> Backorder wait` (220) and `Inspection -> Rework production` (470). Monitor not only median cycle time but p90, because batch, consolidation, and inspection interventions often damage the tail first.

**Conformance monitoring**

- **SP1:** percentage of ground/consolidated shipments where on-time probability was above 85%; target is to reduce the current 22% violation rate among Standard-stock Service-contract without raising their 5% late rate beyond an agreed tolerance.
- **SP2:** percentage of dedicated-van cases with explicit Critical-downtime justification; current gap is 31% missing. Require 100% justification for van use after reclassification.
- **Q1:** percentage of MTO parts over 8 kg with reinforced packaging or inspection certification; correlate violations with packaging rework, returns, and repeat transport.
- **R1:** percentage of Service-contract shipments with at least two viable routes inside the promise; test during carrier-disruption simulations and record fallback-route activation.

**Segment-level service and quality guardrails**

Review weekly by `case_type × customer_type × route_mode × machine_group`: late rate, p90 cycle, cancellation `Cancel due to delay -> END` (currently 530), rework rate, packaging-rework rate, partial-shipment rate, obsolescence rate, energy per good item, scrap per good item, and transport kgCO2e per order. Predefine stop rules: for example, pause ground substitution for a promise band if Service-contract late rate deteriorates; pause CNC_A shifting if CNC_A queue wait becomes the dominant late cause; pause consolidation for any cohort whose p90 breach rate rises despite median improvement.

**Governance of exceptions**

The sustainability exception log should be reviewed weekly by operations, quality, and service owners. Each override needs segment, reason code, promised lead time, route/machine alternative considered, and outcome. Recurring override reasons trigger process changes rather than repeated exceptions — for example, repeated air overrides in a promise band indicate the promise itself is unrealistic, and repeated van overrides indicate Critical classification is too broad.

**Validation design**

Pilot by segment and promise band rather than launching everywhere: first Standard-stock Service-contract ground substitution, then CNC_B/CNC_A routing for selected part families, then consolidated departures for Regular stock, then shared critical lanes. Compare pilot and non-pilot cohorts on the same indicators, including displacement measures — CNC_A queue, inspection queue, batch wait, expedite_flag, partial shipments, cancellations, and 14-day obsolescence. Only scale interventions that improve energy, transport, or scrap per good shipped unit while holding late, rework, and resilience guardrails within tolerance.
