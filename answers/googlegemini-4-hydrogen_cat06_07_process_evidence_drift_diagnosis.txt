## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

The system did not fail from a single cause; **four distinct regimes** overlap across Jan–Oct 2026:

* **Jan–Mar (Baseline stability):** Volume (`3,200–3,410`), maturity (`100%`), variant mix (`V1 ~45%`, `V2 ~35%`, `V3 ~20%`), and part delivery (`28–35 h`) are steady. Discrimination (`AUROC 0.81–0.82`) and calibration (`ECE 0.022–0.025`, predicted `0.18–0.19` vs observed `0.18–0.19`) are healthy.
* **Apr (Schema & data-pipeline incident, NOT real drift):** The April 1 migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. Because the pipeline missed the new label, injected duplicates, and emitted `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` (`17.0%`), prefix extraction fired at wrong or missing anchors and `Missing queue feature` hit `14%`. Model predictions inflated (`0.28` vs `0.19` observed), collapsing AUROC (`0.67`) and calibration (`ECE 0.110`), while the underlying process barely moved (`V1 0.18`, `V2 0.13`, `V3 0.34`, part delivery `34 h`, `100%` maturity). *Falsifier:* If full label normalization and deduplication on raw April logs failed to restore AUROC near `~0.79–0.81` and predicted risk near `~0.19`, real Apr drift would be implicated.
* **May (Incomplete backfill & partial recovery):** The May 6 patch restored most mappings (`Unknown 2.0%`, `AUROC 0.78`), but un-recomputed cached pre-diagnosis counts for Apr and partial duplicate-removal coverage (`post-Apr 20` only) left residual distortion (`ECE 0.050`).
* **Jun (Policy-driven process/routing shift, NOT supplier or pipeline failure):** The June 1 remote-routing policy lifted `V2` share from `34%` to `52%` and lowered `V1` (`43%  30%`) and `V3` (`22%  18%`). Because `V2` has a much lower breach rate (`0.09` vs `0.16`/`0.35`), overall breach rate fell (`0.18  0.15`). Conditional on variant, breach rates did not drift (`V1 0.16`, `V2 0.09`, `V3 0.35`) and `Remote Fix` stayed fast (`3.8–4.6 h`). However, because the model predicts *before* the post-diagnosis routing step and did not know about the new routing policy, its mean predicted risk stayed `0.20`, creating a `5 pp` calibration gap (`ECE 0.060`) while AUROC remained solid (`0.79`). *Falsifier:* If cases with identical pre-diagnosis covariates routed to `V2` in June showed higher breach rates than in March, within-variant concept drift would be present.
* **Jul (Onset of supplier-driven concept & process-performance drift):** After July 10, `Order Part  Part Received` jumped (`42 h  65 h`), pushing `V3` matured breach rate from `0.35` to `0.46` while `V1` (`0.17`) and `V2` (`0.10`) stayed flat. Overall breach rose to `0.19` while predictions stayed `0.18`, beginning the decline in AUROC (`0.76`) and Brier (`0.151`).
* **Aug–Sep (Overlapping supplier concept drift + covariate/population shift + emerging censoring):**
  * In **Aug**, supplier failures escalated (`Order Part  Part Received = 130 h`), `V3` share rose (`22%  35%`), and `V3` matured breach rate doubled (`0.64`). Observed breach jumped (`0.19  0.28`) while predicted risk stayed `0.19`, collapsing AUROC (`0.68`) and ECE (`0.120`). Partner Z was still `0%`, proving supplier drift—not Partner Z—triggered the collapse.
  * In **Sep**, Partner Z onboarded (`24%` of `5,100` cases), adding rural customers, older devices, and heavy `V3` usage (`58%` vs `36%`), alongside `148 h` part delays (`V3 breach 0.61`). Observed breach hit `0.31` (ECE `0.140`, AUROC `0.66`), compounded by *early informative censoring* (`82%` maturity, `42 d` delay, slower maturation for Partner Z). *Falsifier:* If non-Partner-Z `V3` breach rates returned to `~0.33` in Aug–Sep, supplier concept drift would be falsified.
* **Oct (Severe informative label censoring masking ongoing drift):** Case volume reached `5,250`, `V3` stayed `41%`, Partner Z rose to `27%`, and `Order Part  Part Received` worsened to `155 h`. Yet observed breach rate plunged `0.31  0.16`! This apparent "improvement" is an artifact of the October outcome API backlog (`55%` maturity, `52 d` median delay) selectively censoring long-duration `V3`/Partner Z cases—leaving fast-closing `V1/V2` non-breaches overrepresented in available labels. Treating Oct's `0.16` breach rate or `0.64` AUROC as comparable to Jan–Jun conflates censoring bias with true model performance. *Falsifier:* Recomputing Oct metrics once maturity reaches $\ge 95\%$ will fail to show a `0.16` breach rate if censoring is responsible; we predict matured Oct breach rate will exceed `0.30`.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

1. **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting**
   * *Design:* For each start cohort (Jan–Oct), plot `observed_breach_rate(t)`, `AUROC(t)`, and `ECE(t)` as functions of days since case start ($t \in \{14, 21, 30, 45, 60, 75\}$) and evaluate all months at a fixed maturity age (e.g., 45-day-old cases) or via Kaplan–Meier / IPCW (inverse probability of censoring weighting).
   * *Competing expectations:* Under **pure label censoring** (Oct), early-age breach rates (`t=30`) appear low (`~0.16`) while mature curves (`t=60+`) converge above `0.30`, and mature-cohort AUROC stabilizes or tracks Sep. Under **genuine Oct improvement** (e.g., sudden resolution of supplier issues), even `t=60+` mature Oct vintages would remain near `0.16`.

2. **Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning**
   * *Design:* Replay Jan–Jun raw event logs after mapping `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\rightarrow$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, deduplicating identical `(case_id, activity, rounded_timestamp)` events pre- and post-Apr 20, dropping synthetic `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` artifacts where mapped, and recomputing `events_before_diagnosis` and queue features from scratch. Re-score the frozen production model.
   * *Competing expectations:* Under **data-pipeline failure** (Apr/May), AUROC rebounds from `0.67` toward `~0.79–0.81`, predicted risk drops from `0.28` toward `~0.19`, and `Missing queue feature` returns to `~1–2%`. Under **real April concept/covariate drift**, clean recomputation still yields degraded AUROC (`~0.67`) and miscalibration.

3. **Calibration and discrimination stratified by variant, source, and month**
   * *Design:* Compute reliability diagrams, ECE, Brier, and AUROC stratified by `(Month, Variant)` and `(Month, Source  {Partner Z, Legacy})` on mature cohorts ($\ge 95\%$ maturity).
   * *Competing expectations:* Under **June routing/process drift**, within-variant calibration error in June (`V1`, `V2`, `V3` separately, if variant were known) is small, whereas unconditional pre-diagnosis calibration shows over-prediction (`0.20` vs `0.15`). Under **Jul–Sep supplier concept drift**, `V3` calibration collapses (`predicted ~0.32` vs `observed 0.64`) for *both* Legacy and Partner Z, while `V1`/`V2` remain well-calibrated. Under **pure Partner Z population shift**, Legacy-only metrics in Aug–Sep would remain near baseline (`AUROC ~0.80`, `ECE ~0.03`).

4. **Comparison of process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges**
   * *Design:* Extract monthly variant distributions and performance-DFG edge quantiles (`p50`, `p90`) for `Initial Diagnosis Completed  Remote Fix`, `Order Part  Part Received`, and `Dispatch Technician  Repair Completed`, both by event-calendar month and case-start month.
   * *Competing expectations:* Under **June policy drift**, `V2` variant frequency jumps (`34%  52%`) while all edge durations remain stationary. Under **supplier bottleneck**, `Order Part  Part Received` `p50`/`p90` spikes (`42 h  155 h`) starting Jul, while `Remote Fix` stays `~4 h`. Under **general operational overload**, *all* downstream edges (including `Dispatch  Repair` and `Remote Fix`) would degrade simultaneously.

5. **Conditional path-outcome invariance tests ($P(Y \mid X, \text{Variant})$)**
   * *Design:* Fit a diagnostic logistic/GBDT probe predicting `breach` from baseline pre-diagnosis features $X$ + `Variant` + `Month` interaction terms across mature Jan–Sep data (or train on Jan–Mar $(X, \text{Variant}) \to Y$ and test on Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep). Also test whether adding `Order Part  Part Received` duration explains away the Month effect in `V3`.
   * *Competing expectations:* Under **pure covariate/routing drift** (Jun), the `Month=Jun` interaction coefficients given `Variant` are near zero. Under **supplier concept drift** (Jul–Sep), `Month × V3` coefficients are strongly positive, and shrink substantially once `Order Part  Part Received` duration is conditioned on—linking concept drift directly to the unmeasured supplier delay.

6. **Population reweighting and transportability checks for Partner Z**
   * *Design:* On Sep mature/IPCW-weighted data, compute propensity weights $w(X) = P(\text{Partner Z} \mid X_{\text{observed}}) / P(\text{Legacy} \mid X_{\text{observed}})$ using baseline features only, and compare weighted Legacy breach rates to Partner Z breach rates; then repeat adding `rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, and intended `V3` routing.
   * *Competing expectations:* Under **observed covariate shift only**, reweighting Legacy cases on existing model features equalizes breach rates with Partner Z (`~0.45`). Under **unobserved covariate / population shift**, a large residual breach gap remains after reweighting on baseline features, and shrinks only when `rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, and `V3` propensity are included.

7. **Feature missingness, lineage, and censoring-mechanism audits**
   * *Design:* Audit null rates, out-of-range values, feature computation timestamps vs event timestamps, and outcome API latency stratified by variant (`V1/V2/V3`), source (`Partner Z` vs `Legacy`), and case duration.
   * *Competing expectations:* Under **Apr pipeline failure**, missingness and abnormal `events_before_diagnosis` correlate specifically with `source = new_workflow_system` and `timestamp < May 6` / `dedup < Apr 20`. Under **Oct informative censoring**, outcome API missingness correlates strongly with `variant = V3`, `source = Partner Z`, and `case_elapsed_time > 35 d`, proving Missing Not At Random (MNAR) / right-censoring bias rather than a baseline feature bug.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

Because multiple failures overlap, remediation must be staged rather than jumping straight to retraining:

1. **Stage 1 — Immediate containment (Days 1–3):**
   * Suspend automated retraining and halt raw Oct KPI reporting until maturity filters are enforced.
   * Display a **maturity & uncertainty banner** on operational dashboards; replace Oct headline breach metrics with vintage-aligned reporting (e.g., 45-day mature cohort) and IPCW estimates.
   * Flag `V3` / parts-ordered cases and `Partner Z` cases with an explicit operational risk-override / rule-based escalation (since the current model systematically under-predicts `0.21` vs `0.61–0.64` on `V3`), while ensuring downstream routing teams do not rely blindly on uncalibrated scores.

2. **Stage 2 — Feature & pipeline repair (Weeks 1–2):**
   * Permanently normalize `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` $\rightarrow$ `Initial Diagnosis Completed` upstream, enforce end-to-end deduplication across all dates (including pre-Apr 20), and recompute cached `events_before_diagnosis` and queue features for Apr–May.
   * Resolve the October outcome API backlog and record exact `outcome_received_at` timestamps so maturity can be tracked accurately.
   * Add missing predictive signals at diagnosis time: `partner_id / source`, `rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, `intended_routing_policy_flag`, and rolling **supplier lead-time / backlog indicators** (e.g., trailing 7-day `p50/p90` of `Order Part  Part Received` by part category/region).

3. **Stage 3 — Short-term recalibration (Week 2–3, interim bridge):**
   * Once pipeline features are repaired and evaluated on mature Aug–Sep data, apply **post-hoc recalibration** (e.g., temperature scaling or logistic Platt/isotonic recalibration stratified by `source` and `predicted_V3_propensity` / `parts_likely` flag) using mature Aug–early-Sep vintages.
   * *Caveat:* Recalibration fixes the global/subgroup mean bias (`0.21  0.31+`), **not** the lost ranking power (`AUROC 0.66`) caused by missing Partner Z features and supplier lead-time signals. It is strictly an interim bridge.

4. **Stage 4 — Full retraining (Weeks 3–6, gated on maturity & pipeline QA):**
   * Retrain with the repaired feature set + new Partner Z and rolling supplier-backlog features once Sep reaches $\ge 95\%$ maturity (and Oct only once its backlog clears and reaches $\ge 95\%$ maturity).
   * Apply recency weighting or time-aware validation (train on clean Jan–Jul + Aug–Sep with higher weights on post-Jul supplier regime and post-Jun routing policy, validated strictly out-of-time on the latest mature vintage).

### Training-month safety assessment (until resolution)
* **Jan–Mar:** **Safe** for baseline structure and feature relationships, though pre-June routing policy and pre-July supplier regime make them stale for marginal priors.
* **Apr:** **Unsafe** until full label backfill, pre-Apr 20 deduplication, and `events_before_diagnosis` recomputation pass QA.
* **May:** **Conditionally safe** after verifying May 1–5 pre-patch records and duplicate cleanup.
* **Jun:** **Safe for post-policy routing regime**, though pre-supplier-crisis (useful as negative/baseline control if supplier-backlog features are included).
* **Jul:** **Safe** (98% mature); captures early supplier transition.
* **Aug:** **Safe with minor censoring adjustment** (93% mature; wait ~1–2 weeks or use IPCW/vintage cutoff so the slowest 7% of `V3` breaches are not dropped).
* **Sep:** **Unsafe as unweighted raw labels** (`82%` mature, slower Partner Z maturation); **Safe only once maturity $\ge 95\%$** (or with verified IPCW on $\ge 45$-day vintage).
* **Oct:** **Strictly unsafe** (`55%` mature with severe informative censoring on long paths). Do not train or tune calibration on Oct until the API backlog clears and maturity $\ge 95\%$.

### Should we build a segment-specific or variant-specific model?
* **Critical leakage constraint on variant-specific models:** Predictions run *immediately after* `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, **before** the case follows `V1`, `V2`, or `V3`. Conditioning a single deployed model directly on the future realized variant (`V1/V2/V3`) would cause target/post-treatment leakage and fail in live inference.
* **Recommended architecture (Two-stage / mixture-of-experts + source features):** Instead of a leaky variant-conditional model, use a **two-stage routing-aware architecture** at diagnosis time:
  1. Predict intended/expected path probabilities $\hat{p}(V = v \mid X_{\text{diag}}, \text{routing\_policy}, \text{partner})$ (using only features available at diagnosis).
  2. Predict variant-specific breach risk $\hat{r}_v(X_{\text{diag}}, \text{supplier\_lag})$ trained on historical cases that took variant $v$, and combine via $\hat{y} = \sum_{v \in \{V1,V2,V3\}} \hat{p}(V=v \mid X) \cdot \hat{r}_v(X)$.
  This cleanly separates **June-style routing shifts** ($\hat{p}(V \mid X)$) from **August-style supplier execution drift** ($\hat{r}_{V3}(X)$) without leakage.
* **Partner Z segmentation:** Prefer **unified modeling with `partner/source`, `rural_travel_distance`, and `device_age` features** (plus hierarchical/regularized source calibration) rather than a completely isolated Partner Z model initially, because Partner Z has only ~1–2 months of mature data (`~1,200` Sep cases). Revisit a dedicated segment model only if residual calibration/feature interactions differ significantly after 3+ mature months.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

An auditable monitoring stack must decouple **upstream data/process/maturity diagnostics** from **retraining triggers**:

1. **Data-quality & schema monitor (real-time / daily):** Tracks `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` rate (`>1%` alert), unmapped activity names, duplicate `(case_id, activity, timestamp)` rate, feature null rates (`Missing queue feature >2%`), and prefix-anchor alignment (`Initial Diagnosis Completed` match rate).
2. **Label-maturity & censoring monitor (daily):** Tracks % of cases reaching final outcome by age cohort ($30, 45, 60$ days), median label delay, outcome API queue depth, and maturity differential across variants (`maturity(V3) - maturity(V2)`) and sources (`Partner Z vs Legacy`).
3. **Process-variant & routing monitor (weekly/monthly):** Tracks `V1/V2/V3/Unmapped` shares on started cases and early post-diagnosis transitions (to detect Jun-style policy shifts within days, before labels mature).
4. **Performance-DFG execution monitor (daily/weekly):** Tracks `p50` and `p90` elapsed time on key DFG edges (`Order Part  Part Received`, `Diagnosis  Remote Fix`, `Dispatch  Repair`) on an **event-calendar** basis so supplier bottlenecks are caught in real time without waiting 35–52 days for breach labels.
5. **Population & covariate monitor (weekly):** Tracks source mix (`Partner Z share`), volume spikes, and PSI/JS divergence on baseline and newly added covariates (`rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`).
6. **Predictive & calibration monitor (vintage-gated):** Computes AUROC, Brier, ECE, and observed-vs-predicted ratio stratified by `(Month, Source, Variant)` **only on cohorts passing maturity gates** ($\ge 95\%$ mature or IPCW-adjusted $\ge 45$-day vintage).
7. **Intervention / deployment monitor:** Logs policy changes (`remote_routing_v2`), feature flags, schema versions, and score-to-action overrides so post-prediction interventions can be distinguished from concept drift.

### Alert dependencies (preventing false retraining loops)
* **Gate 1 (Data quality):** If `DataQuality == FAIL` (e.g., Apr schema break), block calibration/concept-drift alarms and route immediately to **Data Engineering**; block training-set ingestion.
* **Gate 2 (Label maturity):** If `CohortMaturity < 95%` OR `API_Backlog_Alert == ON` (e.g., Oct), **suppress** AUROC/ECE retraining triggers and downgrade predictive alerts to `LOW_CONFIDENCE_CENSORED`.
* **Fast-path operational alerts (no labels needed):** If `DataQuality == PASS` and `Performance-DFG(Order Part  Part Received) p50 > 60 h` (Jul/Aug), trigger an **early concept-drift warning + operational supplier alert** immediately—even before labels mature.
* **Retraining authorization:** Automated retraining/recalibration is eligible **only when** `DataQuality == PASS` AND `ValidationCohortMaturity >= 95%` AND (`CalibrationDrift == TRUE` OR `ConceptDrift == TRUE` persisted across $\ge 2$ weekly vintages).

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py


def extract_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"], utc=True)

    # 1. Normalize renamed diagnosis label BEFORE feature/variant extraction
    label_map = {
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    }
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(label_map)

    # 2. Deduplicate migration-induced duplicate events (exact/1-sec duplicates per case+activity)
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp", "concept:name"])
    df["ts_sec"] = df["time:timestamp"].dt.floor("1s")
    df = df.drop_duplicates(
        subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "ts_sec"], keep="first"
    ).drop(columns=["ts_sec"])

    # Format cleanly for PM4Py
    event_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )

    # Case-start timestamps define case-start cohorts (fixed intake population)
    case_starts = (
        event_log.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"]
        .min()
        .rename("case_start_ts")
    )
    event_log = event_log.merge(case_starts, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

    months = pd.period_range(
        event_log["time:timestamp"].min().tz_localize(None).to_period("M"),
        event_log["time:timestamp"].max().tz_localize(None).to_period("M"),
        freq="M",
    )

    summaries = []
    for m in months:
        start_dt = m.to_timestamp(how="start").tz_localize("UTC")
        end_dt = (m + 1).to_timestamp(how="start").tz_localize("UTC")

        # Cohort A: Event-calendar slice (events occurring in month m -> captures live bottlenecks)
        ev_slice = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            event_log, start_dt.isoformat(), end_dt.isoformat(), mode="events"
        )
        # Cohort B: Case-start cohort (cases started in month m -> aligns with intake/model vintage)
        cs_slice = event_log[
            (event_log["case_start_ts"] >= start_dt)
            & (event_log["case_start_ts"] < end_dt)
        ].copy()

        # Performance DFG on event-calendar slice for real-time edge congestion
        part_median_h = None
        remote_median_h = None
        if not ev_slice.empty:
            _dfg, _sa, _ea = pm4py.discover_dfg(ev_slice)
            perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(ev_slice)
            part_edge = perf_dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"))
            remote_edge = perf_dfg.get(("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix"))
            if isinstance(part_edge, dict) and "median" in part_edge:
                part_median_h = round(part_edge["median"] / 3600.0, 2)
            if isinstance(remote_edge, dict) and "median" in remote_edge:
                remote_median_h = round(remote_edge["median"] / 3600.0, 2)

        # Variants & temporal features on case-start cohort (comparable across vintages)
        n_cases = cs_slice["case:concept:name"].nunique()
        unknown_share = (
            (cs_slice["concept:name"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()
            if not cs_slice.empty
            else 0.0
        )
        top_variants = {}
        median_case_dur_h = None
        if not cs_slice.empty:
            variants = pm4py.get_variants(cs_slice)
            total_v = sum(variants.values())
            top_variants = {
                "->".join(k) if isinstance(k, tuple) else str(k): round(v / total_v, 3)
                for k, v in sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda x: -x[1])[:5]
            }
            temp_feats = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(cs_slice)
            if "case_duration" in temp_feats.columns:
                median_case_dur_h = round(temp_feats["case_duration"].median() / 3600.0, 2)

        summaries.append(
            {
                "month": str(m),
                "cases_started": n_cases,
                "unknown_activity_share": round(float(unknown_share), 4),
                "ev_cal_order_to_part_median_h": part_median_h,
                "ev_cal_diag_to_remote_median_h": remote_median_h,
                "case_start_median_duration_h": median_case_dur_h,
                "case_start_top_variants": top_variants,
            }
        )

    return pd.DataFrame(summaries)
```

*(Interpretation note: **Event-calendar** cohorts measure congestion experienced across all active cases during that calendar month—ideal for catching supplier delays in real time—whereas **case-start** cohorts track fixed intake vintages, which is required for aligning variant mixes and model calibration with label maturity.)*

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature, informatively censored labels (October fallacy)**
   * *Failure mode:* Treating Oct's `0.16` observed breach rate as ground truth and retraining on Oct's `55%`-mature cohort teaches the model that `V3` (`41%` share) and `Partner Z` (`27%` share) are low-risk simply because their breaches have not yet arrived via the backlogged API. This causes severe under-prediction of high-risk repairs right when supplier delays are worst (`155 h`).
   * *Mitigation:* Enforce a hard maturity gate ($\ge 95\%$ mature or verified IPCW-adjusted fixed-age vintage) before any cohort enters training, calibration, or headline KPI evaluation.

2. **Calibrating or retraining around a data-pipeline bug (April fallacy)**
   * *Failure mode:* Interpreting April's `AUROC 0.67` and `0.28` risk spike as concept drift and fitting a recalibrator or retraining on un-backfilled Apr data bakes corrupted `events_before_diagnosis` features, duplicate events, and `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` artifacts into model weights—breaking performance once May/June clean data arrives.
   * *Mitigation:* Run schema, missingness, and activity-normalization gates *before* drift attribution; exclude or fully recompute Apr logs from raw immutable events before any modeling step.

3. **Dismissing genuine supplier-driven concept drift as mere calibration noise or transient shift (July–September)**
   * *Failure mode:* Applying only a global intercept/Platt recalibration (shifting everyone's risk from `~0.19` to `~0.30`) without addressing the `V3`-specific supplier breakdown (`0.32  0.64` breach) leaves `V1/V2` over-predicted and `V3` still heavily under-predicted, keeping discrimination broken (`AUROC 0.66–0.68`) and misallocating dispatch/expediting capacity.
   * *Mitigation:* Pair stratified monitoring by variant/edge with dynamic operational features (rolling `Order Part  Part Received` lead times) and routing-aware retraining.

4. **Attributing Partner Z's population shift to unfair/biased model behavior without stratified evidence**
   * *Failure mode:* Seeing Partner Z's `0.45` breach rate vs `0.27` legacy rate and concluding the model is unfairly discriminating—leading teams to suppress `source` or apply blind demographic/group parity thresholds—will mask legitimate risk driven by `58%` `V3` usage, rural travel distances, older devices, and slower label maturation, causing missed SLA interventions for rural customers. Conversely, blaming Partner Z for August's collapse ignores that supplier drift hit `0.64` before Partner Z onboarded.
   * *Mitigation:* Perform propensity-reweighted transportability and maturity-adjusted subgroup calibration audits; incorporate causal/operational predictors (`rural_travel_distance`, `device_age`, `V3` propensity) rather than blindly censoring group identity or ignoring subgroup miscalibration.

5. **Conflating June's policy-induced routing shift with model degradation and introducing post-diagnosis leakage**
   * *Failure mode:* Assuming June's `0.20 vs 0.15` gap requires adding the realized downstream `Variant` directly as an input feature to the post-diagnosis model creates severe target/future leakage (the model cannot know at `Initial Diagnosis Completed` whether `Order Part` or `Remote Fix` *will* happen later unless determined by an explicit triage rule).
   * *Mitigation:* Only ingest features strictly available at prediction time (e.g., routing-policy version and triage/intended-path indicators) and model downstream routing via a two-stage formulation rather than realized future events.
