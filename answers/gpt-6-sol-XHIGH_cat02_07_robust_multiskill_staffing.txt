# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

**Observed inputs.** The prompt supplies expected arrivals by block, marginal priority and case-type shares, service ranges and tail probabilities, qualification-dependent speed multipliers, calendars, and penalties. It supplies no joint priority/type distribution, arrival timestamps, historical backlog, or correlations among activity durations. The SLAs use elapsed time from arrival, including closed hours and breaks.

**Model assumptions, not observations.** For the calculations below, an ordinary or tail service time is uniform on its stated range, with independent tail indicators; priority and case type are independent within each block; and arrivals are uniform within a block. The Python experiment draws Poisson block counts. These choices permit an auditable illustration; different burstiness, correlation, or starting backlog could change every SLA estimate. U2 is read as *fixed total arrivals with doubled E and ES weights followed by normalization*. If “demand doubles” means absolute E/ES counts instead, U2 is much harsher (see Section 2). U3 removes Chen on the shock day; the experiment allows a normal no-arrival drain day afterward.

A two-branch case is parallel only when two distinct qualified resources can take it and the estimated critical-path saving over a cross-skilled sequential assignment exceeds the 8-minute coordination delay plus anticipated transfers. Sequential ME/ES work should retain the same qualified resource when possible. This is a decision, not an intrinsic property of the case. The remaining decisions are resource assignment, queue order, P1 capacity reservation, overflow, and whether and whom to preempt. A preemption is legal only for a lower-priority diagnostic case that has not already been preempted; its extra work is 10% of *remaining active service*, not 10% of the original draw.

Two useful objective formulations are:

1. **SLA-first robust objective:** lexicographically minimize across Base and U1–U5 the worst P1 miss shortfall, then worst P2 miss shortfall, then P3 shortfall and the P1 elapsed-time CVaR at the 90th percentile. A miss shortfall is `max(0, 0.90 - observed SLA fraction)`. This makes the 120-minute P1 tail matter more than mean time.
2. **Balanced operational objective:** minimize expected elapsed time plus weighted p90 elapsed time, handover/setup minutes, and the maximum scenario regret, subject to planned per-resource utilization at most 90%, at most 30 minutes overtime, and a fairness penalty for the utilization range among scheduled staff. Weights and the treatment of emergency staffing must be agreed before comparing policies. For the fixed roster, the utilization constraint is infeasible even in Base, so this objective also chooses a capacity action or an explicit demand reduction.

“Robust” here means acceptable worst-scenario SLA loss and limited overload/fairness deterioration across all six scenarios, not the smallest Base mean. A policy cannot be called SLA-compliant from expected arrivals alone.

# 3. Candidate policies

**Strict specialization with fixed queues.** Ada and Ben take Mechanical; Chen and Deepa take Electrical; Emil takes Software; Farah takes Intake and Validation. Ada may take Intake, and Ben/Deepa/Emil Validation, only as defined by their fixed front/back queue. Within each queue, order P1, then P2, then P3, FIFO within priority; no cross-queue overflow and no diagnostic preemption. Start both ME/ES branches on their fixed specialists as soon as they are available; Validation waits for both and the 8-minute coordination interval. Reuse the Intake/diagnostic owner for Validation when the fixed rules permit. This is simple and has stable expertise, but its early Software and shock-absence coverage gaps are deliberate disadvantages.

**Full qualified pool.** Any listed skill is eligible. At each free-resource event, dispatch P1 first, then the smallest projected deadline slack (`arrival + SLA − now − predicted remaining work`); among comparable slacks choose the earliest completion using speed, handover, and setup costs. Use a pair of distinct resources for a two-branch case only if the predicted parallel finish plus coordination and transfers beats a cross-skilled sequential finish by at least 12 minutes. Preempt the lowest-cost lower-priority diagnostic victim when a waiting P1 has less than 15 minutes projected slack and no idle eligible worker; charge the remaining-work penalty and enforce one preemption per case. All decisions and eligible alternatives are logged. Pooling fills gaps, but it can create transfers and consume specialist availability on low-value work.

**Hybrid with soft P1 reservation and controlled overflow.** Keep a named primary family for each worker: Ada/Ben for M, Chen/Deepa for E with Chen guarding early S, Emil for S from noon, and Farah for common work and early S relief. Admit any qualified worker as overflow when projected P1 slack falls below **35 minutes**, a P2 family queue is forecast to wait over **45 minutes**, or a family has no available primary for **15 minutes**. Re-evaluate every 15 minutes. Treat the next available compatible diagnostic slot as reserved for waiting P1; reclaim a lower-priority diagnostic slot by legal preemption at the 35-minute P1 slack trigger rather than holding a specialist idle all day. Among eligible choices, penalize a transfer/setup twice as heavily as the raw time for tie-breaking. Parallelize only with distinct resources and over **20 minutes** predicted saving; otherwise retain a cross-skilled worker sequentially. P3 ages into P2 dispatch precedence after 4 elapsed hours of queueing, subject to P1 protection. The provided code implements the reservation via earlier P1 preemption, speed-aware choices, and transfer penalties; its 45/15-minute overflow and P3 aging controls are deployment rules to calibrate in the pilot, not validated simulation outcomes.

For each policy, Intake and Validation still compete for qualified staff. A P1 “reservation” that idles a worker while the system is overloaded is counterproductive; the hybrid reserves **response capability**, not an empty chair. Every assignment should display priority, deadline slack, estimated finish by eligible resource, and the reason for an overflow or preemption.

# 5. Robust recommendation

Use **Hybrid as the operating rule with explicit relief capacity and a Full Pool emergency fallback**, provisionally. Its interpretable primary assignments preserve specialist response, while cross-skill overflow and early, bounded P1 preemption provide a controlled way around coverage gaps. The simulation gives no statistically secure claim that Hybrid beats Pool: Pool is better on Base mean and several P1 rows, and Hybrid's worst-case advantage is small relative to seed variation. If the agreed objective weights ordinary-day mean delay most heavily and transfer burden is acceptable, Full Pool is defensible as the primary policy. Strict fixed queues are not defensible for an SLA-first objective because early S and absence gaps are structural.

**Capacity is part of this recommendation.** For Base, schedule at least two *additional* full-shift equivalents as an arithmetic starting point, with one qualified E/S worker spanning the morning and one late cross-skilled Intake/Validation plus diagnostic worker. Validate skill-specific coverage and the 90% ceiling with an optimization or replay before fixing headcount; two may still be insufficient. Arrange an on-call E/S-qualified replacement for Chen and an Intake outage procedure. Under U1 the ideal 90%-use lower bound rises to three added shifts; under U5 to four, and temporal concentration may require more. If those resources cannot be secured, publish a reduced-capacity SLA forecast, protect P1 through escalation, and pace/defer P3 arrivals; routing cannot manufacture the missing minutes.

**Switching triggers.** Enter Hybrid overflow if projected P1 slack <35 minutes, family P2 wait >45 minutes, or a primary family is uncovered for 15 minutes. Switch to emergency Full Pool plus on-call relief when a two-hour forecast exceeds 90% of qualified available minutes in either E/S or Intake/Validation, when Chen is absent, or when any P1 is projected to miss 120 minutes even after legal preemption. During U5's 09:30–10:00 outage, log arrival and SLA clock immediately and queue Intake; assign non-Intake work to freed qualified staff, then clear P1 Intake first when service resumes. Return from emergency mode only after two consecutive 15-minute checks show no P1 at risk and both family queues below 30 minutes. These are proposed, auditable thresholds, not calibrated guarantees.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- **Estimate gaming:** A dispatcher could understate remaining work to win a fast resource or overstate it to trigger preemption. Freeze the initial estimate, log later revisions and reasons, compare predicted/actual time by case type and worker, and audit overrides.
- **P3 starvation:** Priority-only dispatch can leave P3 behind all day, especially with 18:00–20:00 arrivals and next-day carryover. Use the proposed four-hour queue aging and a daily P3 backlog cap, while preserving the P1 deadline rule. Measure P3 p90 and 24-hour attainment, not only daily completions.
- **Skill erosion and fairness:** Continual speed-based assignment concentrates E/S on Chen and Emil and can deprive others of practice. Rotate supervised low-risk cases, use training time as planned capacity, and review workload and overtime by person. Exclude an absent worker from same-day fairness comparisons.
- **Preemption cost:** Repeatedly interrupting P2/P3 consumes the 10% restart penalty and can make their deadlines worse. One interruption per case is a hard limit; count proposed and executed preemptions, remaining work at interruption, and the P1 minutes actually saved.
- **Parallel overhead:** ME/ES can shorten critical path but adds 8 minutes before Validation, may create two handovers, and occupies two servers. A sequential cross-skilled route can win when the second specialist is busy or during a surge. Check the actual join time, not only branch service totals.
- **Validation migration:** Faster diagnostics can simply pile cases at Validation, which all cases require. Track Validation queue separately and keep an eligible late-shift validator; reserve slack there for P1 when diagnostics are accelerated.
- **Interpretability:** Display a short reason code for each non-primary route, parallel split, preemption, and exception. A rule that operators cannot predict will invite manual workarounds; audit override frequency and outcomes.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

Record immutable case ID, arrival timestamp, priority, case type, block, and SLA deadline. For **each task or resumed segment**, record queue-entry, assignment-decision time and reason, the eligible-resource set with predicted completion by candidate, selected resource, skill used, planned and actual active service, resource multiplier, stage start/end, pause/resume and cause, remaining work at preemption, handover source/target and 7-minute charge, cross-team 5-minute charge, diagnostic setup 5-minute charge, coordination start/end, policy version, scenario/shock flag, shift/break/overtime state, and the continuously running SLA clock. Keep parallel branch IDs and a Validation join marker. Preserve rejected candidates and manual overrides so policy compliance can be checked.

Deploy a dashboard by priority, type, and arrival block: arrival-to-completion mean/p90/p95 and SLA fractions with denominators and confidence intervals; queue-entry-to-start wait for each stage; active service by skill/resource and tail quantiles; backlog age; parallel-versus-sequential critical path; preemptions and restart minutes; handover/setup minutes; first-day and rolling utilization/fairness; overtime; and Chen/Farah/Emil coverage. Alert on projected P1 misses and recurrent Validation or late-shift queues. Compare these measures with baseline by matched arrival blocks, not just calendar-day averages.

With interval events, `pm4py.format_dataframe` establishes the keys. `pm4py.get_service_time` can reveal changes in *recorded event-segment duration* by activity; compare it with explicit active-service fields because a segment may contain setup/handover or be split by a pause. `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` reveals changed resource-to-resource transitions; use the explicit transfer-charge fields for monetary/time burden because a parallel trace has ambiguous event ordering. Case variants or a Petri-net/DFG view can expose unexpected loops, missing Validation, altered ME/ES branching, or more rework; compare by policy version and shock. Recalculate activity and resource wait/sojourn by block to locate bottleneck migration from E/S into Intake or Validation. `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` reports **case-level** service, sojourn, and waiting columns in seconds, repeated on case events; it does *not* give stage-queue wait. With overlapping parallel branches, its summed service can exceed case sojourn and its derived waiting can be negative. Use interval union for wall-clock busy time and explicit queue-entry timestamps for stage waits; never interpret that negative value as a real queue.

# 8. Reproducible Python

The code is self-contained apart from `numpy`, `pandas`, and `pm4py`. It produces a **cohort simulation**, not a calibrated forecast. It samples only one arrival day and lets cases drain without next-day arrivals, so it understates repeated-day backlog; an absent Chen returns on the drain day. It treats handover/setup as assignee-occupied time, coordination as case-only wait, and the last finishing diagnostic branch as Validation's handover predecessor; exact human coordination and true parallel handover accounting are unknown. It omits priority/type correlation, service correlation, learning/fatigue, future-arrival prediction, P3 aging, the proposed 45/15-minute overflow switch, and external relief staff. These omissions should be changed before operational use. The fixed-queue policy deliberately does not preempt. P1 preemption in the other two policies is charged on remaining work and limited to once per interrupted case. Five seeds expose some Monte Carlo spread; many more days and a carried-over backlog are needed for stable SLA estimates.

```python
import math
from collections import Counter
from datetime import datetime, timedelta, timezone
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

#Input tables are transcribed from the question. Time is minutes after midnight.
SERVICE = {"Intake": (5,8,10,14,.08), "Mechanical": (18,28,35,55,.15),
           "Electrical": (22,35,40,65,.18), "Software": (25,40,45,80,.20),
           "Validation": (10,18,22,35,.12)}
SKILL = {
 "Ada": {"Intake":1,"Mechanical":1,"Electrical":1.35,"Validation":1.15},
 "Ben": {"Intake":1.10,"Mechanical":1,"Validation":1},
 "Chen":{"Intake":1.10,"Electrical":1,"Software":1.25,"Validation":1.10},
 "Deepa":{"Intake":1,"Mechanical":1.25,"Electrical":1,"Validation":1},
 "Emil":{"Intake":1.10,"Electrical":1.30,"Software":1,"Validation":1},
 "Farah":{"Intake":1,"Mechanical":1.40,"Electrical":1.40,"Software":1.40,"Validation":1.20}}
SHIFT = {"Ada":(480,960,735,765),"Ben":(480,960,720,750),
         "Chen":(480,960,750,780),"Deepa":(600,1080,840,870),
         "Emil":(720,1200,960,990),"Farah":(480,720,0,0)}
DEMAND = pd.DataFrame([
 (480,7, .10,.55,.35,.30,.25,.15,.20,.10),
 (600,9, .15,.55,.30,.25,.25,.15,.25,.10),
 (720,7, .12,.58,.30,.22,.28,.18,.20,.12),
 (840,10,.18,.52,.30,.20,.30,.18,.20,.12),
 (960,6, .10,.50,.40,.20,.25,.25,.15,.15),
 (1080,3,.05,.45,.50,.25,.20,.30,.10,.15)],
 columns=["start","arrivals","P1","P2","P3","M","E","S","ME","ES"])
SCENARIOS = ["Base","U1","U2","U3","U4","U5"]
POLICIES = ["Strict","Pool","Hybrid"]
TYPES = {"M":["Mechanical"],"E":["Electrical"],"S":["Software"],
         "ME":["Mechanical","Electrical"],"ES":["Electrical","Software"]}
FAMILY = {"Mechanical":"M","Electrical":"X","Software":"X"}
STRICT = {"Ada":{"Mechanical","Intake"},"Ben":{"Mechanical","Validation"},
          "Chen":{"Electrical"},"Deepa":{"Electrical","Validation","Intake"},
          "Emil":{"Software","Validation"},"Farah":{"Intake","Validation"}}
ORIGIN = datetime(2026,1,5,tzinfo=timezone.utc)

def sample_work(rng, activity, long_tail):
    lo,hi,tlo,thi,p = SERVICE[activity]
    if long_tail and activity in FAMILY: p = min(1,2*p)
    return float(rng.uniform(*( (tlo,thi) if rng.random()<p else (lo,hi) )))

def make_cases(scenario, rng):
    cases=[]
    for row in DEMAND.itertuples(index=False):
        n = row.arrivals * (1.7 if scenario in ("U1","U5") and row.start<720 else 1)
        ptypes=np.array([row.M,row.E,row.S,row.ME,row.ES],float)
        if scenario=="U2" and 720<=row.start<960:
            ptypes[[1,4]]*=2; ptypes/=ptypes.sum() # fixed block total interpretation
        pprio=np.array([row.P1,row.P2,row.P3],float)
        if scenario in ("U1","U5") and row.start<720:
            pprio[0]+=.08; pprio[1:]*=(1-pprio[0])/sum(pprio[1:])
        for _ in range(rng.poisson(n)):
            kind=rng.choice(list(TYPES),p=ptypes)
            prio=rng.choice([1,2,3],p=pprio)
            acts=["Intake"]+TYPES[kind]+["Validation"]
            cases.append(dict(id=str(len(cases)),arr=int(row.start+rng.integers(0,120)),
                kind=kind,priority=int(prio),work={a:sample_work(rng,a,scenario=="U4") for a in acts},
                remaining={},done={},running=set(),owner=None,last_family=None,
                diag_owner=None,parallel=None,preempts=0,coord=0,finish=None,
                handovers=0,setups=0,penalty=0,queued_at={},segment_q={},segment_meta={},attempts={},queue_wait=0,scenario=scenario))
    return cases

def regular(r,t,scenario):
    day=t//1440; clock=t%1440
    if scenario in ("U3","U5") and r=="Chen" and day==0: return False
    a,b,x,y=SHIFT[r]
    return a<=clock<b and not x<=clock<y

def work_allowed(r,t,scenario):
    if regular(r,t,scenario): return True
    clock=t%1440; _,end,x,y=SHIFT[r]
    return end<=clock<end+30 and not x<=clock<y # overtime only for work already running

def ready(c,a,t):
    if c["finish"] is not None or a in c["done"] or a in c["running"]: return False
    if a=="Intake": return t>=c["arr"]
    if "Intake" not in c["done"]: return False
    if a=="Validation":
        return all(x in c["done"] for x in TYPES[c["kind"]]) and t>=max(c["done"][x] for x in TYPES[c["kind"]])+c["coord"]
    d=TYPES[c["kind"]]
    if c["parallel"] is False and len(d)>1 and a==d[1]: return d[0] in c["done"]
    return True

def choose_parallel(c,policy,t,scenario):
    d=TYPES[c["kind"]]
    if len(d)==1: return False
    if policy=="Strict": return True
    available=[r for r in SKILL if regular(r,t,scenario)]
    if len(available)<2: return False
    pairs=[(r1,r2) for r1 in available for r2 in available
           if r1!=r2 and d[0] in SKILL[r1] and d[1] in SKILL[r2]]
    if not pairs:return False
    cross=[r for r in available if all(a in SKILL[r] for a in d)]
    sequential=min((sum(c["work"][a]*SKILL[r][a] for a in d) for r in cross),default=math.inf)
    parallel=min(max(c["work"][d[0]]*SKILL[r1][d[0]],
                     c["work"][d[1]]*SKILL[r2][d[1]])+8+7 for r1,r2 in pairs)
    return sequential-parallel > (12 if policy=="Pool" else 20)

def route_allowed(c,a,r,policy):
    if a not in SKILL[r]: return False
    if policy=="Strict" and a not in STRICT[r]: return False
    d=TYPES[c["kind"]]
    if a in d and len(d)>1 and c["parallel"] is False:
        if a==d[0] and not all(x in SKILL[r] for x in d): return False
        if a==d[1] and c["diag_owner"] is not None and r!=c["diag_owner"]: return False
    return True

def record(log,c,a,r,start,end,reason):
    if end<=start:return
    log.append({"case:concept:name":c["id"],"concept:name":a,
        "start_timestamp":ORIGIN+timedelta(minutes=start),
        "time:timestamp":ORIGIN+timedelta(minutes=end),"org:resource":r,
        "priority":c["priority"],"case_type":c["kind"],"segment_end":reason,
        "queue_entry":ORIGIN+timedelta(minutes=c["segment_q"].get(a,start)),
        "skill_used":a,"occupied_segment_minutes":end-start,
        "actual_service_minutes":max(0,(c["segment_meta"].get(a,{}).get("base_remaining_at_start",0)
             -max(0,c["remaining"].get(a,0)))*SKILL[r][a]),"scenario":c["scenario"],
        "policy_version":c["policy"],"sla_clock_start":ORIGIN+timedelta(minutes=c["arr"]),
        "sla_due":ORIGIN+timedelta(minutes=c["arr"]+{1:120,2:480,3:1440}[c["priority"]]),
        **c["segment_meta"].get(a,{})})

def simulate(scenario,policy,seed):
    rng=np.random.default_rng(seed); cases=make_cases(scenario,rng)
    for c in cases:c["policy"]=policy
    busy={r:None for r in SKILL}; last_skill={r:None for r in SKILL}; last_diag_case={r:None for r in SKILL}; log=[]
    # One arrival day, then up to two no-arrival drain days. These are cohort metrics.
    for t in range(480,480+3*1440):
        # Complete tasks and release resources at minute boundaries.
        for r,v in list(busy.items()):
            if v is None: continue
            c,a,start,over=v
            if c["remaining"][a]<=1e-9 and over<=0:
                record(log,c,a,r,start,t,"complete");busy[r]=None;c["running"].remove(a)
                c["done"][a]=t;c["owner"]=r
                if a in FAMILY:
                    c["last_family"]=FAMILY[a];c["diag_owner"]=r;last_skill[r]=FAMILY[a]
                    if all(x in c["done"] for x in TYPES[c["kind"]]):
                        c["coord"]=8 if c["parallel"] else 0
                if a=="Validation":c["finish"]=t
            elif not work_allowed(r,t,scenario) or (scenario=="U5" and t//1440==0 and 570<=t<600 and a=="Intake"):
                record(log,c,a,r,start,t,"calendar_pause");busy[r]=None;c["running"].remove(a)
                c["queued_at"][a]=t;c["owner"]=r # no restart penalty for calendar pause
        if all(c["finish"] is not None for c in cases):break
        for c in cases:
            if c["parallel"] is None and "Intake" in c["done"]:
                c["parallel"]=choose_parallel(c,policy,t,scenario)
        # P1 may interrupt one lower-priority diagnostic activity per interrupted case.
        if policy!="Strict":
            urgent=[(c,a) for c in cases if c["priority"]==1 for a in c["work"] if ready(c,a,t)]
            urgent.sort(key=lambda z:z[0]["arr"])
            for pc,pa in urgent:
                if any(busy[r] is None and regular(r,t,scenario) and route_allowed(pc,pa,r,policy) for r in SKILL):continue
                slack=pc["arr"]+120-t-sum(pc["work"][x] for x in pc["work"] if x not in pc["done"])
                if slack>(35 if policy=="Hybrid" else 15):continue
                victims=[]
                for r,v in busy.items():
                    if v is None or not route_allowed(pc,pa,r,policy):continue
                    vc,va,start,over=v
                    if va not in FAMILY or over>0 or vc["priority"]==1 or vc["preempts"]>=1:continue
                    rem=vc["remaining"][va]
                    if rem>10: victims.append((rem,r,v))
                if victims:
                    _,r,(vc,va,start,over)=min(victims,key=lambda z:z[0])
                    record(log,vc,va,r,start,t,"P1_preempt")
                    vc["remaining"][va]*=1.10;vc["penalty"]+=.10*vc["remaining"][va]/1.10
                    vc["preempts"]+=1;vc["running"].remove(va);vc["owner"]=r
                    vc["queued_at"][va]=t;busy[r]=None
        for c in cases:
            for a in c["work"]:
                if ready(c,a,t):c["queued_at"].setdefault(a,t)
        # Greedy dispatch over all free resource / ready task pairs.
        while True:
            options=[]
            for r in SKILL:
                if busy[r] is not None or not regular(r,t,scenario):continue
                for c in cases:
                    for a in c["work"]:
                        if not ready(c,a,t) or not route_allowed(c,a,r,policy):continue
                        if scenario=="U5" and t//1440==0 and 570<=t<600 and a=="Intake":continue
                        owner=c["owner"]
                        handover=7 if owner is not None and owner!=r else 0
                        if a in FAMILY and c["last_family"] is not None and c["last_family"]!=FAMILY[a] and owner!=r:handover+=5
                        setup=5 if a in FAMILY and last_skill[r] is not None and last_skill[r]!=FAMILY[a] and last_diag_case[r]!=c["id"] else 0
                        duration=c["remaining"].get(a,c["work"][a])*SKILL[r][a]+handover+setup
                        deadline=c["arr"]+{1:120,2:480,3:1440}[c["priority"]]
                        slack=deadline-t-duration
                        # P1 first; deadline slack next. Pool picks fastest, hybrid limits costly transfers.
                        score=(c["priority"],slack/({1:1,2:2,3:4}[c["priority"]]),
                               duration+(handover+setup)*(2 if policy=="Hybrid" else 0))
                        if policy=="Strict":score=(c["priority"],c["arr"],duration)
                        options.append((score,r,c,a,handover,setup))
            if not options:break
            _,r,c,a,handover,setup=min(options,key=lambda x:x[0])
            q_at=c["queued_at"].pop(a,t);c["queue_wait"]+=t-q_at;c["segment_q"][a]=q_at
            eligible=",".join(q for q in SKILL if regular(q,t,scenario) and route_allowed(c,a,q,policy))
            c["segment_meta"][a]={"eligible_resource_set":eligible,
                "planned_service_minutes":c["remaining"].get(a,c["work"][a])*SKILL[r][a],
                "planned_overhead_minutes":handover+setup,
                "base_remaining_at_start":c["remaining"].get(a,c["work"][a]),
                "assignment_decision":"priority,slack,penalized_finish",
                "resumed":c["attempts"].get(a,0)>0}
            c["attempts"][a]=c["attempts"].get(a,0)+1
            c["remaining"].setdefault(a,c["work"][a]);c["running"].add(a)
            if a in TYPES[c["kind"]] and c["diag_owner"] is None:c["diag_owner"]=r
            c["handovers"]+=handover;c["setups"]+=setup
            busy[r]=(c,a,t,handover+setup)
            if a in FAMILY:last_skill[r]=FAMILY[a];last_diag_case[r]=c["id"]
        # One minute of activity: overhead occupies the assignee, then base service.
        for r,v in list(busy.items()):
            if v is None:continue
            c,a,start,over=v
            if over>0:busy[r]=(c,a,start,over-1)
            else:c["remaining"][a]-=1/SKILL[r][a]
    completed=[c for c in cases if c["finish"] is not None]
    delays=np.array([c["finish"]-c["arr"] for c in completed])
    cap=pd.Series({r:SHIFT[r][1]-SHIFT[r][0]-(SHIFT[r][3]-SHIFT[r][2]) for r in SKILL})
    # First-day utilization is comparable; capacity is 450 or 240 min. OT may exceed 100%.
    day_end=ORIGIN+timedelta(days=1)
    day_active=pd.Series({r:sum(max(0,(min(x["time:timestamp"],day_end)-x["start_timestamp"]).total_seconds()/60)
                      for x in log if x["org:resource"]==r and x["start_timestamp"]<day_end) for r in SKILL})
    util=(day_active/cap).drop("Chen") if scenario in ("U3","U5") else day_active/cap
    def sla(p):
        sub=[c for c in cases if c["priority"]==p]
        return sum(c["finish"] is not None and c["finish"]-c["arr"]<={1:120,2:480,3:1440}[p] for c in sub)/len(sub) if sub else np.nan
    return dict(scenario=scenario,policy=policy,n=len(cases),completed=len(completed),
        mean_delay=delays.mean() if len(delays) else np.nan,p90_delay=np.quantile(delays,.9) if len(delays) else np.nan,
        P1_SLA=sla(1),P2_SLA=sla(2),P3_SLA=sla(3),max_util=util.max(),
        util_range=util.max()-util.min(),overhead=sum(c["handovers"]+c["setups"] for c in cases)/max(1,len(cases)),
        queue_wait=sum(c["queue_wait"] for c in cases)/max(1,len(cases)),
        preemptions=sum(c["preempts"] for c in cases),unfinished=len(cases)-len(completed)),pd.DataFrame(log)

if __name__=="__main__":
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected arrivals are not a timestamped arrival process, and the ranges do not identify the true service distributions, correlations, priority/type mix, existing work in progress, rework, exact handover occupation, or whether U2 raises absolute volume. Therefore the arithmetic bounds establish a capacity problem, but no numerical SLA in Section 4 is a reliable forecast, and the small modeled Hybrid-versus-Pool gaps cannot be treated as causal evidence. A one-day cohort with no next-day arrivals is especially optimistic for a process whose Base daily offered work already exceeds regular capacity. A 90% P1 estimate requires far more P1 observations than this toy experiment provides.

First, collect several weeks of interval logs with the Section 7 fields, reconcile resource calendars and rework, and fit block-specific arrival burstiness, joint type/priority shares, active-service distributions, and interruption effects. Replay historical days through all three policies with identical arrivals and random service draws; include a warm-up backlog, repeated days, both U2 readings, stochastic absence/outage timing, and uncertainty intervals. Verify the implementation against hand-calculated single-case and parallel-join examples and against recorded queue waits.

Then run a controlled, reversible pilot: start with shadow recommendations, use Full Pool and Hybrid only on selected comparable blocks or teams, and keep an operator P1 override and on-call coverage. Do not expose P1 to a known uncovered Software queue under Strict. Predeclare the primary outcome as worst-block P1 120-minute attainment (with P2/P3 and fairness guardrails), report denominators and confidence intervals, and stop or add relief when projected P1 misses, overtime, or staff utilization breach the agreed thresholds. Expand only after the pilot demonstrates adequate capacity and stable tail performance across ordinary and stressed days.
