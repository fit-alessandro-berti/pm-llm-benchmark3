## 1. Intervention definitions and estimands
| estimand ID | intervention contrast | unit and population | outcome | total/direct/indirect or interventional analogue | well-defined? | main identification obstacle |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | P1 vs P0 | all new cases arriving in 2026 | Close – Arrival (cycle-time, minutes) | Total policy effect |  | interference through shared queues |
| E2 | P1 vs P0 | same population | Triage_done – Arrival | Direct effect on triage completion |  | time-varying congestion confounding |
| E3 | (P2 vs P0) mediated by “manual_review=1” | cases whose manual-review status is manipulable under both policies | Triage_done – Close | Interventional indirect effect via occurrence of manual review |  (possible with IIE) | exposure-induced confounding: risk revealed during triage affects review decision |
| E4 | (P3 vs P0) mediated by “manual_queue_wait” | flagged cases | Manual_start – Manual_done | Interventional indirect effect via review waiting time |  | queue interference and capacity limits |
| E5 | (P4 vs P0) mediated by escalation | escalated-eligible cases | Escalation_done – Manual_done | Controlled direct effect fixing review occurrence |  | non-overlap: few unflagged escalations |
| E6 | P3 vs P0 | flagged cases | Close – Arrival | Total cycle-time effect with added protected capacity |  | strategic behaviour by reviewers |
| E7 | P4 vs P0 | all cases | Decision quality (reject/error rate) | Total effect on quality |  | measurement of “true” quality & risk confounding |

Framework notes:  
• Total effects (E1, E6, E7) use standard interventional contrasts.  
• Mediation claims (E3–E5) use interventional (in)direct effects rather than natural effects to avoid cross-world terms that require simultaneous setting of mediator paths that cannot co-occur in queues.  
• Controlled direct effect (E5) fixes manual review but lets escalation vary, isolating the escalation gate.

```
[Arrival @ t0] --arrival_load-->
  (priority_lane decision) ----> Triage_wait ----> Triage_service ---- t1
         |                                          |
         |<--risk revealed & human judgement--------|
  (manual_review?) ----------------> Manual_queue_wait --> Manual_service -- t2
         |                                                                   |
  (escalate?) ---------------------> Esc_queue_wait --> Senior_service -- t3
                                                                          |
                                                                Close ---- t_close
```
Interference arises because waiting times share a finite server pool (`U_queue_interference`). Exposure-induced confounding appears when the flag changes the number of items in the manual-review queue, which then feeds back to all cases’ waiting times. Treatment–confounder feedback exists because triage findings can update risk perception and alter the decision to send to manual review or escalate.

Observed contrasts support “acceleration at triage” (2 h saved) and “delay downstream” (4 h longer manual-queue wait and 7 h longer escalation chain). Because queues are shared, these contrasts do not by themselves prove that the flag causes the delay: (1) flagged cases are riskier and more complex; (2) the policy obliges almost all flagged cases to enter the already scarce manual and senior queues, diverting capacity from others; (3) congestion states evolve over the day, so order of arrival matters.

## 5. Identification strategy and falsification
| question | proposed design or analysis | adjustment or stratification set | assumptions | diagnostic or falsification test | result that would change your conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is triage speed gain caused by priority lane? | regression-discontinuity at risk-score threshold that triggers flag | {arrival_load, hour} | local random assignment near threshold | continuity of pre-flag covariates | no jump in triage_wait  priority not causal |
| Does queue interference explain manual delays? | queue-state-aware modelling: include #cases-in-queue at entry | {arrival_load, queue length} | accurately observed queue length | residual manual_wait still longer after adjustment | explains >80 % of delay  blame capacity, not flag |
| Total policy effect? | stepped-wedge removal of flag display (P1) | none if randomised by week | parallel trends | compare weeks before/after | no cycle-time change  effects are behavioural not informational |
| Mediated via manual occurrence? | interventional indirect effect using g-formula | {complexity, risk_score} | sequential ignorability | sensitivity to unmeasured U_risk | effect vanishes when varying U_risk  not robust |
| Negative-control outcome | time from Escalation_doneClose (should be independent) | {complexity} | same confounding | difference by flag would signal bias | difference present  hidden risk or processing |
| Sensitivity to hidden risk | Rosenbaum bounds on decision quality | {risk_score} | monotone hidden bias | bound exceeded at =1.5 | large bias tolerated  mechanism uncertain |
| Interference-aware design | cluster-based randomisation by arrival hour | {arrival_load} | partial interference only within hour | spill-over estimate | large cross-hour spill-over  capacity pooling wider |

## 6. Policy comparison
| policy | expected triage effect | expected downstream queue effect | expected cycle-time effect | quality/compliance risk | capacity spillover | evidence strength | recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 status quo | fast triage for flagged | severe manual & escalation queues | long for flagged, moderate overall | baseline | high (flagged hurt others) | medium | maintain only as baseline |
| P1 hide flag | triage slows | manual volume drops | cycle time similar or longer overall | possible miss of true risk | reduced interference | weak (counterfactual) | not advised without pilot |
| P2 keep priority, drop auto-review | triage fast | manual queue relief | shorter cycle time | oversight risk  | small spillover | moderate | pilot with audit back-check |
| P3 add protected review capacity | triage fast | queue wait drops for flagged, neutral for others | cycle time decreases for all | cost of extra staff | minimal spillover | strong (capacity models) | preferred pilot |
| P4 adjust escalation | triage fast | manual queue unchanged, senior queue relieved | moderate improvement | may miss complex fraud | spillover to ordinary analysts | weak | complementary after P3 |

Recommended pilot: P3 (priority triage + mandatory review + reserved reviewers).  
Eligibility: new flagged cases during weekday day-shift.  
Assignment: 50 % of flagged cases randomly routed to protected review pool with two dedicated reviewers; remainder follow status-quo queue.  
Guardrail: dedicated pool is capped to avoid starving unflagged cases; overflow reverts to normal queue.  
Primary outcome: ArrivalClose time.  
Secondary: manual_queue_wait; decision accuracy via post-hoc senior audit on 10 % sample; impact on unflagged waiting times.  
Data: stage start/finish times, queue lengths, reviewer IDs, subsequent reversals.  
Stopping: terminate after 4 weeks or if protected pool utilisation <50 % or audit finds >3 % error increase.

## 8. Reversal conditions
1. Hidden risk: if unmeasured fraud severity both triggers the flag and independently prolongs downstream processing, the observed delay is confounded.  
2. Queue interference: if flagged capacity is expanded and unflagged delays still rise, shared-queue mechanisms dominate and conclusions change.  
3. Decision quality: if audits show that removing mandatory review materially increases error, any cycle-time benefit is unacceptable.  
4. Timestamp semantics: if recorded “start” times are logging artefacts (e.g., open-tab) not true work start, stage durations are mis-measured.  
5. Positivity/overlap: if some complexity/risk strata never appear unflagged, mediation identification fails.  
6. Reviewer behaviour: if dedicated capacity alters reviewer diligence (e.g., rushed decisions), manual-service time and quality shift, invalidating projections.

## 9. Final synthesis
Observed: After the flag was introduced, cases with the flag reach triage about two hours sooner than similar unflagged cases and spend ~20 min less in the triage step itself. Yet their total completion time is roughly double, driven by six-hour longer waits for manual review and seven-hour queues before senior escalation. Almost every flagged file is channelled into both downstream steps, whereas only a minority of unflagged files are.

Mechanism: The flag does not embody risk; it triggers a policy bundle—priority triage plus mandatory manual review. Priority lane accelerates the first stage, but the surge of flagged work concentrates in the single manual-review queue, starving capacity. Because the same scarce reviewers and senior team serve all cases, this creates negative spill-overs (interference) that lengthen waits for everyone, especially the flagged group that must traverse every stage.

Unidentified: We cannot cleanly separate delay caused by higher latent risk or complexity from delay caused by the policy itself. Exposure-induced mediator–outcome confounding and shared queues violate standard causal mediation assumptions.

Next evidence: A randomised “protected capacity” pilot (P3) is most informative. By holding the routing policy constant while adding review bandwidth for a random half of flagged cases, it tests whether queueing—rather than intrinsic difficulty—drives downstream delay, while monitoring decision accuracy.
