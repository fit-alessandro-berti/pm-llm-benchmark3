## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The gap between `raw_model_fitness` (0.51-0.55 from March onward) and `mapped_model_fitness` (0.86-0.91) isolates the cost of label and schema misalignment, not business non-conformance. The old normative model expects `Validate Eligibility` and single `Open Case`; after 2026-03-01 about 78% of eligibility events carry the new label `Eligibility Screen` and 4-6% have a duplicated technical `Open Case`. An alignment therefore inserts a synchronous penalty for every renamed or extra event, collapsing fitness by ~0.35-0.40 even if the trace is otherwise compliant. Mapping `Eligibility ScreenValidate Eligibility` and deleting duplicate `Open Case` recovers ~0.35 fitness, leaving residual ~0.02-0.05 below January baseline.

That recovery does **not** prove the business process was unchanged. Mapping is a semantic assertion: we assume the two labels denote the same activity, executed by the same role at the same point in the model. If the screening logic actually changed during migration, mapping would hide it and artificially inflate fitness. Normalization fixes only what we explicitly declare. Similarly, `missing_resource_pct` rising 1.1%6.5% and `distinct_activity_labels` 911 affect different analyses: conformance and DFG counts if labels are raw, resource utilization / social network / workload analyses if missingness is non-random, and throughput estimates if duplicates create false self-loops. Filtering duplicates before discovery removes a spurious `Open CaseRegister` edge but does not correct bias in staffing analyses.

Post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone. The normalized DFG still shows `AssessRequest Info` doubling 1229/100, `CollectDocsAssess` dropping 9589/100, and `Decision` rate falling 8372/100, with `mapped_fitness` dropping further 0.910.86 while raw fitness is flat. Median and p90 cycle times rise 30.8h45h/+42h while `Assess` service time rises only 0.6h. Logging cannot generate a rework loop, selective document-phase thinning, or queue-dominated delay.

### 3.2 Normalized DFG-distance analysis

Using the 7 relation rates per 100 cases after mapping/de-duplication. Vector `v_m = [RE, EC, CA, ARI, RIC, ASA, A/SADec]`.

**Distance measure:** Manhattan (L1) `D(m1,m2)= |v_m1[i]-v_m2[i]|` and mean absolute deviation `MAD = D/7`. Euclidean yields same ranking. Assumption: omitted edges (e.g., `AssessDecision` split by Approve/Reject, self-loops, infrequent paths) are **excluded**; `D` is therefore a *lower bound* on true DFG distance. `RegisterEligibility` is fixed at 100 after normalization, contributing zero. Seasonal case-mix may inflate `ASA` without representing routing logic change — flagged separately.

### 3.4 Candidate change points

No formal test applied; flagged by eye + simple CUSUM/KPI thresholds (see §8).

* **Logging drift:** 2026-03-01 (strong, L1=3 after normalization vs 40 before), secondary persistent drift 2026-03 to 2026-12 in `missing_resource`/`duplicate_open_case` plateau (not a point).
* **Behavioral drift:** 2026-05-05 (±1 week) — RI-loop step-change 1224/100; 2026-10-01 — `ASA` 722/100 (seasonal routing); also 2026-07-01 candidate where `ASA` begins creeping 79 (priority/moderate behavior).
* **Performance drift:** 2026-06-15 to 2026-07-15 — median +4.3h month-over-month, p90 +9-11h, queue-dominated; 2026-10-01 — second performance level-shift +6.3h (conflated with population).

### 3.5 One vs multiple change points

Data reject a single global change point. March logging artifact is *orthogonal* to May behavioral onset (mapped fitness vs raw fitness decouple). Performance drift lags behavioral drift by ~4-6 weeks (capacity saturation hysteresis) and has a second seasonal level in October. Formally, logging, control-flow, queueing, and population exhibit different breakpoints; a global test on raw fitness would falsely place change at March, while mapped fitness, DFG L1, and cycle time imply May, June-July, and October respectively. Model with mechanism-specific change points (or regime-switching) required.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |

**Primary monitoring population:** **L1 post-migration, L2 non-Supplier-Z, standard-complexity, resource-complete traces** (or the mapped-label equivalent). Reason: this is the largest stable subgroup least affected by supplier selection bias and historic complexity mislabeling, and with consistent resource recording. It isolates *process* behavior and capacity (queueing) from population shocks. Supplier-Z and high-complexity are monitored as *secondary* populations to track case-mix and external risk, and global L0 is retained for overall SLA. Alerts on primary population indicate genuine behavioral/performance drift; divergence between primary and L0 signals population-driven drift.

If cell too small in peak months, relax to L2 standard-complexity pooled across supplier (with supplier as covariate) but keep reporting stratified rates.

## 7. Monitoring architecture

Seasonality handling: use **same-month-last-year** or 13-month seasonal decomposition reference where history available; otherwise use rolling 3-month seasonally-adjusted baseline (Sep-Nov baseline for Oct). Detrend with case-mix covariate. Multiple testing: group monitors into families (logging, behavioral, performance); control **Benjamini-Hochberg FDR 10%** within family, with Bonferroni for critical conformance alerts; require **two consecutive months** or **CUSUM confirmation** before operational action; visualize with control limits, not single p-value.

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the **March fitness collapse is a logging-semantic artifact**. Evidence is near-decisive: mapped fitness recovers from 0.55 to 0.90, normalized DFG distance March-Jan is 3/100 (essentially noise), and timing aligns day-exact with migration notes (label rename, duplicates, missing resources). Confidence **~95%** that no business process change occurred in March; the residual 0.02-0.03 dip likely reflects minor provenance handling, not behavior.

**Supplier-driven rework is the most plausible driver of the May-July behavioral shift** (confidence **~85%**). `AssessRequestInfo` doubling, rework variant 13%29%, and `CollectDocsAssess` thinning coincide within two weeks of Supplier Z’s disruption, partially reverse in September when Supplier Z recovers (RI-loop 2920, rework 2920). That temporal coupling, plus Supplier Z’s non-random customer/region footprint, makes a pure staffing or seasonal explanation unlikely for this loop. However, **mediation prevents clean attribution**: Supplier Z cases were already more complex, and rework adds arrivals to an already constrained queue, so part of the cycle-time effect attributed to supplier is *through* queueing.

**Staffing-driven queueing dominates the performance deterioration from mid-June through August** (confidence **~80%** for existence, **~60%** for magnitude). Service time rises only 0.6h while median cycle rises 13h and p90 36h, with queue waiting reconstructed as the residual. The onset lags the May rework by 4-6 weeks, peaks after the 15 June exits, and persists into August even as supplier share falls, consistent with capacity loss and backlog hysteresis. Priority rule from 1 July likely redistributes that queue — high-complexity waiting partly shielded, standard cases penalized — but aggregate months hide the split (confidence **~60%**, plausible not proven). Without stratified waiting distributions we cannot separate priority from staffing.

**October-November is primarily seasonal population drift** (confidence **~85%** that population is dominant). Case volume +24-29% vs baseline, high-complexity 20%45%, and `AssessSenior Assessment` 722/100 explain most of the second cycle-time peak and the largest DFG distance (L1 64). This is the expected annual renewal pattern; it also loads the queue, so performance worsens even though throughput logic may be unchanged. December’s improvement (**confidence low, ~50%** for intervention efficacy) confounds seasonal normalization, backlog clearance, overtime, and quality campaign — the data are observationally equivalent.

**Persistent behavioral adaptation remains plausible but unproven** (~55%): `EligibilityCollectDocs` 9895 and `Decision` 8372 remain depressed in December, suggesting hedging behavior after unreliable bundles. Revision triggers: if supplier-stratified queue mining shows standard-case waiting alone drives the July peak, priority attribution strengthens; if 2025 October shows identical SA surge without supplier/staffing issues, seasonal attribution for SA solidifies; if trace-level alignments reveal eligibility skips, March mapping would be invalidated.
