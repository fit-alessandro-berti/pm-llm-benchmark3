## 1. Materiality Framework

### Severity Dimensions

| Dimension | Definition | Weighting Guidance |
|-----------|------------|-------------------|
| **Control Objective** | Does the deviation affect authorization, completeness, accuracy, or timeliness? | Authorization failures (pre-payment approval) = highest severity |
| **Amount/Exposure** | Financial value at stake if control fails | >EUR 50,000 escalates severity regardless of raw cost |
| **Reversibility** | Can the error be corrected without customer/regulatory impact? | Irreversible payments = higher severity |
| **Timing** | Did activity occur before required checkpoint? | Pre-approval payment = severe |
| **Customer/Regulatory Harm** | Risk of financial loss, regulatory penalty, or customer dissatisfaction | High-value claims with missing notifications = elevated |
| **Recurrence** | Is this isolated or systemic across cases? | Pattern frequency increases severity |
| **Evidence Reliability** | Strength of audit trail (immutable logs vs. mutable fields) | Weak evidence linkage reduces confidence |
| **Model Scope** | Is the case within defined scope (payable vs. denied)? | Out-of-scope cases should not penalize conformance |

### Confidence vs. Severity

- **Confidence** reflects how certain we are that a deviation represents actual non-compliance (based on evidence quality, model scope, and data reliability).
- **Severity** reflects the business impact if the deviation is real.
- A case can have **high severity but low confidence** (e.g., CL-608: notification may exist in CRM but evidence is weak).
- A case can have **low severity but high confidence** (e.g., CL-601: UI telemetry is clearly non-business activity).
- **Raw alignment cost measures technical edit distance, not business consequence.**

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Alignment cost measures **technical edit distance** between observed event sequences and model paths. It does not capture:

### 1. **Policy Conditions Not Encoded in Model**
- **CL-609 & CL-610**: Both have raw_cost = 0 because the model allows Auto Approve. However, both violate P1/P2 because data conditions (amount, bank change) were not enforced. The model is technically correct but policy-incomplete.
- *Lesson*: Conformance checking must incorporate data-aware guards, not just control flow.

### 2. **Irreversibility of Control Failures**
- **CL-604**: Raw cost = 3 from two model moves (M(Fraud Screen), M(Pay Claim)). But the real risk is that EUR 90,000 was paid before approval—an irreversible financial exposure. The cost function treats all visible moves equally.
- *Lesson*: Control sequence violations should carry weighted costs reflecting reversibility.

### 3. **Scope Mismatch Inflating Costs**
- **CL-607 & CL-612**: Raw costs = 4 from multiple missing activities. But these cases may be legitimately out-of-scope (denied/withdrawn claims). Penalizing them distorts compliance metrics.
- *Lesson*: Case filtering based on scope precedes alignment computation.

### 4. **Telemetry Noise Masquerading as Deviations**
- **CL-601**: Raw cost = 3 from Open/Autosave/Close Workbench. These are UI artifacts, not business activities. Including them inflates deviation counts without reflecting control risk.
- *Lesson*: Event classification must distinguish business events from system telemetry.

### 5. **Weak Evidence Masking Real Gaps**
- **CL-608**: Raw cost = 1 from M(Notify Claimant). But the notification exists in CRM—just with weak linkage. The alignment flags a gap, but the business question is whether evidence is audit-defensible.
- *Lesson*: Evidence reliability should modulate severity, not just presence/absence.

### 6. **Hidden Moves Being Technically Fitting**
- **CL-605**: Silent move (tau) adds zero cost. This is correct—the hidden transition is part of the model structure, not a deviation. However, users might misinterpret tau as "missing activity."
- *Lesson*: Communication about silent transitions prevents misinterpretation.
