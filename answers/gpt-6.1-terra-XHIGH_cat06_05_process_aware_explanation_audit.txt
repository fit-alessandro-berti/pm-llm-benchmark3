## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Replay each prediction as of `t0`.** Record the first coverage-check event, its reliable ordering, and the prediction instant. Construct features from source versions that were both effective and available to the service by that instant. Check event time, ingestion/availability time, snapshot production time, and feature computation time; a backdated event alone is insufficient. For queues, use a backward as-of join with a documented maximum age, never a nearest join that can select a future snapshot. Treat missing history as unknown, not as proof of availability. Test boundary cases at `t0`, late arrivals, mutable fields and nightly refreshes.
2. **Trace lineage and recompute from the prefix.** Maintain a feature manifest identifying source columns, aggregation windows, joins, update rules and availability timestamps. Flag closure-populated fields, future outcomes, end-of-case roles, full-trace counts and variants, and SQL windows extending beyond `t0`. Compare online values with a historical replay; perturb events after `t0` and assert that the reconstructed `t0` features and explanation inputs do not change. Audit all features, not just the top-ranked ones.
3. **Check activity feasibility against the normative model.** Validate the observed prefix with a process alignment or equivalent state-machine/declarative constraints: registration precedes the first coverage check; evidence cycles follow request, receipt and validation with possible rework; assessment and final decision follow the permitted route; and investigation requires a trigger and may run concurrently. Check closure only after the relevant final decision or withdrawal. An alignment can identify deviations but its result depends on the model, costs, event completeness and concurrency assumptions; it is not proof of a causal sequence.
4. **Handle ties explicitly.** Use a reliable source sequence index where present and validate its provenance. For tied timestamps without one, represent the events as partially ordered, evaluate the feasible orderings, and report whether all, some, or none conform. In Z, `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is an apparent violation only if that order is reliable; the two events are still unavailable at `t0` regardless of their tie ordering. Do not manufacture an ordering from ingestion order when no trustworthy index exists.
5. **Classify references relative to the prefix.** At this `t0`, registration and coverage check are observed. A next permitted assessment on the fast track or document request on the evidence route is future-possible; investigation is future-possible only if its trigger and other conditions are satisfied. An appeal is not currently enabled because no eligible decision has occurred, although it is possible later. Closure, a final decision and other later events are not observed prefix facts. An explanation may describe a possible future route as a conditional scenario, but must not assert it occurred or attribute the current score to it. Distinguish enabled now, impossible in the current state, and possible only after further events; uncertainty about missing events must be reported.
6. **Validate decisions with event semantics.** A second coarse `Settlement Decision Recorded` is legitimate when a prior Partial or Deny is followed by an appeal and reconsideration; check decision outcomes, appeal linkage and ordering. A repeated decision without that support is a deviation or unresolved data-quality issue, not automatically a valid loop or an automatic error based solely on the label.
7. **Use observed transitions as diagnostics, not rules.** Prefix checks establish what was observed by the prediction point; footprints or directly-follows graphs (DFGs) summarize observed adjacency and help find unexpected edges. A frequent DFG edge is not automatically normative, while an infrequent edge is not automatically forbidden. Compare them with the process model and account for parallelism, missing events, loops and timestamp ties.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

These classes overlap. Passing a temporal check establishes eligibility for prediction, not causal effect, actionability or fairness.

- **Defensible predictive signal:** Frozen `initial_damage_complexity` and, once its historical availability is verified, `initial_reserve` can be associated with breach risk. `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` is also legitimate: under a registration-based deadline, more elapsed time means less time remains. The direction and magnitude of any model contribution must be estimated again in a valid model. None of these associations alone proves that changing the feature would change the outcome.
- **Process-state or congestion signal:** A properly timed `queue_length_at_t0` describes observed workload, while prefix age and the observed coverage-check state describe where the claim stands. A prefix-only request count or variant is valid, but has no evidence-cycle information in the given two-event prefixes. Congestion may motivate investigation of workload or routing, but SHAP alone does not identify a beneficial intervention or quantify its effect.
- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review:** `claimant_region`, verified `claimant_channel`, prior-claim history once repaired, and potentially reserve or assignment can encode differences in access, service, socioeconomic circumstances or other correlated attributes. They can be temporally valid predictors without being causal delay drivers or fair to use. Evaluate representation, subgroup error rates and calibration, stability across groups and time, and downstream decisions under applicable rules. Examine service mechanisms and alternatives; do not automatically remove every correlated variable or assume that removing one proxy resolves fairness.
- **Drift/calendar proxy:** `calendar_week` is available, but holiday periods and a policy change are entangled with it. Test across years, holidays, policy regimes and forward time; model explicit known operational conditions where available. Do not interpret week importance as an isolated effect of the calendar or extrapolate an old association without monitoring.
- **Unusable until lineage is repaired:** The current prior-claim SQL, same-day document snapshot, mutable `risk_score_current`, extraction-date employee experience, and the as-of status of channel and initial reserve require historical reconstruction or verification. The closure-populated final team can only be replaced by an independently evidenced `t0` assignment, not assumed from its final value. Complete-trace duration, appeal, request totals, variant, final decision, last role and future sequence patterns must be excluded at `t0`; a separately computed prefix feature is a different feature.

Predictive validity requires point-in-time availability and out-of-sample performance. Causal interpretation requires an appropriate causal design and treatment of confounding. Operational actionability additionally requires a feasible intervention and evidence of its effect. Fairness requires evaluation of impacts and trade-offs across relevant groups and decisions, not merely a check for explicit sensitive attributes.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Contain the leakage and establish an as-of feature store.** Disable the contaminated `t0` prediction and explanation outputs for operational use until validated. Inventory all training and serving features, including employee experience, and store immutable versions with effective time, availability/ingestion time, source, computation time and update history. Enforce backward as-of joins, a documented queue staleness limit and explicit handling of unknown historical values.
2. **Rebuild the historical dataset and model.** Replay every case at the first coverage check, construct prefix-only features and exclude any value without defensible availability. Repair prior claims, document counts and mutable attributes only when history supports it. Use time-separated training and evaluation, with no case or future-data overlap, and compare against the contaminated model only as a leakage diagnostic.
3. **Run automated conformance and leakage tests.** Enforce availability and complete-trace lineage checks; use post-`t0` perturbation tests, process-state and order checks, conditional fraud triggers, repeated-decision semantics and partial ordering of timestamp ties. Review exceptions and source-system defects rather than silently forcing an order. Include tests for late-arriving records, revised values and changed schemas.
4. **Reassess performance and explanations.** Measure discrimination and calibration on a held-out, forward-time set after feature removal; recalibrate on separate appropriate data if needed and recheck calibration by time and subgroup. Regenerate local and global explanations from the rebuilt model. Test stability across resamples, time periods, correlated-feature substitutions and reasonable explainer settings; explain the baseline and output scale. Do not carry forward old probabilities, ranks or SHAP values.
5. **Review proxies, fairness and actionability.** Examine region, channel, repaired history and other correlated inputs for subgroup calibration, errors and decision impacts; investigate data quality and service access, and assess applicable policy or legal constraints. Compare alternatives and document trade-offs. Evaluate proposed operational interventions separately, using an appropriate causal or experimental design where feasible.
6. **Monitor in production.** Track feature freshness and availability violations, missingness, schema and lineage changes, queue staleness, process variants and conformance, policy or holiday regime changes, predictive performance, calibration, subgroup outcomes and explanation drift. Set owners, thresholds and investigation procedures, and revalidate or retrain when meaningful changes occur.
