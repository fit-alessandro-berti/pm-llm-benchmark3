## 1. Feasibility model

The model below distinguishes precedence from resource availability. An activity cannot start merely because its nominal service time has elapsed; its prerequisites must be complete and an appropriate resource must be available.

```text
Register  Triage  Identity
Register  Triage  Document

Identity  Assessment
Document  Assessment
OriginalsReceipt  Document, if originals are required

For every actually issued Request More Info r:
    IssuingAssessment  r  NewDocumentCheck  NewAssessment  Decision
    If r is issued by Compliance Review, a new final Compliance Review
    must also follow the new Assessment and precede Decision.

If high_risk OR high_value:
    FinalAssessment  FinalComplianceReview  Decision  Notify

Otherwise:
    FinalAssessment  Decision  Notify

Identity  Document is permitted when originals are not required,
but Assessment must not start before both checks have completed.

PreliminaryCompliance  FinalComplianceReview
PreliminaryCompliance does not satisfy the final-review requirement.

FinalAssessment.person  FinalComplianceReview.person
```

```text
DECLARATIVE:
- Register is the first activity, and Triage is the second.
- A service interval must have a qualified, available resource.
- A resource cannot perform two service intervals at the same time.
- Queue waiting is not service; task ownership is not actual execution.
- M1 must sign Decision before performing Notify.
- A preliminary screen cannot replace final Compliance Review.
- A formally issued Request More Info cannot be omitted from the
  document-check/assessment successor requirements.
- A conditional approval must explicitly address known deficiencies;
  "proceed with conditions" cannot mean silently ignore them.
- No timestamp may be backdated to manufacture precedence or concurrency.
- The supplied queue values are forecasts, not guaranteed availability,
  service durations, or permission to work outside the staffed calendar.

CONDITIONAL:
- originals_required = true  OriginalsReceipt < DocumentCheck.
- high_risk OR high_value = true  a compliant final review is required
  even if the eventual decision is rejection.
- A request for additional evidence after the second assessment in K-337
  is prohibited by that case's intervention, rather than merely accelerated.
```

- **K-104** is principally a timing/readiness intervention: bring preliminary work forward and attempt to reserve final-review capacity. Any additional preliminary C1 work consumes capacity rather than creating free speed. The final-review precedence remains unchanged.
- **K-411** changes release and orchestration: existing identity and document work may execute concurrently. It does not change their routing, service totals, or the requirement that both finish before assessment.
- **K-219** changes document-resource routing from D1 to D2.
- **K-337** changes the allowed decision policy after Assessment 2. Omitting subsequent checks follows from suppressing the second request; it is not an independent time compression.

No case is reduced to one universal sequence. Optional branches—especially rework—are explicit below.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism:

The causal sequence is not simply “review takes 4.5 hours.” I1's two-case wait and D1's long queue with complex corporate documents push both prerequisite checks into late Monday and early Tuesday. A1 then discovers the ambiguity, but the factual S1 advice starts only after assessment. Consequently, C1 must first reconstruct the evidence position and subsequently waits for capacity: final review does not start until Wednesday 13:10. The early intervention moves some evidence organization ahead of that bottleneck without moving identity, document, or assessment completion.

Minimal intervention:

At Triage, S1 receives the initial ambiguity packet, and C1 is invited to perform limited preliminary intake and obtain a provisional future slot. This path assumes that no additional `Request More Info` is formally issued. If the early exchange generates such a request, the new document-check, new-assessment, and applicable final-compliance requirements must be honored; an early packet cannot waive them.

Temporal propagation:

The illustrative early activities use S1 and C1 on otherwise idle portions of their schedules, while I1 and D1 retain their factual timings. C1's final review remains after A1's Tuesday 19:10 completion. A usable reservation could permit a start near Tuesday 19:40 rather than Wednesday 13:10. With a roughly four-hour final review and the illustrative signing intervals, notification occurs around midnight. A later Tuesday slot adds delay through arithmetic, but earlier packet processing cannot compensate for a missing final-review slot. If the reservation is merely a forecast, the factual downstream schedule can reappear.

Process-feasibility check:

Register and Triage remain first. Identity and documents both finish before A1. The C1 work on Monday is explicitly preliminary; the formal final review follows the latest relevant assessment. A1 and C1 are different people, satisfying four eyes. S1's advisory interval can overlap I1/D2? It can overlap I1 and D2, and C1 preliminary work can overlap identity; the case's own S1 and C1 activities do not conflict because they use different resources. M1 signs before Notify. No early C1 interval is presented as the final review.

Best/base/worst case:

**Best:** C1's preparation is materially useful, a real post-A1 slot is honored immediately, and the review uses about 3–6 hours, producing roughly 39–42 hours of end-to-end time. **Base:** the reservation has priority but slips by one to several hours, or preparation saves less rereading, giving roughly 40–50 hours and approximately 8–20 hours of benefit. **Worst:** the packet is incomplete, C1 must reread substantially, and the provisional slot expires or yields to urgent work; the case approaches the factual 58.5 hours despite the additional preparation. Approval remains possible, but early review might also identify a need for rejection or further evidence.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:

Event logs showing that the C1 hold is not actually protected, that preliminary work consumes more effort than rereading it replaces, or that urgent C1 work must preempt the reservation would reverse the claimed speed benefit. Conversely, measured rereading reduction, a confirmed usable slot, and no displaced urgent cases would support it. Even then, the benefit would be a local benefit, not evidence that the reservation scales.

### K-219

Factual mechanism:

The decisive intervention is routing, not the identity check. Originals arrive Monday 17:30, but the D1 assignment leaves them waiting until Tuesday 09:30. D1's handwriting expertise produces a correct four-hour check in the factual case. A2 then waits until Monday evening, and M1 does not sign until Wednesday. Assigning D2 exploits its potentially shorter queue on Monday; its nominal 4–8-hour standard-document range means that an early start can finish earlier despite being slower than D1's observed four-hour performance.

Minimal intervention:

Triage assigns D2, but service cannot begin until both originals and D2 are available. The illustrative D2 start at 18:00 is only 30 minutes after receipt, not an assumption that the resource is continuously idle. Identity may continue in parallel because it does not depend on originals, but A2 must wait for both identity and the D2 result.

Temporal propagation:

In the no-rework example, D2 completes late Monday rather than Tuesday 13:30. A2 becomes eligible much earlier, yet the change is not automatically a 15-hour end-to-end saving: A2 may already face a multi-hour queue, and M1 may have other commitments. The illustrative Tuesday 11:00 A2 start yields a Tuesday decision. A clarification instead starts a causally different path: A2 must identify the issue, the external response must finish, a new D2 check and A2 assessment must follow, and only then may M1 decide. The 8–36-hour response guidance can dominate all nominal service-time differences.

Process-feasibility check:

Identity completes before assessment, and original receipt precedes D2's start. D2 is not double-booked. A2 follows D2. The no-rework path reaches M1's Decision and then Notify. If a formal request is issued, the explicit branch includes another document check and assessment; skipping directly to Decision would violate Rule 7. No compliance review is mandatory for this low-risk, low-value case.

Best/base/worst case:

**Best:** D2 has a genuine early slot, completes a standard handwritten file in 4–5 hours without clarification, and A2/M1 have usable earlier slots; approximately 0–24 hours can be saved. **Base:** the illustrated path saves about 17 hours, with unchanged approval. **Worst:** D2 is busy, the file needs clarification, or an external response crosses a weekend; one complete recheck loop can add roughly one to two days beyond the factual completion, before considering further errors or appeals. D2 might also produce a finding that ultimately causes rejection after the required reassessment.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:

A blinded comparison of D1 and D2 on comparable handwriting, together with actual queue-entry and service-start data, could show that D2 is occupied at receipt or has materially worse accuracy/clarification rates. Observed A2 and M1 queue delays could also show that the document advance never becomes a case-completion advance. Conversely, equal accuracy, low clarification frequency, and earlier downstream slots would support the expected gain.

### K-337

Factual mechanism:

The first request consumes 20.5 hours and leads to a second document check, but one inconsistency remains. Assessment 2 therefore exposes the principal delay: a 70.5-hour external request spanning the weekend, followed by another D1 check, another assessment, and a new compliance review. The intervention acts precisely at that branching point. It cannot remove the first request or the second document check and assessment, because those are already necessary consequences of the first request.

Minimal intervention:

The prohibition is applied at issuance after A2, not merely to the speed of the supplier's reply. A nominal second request followed by instant closure would recreate the forbidden loop. A2 must instead record an explicit assessment of the remaining discrepancy. The final path either supports conditional approval with concrete risk treatment or rejects. C1 must review the disposition after A2, and M1 must make the decision. A condition that secretly requires unavailable pre-decision evidence is not a workaround.

Temporal propagation:

The first request still ends Thursday 08:00, D1's second check still starts at noon, and A2 still finishes Friday 10:30. The causal difference appears next: rather than waiting through the weekend for a second request, C1 can review Friday's disposition. An illustrative Friday 10:45 start and roughly four-hour review allow a late-Friday decision. Savings arise from eliminating an entire external-wait/check/assessment chain, not from shortening C1 alone.

Process-feasibility check:

All completed checks precede their corresponding assessments. A1 is followed by Request More Info 1, D1 Check 2, and A2. Because Request More Info 2 is not issued, Document Check 3 and Assessment 3 are not prerequisites. Nevertheless, this is a **new** C1 final review after the latest assessment, A2. A1 and C1 satisfy four eyes. Even a rejection follows C1 because the case remains high-risk/high-value. The residual discrepancy is documented and dispositioned, not ignored.

Best/base/worst case:

**Best:** the residual difference is limited and independently judged compatible with enforceable conditions; C1 accepts that treatment, there is no appeal, and the example completes in about 100–102 hours. **Base:** the conditions are defensible but generate later monitoring or appeal work, while the pre-decision cycle remains approximately 35–50 hours shorter. **Worst:** the discrepancy proves material. C1 and M1 reject after the same shortened compliant path, producing little cycle-time gain relative to resolving it properly; alternatively, a poorly supported conditional approval causes loss, appeal, or recovery later. The policy may compress work without improving decision quality.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:

Supplier-contract materiality, the effect of the discrepancy on eligibility or exposure, independent compliance judgment, and evidence that the proposed conditions can be enforced would determine whether early disposition is sensible. If no effective condition is supportable, rejection—not automatic approval—is the defensible conclusion. Late corrections, appeals, or realized losses could also demonstrate that the apparent saving merely externalized risk.

### K-411

Factual mechanism:

The legacy case lock is a release constraint, not a reason that identity and document work inherently require serial execution. Under the intervention, both task records exist after Triage, but service still depends on resource availability. A plausible outcome is I2 running 11:30–14:00 and D2 running 12:00–16:30. This makes the case assessment-ready about three hours before the factual 19:30 document completion. Whether that produces a three-hour case saving depends on a usable A2 interval; otherwise the advance terminates at the assessment queue.

Minimal intervention:

Create both tasks and authorize a controlled case-scoped override with a reason, actor, and audit event. The override releases work but does not give a resource extra capacity. I2 and D2 may genuinely execute concurrently when both are available. If D2 remains queued, its queue-entry, start, and completion must be recorded separately; ownership at 09:35 is not proof of a 09:35 service start.

Temporal propagation:

Document execution overlaps identity rather than waiting until identity completion. The maximum of the two check-completion times, not their average or assignment timestamps, gates A2. In the illustrative base, D2 finishes at 16:30, a later check of the queue finds an A2 slot at 18:00, and the case can finish that evening. If A2 remains unavailable until its factual Wednesday slot, the intervention improves verification readiness but leaves final completion essentially unchanged.

Process-feasibility check:

No originals are required, so concurrent identity/document work is permitted. Register and Triage remain first. A2 begins only after both live completion events. A2 then precedes M1's Decision, and Notify follows it. The case-lock exception is an authorized orchestration change, not a fabricated timestamp. This path does not claim additional identity/document capacity; total nominal service demand remains one identity check and one document check.

Best/base/worst case:

**Best:** both services start early, finish promptly, and an assessment slot opens that evening, yielding approximately 9–15 hours of end-to-end time and up to roughly 18 hours of benefit in a favorable queue state. **Base:** the illustrated path takes 12.25 hours, about 17.25 hours faster. **Worst:** D2 is not actually free, A2 cannot start before Wednesday, or other cases are displaced; end-to-end improvement is near zero or the system effect is negative. The decision may remain approval, subject to normal check quality.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:

Validated live service events, distinct-resource overlap, resource-free/busy records, and an earlier actual A2 start would support genuine concurrency and benefit. If timestamps were entered retrospectively, D2 merely queued, or assessment congestion absorbed the entire advance, the claimed improvement would not hold. A pilot should also test whether the workaround worsens other cases' waiting times.

Each row represents **100 eligible cases under one policy during the same week**, not 100 cases simultaneously satisfying all four attribute sets. The single-case analysis holds competing work approximately fixed; the rollout does not. The snapshots provide neither arrival rates nor service-time distributions, so their changes cannot be converted into guaranteed waiting-time savings.

For **K-104**, 100 final compliance reviews imply roughly **300–600 reviewer-hours**, before preliminary work. If each early screen consumes 1–2 hours, another **100–200 C1 hours** are added. This is especially important because C1 is the only fully certified reviewer. Preliminary work might save 1–3 hours later, but it may cost at least as much, save less, or trigger new evidence work. Hard reservation of scarce compliance capacity can delay other urgent cases. A tentative calendar entry without protected capacity may produce little benefit; an aggressive hard hold can be worse than no hold.

For **K-219**, moving 100 standard checks to D2 can add **400–800 document-hours** to that pool while removing work from D1. The skill mix matters: D1 is faster on complex corporate documents, whereas D2's complex range is 8–14 hours. D1's released capacity should therefore be protected or deliberately rebalanced. Ten additional clarification loops would consume approximately **60–130 combined D2/A2 service-hours**, before external waiting. Those responses can take 8–36 hours and extend over a weekend. D1-to-D2 substitution is consequently a reallocation problem, not free capacity.

For **K-337**, avoiding the second loop avoids a weekend response and associated D1/A1 work, but cannot eliminate the first request or the required C1 review. Released D1/A1 capacity can help other cases. Any risk from a premature decision may instead appear later as appeals, corrections, or loss recovery, shifting work outside the modeled path.

For **K-411**, 100 assessments require roughly **200–500 assessment-hours** before considering other arrivals. Earlier verification can migrate the bottleneck to assessment and M1 rather than eliminate it. The policy creates one identity and one document task per case, not extra service supply. **Timestamp behavior is not true concurrency**: batch-entered starts, a persistent case lock, or busy resources can make two tasks look simultaneous when no parallel execution occurred. Controlled release rates, actual event timestamps, and downstream queue measurement are therefore essential.

## 6. Pilot designs

| pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **eligibility** | Low-risk, low-value, digital standard-document cases with no originals. Exclude cases requiring a legal exception to the case lock or involving exceptional compliance requirements. | Standard handwritten cases requiring originals. Keep complex corporate documents outside the trial so D1's specialist comparative advantage is not confounded with the routing change. Include a range of handwriting difficulties. |
| **assignment rule** | At Triage, create both tasks and use the controlled override. Compare against contemporaneous serial cases or a randomized release schedule, blocked by arrival day and period. Do not assign two operational resources to the same case. | At original receipt, randomize 1:1 to immediate D2 assignment versus usual D1 assignment. Block by arrival day, original-receipt period, and prespecified handwriting difficulty. Do not change original collection. |
| **sample/support logic** | Start with approximately **30–50 cases per release condition**, retaining enough peak/off-peak days to estimate queue effects. Power the final sample from observed variance and the minimum worthwhile verification-stage gain; include all eligible weeks rather than only easy cases. | Start with approximately **30–50 cases per arm**. Oversample difficult handwriting for quality review, while retaining the natural arrival mix for operational estimates. Extend the study if clarification/error rates are too uncertain; a small all-approval sample cannot establish non-inferiority. |
| **primary outcome** | Verification-stage elapsed time: latest identity/document completion minus Triage completion. Also report Register-to-Notify time separately. | Original-receipt-to-Decision elapsed time, alongside total Register-to-Notify time. This prevents an early documents start from being mistaken for an earlier case decision. |
| **process-path outcome** | Percentage of cases with both checks complete before A2 starts; actual identity/document overlap; invalid or retrospective timestamps; number of missing tasks or unauthorized overrides. | First-pass completion, clarification/rework rate, additional document and assessment intervals, and compliance with original-receipt and successor-task requirements. |
| **quality outcome** | Disagreement/error findings in identity or documents, A2 escalations, and sampled decision overturns. Review a blinded sample of completed files. | Blinded comparison of D2 findings with an independent reference review, plus material-error and clarification rates. Track subsequent corrections and appeals. |
| **queue-spillover outcome** | I2/D2 waiting and utilization, A2/M1 queue percentiles, and delays to non-pilot cases. Compare peak and off-peak periods, not just pilot averages. | D1 and D2 queue percentiles and skill mix, A2/external-response load, and displacement of complex-document cases previously served by D1. |
| **logging validation** | Require event-generated queue-entry, start, completion, and override records with resource and case identifiers. Reject retrospective timestamps. Distinguish task ownership from actual service. | Record original receipt, D1/D2 assignment, queue entry, actual service, clarification issue/completion, and recheck start. Audit timestamps against resource calendars and the reference-file review. |
| **stopping rule** | Pause immediately for invalid concurrency evidence or any case beginning assessment before both valid completions. Also apply a pre-registered limit for deterioration in non-pilot assessment/signing delay. | Pause an arm for a material quality error, breach of the predefined accuracy non-inferiority margin, or excessive pre-registered D2/A2 queue displacement. Do not stop merely because cycle time improves. |

## 7. Final synthesis

**K-411 appears most robust:** its low-risk, no-original case can exploit genuinely concurrent I2 and D2 work without changing the final compliance rule, adding a second substantive check, or presuming that assessment capacity is free. It offers potentially large verification-stage gains, but the pilot must measure the full case and displaced assessment work. K-219 is also testable, provided the D2–D1 comparison is stratified by handwriting difficulty and originals readiness rather than averaged away.

**K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potential speed.** Removing the second information request is feasible only because a compliant disposition replaces another loop. Forcing approval would turn uncertainty into financial and compliance exposure; rejecting merely to preserve speed could also be wrong. A conditional decision needs an explicit, supported treatment of the residual discrepancy, and a final compliance review after the last assessment.

The **K-104 counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy**. A local preliminary packet and a reserved final-review slot can coexist with unchanged earlier checks; 100 such reservations compete for the sole certified reviewer. The apparent slot availability is an endogenous consequence of the intervention, not a fixed queue forecast.

The most valuable additional data are timestamped queue-entry, assignment, start, completion, and rework events, separated by resource and case; actual versus planned reservations; document-receipt timestamps; C1 preparation and rereading time; and assessment arrivals and work during the pilot. Decision grounds, condition effectiveness, appeals, losses, and later corrections would reveal whether K-337's speed is genuine efficiency or displaced risk. These data also permit queue simulation and separate faster readiness from genuine system throughput.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **structural** — The supplied activities and dependencies are complete for these cases; suppressing K-337's second request means no Request 2, Document Check 3, or Assessment 3, rather than creating a nominal request that is immediately closed.

2. **temporal** — Illustrative service and downstream slots are feasible examples, not deterministic forecasts; working calendars, resource releases, and M1 availability can move the displayed completion times.

3. **resource** — I2 and D2 can work concurrently in K-411, while A1 and C1 remain distinct people; a C1 reservation can reserve capacity but cannot create a second certified reviewer or permit final review before assessment.

4. **behavioral** — Under K-337, A2 and C1 explicitly assess the residual discrepancy, and M1 chooses supported conditional approval or rejection; the intervention is not interpreted as automatic approval.

5. **quality** — K-104's early packet may save rereading, K-219's D2 handwriting accuracy is unknown, and K-337's remaining discrepancy may or may not be safely conditionable; none of these quality effects is assumed favorable.

6. **interference** — The single-case counterfactuals hold competing work approximately fixed, whereas the weekly rollout permits queue displacement, changed routing, priority changes, and downstream rework.

7. **resource** — D1's complex-document strength and D2's generalist role remain relevant; a blanket transfer of complex or difficult files to D2 is not assumed to be an equivalent substitution.

8. **interference** — The 100-case rollout is evaluated for each eligible policy cohort separately, with heterogeneous arrival times, external receipts, document difficulty, and possible synchronized releases rather than one uniform case repeated 100 times.
