# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin**: `Submit` (first recorded activity for each case)
**Horizon**: 72 hours (SLA target)

**Events**:
- **Resolution**: Case reaches `Resolve` (operational success)
- **Withdrawal**: Case reaches `Withdraw` (terminal, non-success)
- **Rejection**: Case reaches `Reject` (terminal, non-success)
- **Transfer**: Case reaches `Transfer` (external handoff, internal observation stops)
- **Censoring**: Case remains open at observation cutoff

**Estimands**:

1. **SLA Breach Probability (Resolution Failure)**
   - *Definition*: P(T > 72 | case is not withdrawn/rejected/transferred by 72h), where T is time to resolution
   - *Interpretation*: Probability a case is not resolved by 72h, given it hasn't been terminated by another outcome
   - *Contributions*:
     - S03 (resolved at 76h): breach (T=76 > 72)
     - S09 (open at 92h): censored at 72h (T > 72)
     - S06 (withdrawn at 27h): competing event (excluded from risk set at 27h)
     - S08 (transferred at 68h): competing event (excluded from risk set at 68h)

2. **Cumulative Incidence of Resolution After 72h**
   - *Definition*: P(T  t, event=Resolution | T > 72) for t > 72
   - *Interpretation*: Probability of resolving after 72h, accounting for competing risks
   - *Contributions*:
     - S03: contributes to cumulative incidence at 76h
     - S09: at-risk until 72h, then censored (no contribution)
     - S06: competing event (no contribution)
     - S08: competing event (no contribution)

3. **Probability of No Terminal Disposition at 72h**
   - *Definition*: P(T > 72), where T is time to any terminal event (Resolution/Withdrawal/Rejection/Transfer)
   - *Interpretation*: Probability case is still open at 72h
   - *Contributions*:
     - S03: T=76 > 72  not open at 72h
     - S09: T=92 > 72  open at 72h
     - S06: T=27 < 72  not open at 72h
     - S08: T=68 < 72  not open at 72h

**Operational Matching**:
- *SLA Breach Probability*: Best for resource planning (how many cases will need overtime handling)
- *Cumulative Incidence*: Best for understanding resolution patterns post-SLA
- *No Terminal Disposition*: Best for workload forecasting (how many cases will still be active)

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

| observed_status | event_resolution | event_withdrawal | event_rejection | event_transfer | censoring_rule | sensitivity_alternatives |
|-----------------|------------------|------------------|-----------------|----------------|----------------|--------------------------|
| resolved        | 1                | 0                | 0               | 0              | Not censored   | None                     |
| withdrawn       | 0                | 1                | 0               | 0              | Not censored   | Treat as censoring (biased) |
| rejected        | 0                | 0                | 1               | 0              | Not censored   | Treat as censoring (biased) |
| transferred     | 0                | 0                | 0               | 1              | Not censored   | **Assumption 1**: Treat as competing event (default) <br> **Assumption 2**: Treat as censoring (informative) <br> **Assumption 3**: Treat as failure (SLA breach) |
| open            | 0                | 0                | 0               | 0              | Censored at min(duration_or_age_h, 72) | None |

**Transfer Treatment Scenarios**:
1. **Competing Event**: Transfers are terminal outcomes distinct from resolution. Cases are removed from the resolution risk set at transfer time.
2. **Informative Censoring**: Transfers may indicate cases that would have breached SLA. Censoring is not independent of resolution time.
3. **SLA Failure**: Transfers are treated as SLA breaches (equivalent to resolution failure).

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

**Survivor Bias**:
- Cases S09 (open at 92h) and S11 (open at 81h) are excluded from completed-case analysis.
- These cases likely represent slower processes. Excluding them underestimates SLA breach risk.

**Informative Censoring**:
- Case S11 (open at 81h, last_event_gap_h=55) has a long inactivity gap. This may indicate a stalled process, but naive analysis treats it as non-informative.
- Case S26 (open at 104h, last_event_gap_h=72) has an inactivity gap equal to the SLA. This suggests the case may have been abandoned, but it's treated as censored.

**Competing Risks**:
- Cases S06 (withdrawn at 27h) and S08 (transferred at 68h) are removed from the resolution risk set before 72h.
- Naive analysis treats these as censored, overestimating resolution probability.

**Cohort Imbalance**:
- Cohort B has more `AutoCheck` early in prefixes (e.g., S16: `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen>Resolve`).
- Completed-case analysis may conflate policy effects with survivor bias.

**Active Open Cases**:
- Cases below 72h (e.g., S15: 35h, S31: 23h) are censored but may still breach SLA.
- Cases above 72h (e.g., S09: 92h, S26: 104h) have already breached SLA but are treated as censored.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

**Method Comparison**:

| Method                          | Strengths                                      | Weaknesses                                      | Suitability for This Task                     |
|---------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Kaplan-Meier                    | Simple, no competing risks                    | Ignores competing events, biased for SLA risk   | Poor (overestimates resolution probability)   |
| Aalen-Johansen (Cumulative Incidence) | Handles competing risks, non-parametric | Requires large sample for rare events           | **Primary** (accounts for transfers/withdrawals) |
| Cause-Specific Hazards          | Models each event type separately             | Doesn't directly estimate cumulative incidence  | Secondary (for understanding event dynamics)  |
| Subdistribution Hazards (Fine-Gray) | Directly models cumulative incidence      | Complex interpretation, sensitive to model misspecification | Secondary (for SLA breach prediction)         |
| Restricted Mean Time            | Intuitive, no proportional hazards assumption | Less interpretable for SLA breaches              | Poor (not aligned with 72h horizon)          |
| Landmarking                     | Handles time-varying covariates               | Loses early events, requires sufficient landmarks | Secondary (for process-aware features)        |
| Multi-State Model               | Models transitions between states             | Complex, requires strong assumptions            | **Primary** (for process-aware analysis)      |

**Recommendations**:
- **Primary**: Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence for resolution, treating transfers/withdrawals/rejections as competing events. This directly estimates the probability of resolution by time t, accounting for other outcomes.
- **Secondary**: Multi-state model with states: `Initial`, `Screen`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `Rework`, `Terminal`. This captures process dynamics and allows for time-varying covariates (e.g., escalation timing).

**Justification**:
- Aalen-Johansen is non-parametric and handles competing risks, which are critical here (transfers/withdrawals/rejections are not censoring).
- Multi-state models provide process-aware insights (e.g., does escalation reduce SLA breach risk?) but are more complex.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

**Scenarios**:

1. **Transfer Treatment**:
   - *Base*: Transfers as competing events.
   - *Alternative 1*: Transfers as censoring (informative).
   - *Alternative 2*: Transfers as SLA failures.

2. **Withdrawal/Rejection Treatment**:
   - *Base*: Withdrawals/rejections as competing events.
   - *Alternative*: Withdrawals/rejections as censoring (biased).

3. **Informative Censoring**:
   - *Base*: Censoring at `min(duration_or_age_h, 72)`.
   - *Alternative*: Censoring at `min(duration_or_age_h, 72 - last_event_gap_h)` (adjust for inactivity).

4. **Cohort Policy Effects**:
   - *Base*: Stratify by cohort.
   - *Alternative*: Adjust for cohort as a covariate (may not capture rollout compliance).

**Qualitative Reversals**:
- If transfers are treated as SLA failures, the estimated SLA breach probability will increase significantly (e.g., S08 and S21 would contribute to breaches).
- If withdrawals/rejections are treated as censoring, resolution probability will be overestimated (e.g., S06 and S12 would be treated as at-risk for resolution).
- Adjusting for inactivity gaps may reduce the estimated SLA breach probability (e.g., S11 and S25 would be censored earlier).

# 7. Operational interpretation

**Alerts**:
- **SLA Breach Risk**: Flag cases with >50% probability of not resolving by 72h (from cumulative incidence curves).
- **Escalation Timing**: Cases that escalate late (e.g., after 48h) may have higher breach risk. Flag for manual review.

**Workload Actions**:
- **Priority P1**: Allocate additional resources to P1 cases with high breach risk (e.g., S11, S25).
- **Rework Cases**: Cases with `rework_count > 0` (e.g., S07, S13) may need process redesign.

**SLA Governance**:
- **Calibration**: Compare predicted vs. observed SLA breaches by priority/cohort. If P1 cases have higher breach rates than predicted, investigate escalation policies.
- **Fairness**: Check if breach risk is disproportionately high for Cohort B (due to policy change) or low-priority cases.

**Caveats**:
- Do not interpret survival outputs as causal effects (e.g., "escalation reduces breach risk"). These are associations.
- Avoid presenting risk scores as absolute probabilities without confidence intervals.

# 9. Limitations and additional data

**Prioritized Missing Data**:

1. **Exact Event Timestamps**:
   - Current data only provides duration from `Submit` to last event. Missing inter-event times (e.g., time between `Screen` and `Review`).
   - *Impact*: Cannot model time-varying covariates (e.g., escalation timing) accurately.

2. **External Outcomes After Transfer**:
   - Transferred cases (e.g., S08, S21) have unknown eventual outcomes.
   - *Impact*: Cannot distinguish between transfers that would have breached SLA vs. those that would have resolved.

3. **Cutoff and Start Dates**:
   - No information on when cases started or when the observation cutoff occurred.
   - *Impact*: Cannot assess seasonality or workload effects.

4. **Time-Varying Workload**:
   - No data on concurrent case volume or resource availability.
   - *Impact*: Cannot model system-level bottlenecks (e.g., high workload may delay escalations).

5. **Assignment Mechanism**:
   - Cohort B was not randomized, and rollout compliance was incomplete.
   - *Impact*: Cannot disentangle policy effects from selection bias.

6. **Policy Rollout Compliance**:
   - No data on whether `AutoCheck` was applied as intended in Cohort B.
   - *Impact*: Cannot assess the effectiveness of the policy change.

**Additional Data Needs**:
- **Event Logs with Timestamps**: Full sequence of activities with timestamps for each case.
- **External Resolution Data**: Outcomes for transferred cases (e.g., did they resolve externally?).
- **Resource Data**: Staffing levels, case assignments, and workload metrics.
- **Compliance Data**: Which cases in Cohort B actually received the `AutoCheck` policy.

**Strongest Inference Requires**:
- A randomized controlled trial for the `AutoCheck` policy.
- Longitudinal data on transferred cases.
- Time-stamped event logs with inter-event durations.
