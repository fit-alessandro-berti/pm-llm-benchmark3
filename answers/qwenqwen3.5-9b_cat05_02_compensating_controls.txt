## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 (Nominal Four-Eyes Principle) is a structural rule designed to ensure separation of duties. However, in a complex industrial environment, the *spirit* of FEP-1 is to prevent unauthorized or erroneous transfers by ensuring independent verification. Decomposing FEP-1 reveals five distinct control objectives that compensating controls must address:

1.  **Single-Actor Fraud Prevention:** Preventing one individual from initiating a transfer and authorizing the release without independent verification.
    *   *Prevented by:* ED-1 (Emergency Exception), MG-4 (Manager Approval), AV-2 (Automated Validation).
    *   *Fails:* When same-user logic applies without valid ED-1 conditions.
2.  **Data Integrity and Accuracy:** Ensuring the vendor, bank account, and amount are valid and match supporting documentation.
    *   *Prevented by:* AV-2 (Duplicate check, sanctions, bank account change check, PO match).
    *   *Fails:* When AV-2 is bypassed, unavailable, or overridden without justification.
3.  **Commercial Reasonableness:** Ensuring the payment corresponds to actual goods/services received.
    *   *Prevented by:* MG-4 (Review of receipt/evidence), AV-2 (Basic PO match).
    *   *Fails:* When MG-4 is a "rubber stamp" or when AV-2 does not validate receipt status.
4.  **Conflict of Interest and Independence:** Ensuring the approver has no personal or performance-related conflict with the preparer.
    *   *Prevented by:* MG-4 (Independence check, L3+ level).
    *   *Fails:* When the manager is the direct supervisor (performance rating influence) or related to the vendor.
5.  **Recoverability and Loss Mitigation:** Ensuring that if a transfer occurs erroneously, it can be recalled or audited before funds are irretrievable.
    *   *Prevented by:* RA-7 (Audit within recall window), AV-2 (Prevention of release).
    *   *Fails:* When release occurs before settlement, audit is delayed beyond 24 hours, or recall is legally impossible.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating control package is not automatically equivalent to FEP-1. We define sufficiency through a multi-dimensional test:

*   **Timing:** Controls must occur *before* irrevocable bank settlement. MG-4 after settlement is detective/corrective, not preventive.
*   **Independence:** The compensating actor must not have a reporting line or performance relationship with the preparer (e.g., u23/m09 fails here).
*   **Coverage:** Controls must cover the specific risk vector (e.g., AV-2 does not cover commercial reasonableness; MG-4 does not cover bank account theft if not reviewed).
*   **Overrideability:** Automated checks (AV-2) must not be bypassable without compensating evidence (e.g., signed override log).
*   **Recoverability:** The window for corrective action (recall) must be preserved. Audit after settlement is less effective than audit before settlement.
*   **Evidence Quality:** A "pass" must be supported by data (e.g., bank statement match), not just a system flag.
*   **Collusion Resistance:** The test must account for the possibility that the "second eye" is compromised by the "first eye" (e.g., shared workstation, shared credential).

**Why "Same Expected Loss" is not the only standard:**
While expected loss is a financial metric, control objectives often encompass compliance, integrity, and risk of reputational damage. A low-loss payment (AP-201) might still violate FEP-1 policy which requires structural independence to prevent escalation to high-value fraud. Conversely, a high-value payment (AP-208) might be legitimate but carries high risk due to control gaps (AV-2 unavailable). Therefore, the standard must weigh the *probability* of loss against the *severity* of the control gap, not just the financial outcome.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

The classification of controls depends on the point of intervention relative to the "irrevocable" threshold (Bank Settlement).

*   **AV-2 (Automated Validation):** **Preventive.** It occurs before or during Prepare_Payment/Release_Payment. If it blocks, the payment never releases. It prevents errors before they become commitments.
*   **MG-4 (Manager Approval):** **Hybrid.**
    *   If performed *before* Release_Payment: **Preventive.** It stops the transfer from being initiated.
    *   If performed *after* Release_Payment but *before* Bank_Settlement: **Hybrid (Detective/Preventive).** It detects the release action but prevents the *settlement* (the point of no return). In cases where settlement is imminent, this is effectively preventive regarding the final loss.
    *   If performed *after* Bank_Settlement: **Detective/Corrective.** It detects an error post-facto and attempts corrective action (recall), but the transfer is legally "done."
*   **RA-7 (Audit):** **Detective.** It occurs after the fact to identify anomalies. It becomes **Corrective** only if the audit leads to a recall or recovery within the bank's window (typically 24 hours).
*   **ED-1 (Emergency Permission):** **Preventive Exception.** It allows a deviation from FEP-1 under strict conditions to prevent operational paralysis (e.g., production outage).

**Semantics for Manager Action:**
When a Manager ratifies after `Release_Payment` but before `Bank_Settlement`, it is a **Hybrid** control. It detects the release has occurred but prevents the *settlement* (the financial loss). In AP-208, the manager ratified *after* settlement, converting the control from preventive to purely detective, which explains the high residual risk.
