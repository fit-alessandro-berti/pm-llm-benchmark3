# 1. Delay-decomposition definitions  
Let  

•   Cases, i  {A,B,C}.  
• t(c, x)  be the timestamp of event *x* for case .  
• (·) return minutes.  
Then

1. Up-stream elapsed  
upstream = [ t(c,fork_release) – t(c,arrival) ]

2. Branch queuing and service  
queue,i   = [ t(c,i_start) – t(c,fork_release) ]  
service,i = [ t(c,i_end)   – t(c,i_start) ]

3. Join point  
join_ready = max{ t(c,i_end) }  

4. Branch synchronisation waiting  
sync_wait,i = [ join_ready – t(c,i_end) ]  
The *elapsed* fork-join stage is  
fork_join_elapsed = [ join_ready – t(c,fork_release) ] = max(queue,i+service,i).

Why a single max must be used:  
 sync_wait,i = 3·join_ready –  t(c,i_end) double counts the same clock
minutes repeatedly and would inflate total case time.  Only the maximum
branch completion controls progress to the next stage.

5. Down-stream waiting and service  
downstream_queue = [ t(c,final_start) – join_ready ]  
final_service    = [ t(c,final_end)   – t(c,final_start) ]

6. Total flow time  
total_flow = [ t(c,final_end) – t(c,arrival) ]

7. Critical branch  
critical_branch = argmax{ t(c,i_end) }.  
(Ties resolved A  B  C deterministically.)

# 3. Aggregate critical-path analysis  

Branch A  
• always zero queue, constant 50 min service  
• critical in 2/12 cases (17 %)  
• mean synchronisation wait received from other branches: 7 min  

Branch B  
• mean queue 19 min, mean service 37.5 min  
• critical in 8/12 cases (67 %)  
• average sync wait imposed on others: 11 min  
• often still dominates even when its own service is shorter than A because of its queue.

Branch C  
• mean queue 10 min, mean service 20 min  
• critical in 2/12 cases (17 %) – one due to very long queue (35 min), one due to long service (30 min).  
• exerts the smallest overall influence but occasionally creates tail delays.

Influence ranking  
1. B (high critical-path frequency, large “queue + service”, long tails)  
2. A (longest service but usually non-blocking because it starts immediately)  
3. C (short activities, rarely last)

Down-stream station  
• Mean queue 14.7 min, never determines join_ready but adds 13 % to total flow.  
• Removing it would yield more aggregate benefit than speeding A alone.

# 4. Counterfactual redesign analysis  

CF-1  Reduce A service time by 20 % (50  40 min)  
• Direct effect  10 min per case.  
• Only F01 & F11 benefit (5 min and 10 min).  
• Aggregate saving 15 min (1.3 min / case).  
• Masked whenever B or C stays critical; downstream queue unchanged.

CF-2  Halve B queue (scheduling, extra tester, staggered release)  
• B queue drops from 19  9.5 min mean.  
• Eight B-critical cases shorten join_ready by 5-15 min (see code).  
• Aggregate saving  93 min (7.8 min / case) – six times larger than CF-1.  
• In three cases A becomes the new limiter; residual benefit therefore capped.

CF-3  Eliminate downstream queue (add F3 or pull scheduling)  
• Remove 100 % of 176 queued minutes (14.7 min / case).  
• Universally beneficial; no inter-case pre-emption risk under current load.  
• Does not solve upstream lateness but exposes it, so may shift the perceived bottleneck back to branch B.

Secondary effects (all counterfactuals)  
• Earlier releases advance subsequent arrivals to F; with finite server set this could re-introduce queuing – the figures above assume the final station can absorb the change.

# 5. Recommended redesigns and risks  

1. B-branch queue reduction  
   • Actions: second stress rig during 09:30-11:30 peak, or move non-urgent jobs earlier.  
   • Expected result: ~8 min average flow-time gain, biggest impact on 75-percentile tail.  
   • Resource utilisation: extra rig initially < 60 % busy.  
   • Risk: if B ceases to be critical, long A service may become visible; monitor after pilot.

2. Final-station capacity flex  
   • Actions: cross-train an administrative analyst so F1, F2, F3 available 09:00-12:00.  
   • Expected result: remove 15 min average wait, mostly eliminates last queue.  
   • Throughput: unchanged (service times constant) but frees 176 min operator idle time for other work.  
   • New bottleneck will almost certainly be B queues; pilot should measure whether F3 sits idle after intervention, signalling over-capacity.

# 6. Reproducible Python
```python
import pandas as pd, datetime as dt
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as log_converter
from pm4py.statistics.service_times.pandas import get_service_time
from pm4py.algo.performance.dfg import algorithm as dfg_perf
from pm4py.objects.conversion.log import converter as time_converter

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 1. Load the supplied data
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
tbl = [
 ["F01","07:55","08:10","08:10","09:00","08:15","08:55","08:20","08:38","09:08","09:28","F1"],
 ["F02","08:05","08:25","08:25","09:15","08:45","09:25","08:30","08:50","09:40","10:00","F2"],
 ["F03","08:20","08:40","08:40","09:30","09:00","09:45","08:45","09:03","10:00","10:20","F1"],
 ["F04","08:35","08:55","08:55","09:45","09:05","09:35","09:30","09:52","10:10","10:30","F2"],
 ["F05","08:50","09:10","09:10","10:00","09:35","10:10","09:15","09:35","10:20","10:40","F1"],
 ["F06","09:05","09:25","09:25","10:15","09:55","10:30","09:40","10:00","10:45","11:05","F2"],
 ["F07","09:20","09:40","09:40","10:30","10:10","10:55","09:45","10:03","11:10","11:30","F1"],
 ["F08","09:35","09:55","09:55","10:45","10:00","10:30","10:20","10:50","11:05","11:25","F2"],
 ["F09","09:50","10:10","10:10","11:00","10:35","11:15","10:15","10:35","11:30","11:50","F1"],
 ["F10","10:05","10:25","10:25","11:15","10:45","11:20","10:30","10:50","11:40","12:00","F2"],
 ["F11","10:20","10:40","10:40","11:30","10:50","11:20","10:45","11:03","11:50","12:10","F1"],
 ["F12","10:35","10:55","10:55","11:45","11:25","12:10","11:00","11:18","12:20","12:40","F2"],
]
cols = ["case","arrival","fork_release","A_start","A_end",
        "B_start","B_end","C_start","C_end",
        "final_start","final_end","final_resource"]
df = pd.DataFrame(tbl, columns=cols)

# Convert to datetime (all on 2026-05-19)
base = dt.date(2026,5,19)
for c in cols[1:-1]:
    df[c] = pd.to_datetime([f"{base} {x}" for x in df[c]])

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Delay decomposition
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
def minutes(s): return s.dt.total_seconds()/60
df["upstream"]      = minutes(df["fork_release"]-df["arrival"])

for br in ["A","B","C"]:
    df[f"{br}_queue"]   = minutes(df[f"{br}_start"]-df["fork_release"])
    df[f"{br}_service"] = minutes(df[f"{br}_end"]  -df[f"{br}_start"])

df["join_ready"]    = df[["A_end","B_end","C_end"]].max(axis=1)
df["down_queue"]    = minutes(df["final_start"]-df["join_ready"])
df["final_service"] = minutes(df["final_end"] -df["final_start"])
df["total"]         = minutes(df["final_end"] -df["arrival"])

# critical branch (tie-breaker alphabetical)
def crit(r):
    last = {br: r[f"{br}_end"] for br in ["A","B","C"]}
    m = max(last.values())
    return next(br for br in ["A","B","C"] if last[br]==m)
df["critical"] = df.apply(crit, axis=1)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 3. Counterfactuals
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
cf = df.copy()

# ---- CF-1 20 % faster A service -----------------------------------
cf1 = df.copy()
cf1["A_end_new"] = cf1["A_start"] + (cf1["A_service"]*0.8).map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x))
cf1["join_ready_new"] = cf1[["A_end_new","B_end","C_end"]].max(axis=1)
cf1["final_start_new"] = cf1["join_ready_new"] + (df["down_queue"].map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x)))
cf1["final_end_new"]   = cf1["final_start_new"] + (df["final_service"].map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x)))
cf1["total_new"]       = minutes(cf1["final_end_new"] - df["arrival"])
gain1 = (df["total"] - cf1["total_new"]).sum()

# ---- CF-2 halve B queue -------------------------------------------
cf2 = df.copy()
cf2["B_start_new"] = df["fork_release"] + (df["B_queue"]/2).map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x))
cf2["B_end_new"]   = cf2["B_start_new"] + (df["B_service"].map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x)))
cf2["join_ready_new"] = cf2[["A_end","B_end_new","C_end"]].max(axis=1)
cf2["final_start_new"] = cf2["join_ready_new"] + (df["down_queue"].map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x)))
cf2["final_end_new"]   = cf2["final_start_new"] + (df["final_service"].map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x)))
cf2["total_new"]       = minutes(cf2["final_end_new"] - df["arrival"])
gain2 = (df["total"] - cf2["total_new"]).sum()

# ---- CF-3 remove downstream queue ---------------------------------
cf3 = df.copy()
cf3["final_start_new"] = df["join_ready"]
cf3["final_end_new"]   = cf3["final_start_new"] + (df["final_service"].map(lambda x: dt.timedelta(minutes=x)))
cf3["total_new"]       = minutes(cf3["final_end_new"] - df["arrival"])
gain3 = (df["total"] - cf3["total_new"]).sum()

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. PM4Py interval dataframe
# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# Build one interval row per activity; PM4Py expects columns:
# case:id, activity, start_timestamp, time:timestamp
rows = []
for _,r in df.iterrows():
    for br in ["A","B","C"]:
        rows.append([r["case"],br,r[f"{br}_start"],r[f"{br}_end"]])
    rows.append([r["case"],"F",r["final_start"],r["final_end"]])
logdf = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["case","concept:name","start_timestamp","time:timestamp"])
logdf = log_converter.format_dataframe(logdf,
             case_id="case", activity_key="concept:name",
             start_timestamp_key="start_timestamp",
             timestamp_key="time:timestamp")

# Service time directly retrievable; fork/join waits are custom (above)
serv_stats = get_service_time(logdf)
dfg,perf = dfg_perf.apply(logdf)

# ------------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Print compact results ------------------------------------------
print("\n=== Decomposition (minutes) ===")
print(df[["case","upstream","A_queue","B_queue","C_queue",
          "A_service","B_service","C_service",
          "down_queue","final_service","total","critical"]])
print(f"\nCF-1 aggregate minutes saved: {gain1}")
print(f"CF-2 aggregate minutes saved: {gain2}")
print(f"CF-3 aggregate minutes saved: {gain3}")
```
Comments in code lines beginning with “#” highlight where fork-join quantities are custom pandas calculations; standard PM4Py DFG performance reports only directly-follows service and waiting times and cannot represent the max-based synchronisation wait.

# 7. Limits of inference  

• Single-day sample – cannot infer seasonality or steady-state utilisation.  
• Timestamps show when work *started* and *ended*, not resource idle times; we cannot prove that queues imply physical scarcity rather than release policies.  
• Unknown staff counts on branches; parallel tests may run unrecorded.  
• Pre-emption is invisible; an activity recorded as 50 min could be 2 × 25 min with a break.  
• Counterfactuals ignore behavioural adaptation (e.g., technicians may fill freed time with extra work that re-creates queues).  
• Causality: shortening B queue could itself change arrival times to C in future days – unobservable here.  
• Final-station “queue” is inferred; it could equally be a managerial approval delay unrelated to capacity.
