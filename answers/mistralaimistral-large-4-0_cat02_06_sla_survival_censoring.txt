# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

**Time origin:** `Submit` at t = 0.  
**Horizon:** 72 hours.  
**Primary event of interest:** `Resolve`.  
**Competing events:** `Withdraw`, `Reject`, `Transfer`.  
**Terminal internal outcomes:** `Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`.  
**External terminal outcome:** `Transfer` stops internal observation; eventual resolution is unknown.  
**Right censoring:** `open` cases are known event-free only up to their age at cutoff.

Three distinct concepts must not be conflated:

- **Right censoring** applies to `open` cases: we know they have not yet reached a terminal event by `duration_or_age_h`, but we do not know what happens after the cutoff.
- **Competing events** are `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer` when the estimand is resolution: they preclude the resolution event and should not be treated as ordinary censoring.
- **Already-observed breaches** are cases known not to have resolved by 72 hours: resolved after 72 hours, or still open at an age greater than 72 hours.

Useful estimands:

1. **Cumulative incidence of resolution by 72 hours**  
   \(F_{\text{res}}(72) = P(T_{\text{res}} \le 72)\), where \(T_{\text{res}}\) is time from `Submit` to `Resolve`, with `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer` treated as competing events.  
   *Operational decision:* SLA attainment, expected resolved workload by the deadline, capacity planning.

2. **Probability of still having no terminal disposition at 72 hours**  
   \(S_{\text{term}}(72) = P(T_{\text{term}} > 72)\), where \(T_{\text{term}}\) is time to any terminal event: `Resolve`, `Withdraw`, `Reject`, or `Transfer`.  
   *Operational decision:* active-case workload at the SLA horizon, staffing and queue management.

3. **Composite SLA breach probability**  
   \(B(72) = 1 - F_{\text{res}}(72) = P(\text{no `Resolve` by 72})\).  
   *Operational decision:* tail-risk alerting and breach exposure. This estimand is useful but conflates late resolution, withdrawal, rejection, transfer, and still-open cases.

4. **Cause-specific hazard of resolution**  
   \(h_{\text{res}}(t) = \lim_{\Delta \to 0} P(t \le T_{\text{res}} < t+\Delta \mid T_{\text{res}} \ge t, \text{no competing event before } t)/\Delta\).  
   *Operational decision:* process bottleneck diagnosis and comparison of stage-specific hazards.

Case contributions under each estimand:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| S03 resolved at 76 h | resolved after horizon | Not resolved by 72; in CIF analysis censored at 72 because the resolution event occurs after the horizon | Pending at 72; terminal event at 76 | Breach = 1 |
| S09 open at 92 h | open beyond horizon | Known no resolution by 72; censored at 72 in CIF analysis | Known pending at 72 | Breach = 1 |

Alternative: censored at 68, or treated as resolution at 68 | Terminal at 68; not pending at 72 | Primary: breach = 1. Under transfer-as-censoring: unknown. Under transfer-as-resolution: breach = 0 |

# 2. Event and censoring encoding

Event codes used below:  
`0` = censored, `1` = resolution, `2` = withdrawal, `3` = rejection, `4` = transfer.

| observed_status | time \(t\) | resolution event indicator | terminal event indicator | competing-risk code | censoring rule | sensitivity alternatives |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| resolved | `duration_or_age_h` | 1 if \(t \le 72\), else 0 for binary SLA; for time-to-event, event at \(t\) | 1 | 1 | none; observed event | if \(t > 72\), already observed breach; do not censor at 72 for breach estimand |
| rejected | `duration_or_age_h` | 0 | 1 | 3 | competing event at \(t\) | primary: competing; alternative: exclude from denominator; alternative: censor at \(t\) for KM, which overestimates resolution |
| withdrawn | `duration_or_age_h` | 0 | 1 | 2 | competing event at \(t\) | same as rejection |
| transferred | `duration_or_age_h` | 0 under competing; 1 under external-resolution; 0 under censoring | 1 | 4 under competing; 0 under censoring; 1 under external-resolution | primary: competing event at \(t\); alternative: censor at \(t\); alternative: treat as resolution at \(t\) | see transfer assumptions below |
| open | age at cutoff | 0 | 0 | 0 | right-censored at age; if age \(>72\), known no resolution by 72 and known pending at 72; if age \(<72\), censored before horizon | administrative censoring; informative censoring via inverse-probability weights; treat long inactivity gaps as high risk |

**Transfer treatment assumptions:**

- **T1 — Transfer as competing event (primary):** code 4. Internal resolution cannot occur after transfer; external outcome is unknown. This is the most defensible primary assumption for internal SLA attainment.
- **T2 — Transfer as administrative censoring:** code 0 at transfer time for resolution. Assumes transfer is independent of the resolution process. This may overestimate internal resolution if transfer is related to case complexity or escalation.
- **T3 — Transfer as external resolution:** code 1 at transfer time. Assumes the external organization resolves the case successfully. This is optimistic and should be a sensitivity analysis only.
- **T4 — Transfer as composite breach:** for the binary breach estimand, transfer is a failure to resolve internally by 72. For the resolution CIF, this is equivalent to T1.
- **T5 — Transfer as informative censoring:** use inverse-probability-of-censoring weights or a joint model. This is a sensitivity analysis because transfer may be associated with priority, escalation, and case complexity.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

A naive completed-case analysis would compare only resolved cases, for example mean or median `duration_or_age_h` by cohort. That analysis is biased for SLA tail risk.

**Survivor bias.** Resolved cases are conditional on having resolved. Fast resolutions are overrepresented, while long-running cases are more likely to be open, transferred, or resolved after the cutoff. For example, cohort A resolved cases have mean 54.7 h and cohort B resolved cases have mean 45.6 h, suggesting B is faster. But this excludes S26, an open B case aged 104 h; S32, a B transfer at 96 h; S11, an open A case aged 81 h; S09, an open A case aged 92 h; S08, an A transfer at 68 h; and S13, an A case resolved at 110 h. The completed-case comparison hides exactly the tail cases that matter for SLA risk.

**Informative censoring.** Open cases are not a random sample of unresolved cases. Several open cases have long `last_event_gap_h`: S26 is open at 104 h with a 72 h gap since its last event; S11 is open at 81 h with a 55 h gap; S25 is open at 79 h with a 48 h gap; S09 is open at 92 h with a 31 h gap. These long gaps suggest stalled or forgotten cases. Censoring them at the cutoff may underestimate breach risk if stalled cases are less likely to resolve quickly. Transfers may also be informative: S08, S21, and S32 all follow escalation and may represent complex cases leaving the internal system.

**Competing risks.** Withdrawal and rejection preclude resolution. If they are treated as censoring or excluded, the resolution probability is overestimated. For example, S05 is rejected at 9 h. If S05 is censored at 9 h in a Kaplan-Meier analysis of resolution, it leaves the risk set early and inflates the estimated resolution probability. The same issue applies to S12 withdrawn at 15 h, S14 rejected at 63 h, S22 withdrawn at 44 h, S23 rejected at 11 h, and S30 withdrawn at 70 h.

**Cohort imbalance.** Cohort B was not randomized. Cohort B has more P1 cases: 5/17 = 29.4%, versus 3/15 = 20.0% in cohort A. Cohort B also has fewer P3 cases: 5/17 = 29.4%, versus 5/15 = 33.3% in cohort A. Rollout compliance was incomplete: many B cases use `Submit>AutoCheck>Screen`, but some B cases still use `Submit>Screen>AutoCheck` or `Submit>Screen>Review`. A naive cohort comparison is therefore confounded by priority mix and actual process exposure.

**Active open cases below versus above 72 hours.** Open cases below 72 hours — S10 at 47 h, S15 at 35 h, S24 at 67 h, S29 at 51 h, S31 at 23 h — are censored before the horizon; we do not yet know whether they will resolve by 72. Open cases above 72 hours — S09 at 92 h, S11 at 81 h, S25 at 79 h, S26 at 104 h — are already known breaches for resolution-by-72 and known pending at 72. They must not be treated as censored before 72. A naive analysis that drops all open cases or censors them at their current age loses these known breaches.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

**Kaplan-Meier for resolution.** Treats `Withdraw`, `Reject`, and `Transfer` as censoring. This overestimates the probability of resolution because competing events remove cases from the risk set without resolution. Not recommended as primary.

**Aalen-Johansen cumulative incidence function.** This is the primary nonparametric estimator for \(F_{\text{res}}(72)\). It handles competing events correctly and directly answers the SLA attainment question. With 32 cases and few events, a nonparametric estimator is more defensible than a heavily parameterized model.

**Cause-specific hazards.** Estimate separate hazards for resolution, withdrawal, rejection, and transfer. These are useful for understanding which transitions drive SLA failure. They require modeling assumptions and are secondary.

**Subdistribution hazards / Fine-Gray model.** Models covariate effects on the cumulative incidence of resolution. Useful for prediction, but with 32 cases and 18 terminal events before 72 hours, it is prone to overfitting. Use penalization or treat as exploratory.

**Restricted mean time lost / restricted mean cumulative incidence.** Can summarize expected time to resolution within 72 hours or expected resolution count by 72. Useful as a secondary summary, but the CIF at 72 is more directly interpretable for SLA governance.

**Landmarking.** A landmark analysis at 72 hours conditions on cases still open at 72 and estimates their probability of eventual resolution. This is operationally useful for post-SLA workload, but the sample of cases open at 72 is small.

**Multi-state model.** A process-aware model with states such as `Screen`, `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `Rework`, and terminal states can estimate transition-specific hazards. This is the best process-aware secondary approach, but the small sample requires penalization or exact methods.

**Recommended primary analysis:** Aalen-Johansen CIF of resolution by 72 hours, with transfer as a competing event, open cases censored at `min(age, 72)`, stratified by cohort and priority.  
**Recommended secondary analysis:** cause-specific hazard models for resolution and competing events, with time-varying process features derived from prefixes, plus a multi-state model for stage transitions.  
**Sensitivity analyses:** vary transfer treatment, withdrawal/rejection treatment, informative censoring, and cohort-policy adjustment.

# 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards

**Baseline covariates (safe at t = 0):** cohort, priority. These are assigned or known at case start.

**Time-varying covariates (must be measured after start):**

- Current stage: `Screen`, `AutoCheck`, `Review`, `Escalate`, `Rework`, or terminal.
- Number of `Review` visits: repeated review may indicate complexity.
- Rework indicator: `Rework` appears in S07, S13, and S27.
- Escalation timing: time from `Submit` to first `Escalate`, and whether escalation occurs before 72 hours.
- AutoCheck order: whether `AutoCheck` occurs before `Screen` or after `Screen`. This is a rollout-compliance feature, not a baseline feature.
- Inactivity gap: `last_event_gap_h` or time since the last recorded event. Long gaps may predict breach.
- Path variant: the full observed prefix pattern.
- Sequence length: number of activities observed so far.

**Post-treatment / leakage-prone features:** `Escalate`, `Rework`, `AutoCheck` order, and `Resolve` occur after case start. They cannot be used as baseline covariates without a time-aware design. For example, treating `escalated = yes` as a baseline covariate creates immortal-time bias: a case must survive until `Escalate` to be classified as escalated, so early resolutions and withdrawals are excluded from the escalated group. S04 (`Submit>Screen>Escalate>Resolve` at 29 h) illustrates this: escalation is part of the process, not a pre-existing case characteristic.

**Proposed state-transition diagram (text):**

`Submit  Screen`; `Submit  AutoCheck`; `Screen  AutoCheck`; `Screen  Review`; `Screen  Reject`; `Screen  Withdraw`; `Screen  Escalate`; `Screen  Resolve`; `AutoCheck  Screen`; `AutoCheck  Review`; `AutoCheck  Escalate`; `AutoCheck  Resolve`; `Review  Resolve`; `Review  Rework`; `Review  Withdraw`; `Review  Reject`; `Review  Escalate`; `Rework  Review`; `Rework  Resolve`; `Escalate  Resolve`; `Escalate  Transfer`; `Escalate  Review`.

**Stage hazards of interest:**

- `Review  Resolve` hazard: main resolution bottleneck.
- `Review  Escalate` hazard: escalation propensity.
- `Escalate  Transfer` hazard: external handoff risk.
- `Screen  Reject` and `Review  Reject` hazards: early rejection risk.
- `Review  Withdraw` hazard: customer withdrawal risk.
- `Rework  Review` and `Rework  Resolve` hazards: rework loop risk.

These hazards can be estimated with Cox models using time-varying covariates, or with a multi-state model. With 32 cases, use penalized or exact methods and stratify by priority.

# 6. Sensitivity analysis

At least three sensitivity analyses should be run.

**Sensitivity 1 — Transfer treatment.** Compare T1 competing event, T2 censoring, and T3 external resolution. Affected cases: S08 transferred at 68 h, S21 transferred at 26 h, S32 transferred at 96 h. Under the primary competing-event assumption, the overall CIF of resolution by 72 is about 30.4%; cohort A about 29.2%, cohort B about 31.6%. Under transfer-as-censoring, cohort B rises to about 33.2% while cohort A stays about 29.2%. Under transfer-as-resolution, cohort A rises to about 37.7% and cohort B to about 37.9%, nearly eliminating the cohort difference. Thus the conclusion that cohort B has slightly better resolution by 72 is sensitive to transfer treatment.

**Sensitivity 2 — Withdrawal and rejection treatment.** Compare competing events, exclusion from the denominator, and censoring. Affected cases: S05, S06, S12, S14, S22, S23, S30. If withdrawals and rejections are excluded, the resolution rate among remaining cases increases. If they are censored, Kaplan-Meier overestimates resolution. This could reverse conclusions about SLA attainment if early withdrawals/rejections are common.

**Sensitivity 3 — Informative censoring.** Primary analysis assumes open cases are administratively censored. Sensitivity: weight open cases by inverse probability of censoring using `last_event_gap_h`, priority, and cohort; or treat long-gap open cases as high risk. Affected cases: S26, S11, S25, S09. If long inactivity gaps indicate stalled cases, the breach risk is underestimated by administrative censoring. This could reverse the cohort comparison.

**Sensitivity 4 — Cohort-policy effects.** Compare naive cohort comparison, priority-stratified analysis, and time-varying AutoCheck-order analysis. Cohort B has more P1 cases. Among P1 cases, cohort A has CIF resolution by 72 of about 33.3% versus cohort B about 20.0%. Among P2 cases, cohort B is higher: about 48.6% versus cohort A about 38.1%. Among P3 cases, both are about 20.0%. Therefore the overall cohort difference is confounded by priority mix and could reverse after stratification.

**Sensitivity 5 — Already-observed breaches.** Ensure S03 resolved at 76 h, S13 resolved at 110 h, S20 resolved at 74 h, S28 resolved at 88 h, and open cases S09, S11, S25, S26 are counted as known breaches for resolution-by-72. If they are censored at 72 or dropped, breach risk is underestimated.

**Qualitative conclusions that could reverse:** whether cohort B improves SLA attainment; whether cohort B has lower breach risk; whether the policy change reduced tail risk; and whether P1 cases are handled faster.

# 7. Operational interpretation

Survival outputs should be converted into operational actions, not causal claims.

**SLA attainment.** \(F_{\text{res}}(72)\) estimates the probability that a case is resolved by 72 hours. If the target is, for example, 80% and the estimate is about 30%, the process is far from SLA attainment. Expected resolved cases by 72 = \(N \times F_{\text{res}}(72)\).

**Workload at the deadline.** \(S_{\text{term}}(72)\) estimates the probability that a case still has no terminal disposition at 72 hours. Expected active cases at 72 = \(N \times S_{\text{term}}(72)\). This drives staffing, overtime, and queue management.

**Alerts.** Use a landmark model at, say, 48 or 60 hours to predict the probability of resolution by 72 conditional on being still open. Cases with low predicted resolution probability, long inactivity gaps, repeated review, or escalation should be flagged. For example, S26 has a 72-hour inactivity gap and is open at 104 hours; it should be a high-priority operational alert.

**Workload actions.** If pending-at-72 is high, schedule additional reviewers or escalate stalled cases. If the `Escalate  Transfer` hazard is high, improve external handoff. If the `Review  Rework` loop is common, investigate review quality.

**SLA governance.** Monitor \(F_{\text{res}}(72)\) and \(S_{\text{term}}(72)\) by cohort and priority over time. Set thresholds for acceptable breach risk. Track calibration: compare predicted breach probabilities with observed breach outcomes. Track fairness: ensure alert rates and resolution probabilities are not systematically worse for P3 cases or for cohort B. Do not present risk scores as causal effects of the policy change, because cohort assignment was not randomized and rollout compliance was incomplete.

def encode_cif_resolution(data, transfer_mode="competing"):
    """
    Aalen-Johansen-ready data for CIF of resolution by 72.
    Event codes: 0=censored, 1=resolution, 2=withdraw, 3=reject, 4=transfer.
    transfer_mode: "competing", "censor", "resolution".
    """
    recs = []
    for _, r in data.iterrows():
        t = r["t_obs"]
        status = r["observed_status"]
        t_stop = min(t, HORIZON)
        code = 0

        if t <= HORIZON:
            if status == "resolved":
                code = 1
            elif status == "withdrawn":
                code = 2
            elif status == "rejected":
                code = 3
            elif status == "transferred":
                if transfer_mode == "competing":
                    code = 4
                elif transfer_mode == "censor":
                    code = 0
                elif transfer_mode == "resolution":
                    code = 1
                else:
                    raise ValueError(transfer_mode)

        if status == "resolved" and t <= HORIZON:
            known_no_res = 0
        elif status == "resolved" and t > HORIZON:
            known_no_res = 1
        elif status == "open" and t >= HORIZON:
            known_no_res = 1
        elif status == "open" and t < HORIZON:
            known_no_res = None
        elif status in ["withdrawn", "rejected"]:
            known_no_res = 1
        elif status == "transferred":
            if transfer_mode == "resolution" and t <= HORIZON:
                known_no_res = 0
            elif transfer_mode == "censor" and t <= HORIZON:
                known_no_res = None
            else:
                known_no_res = 1
        else:
            known_no_res = None

        recs.append({
            "case": r["case"],
            "cohort": r["cohort"],
            "priority": r["priority"],
            "t_stop": t_stop,
            "event_code": code,
            "transfer_mode": transfer_mode,
            "known_no_resolution_by_72": known_no_res,
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(recs)


def encode_pending(data):
    """
    Kaplan-Meier-ready data for time to any terminal disposition.
    Event code 1 = terminal event (resolved, withdrawn, rejected, transferred).
    """
    recs = []
    for _, r in data.iterrows():
        t = r["t_obs"]
        t_stop = min(t, HORIZON)
        code = 1 if (not r["is_open"] and t <= HORIZON) else 0
        recs.append({
            "case": r["case"],
            "cohort": r["cohort"],
            "priority": r["priority"],
            "t_stop": t_stop,
            "event_code": code,
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(recs)


def encode_breach_known(data, transfer_mode="competing"):
    """
    Binary breach indicator: 1 = known no resolution by 72,
    0 = known resolution by 72, None = unknown at 72.
    """
    recs = []
    for _, r in data.iterrows():
        t = r["t_obs"]
        status = r["observed_status"]
        if status == "resolved" and t <= HORIZON:
            breach = 0
        elif status == "resolved" and t > HORIZON:
            breach = 1
        elif status == "open" and t >= HORIZON:
            breach = 1
        elif status == "open" and t < HORIZON:
            breach = None
        elif status in ["withdrawn", "rejected"]:
            breach = 1
        elif status == "transferred":
            if transfer_mode == "resolution" and t <= HORIZON:
                breach = 0
            elif transfer_mode == "censor" and t <= HORIZON:
                breach = None
            else:
                breach = 1
        else:
            breach = None
        recs.append({
            "case": r["case"],
            "cohort": r["cohort"],
            "priority": r["priority"],
            "breach_72": breach,
            "transfer_mode": transfer_mode,
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(recs)


# ---------- Manual survival estimators ----------

def aalen_johansen_cif(analysis, horizon=HORIZON, cause=1):
    """
    Manual Aalen-Johansen CIF for cause=1 at horizon.
    Returns (cif_at_horizon, overall_survival_at_horizon).
    """
    events = analysis[(analysis["event_code"] != 0) & (analysis["t_stop"] <= horizon)]
    times = sorted(events["t_stop"].unique())
    S = 1.0
    cif = 0.0
    for t in times:
        at_risk = (analysis["t_stop"] >= t).sum()
        d1 = ((analysis["t_stop"] == t) & (analysis["event_code"] == cause)).sum()
        d_other = ((analysis["t_stop"] == t) & (analysis["event_code"] != 0) & (analysis["event_code"] != cause)).sum()
        if at_risk == 0:
            continue
        S_prev = S
        S = S_prev * (1 - (d1 + d_other) / at_risk)
        cif += S_prev * d1 / at_risk
    return cif, S


def km_survival(analysis, horizon=HORIZON):
    """
    Manual Kaplan-Meier survival at horizon for event_code == 1.
    """
    events = analysis[(analysis["event_code"] == 1) & (analysis["t_stop"] <= horizon)]
    times = sorted(events["t_stop"].unique())
    S = 1.0
    for t in times:
        at_risk = (analysis["t_stop"] >= t).sum()
        d = ((analysis["t_stop"] == t) & (analysis["event_code"] == 1)).sum()
        if at_risk > 0:
            S *= (1 - d / at_risk)
    return S


def summarize_cif(analysis, by):
    out = []
    for key, sub in analysis.groupby(by):
        cif, surv = aalen_johansen_cif(sub)
        if isinstance(key, tuple):
            row = dict(zip(by, key))
        else:
            row = {by[0]: key}
        row.update({
            "n": len(sub),
            "cif_res_72": round(cif, 4),
            "surv_no_event_72": round(surv, 4),
        })
        out.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(out)


def summarize_pending(analysis, by):
    out = []
    for key, sub in analysis.groupby(by):
        s = km_survival(sub)
        if isinstance(key, tuple):
            row = dict(zip(by, key))
        else:
            row = {by[0]: key}
        row.update({
            "n": len(sub),
            "pending_72_km": round(s, 4),
        })
        out.append(row)
    return pd.DataFrame(out)

# 9. Limitations and additional data

**Small sample.** 32 cases and only 18 terminal events before 72 hours limit precision. Confidence intervals will be wide, and multivariable models are prone to overfitting.

**Missing exact event timestamps.** The extract provides only `duration_or_age_h` and `last_event_gap_h`. We can derive the last event time as `duration_or_age_h - last_event_gap_h`, but we cannot reconstruct the full timestamped event log. Exact timestamps for every activity are needed for time-varying hazards, stage durations, and proper multi-state modeling.

**External outcomes after transfer.** For S08, S21, and S32, the eventual resolution after transfer is unknown. Follow-up data from the external organization are needed to distinguish successful external resolution from abandonment.

**Cutoff and start dates.** Calendar dates are missing. Without them, we cannot assess seasonality, workload trends, or the exact policy rollout period.

**Time-varying workload.** Staffing levels, queue length, and concurrent case volume are missing. These may affect resolution hazards and censoring.

**Assignment mechanism.** Cohort B was not randomized. We need eligibility criteria, reasons for cohort assignment, and rollout compliance records to reduce confounding.

**Policy rollout compliance.** Some cohort B cases still follow `Submit>Screen>AutoCheck` or `Submit>Screen>Review`, so actual exposure to the automated-screening change is incomplete. The actual process path, not just cohort label, should be modeled.

**Informative censoring.** Open cases with long `last_event_gap_h` may be stalled or abandoned. Reasons for being open at cutoff are needed to assess whether censoring is informative.

**Competing event reasons.** Withdrawal and rejection may be customer-driven, agent-driven, or policy-driven. Reason codes are needed to interpret the composite breach estimand.

**Priority definitions.** P1 is highest priority, but the SLA may need priority-specific targets. Fairness checks should compare calibration and alert rates across priority and cohort.
