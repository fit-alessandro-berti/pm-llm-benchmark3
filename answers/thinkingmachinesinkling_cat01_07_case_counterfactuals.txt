| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 preliminary escalation / C1 reserve | S1 advisory ~1h; C1 preliminary ~2h + reserved final ~3h (compressed schedule) | Final C1 wait for this case; S1 preliminary load distributed | C1 queue for other urgent cases (reservation displaces); S1 advisory queue | Two urgent compliance cases added Mon 16:00; other high-risk cases waiting for C1 | Ambiguous: local positive; system negative if C1 saturated | Zero-sum C1 reservation; preliminary packet quality determines if final work actually shrinks |
| K-219 D2 substitution | D1 workload reduced by 1 task; D2 workload increased by 1 task (~6h handwritten) | D1 queue (one complex/standard task removed) | D2 queue; if rework triggered: D1/D2 and A2 queues grow; external response queue | Cases waiting for D1 (complex corporate) benefit; D2 pool cases face longer waits; assessment queue if loop occurs | Positive if no rework; strongly negative if loop rate >~20% | Rework probability is nonlinear with handwriting ambiguity; weekend delay multiplies loop cost |
| K-337 suppress second loop | Eliminates 1 Doc Check (D1 ~5h), 1 Assessment (A1 ~2.5h), 1 weekend/external wait (~72h), 1 Compliance (C1 ~4h) | D1, A1, C1, and external-response queues locally freed | Quality-assurance / legal-review queues may grow post-decision; appeal risk pool | All other cases competing for D1/A1/C1 benefit from freed capacity; supplier not contacted again | Positive for operational capacity; negative for hidden quality-debt exposure | Suppressing loop changes decision space discontinuously (iterative refinement  one-shot conditional/reject) |
| K-411 parallel verification | I2 and D2 used concurrently instead of sequential; same resource-hours, compressed calendar | Verification calendar time compressed; no direct queue reduction | Assessment queue receives case ~4h earlier; I2/D2 simultaneous load increases pool congestion | Other cases in assessment queue may be displaced; I2/D2 pool tasks face tighter scheduling | Near-neutral or slightly negative (bottleneck stays at assessment) | Workaround effectiveness is binary; false concurrency creates audit risk; queue displacement has diminishing returns |

If all four interventions were applied to 100 similar cases in one week, the system dynamics diverge sharply. For K-104, reserving C1 slots for preliminary and final work for 100 high-risk cases would require ~500 hours of C1 capacity—far exceeding one certified reviewer. The preliminary screens that save 1–3h per case become net losses if packets are incomplete, wasting scarce capacity on misdirected review. Queue displacement at C1 becomes severe: other urgent compliance cases (noted in the Mon 16:00 snapshot) are pushed out, and the local benefit collapses once reservation exceeds available slack. The interference is zero-sum and nonlinear; the policy is only viable with strict eligibility gates (<5 cases/week) and packet-quality filters.

For K-219, substituting D2 for D1 on 100 handwritten-original cases shifts ~600 hours of work onto D2 (generalist) and frees D1. Because D2’s standard service is 4–8h versus D1’s 3–6h, total processing time increases by ~100–200 hours. The critical nonlinearity is rework: if D2’s error rate on handwriting is 20–30%, 20–30 cases enter loops. Each loop consumes another document check (4–8h), an assessment (2–5h), and an external wait (8–36h). Because external delays include weekends, loops spanning Friday trigger 72-hour calendar penalties. By Monday-Tuesday of the following week, D2 and A2 queues would spike with rework, overwhelming the pools. The local substitution is not scalable without a pre-screening quality gate that routes genuinely ambiguous handwriting back to D1.

For K-337, suppressing the second loop eliminates ~4.5 days per case and frees substantial D1, A1, and C1 capacity—potentially freeing 500+ hours across 100 cases. Weekend effects disappear entirely, smoothing weekly load. However, the decision policy change is uniform: every case with a remaining discrepancy must be resolved by conditional approval or rejection. If 10% of discrepancies are material, the system accumulates 10 defective decisions. The bottleneck migrates from operational queues (D1, A1, C1) to quality-assurance, legal-review, and appeal queues, which grow nonlinearly with volume. The intervention is highly effective operationally but creates hidden quality debt.

For K-411, scaling concurrency requires the workaround to be applied to every case creation event. With 100 cases, 200 concurrent task pairs (I2/D2) must be managed within pools of only two resources each, meaning queuing within pools still dominates. The assessment queue receives cases 4 hours earlier, but because A2 is the persistent bottleneck (queue 4–6h), the calendar gain per case remains minimal (~0–2h). The main risk is audit-scale false concurrency: if real-time timestamps are not perfectly aligned with server logs, 100 cases generate 100 potential audit discrepancies. The bottleneck does not migrate; it remains assessment, and the system-wide effect is near-neutral with elevated logging risk.

Across all four, the least transportable local counterfactual is K-411: its benefit depends on a case-specific legacy-lock bug and yields minimal throughput improvement, while scaling introduces system-wide logging complexity. The most dangerous despite speed is K-337, because loop suppression is irreversible for each case and accumulates compliance risk at scale. Real-time queue-state logs linking arrival-to-start intervals, preliminary packet completeness scores, D2 handwritten-accuracy metrics, discrepancy-materiality classifications, and concurrency-timestamp audits are the most valuable additional data.

K-411 ranks lowest because its minimal time benefit does not justify the system-complexity and audit risks at scale.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-104: preliminary escalation with a reserved C1 slot. It operates entirely within existing process rules—preliminary work does not replace the mandatory final compliance review, four-eyes remains intact, and the intervention is fully reversible if the packet proves unhelpful. Its main risk—displacing other urgent cases at the single C1 resource—is observable, measurable, and can be gated by reservation limits and packet-quality filters. For 100 cases, however, this local benefit is the least transportable in raw form because C1 is a zero-sum bottleneck; scaling requires strict eligibility rather than universal application.

The most dangerous intervention despite its speed is K-337: suppressing the second rework loop. Collapsing a 4.5-day cycle into a single Friday decision produces dramatic capacity relief—freeing D1, A1, and C1 across the system and eliminating weekend/external delays entirely. Yet it changes the decision policy discontinuously. The case must decide on incomplete supplier evidence, either by approving with conditions or rejecting. At scale, if even a small fraction of suppressed discrepancies are material, the system accumulates a hidden portfolio of defective approvals, appeals, and compliance failures. The bottleneck migrates from operational queues to quality-assurance and legal-exposure pools that grow nonlinearly with volume.

The local counterfactual least transportable to system-wide policy is K-411. Its gain is minimal—mostly verification parallelism that is quickly absorbed by the assessment bottleneck—while its implementation requires a persistent system workaround for a legacy lock and rigorous real-time timestamp auditing. Scaling to 100 cases multiplies audit risk without proportionally reducing cycle time, because A2’s queue remains the binding constraint. The intervention solves a local logging/creation bug, not a system throughput problem.

What additional data would be most valuable? First, real-time arrival-to-start logs for each pool to validate whether queue forecasts are unbiased predictors or systematically optimistic/pessimistic—this determines whether “earlier entry” actually advances start times. Second, preliminary packet completeness metrics for K-104, to calibrate the 1–3h savings claim. Third, D2 handwritten-document error and rework rates for K-219, because the intervention’s sign depends on a nonlinear threshold. Fourth, discrepancy-materiality classifications for K-337, to determine how often loop suppression produces defensible conditions versus invalid decisions. Fifth, concurrency-timestamp audit trails linking server logs to resource calendars for K-411, because false concurrency is a binary failure mode. Without these, counterfactual reasoning remains calibrated only to point estimates rather than to the true range of plausible futures.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **Structural**: The partial-order constraints (RegisterTriageAssessment, etc.) are invariant except where explicitly altered (e.g., K-337 suppresses loop, K-411 permits concurrency).
2. **Temporal**: Queue forecasts in the system snapshots are unbiased predictors of individual wait times, though they are not deterministic guarantees.
3. **Resource**: S1 advisory work may overlap with other service intervals (Rule 11 exception noted); all other resources are strictly exclusive during service.
4. **Behavioral**: In K-219, D2’s error and rework probability on handwritten standard documents is independent of queue pressure and remains in the ~20–40% range.
5. **Quality**: In K-337, the unresolved discrepancy after Assessment 2 is addressable through an explicit conditional approval or rejection without requiring full supplier resolution.
6. **Interference**: Scaling any intervention to 100 cases behaves linearly except at bottlenecks (C1 reservation zero-sum; D2 pool saturation; assessment queue persistence; preliminary packet quality degradation with volume).
7. **Temporal**: Weekend and external-response delays are fixed calendar effects (~72h for Friday-to-Monday) regardless of case volume or system load.
8. **Resource**: In K-411, the controlled workaround achieves true concurrent resource allocation for I2 and D2 without system-lock reassertion, and real-time timestamps are audit-valid.
