## 1. Target definition

**Primary target.** The unit is one credit application (`case:concept:name`), with one prefix per case. The prediction time \(\tau\) is the timestamp of the first canonical **Credit Review**. The outcome clock is not \(\tau\); it starts at **Account Activated**.

Let \(A=1\) if the case reaches Account Activated under the policy actually in force, let \(T^{\mathrm{act}}\) be that time, and let \(D=1\) if the warehouse field `default_90d` records a default in \((T^{\mathrm{act}}, T^{\mathrm{act}}+90\text{ days}]\). \(D\) is defined only when \(A=1\). A label is mature at analysis time \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\) only when the official feed has arrived and the feed window has closed:

\[
T^{\mathrm{act}}+90+35 \le T_{\mathrm{asof}}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\texttt{label\_available\_at}\le T_{\mathrm{asof}}.
\]

The 35 days are the upper end of the stated 7–35 day feed delay. The primary estimand, for a prefix feature vector \(x\) available at \(\tau\), is

\[
\eta(x)=P(D=1\mid X_{\tau}=x,\ A=1,\ \text{label mature at }T_{\mathrm{asof}}).
\]

The analysis population is activated accounts with a mature official label. The primary as-of time is the extract, 15 February 2026. In backtests, \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\) is the simulated deployment date, not the extract date.

This is the probability of 90-day default among accounts the historical policy actually booked, given the early prefix. It is the quantity supported by `default_90d`. It is a valid monitoring and reserving target when the acceptance policy is stable and the score is acted on for accounts that are activated. It is not the default risk of every started application.

**Why this formulation.** Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still-open applications have no observation of default under activation. Coding them as non-default would mix “never exposed to the loan” with “exposed and paid,” and would report a default rate far below the activated-account rate of \(2{,}700/21{,}600=12.5\%\). Conditioning on activation states the selection explicitly. Maturity is an administrative function of activation date and the extract date, so requiring a closed 90-day window plus feed grace does not require treating immature accounts as non-defaults.

**Alternative target (different question).** A multi-state outcome for all 48,000 applications, measured from application start, with states: still open (administratively censored), rejected, withdrawn, transferred, activated and \(D=1\), activated and \(D=0\) with a mature label, and activated with follow-up still pending. That estimand answers where an application goes under the operating policy, including underwriting load and attrition. Default is only one state, and it remains undefined on paths that never activate. It does not answer “how would this rejected applicant have performed if booked.”

Two further formulations stay off the primary estimand. A survival model for time from activation to default, censored at the as-of date, targets the same booked-account risk and can use immature activated accounts; section 3 treats it as a sensitivity. A counterfactual “default within 90 days if this application were activated” for every starter requires an activation policy and an ignorability assumption the event log does not justify. Underwriter judgment, document contents, and unlogged overrides sit on the path from the prefix to both activation and default.

## 2. Cohort construction

**As-of rule.** Fix \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\) (15 February 2026 for the descriptive cohort; earlier dates in backtests). An event is visible only if `time:timestamp` \(\le T_{\mathrm{asof}}\). A label is usable only under the maturity rule in section 1. A case that is open at \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\) stays open in that snapshot even if a later extract would close it. Terminal state is the earliest visible event among Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, and Account Activated. Absence of those events means still open, including applications that have not reached a decision and applications whose activation is still in the future.

**Inclusion in the primary likelihood.** All of the following: \(A=1\); \(T^{\mathrm{act}}+125\text{ days}\le T_{\mathrm{asof}}\); `label_available_at` \(\le T_{\mathrm{asof}}\); `default_90d` observed; first Credit Review visible at or before \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\). Fold membership is by case start time (section 7). Linked transfer identifiers for the same economic application stay in one fold; the originating case is the analysis case. Post-transfer events on a partner platform are not appended to the prefix.

**Exclusion from the primary likelihood, with the case retained in the cohort accounting.** Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and still open. Activated but the 90-day horizon has not ended. Activated, horizon ended, feed still inside the 35-day grace. Activated, grace ended, feed still absent (permanently missing candidate, especially partner-originated `source_system`). No first Credit Review by \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\) (early KYC reject, early withdrawal, or not yet at the milestone): no primary prefix, and not a non-default. Prefixes that still contain a post-decision activity after canonicalization: data-error hold-out, counted in the audit.

Era is a stratum, not an exclusion. The policy threshold change on 1 July 2025 is attached using the timestamp of first Credit Review, because that is when the new rule can bind. The 15 October 2025 migration is attached the same way. Under a 35-day grace, activation after about 13 October 2025 cannot have a mature negative label by 15 February 2026 (\(T_{\mathrm{asof}}-125\text{ days}\)). Post-migration default metrics are therefore not available from this extract.

The published 3,600 “not yet matured” accounts mix administrative censoring, in-grace feed delay, and possible permanent missingness. Those components have to be split from timestamps in the event file. Subcounts are not invented here.

| Segment | N | Status as of 15 Feb 2026 | Role in the primary estimand |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Applications started | 48,000 | Denominator | Cohort universe |
| Rejected | 9,100 | Competing terminal outcome | Default undefined; excluded from the likelihood |
| Withdrawn | 5,700 | Competing terminal outcome | Default undefined; excluded from the likelihood |
| Transferred | 2,900 | Competing exit; linkage exists for about 60% (~1,740), absent for ~1,160 | Not a non-default; unlinked follow-up is missing, not zero |
| Still open | 5,100 | Activation and default administratively censored | Not a non-default and not a confirmed non-activation |
| Activated, official label mature | 21,600 | 2,700 defaults and 18,900 non-defaults | Primary labeled set |
| Activated, outcome or feed not mature | 3,600 | Mixture of right censoring, delayed feed, and possible permanent missingness | Excluded from the binary complete case; decomposed before any survival use |
| Sum of the six mutually exclusive rows | 48,000 | | |

Milestone eligibility is a further cut on top of this table. Cases that never reach Credit Review contribute to the rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or still-open rows and do not enter \(\eta(x)\).

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

Five mechanisms are kept separate.

**Ordinary right censoring.** The 90-day window has not ended by \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\), or the application is still open and has no terminal event. The outcome is not knowable yet. These cases are censored at the as-of date. They are not labeled \(D=0\).

**Delayed labels.** The horizon has ended and the external feed usually arrives 7–35 days later. Until `label_available_at`, the official label is pending. The primary rule waits until activation \(+125\) days and a non-missing label. Early recorded defaults are not pulled into the binary sample while same-cohort non-defaults are still pending; that would inflate the complete-case default rate.

**Selection through activation.** Only booked accounts can realize \(D\). Rejection, withdrawal, and transfer are competing exits from the application process, not censored defaults. The primary analysis conditions on \(A=1\) and states \(\eta(x)\) as historical booked-account risk. It does not impute labels for \(A=0\).

**Transfer.** Transfer is a competing exit from this lender’s book. For about 60% of transfers a linkage key exists; for about 40% it does not, and the case can reappear under a new identifier. Primary analysis does not import the other platform’s performance. A sensitivity may use linked outcomes only after checking that the other platform’s 90-day definition matches `default_90d`. Unlinked transfers stay missing follow-up.

**Permanently missing feeds.** Partner-originated activated accounts have an elevated chance that the feed never arrives. Once activation \(+125\) days has passed and the label is still absent, the case is a missing-outcome problem. Coding it as censored at day 90 would treat informative missingness as independent censoring and would bias both the survival curve and the 12.5% base rate, because the labeled partner slice would be only the partners whose feed returned.

**Primary strategy.** Calendar-mature complete cases among activated accounts. The binary likelihood uses the 21,600 (after the milestone and as-of filters). Immature, in-grace, and missing-after-grace activated accounts are excluded, not filled with zero. Non-activated terminals stay in the cohort flow with their own state.

**Sensitivity.** (1) Survival on activated accounts whose follow-up is administratively censored or still inside the feed grace: time origin = activation, event = official default if it arrives inside 90 days, censoring time = \(\min(90,\ T_{\mathrm{asof}}-T^{\mathrm{act}})\) while the feed can still arrive. \(P(T\le 90\mid A=1,x)\) is the survival functional corresponding to \(\eta\). Missing-after-grace cases stay out of that censoring model. (2) For missing-after-grace cases, Manski bounds in the partner stratum and overall: set all such outcomes to 0, then all to 1, and report the interval. Do this only after the timestamp split of the 3,600. (3) Refit with grace 7 days instead of 35, which moves the last evaluable activation from about 13 October 2025 to about 10 November 2025 and changes who is labeled. (4) Competing-risk model of reject / withdraw / transfer / activate from application start, then the default model only on the activated state. (5) Among linked transfers only, compare outcomes with retained accounts; do not fill the unlinked 40%.

**What can be checked.** From these data one can estimate the historical distribution of `label_available_at` minus \((T^{\mathrm{act}}+90)\); covariate and prefix shift across rejected, withdrawn, transferred, open, mature-activated, and immature-activated cases; the association of `source_system` with missingness after grace; agreement on mature cases between a Default Recorded event inside 90 days and official `default_90d`; duplicate and unmapped-activity rates around 15 October 2025; and discrimination and calibration of a frozen pre-policy model on post-policy cases that do mature before the extract.

**Partly checkable.** Whether feeds that arrive late have a different default rate than feeds that arrive early, among cases that eventually get a label. That says nothing about feeds that never arrive. Whether the linked 60% of transfers resemble retained accounts. Whether, within an activation month, cases with partial follow-up look like cases with closed windows on pre-outcome covariates.

**Not identified from this extract.** Default had a rejected or withdrawn applicant been activated. Outcomes of unlinked transfers. The true outcomes behind permanently missing partner feeds, beyond bounds. No unmeasured confounding between underwriter information, activation, and default. Results for activation cohorts whose 90-day window is still open at 15 February 2026.

## 4. Prefix extraction

Canonicalize activity names with a versioned crosswalk **before** any prefix is cut, so the 15 October 2025 renames do not move the Credit Review milestone or split one variant into two. Unmapped names remain visible to the audit. Drop exact duplicate events (same case, canonical activity, timestamp, and resource) created by the migration. Do not collapse repeated document cycles; those are real rework. Near-duplicate collapse within a one-second epsilon is a reviewed rule, applied only to the renamed pairs known to have been double-emitted.

**Primary prefix.** Events with `time:timestamp` \(\le \tau\), where \(\tau\) is the first canonical Credit Review, and also \(\le T_{\mathrm{asof}}\). Decision and post-booking activities are removed if a timestamp tie would include them: Accepted, Rejected, Withdrawn, Transferred, Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections Contact, Default Recorded. The prefix is empty of later document loops, because those loops leave Credit Review and return. Rework counts belong to a later checkpoint, not to this one.

**Fixed event count.** As a sensitivity, build length-\(k\) prefixes with `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` on the already truncated milestone log, for \(k\in\{2,3\}\). Traces shorter than \(k\) are omitted from that sensitivity and are not labeled \(D=0\). A length-\(k\) prefix taken from the raw full case is not comparable across cases: the same \(k\) can be “Started → KYC → Credit Review” or already inside a document loop or, after a bad crosswalk, a renamed decision. After canonicalization, report the activity in position \(k\). If that activity is a decision or a collections event, that \(k\) is past the prediction point and is not a feature source for \(\eta\).

**Elapsed time.** For \(\Delta\in\{1,3,7\}\) days, keep events with timestamp \(\le \min(\text{start}+\Delta,\ \tau_{\mathrm{decision}})\), excluding the decision event itself. Cases already rejected, withdrawn, or transferred before \(\Delta\) are a resolved stratum with an observed underwriting outcome; they are not scored as prospective defaults. Cases that have not reached Credit Review by \(\Delta\) are a different information set (still in intake) and are not pooled with the milestone model.

**Short cases.** No milestone by \(T_{\mathrm{asof}}\) means “not eligible for this checkpoint.” The case remains in the cohort flow under its terminal or open state.

**Later checkpoint, not primary.** Prefix at the second Credit Review, or the full pre-activation prefix of events strictly before Account Activated. The second review is where document-loop counts, return-to-review counts, and inter-loop waiting time are known. The pre-activation prefix is the coherent scoring time for a booked account, because \(A=1\) is then known and matches the training population. `final_limit` and `decision_reason` can be used there; they are known at booking and do not reveal \(D\). They stay out of the primary first-review model.

## 5. Process-aware features

All features are computed from events at or before \(\tau\), and from historical aggregates whose own labels and events were knowable at \(\tau\).

**Static case attributes, after checking they do not change inside the prefix.** `case:channel`, `case:region`, `source_system`, and `case:requested_amount` if constant on events up to \(\tau\). If requested amount changes across events, use the value on the last prefix event, not the case-level maximum. Start timestamp yields hour, day of week, and month. These are intake attributes, not outcomes.

**Prefix-derived, primary checkpoint.** Event count; activity indicators and bigrams for Started, Identity/KYC Check, and Credit Review; distinct `org:resource` values; inter-event waiting time from start to KYC and KYC to first review; last `risk_score` on an event at or before \(\tau\); policy-era indicator and days since 1 July 2025; migration-map version if \(\tau\) is after 15 October 2025. Document-request counts and credit-review repetitions are not features of this prefix; they are features of the second-review checkpoint. Queue load at \(\tau\) (open cases in the same region with events so far, counted only from events \(\le\tau\)) is prefix-time context.

**Historical reference, rebuilt as of \(\tau\).** Resource experience is the number of events that resource had strictly before this event. The stored extraction-time experience is not used. Resource, channel, and region default rates use only accounts whose `label_available_at` is on or before \(\tau\), with shrinkage, and are fit inside the training fold. Directly-follows frequencies and variant vocabularies are fit on training prefixes only, then applied forward. A transition that involves Contract Signed, activation, collections, or default is not a training-prefix feature.

**Used only at the later checkpoints.** Loop count, time in document cycle, `final_limit`, and `decision_reason`, and only when the checkpoint is at or after the decision and still strictly before activation and before any collections event.

## 6. Leakage audit

| Feature or data element | Leakage or bias risk | Valid availability rule | Keep/repair/drop | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `final_limit` | Populated only after the final credit decision; encodes the acceptance outcome and the limit the lender actually set | Known only at or after the decision event | Drop at the first-review model; keep only in a pre-activation companion model | At \(\tau\) the limit is in the future. In a booked-account model it is a legitimate underwriting attribute and still does not reveal \(D\) |
| `decision_reason` | Written at or after Accept/Reject; separates approved, declined, and reason codes the prefix must not see | Known only at the decision event | Drop at the first-review model; optional in the pre-activation companion | A reason code is post-decision information and a proxy for the selection \(A\) |
| `collection_status` | Updated after activation and can move through delinquency states that determine \(D\) | Known only after booking, and even then a later value reveals the label | Drop | Any value past \(\tau\), and any value after activation, is outcome information |
| Future events (Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections Contact, Default Recorded, and later Credit Review loops) | The case log continues through outcome recording; these activities determine \(A\) and often \(D\) | Include an event only if its timestamp is \(\le\tau\) and \(\le T_{\mathrm{asof}}\), and its canonical name is not in the forbidden set | Drop by truncation | Activation time is used to define the cohort and the outcome clock. It is not a feature at first review |
| `documents_total` | Nightly snapshot can include uploads later on the same calendar day than \(\tau\) | Join only a snapshot whose timestamp is before the prediction calendar day and whose definition excludes same-day uploads after \(\tau\) | Drop unless that snapshot history exists; otherwise repair to the previous night | A same-day total is lookahead. The column is not safe on the event row as stored |
| `risk_score` | Recomputed at later events, including after the decision and after booking | Value on the latest event with timestamp \(\le\tau\); no backfill, no case-level last value, no max over the case | Repair | The field is legitimate at the event that wrote it and leaked if a later write overwrites it |
| Resource experience | Stored as tenure at extraction (15 Feb 2026), so it includes work after \(\tau\) and after the outcome | Count of that resource’s events with timestamp strictly before the event, computed inside the as-of log | Repair; drop the extraction-time field | Extraction-time experience sorts early cases by how their resources’ careers turned out |
| `default_90d` | The label, repeated on events once known | Usable as \(y\) only if the case is activated, grace has elapsed, and `label_available_at` \(\le T_{\mathrm{asof}}\) | Drop from \(X\); gated use as \(y\) | Putting it on the feature table, or filling missing with 0, recreates the biased export |
| `label_available_at` | Encodes that a feed arrived and when; missingness is informative for partner accounts | Gate for whether \(y\) may be read at this as-of date | Drop from \(X\); use as a label gate | A missing timestamp is a property of the outcome feed, not of the prefix |
| `data_extract_time` | Constant snapshot metadata; using it as a covariate across mixed extracts creates a fake feature | Analysis parameter only | Drop | It is not a case attribute |
| Target-encoded resource or channel default rate | An encoder fit on all matured labels uses future defaults and future policies | Rates from cases with `label_available_at` \(\le\tau\), fit on the training fold only | Repair or drop | Ungated target encoding is label leakage and crosses the July 2025 break |
| `case:requested_amount`, channel, region, `source_system` | Safe if they are intake attributes; a later overwrite of amount is lookahead; `source_system` also marks permanent feed loss | As-of value at \(\tau\); confirm low within-case variance before treating as static | Keep, with an as-of read | Needed for subgroup calibration and for the missing-feed sensitivity. They do not define \(D\) |

A prefix passes the audit only if it contains no forbidden activity, no event after \(\tau\), and none of the drop-listed columns. Directly-follows discovery on training prefixes is a check that end activities are milestone activities, not a feature fit on full traces.

## 7. Validation design

A random case split is a misleading score. It places post-policy and post-migration prefixes in training, so the model is graded on a mixture that includes the future regime. It treats labels that arrive 7–35 days after day 90 as if they had been known at prediction time, which hides the four-month lag between a new booking and a trainable negative label. It can put two linked transfer identifiers for one applicant on opposite sides. It also lets contemporaneous queue conditions and resource rates, which are shared across cases in the same week, leak through a random partition. `pm4py.split_train_test` has that random-case behavior and is only a baseline to show how optimistic it is.

**Primary scheme: expanding as-of backtest, with a predeclared start-time holdout.**

For each simulated deployment date \(D\) from 1 March 2025 through 1 October 2025:

- Training labels are cases with grace end \(\le D\) and `label_available_at` \(\le D\).
- Training prefixes have \(\tau\le D\).
- The model is frozen at \(D\).
- The test slice is cases with \(\tau\in(D, D_{\mathrm{next}}]\).
- Metrics for that slice are computed only after those cases themselves mature, using the frozen model. Cases that have not matured by the extract stay “not evaluable” and are omitted from the denominator. They are not scored as non-defaults.
- Resource and channel encodings inside each fit use only labels available at that \(D\).

Report three eras separately, using \(\tau\) relative to 1 July 2025 and 15 October 2025:

- Pre-policy transport: train on labels knowable before 1 July 2025, test on matured cases with \(\tau\) still before that date.
- Policy-shift test: the same frozen model on cases with \(\tau\) on or after 1 July 2025 whose activations still mature by the extract (roughly activations through mid-October). Also report an expanding refit that adds post-policy labels only once they mature.
- Migration: activity-map coverage, duplicate rate, variant shift, and feature stability only. Do not publish default AUROC for post-15 October 2025 activations under the 35-day rule; the negatives are not mature by 15 February 2026.

The skeleton’s single split is the explicit version of that design, by case start: train before 1 February 2025, validation from 1 February to 1 May 2025, pre-policy test from 1 May to 1 July 2025, post-policy test from 1 July to 15 October 2025, and post-migration held for shift diagnostics. Start time is the split key so a case has one fold. Regime for interpretation is still \(\tau\), not start, when the path crosses 1 July. Linked transfer ids inherit the earliest start’s fold.

**Metrics, matched to the complete-case target.** Prevalence is 12.5%, so accuracy is not a decision metric. Report AUROC, AUPRC, Brier score, Brier skill against the era-specific base rate (a constant 12.5% predictor has Brier \(0.109\); the base rate may move after the policy change), calibration slope and intercept, and recall and precision at one or two fixed review capacities. Uncertainty is a time-blocked bootstrap, not an iid case bootstrap. Subgroup reports, each with its \(n\): channel, region, `source_system`, requested-amount quartile, and policy era. For the survival sensitivity, use IPCW Brier and cumulative/dynamic AUC at day 90, with censoring weights from the administrative clock only. Do not apply those weights to missing-after-grace partner cases.

**Shift.** Compare prefix-feature distributions across the July policy date and the October migration (population stability or a simple era classifier as a diagnostic). Separate covariate shift from probability shift by looking at calibration slope inside each era. A model can keep its rank order and still be miscalibrated after the threshold change, because the same prefix then has a different probability of being booked and a different booked-account default rate.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

**What the study can claim.** Within an era, and for accounts that the prevailing policy activated and that received a mature feed, the early Credit Review prefix has a measured association with official 90-day default. Temporal backtests can show discrimination, calibration, and capacity-constrained recall under that same selection, including how badly a pre-July model travels across the threshold change. The cohort flow shows how much of the 48,000 is unlabeled and why.

**What it cannot claim.** It cannot claim a default probability for rejected, withdrawn, transferred, or still-open applications. It cannot claim the effect of changing the acceptance rule, the limit, or the collection intensity. \(\eta(x)\) is \(P(D=1\mid X_{\tau}=x, A=1)\) under the historical selection, not \(P(D=1\mid X_{\tau}=x, \mathrm{do}(A=1))\). Underwriters act on documents and notes that are not in \(X\), so similar prefixes among rejected cases are not exchangeable with activated cases. It cannot claim post-migration predictive performance from this extract. It cannot turn a missing partner feed into a non-default. Bounds in the sensitivity are the honest summary for that slice.

**Scoring boundary.** A score computed at first Credit Review and then quoted on every open application is an extrapolation: \(A\) is still unknown, and many of those applications will be rejected. The use that matches the estimand is to store the score and treat it as booked-account risk only if the account is later activated, or to describe it as the historical default rate among similar prefixes that this policy booked. Using it as an automatic decline rule changes the population the next training extract will label.

**Three ways deployment changes the data-generating process.**

1. **Selection feedback.** If the score changes accept, limit, or transfer decisions, the next activated cohort is chosen differently. Future estimates of \(\eta\) are then for a new policy. Retraining on that extract without recording the policy version attributes the new default rate to the prefix.
2. **Control-flow feedback.** If a high score triggers extra document requests, a second reviewer, or a longer queue, later prefixes contain rework and waiting time caused by the model. Those features will look predictive because the model’s own path created them.
3. **Outcome feedback.** If a high score triggers earlier collections contact, the intervention can change whether a default is recorded inside 90 days. The historical association between the prefix and \(D\) is then not the loss rate under the new collections policy. A related channel shift has the same structure: brokers who learn the score can move borderline cases to the partner path, which is exactly where the outcome feed is most often permanently missing, so the labeled sample becomes a function of the score.

## 9. PM4Py-oriented Python implementation skeleton

```python
"""As-of cohort, milestone prefixes, temporal folds, and a leakage gate.

Survival and competing-risk likelihoods connect after this frame
(lifelines or scikit-survival). PM4Py does not fit that model.
Assumption: a mature official feed is an administrative function of
activation time and the extract date. Missingness after the grace
period is not independent censoring.
"""

import pandas as pd
import pm4py

HORIZON_DAYS, FEED_GRACE_DAYS = 90, 35
AS_OF = pd.Timestamp("2026-02-15", tz="UTC")
MILESTONE = "Credit Review"
FORBIDDEN = {
    "Accepted", "Rejected", "Withdrawn", "Transferred",
    "Contract Signed", "Account Activated",
    "Collections Contact", "Default Recorded",
}
LEAKY = [
    "final_limit", "decision_reason", "collection_status", "default_90d",
    "label_available_at", "data_extract_time", "documents_total",
    "resource_experience",
]
ACTIVITY_MAP = {  # versioned crosswalk for the 15 Oct 2025 rename
    "CR Review": "Credit Review",
    "KYC": "Identity/KYC Check",
    "Doc Request": "Request Documents",
}


def load_events(path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    df = pd.read_csv(path)
    for col in ("time:timestamp", "label_available_at", "data_extract_time"):
        if col in df.columns:
            df[col] = pd.to_datetime(df[col], utc=True, errors="coerce")
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(ACTIVITY_MAP)
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    # Exact migration copies only. Do not collapse genuine document loops.
    keys = ["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp", "org:resource"]
    return df.drop_duplicates(keys, keep="first")


def case_table(df: pd.DataFrame, as_of: pd.Timestamp) -> pd.DataFrame:
    seen = df.loc[df["time:timestamp"].le(as_of)]
    out = seen.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
        start_time=("time:timestamp", "min"),
        default_90d=("default_90d", "max"),
        label_available_at=("label_available_at", "min"),
        source_system=("source_system", "first"),
    )
    out["prediction_time"] = (
        seen.loc[seen["concept:name"].eq(MILESTONE)]
        .groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    )
    out["activated_at"] = (
        seen.loc[seen["concept:name"].eq("Account Activated")]
        .groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    )
    names = {"Rejected": "rejected", "Withdrawn": "withdrawn",
             "Transferred": "transferred", "Account Activated": "activated"}
    terminal = (
        seen.loc[seen["concept:name"].isin(names)]
        .groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].first().map(names)
    )
    out["terminal"] = terminal.reindex(out.index).fillna("still_open")
    horizon = out["activated_at"] + pd.Timedelta(days=HORIZON_DAYS)
    grace = horizon + pd.Timedelta(days=FEED_GRACE_DAYS)
    activated = out["terminal"].eq("activated")
    arrived = (out["label_available_at"].notna()
               & out["label_available_at"].le(as_of) & out["default_90d"].notna())
    out["label_mature"] = activated & grace.le(as_of) & arrived
    out["admin_censored"] = activated & horizon.gt(as_of)
    out["feed_delayed"] = activated & horizon.le(as_of) & grace.gt(as_of) & ~arrived
    out["missing_after_grace"] = activated & grace.le(as_of) & ~arrived
    out["y_default_90d"] = pd.Series(pd.NA, index=out.index, dtype="Float64")
    out.loc[out["label_mature"], "y_default_90d"] = out.loc[out["label_mature"], "default_90d"]
    return out


def assign_fold(start: pd.Series) -> pd.Series:
    # Start-time folds. pm4py.split_train_test is a random-case baseline and
    # would mix the July policy break, the October migration, and label lag.
    edges = [pd.Timestamp(s, tz="UTC") for s in (
        "2024-01-01", "2025-02-01", "2025-05-01", "2025-07-01",
        "2025-10-15", "2026-01-01")]
    labels = ["train", "validation", "test_pre_policy",
              "test_post_policy", "post_migration_shift_only"]
    return pd.cut(start, bins=edges, labels=labels, right=False)


def milestone_prefix(df: pd.DataFrame, cases: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    keep = cases.loc[cases["prediction_time"].notna(), ["prediction_time"]]
    m = df.join(keep, on="case:concept:name", how="inner")
    m = m.loc[m["time:timestamp"].le(m["prediction_time"])]
    return m.loc[~m["concept:name"].isin(FORBIDDEN)].copy()


def features_from_prefix(prefix: pd.DataFrame, history: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    # Waiting time among point events already truncated at tau.
    # @@service_time is not handling effort unless start and complete exist.
    timed = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(prefix.copy())
    counts = timed.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
        n_events=("concept:name", "size"),
        n_resources=("org:resource", "nunique"),
        wait_mean=("@@waiting_time", "mean"),
    )
    last = timed.sort_values("time:timestamp").groupby("case:concept:name").tail(1)
    feat = last[["case:concept:name", "case:channel", "case:region",
                 "risk_score", "source_system"]].merge(counts, on="case:concept:name")
    hist = history.sort_values(["org:resource", "time:timestamp"]).copy()
    hist["resource_events_before"] = hist.groupby("org:resource").cumcount()
    at = (hist.groupby(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
          ["resource_events_before"].max().reset_index())
    ends = timed.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].max().reset_index()
    ends = ends.merge(at, on=["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"], how="left")
    feat = feat.merge(ends[["case:concept:name", "resource_events_before"]],
                      on="case:concept:name", how="left")
    return feat.drop(columns=[c for c in LEAKY if c in feat.columns])


def leakage_audit(prefix: pd.DataFrame, feat: pd.DataFrame) -> pd.DataFrame:
    hit_act = sorted(set(prefix["concept:name"]).intersection(FORBIDDEN))
    hit_col = [c for c in LEAKY if c in feat.columns]
    late = int((prefix["time:timestamp"] > prefix["prediction_time"]).sum())
    rows = [
        ("future_activities", ",".join(hit_act) or "none", "fail" if hit_act else "pass"),
        ("leaky_columns", ",".join(hit_col) or "none", "fail" if hit_col else "pass"),
        ("events_after_tau", str(late), "fail" if late else "pass"),
    ]
    return pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=["check", "detail", "status"])


def run(path: str = "applications_events.csv"):
    raw = load_events(path)
    log_df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw, case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
    )
    cases = case_table(log_df, AS_OF)
    cases["fold"] = assign_fold(cases["start_time"])
    prefix = milestone_prefix(log_df, cases)
    # Sensitivity only: fixed length, already cut at the first Credit Review.
    # Traces shorter than 3 are omitted and are not coded as non-defaults.
    k3 = pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(prefix), 3)
    train_ids = cases.index[cases["fold"].eq("train") & cases["label_mature"]]
    train_prefix = prefix[prefix["case:concept:name"].isin(train_ids)]
    dfg, _starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(train_prefix)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(train_prefix)  # train-prefix vocabulary only
    if set(ends).intersection(FORBIDDEN):
        raise RuntimeError("training prefix DFG reached a post-decision activity")
    feat = features_from_prefix(prefix, log_df)
    audit = leakage_audit(prefix, feat)
    if (audit["status"] == "fail").any():
        raise RuntimeError("leakage audit failed")
    y = cases.loc[cases["label_mature"], ["y_default_90d", "fold"]]
    model = feat.merge(y, left_on="case:concept:name", right_index=True, how="inner")
    # model is the binary complete-case frame. For a survival sensitivity,
    # add admin_censored and feed_delayed with censoring at the as-of clock.
    # Do not mark missing_after_grace as censored. Competing risks for
    # reject / withdraw / transfer are a different estimand; build them from
    # `terminal`, not by filling y_default_90d with 0.
    flow = {
        "n_cases": len(cases),
        "by_terminal": cases["terminal"].value_counts().to_dict(),
        "mature": int(cases["label_mature"].sum()),
        "admin_censored": int(cases["admin_censored"].sum()),
        "feed_delayed": int(cases["feed_delayed"].sum()),
        "missing_after_grace": int(cases["missing_after_grace"].sum()),
        "k3_traces": len(k3),
        "train_variants": len(variants),
        "train_dfg_edges": len(dfg),
    }
    print(flow)
    print(audit.to_string(index=False))
    return model, cases, audit
```
