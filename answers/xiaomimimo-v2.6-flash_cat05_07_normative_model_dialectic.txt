```python
def rmi_loop() -> OperatorPOWL:
    """Unbounded, optional Request_More_Info loop.
```

### 1.4 Burden-of-proof approach

**Declaring noncompliance** requires the proponent to establish four elements by a preponderance: **(i)** a rule in force at the event timestamp; **(ii)** the case falls within that rule's scope; **(iii)** the observed behaviour departed from the rule, on evidence reliable enough to be acted on; **(iv)** no valid overlay (pilot/emergency/exception) covers the event. M0 nonconformance is *evidence for (iii) only where M0 actually encodes the rule*. It is neither necessary nor sufficient for compliance — S2 proves the "not sufficient" direction, and SUP-707/SUP-713 prove the "not necessary" direction.

**Accepting an exception** imposes a heavier, affirmative burden on the invoking party: produce the instrument's identity, its issuing authority, an effective window covering the *event timestamp* (not the case's start), predicate eligibility evaluated **at decision time** from a recorded snapshot, execution of the preserved/residual controls, and no unresolved contradiction with the higher rule unless the instrument was issued by the higher rule's owner or a superseding body. Mere practice, good faith, factual emergency, or absence of enforcement never satisfies this burden.

## 3. Defense Brief

### 3.1 M0 is not a description of the process — it is an under-approximation of it

A normative conformance model must be *complete enough that conforming is achievable and deviating is meaningful*. M0 fails both tests.

- **No loop.** 47 observed cases perform `Request_More_Info`, an activity Compliance calls desirable and Operations performs routinely (S6, S7). None of them activated before the requested evidence arrived (S9). Under M0 these are all deviations. A model that reports 47/47 deviation on a control that *worked every single time* is measuring itself, not the process.
- **No notification observability.** From 2026-07-01 the notification activity moved to a CRM service and only ~70% is mirrored (S5). Any conformance run over the onboarding log after that date systematically manufactures "missing `Notify_Requester`" findings. SUP-706 is the exemplar: the CRM holds a message at +3 hours; the deficiency is a **mutable text link**, i.e. an identity/join defect, not an absent notification.
- **No resource rules.** CS-9's requester/approver separation is real and enforceable — but not in POWL. SUP-712 shows the model is blind to it; the defence's corollary is that M0's verdict on SUP-712 is *irrelevant*, in both directions: M0 cannot convict and cannot acquit.

### 3.2 M0's concurrency is being misread

`checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])` imposes **no order** between the two checks. In SUP-708, `Quality_Review` before `Sanctions_Check` is therefore **not** a deviation from M0 and not a breach of CS-9 (which requires both before activation, not in any order). The only genuine breaches in SUP-708 are activation-before-sanctions and activation-before-approval. Precision matters: a prosecution that counts ordering noise inflates its own severity and loses credibility on the two findings that are real.

### 3.3 Overlay governance exists and worked where it was respected

- **SUP-702** is a textbook valid exception: LOW-risk, DE, approved group, inside the window, eligibility fields complete, sanctions first, quality the next business day, CRM notification with a **stable `notification_id`** at +2 hours. Every element of the §1.4 exception burden is met from contemporaneous records.
- **SUP-704** is a textbook emergency override: inside FD-7's window, EUR 18,000 under the EUR 20,000 cap, daily monitoring evidenced, quality and retrospective approval next business day. FD-7 preserved sanctions screening, bounded spend, bounded time, and required ratification — exactly the structural features that distinguish a *governed* override from a *evasion*.
- The observer's two statements are not contradictory once "exception" is separated from "amendment" (§1.2 D1/D2). She approved a monitored exception; she declined to rewrite the standard. Treating that as a nullification of the pilot reads the instrument against its own recorded words.

The defence's central symmetry: **FD-7 and PD-3 are the same kind of object.** If an emergency directive may lawfully relax "quality before activation" for three days, a steering-committee pilot may relax it for two months for a defined low-risk population. Accepting one and rejecting the other requires a principled distinction — and the principled distinction the defence offers is *scope and predicates*, not *validity*. That distinction condemns SUP-703 and SUP-714 while exonerating SUP-702 and SUP-704.

### 3.4 Scope defects: SUP-709 and SUP-710

- **SUP-709:** a lead failed sanctions pre-screen and was rejected without a quality review. The process owner says such leads are intake cases; the model owner says M0 begins after pre-screen acceptance; the scope document is ambiguous (D3). One cannot be noncompliant against a rule whose *subject matter* is undefined. Where the model owner controls the scoping instrument, ambiguity in that instrument is chargeable to the model owner, not to the case.
- **SUP-710:** reactivation of a closed supplier. CS-9 has **no explicit reactivation procedure** (D10). Sanctions screening *was* performed; the prior quality certification was *valid*. Applying an onboarding model to a lifecycle event the standard does not regulate is a category error: the missing `Register_Supplier` is not a missing control, it is a missing *case type*.

### 3.5 Label identity and data lineage: SUP-711, SUP-713, SUP-706

Trace deviation  business misconduct.
- **SUP-713:** procedure and UI renamed `Quality_Review`  `QA_Clearance`; audit documents evidence equivalent content; the mapping was approved a week late. That is label-governance lag. The activity happened; only the symbol drifted. Late ratification is a data-governance weakness to fix, not a process breach.
- **SUP-711:** two `Register_Supplier` rows sharing one source transaction ID are one execution duplicated by migration (S9). Conformance on undeduplicated data produces a phantom violation.
- **SUP-706:** the deficiency is an unauthenticated join (S5). Until `notification_id` linkage is immutable, the case is *unresolvable* — classifying it as noncompliant converts a logging architecture decision into an allegation.

### 3.6 Proportionality and the direction of the error

Operations argues M0's high deviation rate is mostly obsolescence and incomplete scope, and the aggregate supports a large part of that claim: 289/312 auto-approvals eligible, 47 loop cases with zero premature activations, a migration-driven notification gap, a rename, a duplicate, an unregulated reactivation, and an undefined intake boundary. A conformance regime that reports these alongside SUP-703 and SUP-708 as a single undifferentiated "deviation count" will be ignored — and ignored controls are the ones that fail silently.

### 3.7 Defense's required concessions

The pilot does not survive its own expiry window; the emergency directive does not survive its own timestamp; eligibility predicates are not advisory; "we intended to make it permanent" is not a policy; SUP-703 (HIGH-risk, EUR 180,000, ineligible) and SUP-708 (activation before sanctions) are substantive failures that no amount of model criticism touches; separation of duties and COI disclosure are real obligations whose invisibility in M0 is an argument for *adding* them, not for excusing their breach; informal practice has no self-executing legal force.

### 5.2 System-level judgment

**(a) Operational compliance — partially compliant, with two material control failures and one control-drift pattern.**

- The **preventive core** (sanctions before activation) held in 14 of 15 sampled cases and failed once (SUP-708); across the auto-approval population, sanctions preceded activation in all PD-3/FD-7 cases by construction. *This control is substantially effective.*
- **Approval-authority discipline is materially deficient.** In the sample: SUP-703, SUP-714; in the population: 14 clearly ineligible + 6 post-expiry = **20 unauthorized auto-approvals (6.4%)**, plus 9 with unprovable eligibility. This is a real, quantified, material noncompliance segment — not an artifact of model fit.
- **Resource and conflict-of-interest controls are unmonitored.** SUP-712 and SUP-715 conform to every model currently in force and still breach explicit obligations. The organization presently has *no effective detection* for its separation-of-duties or COI-disclosure rules. This is a material deficiency in the *assurance system*, independent of case counts.
- **Evidence integrity degraded measurably:** notification observability fell 2%  29%, links are mutable, eligibility attributes are missing in 9 cases, one activity label drifted without contemporaneous mapping. Roughly **a quarter of post-2026-07-01 control questions cannot be answered from the record as it stands.**
- **Net:** the operation is *not* "materially noncompliant" in the aggregate-volume sense the prosecution's raw deviation counts imply (91% of auto-approvals were covered; 100% of information-request cases improved control), but it *is* materially noncompliant in defined segments — approval authority, one sanctions breach, separation of duties, COI disclosure, and unratified post-expiry practice. Enforcement should be **targeted and remedial**, not blanket.

**(b) Model validity — M0 is not fit for purpose as a normative conformance model and must be superseded before any further bulk conformance reporting.**

M0 fails four independent validity tests: **expressiveness** (no `Request_More_Info` loop although 47 cases and both stakeholder groups require it), **observability** (requires `Notify_Requester` in a log that ceased to reliably contain it on 2026-07-01), **scope** (silent on rejected leads, reactivations, withdrawals, and on where a case begins), and **rule coverage** (omits CS-9's separation-of-duties rule, COI disclosure, eligibility predicates, amount caps and overlay windows). A conformance rate computed against M0 is presently **uninterpretable**: it conflates rule violations with model omissions, logging gaps and label drift. Operations' obsolescence/incomplete-scope argument is therefore **sustained as to the model**, and rejected as to the cases.

### 5.3 Where I reject each side's argument

**Rejected from the prosecution:**
1. **That M0's deviation rate evidences material noncompliance.** Refuted: 47 loop cases, 1 label drift, 1 duplicate, 1 missing-join, 1 undefined-scope case, 1 unregulated reactivation — 56 of the observable deviations are model/data defects, not breaches (S6, S7, S9). Using the raw rate would be evidence laundering.
2. **That PD-3 is void because the compliance observer later declined to approve "weakening CS-9."** The instrument grants a bounded *exception*; her repudiation concerns an *amendment*. Reading her words as nullifying the pilot contradicts her own recorded phrase "monitored exception." SUP-702 and the 289 eligible cases are therefore covered.
3. **That desirable behaviour cannot justify anything.** Correct only in the temporal sense: it cannot retroactively authorize. It *does* shift an affirmative duty onto the model owner to revise M0, and failure to do so after 47 documented cases is itself a governance failing.
4. **That SUP-708's pre-clearance interval is aggravating in itself.** The 40-minute window with no transactions is properly mitigating on realized risk; severity rests on the breach of a preventive control, not on the duration.

**Rejected from the defence:**
1. **That normalization and imminent-approval belief mitigate authority.** They mitigate *culpability* and inform the remedy (prospective ratification), and nothing more. SUP-714 and the six August cases remain noncompliant; "we meant to make it permanent" is the classic precursor to permanent unauthorized practice.
2. **That a factually persisting flood emergency carried FD-7 past its timestamp (SUP-705).** Emergency instruments that self-expire exist precisely so that continuation requires a *deliberate act*. Choosing not to make that act while continuing the behaviour converts an emergency override into an unauthorized one.
3. **That everything unfavourable is a logging problem.** SUP-703 (ineligible HIGH-risk, EUR 180,000) and SUP-708 (activation before sanctions) are substantive, contemporaneous, contextually documented failures. The data critique does not touch them.
4. **That M0's blindness to SUP-712/SUP-715 excuses them.** The model's omission is a defect *of the model*, charged to the model owner; the underlying obligations are explicit in CS-9 and COI policy and were breached. Silence in a diagram is not consent in a standard.
5. **That the scope ambiguity in SUP-709/SUP-710 makes those cases compliant.** It makes them *unenforceable as charged* — a distinct finding. The remedy is a scope ruling, not a finding of compliance; SUP-710 in particular exposes an unfilled procedural gap rated medium severity.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Five layers. Each rule is assigned to the layer that can actually enforce it; putting a rule in the wrong layer is how the current failures arose.

### Layer 1 — Control-flow: revised POWL model **M1** (Section 7)
Governs **what may happen, in what order, concurrently, or not at all**.
- Standard route: concurrent `Sanctions_Check`  `Quality_Review`  `Manual_Procurement_Approval`  `Activate_Supplier`.
- Auto-approval route: `Sanctions_Check` + silent master-data validation  `Auto_Approve`  `Activate_Supplier`  `Quality_Review` (post-activation).
- Emergency route: `Sanctions_Check`  `Activate_Supplier`  {`Quality_Review`, `Manual_Procurement_Approval`} unordered, both retrospective.
- Optional, repeatable `Request_More_Info` **before** the decision (loop).
- `Reject_Supplier` both post-checks and at pre-screen (subject to Layer 5 scope ruling).
- `Notify_Requester`  `Close_Case`, mandatory, in every route.
- **POWL cannot carry:** amounts, dates, risk classes, identity, authority, expiry. Those are *not* encoded here and must never be claimed as enforced by M1.

### Layer 2 — Data-aware eligibility, amount and time rules  **pandas / declarative data checks**
Executed as row-level assertions over the event log joined to eligibility, overlay and CRM tables; each check emits a `rule_id` + `version` + pass/fail + snapshot reference.
- `R-01` `Auto_Approve` permitted only if snapshot: `risk_class = LOW  country = DE  group_status  approved  overlay_id = PD-3  event_ts  [effective_from, effective_to)`.
- `R-02` Emergency activation permitted only if `overlay_id = FD-7  event_ts  window  relief_criticality = TRUE  cumulative_spend  cap`.
- `R-03` `Quality_Review` on auto/emergency routes within N business days of activation (business-calendar aware).
- `R-04` `Auto_Approve` outside any valid overlay window  hard fail (detects the SUP-714 pattern).
- `R-05` Amount thresholds per route; escalating approval above cap.
- `R-06` Label-alias table: `QA_Clearance  Quality_Review` with effective-dated, contemporaneously approved mappings (fixes SUP-713).
- `R-07` Guard for the POWL loop: no `Activate_Supplier` while any `Request_More_Info` item is open (the loop's *shape* is POWL; its *guard* is data).
- `R-08` Deduplicate events by immutable `source_transaction_id` before conformance (fixes SUP-711).
- `R-09` Notification join must use immutable `notification_id`; mutable text links are rejected as evidence (fixes SUP-706).

### Layer 3 — Resource and conflict-of-interest rules  **DECLARE / log-skeleton constraints + HR join**
These are *relational* and *identity* constraints; they are meaningless as POWL ordering.
- `C-01` `never co-occur`-style prohibition: the human resolving from `requester_actor_id` must differ from `approver_actor_id` after **alias resolution against an authoritative identity table** (fixes SUP-712).
- `C-02** `Delegation`: approval by a delegate requires a valid, in-window delegation record (`delegation_id`, principal, scope, expiry).
- `C-03` COI: if `reviewer_actor_id` and `approver_actor_id` share a declared relationship or reporting line, a `COI_Disclosure` event must precede `Manual_Procurement_Approval` (fixes SUP-715).
- `C-04` Log-skeleton: `Register_Supplier` exactly once per case (after dedup); `Close_Case` eventually preceded by `Notify_Requester`; absence of `Activate_Supplier` when final outcome = rejected.
- Alias resolution, delegation records and COI registers are **organizational controls** backed by data joins — not model structure.

### Layer 4 — Policy-overlay governance (pilots, emergencies)  **overlay registry, not model forks**
One normalized registry; **every case carries the `overlay_id` under which it acted**, evaluated at decision time from an immutable snapshot. Mandatory fields: `overlay_id`, `instrument_type` (standard | pilot | emergency), `issuing_authority` + `authority_basis`, `incorporated_into_CS9` (boolean), `effective_from`, `effective_to`, `eligibility_predicate`, `amount_cap`, `controls_relaxed[]`, `controls_preserved[]` (**`Sanctions_Check` before activation is non-waivable in every overlay**), `residual_obligations[]`, `ratification_required_by`, `signoff[]` (including dissent, to record the PD-3 observer dispute rather than lose it), `expiry_behavior` (hard-stop vs. requires-written-extension), `status`.
- **Governance rules:** exceptions never amendments unless `incorporated_into_CS9 = TRUE`; hard expiry with automated blocking of the covered activities after `effective_to`; extension only by a dated written instrument; expiry-triggered control that flags continued use (this alone would have caught all six August cases).
- Organizational control: within 5 business days of expiry, either a permanent amendment is issued or a monitored-conformance review closes the pilot with documented dispositions.

### Layer 5 — Explicit scope rules  **case-type taxonomy with per-type mini-models**
| Case type | Definition | Governing model | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ONBOARD_NEW` | New supplier from registration to activation/rejection | **M1** (Section 7) | Default |
| `INTAKE_REJECTED` | Lead failing pre-screen before formal registration | `register?  Reject_Supplier  Notify  Close` | Resolves SUP-709; process owner's position adopted **only if** the scope document is reissued saying so |
| `REACTIVATION` | Reactivation of a previously closed supplier | `sanctions  (quality_validity_check  activate?)  notify  close`, with a **new CS-9 procedure** defining certification-age limits | Resolves SUP-710; currently an unfilled gap rated medium |
| `WITHDRAWAL` | Requester withdraws before decision | `…  Withdraw  notify  close` | Currently unmodelled entirely |
| `AMENDMENT/MASTER-DATA-CHANGE` | Post-activation changes | separate lightweight model | not an onboarding |

**Layer assignment rationale:** POWL answers *ordering/exclusivity/concurrency*; DECLARE/log-skeleton answers *cross-activity relational prohibitions and cardinalities*; pandas/data checks answer *attributes, time windows, amounts, eligibility, joins*; organizational controls answer *who may authorize an exception, who must disclose a conflict, who owns scope*. M0's central failure was answering every question with layer 1.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

### 7.1 Revised POWL model M1 (executable, no external file access)

```python
# =============================================================================
# M1 - revised normative POWL model for supplier onboarding (PM4Py)
# Constructed entirely in memory; no external files are read.
#
# LAYER 1 ONLY.  Amount, risk, date, identity, delegation, overlay-window and
# conflict-of-interest conditions are NOT expressible in POWL and are enforced
# in Layers 2-4 (see the policy-overlay table and data proposal below).
# =============================================================================
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder, OperatorPOWL, Transition, SilentTransition
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


# ----------------------------------------------------------------- leaf helpers
def t(label):
    """Fresh Transition instance: identical label, distinct node identity.

    Mutually exclusive routes own their own instances so that each route's
    node set is unambiguous when the model is flattened.  Duplicate labels
    across exclusive branches are legitimate: they denote the same business
    activity executed under a different route.
    """
    return Transition(label=label)


def rmi_loop():
    """Optional, unbounded Request_More_Info before the decision.

    PM4Py LOOP(X, Y) executes X and then repeats Y -> X, i.e. its language is
    X (Y X)*.  Taking X = tau gives
        tau (RMI tau)*   ==>   projects to RMI^k for any k >= 0,
    which is exactly "zero or more information requests, then continue".

    LIMITATION (stated, not hidden): a POWL loop cannot bind repetitions to the
    arrival of the requested evidence.  That guard lives in the data layer as
```

### 7.3 Stated limitations of the single-POWL encoding

1. **Unbounded loop guard.** The `LOOP(tau, RMI)` shape yields `RMI^k, k  0`, but POWL cannot require that the requested evidence actually arrived. Enforced by data rule **R-07** (no activation while an RMI item is open). This is the honest approximation; encoding a fixed repetition count would misrepresent practice.
2. **Loop placement is permissive.** `Request_More_Info` is concurrent with the two checks inside Route A (and with sanctions/validation inside Route B), so it may legally appear before `Sanctions_Check` as well as after it. This over-approximates slightly but is required to accept SUP-707's observed order (`Sanctions  RMI  RMI  Quality`) without forcing an artificial order between the checks. The tighter constraint that matters — RMI strictly before activation — is guaranteed because every RMI node is ordered before the approval/activation node of its route.
3. **Master-data validation is .** Its events currently live in a service log not joined to the onboarding log; making it visible now would generate false "missing activity" findings. Promote it to a `Transition` the moment `master_data_validation_id` is joined.
4. **No reactivation or withdrawal branch.** Deliberate: per Layer 5 these are separate case types with their own models. Stuffing them into M1 as extra XOR branches would hide a genuine scope gap (SUP-710) inside a permissive diagram.
5. **No amount, date, risk, identity, delegation, overlay-window or COI logic.** Explicitly out of POWL's expressive power; carried by Layers 2–4. A conformance report produced from M1 fitness alone must never be described as a compliance report.
6. **Duplicate labels across exclusive branches** (`Sanctions_Check` in four routes, `Reject_Supplier` in two) are intentional and standard; conformance algorithms key on labels within a single enabled branch.
7. **Pre-screen reject branch (E) is conditional** on Layer 5 formally reissuing the scope document; until then it should be removed and INTAKE_REJECTED cases routed to their own case type.
