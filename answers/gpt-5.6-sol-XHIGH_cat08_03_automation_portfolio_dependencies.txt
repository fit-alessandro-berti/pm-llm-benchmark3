## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

### Logical dependency structure

```text
A1 registration/data capture
        
A2 product and exposure classification
         KYC branch: A3 triage  A4 mismatch assistance  KYC clearance
         Document branch: A5 completeness  A6 extraction/normalization
         Collateral need/data  credit analysis  A7 valuation routing

KYC clearance + normalized financials + credit notes + valuation if required
        
A8 committee pack  Committee decision
        
A9 contract generation/checking
        
A10 signing
        
A11 activation

A12 is cross-cutting: control evidence, exception routing, four-eyes monitoring,
KYC-before-signing monitoring, and decision-to-contract remediation.
```

This is not a fully serial chain. KYC and financial-document review are often concurrent even though the information system records them sequentially. A workflow redesign should launch and monitor both branches independently after classification and join them at committee packaging. Automating the recorded sequence literally would create artificial serialization.

### Upstream data-quality dependencies

- **A1 and A2 influence every later path.** Duplicate merging and exposure classification determine which KYC, collateral, committee, and four-eyes controls apply. Recommendations can be automated, but uncertain or High-exposure classifications require confirmation.
- **A5 is an important prerequisite for A6.** Extraction cannot compensate for missing statements or unclear customer instructions. The 3,300 requests and 2,800 returns show that document completeness must be addressed before normalization is scaled.
- **A6 is an important prerequisite for A8.** Committee packaging requires reconciled financial artifacts. Auto-assembly before normalization quality improves would automate rework rather than eliminate it.
- **A8 or another structured decision source is a prerequisite for A9.** Contract fields must be generated from the final approved decision, not from analyst notes or an obsolete pack.
- **A9, A10, and A11 require authoritative statuses.** A downstream system must not infer that KYC, signing, or contract controls are complete from document presence alone.

### Legal and compliance controls

- **KYC:** A3 may prioritize hits, and A4 may gather evidence and recommend a resolution, but an accountable compliance role retains final clearance. High-risk hits must not be auto-cleared.
- **Signing:** A10 must consume an authoritative KYC-clearance status. The observed provisional-signing variant must be blocked, not made faster.
- **Four-eyes:** For High-exposure SME cases, the user completing credit analysis must be different from the user completing committee packaging. A8 and A12 must use employee identity and role data, including delegated work during month-end.
- **Contract matching:** A9 should compare generated terms field by field with the final committee decision. Until rules are proven, it should produce a draft and discrepancy report for legal approval.
- **Auditability:** Every recommendation needs input evidence, model/rule version, confidence, human action, override reason, final decision, and timestamps. Compliance responsibility remains with named human owners.

### Human escalation capacity

- A3 without A4 or added capacity may increase the queue for the **three compliance specialists**, especially the two senior specialists identified as network bottlenecks.
- A4 should reduce evidence-search and resolution effort, but it may initially surface more latent mismatches. Rollout must track specialist queue age, not only automated classification accuracy.
- A5 creates more complete and earlier arrivals to credit. It should not be scaled until A6 can absorb normalization work or credit-analysis WIP is controlled.
- A6 releases analysts from data preparation, but demand shifts toward judgment-heavy credit analysis, committee notes, and review of extraction exceptions.
- A12 may increase work for compliance, legal, and policy owners as previously hidden violations and ambiguous rules become visible.
- A9 and A10 can move work to legal ops, customer support, activation, and remediation rather than eliminating it.

### External valuation dependency

A7 cannot remove the external service time. It can:

- identify likely valuation needs earlier, especially for Equipment finance;
- select valuers based on geography, asset type, capacity, and quality history;
- parallelize preparation with KYC and financial review where policy permits;
- monitor lateness and reroute cases before the committee-packaging join.

Formal valuation requests should still wait until sufficient collateral information is stable. Routing faster with poor data would create quality disputes and repeated valuations. Even successful A7 deployment may leave High-exposure cases waiting for the weekly committee.

### Core-banking integration constraints

Only two modules should receive deep core integration in the first six months. For the recommended portfolio:

1. **A12** receives one integration slot for authoritative KYC, committee, contract, role, signing, and remediation statuses.
2. **A4** receives the second slot for controlled access to customer-master and KYC evidence.

During the same period:

- **A5** should use the portal, CRM, and document-management layer without writing core decision fields.
- **A6** should operate in shadow mode or a controlled credit-workbench staging area. Posting normalized values into core/credit systems should occur after month six and only after validation.
- No early integration capacity should be spent on A10 or A11 while upstream control states remain unreliable.

A4 must be scoped as an auditable KYC control module to satisfy the control requirement |

## 4. Expected process-level effects

Effects are directional rather than guaranteed. Customer response, external valuers, committee cadence, and human judgment remain material sources of elapsed time.

### Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A5, A12

- **Cycle time:** Likely modest overall improvement. A1 addresses `Receive  Register` in 11,880 traces, but registration has only an 8-minute service time and 1.5-hour wait. Most cycle-time improvement would come from A5 reducing document loops.
- **Waiting time:** Registration and classification queues should decline. Customer-response delay may decline through clearer instructions and reminders, but the 31-hour mismatch, 20-hour normalization, 58-hour valuation, and 44-hour signing waits remain.
- **Rework:** A2 can reduce misrouting; A5 targets the 3,300 missing-document requests and 2,800 returns; A12 improves handling of `Contract preparation  Remediate control issue` and the 510 returns to contract preparation.
- **Handovers:** Fewer portal-to-clerk and RM-to-back-office contacts. A5 can reduce repeated RM/customer handovers if RMs use the standardized checklist.
- **Resource demand:** Back-office workload falls, but that pool already drops below 65% utilization after 15:00. Earlier intake increases demand on KYC ops, specialists, and credit analysts. A12 adds initial work for control and policy owners.
- **Control violations:** A2 improves routing only if uncertain classifications are reviewed. A12 improves visibility and remediation, but separate hard gates are still needed for KYC-before-signing and High-exposure four-eyes compliance.

### Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A7

- **Cycle time:** Strongest expected reduction in operational elapsed time. A4 addresses cases where mismatch is on the critical path; A5/A6 improve the main financial branch; A7 targets collateral-heavy variants.
- **Waiting time:** Directly addresses the 31-hour mismatch queue, the repeated missing-document/customer wait, the 20-hour wait before normalization, and part of the 58-hour external valuation delay. A7 cannot remove valuer service time, but it can reduce late assignment and idle time.
- **Rework:** A4 should reduce `KYC  mismatch  KYC` cycling, including the 1,160 returns. A5 targets document-review loops, while A6 reduces manual normalization corrections. Better valuation routing may reduce repeated or disputed valuation work.
- **Handovers:** Fewer compliance evidence searches, RM/customer chases, analyst data-preparation handoffs, and collateral-team/valuer coordination contacts.
- **Resource demand:** A6 releases analyst capacity to absorb the cleaner inflow from A5. A4 reduces work per mismatch but may initially increase specialist referrals. A7 transfers some coordination demand to partner monitoring. Faster branch completion can create a new queue at committee packaging or the weekly High-exposure committee.
- **Control violations:** A4 improves KYC evidence and traceability but cannot auto-clear entities. This portfolio does not directly fix the 5.8% four-eyes violation or decision-to-contract mismatch; compensating workflow controls are essential.

### Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12

- **Cycle time:** Moderate improvement, concentrated in KYC exception cases and contract preparation. It does not materially improve the financial-document or collateral branches.
- **Waiting time:** Can reduce the four-hour KYC queue, 31-hour mismatch wait, and 22-hour contract-preparation wait. Committee packaging may still wait for normalized financials or valuation.
- **Rework:** A3/A4 address KYC hits and entity loops. A9/A12 target the 8.5% contract-remediation rate and the 14% rate for Credit-line renewal.
- **Handovers:** KYC evidence and contract discrepancies become more structured, reducing informal specialist/legal handoffs. Conversely, conservative KYC thresholds can create more formal escalation handovers.
- **Resource demand:** The main risk is overloading the three specialists. A9 may smooth legal drafting but push cases sooner to customer signing. A12 initially increases policy clarification and control-investigation work. Analysts remain burdened by normalization.
- **Control violations:** This is the strongest control portfolio. It supports KYC traceability, signing-gate monitoring, contract-to-decision checks, and four-eyes detection. Raw cycle time may initially rise because prohibited provisional signing and incomplete contracts are stopped rather than remediated later.

### Balanced first-year portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A12

- **Cycle time:** Expected to improve broadly because it addresses both a high-impact KYC exception and the financial-document path while avoiding unsafe downstream straight-through processing.
- **Waiting time:** Reduces the 31-hour entity-mismatch wait, document-request delays, and the 20-hour normalization wait. It does not directly eliminate the 58-hour valuation wait or 44-hour signing wait.
- **Rework:** Targets the `KYC  mismatch  KYC` and `Financial review  missing documents  Financial review` loops. A12 structures the `Contract  remediation  Contract` loop and exposes policy causes.
- **Handovers:** Reduces customer/RM/back-office document chasing and analyst data-preparation handoffs. KYC exception handovers become evidence-based rather than dependent on informal access to two senior specialists.
- **Resource demand:** A6 protects analysts from the clean-inflow surge created by A5. A4 may temporarily increase specialist demand as latent mismatches are found. A12 shifts demand to compliance, legal, and policy owners before remediation falls. Committee packaging and legal month-end queues must be monitored as likely next constraints.
- **Control violations:** A12 provides audit and remediation support for the 6.5% provisional-signing variant, the 1.1% requiring remediation, contract mismatch, and the 5.8% High-exposure four-eyes violation. Preventive workflow gates and accountable human approval remain mandatory.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

| risk introduced or amplified | relevant modules | compensating controls |
|---|---|---|
| **Incomplete audit trail** | All | Store input evidence, rule/model version, confidence, suggestion, assigned reviewer, override reason, final decision, and timestamps. Make records immutable and available for case reconstruction |
| **KYC false negatives** | A3, A4 | No autonomous clearance for material hits or mismatches; conservative thresholds; mandatory specialist approval; retrospective sampling; separate validation by entity type and exposure class |
| **False positives and escalation overload** | A3, A4, A12 | Severity-based queues, deduplication, specialist capacity monitoring, aging alerts, and threshold adjustment. Do not judge the model only by sensitivity while ignoring queue congestion |
| **Extraction or classification errors** | A2, A6 | Confidence thresholds, source-to-output reconciliation, visible source citations, mandatory review for High-exposure cases and low-quality PDFs, and fallback to manual processing |
| **Model or rule drift** | A2, A3, A4, A6 | Monitor by Micro/SME/High-exposure, product, document type, language, and source channel; version models; periodic revalidation; champion/challenger testing; rollback capability |
| **Automation bias** | Recommendation modules | Show uncertainty and source evidence rather than only a recommended answer; require meaningful review; analyze override patterns; quality-review both accepted and overridden suggestions |
| **Orphaned exceptions or retry loops** | All | Named exception owner, SLA and aging dashboard, limited automated retries, manual fallback, and explicit closure reasons. Exceptions must remain in the same case history |
| **Garbage-in/garbage-out propagation** | A6, A8, A9, A11 | Schema validation and reconciliation at each handoff; do not treat document presence as evidence of approval; prevent downstream release if required fields conflict |
| **Artificial serialization of concurrent work** | Workflow changes around A3–A7 | Represent KYC and document review as parallel branches with separate start/end events and an explicit join at committee packaging; validate against operational observation, not only DFG order |
| **Shifted bottlenecks** | A1, A5, A6, A7, A9, A10 | WIP limits, controlled release, queue-age monitoring, cross-training, and staged scaling. Track compliance, analyst, committee, legal, signing, and activation queues |
| **Four-eyes violation** | A8, A12 | Compare employee identity across Credit analysis and Committee packaging; role-based assignment restrictions; no self-approval; independent reassignment during month-end; exception reporting to the control owner |
| **Signing before KYC clearance** | A10, A11, A12 | Hard gate against the authoritative KYC status; no local override for provisional signing; abort or return the case to compliance rather than allowing later remediation |
| **Contract terms not matching the decision** | A9, A12 | Field-level comparison to the final approved decision, legal approval for exceptions, controlled template/rule ownership, effective dates, and regression testing for Credit-line renewal |
| **Customer friction or exclusion** | A5, A10 | Co-design instructions with RMs, product-specific checklists, accessible formats, manual assistance, channel choice, measured complaint/abandonment rates, and restraint on reminder frequency |
| **External valuer quality or concentration risk** | A7 | Approved-valuer panel, conflict checks, quality and lateness scorecards, documented routing criteria, appeal/review process, and fallback allocation |
| **Privacy and security exposure** | A3–A6, A10, A12 | Least-privilege access, encryption, data minimization, retention controls, consent where required, and monitoring of bulk document/entity access |
| **Policy ambiguity exposed by automation** | A12, A9 | Named policy owner, decision log, formal interpretation process, and prohibition on encoding unresolved policy as an automated rule |

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

The rollout uses the **balanced portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A12**. Full-production order should be **A12  A4  A6  broad A5 scaling**, although a limited A5 pilot can start early as the visible quick win.

### Phase 0 — Foundation and baseline, month 1

- Correct the process model so KYC and financial review are represented as concurrent branches.
- Establish baselines by product and exposure class for:
  - end-to-end median and tail cycle time;
  - activity waiting time and queue age;
  - `KYC  mismatch  KYC` loop rate;
  - `Financial review  missing documents  Financial review` loop rate;
  - analyst, specialist, committee, and legal WIP;
  - provisional signing, remediation, decision-to-contract mismatch, and four-eyes violations.
- Define the common audit schema for suggestions, overrides, and final decisions.
- Assign exception owners and escalation SLAs.
- Configure mandatory existing-workflow guardrails: KYC before signing and identity-based four-eyes checks. These are control prerequisites, not an additional automation module.

**Gate:** Compliance and process owners approve control definitions, authoritative status sources, exception ownership, and baseline measurement quality.

### Phase 1 — Control foundation and limited customer pilot, months 2–3

- Deploy **A12** first for control evidence, exception routing, and remediation analysis.
- Use the first deep-integration slot for A12’s authoritative status and employee-role feeds.
- Pilot **A5** with selected Micro and SME working-capital cases through portal/document-management interfaces, not core banking.
- Co-design checklists and reminder wording with RMs; retain assisted and manual channels.
- Start **A6** in offline shadow mode to collect document-quality and extraction-error data without writing production financial values.

**Gate criteria:**

- Every A12 event has a complete suggestion/action/final-outcome trail.
- No increase in signing-before-KYC or four-eyes violations.
- A5 shows lower repeated-request behavior without unacceptable complaint, abandonment, or RM work-around rates.
- Downstream analyst queue age does not materially deteriorate; otherwise keep the A5 pilot throttled.

### Phase 2 — KYC bottleneck and extraction validation, months 4–6

- Deploy **A4** in assist-only mode for entity mismatches.
- Use the second and final first-half deep-integration slot for controlled customer-master and KYC-evidence access.
- Require specialist approval for every substantive resolution; do not allow autonomous entity merge or KYC clearance.
- Validate A6 by document type, product, exposure, accounting format, and PDF quality.
- Keep A5 volume limited until A6 and analyst capacity are ready.

**Gate criteria:**

- A4 reduces evidence-search or queue time without exceeding compliance’s false-negative tolerance.
- Specialist queue age and dependency on the two senior specialists improve or at least do not worsen.
- No unauthorized entity merge or automated clearance occurs.
- A6 field accuracy, reconciliation, and exception detection meet risk-owner thresholds across difficult documents, not only clean PDFs.

### Phase 3 — Human-in-the-loop normalization and controlled scaling, months 7–9

- Move **A6** into production with human confirmation before normalized figures feed credit analysis.
- Integrate A6 more deeply only after the first-six-month restriction expires.
- Scale A5 only after A6 is absorbing the cleaner inflow and credit-analysis waiting time remains controlled.
- Expand A4 to additional entity types after segment-level validation.
- Use A12 findings to resolve ambiguous contract, KYC, and assignment policies.

**Gate criteria:**

- The `Financial review  Normalize  Credit analysis` path shows lower waiting/rework.
- Analyst time shifts from transcription to credit judgment without a sustained rise in the 28-hour wait before credit analysis.
- Extraction corrections do not create downstream credit or committee rework.
- Control-violation rates do not worsen as throughput increases.

### Phase 4 — Stabilize, optimize, and prepare the next portfolio, months 10–12

- Scale the four modules only across segments that passed their gates.
- Rebalance staffing and schedules using observed demand:
  - redeploy back-office capacity toward exception support rather than assuming headcount removal;
  - reduce dependence on two senior compliance specialists through documented evidence packs and cross-training;
  - prepare committee and legal capacity for more regular clean inflow.
- Use manual/operational measures for collateral in year one: earlier identification where policy permits, valuer SLA monitoring, and visibility of aged external waits.
- Decide whether **A7** or **A9** should be the next module. A7 is favored if Equipment-finance tail time remains dominant; A9 is favored if contract mismatches remain the principal control/rework issue.

### Monitoring dashboard

Monitor by product and exposure class, not only in aggregate:

- median and tail end-to-end cycle time;
- active service time versus waiting time;
- critical-path incidence of entity mismatch and valuation;
- loop counts for KYC mismatch, missing documents, and contract remediation;
- first-pass document and normalization quality;
- specialist and analyst queue age, WIP, utilization, and overtime;
- committee packaging wait and weekly-committee carryover;
- legal month-end backlog;
- provisional signing, KYC-before-signing breaches, four-eyes violations, and decision-to-contract mismatches;
- recommendation acceptance, override, false-positive/false-negative, drift, and exception-aging measures;
- customer complaints, portal abandonment, and reminder opt-outs.

## 7. Final recommendation

Adopt the **balanced first-year portfolio: A4, A5, A6, and A12**.

- **A4** is justified despite low frequency because entity mismatch carries a 31-hour wait, a 38% exception rate, and is on the critical path in 82% of affected cases.
- **A5** removes a broad rework mechanism rather than merely shortening one task.
- **A6** prevents the cleaner inflow from A5 from overwhelming the central nine-person analyst pool and improves a prerequisite for credit analysis and committee packaging.
- **A12** meets the control-strengthening requirement and addresses the observed provisional-signing, contract-remediation, and four-eyes problems before downstream automation is attempted.

Do **not** make A1 the first full-scale automation merely because registration is the highest-frequency candidate. Registration is already in position 2 for 98% of cases, has limited service and waiting time, and is handled by a back-office pool with afternoon slack. Its principal effect would be to accelerate cases into classification, KYC, and document/analyst queues. That creates visible labor savings without necessarily improving end-to-end performance.

A5 can provide management with a feasible, customer-visible quick win, but it should be piloted rather than scaled until A6 and analyst capacity are ready. A7 is the most important deferral because of the 58-hour valuation wait, but its benefit is concentrated in collateral variants and remains dependent on external partners. A9, A10, and A11 should also be deferred: automating contracts, signing, or activation before upstream KYC, decision, and role controls are reliable would make control failures faster rather than make the process better.
