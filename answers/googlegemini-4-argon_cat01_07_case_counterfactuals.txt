## 1. Feasibility model

```text
PARTIAL-ORDER RELATIONS (Precedence: X -> Y means X must complete before Y starts)
1. Register -> Triage
2. Triage -> Identity Check
3. Triage -> Document Check
4. Identity Check -> Assessment
5. Document Check -> Assessment
6. Assessment (final) -> Decision
7. Decision -> Notify

CONDITIONAL PRECEDENCE & CONCURRENCY
- If Originals Required = True:
    Triage -> Originals Receipt -> Document Check
    (Identity Check || Originals Receipt is permitted)
- If Originals Required = False:
    Identity Check || Document Check (concurrent execution permitted after Triage)
- If High Risk = True OR High Value = True:
    Assessment (latest) -> Compliance Review (final) -> Decision
- Advisory / Preliminary Overlaps:
    Triage -> Escalation Advice (S1) [may run || with Identity/Document/Assessment]
    Triage -> Preliminary Compliance Screen (C1) [may run || with Identity/Document/Assessment]
```

```text
CONDITIONAL AND DECLARATIVE CONSTRAINTS
C1 (Rework Loop): If Assessment_i or Compliance Review_i issues 'Request More Info' (RMI_i), then:
    RMI_i -> Document Check_{i+1} -> Assessment_{i+1} -> Decision.
C2 (Compliance Reaffirmation): If (High Risk OR High Value) AND RMI_i occurs, a new or explicitly reaffirmed Final Compliance Review must occur after Assessment_{i+1} and before Decision.
C3 (Four-Eyes Rule): Resource(Assessment_final) != Resource(Compliance Review_final).
C4 (Preliminary Screen Non-Substitution): Preliminary Compliance Screen does not satisfy C2; Final Compliance Review must still occur after Assessment_final.
C5 (Resource Mutex): For any resource R in {T1, T2, I1, I2, D1, D2, A1, A2, C1, S1, M1}, active service intervals cannot overlap in time.
C6 (Substantive Resolution): Material discrepancies surfaced in Assessment cannot be silently ignored; if rework is suppressed, Decision must reflect unresolved risk (reject or conditional approval).
```

**Classification of Interventions:**
* **K-411 (Parallelize Verification)** alters **timing and task-release concurrency only**: the resource assignment and activity set remain identical, removing an artificial technical lock between `Identity Check` and `Document Check`.
* **K-104 (Earlier Escalation/Compliance Prep)** alters **timing and preparatory control-flow**: it shifts `Escalation Advice` earlier, adds a `Preliminary Compliance Screen` in parallel with core checks, and changes queue scheduling via slot reservation, while keeping final authority intact.
* **K-219 (Different Document Resource)** alters **resource routing** (substituting D2 for D1), which probabilistically induces a **control-flow alteration** if D2 triggers a rework loop (`Request More Info` $\rightarrow$ `Document Check 2` $\rightarrow$ `Assessment 2`).
* **K-337 (Suppress Second Rework Loop)** alters both **control-flow and the allowed decision policy**: it truncates the iterative verification loop and forces `Compliance Review` and `Decision` to adjudicate an unresolved evidentiary discrepancy rather than awaiting full external reconciliation.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
**Factual mechanism:** In the factual trace, `Triage` (T1, finishing Mon 08:50) noted a sanctions ambiguity on a complex, high-risk/high-value case but did not escalate it. The case waited in `Identity Check` (I1, completing Mon 12:50) and a long `Document Check` queue for specialist D1 (starting Mon 21:40, completing Tue 05:40). Senior assessor A1 started at Tue 15:10, finished at Tue 19:10, and only then triggered `Escalation Advice` (S1, Tue 19:20–21:20). Because the case entered C1's queue cold at Tue 21:20—after two urgent compliance cases had inflated the backlog—`Compliance Review` did not start until Wed 13:10 and took 4.5 hours from scratch, delaying `Decision` and `Notify` to Wed 18:40.

**Minimal intervention:** At `Triage` completion (Mon 08:50), T1 immediately routes a preliminary escalation packet to S1 and books a provisional `Compliance Review` slot with C1 for Tuesday evening, while `Identity Check` (I1) and `Document Check` (D1) proceed exactly as in the factual trace.

**Temporal propagation:** I1 (Mon 10:40–12:50) and D1 (Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40) are unchanged, so the case still becomes ready for `Assessment` at Tue 05:40, and A1 still executes `Assessment` from Tue 15:10 to Tue 19:10 (or finishes 30–60 minutes earlier, at Tue 18:10–18:40, because S1's sanctions memo is already attached). Meanwhile, S1 completes `Escalation Advice` on Monday morning (e.g., Mon 09:30–11:30), and C1 performs a 1.5–2 hour `Preliminary Compliance Screen` during a gap on Tuesday morning. Because S1's advice is already complete when A1 finishes at Tue 19:10, the 2-hour post-assessment delay (Tue 19:10–21:20) is eliminated. Furthermore, because C1 reserved a provisional slot and already digested the corporate structure during the preliminary screen, final `Compliance Review` starts between Tue 19:30 and Wed 08:00 (rather than Wed 13:10) and requires only 2.0–3.0 hours instead of 4.5 hours. `Decision` (M1) and `Notify` (M1) then complete by Wed 09:00–11:30.

**Process-feasibility check:** Rules 1–3 and 8 are preserved (`Register` $\rightarrow$ `Triage` $\rightarrow$ `[I1 || D1]` $\rightarrow$ `Assessment` $\rightarrow$ `Compliance Review` $\rightarrow$ `Decision` $\rightarrow$ `Notify`). Rule 11 (advisory overlap) explicitly permits S1 to advise concurrently with I1/D1. Rule 10 is respected because C1's preliminary screen does not replace the final `Compliance Review`, which still occurs strictly after A1's final `Assessment` (Rule 5). The four-eyes rule (Rule 9) holds because A1 performs `Assessment` and C1 performs `Compliance Review`.

**Best/base/worst case:**
* *Best case (-16.5 h, completes Wed 02:10 or Wed 08:40 business hours):* S1's memo saves A1 30 minutes (finishes Tue 18:40); C1 starts the reserved slot immediately at Tue 19:00, finishes in 2.5 hours (Tue 21:30), and M1 signs first thing Wednesday morning (08:30–08:40) or via night shift.
* *Base case (-9.5 h, completes Wed 09:10):* A1 finishes at Tue 19:10; C1 starts final review at Tue 20:00 (or Wed 06:00), taking 2.5 hours; M1 completes `Decision` and `Notify` by Wed 09:10.
* *Worst case (-5.5 h, completes Wed 13:10):* Early packet at Triage lacks key corporate ownership details only uncovered by D1 at Tue 05:40, limiting preliminary C1 savings to 1 hour, and Monday's urgent compliance arrivals push C1's reserved slot to Wed 09:00–12:30.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If historical logs show that sanctions ambiguities identified at Triage are false positives >70% of the time (Until D1 parses the corporate registry), or if C1 operates under strict non-preemptive FIFO where Monday's two urgent compliance cases legally override provisional reservations, early escalation would waste S1/C1 capacity without advancing Wednesday's completion.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:** K-219 is a standard, non-high-risk case requiring physical originals, with difficult handwriting. `Originals Receipt` occurred at Mon 17:30 (while `Identity Check` by I2 finished earlier at Mon 16:20). Because T2 routed the case to specialist D1, the case sat in D1's heavy Monday backlog for 16 hours (Mon 17:30 to Tue 09:30). D1 then completed a clean 4-hour check (Tue 09:30–13:30), followed by a 6.5-hour wait for A2 (`Assessment` Tue 20:00–22:30) and overnight wait for M1 (`Decision`/`Notify` Wed 08:30–09:00).

**Minimal intervention:** Reassign `Document Check` from D1 to generalist D2 immediately upon `Originals Receipt` at Mon 17:30.

**Temporal propagation:** At Mon 17:30, the Monday 16:00 snapshot shows D2's queue at ~5 hours (versus D1's 11 hours). Routing to D2 allows `Document Check` to begin around Mon 22:00–22:30 instead of Tue 09:30 (an 11-hour head start). However, D2 is slower on difficult handwriting: whereas D1 took 4 hours, D2 requires 5.5–7.5 hours (per guidance: 4–8 h for standard, trending high due to handwriting). Thus, D2 finishes between Tue 04:00 and Tue 06:00—still 7.5 to 9.5 hours earlier than D1's factual Tue 13:30 completion. Because `Identity Check` (I2) already finished at Mon 16:20, the case enters A2's queue at Tue 04:00–06:00. Snapshot data at Tue 08:00 shows A2's queue is 6 hours. A2 therefore starts `Assessment` around Tue 11:00–13:00 (instead of Tue 20:00) and finishes in 2.5 hours (Tue 13:30–15:30).
* *Branch A (Clean read):* M1 signs `Decision` and `Notify` on Tuesday afternoon (Tue 16:30–19:00), avoiding the overnight wait to Wednesday morning.
* *Branch B (Handwriting clarification triggered):* At Tue 14:30, A2 (or D2) issues `Request More Info`. External response takes 12–24 hours (arriving Wed 02:30–14:30), requiring `Document Check 2` (3–5 h) and `Assessment 2` (2 h), pushing `Decision` to Thursday morning or afternoon.

**Process-feasibility check:** Rule 4 is satisfied (`Originals Receipt` at Mon 17:30 precedes D2's `Document Check` at Mon 22:00, while I2's `Identity Check` legally ran earlier from Mon 14:20–16:20). Rule 2 is satisfied (`I2` and `D2` both finish by Tue 06:00 before A2 starts). Because the case is neither high-risk nor high-value, `Compliance Review` is not required (Rule 5). If Branch B occurs, Rule 7 is obeyed by executing both `Document Check 2` and `Assessment 2` after `Request More Info`.

**Best/base/worst case:**
* *Best case (-14.0 h, completes Tue 19:00):* D2 starts at Mon 22:00, finishes in 5.5 hours (Tue 03:30) with no ambiguity; A2 assesses from Tue 11:00–13:30; M1 completes `Decision` and `Notify` by Tue 19:00.
* *Base case (-11.5 h, completes Tue 21:30, or Wed 08:30 if M1 is day-shift only):* D2 takes 6.5 hours (finishes Tue 05:00), A2 finishes at Tue 15:30, and M1 signs before end of day Tuesday (or first thing Wednesday, yielding a 0.5 h elapsed gain but freeing D1).
* *Worst case (+28.0 h, completes Thu 13:00):* D2 misreads or flags illegible handwritten figures; A2 issues `Request More Info` at Tue 15:00; external reply arrives Wed 11:00; second document and assessment checks push `Decision` and `Notify` to Thu 13:00.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Empirical error/rework logs showing that D2's `Request More Info` rate on handwritten documents exceeds ~25%–30% (since a 30% chance of a +28 h penalty outweighs a 70% chance of a -12 h gain in expected value and resource consumption).

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:** K-337 is a high-risk, high-value case requiring originals. After `Document Check 1` (D1) and `Assessment 1` (A1) identified inconsistent supplier totals, `Request More Info 1` ran from Wed 11:30 to Thu 08:00. `Document Check 2` (D1, Thu 12:00–17:00) and `Assessment 2` (A1, Fri 08:00–10:30) found that one minor or secondary inconsistency remained. Issuing `Request More Info 2` at Fri 10:30 trapped the case over the weekend until Mon 09:00, requiring a third D1 check (Mon 13:00–16:30), a third A1 assessment (Tue 08:00–10:30), `Compliance Review` (C1, Tue 15:30–19:30), and `Decision`/`Notify` on the second Wednesday (Wed2 08:30–09:10).

**Minimal intervention:** At the completion of `Assessment 2` (Fri 10:30), prohibit `Request More Info 2` and route the case directly to `Compliance Review` (C1) and `Decision` (M1) with the unresolved supplier discrepancy documented in the assessment file.

**Temporal propagation:** Up to Fri 10:30 (`Assessment 2` completion), the timeline is identical to the factual trace. Eliminating `Request More Info 2`, `Document Check 3`, and `Assessment 3` removes the entire Fri 10:30 to Tue 10:30 interval (96 hours of elapsed time, including the weekend). At Fri 10:30, the case enters C1's queue. Extrapolating from the Thursday 08:00 snapshot (where C1 queue was down to 5 hours under normal load), C1's queue on Friday morning is ~3–5 hours. C1 begins `Compliance Review` around Fri 13:30–15:30. However, because the file contains an unresolved supplier discrepancy, C1 cannot perform a routine sign-off; C1 must evaluate whether the discrepancy breaches risk tolerance or can be mitigated via a conditional covenant, taking 4.5–6.0 hours (finishing Fri 18:00–21:30). M1 then executes `Decision` and `Notify` either late Friday evening (if urgent desk coverage exists) or Monday morning (Mon 08:30–09:10).

**Process-feasibility check:** Rules 1–4 and the first rework loop (Rule 7 for `RMI 1` $\rightarrow$ `Document Check 2` $\rightarrow$ `Assessment 2`) are already satisfied in the prefix up to Fri 10:30. Routing from `Assessment 2` (A1) directly to `Compliance Review` (C1) and then `Decision` (M1) satisfies Rule 5 (`Compliance Review` after latest `Assessment` and before `Decision`) and Rule 9 (four-eyes rule: A1 $\neq$ C1). Crucially, under the intervention's normative constraint, the discrepancy cannot be silently ignored: M1's decision shifts from a clean `Approve` to either `Approve with conditions` (e.g., escrow holdback/audit covenant covering the discrepancy) or `Reject`.

**Best/base/worst case:**
* *Best case (-111.0 h, completes Fri 18:10):* C1 starts at Fri 13:00, drafts a standard holdback condition in 4.5 hours (Fri 17:30), and M1 issues a conditional approval and notification before Friday close at 18:10.
* *Base case (-96.0 h, completes Mon 09:10):* C1 finishes review late Friday evening (Fri 20:00); M1 reviews the contested file on Monday morning and issues `Reject` or `Approve with conditions` by Mon 09:10 (saving exactly 48 business/elapsed hours across Mon–Wed).
* *Worst case (-72.0 h elapsed, but negative business value):* C1 and M1 reject the case on Mon 09:10 due to zero-tolerance compliance rules on unverified high-risk supplier totals; the client files a formal appeal with the corrected supplier schedule on Monday afternoon, spawning a new case or administrative appeal that consumes more total work hours than `RMI 2` would have.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Audit or credit-loss records showing that unresolved secondary supplier discrepancies correlate strongly with fraud/sanctions evasion, or commercial data showing that conditional approvals/rejections on high-value accounts lead to high client attrition or costly manual appeals.

### K-411
**Factual mechanism:** K-411 is a standard digital case (no originals, not high-risk/high-value). At `Triage` completion (Tue 09:35), a legacy system limitation spawned only the `Identity Check` task and placed a case lock on the file during I2's execution (Tue 11:30–13:20). Consequently, the `Document Check` task was not even created until Tue 13:20. D2 started at Tue 15:00 and finished at Tue 19:30, missing the Tuesday daytime assessment window and entering A2's growing queue overnight (`Assessment` Wed 09:30–12:00, `Decision`/`Notify` Wed 14:20–14:30).

**Minimal intervention:** At `Triage` completion (Tue 09:35), spawn both verification tasks simultaneously and apply a controlled case-lock workaround so I2 and D2 can access the case concurrently with mandatory real-time timestamp logging.

**Temporal propagation:** I2's timeline (`Identity Check` Tue 11:30–13:20) is unchanged. For D2, entering the Document queue at Tue 09:35 instead of Tue 13:20 means facing the Tuesday 08:00 snapshot queue (~4 hours for D2), or slightly less depending on when the 1.67-hour wait observed after 13:20 originated. Specifically, in the factual trace, D2 was free to start K-411 at Tue 15:00; if K-411 had been in D2's queue since Tue 09:35, it would have jumped ahead of document tasks that arrived between 09:35 and 13:20, allowing D2 to start between Tue 12:30 and Tue 13:30. With a 4.5-hour service time, D2 finishes between Tue 17:00 and Tue 18:00 (1.5 to 2.5 hours earlier than the factual Tue 19:30). Since I2 already finished at Tue 13:20, both prerequisites for `Assessment` are met at Tue 17:00–18:00. Entering A2's queue 1.5–2.5 hours earlier moves K-411 ahead of late-Tuesday arrivals. Instead of starting `Assessment` at Wed 09:30, A2 starts earlier on Wednesday morning (e.g., Wed 07:30–08:00) or late Tuesday night, finishing `Assessment` by Wed 10:00–10:30 and `Decision`/`Notify` by Wed 11:00–12:00.

**Process-feasibility check:** Because `Originals Required = no`, Rule 3 explicitly allows `Identity Check` and `Document Check` to execute concurrently. Rule 2 is satisfied because `Assessment` waits for `max(complete(I2), complete(D2))` = Tue 17:00–18:00. Rule 11 (resource mutex) holds because I2 and D2 are distinct resources; only the software case lock prevented concurrency.

**Best/base/worst case:**
* *Best case (-18.0 h, completes Tue 20:30, or -6.0 h at Wed 08:30):* D2's morning queue clears early, allowing D2 to start at Tue 11:00 and finish at Tue 15:30; A2 picks up the case at Tue 17:30–20:00, and M1 signs at Tue 20:30 (or Wed 08:30).
* *Base case (-3.0 h, completes Wed 11:30):* D2 starts at Tue 13:00 (overlapping I2's last 20 minutes and avoiding the 1.8 h post-lock delay), finishes at Tue 17:30; A2 assesses from Wed 08:00–10:30, and M1 finishes `Notify` at Wed 11:30.
* *Worst case (0 h to +1.0 h, completes Wed 14:30–15:30):* D2 was completely occupied until Tue 15:00 by pre-09:35 arrivals anyway (so earlier task creation merely increases measured queue wait from 1.7 h to 5.4 h without changing D2's 15:00 start), OR concurrent editing via the workaround causes a save conflict at Tue 13:20 that forces D2 to re-enter notes.

**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** Queue-log reconstruction showing that all tasks served by D2 between Tue 09:35 and Tue 15:00 had already entered D2's queue before Tue 09:35 (meaning FIFO order wouldn't change), or IT incident logs showing that bypassing the legacy case lock corrupts audit trails or overwrites fields.

### 100-Case Weekly Rollout Analysis
Scaling these four interventions from single cases to 100 cases per week fundamentally changes system behavior because resources **C1**, **D1**, and **A1/A2** operate near saturation during Monday–Tuesday peaks.

Under a 100-case rollout of **K-104**, reserving provisional slots on **C1** (the sole certified compliance officer, already facing a 20-hour Monday queue) causes severe queue displacement. Because upstream `Document Check` (D1) and `Assessment` (A1) times have high variance, many cases will miss their reserved C1 slots. Holding C1 idle for delayed cases wastes scarce bottleneck capacity, while false-positive escalations from Triage waste preliminary screening hours. Unreserved urgent cases (such as Monday afternoon's urgent arrivals) are pushed into multi-day delays.

For **K-219**, diverting 100 handwritten cases from **D1** to **D2** relieves D1's complex-case backlog, helping cases like K-104. However, D2 takes ~2–3 hours longer per case than D1 (consuming 200–300 additional resource-hours across 100 cases) and triggers `Request More Info` loops at a higher rate. Each rework loop adds a second `Document Check` and `Assessment`, quickly overwhelming D2 and starving digital cases like K-411.

In **K-337**, suppressing the second rework loop across 100 cases saves substantial **D1** and **A1** capacity and eliminates the **weekend external-response trap** (where Friday RMIs sit until Monday). Yet this shifts the bottleneck directly onto **C1**: instead of waiting externally over the weekend, dozens of contentious, unresolved files flood C1 on Friday afternoon, each requiring longer legal/compliance scrutiny and generating downstream customer appeals.

Finally, rolling out **K-411**'s parallel task creation across 100 cases eliminates artificial hold time, causing cases to complete verification 2–4 hours earlier. However, this causes **bottleneck migration** straight into the **Assessment queue (A1/A2)**—which is already constrained on Tuesdays due to an assessor in training. Moreover, if operators fail to maintain strict **real-time timestamp discipline** under heavy load and revert to batch logging when closing the workaround, the event log will display illusory concurrency or zero-duration steps without delivering real throughput gains.

## 7. Final synthesis

The **most robust intervention is K-411 (parallelizing verification via a controlled case-lock workaround)**. Because it removes an artificial technical serialization between `Identity Check` and `Document Check` without altering resource skill matching, compliance rules, or decision standards, its downside risk is strictly bounded to technical execution. Even when downstream congestion in the `Assessment` queue absorbs part of the local time savings on busy Tuesdays, parallel release smooths D2 utilization and avoids pushing cases across overnight boundaries. **K-219 (routing handwritten standard documents to D2)** ranks second as a selective policy—valuable when D1's backlog is extreme, provided Triage enforces a legibility and queue-differential gate.

Conversely, **K-337 (prohibiting the second rework loop) is the most dangerous intervention despite offering the largest raw speed gain (~4 days)**. Suppressing `Request More Info 2` does not resolve the underlying evidentiary defect; it forces `Compliance Review` and `Decision` to either absorb unverified risk on high-value/high-risk files or reject legitimate clients who then re-enter the system via costly appeals.

**Local counterfactuals are least transportable to a system-wide policy in K-104 (early escalation with C1 slot reservation) and K-219**. In a single-case view, reserving a slot on C1 looks nearly free; across 100 cases, reserving time on a single-server bottleneck ($C1$) with a 20-hour Monday backlog and high upstream D1/A1 variance creates idle reservation blocks and starves unreserved urgent cases. Similarly, routing one case to D2 saves 12 hours, but routing 100 cases slows the system down because D2 requires more service hours per case and generates compounding rework loops.

To move from plausible ranges to sharp operational predictions, the **most valuable additional data** are: (1) **task-ready/queue-entry timestamps** (distinct from service start times), (2) **shift schedules and working-hours calendars** (to separate overnight off-shift hours from active queue delay), and (3) **structured rework reason codes** linking `Request More Info` events to specific document defects.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. **`temporal` — Off-shift vs. active queue intervals:** We assume that overnight gaps in the factual logs (e.g., K-219 waiting for M1 from Tue 22:30 to Wed 08:30, or K-337 waiting for A1 until 08:00) reflect standard shift boundaries for A1, A2, C1, and M1, whereas D1 operates extended/night shifts (evidenced by Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40 in K-104); thus, finishing an upstream step before ~17:00 can save an entire overnight cycle.
2. **`structural` — Advisory and preliminary non-blocking concurrency:** We assume that S1's `Escalation Advice` and C1's `Preliminary Compliance Screen` in K-104 can read case files concurrently with I1 and D1 without triggering the legacy case-lock mechanism observed in K-411.
3. **`resource` — Preemption and reservation rules on C1:** We assume that a provisional slot reservation on C1 (K-104) grants priority over standard compliance backlog at the scheduled window, but requires holding a buffer that reduces C1's effective utilization when upstream D1/A1 completions jitter.
4. **`quality` — Skill-dependent rework probability in K-219:** We assume that D1's zero-rework outcome on K-219 was partly attributable to D1's specialist proficiency, so routing the same handwritten documents to generalist D2 increases both active service duration (from 4 h to 5.5–7.5 h) and the probability of issuing `Request More Info`.
5. **`behavioral` — Operator compliance with real-time logging:** In K-411, we assume that operators using the manual lock-bypass workaround will log task starts and completions in real time rather than hoarding completed work and batch-updating the system at the end of their shift.
6. **`structural` — Substantive compliance scrutiny under incomplete evidence:** In K-337, we assume that C1 and M1 are bound by regulatory duty and cannot rubber-stamp an unresolved high-risk supplier discrepancy; consequently, suppressing `Request More Info 2` lengthens C1's review time (4.5–6 h) and forces a conditional approval or rejection.
7. **`interference` — FIFO queue discipline within resource pools:** In K-411 and K-219, we assume that D2 and A2 process standard cases in approximate First-In-First-Out (FIFO) order, meaning that entering a queue 2–4 hours earlier genuinely advances service start time only if competing arrivals entered the queue during that 2–4 hour window.
8. **`behavioral` — External client response to adverse/conditional decisions:** In K-337, we assume that high-value clients who receive a rejection or onerous holdback condition due to a truncated verification loop have a non-trivial probability of disputing the decision or re-submitting, transferring workload from the `Request More Info` loop to an appeals channel.
