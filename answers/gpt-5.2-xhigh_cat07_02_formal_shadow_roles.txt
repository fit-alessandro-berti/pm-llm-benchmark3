# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

- **Automated intake & entitlement precheck (R14)**  
  - **Core**: A1/A2 at very high frequency; always at the start of dominant variants.  
  - **Peripheral**: None (by design).  
  - **Interpretation**: Treat as an automation boundary role; it shapes workload distribution but does not represent human capacity, training, or authority.

- **Tier‑1 front-office service (R01/R02)**  
  - **Core**: A4 and A12 (high-volume completion), plus A2 when automation is insufficient/overridden.  
  - **Peripheral**: Small but meaningful A8 (negotiation) and A7 (pricing touch) for Amira; light A3/A5 indicates “triage” before escalation to Tier‑2.  
  - **Complexity/authority**: Mostly low/medium; Amira’s higher complexity and negotiation suggests informal case ownership on harder cases even without formal approval rights.

- **Seasonal Tier‑1 closure support (R11/R12)**  
  - **Core**: A1/A2/A4/A12 in low-complexity flows.  
  - **Peripheral**: Almost no A3/A5/A10/A11; this absence is a capability/permission signal, not just preference.  
  - **Substitution signal**: Reopen spike tied to entitlement notes indicates that “Validate Entitlement” quality matters more than count; their A2 frequency should be interpreted alongside rework.

- **Tier‑2 technical resolution (R03/R04)**  
  - **Core**: A3/A5/A10; these define technical throughput and time-to-resolution for non-trivial variants.  
  - **Peripheral**: Chen’s A6 involvement and higher reopen/KB suggests senior “security-aware” troubleshooting; Dina’s lower complexity indicates a narrower or more supervised technical scope.  
  - **Complexity/authority**: High complexity exposure without formal approval authority; decisions are technical, not governance.

- **Security risk gate & critical intervention (R05)**  
  - **Core**: A6; this is a control point that can become an SLA bottleneck under incident load.  
  - **Peripheral**: A10/A11/A14 are consistent with “assist + assure + document” in critical incidents.  
  - **Substitution/triage**: Mateo’s filtering shows that referral policy (who gets sent to security) is part of the security workload model; fewer A6 events can be good (less noise) or bad (delayed criticals).

- **Commercial exceptions & negotiation (R06)**  
  - **Core**: A7/A8; commercial throughput is visible and sensitive to absence (duration doubled under Giorgio’s coverage).  
  - **Peripheral**: Some A9 indicates that pricing exceptions sometimes require formal sign-off; occasional A2 suggests preconditions/entitlement checks embedded in pricing workflow.  
  - **Authority**: Commercial authority is domain-specific; it is not interchangeable with supervisory approval.

- **Hybrid senior service / commercial shadow (R07)**  
  - **Core**: Dual core depending on case: front-office A2/A4/A12 for routine work, and A7/A8 for complex/high-stakes customers.  
  - **Peripheral**: A9 is present but limited—consistent with delegated or preliminary approval rather than final governance.  
  - **Substitution**: Weeks 3–4 coverage proves continuity value but not equivalence (duration increase is a capability/efficiency gap).

- **Supervisory approval & governance (R08)**  
  - **Core**: A9, plus escalation/reopen oversight (A13) as governance of outcomes.  
  - **Peripheral**: Negotiation/quality touches reflect intervention on hard cases, not a routine workflow step.  
  - **Authority**: The recheck pattern after Mateo’s preliminary approvals is the clearest “final authority” indicator in the evidence.

- **Quality gatekeeping & knowledge management (R09/R10)**  
  - **Core**: A11 and A14; quality is both a gate (before close) and a learning loop (KB updates).  
  - **Peripheral**: A10/A5/A3 can mean “fix-forward” during QA or support for late-shift resolution; interpretation depends on sequence position (do they implement before or after A11?) and permissions.  
  - **Authority**: They can block closure (procedural authority) without being supervisors (organizational authority).

- **Cross-functional incident coordination / escalation broker (R13)**  
  - **Core**: Coordination expressed as reopens management (A13) plus frequent handoffs to specialists; touches many activities because coordination leaves “breadcrumbs” in the log.  
  - **Peripheral**: Any single execution activity (A3/A5/A6/A7/A8/A10/A11) is usually contextual—helping unblock, routing, collecting information, or running playbooks.  
  - **Authority**: Preliminary A9 during Hana’s absence suggests delegated triage authority, but rechecks show governance remains with Hana.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

- **R07_Giorgio as a commercial shadow role (front-office ↔ commercial bridge)**  
  - **What it is**: A senior service agent who routinely crosses into commercial exception handling on complex cases and acts as the primary backup when the commercial specialist is absent.  
  - **Evidence**: Meaningful A7/A8/A9 volume in normal weeks; explicit substitution episode (31 pricing reviews, 18 negotiations) with measurable performance impact (duration 38 → 74 minutes).  
  - **Benefits**: Increases resilience to single points of failure; keeps customer-facing continuity for high-value cases; enables “one throat to choke” case ownership.  
  - **Risks**: Longer cycle time and potentially inconsistent commercial policy application; shadow authority can bypass formal controls if not monitored; may create dependency on one hybrid individual rather than a designed backup pool.

- **R13_Mateo as cross-functional escalation broker (router with partial authority)**  
  - **What it is**: A coordination role that brokers between technical, commercial, security, and management, especially on reopens and escalations.  
  - **Evidence**: Broad activity footprint plus heavy A13 (42) and A9 (44); central in fragments involving reopens and security coordination; Week 7–8 shows active filtering of security referrals (a policy/triage function).  
  - **Benefits**: Reduces specialist overload by filtering/structuring work; improves cross-team throughput; provides a single coordination point during high incident load or managerial absence.  
  - **Risks**: If the broker is wrong, critical cases can wait (observed >2 days); excessive centralization creates another bottleneck; logs can misrepresent “who did the work,” complicating accountability and performance measurement.

- **R05_Erik as rare but critical specialist (risk-control with technical spillover)**  
  - **What it is**: A control gate for security-sensitive cases, occasionally performing hands-on fixes/KB updates due to urgency.  
  - **Evidence**: Dominates A6 with high critical-case share; appears in the security-centric fragment prior to coordination and implementation; critical waits during filtered referrals show that demand management matters as much as capacity.  
  - **Benefits**: Maintains risk posture and compliance; reduces downstream rework and breach exposure; provides expert judgment in ambiguous incidents.  
  - **Risks**: Low-frequency activities can be under-resourced; if “security review” is treated as optional, severe tail risk increases; if treated as mandatory for too many cases, throughput collapses.

- **R14_Nora as an automation boundary role (system ↔ human handoff)**  
  - **What it is**: A system actor that changes the meaning of A1/A2 (precheck vs true validation) and shapes who sees which cases.  
  - **Benefits**: 24/7 throughput; reduces Tier‑1 load; standardizes precheck.  
  - **Risks**: Overconfidence in bot validation can amplify entitlement-note defects (especially with temps); process mining may over-attribute “work” to automation and under-attribute downstream rework to earlier automated decisions.

- **R09/R10 as quality gatekeepers (technical ↔ closure bridge)**  
  - **What it is**: A procedural control that sits between technical completion and case closure, plus a learning function via KB updates.  
  - **Benefits**: Detects defects before closure; institutionalizes learning; can reduce reopens.  
  - **Risks**: If QA becomes a throughput gate without capacity alignment, it becomes a bottleneck; occasional implementation by QA can blur accountability unless explicitly defined.

# 4. Ambiguous assignments and alternative interpretations

- **R07_Giorgio (hybrid)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: Senior Tier‑1 agent with delegated commercial backup duties for complex cases.  
  - **Interpretation B**: De facto “commercial specialist for a subset” (e.g., certain customer segments) who routinely owns negotiation/pricing.  
  - **What would decide**: Compare Giorgio’s A7/A8 share by `customer_segment`/`product_line` to Fatima’s; inspect `temporary_substitution==True` periods to separate routine hybrid work from coverage; check if Giorgio’s A9 events cluster at higher `escalation_level` than typical Tier‑1 work.

- **R13_Mateo (coordination vs acting supervisor)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: Incident coordinator whose A9 events are preliminary checks/queue releases under delegated rules.  
  - **Interpretation B**: “Shadow supervisor” who occasionally makes binding decisions during management absence or high load.  
  - **What would decide**: Sequence-position analysis: does Mateo’s A9 directly precede closure/implementation without later Hana A9? Quantify recheck rate (already hinted by Week 5); correlate Mateo’s A9 with `temporary_substitution` and spikes in `escalation_level`.

- **R03_Chen vs R04_Dina (same role vs senior/junior split)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: Same technical role; differences are driven by shift (late vs day) and random routing.  
  - **Interpretation B**: Chen is a senior technical lead handling higher-risk cases and security-adjacent work; Dina handles standard technical resolution.  
  - **What would decide**: Compare distributions of `case_complexity`, `escalation_level`, and `product_line=="Enterprise Security"`; measure Chen’s involvement before/after A6 and frequency of “reopen → technical → KB update” loops.

- **R09_Ivan and R10_Julia (pure QA vs resolution-support QA)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: QA-only gatekeepers who sometimes “touch” technical events for administrative reasons.  
  - **Interpretation B**: QA that also performs fix-forward implementation/configuration (especially on late shift) before documenting in KB.  
  - **What would decide**: Event ordering: does A10 by R09/R10 occur before A11 (true fixing) or after A11 (administrative logging)? Also check whether their A10/A5 events concentrate in reopens and high-complexity cases.

- **R01_Amira vs R02_Ben (interchangeable Tier‑1 vs case-owner skew)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: Interchangeable Tier‑1 generalists, differences driven by volume and chance.  
  - **Interpretation B**: Amira is a “complex-case front-office owner” (more negotiation and higher complexity), Ben is high-volume routine throughput.  
  - **What would decide**: Compare negotiation rate (A8) and pricing touch (A7) conditional on complexity; inspect whether Amira’s cases have more reopens or more escalations handled without handoff.

- **R06_Fatima (pricing reviewer vs broader commercial authority)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: Specialist who reviews pricing exceptions, with negotiation as an attached step.  
  - **Interpretation B**: End-to-end commercial owner for exceptions, with authority to negotiate and sometimes trigger/collect approvals.  
  - **What would decide**: Check whether Fatima initiates A9 at higher `escalation_level` and whether she appears early (right after validation) vs late (after technical work), indicating whether pricing is a gating precondition or a late-stage concession.

- **R05_Erik (gatekeeper vs incident responder)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: Pure security reviewer; A10/A14 reflect documentation or post-review compliance tasks.  
  - **Interpretation B**: Security incident responder who sometimes implements fixes directly under urgency.  
  - **What would decide**: Sequence timing: how often does Erik do A10 in the same case after his A6? Do those cases have higher `critical` classification and higher `escalation_level`?

- **R14_Nora (full automation vs selective automation)**  
  - **Interpretation A**: Always registers and prechecks entitlements; humans validate only for overrides/exceptions.  
  - **Interpretation B**: Automation applies mainly to low-complexity or certain segments; humans perform A1/A2 for complex segments.  
  - **What would decide**: Calculate the share of cases where A1/A2 is performed by humans vs bot, stratified by `customer_segment`, `product_line`, and early observed complexity proxies (e.g., escalation_level at first human touch).

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

df = pd.read_parquet("order_to_resolution.parquet")  # example input (or read_csv with parse_dates)

df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
)

df["case_complexity"] = pd.to_numeric(df["case_complexity"], errors="coerce")
df["temporary_substitution"] = df["temporary_substitution"].astype("boolean")
df["escalation_level"] = pd.to_numeric(df["escalation_level"], errors="coerce")

ACT = "concept:name"
RES = "org:resource"
CASE = "case:concept:name"

roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(
    df,
    activity_key=ACT,
    resource_key=RES
)

role_rows = []
for idx, role in enumerate(roles):
    for r in role.get("resources", []):  # adapt to actual PM4Py return format
        role_rows.append({"discovered_role_id": idx, RES: r})
role_membership = pd.DataFrame(role_rows)

sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(
    df,
    activity_key=ACT,
    resource_key=RES
)

handover = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df, case_id_key=CASE, resource_key=RES)
working_together = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df, resource_key=RES)
subcontracting = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df, case_id_key=CASE, resource_key=RES)

res_stats = (
    df.groupby(RES)
      .agg(
          events=(ACT, "size"),
          cases=(CASE, "nunique"),
          complexity_median=("case_complexity", "median"),
          complexity_p90=("case_complexity", lambda s: s.quantile(0.90)),
          critical_share=("case_complexity", lambda s: (s >= s.quantile(0.95)).mean()),  # proxy if no explicit flag
          subst_rate=("temporary_substitution", "mean"),
          escalation_p90=("escalation_level", lambda s: s.quantile(0.90)),
          approval_count=(ACT, lambda s: (s == "A9. Supervisor Approval").sum()),
          security_count=(ACT, lambda s: (s == "A6. Security Review").sum()),
          pricing_count=(ACT, lambda s: (s == "A7. Pricing Exception Review").sum())
      )
      .reset_index()
)

activity_counts = df.groupby([RES, ACT]).size().unstack(fill_value=0)
p = activity_counts.div(activity_counts.sum(axis=1), axis=0)
entropy = -(p * np.log(p.replace(0, np.nan))).sum(axis=1).fillna(0)
res_stats = res_stats.merge(entropy.rename("activity_entropy"), left_on=RES, right_index=True, how="left")

res_stats = res_stats.merge(role_membership, on=RES, how="left")

role_stats = (
    res_stats.groupby("discovered_role_id")
      .agg(
          members=(RES, lambda s: sorted(s.unique().tolist())),
          complexity_median=("complexity_median", "median"),
          subst_rate=("subst_rate", "mean"),
          approvals=("approval_count", "sum"),
          security=("security_count", "sum"),
          pricing=("pricing_count", "sum"),
          entropy=("activity_entropy", "mean")
      )
      .reset_index()
)

df_subst = df[df["temporary_substitution"] == True]
subst_mix = df_subst.groupby([RES, ACT]).size().unstack(fill_value=0)

shadow_candidates = res_stats[
    (res_stats["activity_entropy"] > res_stats["activity_entropy"].quantile(0.75)) |
    (res_stats["subst_rate"] > res_stats["subst_rate"].quantile(0.75)) |
    (res_stats["approval_count"] > 0) & (res_stats["complexity_median"] > res_stats["complexity_median"].median())
].sort_values(["activity_entropy", "subst_rate"], ascending=False)

print(role_stats)
print(shadow_candidates[[RES, "discovered_role_id", "complexity_median", "subst_rate", "approval_count", "pricing_count", "security_count", "activity_entropy"]])
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

- **Staffing and resilience**  
  - Treat **Commercial (R06)** and **Security (R05)** as explicit single-point-of-failure risks; formalize backup coverage (e.g., train a second commercial reviewer; define security on-call) rather than relying on ad-hoc shadowing.  
  - Recognize **Coordination (R13)** as capacity that prevents overload elsewhere; if you cut it because it “touches many activities,” you may increase specialist idle time and reopen churn.

- **Training and quality control**  
  - The temp-agent reopen episode indicates that **entitlement validation quality** is a leverage point. Use checklists/templates and targeted coaching for A2 notes; pair temps with QA feedback loops (R09/R10) rather than increasing closure throughput alone.  
  - For hybrid roles (R07), provide **commercial policy micro-training** and decision boundaries; the goal is safe delegation, not perfect equivalence to the specialist.

- **Risk control and escalation policy**  
  - Security referral filtering (Week 7–8) reduced noise but created critical tail delays. Management should set **explicit triage rules** (what must go to security) and monitor **tail latency** for critical cases, not just mean throughput.  
  - Distinguish **procedural authority** (QA can block closure) from **organizational authority** (supervisor approves). Role mining must be interpreted with these different authority types in mind.

- **Two cautions about changing staffing based only on mined roles**  
  - **Caution 1 (authority vs activity)**: Similar activity profiles do not imply interchangeable authority. For example, R13 and R08 both touch escalations, but Week 5 rechecks show final decision rights remain with R08. Reassigning authority based on similarity can break governance.  
  - **Caution 2 (substitution ≠ competence)**: Temporary substitution proves continuity, not equal proficiency. The measurable duration increase during R07’s pricing coverage is exactly the kind of signal that should prevent overconfident role consolidation.
