# 1. Data audit and estimands

**Units and conventions**  
- `arrivals` and `completed` are daily counts observed **only during opening hours 07:00–19:00** (12 hourly bins). These counts include all cases that arrived or completed on that day while the center was open; cases arriving overnight or completing after closing are not included in daily totals.  
- `median_service_min` and `p90_service_min` describe **active handling time only** (time spent in service, excluding waiting time); these are measured per case while it is being processed.  
- `mean_wip` is the **average number of cases in the system (queue + service) across 15-minute snapshots** taken during the day; it reflects intra-day variation but is reported as a single daily mean.  
- `staff_fte` is the **mean number of full-time-equivalent staff available concurrently during open hours** (e.g., 6.0 means 6 FTEs are present on average; this does not imply constant staffing throughout the day unless stated).  
- `complex_pct` is the **percentage of daily arrivals** that follow the multi-step complex variant (dimensionless, expressed as a percentage).  
- `peak_08_10_pct` is the **percentage of daily arrivals** observed in the 08:00–09:59 bin (computed from representative hourly vectors or inferred from daily aggregates; daily table does not give hourly arrival fractions directly).  
- `outage_min` is the **total outage duration in minutes** for the day (e.g., 180 minutes on 2026-04-15 corresponds to approximately 11:00–14:00; exact start/end times within the day are not logged). No service occurs during outages, and `completed` counts are zero during outage periods in representative vectors.  
- Dates are daily aggregates; **no overnight service occurs**, so backlog (cases in the system at 07:00) can carry over from the previous day. Backlog at 07:00 is not supplied for every day.  

**Limitations of the aggregates**  
- Daily totals hide intra-day patterns (e.g., bursty arrivals, outage timing); `mean_wip` is a snapshot average, not a steady-state value.  
- `median_service_min`/`p90_service_min` refer only to the service phase; they cannot be directly used as proxies for total flow time (waiting + service).  
- `arrivals` and `completed` may include cases that arrived earlier in the week and finished on the current day; thus completions are not purely "same-day" throughput.  
- `staff_fte` is a mean availability; it does not specify when staff are scheduled (e.g., could vary by hour).  
- `complex_pct` may reflect a real case-mix shift, a coding change, or both; the data alone cannot distinguish these causes.  
- The temporary staffing intervention began on 2026-04-29, but the exact implementation time (e.g., hour of day) was not logged; it could start at opening, midday, or vary by day.  
- Representative hourly vectors are illustrative examples (four days), not daily averages; they show possible intra-day behavior but cannot be assumed to apply to all days in a regime without validation.  
- Daily rows are not independent due to carry-over backlog; e.g., WIP on a Saturday may depend on WIP on the preceding Friday.

**At least five threats to naive inference** (explicitly labeled as observations, not assumptions)  
1. **Pooling days with vs. without backlog carry-over**: Pooled statistics (e.g., average `mean_wip`) may hide that post-outage days (e.g., 04-15–04-17) have elevated WIP due to overnight backlog, while normal days have lower WIP. Using a single pooled queueing model ignores this transient state.  
2. **Using median service time as flow-time proxy**: Median service time (e.g., 24–25 min)  average system time (waiting + service). Simpson’s paradox could arise if complex cases have longer service but lower peak arrivals, leading to misleading pooled flow estimates.  
3. **Ignoring burstiness in hourly reps**: Representative vectors show CV > 1 for some days (e.g., 04-21 with arrivals [2,3,8,34,8,…], CV  2.5), but daily averages smooth this. A pooled Poisson arrival model would underestimate variance and overestimate stability.  
4. **Treating outage recovery as normal high throughput**: On 04-15, completions drop to zero during the outage (11:00–14:00), yet pooled completions (~58 vs. 105 arrivals) might suggest "normal" throughput if only daily totals are used. This ignores intra-day zero completions during the outage.  
5. **Assuming stationarity across weekdays/weekends without check**: Weekends have ~40% fewer arrivals, ~2.5 FTE vs. ~6.0 FTE on weekdays, and lower `complex_pct` (~20–25% vs. ~30–50% post-outage). Pooling weekday and weekend data could misrepresent required capacity or flow times.  
6. (Additional, for completeness) **Staff FTE mean as constant rate**: `staff_fte` = 6.0 does not equal a fixed service rate; rate depends on service-time distribution (e.g., p90 vs. median). Using pooled staff FTE with a single service-time estimate could overestimate capacity during peak complex loads.

# 6. Little's-Law applicability matrix

**Definitions used (consistent with output):**
- Window: a regime period (e.g., NS = 04-06–04-10 weekdays + 04-11–04-12 weekends), with boundary defined by regime label.
- Throughput proxy: use `completed` (daily completions) as  proxy for "throughput during open hours" if no active backlog carry-over at window start; i.e., for days where WIP is near baseline (no visible backlog from previous day), `completed`   × W. For days with high WIP (e.g., RE), `completed` may be less than  × W due to outage or backlog inflow, so LL not directly applicable for those days.
- WIP comparability: `mean_wip` (daily snapshot mean) approximates L (average number in system) if the day is in steady state within the window (no ongoing backlog carry-over from previous window).
- Carry-over risk: whether overnight backlog can affect L at window start (e.g., RE days start with carry-over from outage).
- Stationarity assessment: rough assessment based on visible daily WIP/service variation within window (e.g., NS daily WIP ~18–21, small variation; RE daily WIP ~12–52, larger variation due to outage).
- Implied flow-time: only calculate if boundary stability and carry-over risk are low (i.e., window days typically have no carry-over backlog), using F = L /  where L  mean_wip,  = completions / 12 hours (cases per hour), so avg system time (flow time in queue+service)  mean_wip × (12 / completions) hours per case. **Do not use median service time; use  from completions.**
- Verdict: `applicable` (LL holds with minor caveats), `approximate only` (LL can be used with caveats, e.g., exclude outage bins), `not defensible` (carry-over high, intra-day variation, outage present).

> **Why not use median service time for flow time**: Little's Law uses average flow time = L / , where L is average number in system (WIP) and  is throughput rate (completions per time). Median service time is a phase-only statistic; waiting time depends on arrival process and capacity, not just service median. Using median service would give incorrect flow time (e.g., median service 24 min vs avg system time ~1.5–2.5h). Always use  from completions (or hourly completions if hourly data available) and L from mean_wip when appropriate boundary conditions hold.

# Burstiness indicators for reps
def dispersion_and_corr(series_list):
    cvs = []
    ids = []
    corrs = []
    for s in series_list:
        mean_val = s.mean()
        if mean_val > 0:
            cv = s.std() / mean_val
            id_val = s.var() / mean_val
            cvs.append(cv)
            ids.append(id_val)
            if len(s) > 1:
                corr = np.corrcoef(s[:-1], s[1:])[0,1]
                # Handle nan if constant
                corrs.append(corr if not np.isnan(corr) else np.nan)
            else:
                corrs.append(np.nan)
        else:
            cvs.append(np.nan)
            ids.append(np.nan)
            corrs.append(np.nan)
    return np.nanmean(cvs), np.nanmean(ids), np.nanmean(corrs)
