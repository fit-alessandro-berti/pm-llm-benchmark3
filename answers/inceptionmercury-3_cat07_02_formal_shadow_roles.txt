# 2. Core versus peripheral activities

This section clarifies that “core” means the activity is central to the role’s purpose, not simply the most frequent activity.

- **R14_Nora — Digital front-door automation**  
  Core: A1 and A2.  
  Peripheral: none.  
  The bot has the highest overall event volume, but it is a system boundary. It does not diagnose, decide, approve, or take responsibility for outcome quality. Its complexity is overwhelmingly low.

- **R01_Amira and R02_Ben — Standard front-office service agents**  
  Core: A1, A2, A4, A12.  
  Peripheral: A3 (preliminary triage), A5, A7/A8, A13, A14.  
  Amira has more medium/high/critical case exposure and more commercial touchpoints; Ben is more purely routine. Their authority is first-line service ownership, not approval authority. Temporary agents can cover their volume, but not their broader judgment.

- **R11_Kai and R12_Lina — Temporary front-office agents**  
  Core: A1, A2, A4, A12.  
  Peripheral: small numbers of A3 and A8.  
  Their profile looks like permanent agents, but they lack certifications and have zero high/critical case exposure. Their incomplete validation notes caused 17 reopens. They are capacity support, not unsupervised generalists.

- **R07_Giorgio — Senior service agent / commercial shadow**  
  Core: A1, A2, A4, A7, A8, A12.  
  Peripheral: A3, A5, A9, A11, A13, A14.  
  He has both entitlement and pricing certification. When Fatima was absent, he performed 31 A7 and 18 A8 events, so he can cover commercial work. However, the observed rise in median pricing duration suggests he is a capable shadow, not an exact substitute with the same fluency.

- **R03_Chen and R04_Dina — Technical analysts**  
  Core: A3, A5, A10.  
  Peripheral: A2, A4, A6, A11, A13, A14.  
  Dina performs similar activities but on lower-complexity cases. Chen handles critical and security-adjacent cases. A similarity score of 0.94 should not be read as “same risk level.”

- **R05_Erik — Security specialist**  
  Core: A6.  
  Peripheral: occasional diagnosis, configuration, implementation, quality, KB updates.  
  Erik’s total event count is low, but his role is a rare, critical intervention. When Mateo filtered low-risk security referrals, false escalation complaints decreased, but two critical cases waited more than two days. This shows Erik is not merely a high-volume worker; he is a risk control point.

- **R06_Fatima — Commercial specialist**  
  Core: A7 and A8.  
  Peripheral: A2, A3, A4, A9, A10, A12, A13.  
  Her A9 events are worth investigation. They may be completed commercial approvals or pre-approvals, not final supervisor authority. The process fragment shows she reviews pricing and negotiates, then Hana performs the final supervisor approval.

- **R08_Hana — Supervisor**  
  Core: A9, and A13 as an oversight/reopen control.  
  Peripheral: participation in many other activities when she reviews escalated cases.  
  Her authority is not visible in activity frequency. Mateo can perform preliminary A9 events, but 13 of 21 were later rechecked by Hana, confirming he does not have her final authority.

- **R09_Ivan and R10_Julia — Quality analysts / KB gatekeepers**  
  Core: A11 and A14.  
  Peripheral: A3, A5, A10, A12, A13.  
  They appear in A5/A10 because they may verify configuration or do close-out implementation work. Management should check whether they ever perform A10 and A11 on the same case; if so, the quality check may no longer be independent.

- **R13_Mateo — Incident / escalation coordinator**  
  Core: A13, triage, coordination, preliminary A9.  
  Peripheral: moderate numbers of almost every activity.  
  He is a cross-functional broker, not a final approver. His role improves routing and reduces false escalations, but his filtering can also hide critical work, as seen in the two delayed security cases.

# 3. Shadow and bridging roles

Shadow roles are not necessarily improper. They are informal or semi-formal arrangements that increase resilience and cross-functional flexibility, but they also need governance.

### R07_Giorgio as a potential commercial shadow

- What it means: Giorgio is formally a senior service agent, but his behavior overlaps Fatima’s commercial role through A7 and A8.
- Benefits:
  - He can cover commercial work during absence or high load.
  - He already knows the front-office context and the customer history.
  - His pricing certification reduces the risk of unqualified substitution.
- Risks:
  - If he negotiates pricing without the same commercial guardrails as Fatima, decisions may be inconsistent.
  - The observed increase in median pricing duration from 38 to 74 minutes during substitution suggests he is slower or less fluent.
  - If his manager relies on him indefinitely, the commercial role may become ambiguous, with no clear owner.
- Recommendation: Formalize him as an approved backup with limits, training, and escalation criteria.

### R13_Mateo as a cross-functional escalation broker

- What it means: Mateo appears across all functional areas. He is a coordinator and router, not a domain expert.
- Benefits:
  - He can triage reopens and assign them to the correct specialist.
  - In weeks 7–8, his filtering of low-risk security referrals reduced false escalation complaints.
  - He connects technical, commercial, security, and quality work without forcing unnecessary handoffs.
- Risks:
  - When he performs preliminary A9 approvals, he is not a true supervisor. Week 5 showed many of his approvals were rechecked by Hana.
  - Filtering can also be too aggressive. Two critical security cases waited more than two days.
- Recommendation: Treat him as a “router and safety valve,” not as a substitute approver. Give him clear escalation thresholds and time-based triggers.

### R05_Erik as a rare but critical specialist

- What it means: Erik has a narrow, low-frequency profile, but his A6 security review is mandatory for security-relevant cases.
- Benefits:
  - Concentration of security expertise creates a clear owner for high-risk decisions.
  - His rare interventions are more specialized than ordinary technical work.
- Risks:
  - He is a single point of failure. If he is unavailable, critical cases wait.
  - Frequency-based role discovery may underweight him because his event count is small.
- Recommendation: Identify a backup security reviewer and create explicit critical-case escalation paths so Erik is not the only person who can release a security gate.

### Other shadow / boundary roles

- R14_Nora is an automation boundary role. It is not a human resource and should be separated from human role clusters.
- R11_Kai and R12_Lina are temporary-workforce shadows of the front-office role. They mimic permanent agents but have a narrower authority and higher rework risk.
- R09_Ivan and R10_Julia are quality gatekeepers. They cross into technical implementation work, but their main purpose is checking and maintaining knowledge; they must remain independent of the work they inspect.

# 4. Ambiguous assignments and alternative interpretations

### R07_Giorgio

- Interpretation A: He is a senior front-office service agent with an explicit commercial shadow role.
- Interpretation B: He is effectively a commercial specialist who is also carrying front-office volume to fill his schedule.
- Evidence that would decide:
  - Check his reporting line and home team. If he reports to Commercial rather than Front Office, Interpretation B is stronger.
  - Compare control-flow positions: Are his A7/A8 events followed by Hana approval as often as Fatima’s? If yes, he is acting under delegated commercial authority.
  - Compare outcomes: If his pricing reviews have more errors, longer duration, or more rework, he is a shadow, not a full specialist.
  - The longer duration during Fatima’s absence supports Interpretation A.

### R13_Mateo

- Interpretation A: He is an incident coordinator and escalation broker.
- Interpretation B: He is an assistant supervisor or acting approver because he performs A9 and appears broadly across the process.
- Evidence that would decide:
  - Follow handover edges from Mateo’s A9 events. If many are followed by Hana’s A9, he is only preparing or pre-approving.
  - Week 5 confirms 13 of 21 preliminary approvals were later rechecked by Hana.
  - Check whether Mateo can close reopened cases without sending them to a specialist or Hana. If he can, he has more authority than a pure coordinator.
  - Check critical case delays when he filters security referrals. If he delays high-severity cases, he is not a substitute for a supervisor.

### R11_Kai and R12_Lina

- Interpretation A: They are temporary agents with a restricted front-office role.
- Interpretation B: They are the same role as permanent agents, and the low-complexity work is just a seasonal reflection.
- Evidence that would decide:
  - Contract type, tenure, and certifications strongly support Interpretation A.
  - The 17 reopens caused by incomplete validation notes show they are not yet qualified for unsupervised full case ownership.
  - Check whether they ever handle A5, A6, A7, A9, A10, or A11 when those activities are present in a case. The matrix says almost never.
  - The absence of high/critical cases is not random; it reflects deliberate assignment.

### R03_Chen and R04_Dina

- Interpretation A: They are the same technical analyst role, with Chen as the more senior or complex-case resource.
- Interpretation B: They are two distinct roles: “complex incident technical specialist” and “standard solution technician.”
- Evidence that would decide:
  - Compare case complexity distributions. Chen has 42 critical cases versus Dina’s 9; Chen also has more A6 security-adjacent work.
  - Check whether Dina is ever assigned to critical cases alone. If not, the resources are not interchangeable.
  - Activity similarity of 0.94 measures what they do, not the risk level at which they do it.

### R06_Fatima and the A9 count

- Interpretation A: Fatima prepares commercial approvals, and Hana performs the final approval.
- Interpretation B: Fatima has delegated approval authority for low- to medium-risk pricing exceptions.
- Evidence that would decide:
  - Inspect the exact sequence: Does Fatima’s A9 occur before or after Hana’s A9?
  - If Fatima’s A9 cases are never followed by Hana, delegation is plausible; if they are always followed by Hana in high-value cases, Fatima is only preparing.
  - Use case complexity and monetary value. Threshold-based delegation would show Fatima approving only low/medium cases and Hana approving critical cases.

### R09_Ivan and R10_Julia

- Interpretation A: They are quality analysts and knowledge gatekeepers.
- Interpretation B: They are implementation/test technicians whose A10 work overlaps the technical role.
- Evidence that would decide:
  - Check whether they perform A10 and A11 on the same case. If yes, the quality check is not independent.
  - Check whether their A5/A10 work happens after Chen/Dina’s implementation and before the final quality check. If so, it is verification, not main configuration.
  - Their formal home team and certification favor Interpretation A.

# 5. PM4Py role-discovery and similarity code

The code below is illustrative. It uses PM4Py-style function names and assumes the raw dataframe has the standard columns described in the scenario.

```python
import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py

def load_log(filepath):
    df = pd.read_csv(filepath)

    # Ensure timestamps are parsed
    df["time:timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(df["time:timestamp"])

    # If start timestamps are not present, use the regular timestamp
    if "start_time:timestamp" not in df.columns:
        df["start_time:timestamp"] = df["time:timestamp"]

    # Format the dataframe to PM4Py standard column names
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
        start_timestamp_key="start_time:timestamp",
        resource_key="org:resource",
    )

    # PM4Py discovery functions usually operate on an event log object
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)
    return df, log

# Load and format the log
df, log = load_log("order_to_resolution.csv")

# Optional: inspect top variants to understand routine and rare sequences
variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
top_variants = sorted(variants.items(), key=lambda x: x[1], reverse=True)[:10]
print("Top variants:")
for variant, count in top_variants:
    print(count, variant)

# Discover organizational roles from activity frequencies
roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log)
print("\nDiscovered organizational roles:")
for role, resources in roles.items():
    print(role, resources)

# Activity-based resource similarity
activity_sim = pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log)
print("\nActivity-based resource similarity:")
print(activity_sim)

# Handover of work network
hon = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log, beta=0)
print("\nHandover-of-work edges with weight >= 10:")
for edge, weight in hon.items():
    if weight >= 10:
        print(edge, round(weight, 3))

# Working together network
wtn = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log)
print("\nWorking-together network edges with weight >= 10:")
for edge, weight in wtn.items():
    if weight >= 10:
        print(edge, round(weight, 3))

# Subcontracting network
scn = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log)
print("\nSubcontracting network edges with weight >= 10:")
for edge, weight in scn.items():
    if weight >= 10:
        print(edge, round(weight, 3))

# Join role-discovery output with complexity and substitution indicators
# Resource activity profile
profile = pd.crosstab(df["org:resource"], df["concept:name"], normalize="index")

# Case complexity distribution by resource
complexity_pivot = pd.crosstab(df["org:resource"], df["case_complexity"])

# Temporary substitution rate by resource
# Change "yes" to True if the column is Boolean
temp_sub_rate = df.groupby("org:resource")["temporary_substitution"].apply(
    lambda s: s.astype(str).eq("yes").mean()
)

# Count A9 approval touches by resource
a9_count = df[df["concept:name"] == "A9. Supervisor Approval"].groupby(
    "org:resource"
).size()

merged = (
    profile
    .join(complexity_pivot, how="outer")
    .join(temp_sub_rate.rename("temp_sub_rate"), how="outer")
    .join(a9_count.rename("A9_count"), how="outer")
    .fillna(0)
)

print("\nJoined resource profile, complexity, substitution, A9 count:")
print(merged.sort_values("A9_count", ascending=False))

# Filter substitution episodes
# Use the actual encoded value, here "yes"
substitution_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log,
    "temporary_substitution",
    ["yes"],
    level="case",
    retain=True,
)

# Inspect behavior during substitution for selected resources
for resource in ["R07_Giorgio", "R06_Fatima"]:
    resource_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        substitution_log,
        "org:resource",
        [resource],
        level="event",
        retain=True,
    )
    print(f"\nSubstitution-period variants for {resource}:")
    print(pm4py.get_variants(resource_log))

# Filter specific product line / escalation context
security_log = pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
    log,
    "product_line",
    ["Enterprise Security"],
    level="case",
    retain=True,
)

# Optional: compute service time
service_time = pm4py.get_service_time(log, resource_key="org:resource")
print("\nService time by resource:")
print(service_time)
```

# 6. Operational interpretation

Management should use the discovered role structure as a capability map, not as a fixed chart.

- **Staffing:** Use the role table to allocate work by complexity. Temporary agents should handle simple request closure under supervision. Technical analysts should handle medium/high complexity resolution. Security and approval roles should be preserved for mandatory control points.
- **Training:** Use the substitution episodes to identify backup training needs. Giorgio should be trained as a commercial backup with limits. Mateo should be trained on escalation thresholds, especially for security and approval tasks. Temporary agents should be trained on complete entitlement validation notes to prevent reopens.
- **Risk control:** Monitor the resource-specific indicators: pricing duration for Giorgio versus Fatima, critical security waiting time after Mateo’s filtering, recheck rates after Mateo’s preliminary approvals, and the reopen rate of temporary-agent closures. Use the handover-of-work network to identify single points of failure.
- **Resilience:** Build explicit backup paths for R05_Erik, R08_Hana, and R06_Fatima. Shadow roles should be formalized with authority limits. The goal is not to eliminate shadow roles but to make their temporary use predictable and safe.

### Cautions

1. **Do not staff or reorganize based only on mined roles.** A discovered role is an observed pattern, not a required organizational design. It may reflect past bottlenecks, manual exceptions, or temporary substitutions. Role mining should be followed by process interviews and validation.
2. **Do not treat temporary substitution as proof of permanent competence.** Giorgio covered Fatima, but pricing duration rose; Mateo covered Hana, but his approvals had to be rechecked; temporary agents closed simple cases, but 17 later reopened. Similarity and frequency are not enough to prove interchangeability.
3. **Do not assume similar activity profiles imply similar authority or risk exposure.** R08_Hana and R13_Mateo both touch A9, but only Hana has final approval authority. R03_Chen and R04_Dina look similar but have different complexity loads. Authority and risk must be evaluated from control-flow position and case complexity, not from event counts alone.
