## 1. Materiality Framework

CL-602 and CL-604 warrant the most urgent review. CL-610 and CL-609 also require prompt intervention despite perfect structural alignment. The other cases predominantly concern evidence, event representation, or the boundaries of the model. These are reasoned priorities under the supplied facts, not a uniquely correct ranking.

Severity describes the materiality of the control concern and the urgency of response. It is not a finding of fraud, a loss estimate, or a count of missing events.

| Severity | Interpretation | Response standard |
|---|---|---|
| 5 — Critical | Material payment-gating failure, or an unverifiable mandatory preventive control combined with very large exposure and strong risk indicators. | Immediate investigation and containment of remaining exposure; preserve evidence and establish recovery options. |
| 4 — High | Clear substantive policy failure exposing a payment to unauthorized processing, even where the amount is smaller or loss is unconfirmed. | Same-day review and prompt prevention of recurrence. |
| 3 — Moderate | Meaningful control or customer-service weakness without the critical combination above; unresolved evidence could materially change the conclusion. | Time-bound investigation and corrective action. |
| 2 — Low | Primarily an evidence, mapping, or model-scope problem, with substantive performance supported or a legitimate nonpayment outcome indicated. | Repair assurance or modeling; escalate if supporting evidence fails. |
| 1 — Minimal/no material deviation established | Fitting execution or technical noise with no indicated business-control failure. | Routine maintenance or no case intervention. |

Apply these levels using the following considerations together:

- **Control objective:** Coverage, loss assessment, fraud screening, valid authorization, correct beneficiary payment, and timely communication protect different interests. A missing fraud-screen record on a risky payment is different from a duplicate notification transport record.
- **Amount and exposure:** Claim amounts indicate funds potentially affected, not actual loss or expected loss. CL-602's EUR 480,000 matters greatly when a mandatory fraud control is unsubstantiated; CL-608's EUR 250,000 does not automatically make its notification-link weakness critical. A denied or withdrawn claim has no authorized payment exposure merely because the model proposes payment.
- **Reversibility and timing:** Funds accepted by a bank may be difficult to recover. Approval after release cannot supply the preventive protection that approval before release was intended to provide. A recording delay must be distinguished from an actual decision delay.
- **Customer and regulatory harm:** Consider misdirected funds, unauthorized settlement, delayed or missing communications, and inability to substantiate compliance. No specific legal breach, fraud, financial loss, or customer injury is established solely by these alignments.
- **Recurrence:** Repetition can reveal a systemic control weakness, but 74 harmless retries are not equivalent to 74 unauthorized payments. Evaluate frequency, the affected population, and the mechanism. Monthly denominators are not supplied.
- **Evidence reliability:** Contemporaneous signed reports, authoritative bank records, and durable case identifiers carry more weight than recollection or mutable text links. Distinguish activity occurrence, correct case attribution, completion time, and evidence retention.
- **Model scope:** Determine whether the payable-claim model applies before interpreting its completion requirements as business obligations. P6 expressly identifies denied leads and reopened claims as disputed; CL-612 separately exposes the missing withdrawal outcome. There is no supplied reopened case to assess.

**Confidence is separate from severity.** Confidence states how well the evidence supports a particular interpretation. For CL-602, confidence is high that verifiable fraud-screen evidence is missing, but lower that screening never occurred. The potentially critical exposure justifies urgent investigation despite that uncertainty. For CL-603, both confidence in the retry explanation and confidence in low business severity are high. Do not reduce a serious potential exposure merely because the evidence is incomplete, or describe uncertainty as proof of misconduct.

The ratings below are current triage judgments, with explicit conditions for revision. A synchronous notification proves a matching event, not compliance with the 24-hour deadline. Except for CL-608's supplied +3-hour evidence, notification timing is not established. Similarly, synchronous checks establish logged activities, not their substantive quality or the actor's authority in every respect.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

For a supplied alignment A, technical cost is the sum of its move costs:

`C(A) = sum(cost of log moves) + sum(cost of visible model moves) + sum(cost of synchronous and hidden moves).`

Here every visible log/model move costs 1, and synchronous and listed hidden moves cost 0. Consequently, the supplied raw cost is just the number of visible unmatched moves. An alignment algorithm generally optimizes this configured objective; it does not optimize expected monetary loss, regulatory consequences, customer harm, or confidence. We retain the supplied alignments as authoritative even if another equally good or cheaper explanation emerges under a different setup.

Several independent mechanisms break any monotonic relationship between cost and severity:

1. **Cost-function design ignores business importance.** CL-602's absent fraud-screen match costs 1 on EUR 480,000 with HIGH risk and a new beneficiary account. CL-603's retry also costs 1, but has strong evidence of harmless duplication. CL-601 costs 3 for UI events and is less material than either major payment-control concern. Equal penalties encode edit preferences, not equal consequences. Business-specific weights could improve triage, but would still require justified context and evidence rules.
2. **Timing and irreversibility survive an inexpensive edit repair.** In CL-604, the log-side early payment and model-side later payment help reconcile event order on paper. They neither undo bank acceptance nor turn an approval recorded 17 minutes later into a preventive approval. The alignment has only one observed payment. Cost 3 is silent on the ability to recall funds, beneficiary legitimacy, and the damage caused by release without the applicable authorization.
3. **The tree cannot evaluate unmodeled data conditions.** CL-609 and CL-610 both cost 0 because the XOR structurally permits Auto Approve. Yet CL-609 fails P1 on amount and risk, and CL-610 fails P1 and triggers P2 through the recent bank change. Data-aware checks must join the event sequence to decision-time and release-time attributes. A zero-cost trace is not necessarily compliant.
4. **Hidden transitions represent model routing.** CL-605's `T(tau,c=0)` is fitting. Counting it as a missing action would invent a business requirement. Conversion choices can introduce silent transitions without changing the intended business process; tool defaults may also price them differently. Such implementation details should not determine business severity.
5. **Scope changes the meaning of apparent omissions.** CL-607 and CL-612 cost 4 because the payable model expects continued processing. For an authorized denial or withdrawal, missing approval and payment are appropriate. The correct response is scope/outcome modeling plus validation of termination and notice, not compelling payment. Conversely, labeling everything out of scope would conceal real failures; scope requires governed criteria.
6. **Missing core events can coexist with substantive performance.** CL-606's signed external report supports assessment before screening and approval, whereas CL-602 has no comparable fraud evidence. CL-608's permitted portal message supports timely notice despite `M(Notify Claimant)`, with a residual retention/linking concern. The same cost 1 can therefore indicate an operational omission, an evidence-correlation problem, or a complete activity recorded elsewhere. Confidence depends on the evidence, not the cost.
7. **Activity granularity and labels alter distance without altering business work.** CL-611's one potential naming difference becomes both a log move and a model move, costing 2. CL-601's workbench instrumentation adds three records around otherwise normal work. Splitting one business activity into several lifecycle/UI events, or merging multiple steps under one label, changes edit counts without proportionally changing controls.
8. **Duplicate telemetry inflates recurrence statistics.** The 74 same-message-ID retry cases suggest a shared connector issue. Counting them as 74 distinct customer-service failures would confuse records with business outcomes. Delivery evidence is needed to establish whether the retry had a meaningful customer effect.

Across the twelve supplied alignments, raw costs sum to 20. CL-601, CL-603, CL-607, and CL-612 contribute 12 units, or 60%, largely through noise and termination-model mismatch, although CL-612 retains an unresolved notice concern. The two clear illustrated auto-eligibility violations contribute none. Even aggregating costs over many cases preserves this bias. Neither raw cost nor fitness is a calibrated risk score, and model/log repair can improve these metrics without changing the historical operational exposure.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

The following block uses inline evidence and installed `pandas` and `pm4py`. It reconstructs observed traces from synchronous and log moves, runs alignment diagnostics against the specified tree, and separately aggregates the authoritative supplied move summaries. Model moves and silent transitions are never inserted as observed events.

Computed PM4Py alignments may choose another branch or optimal alignment, use different formatting, and apply different default cost scales. Their native costs therefore remain separate from the supplied uniform-cost totals and the narrative severity judgments. Synthetic event timestamps preserve ordering only; the genuine 10:03/10:20 and +3-hour evidence remains explicit in the evidence table. The review order is a declared human judgment, not a formula for expected loss.

```python
# Requires installed pandas and pm4py. All case evidence is inline; no input files or network calls are used.
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

TREE = "-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss', X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ), 'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)
net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# Compact transcription of the authoritative supplied moves.
# S and T cost 0; L and M cost 1 under the question's cost function.
start = "S:Register Claim|S:Validate Coverage|"
assessed = start + "S:Assess Loss|"
paid_notified = "S:Pay Claim|S:Notify Claimant"
supplied = {
    "CL-601": start + "L:Open Workbench|S:Assess Loss|L:Autosave|S:Auto Approve|" + paid_notified + "|L:Close Workbench",
    "CL-602": assessed + "M:Fraud Screen|S:Manual Approve|" + paid_notified,
    "CL-603": assessed + "S:Auto Approve|" + paid_notified + "|L:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-604": assessed + "L:Pay Claim|M:Fraud Screen|S:Manual Approve|M:Pay Claim|S:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-605": assessed + "T:tau|S:Auto Approve|" + paid_notified,
    "CL-606": start + "M:Assess Loss|S:Fraud Screen|S:Manual Approve|" + paid_notified,
    "CL-607": start + "L:Deny Claim|M:Assess Loss|M:Auto Approve|M:Pay Claim|S:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-608": assessed + "S:Fraud Screen|S:Manual Approve|S:Pay Claim|M:Notify Claimant",
    "CL-609": assessed + "S:Auto Approve|" + paid_notified,
    "CL-610": assessed + "S:Auto Approve|" + paid_notified,
    "CL-611": start + "L:Assess Damage|M:Assess Loss|S:Fraud Screen|S:Manual Approve|" + paid_notified,
    "CL-612": assessed + "L:Withdraw Claim|M:Auto Approve|M:Pay Claim|M:Notify Claimant",

# None means the question does not supply the attribute, not that it is false.
# Severity and review order are explicit narrative judgments, not cost-derived scores.
case_rows = [
    ("CL-601", 1200, "LOW", False, "payable", 3, 1, 10, "High: UI noise"),
    ("CL-602", 480000, "HIGH", True, "payable", 1, 5, 1, "High: evidence gap; screening performance uncertain"),
    ("CL-603", 3200, "LOW", False, "payable", 1, 1, 11, "High: retry evidence"),
    ("CL-604", 90000, "MEDIUM", False, "payable", 3, 5, 2, "High: recorded chronology; verify timestamp meaning"),
    ("CL-605", 800, "LOW", False, "payable", 0, 1, 12, "High: fitting silent routing"),
    ("CL-606", 75000, "HIGH", None, "payable", 1, 2, 5, "Moderate-high: external assessment supported"),
    ("CL-607", None, "UNKNOWN", None, "disputed-denied", 4, 2, 8, "High: denial observed; scope unresolved"),
    ("CL-608", 250000, "HIGH", None, "payable", 1, 2, 6, "Moderate: timely message, weak link provenance"),
    ("CL-609", 18000, "MEDIUM", False, "payable", 0, 4, 4, "High: P1 violation"),
    ("CL-610", 4500, "LOW", True, "payable", 0, 4, 3, "High: P1/P2 violation"),
    ("CL-611", 62000, "HIGH", None, "payable", 2, 2, 7, "Moderate: likely historical label mismatch"),
    ("CL-612", 2000, "LOW", None, "disputed-withdrawn", 4, 2, 9, "High: withdrawal; notification unresolved"),
]
cases = pd.DataFrame(case_rows, columns=[
    "case", "amount_eur", "risk", "recent_bank_change", "scope",
    "supplied_raw_cost", "business_severity_1_5", "review_order", "confidence",
])
cases["amount_eur"] = cases["amount_eur"].astype("Float64")
cases["recent_bank_change"] = cases["recent_bank_change"].astype("boolean")
cases["reason"] = cases["case"].map({
    "CL-601": "Telemetry only; auto path eligible.",
    "CL-602": "480k/high risk/recent bank change; mandatory fraud check unsubstantiated.",
    "CL-603": "Same message ID and transport retry; no distinct business notice established.",
    "CL-604": "Bank accepted 10:03; approval recorded 10:20; no matched fraud screen.",
    "CL-605": "Silent routing is fitting; auto path eligible.",
    "CL-606": "Signed external assessment at 10:05 precedes screening and approval.",
    "CL-607": "Coverage denial; payable-only scope disputed; payment was not intended.",
    "CL-608": "Portal notice at +3 hours; mutable case link weakens retained evidence.",
    "CL-609": "18k and MEDIUM risk both disallow auto; P2 itself is not triggered.",
    "CL-610": "Bank change one hour before payment disallows auto and triggers P2.",
    "CL-611": "Assess Damage may represent Assess Loss; mapping changed after case.",
    "CL-612": "Withdrawal before approval/payment; no notice evidence supplied.",
})

move_rows = []
for case_id, text in supplied.items():
    for position, token in enumerate(text.split("|")):
        kind, activity = token.split(":", 1)
        assert kind in {"S", "L", "M", "T"}
        move_rows.append({
            "case": case_id, "position": position, "move_type": kind,
            "activity": activity, "technical_move_cost": int(kind in {"L", "M"}),

moves = pd.DataFrame(move_rows)
calculated = moves.groupby("case", as_index=False).agg(
    calculated_raw_cost=("technical_move_cost", "sum")
)
cases = cases.merge(calculated, on="case", validate="one_to_one")
assert cases["supplied_raw_cost"].eq(cases["calculated_raw_cost"]).all()

# Only S and L consume observed log events. M is never inserted into the real trace;
# T is a model routing move, not an observed business event.
observed = moves[moves["move_type"].isin(["S", "L"])].sort_values(["case", "position"])
traces = observed.groupby("case", sort=False)["activity"].agg(list).to_dict()
assert traces["CL-604"].count("Pay Claim") == 1
records = []
for case_number, case_id in enumerate(cases["case"]):
    for event_number, activity in enumerate(traces[case_id]):
        records.append({
            "case:concept:name": case_id,
            "concept:name": activity,
            # Synthetic timestamps preserve supplied order ONLY: not SLA evidence.
            "time:timestamp": pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01", tz="UTC")
                + pd.Timedelta(days=case_number, minutes=event_number),
        })
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    pd.DataFrame(records), case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp",

log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(events)
diagnostics = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
    log, net, initial_marking, final_marking

computed = pd.DataFrame([

     "pm4py_native_alignment_cost": diagnostic["cost"]}
    for trace, diagnostic in zip(log, diagnostics)

cases = cases.merge(computed, on="case", validate="one_to_one")
# Alternative optimal alignments and PM4Py formatting may differ from the supplied
# authoritative moves. PM4Py defaults can also use a different cost scale or silent
# transition cost. Do not substitute native costs for the supplied raw costs.

# Three-valued policy evaluation preserves missing business attributes.
def tri_all(values):
    if any(v == "no" for v in values):
        return "no"
    return "yes" if all(v == "yes" for v in values) else "unknown"

def tri_any(values):
    if any(v == "yes" for v in values):
        return "yes"
    return "no" if all(v == "no" for v in values) else "unknown"

def yn(condition):
    return "yes" if condition else "no"

def policy_evidence(row):
    amount, risk, bank = row.amount_eur, row.risk, row.recent_bank_change
    auto_eligible = tri_all([
        "unknown" if pd.isna(amount) else yn(amount <= 5000),
        "unknown" if risk == "UNKNOWN" else yn(risk == "LOW"),
        "unknown" if pd.isna(bank) else yn(not bool(bank)),
    ])
    p2_required = tri_any([
        "unknown" if pd.isna(amount) else yn(amount > 50000),
        "unknown" if risk == "UNKNOWN" else yn(risk == "HIGH"),
        "unknown" if pd.isna(bank) else yn(bool(bank)),
    ])
    activities = traces[row.case]
    paid = "Pay Claim" in activities
    pre_pay = activities[:activities.index("Pay Claim")] if paid else []
    auto_used = "Auto Approve" in activities
    p1_violation = (
        "no" if not auto_used else
        "yes" if auto_eligible == "no" else
        "no" if auto_eligible == "yes" else "unknown"
    )
    controls_shown = (
        {"Fraud Screen", "Manual Approve"}.issubset(pre_pay)
        and pre_pay.index("Fraud Screen") < pre_pay.index("Manual Approve")
    )
    p2_core_gap = (
        "not_applicable" if not paid else
        yn(not controls_shown) if p2_required == "yes" else
        "no" if p2_required == "no" else "unknown"
    )
    if not paid:
        approval_gap = "not_applicable"
    elif "Manual Approve" in pre_pay:
        approval_gap = "no"  # Separate from any missing fraud-screen evidence.
    elif "Auto Approve" in pre_pay:
        approval_gap = {"yes": "no", "no": "yes", "unknown": "unknown"}[auto_eligible]
    else:
        approval_gap = "yes"
    early_recorded_payment = (
        paid and "Manual Approve" in activities
        and activities.index("Pay Claim") < activities.index("Manual Approve")
    )
    notification = (
        "portal +3h; mutable link" if row.case == "CL-608" else
        "no notice evidence; withdrawal rule/trigger to resolve" if row.case == "CL-612" else
        "core notice observed; 24h timing unverified"
    )
    flags = []
    if p1_violation == "yes":
        flags.append("P1_INELIGIBLE_AUTO")
    if p2_core_gap == "yes":
        flags.append("P2_PREPAY_CORE_EVIDENCE_GAP")
    if approval_gap == "yes":
        flags.append("APPLICABLE_APPROVAL_NOT_SHOWN_BEFORE_PAY")
    if early_recorded_payment:
        flags.append("PAY_BEFORE_RECORDED_MANUAL")
    if row.case == "CL-608":
        flags.append("P4_LINK_PROVENANCE_WEAK")
    if row.case == "CL-612":
        flags.append("P4_NOTICE_UNRESOLVED")
    if row.scope != "payable":
        flags.append("SCOPE_OR_OUTCOME_MODEL_REVIEW")
    return {
        "case": row.case, "p1_auto_eligible": auto_eligible,
        "p2_controls_required": p2_required, "p1_auto_violation": p1_violation,
        "p2_pre_pay_core_gap": p2_core_gap, "applicable_approval_core_gap": approval_gap,
        "pay_before_recorded_manual": early_recorded_payment,
        "notification_evidence": notification,
        "policy_overlay_flags": ";".join(flags) if flags else "NO_FLAG_FROM_SUPPLIED_FACTS",
    }

# Aggregate within each case first, so repeated moves do not inflate its amount
# inside a pattern. A case may still occur in multiple patterns: totals across
# patterns are NOT additive. Amounts are claim face values, not actual losses.
per_case_pattern = moves[moves["move_type"].ne("S")].groupby(
    ["case", "move_type", "activity"], as_index=False
).agg(move_count=("position", "size"), pattern_raw_cost=("technical_move_cost", "sum"))
per_case_pattern = per_case_pattern.merge(
    cases[["case", "amount_eur", "risk", "policy_overlay_flags"]],
    on="case", validate="many_to_one",
)

def unique_values(series):
    return ", ".join(sorted(set(series.dropna().astype(str))))

patterns = per_case_pattern.groupby(["move_type", "activity"], as_index=False).agg(
    affected_cases=("case", "nunique"), cases=("case", unique_values),
    move_count=("move_count", "sum"), supplied_pattern_raw_cost=("pattern_raw_cost", "sum"),
    cases_with_known_amount=("amount_eur", "count"),
    known_claim_face_value_eur=("amount_eur", lambda s: s.sum(min_count=1)),
    risk_levels=("risk", unique_values), policy_overlay_flags=("policy_overlay_flags", unique_values),
)
# Move-only aggregates cannot reveal zero-move policy violations. Aggregate ALL
# cases separately by policy flags and risk, including synchronous-only traces.
policy_groups = cases.groupby(["policy_overlay_flags", "risk"], as_index=False).agg(
    case_count=("case", "size"), cases=("case", unique_values),
    supplied_raw_cost=("supplied_raw_cost", "sum"),
    known_claim_face_value_eur=("amount_eur", lambda s: s.sum(min_count=1)),
)
monthly = pd.DataFrame([
    ("Retry duplicate notifications", 74, "One connector; same message ID"),
    ("Core assessment gap with external document", 9, "Two of these nine lack a stable case/document ID"),
    ("Synchronous auto path violating P1/P2", 4, "Do not add the two illustrated cases to this count"),
    ("Denied/withdrawn among raw cost >= 4", pd.NA, "Most; numerator and denominator not supplied"),
], columns=["monthly_pattern", "reported_cases", "evidence"])

# A review queue supporting human judgment; neither sorting nor severity is a
# numerical loss model. The first four cases merit immediate/same-day review.
evidence_table = cases.sort_values("review_order")[[
    "review_order", "case", "business_severity_1_5", "confidence",
    "supplied_raw_cost", "pm4py_native_alignment_cost", "amount_eur", "risk",
    "recent_bank_change", "p1_auto_eligible", "p2_controls_required",

print(patterns.to_string(index=False))
```
