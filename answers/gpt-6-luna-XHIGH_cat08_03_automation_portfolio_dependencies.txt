## 2. Dependency and prerequisite map

Treat the process as a dependency network, not a single sequence. Financial document review and KYC are often concurrent even though the system records them sequentially. Committee packaging joins their outputs with normalized statements, collateral valuation when required, and credit-analysis notes. An improvement on one branch can simply make another branch or shared resource queue more visible.

- **Upstream data quality and routing:** A1 can improve identifiers and registration completeness; A2 must then classify product and exposure correctly. That classification determines the applicable control path and whether collateral is required. A2 errors can contaminate KYC routing, document checklists, valuation, and committee preparation. A5 depends on maintained, product-specific document requirements. A6 depends on receiving complete, legible documents and should preserve links from each extracted value to its source.
- **KYC, legal, and compliance controls:** A3 can prioritize hits but must leave clearance to authorized compliance staff. A4 assists entity resolution and should return unresolved or ambiguous identities to specialists. The observed KYC check → Resolve entity mismatch → KYC check loop (1,160 returns) is a genuine dependency and specialist workload, not an exception to erase. KYC clearance is a hard prerequisite to signing. A9 needs authoritative KYC and committee decision data, a current legal rule library, and a hard stop when either is missing or inconsistent. A12 can route remediation, but policy owners must settle ambiguous rules before they are automated.
- **Human escalation capacity:** A3 and A4 shift work toward the three compliance specialists. A5 shifts work toward customer support or RMs when customers cannot follow a checklist; incomplete RM instructions are already a source of missing-document loops. A6 reduces preparation time but may increase clean flow into the nine central Credit analysts. A8 still needs accountable review of assembled evidence. For each module, define who owns exceptions, their service target, and how capacity will be protected before broadening automation.
- **External valuation:** A7 can prioritize cases, match eligible work to valuers, and support parallel initiation once collateral need is known. It cannot automate away the 58h external wait. Equipment finance has a much higher valuation requirement than Working-capital, so evaluate routing and turnaround by product. Committee packaging must still wait for a valid valuation when required; do not mark that dependency complete just because a request was routed.
- **Core-banking integrations:** The first six months allow only two deep integrations. Choose two modules for that window and keep the rest in shadow or limited workflow pilots. A9 and A11 should depend on trusted, synchronized KYC, committee, contract, and account states; stale or partial feeds make downstream automation unsafe. Integration sequencing must include event/status reconciliation, not just screen or document generation.

## 3. Portfolio alternatives

Each option uses at most four modules. The quick-win and bottleneck portfolios are alternatives to the recommended control-balanced portfolio, not extra modules to stack on top of the annual budget.

- **Quick-win portfolio: A1, A2, A5, A10.** This optimizes visible clerical savings, routing quality, fewer missing-document loops, and faster signing. It sacrifices attention to the 31h entity-mismatch delay, analyst normalization workload, and contract-term remediation. A10 is conditional on an enforced KYC gate; it must not increase provisional signing. Roll out A1/A2 only with queue monitoring so cleaner and faster intake does not swamp KYC or analysts.
- **Bottleneck/synchronization portfolio: A4, A5, A6, A7.** This targets blocking mismatch cases, customer-document loops, financial normalization, and the external valuation handoff. It optimizes critical-path delay and readiness of committee inputs. It sacrifices direct high-frequency registration savings and automated contract drafting; it also cannot remove external valuer capacity variability or analyst judgment.
- **Compliance-first portfolio: A3, A4, A9, A12.** This strengthens KYC triage and entity resolution, contract rule checks, and remediation evidence. It optimizes control traceability and containment of known KYC/signing and contract-remediation risks. It sacrifices some customer/document and analyst cycle-time improvements. Its success depends on specialist escalation capacity, current legal rules, and resolving policy ambiguity before configuring A12.

## 5. Risks and compensating controls

- **Auditability and accountability:** Store the model/rule version, input data references, suggestion, confidence or reason, human override, final action, actor, and timestamp for every assisted decision. Keep a retrievable event trail through handoffs. A named employee remains accountable for KYC clearance, credit judgment, committee decision, legal terms, and funding controls.
- **False positives and false negatives:** For A3, optimize for safe escalation rather than autonomous clearance; review false negatives as severe incidents and sample cleared cases. For A4, never merge entities or clear a mismatch solely from a low-confidence match. For A2, require human confirmation for uncertain product/exposure routes. Track both unnecessary escalations and missed risk by segment.
- **Extraction errors and model drift:** Run A6 in shadow mode against analyst-verified values, stratify results by PDF quality, product, and applicant type, and retain source snippets. Monitor field-level corrections and downstream credit overrides. Revalidate after changes in forms, products, or document mix; pause affected fields when quality drifts.
- **Rule and data quality:** Version A9 legal rules, document their owner and effective date, and require Legal/compliance approval before release. Reconcile source decision, KYC status, contract terms, and activation status. Hard-stop if data is stale, absent, contradictory, or rule coverage is unknown. Maintain rollback to an approved manual process.
- **Exception handling and workload:** Define queues, owners, severity, and aging alerts for manual exceptions before launch. Measure human minutes per automated case and escalation backlog as well as automated throughput. Staff compliance specialists for A3/A4 peaks; reserve analyst validation capacity for A6; provide RMs with customer-ready guidance for A5. Avoid turning an automation exception into an unowned queue.
- **Signing and four-eyes controls:** Enforce a system-level prohibition on signing before KYC clearance, including provisional signing; record consent and signer identity. For High-exposure SME cases, prevent the same person from completing Credit analysis and Committee packaging, with a hard stop or independent approval before committee submission. Test month-end workloads specifically. Contract generation must reconcile to the committee decision; activation must remain blocked until KYC, approval, signing, and remediation controls are complete.
- **Customer friction and partner disputes:** Test A5 instructions with customers and RMs, allow a supported exception path, and monitor abandonment and repeat contacts. For A7, monitor valuer turnaround, reassignment, dispute rates, and valuation quality; retain human approval of valuation exceptions.

## 6. Recommended phased rollout

**Phase 0 — Baseline and control design (weeks 0–6).** Establish case-level baselines segmented by Micro/SME/High-exposure SME, product, time of day, and month-end. Reconcile event logs with actual concurrency, define exception ownership, and document current KYC, signing, four-eyes, contract, and activation gates. Set up dashboards for end-to-end elapsed time, queue age, loop rates, overrides, and resource demand.

**Phase 1 — First two deep integrations (months 2–6): A4, then A5.** Use the two first-six-month deep integration slots for the entity-resolution workflow and customer checklist/portal nudging. Pilot A4 with compliance specialists: recommendations only, explicit confidence and evidence, specialist approval, and no automatic KYC clearance. Pilot A5 on a limited set of products with RM review of the checklist and an easy customer-support path. This first wave strengthens KYC identity controls while addressing document loops. Keep A1 registration automation and A3 hit classification in shadow or manual-assist evaluation; do not consume another deep integration slot.

**Phase 2 — Financial normalization (months 7–9): A6.** Start in shadow mode with analyst comparison, then assist on high-quality documents with required analyst validation. Expand only by document type and segment after correction rates, source traceability, and analyst workload are acceptable. Coordinate intake to avoid simply moving the queue from normalization to Credit analysis.

**Phase 3 — Contract control and generation (months 9–12): A9.** Release only after Legal has approved a versioned rule set and KYC/decision feeds reconcile reliably. Begin with draft generation and pre-sign validation; allow automated generation only for cases that pass all checks. Require legal review for exceptions, Credit-line renewal edge cases, and changed rules. Keep activation independently gated.

**Monitoring and gate criteria:** At each stage compare pilot cohorts with matched baseline cases, including end-to-end cycle time and waiting, repeated document requests, mismatch age and returns, extraction corrections, contract-remediation transitions, human escalation minutes/backlog, customer abandonment, and downstream queue age. Report high-exposure four-eyes compliance and KYC-before-signing separately. Require complete audit records for every assisted action; no automation-caused signing-before-KYC or four-eyes bypass in testing; no material deterioration in control error or customer abandonment; and an exception backlog within the named teams' capacity before expanding. Investigate any breach or adverse segment drift, suspend the relevant automated action, and retain manual processing until corrected. Track released Back office time as redeployed capacity and end-to-end performance, not assumed headcount savings.

## 7. Final recommendation

Select the bottleneck portfolio with a control-balanced substitution: **A4, A5, A6, and A9**. It addresses the critical-path entity mismatch delay, repeated missing-document work, analyst-intensive normalization, and known contract-control loops. A4 and A9 strengthen controls in the first year; A5 tackles the RM/customer source of document rework; A6 improves the quality and readiness of analyst inputs. The order matters: establish reliable KYC and customer-document workflows first, validate normalization next, then generate contracts from reconciled KYC and committee data under approved rules.

Do not make A1 the sole first move just because Register application is nearly universal and easy to automate. It is a reasonable bounded quick win, and can be piloted, but its direct labor benefit is not automatically an end-to-end benefit: Back office utilization already falls below 65% after 15:00, while entity mismatch, analyst work, external valuation, and committee cadence create much larger waits for affected cases. Faster registration can expose or worsen those queues. The recommended portfolio therefore prioritizes delay, rework, and control dependencies over raw frequency, while preserving A1 for a later scale decision if measured redeployment and downstream capacity support it.
