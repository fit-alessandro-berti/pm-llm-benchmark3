## 1. Interpretive Assumptions and Decision Method

**Facts (not assumptions):**
- Normative sources have different issuers, scopes, and effective dates.
- The ERP outage affects evidential weight but not normative validity.
- The Chief Risk Officer (CRO) was unavailable and did not ratify ECD-2.
- Incident Command (IC) members are operational decision-makers but not explicitly budget owners.

**Assumptions:**
1. **Authority hierarchy:** Board-approved global policy (GPIP-4) > local SOP (DE-AAC-PROC-17) > incident directive (ECD-2) > CFO email, unless a lower source is more specific to the emergency context and does not conflict with mandatory integrity controls.
2. **Specificity principle:** A more specific rule (e.g., emergency procedures) overrides a general one (e.g., routine approvals) if it does not violate mandatory segregation of duties (SoD) or compliance controls.
3. **Later-in-time:** ECD-2 is later than GPIP-4 and the SOP but is time-limited and does not explicitly override all GPIP-4 controls. The CFO email is later than ECD-2 but lacks formal policy status.
4. **Emergency necessity:** Emergency status justifies deviations from competitive tendering and routine timing but not from mandatory integrity controls (e.g., SoD, Compliance clearance for new suppliers or high-value purchases).
5. **Retrospective evidence:** Contemporaneous records (e.g., supplier acknowledgments, signed PDFs) outweigh late ERP timestamps if they prove the act occurred within the required timeframe.
6. **Burden of proof:** The organization must demonstrate compliance with mandatory controls. Ambiguity in scope or authority favors a finding of noncompliance or indeterminacy.

**Decision method:**
- Assess each case at the time of the *controlling act* (e.g., Commit_to_Supplier, Emergency_Authorize).
- Apply the most specific applicable rule, then check for conflicts with higher-authority rules.
- If a conflict exists, determine whether the lower rule is a valid exception (e.g., ECD-2’s emergency scope) or an unauthorized deviation.
- Evaluate evidence for compliance with temporal and actor constraints.

## 5. Strongest Alternative Interpretations

1. **P-502 (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION  COMPLIANT):**
   - *Premise change:* ECD-2 implicitly waives Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers during emergencies.
   - *Credibility:* Weak. GPIP-4 (A3) requires Compliance_Clearance for new suppliers, and ECD-2 (C5) does not mention it. The CRO’s authority is reserved for exceptions.
   - *Why rejected:* Compliance controls are mandatory integrity safeguards, not "ordinary workflow steps."

2. **P-503 (NONCOMPLIANT  JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION):**
   - *Premise change:* Incident Command authorization satisfies SoD because IC members are independent of procurement.
   - *Credibility:* Medium. IC members are operational but not explicitly budget owners. GPIP-4 (A1) prohibits the same person from committing and approving, regardless of role.
   - *Why rejected:* PROC-3 acted as requester, committer, and IC authorizer, violating SoD. ECD-2 does not override R1.

3. **P-505 (INDETERMINATE  COMPLIANT):**
   - *Premise change:* Supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 proves commitment occurred before ECD-2 expiry.
   - *Credibility:* Medium. The ERP timestamp (18:06) is unreliable, but the supplier email is contemporaneous.
   - *Why rejected:* The burden of proof is on the organization. Without a clear commitment time, the case remains indeterminate.

4. **P-507 (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION  NONCOMPLIANT):**
   - *Premise change:* Finance_Approve is mandatory even under ECD-2, and the CFO email cannot override GPIP-4.
   - *Credibility:* High. GPIP-4 (A2) requires Finance_Approve for  EUR 50,000, and the CFO lacks authority to waive it.
   - *Why rejected:* The CFO email explicitly allows IC authorization to substitute for Finance_Approve  EUR 150,000, and P-507 is within this limit.

## 7. Recommended Policy Clarification

**Proposed GPIP-4 Amendment (Emergency Exceptions Clause):**

1. **Emergency Definition:**
   An emergency is an imminent safety harm, production stoppage  6 hours, or a time-bound incident directive (e.g., ECD-2) explicitly declaring an emergency. Non-production items (e.g., cafeteria equipment) are excluded unless explicitly listed in the directive.

2. **Authority Hierarchy:**
   - A signed incident directive (COO + Incident Commander) overrides:
     - Routine timing (PO before commitment).
     - Approved-supplier register.
     - Local SOP workflows (e.g., Plant_Approve).
   - It does **not** override:
     - Segregation of duties (R1).
     - Compliance clearance for new suppliers or purchases  EUR 100,000 (R3).
     - Finance_Approve for purchases  EUR 50,000 (R2), unless the CFO issues a written waiver for amounts  EUR 150,000.

3. **Incident Directive Requirements:**
   - Must specify:
     - Scope (e.g., "critical sterilization-line lots").
     - Time window ( 72 hours; extensions require CRO approval).
     - Which GPIP-4 controls are waived (e.g., "Finance_Approve for  EUR 150,000").
   - Must be ratified by the CRO within 48 hours of issuance.

4. **Retrospective Records:**
   - Commitments during ERP outages must be recorded in a manual log with:
     - Timestamp (from supplier acknowledgment or signed PDF).
     - Actor names.
     - Scope justification.
   - Logs must be uploaded to the ERP within 24 hours of system recovery.

5. **Safeguard Against Emergency Normalization:**
   - All emergency purchases must be reviewed by Compliance within 7 days.
   - The CRO must report to the Board quarterly on:
     - Number of emergency purchases.
     - Controls waived.
     - Root causes of emergencies.
