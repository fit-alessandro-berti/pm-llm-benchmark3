## 2. Competing Explanations and Timeline

### January–March: Stable Baseline
All metrics are healthy and stable: AUROC 0.81–0.82, Brier 0.130–0.132, ECE 0.022–0.025, label maturity 100%, variant mix constant (V1 ~45%, V2 ~35%, V3 ~20%). No deterioration. These months define the reference distribution for all subsequent comparisons.

### April: Schema Migration Pipeline Incident
On 1 April the workflow system renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. The feature pipeline recognized only the old label, so prediction prefixes were built at the wrong event or not at all. Events were duplicated and some assigned `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` (17% unknown activity share). The queue feature went missing in 14% of cases.

The metric collapse — AUROC 0.67, Brier 0.184, ECE 0.110, mean predicted risk 0.28 vs 0.19 observed — reflects **corrupted inputs, not model deterioration**. The model is being asked to predict on garbage features. Treating this as concept drift or population shift would be a category error: the model's internal logic is unchanged; the data feeding it is broken. The 18% unmapped/other variant share confirms that the pipeline could not even correctly identify the process path for a large fraction of cases.

**This month should be quarantined from training and evaluation until features are re-extracted.**

### May: Partial Recovery, Residual Pipeline Damage
The mapping patch restored activity recognition (unknown activity dropped to 2%, missing queue to 3%). Metrics partially recovered (AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050) but did not return to baseline. The cached "number of events before diagnosis" feature was not fully backfilled for April, and duplicate removal was applied only to the new source after 20 April. This month represents **incomplete pipeline repair**, not new drift. The remaining gap between May and baseline (ECE 0.050 vs ~0.024) is most parsimoniously explained by residual feature corruption.

### June: Routing Policy Change — Process Drift Without Concept Drift
The new remote-routing policy shifted V2 from ~35% to 52% of cases. The observed breach rate dropped to 0.15, consistent with V2's lower per-variant breach rate (~0.10). Mean predicted risk was 0.20, producing ECE 0.060 — the model overpredicts because the population now contains more low-risk V2 cases, but the model's overall mean prediction hasn't adjusted to the new mix.

Critically, **per-variant matured breach rates are unchanged**: V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35. The model's discrimination within each variant is likely intact. The miscalibration arises from the **population shift in variant mix**, not from the predictive relationship changing within any variant. This is process drift (the routing policy changed) causing a mix shift, but it is **not concept drift**. Retraining would be premature; recalibration or variant-conditional prediction would be more proportionate.

### July: Parts Supplier Begins Missing Targets — Early Concept Drift in V3
On 10 July the parts supplier began missing delivery targets. The Order PartPart Received edge jumped from 42h to 65h. V3 matured breach rate rose to 0.46 (from 0.35), while V1 (0.17) and V2 (0.10) remained stable. Label maturity was 98% — nearly complete.

This is the first signal of **genuine concept drift localized to V3**: the same V3-case features now predict a higher breach probability because an exogenous factor (parts delay) has changed the outcome-generating process. If the model lacks a parts-delay feature, P(breach|features) has shifted for V3 cases specifically. AUROC dropped to 0.76, consistent with the model's V3 predictions becoming miscalibrated while V1/V2 predictions remain accurate.

### August: Overlapping Deterioration
The parts issue became severe: Order PartPart Received jumped to 130h. Multiple simultaneous changes make August the first genuinely compound month:

1. **Process drift continues**: parts delivery time doubled from July.
2. **Concept drift in V3**: V3 breach rate reached 0.64 at 93% label maturity — this is not a label artifact; the matured rate is genuinely elevated.
3. **Population shift**: V3 share increased to 35% (from 22%), amplifying the overall breach rate (0.28).
4. **Early label immaturity**: 93% maturity means some breaches are unobserved, so the true rate may be slightly higher.

August cannot be reduced to a single cause. The V3-specific concept drift is real (0.64 at 93% maturity is far above the baseline 0.30–0.35), but the overall metrics are also influenced by the V3 mix increase and minor label censoring. AUROC 0.68 and Brier 0.197 reflect all three effects.

### September: Partner Z Compounds the Problem
Partner Z was onboarded (24% of cases), serving rural customers with older devices and routing 58% of cases through V3 (vs 36% for non-Z). V3 share reached 42%. Parts edge time reached 148h.

The compound now includes:
- **Continued concept drift in V3** (breach rate 0.61 at 82% maturity — likely understated).
- **Population shift**: Partner Z brings systematically different covariates (rural distance, device age) that are absent from the model. Partner Z breach rate is 0.45 vs 0.27, but Z labels mature more slowly.
- **Label immaturity**: 82% maturity, 42-day median delay. The observed overall breach rate (0.31) is almost certainly understated.
- Mean predicted risk (0.21) underpredicts relative to observed (0.31), but the gap is confounded by label immaturity.

September is the most complex month. The model is simultaneously facing a new population it wasn't trained on (Partner Z), an ongoing concept drift in its most failure-prone variant (V3 parts delay), and label measurements that are not yet trustworthy. ECE 0.140 is the worst in the series, but some of that gap will close as labels mature — and some will not.

### October: Measurement Artifact — Do Not Act
The outcome API developed a backlog. Label maturity collapsed to 55%. Median label delay reached 52 days. The observed breach rate of 0.16 is implausibly low — it is lower than January's 0.18 despite ongoing parts delays, Partner Z growth (27%), and V3 comprising 41% of cases. The API backlog disproportionately affects longer paths (V3), so the censoring is biased toward high-risk segments.

October's metrics (AUROC 0.64, Brier 0.205, ECE 0.130) **should not be compared to any prior month**. The apparent overprediction (mean risk 0.23 vs observed 0.16) is almost entirely a label-censoring artifact. The true breach rate for October is unknown and will be substantially higher than 0.16 once labels mature. Any model decision based on October data — retraining, recalibration, performance alerts — would be built on a false premise.

**October must be quarantined until labels mature to 90%.**

## 3. Diagnostic Tests

### Test 1: Label-Maturity Curves and Fixed-Vintage Backtesting

**Method**: For each monthly cohort, plot cumulative breach rate as a function of days since case start (e.g., at 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60+ days). Then re-evaluate all monthly cohorts at a fixed maturity threshold (e.g., only cases with 45 days of observation).

**Hypothesis A (October is pure label delay)**: The maturity curve for October rises steeply after the reporting cutoff and converges toward a rate comparable to or higher than September's 0.31. Fixed-vintage metrics at 45 days show October's performance is similar to September's.

**Hypothesis B (October also has genuine performance deterioration)**: Even at 45-day fixed vintage, October's AUROC and calibration are worse than September's, and the matured breach rate exceeds what label delay alone would predict.

**Falsification of label-delay-only**: If October's matured breach rate exceeds 0.31, then the 0.16 observed rate was not just delay but also unobserved genuine breaches — but this still confirms label delay was the dominant confound. If the matured rate is 0.18, then October genuinely had low breaches (unlikely given ongoing parts delays).

### Test 2: Recomputation After Activity-Label Normalization and Duplicate Cleaning

**Method**: Re-extract all features for April–May after: (a) replacing `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` with `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, (b) removing `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` events or flagging them, (c) deduplicating all events across both sources, (d) recomputing the cached "number of events before diagnosis" feature from scratch. Re-score the original model on cleaned features.

**Hypothesis A (April was pipeline failure)**: After cleaning, April's AUROC returns to 0.78, ECE drops to 0.04, mean predicted risk returns to ~0.19. The model's predictions on cleaned data are well-calibrated.

**Hypothesis B (April also had genuine deterioration)**: After cleaning, April's metrics remain degraded. This would suggest the schema rename coincided with a real process change — but given the evidence, this is unlikely.

### Test 3: Calibration and Discrimination Stratified by Variant, Source, and Month

**Method**: Compute AUROC, Brier, and calibration curves (reliability diagrams) separately for V1, V2, and V3 in each month. In September–October, further stratify by Partner Z vs non-Partner Z.

**Hypothesis A (June is mix shift without concept drift)**: Within-variant calibration and AUROC in June are similar to January–March. The overall miscalibration is fully explained by the changed variant proportions.

**Hypothesis B (June also has concept drift)**: V2 calibration in June is systematically off even within-variant, suggesting the routing policy also changed V2's outcome relationship. (Contradicted by stable V2 matured breach rate 0.09.)

**Hypothesis C (September Partner Z gap is population shift)**: Within V3, Partner Z and non-Partner Z show similar calibration once conditioned on available features. The overall gap is driven by Z's higher V3 share and different demographics.

**Hypothesis D (September Partner Z gap is model bias)**: Within V3 and after conditioning on rural/distance/device-age, Partner Z still shows higher predicted risk or worse calibration than non-Z, suggesting the model handles Z's feature combinations poorly.

### Test 4: Comparison of Process Variants and DFG/Performance-DFG Edges

**Method**: For each monthly case-start cohort, discover the DFG and performance DFG. Extract median and quartile elapsed times for key edges: `Order PartPart Received`, `DiagnosisRemote Fix`, `DiagnosisDispatch Technician`. Track variant counts and variant share over time.

**Hypothesis A (Parts delay is the dominant process driver for V3)**: The `Order PartPart Received` edge time correlates with V3 breach rate across months (42h0.35, 65h0.46, 130h0.64, 148h0.61). The `DiagnosisRemote Fix` edge remains stable (3.8–4.6h) and V2 breach rate is unchanged.

**Hypothesis B (Partner Z's routing, not parts delay, drives V3 increase)**: Within V3, Partner Z cases have higher breach rates than non-Z V3 cases, and the increase is explained by Z's demographics rather than parts delay. (Test by comparing Z-V3 vs non-Z-V3 breach rates in September.)

### Test 5: Conditional Outcome Relationship Tests

**Method**: Train a simple logistic regression on pre-July V3 cases using the model's existing features. Apply it to July–September V3 cases and compare predicted vs observed breach rates. Then add parts-edge-time as an additional feature and refit.

**Hypothesis A (Concept drift — missing feature)**: The pre-July model systematically underpredicts July–Sep V3 breaches. Adding parts-edge-time as a feature restores calibration, confirming the drift was driven by an unmodeled covariate that changed value (covariate shift in a missing feature) rather than a fundamental change in P(Y|all features).

**Hypothesis B (True concept drift — relationship changed)**: Even after adding parts-edge-time, the model underpredicts V3 breaches, meaning the relationship between parts delay and breach probability itself changed (e.g., the supplier's unreliability became more variable, not just slower).

### Test 6: Population Reweighting / Transport Checks for Partner Z

**Method**: Reweight September cases to the pre-Partner-Z variant distribution (e.g., V1:45%, V2:35%, V3:20%). Compare the reweighted overall breach rate to June's 0.15. Then apply a transport-style correction: estimate P(breach|features) on non-Z data and evaluate the expected breach rate at Z's feature distribution.

**Hypothesis A (Population shift explains the gap)**: After reweighting to the pre-Z variant mix, September's breach rate drops substantially. The transport estimate at Z's feature distribution (using non-Z model) matches Z's observed rate, confirming the gap is explained by feature distribution differences.

**Hypothesis B (Partner Z has a genuinely different outcome relationship)**: After reweighting and transport correction, Z's observed breach rate exceeds the transported prediction, meaning P(breach|features) is different for Z — true population-level concept drift, not just covariate shift.

### Test 7: Feature Missingness and Lineage Audits

**Method**: Compute missingness rates for every model feature by month. Trace each feature's dependency graph back to source events, identifying which features depend on the diagnosis activity. Verify whether the May backfill recomputed each dependent feature for April.

**Hypothesis A (April degradation is fully pipeline-induced)**: Missingness spikes in April are concentrated in diagnosis-dependent features (queue feature at 14%). After recomputation, missingness returns to ~1%.

**Hypothesis B (Some April features were correctly computed despite the rename)**: If the pipeline had partial fallback logic, some diagnosis-dependent features may have been computed correctly, and the degradation is driven by a subset of features. This would narrow the repair scope.

## 4. Recalibration-Versus-Retraining Decision

### Stage 1: Immediate Containment (Days 1–7)

- **Quarantine October data** from all model decisions. Apply a label-maturity gate: do not evaluate or train on any cohort with <90% maturity. For September, treat current metrics as provisional.
- **Quarantine April data** from training. May data is marginal — exclude unless features are fully recomputed.
- **Suppress all performance alerts** for months where data-quality or label-maturity monitors are red.
- **Communicate** to stakeholders that October's observed breach rate (0.16) is a measurement artifact, not a genuine improvement.

### Stage 2: Short-Term Recalibration (Weeks 2–4)

- **June recalibration**: Apply isotonic or Platt calibration per variant. The model's discrimination is likely fine; only the overall calibration is off due to the mix shift. Do not retrain.
- **V3-specific calibration adjustment for July–August**: If a parts-delay feature can be quickly engineered from the performance DFG (Order PartPart Received elapsed time), add it and recalibrate V3 predictions. If not, apply a V3-specific probability adjustment as an interim patch, clearly documented as a temporary measure.
- **Do not recalibrate on September–October** until labels mature further and pipeline issues are confirmed resolved.

### Stage 3: Feature Repair (Weeks 2–6)

- **Complete the April–May backfill**: Recompute all diagnosis-dependent features from scratch, including "number of events before diagnosis." Apply deduplication to all sources, not just the new source post-20 April.
- **Add missing features**:
  - Parts-edge elapsed time (Order PartPart Received) or a supplier-delay indicator — directly addresses V3 concept drift.
  - Rural/urban indicator and travel distance — addresses Partner Z population shift.
  - Device age — addresses Partner Z population shift.
  - Partner/source indicator — allows the model to learn source-specific adjustments.
  - Variant identifier — allows the model to condition predictions on the process path.
- **Add schema-migration regression tests**: Automated tests that verify feature extraction succeeds when activity labels are renamed, duplicated, or assigned generic labels.

### Stage 4: Full Retraining (Months 2–4, Conditions Dependent)

**Safe for training** (once features are clean and labels mature):
- January–March: fully mature, clean pipeline, stable process. Primary training set.
- June: mature labels, clean pipeline. Usable after adding variant features.

**Unsafe for training** until resolved:
- April–May: pipeline damage. Safe only after full feature recomputation and validation that cleaned April matches baseline distributions.
- July–August: partially immature (Aug 93%). Usable once labels mature to 95% AND parts-delay feature is added. Otherwise, the model will learn a V3 relationship that doesn't account for supplier delay.
- September: 82% maturity. Wait for maturation. Also, new features (rural, device age) must be added before training, or the model cannot distinguish Partner Z's population shift from concept drift.
- October: 55% maturity. Do not use until 90% maturity. Even then, evaluate separately to confirm the API backlog did not introduce systematic label bias.

**Retraining should wait until**: (1) all pipeline issues are resolved and validated; (2) new features (parts delay, rural, distance, device age, partner, variant) are engineered and validated; (3) September–October labels have matured to 90%; (4) the parts supplier situation has either stabilized or the parts-delay feature reliably captures its effect; (5) a fixed-vintage backtest confirms the new model outperforms the recalibrated old model on matured August–September data.

### Segment-Specific vs Unified Model

**Variant-specific calibration is justified immediately.** V1, V2, and V3 have fundamentally different breach rates (0.16, 0.10, 0.30–0.64) and different drivers. A single isotonic calibration curve cannot properly serve all three.

**A V3-specific sub-model is justified for retraining.** V3's outcome is driven by parts delivery timing and supplier reliability — factors that are irrelevant to V1 and V2. A V3-specific model with parts-delay features would capture the concept drift more directly than a unified model that must also serve V1/V2. V1 and V2 can share a unified model or be calibrated separately.

**A Partner-Z-specific model is not yet justified** without stratified evidence. The test in Section 3 (Test 6) must first determine whether Z's higher breach rate is explained by legitimate population differences (rural, device age, V3 share) or by a genuinely different outcome relationship. If the former, adding the new features to the unified/V3 model suffices. If the latter, a Z-specific model or Z-specific calibration may be warranted.

### Monthly Process-Evidence Extraction Code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py
from collections import Counter

def build_monthly_audit(csv_path: str = "field_service_events.csv") -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Extract auditable monthly process summaries from field-service event data.
    Does NOT fit a model; returns descriptive summaries only.
    """
    #  Load and format as PM4Py event log 
    df = pd.read_csv(csv_path)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )

    #  Normalize renamed diagnosis labels BEFORE feature extraction 
    #    Schema migration on 1 April renamed the activity. Map back.
    df["concept:name"] = df["concept:name"].replace(
        {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}
    )
    # Flag unmapped activities for audit; do not silently drop
    df["is_unknown_activity"] = df["concept:name"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY")

    #  Duplicate handling 
    #    New source duplicated events; keep first occurrence per
    #    (case, activity, timestamp) triple. Sort first to ensure
    #    deterministic "first" selection.
    df = df.sort_values("time:timestamp")
    df = df.drop_duplicates(
        subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"],
        keep="first",
    )

    #  Precompute case start timestamps for cohort assignment 
    case_start = df.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()

    records = []

    for month_start in pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-10-01", freq="MS"):
        month_end = month_start + pd.DateOffset(months=1)
        label = month_start.strftime("%Y-%m")

        #  Event-calendar cohort: all events whose timestamp falls 
        #    within this calendar month. Useful for data-quality
        #    monitoring (unknown-activity rate, duplicate count).
        month_events = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, month_start, month_end, mode="events"
        )
        unknown_pct = (
            month_events["is_unknown_activity"].mean() * 100
            if len(month_events) > 0
            else 0.0
        )

        #  Case-start cohort: all events for cases whose first event 
        #    falls in this month. Useful for outcome/performance
        #    monitoring (variant mix, breach rate, edge timings).
        start_cases = case_start[
            (case_start >= month_start) & (case_start < month_end)
        ].index
        cohort = df[df["case:concept:name"].isin(start_cases)]

        if len(start_cases) == 0:
            records.append({"month": label, "n_cases": 0})
            continue

        #  Variant discovery 
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort)
        variant_counts = {
            str(v): len(cases) for v, cases in variants.items()
        }

        #  Performance DFG for edge timing extraction 
        dfg, sa, ea = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cohort)

        # Extract median elapsed time for key edges
        edge_key_parts = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        edge_key_remote = ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")
        parts_edge = dfg.get(edge_key_parts)
        remote_edge = dfg.get(edge_key_remote)

        #  Temporal features (for distribution monitoring) 
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(cohort)

        records.append({
            "month": label,
            "n_cases": len(start_cases),
            "n_events_in_month": len(month_events),
            "n_variants": len(variants),
            "variant_counts": variant_counts,
            "unknown_activity_pct": round(unknown_pct, 2),
            "parts_edge_perf": parts_edge,
            "remote_edge_perf": remote_edge,
            "temporal_features_shape": temporal.shape,
        })

    return pd.DataFrame(records)


if __name__ == "__main__":
    summary = build_monthly_audit("field_service_events.csv")
    # Print concise monthly summary; full details available in DataFrame
    cols = ["month", "n_cases", "n_events_in_month", "unknown_activity_pct"]
    print(summary[cols].to_string(index=False))
```

**Interpretation notes for the two cohort types**:
- The **event-calendar cohort** (`month_events`) captures all events in a calendar month regardless of when the case started. It is appropriate for data-quality monitoring (unknown-activity rate, duplicate rate) because these are properties of the event stream at a point in time.
- The **case-start cohort** (`cohort`) captures all events for cases that started in a given month. It is appropriate for outcome and process-performance monitoring (variant mix, edge timings, breach rate) because outcomes are attributable to the cohort in which a case originated. Mixing these two cohorts would conflate data-quality issues (which are timestamp-based) with outcome performance (which is case-origin-based).

## 6. Risks of Acting on the Wrong Diagnosis

### Risk 1: Retraining on Immature Labels (October)

**Failure mode**: If October's 55%-maturity data is included in retraining, the model learns an artificially low breach rate (0.16) as the target. Once labels mature and the true rate proves to be ~0.30+, the model systematically underpredicts. High-risk V3 cases (which are disproportionately censored due to the API backlog affecting longer paths) are especially underrepresented, so the model becomes blind to the highest-risk segment.

**Mitigation**: Implement a hard label-maturity gate (90%) for any training or evaluation data. Use fixed-vintage backtesting: evaluate all monthly cohorts at the same days-since-start threshold. For October specifically, do not use the data for any model decision until labels mature to 90%. Add an automated check that rejects training data with median label delay >40 days.

### Risk 2: Calibrating Around a Pipeline Bug (April–May)

**Failure mode**: If April's overprediction (mean risk 0.28 vs observed 0.19) is treated as a calibration problem and an isotonic regression is fit to "correct" it, the calibration map learns a downward adjustment that is specific to the corrupted feature distribution. Once the pipeline is properly fixed and features return to their correct distribution, the same calibration map now overcorrects, causing underprediction. The fix becomes a new source of error.

**Mitigation**: Never calibrate on data with known pipeline issues. Fix the pipeline first (normalize labels, remove duplicates, recompute all derived features), then re-evaluate whether calibration adjustment is needed. Add a regression test that verifies feature extraction produces baseline-distribution outputs after schema changes. If calibration is still needed after pipeline repair, fit it only on clean months (Jan–Mar, post-clean April–May).

### Risk 3: Ignoring Genuine Supplier-Driven Concept Drift (August–September)

**Failure mode**: If all August–September degradation is attributed to label immaturity or Partner Z population shift, and the response is to "wait for labels to mature," the model continues to underpredict V3 breach risk during an active supply-chain crisis. Field technicians are not flagged for cases that will breach. Customer SLAs are violated. The concept drift in V3 is visible even in the matured subset (August V3: 0.64 at 93% maturity), so waiting does not resolve it.

**Mitigation**: Always stratify by variant before attributing degradation to label delay or population shift. V1 and V2 are stable, so any degradation in those segments is likely label-related. V3 shows genuine concept drift regardless of label maturity. Add a parts-delay feature immediately and apply a V3-specific calibration adjustment as an interim measure, even before full retraining.

### Risk 4: Attributing Partner Z's Higher Breach Rate to Unfair Model Behavior

**Failure mode**: Partner Z's observed breach rate (0.45) is 1.67× the non-Z rate (0.27). If this is interpreted as model bias against Z — and a fairness constraint or post-hoc adjustment is applied to equalize predicted risk across sources — the model would artificially lower Z's risk scores despite Z's genuinely higher risk (rural locations, older devices, more V3 routing). High-risk Z cases would not be flagged, and breaches would increase.

**Mitigation**: Conduct the stratified analysis described in Test 3 and Test 6 before drawing conclusions. Compare Z vs non-Z within the same variant and with similar feature values. If the gap is explained by V3 over-representation, rural distance, and device age (all legitimate risk factors), the model is not biased — it is correctly identifying higher risk. The remedy is to add the missing features (rural, distance, device age) so the model can explain the gap transparently, not to constrain the model to ignore it. Only if the gap persists after conditioning on all available features should model bias be investigated.

### Risk 5: Treating June's Mix Shift as Model Failure and Retraining Prematurely

**Failure mode**: June shows ECE increasing to 0.060 and overall AUROC appearing to decline. If this triggers a full retraining, resources are wasted on a model whose per-variant discrimination is unchanged. The retrained model may overfit to the new V2-heavy distribution and perform worse when the mix shifts again. The retraining also introduces a new model version that is harder to audit against the stable January–March baseline.

**Mitigation**: Always evaluate per-variant metrics alongside overall metrics. If per-variant performance is stable (as it is in June: V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35 — all within baseline), the correct response is recalibration to the new mix, not retraining. Add variant-conditional calibration and variant-aware features so the model can adapt to mix shifts without full retraining. Reserve retraining for cases where per-variant discrimination is genuinely degraded (as in V3 from July onward).
