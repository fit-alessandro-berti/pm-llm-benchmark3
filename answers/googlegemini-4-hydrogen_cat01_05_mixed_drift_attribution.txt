## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Core Structural Stratum** | `month` × `supplier_group` (Supplier Z vs. Other) × `complexity_tier` (Standard vs. High) | Isolate population/case-mix shifts (Supplier Z disruption, seasonal complexity) from conditional process behavior. |  30 completed cases per cell per month (pool adjacent low-volume weeks if needed; suppress/flag cells < 30). | Within-stratum rework rates, DFG distances, and mapped fitness across May, Sep, and Oct–Nov. | Conditioning on post-arrival assigned complexity could introduce bias if assessors reclassify complexity *after* encountering missing docs. |
| **Tier 2: Priority & Capacity Regime** | `time_regime` (Pre-Mig, Post-Mig, Supplier-Shock, Post-Staffing/Priority, Recovery, Renewal, Dec-Intervention) × `complexity_tier` | Test Jul 1 priority redistribution and Jun 15 staffing impact with sufficient statistical power for tail quantiles (P90). |  100 cases per regime-complexity cell (required for stable P90/P95 waiting-time estimation). | Pre/post Jul 1 wait-time quantiles for High vs. Standard complexity; Little's Law queue diagnostics. | Regime boundaries close together (Jun 15 vs. Jul 1) risk mixing transient backlog carry-over across regime windows. |
| **Tier 3: Operational Pathway / Variant Class** | `supplier_group` × `rework_flag` (0 vs. 1 `Request Info`) | Separate pure queue/service performance drift from rework-induced cycle-time inflation. |  40 cases per cell; use rolling 4-week windows in low-rework baseline months (Jan–Feb). | Cycle time of zero-rework (`fast_variant`) cases over time to isolate pure queueing delay from Supplier Z document defects. | **High conditioning/collider bias:** conditioning on `rework_flag` conditions on a post-treatment outcome influenced by both supplier quality and staff discretion. |
| **Tier 4: Team / Resource Cluster** | `team_or_region` × `activity_group` (Legacy vs. Migrated) | Audit resource missingness, localized migration compliance, and team-level workload redistribution post-Jun 15. |  50 events per team-activity cell and resource completeness  90% (otherwise flag as data-quality audit only). | Service-time shifts and `Senior Assessment` logging consistency across regional teams. | Excluding cases with missing resources creates severe selection bias because missingness concentrates in migrated steps and specific teams. |

**Primary monitoring population:** **Tier 1 (`supplier_group` × `complexity_tier`, anchored on the Non-Supplier-Z, Standard-Complexity cohort as the core control sentinel, tracked alongside the full Tier 1 matrix)**.
- *Why:* Non-Supplier-Z Standard-Complexity cases represent the largest homogeneous segment with minimal exposure to both the Supplier Z API disruption and the October seasonal complexity surge, while remaining directly exposed to internal staffing cuts and priority-rule spillover. Monitoring it alongside the other three Tier 1 cells prevents overaggregation (which hides priority redistribution and supplier effects) and avoids tiny cells (each Tier 1 cell comfortably exceeds 50–300 cases/month), while avoiding the collider bias of conditioning on downstream rework outcomes.

| **11. **Handling Seasonality and Multiple Testing:**
- **Seasonality:** Every volume, case-mix, and routing monitor (`DFG`, `Case Mix`, `Outcomes`) uses a **dual-reference design**: comparing simultaneously against a *short-term rolling window* (to catch sudden non-seasonal shocks like May 5 or Jun 15) and a *year-over-year same-calendar-window* (to verify whether Q4 shifts match historical renewal elasticity). When historical data span < 1 year, seasonal-trend decomposition (STL) or complexity-standardized rates prevent flagging expected Oct–Nov complexity increases as structural anomalies.
- **Multiple Testing:** Running 11 monitors across multiple Tier-1 strata weekly inflates family-wise false positives. We apply **Benjamini-Hochberg False Discovery Rate (FDR) control ($q = 0.05$)** across stratified hypothesis tests within each weekly monitoring batch, paired with **persistence rules** (requiring 2 consecutive weekly breaches or an accompanying effect-size floor, e.g., +5pp or PSI > 0.15) before escalating from automated diagnostic logging to operational intervention.

def prepare_pm4py_dataframe(
    raw_df: pd.DataFrame,
    case_col: str = "case_id",
    activity_col: str = "activity",
    timestamp_col: str = "timestamp",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Format an event-level DataFrame for PM4Py without mutating the caller's DataFrame."""
    import pm4py

    df_copy = raw_df.copy()
    df_copy[timestamp_col] = pd.to_datetime(df_copy[timestamp_col], utc=True)
    formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_copy,
        case_id=case_col,
        activity_key=activity_col,
        timestamp_key=timestamp_col,
    )
    return formatted.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)


def create_normalized_log_view(
    formatted_df: pd.DataFrame,
    label_mapping: Optional[Dict[str, str]] = None,
    dedup_activity: str = "Open Case",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """
    Create a normalized copy of the event log without overwriting raw labels.
    Applies semantic label mapping and removes consecutive duplicated technical events per case.
    """
    if label_mapping is None:
        label_mapping = {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}

    norm_df = formatted_df.copy()
    norm_df["raw_concept:name"] = norm_df["concept:name"]
    norm_df["concept:name"] = norm_df["concept:name"].replace(label_mapping)

    if dedup_activity:
        norm_df = norm_df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).reset_index(drop=True)
        prev_act = norm_df.groupby("case:concept:name")["concept:name"].shift(1)
        is_dup_tech = (norm_df["concept:name"] == dedup_activity) & (prev_act == dedup_activity)
        norm_df = norm_df.loc[~is_dup_tech].reset_index(drop=True)
    return norm_df


def slice_window(
    formatted_df: pd.DataFrame,
    start_dt: str,
    end_dt: str,
    mode: str = "traces_contained",
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Slice an event log using PM4Py time-range filtering."""
    import pm4py

    return pm4py.filter_time_range(formatted_df, start_dt, end_dt, mode=mode)


def compute_monthly_dfg_and_variants(
    formatted_df: pd.DataFrame,
    month_col: Optional[str] = None,
) -> Dict[str, Dict[str, Any]]:
    """Compute monthly DFGs and variant counts using PM4Py."""
    import pm4py

    df = formatted_df.copy()
    if month_col is None:
        df["_month"] = df["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
        month_col = "_month"

    results: Dict[str, Dict[str, Any]] = {}
    for month, sub_df in df.groupby(month_col):
        dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_dfg(sub_df)
        raw_variants = pm4py.get_variants(sub_df)
        variant_counts = {
            k: (len(v) if isinstance(v, list) else int(v))
            for k, v in raw_variants.items()
        }
        results[str(month)] = {
            "dfg": dfg,
            "start_activities": start_acts,
            "end_activities": end_acts,
            "variants": variant_counts,
            "n_cases": sub_df["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        }
    return results

## 9. Provisional attribution

The observed 12-month trajectory reflects **four distinct, overlapping mechanisms** rather than a single process failure.

Our **strongest conclusions** (high confidence) concern the separation of March logging drift from subsequent operational drift. The March 1 system migration cleanly accounts for the sudden `raw_model_fitness` collapse (0.93 to 0.55), increased distinct labels (9 to 11), duplicate `Open Case` events (4–6%), and elevated resource missingness (4.8–6.5%), because label mapping restores conformance to 0.90–0.91 and normalized DFG distance remains minimal (L1=3). Equally strong is the attribution of the May rework spike (`Assess -> Request Info` doubling from 12 to 24 per 100 cases) and September partial recovery to **Supplier Z's May 5 disruption and September 10 recovery**.

**Plausible but less tightly bounded conclusions** (moderate confidence) relate to the June 15 staffing cut, July 1 priority rule, and December recovery. The sharp P90 divergence (84h in May to 110h in August) alongside flat `Assess` service times (4.8h to 5.4h) strongly points to **capacity-driven pre-`Assess` queueing** after four assessors departed. It is also plausible that the July 1 priority rule protected high-complexity wait times at the expense of standard-case tails, and that October's volume/complexity surge (41–45% high complexity, `Assess -> Senior Assessment` rising to 22/100) reflects **annual renewal seasonality**.

**Mediation and interaction prevent clean additive attribution** in three places: Supplier Z's May share jump (18% to 43%) is confounded with regional/complexity selection; rework loops inject repeat arrivals into an already understaffed queue, creating super-additive delay; and December's partial improvement coincides with both post-renewal load decline and the December 5 overtime/quality interventions.

I would **revise this account** if event-level logs showed that: (a) rework also doubled in non-Supplier-Z, matched-complexity cases in May; (b) standard-case wait times did not worsen relative to high-complexity cases after July 1; (c) October `Senior Assessment` increases occurred solely through re-labeling previously unrecorded senior reviews; or (d) historical 2025 Q4 data showed no comparable complexity surge.
