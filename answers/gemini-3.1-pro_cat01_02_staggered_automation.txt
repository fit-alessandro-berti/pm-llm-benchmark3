## 1. What is observed

The dataset provides branch-month operational aggregates demonstrating the staggered rollout of an automated triage system. **Availability of automation** was introduced in April 2026 for Alpha and Beta, and in July 2026 for Gamma, while Delta and Epsilon remained entirely untreated. However, availability does not equate to immediate full application; **actual use (treatment intensity)**, represented by the percentage of cases traversing the automated fast path, ramped up gradually from a baseline of 4-6% to approximately 50% in treated branches over several months.

**Branch selection into rollout** was highly endogenous. Alpha and Beta were explicitly chosen because of their historically poor cycle times and high volume, heavily exposing the analysis to regression-to-the-mean biases. The data is further complicated by **contemporaneous operational changes**: a central fraud desk opened in March, driving up escalations globally; a routing campaign directed 5-10% more volume to treated branches; and a product change in May triggered a universal spike in case complexity and cycle times. 

Finally, **outcome measurement limitations** severely restrict direct interpretation. Aggregated branch-month medians and p90s mathematically obscure the underlying bimodal distribution of fast-tracked versus specialist-escalated cases. Spillovers exist via unrecorded waiting times at the centralized fraud desk, and the `csat_1to5` variable is compromised by varying response rates (20-45%), preventing robust claims regarding customer satisfaction.

*Note on SUTVA:* The existence of a centralized fraud desk serving all branches means that increased escalations from Alpha/Beta likely congest the queue for Delta/Epsilon. This interference violates SUTVA, meaning control branches do not represent a pure, unpolluted counterfactual.

## 3. Identification strategy

| strategy | treatment timing representation | comparison group | adjustment variables | estimand targeted | identifying assumptions | diagnostic checks | main failure mode |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary: Staggered Event-Study DiD** | Cohort indicators (April, July) across relative time | Not-yet-treated + never-treated (Delta, Epsilon) | `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals`/`fte` (workload) | `Dynamic_ITT` | Parallel trends post-adjustment; no anticipation; SUTVA holds | Pre-trend event-study plots; leave-one-out robustness | SUTVA violations (fraud desk congestion slows control branches) |
| **Alt 1: Treatment Intensity DiD** | Continuous dose (`automated_fast_path_pct`) | Branches with zero dose | `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals`/`fte` | `ATET_Intensity` | Intensity is exogenous conditional on observables | Residual correlation checks against capacity | Intensity is endogenously reduced when teams are overwhelmed |
| **Alt 2: Interrupted Time Series (ITS)** | Step function + ramp | Within-branch historical baseline | `high_complexity_pct`, `arrivals`/`fte` | `ITT_Median` | Break in trend is solely due to automation rollout | Autocorrelation function analysis | Contemporaneous shocks (April routing) completely mirror adoption |

A modern staggered-adoption event-study design (such as Callaway-Sant'Anna), heavily supplemented by covariate adjustment, is the preferable primary strategy for these data. A naïve two-way fixed-effects (TWFE) regression is inappropriate here because treatment timing is staggered (April vs. July) and treatment effects are dynamic, growing as system intensity ramps up. Standard TWFE produces “forbidden comparisons” by incorrectly using earlier-treated units (Alpha/Beta) as negative-weighted controls for later-treated units (Gamma), artificially suppressing the effect size.

Furthermore, adjusting for observable time-varying confounders—specifically `high_complexity_pct` and the workload ratio—is structurally critical. The May product-complexity shock and the April targeted routing campaigns occurred concurrently with the rollout, creating massive secular spikes in cycle time that mask the automation’s benefits in raw pre/post differences. By leveraging the never-treated branches as the primary comparison group in a cohort-specific framework, we can net out the universal May complexity shock. While the extremely small number of clusters (5 branches) invalidates asymptotic errors, the staggered framework still isolates the treatment trajectory most cleanly against secular network shocks.

## 5. Quantitative evidence

**1. Adopter Pre/Post Changes (Raw Averages):**
*Alpha (Adopted April):* Pre (Jan-Mar) vs Post (Apr-Aug)
Median: 48.9h → 48.5h | P90: 98.5h → 108.1h | Rework: 13.2% → 13.7% | Esc: 8.5% → 11.4%
*Beta (Adopted April):* Pre (Jan-Mar) vs Post (Apr-Aug)
Median: 46.1h → 47.8h | P90: 92.9h → 102.7h | Rework: 12.8% → 12.2% | Esc: 8.2% → 10.7%
*Gamma (Adopted July):* Pre (Jan-Jun) vs Post (Jul-Aug)
Median: 40.6h → 37.2h | P90: 85.8h → 84.9h | Rework: 13.1% → 12.6% | Esc: 8.2% → 10.7%

**2. Comparison Against Not-Yet-Treated (Difference-in-Differences Intuition):**
In April, when Alpha and Beta adopted automation, control branches suffered a secular cycle time degradation (Delta median +2.4h, Epsilon median +1.9h). In contrast, Alpha's median *dropped* by 1.1h, and Beta's rose by only 0.9h. The treated branches clearly outperformed the secular trend by 1.0 to 3.5 hours. Similarly, in July, Gamma's median dropped by 7.5h compared to a milder 3-4h drop in controls.

**3. Treatment Intensity Analysis:**
As `automated_fast_path_pct` rises from 4% to 50%, absolute median cycle times rose in May/June. However, an analysis adjusting for the May `high_complexity_pct` shock reveals that high-intensity branches absorbed the complexity shock better than zero-intensity branches. The automation buffered the impact.

**4. Pre-trends and Regression to the Mean:**
Alpha and Beta had structurally worse cycle times pre-rollout, creating a strong risk that any improvement is mere regression to the mean. However, their Jan-Mar baselines were remarkably flat/stable. The sudden outperformance strictly aligns with the April rollout and the April routing campaign, making treatment a much stronger explanatory variable than random mean reversion.

**5. Sensitivity and Bounded Conclusions:**
*Median Cycle Time:* The causal effect of availability is likely bounded between a 1.5h and 4.0h reduction, masked in raw data by the May complexity shock.
*P90 Cycle Time:* Bounded between 0.0h (neutral) and +5.0h (harm). Fast-pathing easy cases means the specialist queues become highly concentrated with difficult cases, potentially amplifying extreme tail delays, exacerbated by the fraud desk bottleneck.

## 8. Rollout recommendation

- `expand with an embedded evaluation`

Next branch or subset: Rollout should proceed to Epsilon, as it handles high volumes and complex case mixes, making it highly representative of the broader network's structural challenges while leaving Delta as an ongoing clean control.

Treatment assignment or phase-in mechanism: Rather than flipping a branch-wide switch, Epsilon must undergo a true case-level randomized controlled trial (A/B test). During phase-in, 50% of incoming Epsilon cases should be randomly assigned to the legacy manual triage path, while the other 50% are eligible for automated triage. This solves the small-cluster confounding problem by generating intra-branch randomization and bypassing the SUTVA issues inherent in branch-to-branch comparisons.

Minimum pre-period and post-period measurements: Maintain a minimum 8-week case-level post-period. Because Epsilon currently serves as an 8-month baseline control, the pre-period is sufficiently established.

Process-mining and queue-state data: Aggregate branch-month statistics must be abandoned. The evaluation requires granular event logs capturing exact timestamps for `Registration`, `Queue Entry`, `Triage Completion`, `Specialist Review Entry/Exit` (crucially including the central fraud desk), and `Close`. We must capture queue depth at the moment of routing to evaluate congestion directly. 

Spillover monitoring: Central fraud-desk queues are the primary SUTVA vulnerability. The evaluation must actively track fraud-desk turnaround times; if Epsilon’s automation floods the desk, the cycle-time degradation for Alpha, Beta, and Gamma must be accounted for as a negative operational spillover. 

Guardrails for customer harm: Implement a real-time tail-risk watchdog. If a case in the manual or specialist queue breaches 120 hours (approaching the historical p90), it must trigger an immediate SLA alert for human surge intervention. 

Decision rule for scale-up: Full organizational scale-up (to Delta and 100% of Epsilon) will be approved only if the case-level evaluation demonstrates a statistically significant (p < 0.05) >5% reduction in median cycle time for the automated arm, with no statistically significant increase in rework percentage, and stable wait times at the central fraud desk.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Automation adoption reached approximately 50% fast-path usage while simultaneously escalating more cases to the central fraud desk.
- `Suggestive:` Automation buffered treated branches against a concurrent volume and complexity shock, reducing median cycle times relative to the control group trend.
- `Conditionally causal:` Assuming parallel trends and adjusting for case-mix changes, system availability causes a 1-to-3 hour reduction in typical case cycle times.
- `Not supported:` Automation improves tail (p90) cycle times or universally increases customer satisfaction (CSAT) scores.
