# 1. Disruption timeline

*   **T0 (Baseline, Day -30 to -1):** Normal operations handling 42,000 cases with high normative model fitness (0.96) and predictable cycle times (8h critical to 36h bulk). Compliance and traceability are strongly maintained in real-time, with exceptions well under 1%.
*   **T1 (Incident onset, Day 0 morning):** WMS becomes unavailable due to a cyber incident, halting the standard automated process flow and blinding the organization to real-time inventory and authorization states.
*   **T2 (Manual workaround phase, Day 0 afternoon to Day 2):** Operations pivot to manual improvisations. The operational state is chaotic (fitness drops to 0.71, fitting traces 44%). Teams successfully protect Hospital-critical orders (91% fill rate) by deferring non-critical orders. However, the risk posture shifts to severe concealed noncompliance: traceability exceptions spike to 9.5%, rework to 12.5%, and critical controls (scans, quality checks) are frequently skipped to maintain throughput.
*   **T3 (Partial recovery, Day 3 to Day 6):** Order intake systems return but inventory remains out of sync. Model fitness improves (0.82). The operational state blends manual reconciliation with system processing. Risk posture improves but remains elevated as traceability exceptions sit at 5.4%. 
*   **T4 (Stabilization, Day 7 to Day 21):** Cycle times and front-line throughput return to baseline levels. Model fitness recovers to 0.90. However, the operational state masks a massive, hidden post-incident workload. The risk posture is characterized by heavy reliance on "after-the-fact" audit remediation (Audit remediation -> END: 2100) to reconstruct the compliance that was skipped during T2 and T3.

# 3. Resilience versus concealed noncompliance

In this context, **resilience** is the organization's ability to absorb the cyber shock and adapt its processes to protect its core mission—delivering critical life-safety supplies to hospitals—while gracefully degrading non-critical functions. Adaptations like "Defer noncritical order" (I) and the "Hospital-critical fast lane" (A) demonstrate true resilience; they explicitly trade overall throughput and bulk volume to preserve capacity for high-risk, high-urgency needs, utilizing "Temporary release controls" (F) to maintain safety. 

Conversely, **concealed noncompliance (risky shortcuts)** occurs when adaptations maintain the *appearance* of normal operational throughput but silently drop critical safety, traceability, and regulatory controls. "Skip system authorization check" (C) and "Dispatch without scan" (G) are prime examples. They preserve physical volume but void the pharmaceutical chain of custody, creating unmanaged downstream liabilities. 

Some adaptations are **ambiguous** depending on timing. "Manual inventory snapshot checks" (D) represent resilience in the first hours of an outage, but as the snapshot grows stale, it transforms into a risky shortcut that drives severe rework (12.5% in T2). Finally, relying on massive "Audit remediation" (L) creates a false sense of security; post-incident documentation reconstruction does not retroactively ensure real-time patient safety or compliance during the manual phase.

# 4. Recovery process model

**Trigger conditions:** 
*   WMS or core authorization system unavailable for > 60 minutes.
*   Mandatory network isolation due to a declared cyber incident.

**Triage rules:**
*   Halt all Wholesale-bulk orders immediately.
*   Route Pharmacy-standard to the backlog queue unless tagged as life-safety.
*   Activate "Hospital-critical fast lane" for immediate offline processing.

**Minimum controls by product/customer risk:**
*   **Ambient:** Validate against cached offline customer-eligibility list. Paper pick list. Mandatory paper manifest signed by courier.
*   **Cold-chain:** In addition to Ambient rules, mandatory paper temperature-logger attachment at Pack, verified visually by Quality Release team.
*   **Controlled-substance:** In addition to Ambient rules, mandatory dual-pharmacist visual check and dual-signature on paper manifest at Release.

**Manual evidence capture:**
*   Implement offline-capable batch barcode scanners to record Pick, Pack, and Dispatch events.
*   If scanners fail, use serialized, pre-printed tripartite paper manifests (Warehouse copy, Courier copy, Customer copy).

**Backlog prioritization (upon system return):**
1.  Hospital-critical (new and backlogged)
2.  Delayed Cold-chain (to minimize spoilage risk)
3.  Pharmacy-standard
4.  Wholesale-bulk

**Reconciliation and audit-remediation flow:**
*   *Step 1:* Dedicated recovery team docks offline scanners to batch-upload timestamps and traceability data.
*   *Step 2:* System auto-matches offline dispatch records with WMS orders.
*   *Step 3:* Inventory synchronization (Reconcile manual reservation) executes before opening standard order intake.
*   *Step 4:* Exceptions (missing signatures, temp docs) are routed to a ring-fenced Audit Remediation queue, isolating this workload from front-line T4 operations.

# 5. Resilience indicators

**Leading indicators:**
*   **Offline Cache Freshness:** Time elapsed since the last automated export of the offline inventory and authorization lists (target < 12h).
*   **Triage Effectiveness:** Ratio of critical to non-critical orders entering the manual processing queue (high ratio indicates effective load shedding).
*   **Control Completeness at Pack:** Percentage of manual orders possessing full physical signatures and temperature documentation prior to dispatch.

**Lagging indicators:**
*   **Service Protection:** Hospital-critical fill rate vs. baseline during the disruption.
*   **Traceability Gap Rate:** Percentage of orders requiring after-the-fact manual reconstruction of the chain of custody.
*   **Hidden Workload:** Total person-hours spent in the `Invoice close -> Audit remediation` loop after cycle times have normalized.
*   **Backlog Age:** Time required to process deferred Wholesale-bulk and Pharmacy-standard orders post-recovery.
*   **Fairness across groups:** Variance in cancellation rates between protected (Hospital) and degraded (Pharmacy/Wholesale) customer tiers.

# 6. Recommendations for future disruptions

1.  **Deploy Offline-Capable Batch Scanners (Priority 1)**
    *   *Implementation effort:* Medium. 
    *   *Expected benefit:* High. Captures real-time Pick and Dispatch data offline, eliminating "Dispatch without scan" and drastically reducing downstream "Audit remediation".
    *   *Residual risk:* Hardware failure, battery depletion, or physical loss of scanners during the incident.
2.  **Automate Daily Offline Caching of Authorizations (Priority 1)**
    *   *Implementation effort:* Low.
    *   *Expected benefit:* High. Allows compliance teams to perform "Manual authorization checks" instantly, preventing the dangerous "Skip system authorization check" workaround.
    *   *Residual risk:* Up to 24 hours of staleness; recently suspended licenses might erroneously be approved.
3.  **Formalize Paper-Based Temporary Release Controls (Priority 2)**
    *   *Implementation effort:* Low to Medium.
    *   *Expected benefit:* Medium. Explicitly adds Cold-chain temperature checks to the manual Pharmacist release process, closing the 4.8% missing documentation gap.
    *   *Residual risk:* Human error and compliance fatigue inherent in paper-based manual checks.
4.  **Establish a Ring-Fenced Reconciliation Team (Priority 3)**
    *   *Implementation effort:* Medium (requires cross-training).
    *   *Expected benefit:* Medium. Separates front-line T4 operations from the massive T3/T4 hidden workload of manual reservations and audit remediation.
    *   *Residual risk:* Diverts some staff capacity away from clearing the physical order backlog.

# 7. Management trade-off statement

Management must formally accept the severe degradation of overall throughput and the intentional deferral of Wholesale and standard Pharmacy orders during a system outage in order to protect Hospital-critical supply capacity. However, management must explicitly not accept the silent abandonment of traceability (e.g., dispatching without scans) or authorization controls (e.g., assuming ambient product authorization). We must trade total case volume for verifiable safety and compliance. This recommendation to strictly enforce manual compliance chokepoints would only change if regulatory bodies issue explicit, documented emergency waivers permitting relaxed traceability standards during declared cyber incidents, allowing us to legally prioritize raw physical flow over chain-of-custody documentation.
