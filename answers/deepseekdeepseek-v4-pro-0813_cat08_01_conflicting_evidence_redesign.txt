## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The DFG shows a process dominated by the fast auto path: 3,600 of 5,000 cases move from intake to auto eligibility screen, 2,550 auto-screened cases go to fast approval, and 2,300 fast approvals go directly to dispatch replacement. This path is fast in nominal terms: intake-to-auto p90 is 1.8 hours, auto-to-fast approval p90 is 4.0 hours, fast approval-to-dispatch p90 is 8.0 hours, and dispatch-to-close p90 is 14.0 hours.

But the fast path is not clean.

**Main quality and rework problems**

- Fast approval cases have a complaint/reopen rate of 14.1%, compared with only 3.3% for cases following the repair-quote path.
- Fast approval is 31% cheaper before rework, but only 9% cheaper after accounting for complaints, reshipments, and service credits.
- Dispatch replacement-to-customer complaint/reopen is a long-tail pattern: mean 96 hours, median 70 hours, p90 180 hours. This is linked to incorrect replacement part selection.
- Request missing evidence occurs at least twice in 26% of cases that enter it. The edge request missing evidence-to-manual eligibility review has mean 34 hours, median 26 hours, p90 88 hours.
- Rework repair occurs in 12.5% of repaired high-value devices, compared with 4.0% of low-risk devices.

**Compliance problems**

- Token-based replay fitness is 0.91, but only 68% of traces fit the normative Petri net.
- Frequent move-on-log deviations include fast approval before evidence completeness, replacement dispatch without risk review for high-value devices, and customer complaint/reopen after close.
- R1 requires manual eligibility review or fraud/risk review before fast approval for claims above EUR 400.
- R2 requires evidence completeness before replacement dispatch, except for Premium low-risk accessory claims.
- R3 requires any reopened claim to pass manual eligibility review before final close or denial.
- Precision is low because many low-frequency paths combine auto approval, risk review, and repair in ways not allowed by policy.

**Customer-experience and segment problems**

- Premium low-risk accessory cases on the fast path have median cycle time 8 hours, but on manual path 46 hours. Since service-credit exposure is high above 24 hours, manually routing these cases is often worse.
- Premium high-value devices on the fast path have median cycle time 20 hours, but complaint/reopen is 22%, versus 5% on reviewed/repair path.
- Standard high-value devices on the fast path have median cycle time 28 hours, but complaint/reopen is 18%, versus 6% on reviewed/repair path.
- The unclear defect/missing evidence segment has median cycle time 140 hours and the highest abandonment rate.

**Resource problems**

- Manual eligibility review has 7 reviewers and queue pressure grows sharply above 82% utilization.
- Fraud/risk review is a bottleneck with only 2 specialists, especially on Mondays and after marketing campaigns.
- Repair quote delay is mostly waiting for part-price confirmation.
- Quality check is underused except after repair campaigns.
- Escalation board has only one daily 45-minute slot.

**The central contradiction**

The most frequent path is attractive on nominal speed and cost, but produces more downstream complaints, rework, and compliance exceptions. Slower reviewed/repair paths are more expensive and slower, but have better quality and customer outcomes for high-value or complex claims. This means average cycle-time optimization is misleading. The process needs segment-specific rebalancing: preserve fast paths where speed is safe, and insert controls where the hidden cost of error is high.

## 2. Redesign A: Segment-aware risk routing with front-end compliance hold points

**Description**

Replace the outdated one-size auto screen with segment/value/defect-aware routing rules. The new flow is:

- Intake claim -> segment-aware triage.
- Premium low-risk accessory and Standard low-risk accessory with claim amount at or below EUR 400 and evidence already complete may go: Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement.
- High-value devices, claims above EUR 400, unclear-defect cases, reopened claims, and cases with prior evidence loops must go: Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before any Fast approval.
- Add a compliance hold before Fast approval to check R1 and R2 conditions.
- If evidence is incomplete, route to Request missing evidence before final approval or dispatch.
- If a high-value device has not had risk review, route to Fraud/risk review before replacement.

**Targets**

This redesign targets the intake/auto screen, manual eligibility review, fraud/risk review, and fast approval gateway. Its primary segments are high-value devices, claims above EUR 400, reopened claims, and unclear-defect cases.

**Mechanism of improvement**

It reduces R1 and R2 violations by preventing high-value and high-amount claims from reaching Fast approval without manual or risk review. It also reduces the high complaint/reopen rates seen on the fast path: Premium high-value fast path complaint is 22% versus 5% on reviewed/repair; Standard high-value fast path complaint is 18% versus 6% on reviewed/repair. By moving high-risk cases to a reviewed path, it avoids some high-cost replacement errors, especially the EUR 410 wrong-replacement cost for high-value devices.

At the same time, it deliberately preserves the fast path for low-risk accessories, where the fast path is relatively safe and Premium service-credit exposure is high above 24 hours.

**Sacrifices or unresolved issues**

This redesign increases manual review inflow by an estimated 15–35% if auto-screen rules are updated broadly. It may push manual reviewers above the 82% utilization threshold, increasing queue time. It deliberately sacrifices some speed for high-value and above-EUR 400 cases. It does not directly fix the missing-evidence loop, repair-quote delay, or incorrect replacement-part selection. It also risks over-triaging if rules are too broad, causing Premium low-risk cases to miss the 24-hour service threshold.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first digital portal with human-assisted collection

**Description**

Restructure the missing-evidence process around a structured evidence portal rather than the current request-missing-evidence loop. The flow becomes:

- After intake or auto screen, the system determines required evidence by segment, defect type, and risk.
- Customers receive a portal link with a checklist for photos, proof of purchase, serial number, and defect description.
- The portal validates completeness before the case can proceed to Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, or Fast approval.
- Low-digital-literacy customers and Premium customers can use assisted collection by phone or chat.
- Evidence completeness is recorded as a required state before dispatch, except for the R2 Premium low-risk accessory exception.

**Targets**

This redesign targets the Request missing evidence loop, the unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment, claim abandonment, and R2 evidence-completeness violations.

**Mechanism of improvement**

The current evidence loop is expensive: request missing evidence occurs from auto screen 650 times and from manual review 780 times; 890 cases return to manual review, and 320 abandon. Request missing evidence-to-manual review takes median 26 hours and p90 88 hours. Repeated evidence requests occur in 26% of cases entering that loop.

A well-designed portal could reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%. That would reduce the unclear-defect segment’s 140-hour median cycle time, reduce abandonment, and reduce evidence-related R2 violations. Status visibility also helps Standard low-risk customers, who tolerate up to 72 hours if visibility is good.

**Sacrifices or unresolved issues**

The portal requires upfront investment and may increase abandonment among low-digital-literacy customers unless human-assisted collection is available. It does not directly fix wrong replacement selection, high-value fast-approval risk, fraud-bottleneck capacity, or repair-quote delays. If the portal increases the number of complete claims reaching manual review, it may shift pressure to manual reviewers and fraud/risk specialists.

## 4. Redesign C: Right-first-time fulfillment with pre-dispatch quality gate and closed-loop complaint prevention

**Description**

Add a lightweight pre-dispatch quality gate before replacement dispatch, especially for high-value devices, cases with prior reopens, and cases with incomplete-evidence exceptions. The flow becomes:

- Fast approval -> Pre-dispatch quality gate -> Dispatch replacement.
- The gate verifies replacement part/model, serial compatibility, shipping address, service entitlement, and evidence completeness.
- For low-risk accessories, the gate is risk-sampled or skipped to protect the Premium 24-hour service window.
- All Customer complaint/reopen cases must route to Manual eligibility review before final close or denial, as required by R3.
- Escalation board is reserved for high-risk or disputed cases that manual review cannot resolve.
- Data from complaints, rework repair, and gate catches feed a weekly rules update for auto screen and replacement-part selection.

**Targets**

This redesign targets Fast approval-to-Dispatch replacement, Dispatch replacement-to-Customer complaint/reopen, Repair device-to-Quality check-to-Rework repair, and the complaint/reopen-to-Manual review loop.

**Mechanism of improvement**

Dispatch replacement-to-complaint/reopen has mean 96 hours, median 70 hours, and p90 180 hours, linked to incorrect replacement part selection. A pre-dispatch quality gate can catch wrong replacement selection before shipping. That should lower the fast-path complaint/reopen rate from 14.1% toward the repair-path rate of 3.3%.

It also reduces rework repair, especially for high-value devices where rework repair is 12.5% versus 4.0% for low-risk devices. Routing reopened claims through manual review improves R3 compliance. The feedback loop turns complaint and rework data into better auto-screen and dispatch rules.

**Sacrifices or unresolved issues**

The gate adds 6–18 hours depending on resource design. That could push Premium low-risk accessory cases above the 24-hour service-credit threshold if applied too broadly. It increases quality-inspector or gate-resource utilization, turning an underused resource into a potential bottleneck. It does not directly fix the missing-evidence loop or manual-review capacity. If the gate catches many issues, the repair/rework path may also become busier.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

### Redesign A

**Failure modes**

1. **Over-triage**: The updated auto-screen rules may route too many cases to manual or fraud/risk review. Manual reviewer utilization could exceed 82%, causing queue growth. Premium low-risk cases routed to manual could miss the 24-hour service threshold and increase service-credit exposure.
2. **Rule decay**: The new rules may fix current known deviations but become stale as new defect types or fraud patterns emerge. Compliance violations may reappear in new low-frequency paths.

**Bottleneck migration**

The bottleneck migrates from auto/fast-path rework to manual eligibility review and fraud/risk review. Fraud/risk is already constrained with two specialists and Monday/marketing peaks.

### Redesign B

**Failure modes**

1. **Digital abandonment**: If the portal is not supported by human-assisted collection, low-digital-literacy customers may abandon at higher rates. The unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment already has the highest abandonment rate.
2. **Incomplete digitization**: The portal may collect more documents but not the right structured evidence. Cases could still loop through manual review or fraud/risk, and R2 violations may persist if evidence completeness is not enforced before dispatch.

**Bottleneck migration**

The bottleneck migrates from the repeated-evidence loop to manual eligibility review and fraud/risk review, because more complete claims will proceed deeper into the process. Repair quote may also become a new wait point as more cases reach repair decisions.

### Redesign C

**Failure modes**

1. **Gate delay and overrides**: The pre-dispatch gate could add more than expected time, especially p90 cases. Agents may override or skip the gate to meet service-level pressure, reintroducing wrong replacements. Premium low-risk cases may breach the 24-hour threshold.
2. **Shifted defects**: The gate may catch part-number errors but not deeper diagnostic or eligibility errors. Complaints could shift from immediate wrong-part issues to later functional failures or compliance-close exceptions.

**Bottleneck migration**

The bottleneck migrates from downstream complaint/reopen and rework repair to the pre-dispatch quality gate and quality check. Quality inspectors are currently underused, but a full gate rollout could make them a new bottleneck, especially after repair campaigns.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

### First pilot

Start with **Redesign A** on a high-value-device and above-EUR-400 subpopulation in one region or product family. This is the highest compliance and cost-risk segment: Premium high-value fast path has 22% complaint/reopen versus 5% on reviewed/repair; Standard high-value fast path has 18% versus 6%; wrong replacement costs EUR 410.

The pilot should enforce:

- Manual eligibility review or fraud/risk review before Fast approval for high-value or above-EUR 400 claims.
- Evidence-completeness hold before approval.
- Fast path preserved only for low-risk accessory exceptions allowed by R2.

### Metrics to monitor

- R1/R2 violation counts from conformance alignment.
- Complaint/reopen rate for high-value fast path versus reviewed/repair path.
- Median and p90 cycle time by segment, especially Premium high-value and Premium low-risk.
- Manual reviewer utilization and queue wait.
- Fraud/risk queue and wait.
- Service-credit exposure for Premium cases.
- Cost per case including rework, reshipments, and credits.
- Abandonment rate.

### Stop/go thresholds

**Go if:**

- R1/R2 violations for high-value claims fall by at least 70%.
- High-value fast-path complaint/reopen falls from 18–22% toward 10–12% or lower.
- Manual reviewer utilization remains below 82–85%.
- Premium high-value p90 does not worsen beyond roughly 120 hours, or service-credit exposure does not rise materially.

**Stop or redesign if:**

- Manual reviewer utilization exceeds 85% and queue wait grows substantially.
- Premium low-risk or Premium high-value service-credit exposure increases sharply.
- High-value complaints do not fall enough to justify the added manual review cost.
- Fraud/risk becomes a sustained bottleneck with p90 wait above its current 38 hours.

### Sequencing logic

After the first pilot stabilizes high-value routing, add **Redesign B** as a second pilot on the unclear-defect/missing-evidence segment. This targets the 140-hour median cycle time and high abandonment. Monitor repeated evidence requests, abandonment, and R2 evidence completeness.

Then add **Redesign C** as a third pilot on replacement-heavy high-value cases. This tests whether pre-dispatch quality gating catches the residual wrong-part errors that routing and evidence improvements do not address.

### Learning whether improvements are real rather than shifted downstream

Use control groups and track cases for at least 30–60 days after close. Metrics must include:

- Reopen/complaint after close, not only first-pass close.
- Total rework-inclusive cycle time, not only first-pass cycle time.
- Total cost after reshipments, service credits, and rework, not only initial path cost.
- Downstream compliance exceptions at audit, not only immediate conformance.
- Manual queue, fraud/risk queue, quality gate queue, and escalation backlog.

If first-pass cycle time improves but complaints, rework, or compliance exceptions increase later, the change is shifting cost and risk downstream rather than removing it.
