## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 protects more than a pair of different log-in names. It seeks to prevent one human actor from both initiating and releasing a payment, thereby reducing unilateral error, unauthorized initiation, credential misuse, and fraud. AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1 address different portions of that threat.

| Threat or objective | Preventive contribution | Detective contribution | Corrective contribution | Important limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Unintentional error in invoice, amount, currency, or payment details** | AV-2 can prevent errors within its covered checks. Independent MG-4 review before release can prevent errors before authorization. | RA-7 can identify errors after release. | A manager can stop the payment before settlement; timely recall may recover funds after release. | A clean AV-2 pass does not cover every field or commercial issue. Post-release audit alone is generally too late. |
| **2. Unilateral fraudulent initiation or release, including misuse of the preparer’s credentials** | A genuinely independent releaser provides the strongest preventive separation. MG-4 can help if the manager is independent and reviews effectively. | RA-7 can detect the transaction or its consequences. | Pre-settlement intervention or recall can limit loss. | AV-2 does not establish who controlled a compromised account. Different resource IDs alone do not exclude shared credentials or collusion. |
| **3. Deliberate override of duplicate, limit, bank-change, or other warnings** | AV-2 blocks covered hard-stop conditions. ED-1 prohibits an override. MG-4 is valuable only if the override decision is independently authorized and cannot be controlled by the initiator. | Override logs and RA-7 provide evidence for investigation. | An authorized manager can prevent release; otherwise, an immediate hold or recall is needed. | A warning is not equivalent to a clean pass. A supervisor’s override credential does not establish independent review. |
| **4. Wrong or compromised beneficiary bank account, particularly following a recent bank change** | AV-2 covers recent bank changes and, where available, bank-account ownership. MG-4 can independently inspect the destination. ED-1 requires no recent change, a full AV-2 pass, and independent approval. | RA-7 can detect suspicious changes or ownership problems. | Review or recall before the recovery window closes. | Seven-day recency is a limited risk screen, not complete ownership validation. AV-2 cannot help when unavailable, and a post-settlement review does not validate the original destination. |
| **5. Duplicate payment, sanctions exposure, excessive operator limits, or basic purchase-order mismatch** | AV-2 provides the principal preventive coverage. ED-1 requires a full, unoverridden pass. | Audit can identify failures or recurring bypasses. | A block before release prevents loss; otherwise recall is the principal correction. | MG-4 does not replace specialist sanctions or duplicate checks. A payment being legitimate does not retrospectively make an override compliant. |
| **6. Payment without genuine receipt of goods/services or commercial reasonableness** | Effective review of the invoice, purchase order, and supporting receipt by an independent manager can prevent release. | RA-7 can uncover fabricated or missing support. | Pre-settlement review or recall can stop or recover an unjustified payment. | AV-2 explicitly does not establish receipt. Two nominally different users can still collude or share the same evidentiary and workstation environment. |
| **7. Collusion, conflicting relationships, or concentration among preparers, releasers, and approvers** | Genuine reporting-line, device, credential, and conflict separation can resist collusion. MG-4 requires independence and conflict screening. | Pair concentration, shared workstations, and review-quality monitoring can expose elevated exposure. | Escalation, credential revocation, and payment hold can respond to a suspected compromise. | A separate manager label does not resolve an unresolved conflict. A dedicated payment cell may be legitimate, but its common failure modes remain relevant. |
| **8. Irreversible execution before detection, intervention, or recovery** | MG-4 before release—or in time to stop the instruction before bank settlement—provides the critical preventive boundary. | Settlement records and audit identify whether correction remains possible. | Recall within the recovery window, or other evidenced recovery, is corrective. | A manager ratification after settlement is detective. Audit beginning after the recall window provides little assured corrective capacity. |

ED-1 is an exception-governance mechanism, not an independent preventive actor. Its effectiveness depends on all seven conditions being genuine, timely, and evidenced. It neither removes the four-eyes objective nor automatically validates a particular payment.

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

I would assess a package against the following gates.

1. **Applicability and completion**
   - Establish whether a completed `Release_Payment` occurred.
   - Establish whether ED-1 was actually invoked and authorized.
   - Do not assess a blocked attempt as though funds had been released.
   - Distinguish missing settlement evidence from evidence that settlement did not occur.

2. **Timing**
   - Preparation and release must involve different human actors under FEP-1.
   - The strongest compensating review occurs before release, or early enough after release to prevent the irrevocable bank instruction.
   - The emergency token must be active before preparation.
   - A settlement-aware review boundary matters more than the nominal `Manager_Ratify` event name.

3. **Independence and authorization**
   - Establish separate human identities, authority to release, and absence of an unresolved conflict.
   - Supervisor status, performance-reporting authority, delegated credentials, and shared approval channels are relevant.
   - A system account, the initiator’s override credential, or two names at one workstation is not sufficient evidence of independent judgment.

4. **Coverage and overrideability**
   - Map each material threat to a specific control.
   - A clean AV-2 pass covers only the enumerated checks; it does not establish receipt or commercial reasonableness.
   - An overridden warning, unavailable validation, or unsigned manual substitute cannot be treated as equivalent to a full pass.
   - A compensating control must not be routinely bypassable by the person it is intended to constrain.

5. **Evidence quality**
   - Require the actual validation result and policy version, not merely an `Automated_Validate` event.
   - Require evidence of substantive manager review, not just an approval event.
   - Distinguish audit initiation from completed audit, finding, escalation, and remediation.
   - Treat log timestamps and token metadata as evidence requiring authentication and clock-quality validation.

6. **Recoverability and residual exposure**
   - Determine whether the payment could still be held or recalled.
   - Consider severity, beneficiary ownership, evidence of fraud, and the payment’s remaining exposure—not just its amount.
   - A legitimate payment with weak controls is still a control failure, although it lowers evidence that a loss mechanism actually occurred.

7. **Collusion and common-mode failure resistance**
   - Ask whether preparer, releaser, and manager can coordinate, share credentials, or fail for the same reason.
   - Repeated resource pairs, shared workstations, common overrides, and missing organizational design evidence warrant review.
   - They are risk indicators, not proof of misconduct.

8. **Risk-proportional judgment and follow-up**
   - Determine whether residual risk is acceptable for the particular case and whether any exception has a named approver, rationale, and expiry.
   - Small, verified, low-exposure exceptions can be tolerable despite technical nonconformance.
   - Recent bank changes, weak overrides, unavailable controls, or actual false support can make otherwise small payments materially serious.

The resulting categories are:

- **Fully equivalent:** No material relevant threat is left inadequately covered for the defined payment and exception scope. Any compensating arrangement has credible independence, timely intervention, adequate coverage, and an acceptably bounded residual risk.
- **Partially equivalent:** The package materially reduces risk and covers important threats, but some objective coverage, independence, timing, or evidence remains incomplete.
- **Merely mitigating:** The package provides useful evidence or recovery chances but leaves the principal prevention boundary substantially exposed.
- **Ineffective:** A material prevention or authorization gate failed, or the controls are too late, conflicted, unavailable, or outside their coverage to address the relevant loss mechanism.

For a particular bounded emergency, a fully evidenced ED-1 package could potentially support case-specific equivalence. It does not establish universal equivalence with an independent second releaser. The classifications below use `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` where the exception is supported and risk is substantially reduced, but release-stage independence or important evidence remains absent.

**“Same expected loss” is not the sole standard.** A residual comparison should also consider control uncertainty, detectability, recovery time, near misses, unauthorized activity even without financial loss, regulatory or operational consequences, and whether compensating controls fail for correlated reasons. A low estimated loss does not make an unavailable control or conflicted approval equivalent to independent authorization.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

**Semantics used here:** *Preventive* means capable of stopping the payment before the relevant commitment point. *Detective* means identifying a problem after an action or signal. *Corrective* means stopping, reversing, or recovering it in time to limit loss.

A manager action **after `Release_Payment` but before bank settlement is best treated as hybrid**. It is detective when performed because the release event reveals a condition requiring review. It can also be preventive relative to the irreversible bank commitment, and corrective if it halts or changes the instruction. It is not equivalent to an independent authorization before `Release_Payment`, but neither is it merely a late audit if a genuine stop window remains.

| Control | Principal preventive function | Detective function | Corrective function | Sufficiency conditions and principal limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **AV-2** | Strong pre-release prevention for duplicate invoices, covered sanctions status, covered bank changes, operator limits, and basic PO matching. | Validation events, warning reasons, and block events establish which automated checks operated. | A hard block prevents completion. An emitted warning is not corrective by itself. | Requires a full operational pass, no override for ED-1, and correctly authenticated inputs. It does not prove receipt, commercial reasonableness, independence, or control of a compromised account. |
| **MG-4** | Strong prevention when a genuinely independent, qualified manager reviews invoice, bank details, and receipt before release. | Provides detective insight even after release; can expose override misuse, false support, or account changes. | Before settlement, can halt or correct the instruction. After settlement, it is mainly detective unless immediate recall succeeds. | Requires L3-or-equivalent authority, independence, conflict screening, and substantive review. A supervisor relationship, delegated credential, or rubber stamp weakens equivalence. |
| **RA-7** | Normally not preventive: it acts after the initiating or releasing action. | Detects unsupported payments and control deviations, with emergency coverage requiring 100% audit. | Becomes partly corrective if it still leads to a successful hold, recall, or recovery. | Must be timely enough for intervention and sufficiently independent and resourced to investigate. A four-day review of a settled payment is detective, not assured correction. |
| **ED-1** | Does not itself prevent error. It permits a narrow single-operator package only when the full preventive prerequisites are met. | Requires evidence about tokens, validation, and approval; its controls generate detection and investigation opportunities. | Requires timely audit while correction remains possible. | All seven conditions are cumulative. No emergency token, high amount, young vendor, recent bank change, override, validation outage, late approval, or late audit defeats the relevant condition. |

A retrospective audit is therefore not a substitute for the missing authorization boundary merely because it eventually finds an error. Similarly, a pre-settlement manager review can materially compensate for same-user handling, but its independence, coverage, and decision rights must be demonstrated.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Rules

### Evidence to collect

1. **Payment execution**
   - Bank instruction, acceptance, settlement, cancellation, recall request, and recall outcome.
   - Actual beneficiary name and account at release, not merely the current vendor-master value.
   - Distinguish absence of a settlement event from positive evidence that no bank instruction existed.

2. **Automated validation**
   - Validation policy/version, check-by-check result, warning code, block reason, override actor, override reason, and authorizing approver.
   - Service-availability and fail-open/fail-closed state.
   - Signed manual substitutes, their author, evidence reviewed, and approval authority.
   - Do not infer a full pass from the existence of an `Automated_Validate` event.

3. **Emergency authorization**
   - Trusted token issuance, activation, scope, expiry, and revocation records.
   - Clock synchronization and an explanation for discrepancies between outage, preparation, and token logs.
   - Vendor-age source, amount currency, and account-change source.
   - A named exception approver and reason; ED-1 is unavailable when its full-pass AV-2 condition is not met.

4. **Manager review**
   - Manager level, reporting relationships, conflicts, delegated authority, and whether the approver controlled the release.
   - Materials actually viewed: invoice, bank details, purchase order, receipt/service report, and commercial rationale.
   - Decision, conditions, rejection or hold action, and timing relative to release and settlement.
   - Approved overrides should identify the human decision-maker separately from the credential used.

5. **Audit and recovery**
   - Audit open and completion timestamps, reviewer independence, findings, escalation, and remediation.
   - Actual recall or recovery outcomes.
   - Calculate business-day deadlines using the applicable working calendar and timezone; a 24-hour calculation is not automatically a one-business-day calculation.

6. **Independence**
   - Human identity mapping for service accounts.
   - MFA status, shared credentials, shared workstations, browser/device sessions, and release authority.
   - Reporting lines, team design, and segregation-of-duties documentation for dedicated payment cells.

### Monitoring rules

| Metric or pattern | Monitoring rule | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Same-user rate** | Report completed same-user payments as a percentage of completed payments, separately for ordinary and ED-1 cases. Flag every non-ED-1 instance. | A nonzero rate is a process exception, not proof that a particular payment was improper. Segment by workflow and organization without treating amount as the verdict. |
| **Override rate** | Track warning overrides by initiator, credential, approver, reason, warning type, bank proximity, and business unit. Detect repeated self-approval or supervisor-only review. | A legitimate override is not automatically misconduct; a pattern bypassing covered warnings warrants investigation. |
| **Late manager approval** | Classify approval as before release, after release but before settlement, after settlement, missing, or settlement unknown. Separately flag post-release windows too short for meaningful intervention. | After-release/pre-settlement approval is hybrid, not a clean pre-release control. |
| **Audit before recall window** | Compare audit opening and completion with release, settlement, and the applicable recall deadline. Report missing timestamps. | Starting within 24 hours is not proof of completion or successful recall. A review after that window is primarily detective. |
| **Repeated resource pairs** | Count distinct payments per ordered and unordered resource pair over rolling 30/90-day periods; prioritize high recurrence, overrides, shared devices, or findings. | A dedicated legitimate cell can explain repetition. The alert is a concentration and common-mode-risk signal, not proof of collusion. |
| **Shared workstations** | Correlate endpoint, session, badge, and MFA telemetry with preparation and release. Treat absence of data as unknown, not “not shared.” | AP-204 shows why nominally different IDs do not establish practical separation. |
| **Bank-change proximity** | Flag changes within seven days, with stronger investigation for the first 24–72 hours and for ownership mismatches. Include older changes where ownership remains uncertain. | A recent change increases destination risk, but does not establish fraud. |
| **Control outages** | Identify every release during an AV-2 or identity-service outage. Require fail-closed behavior or a separately authorized contingency; ED-1 still requires full AV-2 operation and a pass. | An outage is not a neutral explanation for proceeding without validation. |
| **Emergency timing and recurrence** | Compare token activation with preparation, release, and settlement; monitor repeated emergencies, low-value clustering, and post-hoc justification. | Urgency can explain an exception but does not repair a late token or missing approval. |
| **Evidence quality** | Monitor missing receipt evidence, unsigned checklists, rubber-stamp indicators, unchanged rationale text, and manager reviews completed after settlement. | Detection of poor evidence is distinct from proving the underlying payment was false. |

Alerts should trigger proportionate review, preservation of evidence, and risk-owner escalation. They should not automatically presume guilt, or automatically label a dedicated payment cell improper.

CID = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"

PREPARE = "Prepare_Payment"

ATTEMPT = "Attempt_Release"

raw = pd.read_csv(
    io.StringIO(CSV_TEXT),
    dtype=str,
    keep_default_na=False
)
raw[TS] = pd.to_datetime(raw[TS], utc=True, errors="coerce")
raw[RES] = (
    raw[RES].str.strip().str.upper().replace("", "SYSTEM")
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 2. Parse UTC and convert to a PM4Py-compatible DataFrame
# ------------------------------------------------------------
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    raw,
    case_id=CID,
    activity=ACT,
    timestamp=TS,
    resource=RES
)
if log is None:
    raise RuntimeError(
        "PM4Py did not return a formatted DataFrame; check package version."
    )

log = log.sort_values([CID, TS, ACT]).reset_index(drop=True)
print("Canonical PM4Py columns:", list(log.columns))

#    reported_pair_count_30d is a minimum from the narrative facts,

FACT_COLUMNS = [

facts = pd.DataFrame([

)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# Helper functions for event evidence
# ------------------------------------------------------------
def values_for(case, activity, column=RES):
    return (
        log.loc[
            (log[CID] == case) & (log[ACT] == activity),
            column
        ]
        .dropna()
        .unique()
        .tolist()
    )


def display_values(values):
    return "|".join(str(v) for v in values) if values else "NONE"


def first_timestamp(case, activities):
    mask = log[CID].eq(case) & log[ACT].isin(activities)
    if not mask.any():
        return pd.NaT
    return log.loc[mask, TS].min()


# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 4. Reconstruct one preliminary record per case
# ------------------------------------------------------------
case_ids = raw[CID].drop_duplicates().tolist()
records = []

for case in case_ids:
    case_mask = log[CID].eq(case)
    has_release = bool(case_mask & log[ACT].eq(RELEASE).any())
    has_attempt = bool(case_mask & log[ACT].eq(ATTEMPT).any())
    has_block = bool(
        case_mask & log[ACT].eq("Automated_Validate_Block").any()
    )
    has_cancel = bool(
        case_mask & log[ACT].eq("Cancel_Payment").any()
    )
    has_outage = bool(
        case_mask
        & log[ACT].eq("Validation_Service_Unavailable").any()
    )

    if has_release:
        status = "COMPLETED_RELEASE_EVIDENCED"
    elif has_attempt and (has_block or has_cancel):
        status = "ATTEMPT_STOPPED_BEFORE_RELEASE"
    elif has_attempt:
        status = "ATTEMPT_WITHOUT_COMPLETED_RELEASE"
    else:
        status = "NO_RELEASE_OR_ATTEMPT_EVIDENCED"

    records.append({

        "prepare_resource": display_values(values_for(case, PREPARE)),
        "release_resource": display_values(values_for(case, RELEASE)),

        "release_time": first_timestamp(case, [RELEASE]),
        "attempt_time": first_timestamp(case, [ATTEMPT]),
        "manager_time": first_timestamp(

        "settlement_time": first_timestamp(case, ["Bank_Settlement"]),
        "audit_time": first_timestamp(case, ["Audit_Review"]),
        "has_completed_release": has_release,
        "has_attempt": has_attempt,

        "has_cancel": has_cancel,

    release_resources = set(values_for(row["case"], RELEASE)) - {"SYSTEM"}

    cases.at[i, "nominal_four_eyes_fail"] = (
        bool(row["has_completed_release"])
        and bool(prep_resources.intersection(release_resources))
    )

# Repeated ordered pairs within the embedded extract.

    cases["has_completed_release"],
    ["case", "prepare_resource", "release_resource"]
].drop_duplicates()

    completed_pairs.groupby(
        ["prepare_resource", "release_resource"]
    )["case"]
    .nunique()
    .rename("pair_cases_in_extract")
    .reset_index()
)

cases = cases.merge(
    pair_counts,
    on=["prepare_resource", "release_resource"],
    how="left",
    validate="one_to_one"
)

    cases["pair_cases_in_extract"].fillna(0).astype(int)
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 5. Required PM4Py four-eyes call on the full log
# ------------------------------------------------------------
nominal_filter_result = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
    log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"
)


def case_ids_from_filter_result(result):
    """Support common DataFrame and EventLog return representations."""
    if isinstance(result, pd.DataFrame):
        for column in ("case", CID):
            if column in result.columns:
                return set(result[column].dropna().astype(str))
        raise RuntimeError(
            "Cannot identify case column in PM4Py filter result."
        )

    # PM4Py EventLog: each trace normally has concept:name attributes.
    found = set()
    for trace in result:
        attributes = getattr(trace, "attributes", {})
        value = attributes.get(CID, attributes.get("case"))
        if value is not None:
            found.add(str(value))
    return found


pm4py_full_log_cases = case_ids_from_filter_result(nominal_filter_result)

# Also run the same two-argument check within each completed-payment
# trace. This avoids relying solely on cross-case/global behavior.
pm4py_case_flags = {}
completed_cases = cases.loc[
    cases["has_completed_release"], "case"
].tolist()

for case in completed_cases:
    case_log = log.loc[log[CID].eq(case)].copy()
    filtered_case = pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(
        case_log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment"
    )
    pm4py_case_flags[case] = (
        case in case_ids_from_filter_result(filtered_case)
    )

cases["pm4py_completed_trace_flag"] = cases["case"].map(
    pm4py_case_flags
)
cases["pm4py_full_log_flag"] = cases["case"].isin(
    pm4py_full_log_cases
)

# ------------------------------------------------------------

    facts,

blocked_attempts = joined.loc[

        | joined["has_cancel"]

# Completed nominal violations, reconciled to actual named resources.

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 8. Emit preliminary evidence flags
# ------------------------------------------------------------
av_flags = {
    "FULL_PASS": "COVERED_CHECKS_ONLY; NOT RECEIPT/INDEPENDENCE PROOF",
    "WARNING_OVERRIDE": "WARNING_OVERRIDDEN; NOT A CLEAN PASS",
    "BLOCKED": "BLOCKED_BEFORE_COMPLETED_RELEASE",
    "UNAVAILABLE": "NO_AV2_COVERAGE",
}

joined["flag_emergency_token_before_prepare"] = ""
joined["flag_ed1_amount_below_100k"] = ""
joined["flag_ed1_vendor_at_least_180d"] = ""
joined["flag_bank_change_7d"] = ""
joined["flag_av2_coverage"] = joined["av2_result"].map(av_flags).fillna(
    "UNKNOWN"
)
joined["flag_manager_timing"] = ""
joined["flag_audit_elapsed_hours"] = np.nan
joined["flag_audit_opened_within_24h"] = ""
joined["flag_repeated_pair"] = ""
joined["flag_identity_and_independence"] = (
    "RESOURCE IDENTITIES DO NOT PROVE HUMAN INDEPENDENCE"
)

for i, row in joined.iterrows():
    emergency = bool(row["emergency_claimed"])

    # ED-1 token timing.
    if not emergency:
        token_flag = "N/A_NO_EMERGENCY_CLAIMED"
    elif pd.isna(row["token_created"]):
        token_flag = "UNKNOWN_TOKEN_ACTIVATION_NOT_SUPPLIED"
    elif row["token_created"] < row["prepare_time"]:
        token_flag = "YES_TOKEN_BEFORE_PREPARE"
    else:
        token_flag = (
            "NO_IF_TOKEN_TIMESTAMP_REPRESENTS_ACTIVATION"
        )

    joined.at[i, "flag_emergency_token_before_prepare"] = token_flag

    # Other ED-1 evidence flags are prerequisites, not a final verdict.
    if not emergency:
        amount_flag = "N/A_NO_EMERGENCY_CLAIMED"
        vendor_flag = "N/A_NO_EMERGENCY_CLAIMED"
    else:
        amount_flag = (
            "YES" if row["amount_eur"] < 100000 else "NO"
        )
        vendor_flag = row["vendor_active_180d"]

    joined.at[i, "flag_ed1_amount_below_100k"] = amount_flag
    joined.at[i, "flag_ed1_vendor_at_least_180d"] = vendor_flag

    # Bank-change proximity.
    change_hours = row["bank_change_hours"]
    if pd.isna(change_hours):
        bank_flag = "UNKNOWN"
    elif change_hours == 0:
        bank_flag = "NO_RECENT_CHANGE_REPORTED"
    elif 0 < change_hours < 168:
        bank_flag = "CHANGE_WITHIN_7D"
    else:
        bank_flag = "CHANGE_OLDER_THAN_7D"
    joined.at[i, "flag_bank_change_7d"] = bank_flag

    # Manager timing relative to release and settlement.
    manager_time = row["manager_time"]
    release_time = row["release_time"]
    settlement_time = row["settlement_time"]

    if pd.isna(manager_time):
        manager_flag = "NO_MANAGER_EVENT"
    elif pd.isna(release_time):
        manager_flag = "NO_COMPLETED_RELEASE_FOR_COMPARISON"
    elif manager_time < release_time:
        manager_flag = "BEFORE_RELEASE"
    elif (
        pd.notna(settlement_time)
        and release_time <= manager_time < settlement_time
    ):
        manager_flag = "AFTER_RELEASE_BEFORE_SETTLEMENT_HYBRID"
    elif pd.notna(settlement_time) and manager_time >= settlement_time:
        manager_flag = "AFTER_SETTLEMENT"
    else:
        manager_flag = "AFTER_RELEASE_SETTLEMENT_UNKNOWN"

    joined.at[i, "flag_manager_timing"] = manager_flag

    # Audit timing: 24-hour proxy only; not a business-day adjudication.
    if pd.isna(row["audit_time"]):
        audit_flag = "NO_AUDIT_TIMESTAMP"
    elif pd.isna(release_time):
        joined.at[i, "flag_audit_elapsed_hours"] = np.nan
        audit_flag = "NO_COMPLETED_RELEASE_FOR_ELAPSED_TIME"
    else:
        elapsed_hours = (
            row["audit_time"] - release_time
        ).total_seconds() / 3600.0
        joined.at[i, "flag_audit_elapsed_hours"] = elapsed_hours
        audit_flag = (
            "OPENED_WITHIN_24H"
            if elapsed_hours <= 24
            else "OPENED_AFTER_24H"
        )

    joined.at[i, "flag_audit_opened_within_24h"] = (
        audit_flag + "; COMPLETION_NOT_PROVEN"
    )

    # Repetition evidence combines the extract with reported history.
    if not row["has_completed_release"]:
        repeated_flag = "N/A_NO_COMPLETED_RELEASE"
    elif row["reported_pair_count_30d"] > 1:
        repeated_flag = (
            "REPORTED_HISTORY_REPEATED_PAIR_"
            + str(int(row["reported_pair_count_30d"]))
        )
    elif row["pair_cases_in_extract"] > 1:
        repeated_flag = "REPEATED_WITHIN_EXTRACT"
    else:
        repeated_flag = "NO_REPEAT_EVIDENCE_IN_AVAILABLE_DATA"

    joined.at[i, "flag_repeated_pair"] = repeated_flag

# ------------------------------------------------------------
# 9. Display evidence, explicitly not final compliance verdicts
# ------------------------------------------------------------
print("\nPRELIMINARY EVIDENCE ONLY — NOT FINAL COMPLIANCE VERDICTS")
print("A PM4Py four-eyes flag does not establish independence, control")
print("coverage, successful audit, or satisfaction of ED-1.")

        [

            "prepare_resource",
            "release_resource",
            "pm4py_completed_trace_flag",

)

print("\nSeparate attempted-but-stopped release evidence:")
print(
    blocked_attempts[
        [
            "case",
            "event_status",
            "prepare_resource",
            "has_block",
            "has_cancel",
            "has_validation_outage",
            "av2_result",
        ]
    ].to_string(index=False)
)

print("\nPreliminary case-level evidence flags:")
flag_columns = [
    "case",
    "event_status",
    "nominal_four_eyes_fail",
    "pm4py_completed_trace_flag",
    "flag_emergency_token_before_prepare",
    "flag_ed1_amount_below_100k",
    "flag_ed1_vendor_at_least_180d",

    "flag_identity_and_independence",

print(joined[flag_columns].to_string(index=False))

print(
    "\nReconciled nominal cases:",
    sorted(tool_cases)

The code’s “nominal violation” list is based on the required PM4Py call **and** explicit reconciliation to a completed `Release_Payment`. The package does not promote that list to a final control-objective verdict: that decision still requires the timing, coverage, independence, override, evidence, and recoverability assessment described above.
