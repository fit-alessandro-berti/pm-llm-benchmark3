## 1. Decision rule

### Operational Scope and Stage Eligibility Filter
The Senior Diagnostic Swarm is an intensive, 45-minute multi-specialist intervention designed to accelerate diagnosis, surface missing telemetry, or re-route stalled incident investigations.
1. **Hard Stage Gate:** A case is eligible for a swarm if and only if its current lifecycle stage is prior to diagnosis completion:
   $$\text{Stage} \in \{\text{Triage}, \text{Diagnosis Started}\}$$
   Cases in $\text{Diagnosis Complete}$, $\text{Resolution Work}$, $\text{Customer Validation}$, or $\text{Closed}$ are strictly ineligible ($\Delta p \approx 0$).
2. **SLA Execution Feasibility Window:** 
   $$\text{Time to SLA} \ge t_{\text{min\_exec}} \quad (\text{nominally } 60\text{ minutes})$$
   A 45-minute swarm cannot avert breach if downstream resolution and customer validation require more time than remains. Conversely, cases with $\text{Time to SLA} > 8.0\text{ hours}$ in early triage are deferred to standard queue progression unless an acute escalation cliff is identified.

### Probability Calibration and Robustification Layer
The raw model output $p_{\text{now}}$ represents an uncalibrated, observational association. Before computing decision metrics, probabilities are adjusted for known segment-level biases and data-quality degradation:
1. **SME Segment:** Systematic underprediction by $0.04$–$0.09$.
   $$p_{\text{adj}} = \min(1.0, \, p_{\text{now}} + \delta_{\text{SME}}), \quad \text{where } \delta_{\text{SME}} \sim \text{Uniform}(0.04, 0.09), \; \text{point estimate } +0.065$$
2. **Strategic Enterprise Segment:** Systematic overprediction by $0.05$–$0.12$ following recent workflow changes.
   $$p_{\text{adj}} = \max(0.0, \, p_{\text{now}} - \delta_{\text{ENT}}), \quad \text{where } \delta_{\text{ENT}} \sim \text{Uniform}(0.05, 0.12), \; \text{point estimate } -0.085$$
3. **Public Sector:** Broadly calibrated ($\delta_{\text{PUB}} \approx 0.00$), but confidence bounds are expanded by $\pm 0.05$ to reflect moderate historical sample size.
4. **Non-Native-Language Support:** Sparse validation sample with compounded epistemic uncertainty in both baseline calibration and treatment effect. We adopt a minimax robust formulation: baseline probability is maintained at $p_{\text{adj}} = p_{\text{now}}$, but treatment effect bounds $[\Delta p_{\text{min}}, \Delta p_{\text{max}}]$ are widened by $25\%$ in downstream risk modeling.
5. **Data Quality / Imputation Discount:** If critical dynamic telemetry is missing and imputed (e.g., live queue depth), a reliability penalty is applied to sudden delta spikes:
   $$p_{\text{adj}} = p_{\text{prev}} + \gamma_{\text{DQ}} \cdot (p_{\text{now}} - p_{\text{prev}}), \quad \gamma_{\text{DQ}} = 0.50$$
   This prevents automated dispatch based on imputation artifacts until human or telemetry verification occurs.

### Decision-Theoretic Prioritization Objective
Ranking cases by $p_{\text{now}}$ or $p_{\text{adj}}$ alone is economically invalid. Prioritization must be driven by the **Net Portfolio Expected Value (NPEV)**, which balances causal risk reduction, case value, direct intervention and disruption costs, and negative queue externalities (spillovers on untreated cases):

$$\text{NPEV}_i = \mathbb{E}[\Delta p_i] \cdot V_i - C_{\text{swarm}} - \text{FA}_i - \text{SpilloverHarm}(K)$$

Where:
- $\mathbb{E}[\Delta p_i]$ is the expected absolute risk reduction from a swarm (the causal effect, bounded by $[\Delta p_{i,\text{min}}, \Delta p_{i,\text{max}}]$), **not** the baseline breach risk $p_i$.
- $V_i$ is the contractual and economic value of avoiding breach.
- $C_{\text{swarm}} = €450$ is direct labor and coordination cost.
- $\text{FA}_i$ is the case-specific customer disruption burden incurred if the intervention is perceived as an unwarranted false alarm.
- $\text{SpilloverHarm}(K)$ is the aggregate external cost imposed on non-intervened cases in the diagnosis queue due to diverting two senior specialists:
  $$\text{SpilloverHarm} = \sum_{j \in \text{Untreated Diag}} \Delta p_{\text{spill}, j} \cdot V_j \approx M \cdot \overline{\Delta p}_{\text{spill}} \cdot \overline{V}_{\text{diag}}$$
  With $M = 6$ untreated diagnosis cases, $\overline{\Delta p}_{\text{spill}} \in [0.01, 0.03]$, and average diagnosis queue value $\overline{V}_{\text{diag}} \approx €15,000$, each used slot generates an expected queue spillover penalty of approximately $€900$ to $€2,700$ (midpoint $\approx €1,800$).

### Capacity Rationing and Dynamic Reservation Policy
- **Total Slot Capacity:** 4 slots remaining today.
- **Regulatory Reserve Rule:** Exactly 1 slot is held in reserve until 14:30 for high-consequence unlisted regulatory incidents, unless:
  $$\max_{i} \text{NPEV}_i > \mathbb{E}[V_{\text{reg\_averted}}] \cdot P(\text{Regulatory Arrival}) \approx €35,000 \cdot 0.40 = €14,000$$
  Since no single current case clears this hurdle over its baseline, the 1-slot reserve is strictly enforced. Maximum active dispatches at 12:00 = 3 slots.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

### Operational Alert Lifecycle Architecture
Repeated predictions every 30 minutes in live service operations inevitably cause alert flooding. To prevent specialist desensitization, automated alerting must follow a formal state machine governing cooldown, state-invariance suppression, and evidence-driven re-entry.

```
       +---------------------------------------------------+
       |                 Model Prediction                  |
       +---------------------------------------------------+
                                 |
                     [ p_adj >= Alert Threshold ]
                                 |
                                 v
                     +-----------------------+
                     | Is Case in Cooldown?  |
                     +-----------------------+
                        /                 \
                     [Yes]               [No]
                      /                     \
       +-------------------------+     +--------------------------+
       | Any Material New Event? |     | Evaluate for Swarm Slot  |
       +-------------------------+     +--------------------------+
          /                   \                     |
       [No]                  [Yes]                  v
        /                       \         +--------------------+
+--------------------+   +----------------| Action Decision:   |
| Suppress Alert     |   | Clear Cooldown | Intervene vs. Wait |
| (State Invariant)  |   | & Re-evaluate  | +------------------+
+--------------------+   +----------------+
```

### Protocol Definitions
1. **Post-Evaluation Cooldown:**
   - Any case evaluated as **Wait** enters an automatic **90-minute cooldown** (3 refresh cycles).
   - Any case receiving an **Intervention** enters a **Permanent Swarm Lockout**. Repeated swarms on the same case are strictly forbidden unless a catastrophic technical scope redefinition occurs (e.g., secondary disaster declaration).
2. **State-Invariant Suppression:**
   - If the system feature vector $\mathbf{x}_t$ at cycle $t$ contains no new operational events relative to cycle $t-1$ ($\Delta \mathbf{x} = \mathbf{0}$), any increase in $p_{\text{now}}$ is purely a mathematical artifact of the SLA clock running down ($\frac{\partial p}{\partial t} > 0$).
   - Automated notification is **suppressed**. The case remains under passive queue monitoring without alerting dispatchers.
3. **Evidence-Driven Re-entry and Escalation:**
   Cooldown is immediately bypassed, and the case re-enters the active prioritization pool, if and only if:
   - **Discrete Event Ingestion:** A new diagnostic artifact is attached (e.g., customer diagnostic log, core dump, telemetry trace).
   - **Stage Transition:** The case progresses from `Triage` to `Diagnosis Started`.
   - **Surge Velocity:** Causal risk accelerates faster than clock erosion ($\Delta p_{\text{adj}} / \Delta t > 0.15/\text{hour}$).
   - **Critical Deadline Window:** Time to SLA drops below $2.0\text{ hours}$ while stuck in an uncompleted stage.

### Explicit Comparison: I-05 versus I-07
The divergent handling of I-05 and I-07 illustrates this temporal policy in practice:

- **Case I-05 (Suppress & Defer):**
  - *Context:* Third alert, Triage, 5.0 h to SLA, SME segment, $p_{\text{prev}} = 0.55 \to p_{\text{now}} = 0.57$.
  - *Telemetry Audit:* No new logs, customer messages, or triage actions have occurred. The $+0.02$ risk delta is entirely driven by 30 minutes of elapsed time against the SLA clock.
  - *Policy Action:* **Suppression.** Because $\Delta \text{Evidence} = 0$, I-05 is locked under cooldown. Dispatching a swarm here would reward queue stagnation, squander scarce specialists 5 hours before deadline, and impose uncompensated delays on the diagnosis queue.
- **Case I-07 (Escalate & Intervene):**
  - *Context:* Third alert, Triage, 1.5 h to SLA, SME segment, $p_{\text{prev}} = 0.75 \to p_{\text{now}} = 0.72$.
  - *Telemetry Audit:* Although the nominal risk dipped slightly ($-0.03$), a **material operational event** occurred: the customer uploaded a comprehensive technical diagnostic log.
  - *Policy Action:* **Immediate Re-entry and Escalation.** The log provides the exact technical artifact required for senior specialists to execute an accelerated root-cause identification in 45 minutes. Combined with severe SLA urgency (90 minutes left), SME model underprediction ($p_{\text{adj}} \approx 0.78$), and low customer disruption risk (€50), I-07 qualifies for immediate intervention under Slot 3.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

### 1. Value-Based Starvation of Lower-Tier Clients
- **Risk:** Optimizing purely for direct financial benefit ($\Delta p \cdot V_i$) systematically routes senior specialists to Enterprise accounts (€25k–€100k), starving SME cases (€5k–€11k) of diagnostic support. Lower-value cases sit in congested queues, suffer higher relative breach rates, and experience service degradation.
- **Concrete Safeguards:**
  - *Guaranteed Capacity Floor:* Mandate that at least 1 of the 4 daily swarm slots is ring-fenced for non-enterprise (SME/Public Sector) incidents that cross an acute SLA breach threshold ($p_{\text{adj}} \ge 0.70, \text{Time} \le 3.0\text{ h}$), insulated from monetary competition.
  - *Diminishing Marginal Return Function:* Apply a concave transformation to case value in the priority score: $U(V) = V^\alpha$ (with $\alpha = 0.5$). This preserves sensitivity to financial penalties while preventing a €100k case with 8 hours of runway from dominating an urgent €5k case facing contract default.

### 2. Epistemic Bias in Non-Native-Language Support (I-09)
- **Risk:** Due to small validation sample sizes, language-barrier cases exhibit wide predictive variance. A risk-averse or variance-penalized decision rule would systematically down-rank non-native language tickets as "too uncertain to treat," entrenching structural exclusion.
- **Concrete Safeguards:**
  - *Optimism Under Uncertainty (Upper Confidence Bound Allocation):* For under-represented cohorts, rank cases using the upper bound of treatment efficacy: $\Delta p_{\text{eval}} = \mu_{\Delta p} + 0.5 \cdot \sigma_{\Delta p}$.
  - *Bilingual Technical Pairing:* Supplement I-09 swarms with a dedicated language specialist or validated translation bridge, ensuring that communication friction does not diminish the diagnostic speed of the technical specialists.

### 3. Differential Calibration Across Segments
- **Risk:** Treating raw probabilities uniformly distorts operational priorities: SME cases are neglected because the model underpredicts their risk by $0.04$–$0.09$, whereas Strategic Enterprise cases trigger false alarms because the redesign causes $0.05$–$0.12$ overprediction.
- **Concrete Safeguards:**
  - *Segmented Recalibration Layers:* Deploy independent isotonic regression recalibration maps per customer segment.
  - *Dynamic False-Alarm Budgeting:* Enterprise tickets must cross a higher calibrated probability threshold ($p_{\text{adj}} \ge 0.65$) to trigger an alert, directly offsetting their €2,000–€5,000 false-alarm disruption penalties.

### 4. Selective Intervention Confounding (Performative Drift)
- **Risk:** Successfully swarming high-risk cases converts breaches into non-breaches ($y = 0$). Naive retraining on observed outcomes causes future model iterations to interpret the features of swarmed cases as inherently "benign" or "low risk." Conversely, neglected cases breach and are labeled "toxic," creating an artificial self-fulfilling feedback loop.
- **Concrete Safeguards:**
  - *Counterfactual Logging and Treatment Masking:* Log every ticket with an explicit treatment indicator $W \in \{0, 1\}$ and estimated counterfactual propensity $e(\mathbf{x}) = P(W = 1 \mid \mathbf{x})$. Retraining models must employ Inverse Probability of Treatment Weighting (IPTW) or doubly robust causal estimators.
  - *Randomized Policy Epsilon-Band:* Reserve $5\%$ of borderline eligible cases as an un-swarmed control group (paired with intensified standard queue tracking) to maintain an unconfounded baseline for counterfactual validation.

### 5. Asymmetric Queue Spillover Externalities
- **Risk:** Diverting senior specialists to swarm Strategic Enterprise cases imposes an aggregate delay of 8–20 minutes on the shared diagnosis queue. These negative externalities fall systematically on non-intervened, lower-priority cases currently in diagnosis, effectively transferring SLA risk from large accounts to small accounts.
- **Concrete Safeguards:**
  - *Spillover Ineligibility Gate:* A swarm cannot be dispatched if more than two untreated diagnosis-stage cases are within 45 minutes of their SLA buffer.
  - *Dedicated Swarm Shift Decoupling:* Reorganize specialist staffing so that senior swarmers are staffed on a dedicated rotating "escalation desk" rather than pulled directly off active diagnosis queues, eliminating real-time queue cannibalization.

### Protocol for Detecting Policy Drift and Recalibrating Rules
1. **Continuous Telemetry and Feature Sanity Validation:**
   - Real-time pipeline tests must check for missingness in dynamic features (e.g., live queue depth). If feature imputation occurs on $> 1\%$ of tickets, the model automatically switches to an imputation-robust fallback model, suppressing artificial risk surges like the one observed in **I-06**.
2. **Weekly Segment-Level Calibration Audits:**
   - Every Monday, compute the Expected Calibration Error (ECE) across SME, Strategic Enterprise, Public Sector, and Non-Native-Language Support.
   - If the Enterprise redesign overprediction ($0.05$–$0.12$) narrows or widens by $\pm 0.03$, update the intercept offsets $\delta_{\text{ENT}}$ in the decision rule. If sample size in Non-Native-Language Support reaches $N \ge 100$, fit a dedicated isotonic recalibration model.
3. **Quasi-Experimental Causal Effect Estimation:**
   - Because full randomized controlled trials are constrained by contractual SLAs, intervention efficacy $\Delta p_i$ must be estimated using an **Instrumental Variable (IV)** and **Doubly Robust Learner (X-Learner)** framework.
   - Use specialist shift overlap and historical slot exhaustion as exogenous instruments: cases arriving when 0 slots remained serve as natural controls for cases arriving when slots were open. Retrain the uplift model monthly to update case-specific $[\Delta p_{\text{min}}, \Delta p_{\text{max}}]$ bounds.
4. **Queue Spillover Empirical Re-Estimation:**
   - Continuously measure the empirical breach rate of diagnosis cases that were active in the queue during a 45-minute swarm versus identical cases during non-swarm windows.
   - If empirical spillover exceeds $0.03$ breaches per untreated case (indicating severe queue congestion), the allocation policy automatically:
     a) Increases the minimum direct value hurdle for swarms by $35\%$.
     b) Restricts swarm duration from 45 minutes to a targeted 30-minute timebox.
5. **Formal Policy Governance Triggers:**
   The capacity allocation rule and alert thresholds must be frozen and submitted to a formal governance review if any of the following occur:
   - **Net Portfolio Inversion:** In the adverse regime, if cumulative monthly spillover breach costs exceed avoided breach benefits ($\sum \text{Spillover} > \sum \text{Direct Benefit}$), suspend multi-specialist swarms immediately in favor of single-specialist diagnostic assists.
   - **Regulatory Reserve Misallocation:** If the reserved slot goes unused for 10 consecutive working days while high-value Tier 1 cases breach due to slot exhaustion, adjust the reservation policy from a hard daily hold to an options-pricing release triggered at 13:30.
   - **Demographic SLA Disparity:** If the SME contractual breach rate exceeds Enterprise breach rate by more than $15\%$ over a rolling 30-day window, dynamically increase the SME capacity floor from 1 slot to 2 slots.
