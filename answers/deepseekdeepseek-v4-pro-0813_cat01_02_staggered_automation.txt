### 5.3 Treatment intensity analysis

For Alpha/Beta post-adopter months only, split by actual use:

| intensity group | n | automated_fast_path_pct | median_cycle_h | p90_cycle_h | rework_pct | escalation_pct | csat_1to5 |

Within Alpha/Beta post-adopter months, a raw linear association of `automated_fast_path_pct` with outcomes is approximately:

- median: -1.1 hours per +10 pp automated fast path
- p90: +1.4 hours per +10 pp automated fast path

This is arithmetic association, not causal effect. High use coincided with later months, higher complexity, and different staffing.

### 5.4 Pre-trends and regression to the mean

Alpha’s pre-period median was volatile: 50.4, 46.9, 49.4. Beta’s pre-period was stable: 45.4, 45.9, 46.4. Gamma’s pre-period rose after April as complexity increased. Delta and Epsilon also worsened in Apr-Jun, suggesting a common operational shock, not just automation effects.

Regression to the mean is plausible for Alpha and Beta because they were selected for poor historical cycle times. Some late improvement may be mean reversion rather than automation. The data cannot separate reversion from treatment.

### 5.5 Sensitivity and ranged conclusion

For median cycle time, favorable control-adjusted point estimates imply roughly -3 to -8 hours relative to controls, or about -7% to -17% of adopter pre-period medians. A conservative range including confounding, selection, and regression to the mean is approximately -15% to +5% absolute median-cycle effect.

For p90 cycle time, favorable control-adjusted point estimates imply roughly -3 to -10 percentage points relative to controls, or about -3% to -10%. But absolute p90 rose in Alpha and Beta. A conservative range is -15% to +5% absolute p90 effect. Median and p90 do not support one single cycle-time effect.

## 6. Threats, tests, and decision consequences

| threat | why it matters here | direction of likely bias | test or additional evidence | consequence if threat is severe |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Selection into adoption | Alpha/Beta chosen for poor cycle time, so pre period may be unusually bad | Overestimate improvement | Compare pre-period stability; include historical baseline before Jan | Automation may look effective due to regression to mean |
| Nonparallel trends | Control branches had different complexity and routing trajectories | Ambiguous; may overestimate if controls worsened more | Pre-trend tests; placebo event timing | Relative effects become unreliable |
| Time-varying confounding | Fraud desk, routing, product change coincided with rollout | Ambiguous; likely overestimate if untreated shocks differ | Collect incident-level covariates and queue data | Causal effect on cycle time cannot be isolated |
| Treatment intensity | Availability does not imply use; use grew over time | Attenuates or distorts binary effect | Model `automated_fast_path_pct`; measure case-level adherence | Binary ITT may hide learning or substitution |
| Spillovers through fraud desk | Adopter escalations can congest shared desk serving controls | Overestimate benefit because controls are harmed | Track fraud-desk queue and wait by branch | SUTVA violated; branch-level effects contaminated |
| Staffing changes | Alpha lost FTEs, others gained/lost | Likely underestimate if treated branches lost capacity | Adjust by arrivals per FTE; collect actual productive time | Automation effect may be masked by capacity strain |
| Case mix | High-complexity share rose in multiple branches | Ambiguous; raw outcomes may worsen even if automation helps | Stratify by risk class; use case-level risk adjustment | Median and p90 mix shifts confused with process change |
| Anticipation | Gamma had a readiness pilot before July | Ambiguous; pre-period may already reflect changes | Review pilot activity; exclude contaminated pre months | Pre-period no longer clean counterfactual |
| Survey nonresponse | CSAT response rate varied 20-45% without counts | Ambiguous; selection can bias up or down | Obtain response counts; compare respondent mix | CSAT effect may be nonresponse artifact |
| Measurement aggregation | Branch-month medians and p90 hide case-level variation | Ambiguous; Simpson’s paradox possible | Collect case-level event logs; condition on variant and complexity | Aggregated effects may misstate individual-level effect |
| Small number of branches | Only five clusters, three treated | False precision; high variance | Randomization, stepped wedge, or longer panel | Cluster-robust inference is unreliable |
| Dynamic treatment effects | Learning, configuration, and workarounds change over time | Simple averages misrepresent early vs late effects | Estimate relative-time effects; monitor weekly | Early harm or later benefit may be missed |

## 7. Effect assessment

| outcome | qualitative effect assessment | plausible range or scenario interval | confidence 0-100 | strongest supporting evidence | strongest counterevidence | claim leadership may safely make |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `median_cycle_h` | Suggestive but not causally identified | Point: -3 to -8 h vs controls; conservative: -15% to +5% absolute | 30 | Alpha late and Gamma post improved relative to controls; Beta late near flat | Early Alpha/Beta raw median worsened; only three treated branches; confounding | Automation availability is not associated with consistent median worsening after a learning period, but a causal percentage is not defensible |
| `p90_cycle_h` | No clear absolute reduction; possible avoided deterioration | Point: -3 to -10 pp vs controls; conservative: -15% to +5% absolute | 20 | Adopters deteriorated less than Delta/Epsilon despite complexity increases | Alpha/Beta absolute p90 rose 7-10 h; tail congestion risk remains | The tail has not shown clear automation-driven improvement; claims should be limited to relative avoided deterioration |
| `rework_pct` | Suggestive small improvement or avoided increase | Point: -1.5 to -2.5 pp vs controls; conservative: -3 to +0.5 pp | 25 | Early Alpha/Beta rework lower than controls; Gamma lower post | Alpha rework returned to 14.5% by late post; product mix confounding | Automation may modestly reduce or avoid rework, but the effect is not robustly identified |
| `escalation_pct` | Likely increase; may be appropriate routing | Point: +0.5 to +2.0 pp vs controls; conservative: 0 to +3 pp | 60 | All adopters increased escalation more than controls; automation designed to escalate borderline cases | No specialist/fraud wait data to judge harm | Automation likely increases measured escalation; whether this is harmful requires queue-state data |
| `csat_1to5` | No clear harm; possible small improvement | Point: -0.05 to +0.15 on 1-5 scale | 15 | Gamma post CSAT improved from 4.00 to 4.09; Alpha late improved to 3.92 | Response rates varied 20-45% and response counts are absent | CSAT has not clearly worsened, but no defensible causal improvement claim is possible |

## 8. Rollout recommendation

`expand with an embedded evaluation`

Delta should be the next branch, with Epsilon held as a delayed control for at least one quarter. If branch-level randomization is feasible, randomize Delta versus Epsilon under a stepped-wedge design. If not, use Delta first and Epsilon later, while both collect the same pre-period measurements.

The minimum pre-period should be at least three stable months after the fraud-desk and May product-change effects have settled, with six months strongly preferred. During this period, collect branch-month aggregates plus case-level event logs, risk class, variant, handoffs, rework loops, and queue-state data. Post-period measurement should be at least three to six months because `automated_fast_path_pct` exhibited learning effects.

Process-mining outcomes should include case-level automated-fast-path proportion, manual-fast-path proportion, escalated-path proportion, waiting times at specialist and fraud-desk queues, queue length, rework-loop frequency, and handoff counts. Do not rely only on branch-median summaries.

Spillover monitoring must track the central fraud desk and specialist queue by branch, including wait times for treated and control branches, to detect congestion caused by automation escalations. Monitor Delta’s outcomes against Epsilon’s outcomes and against their own pre-period trend.

Guardrails should include a pause or redesign trigger if CSAT drops by more than 0.2 points, p90 worsens by more than 15% relative to control, rework increases by more than 2 percentage points, or fraud-desk waiting time materially increases.

The scale-up decision rule should be pre-registered: if after three to four post-period months Delta shows non-inferior median cycle time, non-inferior p90, no CSAT harm, and no harmful queue spillover, expand to Epsilon using the same measurement protocol. If the signal is mixed, pause and run diagnostics before scaling.

## 10. Final claim ladder

- `Observed:` Adopter branches increased actual automated-fast-path use from 4% pre-rollout to 25-50% post-rollout, while escalation percentages rose relative to untreated branches.
- `Suggestive:` Relative to Delta and Epsilon, adopter branches showed less deterioration in median cycle time and p90 after the learning period, though raw absolute effects are mixed.
- `Conditionally causal:` If parallel trends, no spillovers, and no anticipation hold, automation availability may be associated with a small median-cycle improvement and avoided p90 deterioration, but these effects are not identified with only five branches.
- `Not supported:` A single statement that “automation reduces cycle time by X%” or that it causally improves p90, rework, or CSAT across branches is not defensible from the supplied data.
