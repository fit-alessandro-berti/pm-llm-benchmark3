## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104
**Factual mechanism:** Compliance Review waited behind an 18-hour queue on Wed morning because it only started after Assessment finished on Tue evening.
**Minimal intervention:** Triage triggers `Compliance Prep` task for C1 immediately. C1 performs preliminary scan during Wed morning queue wait.
**Temporal propagation:** `Compliance Review` start remains Wed 13:10 (due to queue), but duration shrinks from 4.5h to ~2h because evidence was pre-scanned. Alternatively, if slot reserved, start moves to Tue 22:00 (post-Assessment) avoiding Wed morning peak.
**Process-feasibility check:** Valid. Rule 10 allows preliminary work. Rule 5 ensures final review follows Assessment.
**Best/base/worst case:** Best: Prep saves 3h. Base: Prep saves 1h. Worst: Prep incomplete, requires full re-read (+0h net gain).
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If C1 queue exceeds 30h, reserving a slot causes critical displacement of higher-value cases.

### K-219
**Factual mechanism:** D1 held case in queue until Tue 09:30 despite originals arriving Mon 17:30. D1 specialized in complex docs.
**Minimal intervention:** Route to D2 immediately upon Mon 17:30 receipt.
**Temporal propagation:** D2 starts Mon 17:30. If D2 takes 6h (vs D1's 4h effective), completes Tue 00:30. Assessment moves forward by ~19h compared to factual Tue 20:00 start.
**Process-feasibility check:** Valid. D2 pool is interchangeable for standard docs.
**Best/base/worst case:** Best: D2 finishes Tue 01:00. Base: D2 finishes Tue 04:00. Worst: D2 flags illegibility Tue 20:00  Rework loop adds 24h.
**Evidence that would reverse the conclusion:** If handwriting contains legal signatures requiring D1 certification level, routing to D2 violates compliance policy.

### K-337
**Factual mechanism:** Supplier inconsistency triggered two RMI loops over 5 days (Wed-Fri-Mon-Tue).
**Minimal intervention:** At Fri 10:30 (Assessment 2 end), Manager M1 authorizes proceeding without further supplier contact.
**Temporal propagation:** Skips `Request More Info 2`, `Document Check 3`, `Assessment 3`. `Compliance Review` starts Mon 13:00 instead of Tue 15:30.

### K-411

**100-Case Weekly Rollout Analysis**
If applied to 100 similar cases weekly, interference patterns shift significantly. For K-104, reserving C1 slots for 100 high-risk cases would exhaust C1’s capacity (currently ~8h clearance/day). This creates a bottleneck migration where compliant cases wait longer for the single reviewer, potentially violating SLAs for other departments. The "preparation" work might become a de facto queue, reducing the intended speed gain.

For K-219, substituting D1 with D2 across 100 cases assumes D2 can handle the volume. However, the nonlinearity lies in handwriting difficulty. If 20% of these "standard" handwritten documents contain ambiguous figures, D2’s rework rate spikes. This pushes workload back to D1 anyway, negating the D1 queue relief and clogging D2’s queue with rework items.

K-411 presents the highest systemic throughput gain. By parallelizing verification, we reduce WIP inventory. However, if 100 cases arrive simultaneously, the Assessment queue (A1/A2) becomes the new choke point. Earlier readiness simply means more cases pile up before A1/A2, potentially increasing overtime costs for managers approving decisions.

Finally, K-337 removes external dependency variance. While this stabilizes internal scheduling, suppressing RMI loops on high-risk cases increases the probability of regulatory findings. Across 100 cases, even a 5% error rate could trigger audits that halt processing entirely, creating a catastrophic system stoppage versus minor delays.

## 7. Final synthesis

The most robust intervention is K-411 (Parallelize Verification). It addresses a technical constraint rather than a policy one, offering clear cycle-time gains without compromising compliance or quality standards. The risk is manageable congestion in the Assessment queue, which can be monitored via existing metrics. Conversely, K-337 (Suppress Rework) is the most dangerous. Despite offering the largest theoretical time savings, it fundamentally alters the decision policy for high-risk cases. Proceeding on incomplete evidence exposes the organization to financial loss and regulatory penalties that far outweigh operational efficiency gains.

The local case counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy in K-104 (Early Escalation). While beneficial for a single high-value case, scaling this requires reserving scarce Compliance (C1) capacity. In a system-wide rollout, this reservation behavior would starve other urgent cases, creating a new bottleneck that negates individual benefits.

To validate these interventions, the most valuable additional data is real-time queue depth at the moment of task creation (not just snapshot averages) and granular error logs distinguishing between "legibility issues" and "data errors" for handwritten documents. This distinguishes whether D2 failures are due to resource capability or document quality, guiding future training versus routing changes.
