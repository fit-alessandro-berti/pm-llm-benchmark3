# 1. Assumptions, objectives, and decision variables

I define delay as elapsed time from case arrival to completed Validation. A stage-specific queue delay is measured from queue entry to active service start; it is not interchangeable with case-level service or waiting time.

The following assumptions are needed to make the ranges operational:

- Arrivals within a two-hour block are uniform in time, and the expected count is the mean of a Poisson count. Priority and case type are multinomial draws using the displayed percentages. The U1 increase of eight percentage points is taken from P2, leaving P3 unchanged.
- Each displayed service range is a uniform distribution. Thus the ordinary means are 6.5, 23, 28.5, 32.5, and 14 minutes for Intake, Mechanical, Electrical, Software, and Validation, respectively. Tail means are 12, 45, 52.5, 62.5, and 28.5. A tail draw is made separately for each activity. This is conservative about correlated complexity: correlated tails would make the high quantiles worse than the estimates below.
- For U2, I interpret “proportions are renormalized” as holding arrivals in 12:00–16:00 fixed while doubling the E and ES type weights and renormalizing all five type weights. If “doubles” instead means extra arrivals, the Electrical load and all delay estimates below are lower bounds.
- Shifts repeat on subsequent days, but the prompt supplies no overnight staff. Consequently, a case arriving late in the day can wait for the next 08:00 shift. The roster has at most 30 minutes of overtime per resource; no unlisted night worker is assumed. This is material to the P2 target.
- A branch is parallel only when two distinct qualified resources can be allocated without violating a P1 reservation or a 90% planned-utilization guard. Otherwise branches are sequential, in the order shown by the case type. Parallel execution adds 8 minutes before Validation; sequential execution avoids those 8 minutes but adds the other stage and handover delays.
- Handover is charged when the resource changes, and the additional five-minute team handover is charged when the mechanical team and electrical/software team change, except when the same cross-skilled resource retains the case. A diagnostic resource changing skill family between consecutive cases incurs five minutes of setup. A P1 can preempt one lower-priority diagnostic activity only; the restart cost is 0.10 times the remaining active service time, and no second preemption is allowed.

The main decision variables are the eligible-resource set for each stage, queue order, whether a cross-skilled resource is released from its home queue, the ME/ES parallel flag, P1 capacity reservation, whether an allowed preemption is used, overtime activation, and the policy version recorded for the decision.

Two possible objective formulations are:

1. A service-risk objective minimizes
   `E[total elapsed delay] + 0.25*CVaR_95(delay) + 1000*P(P1 misses 120 minutes) + 300*P(P2 misses 480 minutes)`, with hard constraints of at least 90% P1 and P2 attainment whenever the capacity forecast makes those constraints feasible.
2. A robust-fair objective minimizes the worst scenario value of `p95 delay + 500*P1 miss rate + 250*P2 miss rate + 100*max_resource_utilization`, plus a penalty for `max(utilization)-min(utilization)` and for handover/setup minutes. This explicitly rejects a policy that meets a base mean by overloading one person or one skill queue.

The second objective is the one used for the recommendation: protect P1 first, preserve acceptable P2/P3 flow, and minimize the worst degradation over U1–U5 rather than optimize only the base mean.

# 3. Candidate policies

**Policy S — strict specialization with fixed queues.**

Use predetermined activity teams and do not reassign a person between queues during a shift. A concrete base roster is Intake Ada/Ben, Mechanical Ada/Ben/Deepa, Electrical Chen/Deepa/Emil, Software Chen/Emil/Farah, and Validation Ben/Deepa/Emil; a fixed owner handles each queue and idle time is not automatically pooled. Within each queue, order is P1 first, then earliest SLA deadline, then FIFO, but a P1 cannot borrow another queue’s person. ME and ES are sequential unless a previously agreed pair of fixed queues is available, in which case parallelism is allowed only for P1. Handover is low and auditable, but Chen’s absence and any fixed assignment imbalance create long queues. Overflow is a manual escalation when a queue exceeds 60 minutes of expected work; the escalation is slow because it still requires a roster change.

**Policy P — full pooling with risk-adjusted earliest finish.**

Any qualified active resource may take any stage. Dispatch the case with the smallest tuple `(priority rank, SLA slack, queue age)`, where P1 rank is first and slack is deadline minus now minus expected remaining work. For a resource tie, select the earliest estimated finish plus seven minutes for a resource change, five minutes for a team change, and five minutes for a diagnostic skill-family setup. Use aging so P3 gains one priority rank after 60 minutes of waiting and cannot be starved indefinitely. Permit one P1 preemption when its slack is below 30 minutes; charge 10% of remaining active work and record the preemption. Use parallel ME/ES when two distinct eligible resources finish the two branches at least 12 minutes sooner than the best sequential option after the eight-minute coordination cost. This policy responds rapidly to U2 and U3 but can create high handover/setup load and consume all capacity needed by P1.

**Policy H — hybrid pooling with dynamic P1 reservation.**

Maintain a virtual reservation of at least 20% of each currently critical skill family’s next two-hour capacity for P1 work. The reservation is not a permanently idle person: it is released to P2/P3 if no P1 is waiting and the projected P1 slack remains above 30 minutes, then reclaimed at the next dispatch boundary. Dispatch P1 by smallest slack, then P2 by deadline slack, then P3 by age; use expected remaining work rather than raw case age as the tie-breaker.

Controlled cross-skill overflow is enabled when a family’s projected work/capacity exceeds 0.75 for the next two hours, its oldest queue exceeds 30 minutes, or a shock flag is active. The overflow resource must be qualified, have at least 15% uncommitted capacity after the assignment, and incur the explicit handover/setup charge. Prefer retaining a case on the same cross-skilled resource through sequential ME/ES work. Use parallel branches for P1, for predicted SLA risk, or when the critical-path saving after the eight-minute coordination cost is at least 12 minutes and both branch resources remain below the fairness cap. Permit at most one P1 preemption, only when P1 slack is below 30 minutes. Intake and Validation are pooled, but each gets a P1 fast lane and neither may be consumed by diagnostic overflow when its projected queue exceeds 30 minutes.

Policy H has an operationally visible switch: normal, overflow, surge, and emergency. Surge mode activates a pre-authorized relief resource or extra shift; emergency mode protects P1 and stops accepting discretionary parallelism when the lower-bound workload/capacity ratio exceeds 1.0. This makes an infeasible forecast visible rather than hiding it in an overworked pooled roster.

# 5. Robust recommendation

Adopt Policy H as the primary policy, with the four-state normal/overflow/surge/emergency controller. It dominates strict specialization on shock resilience and dominates unreserved pooling on P1 tail risk and fairness. Its extra handover cost is a controlled price for using the skill matrix as capacity when a family queue becomes the bottleneck.

The recommendation is conditional: the system must have a pre-authorized relief mechanism. The minimum regular-capacity gaps, using idealized lower bounds and ignoring penalties, are approximately 231 minutes in base, 952 in U1, 681 after the Chen absence in U3, 564 in U4, and 1,402 in U5. Those correspond roughly to one four-hour equivalent of qualified relief in base, about two full-shift equivalents in U1, one and a half full-shift equivalents in U3, and several equivalents in U5. The relief must be available in the affected skill and time window; a generic late worker cannot repair a morning P1 or Software-specific deficit.

Switch from normal to overflow when any of the following is true: projected two-hour family utilization exceeds 75%; the oldest P1 waits 15 minutes or its projected slack is under 30 minutes; the oldest P2 waits 45 minutes; or the rolling P95 service time exceeds 1.25 times its baseline estimate. Switch to surge when the lower-bound work/remaining-capacity ratio exceeds 0.90, Chen is absent, the U1/U5 flag is raised, or Intake has an outage. Switch to emergency at a ratio above 1.00: reserve all available qualified capacity for P1 and due P2, disable discretionary parallelism, activate relief/on-call coverage, and announce that unprotected P2/P3 targets are at risk.

Use Policy P instead of H when the demand mix is highly unpredictable, there is no meaningful P1 reservation requirement, and cross-skill handover is cheaper than waiting. Use Policy S for a stable low-volume service where audit simplicity and minimal setup dominate; it is not appropriate as the resilience policy for U3 or U5.

# 6. Secondary effects and implementation risks

- Estimate gaming is likely if a dispatcher can improve its score by inflating remaining time, classifying a case as ordinary, or declaring a resource unavailable. Store the original estimate, the override reason, the model version, and the realized active time. Audit estimates by resource, case type, priority, and tail flag.
- P3 starvation is a predictable side effect of P1 reservation and due-date dispatch. Use aging, a small guaranteed P3 slice when P1 slack permits, and a maximum P3 wait alert. Do not silently convert all P3 cases into emergency work.
- Cross-skill pooling can erode skills if people are used only for fast, familiar tasks. Track skill-family minutes, require periodic supervised work in each qualified skill, and avoid using a multiplier as a permanent substitute for competence.
- Preemption can become excessive if every P1 is treated as urgent. Enforce the one-preemption rule, require a slack threshold, charge the restart penalty, and report preemptions per 100 cases and duplicated active minutes.
- Parallel branches can reduce elapsed time while increasing coordination, handovers, and competition for Validation. Apply the 12-minute net-saving threshold and monitor the percentage of parallel cases whose branches finish more than eight minutes apart.
- Validation is a common downstream gate. A diagnostic improvement that creates a Validation queue is not a process improvement. Alert on stage-specific waiting, not only case-level lead time.
- Fairness must be assessed by planned utilization including assigned handover/setup work, overtime, non-discretionary break displacement, and difficult skill mix. A low average utilization can conceal that one person is continuously handling tails or P1 interruptions.
- The dispatch score must be human-readable: priority, slack, expected remaining minutes, eligibility, reservation status, and explicit penalty components should be visible at assignment time. A supervisor needs to be able to reconstruct why a resource was chosen.

# 7. Monitoring and process-mining design

Emit an interval event for every active stage, handover, setup, coordination delay, pause, resume, and restart penalty. Required attributes include:

- `case:concept:name`, case type, priority, arrival block, tail indicators, scenario/shock flag, SLA deadline, and policy version;
- queue-entry timestamp and assignment-decision timestamp;
- the eligible-resource set at decision time, chosen `org:resource`, skill used, home queue, and whether the resource was reserved;
- planned service minutes, actual active service minutes, start/end timestamps, and a reason code for delay or estimate override;
- `pause`, `resume`, `preemption_count`, `handover`, `setup`, `coordination`, `parallel_flag`, branch identifier, and stage name;
- an SLA clock anchored to arrival, plus separate stage clocks anchored to queue entry.

The case-level service/waiting fields from PM4Py must be labeled carefully. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `get_service_time` describe case-level elapsed/service constructs over the event log; they do not by themselves say how long a case waited in Intake, Electrical, Software, or Validation. Calculate queue wait as `assignment_start - queue_entry` and stage active time from interval events, grouped by stage, branch, resource, priority, and scenario.

Useful analyses are a performance DFG or directly-follows graph split by policy and shock, throughput-time distributions by priority, and resource utilization from interval overlap with each person’s shift. `discover_handover_of_work_network` reveals resource-to-resource edges and whether U1/U3/U5 create new handover hubs. Rolling control charts for active service time and queue wait expose drift in speed multipliers; stage-by-scenario heat maps expose bottleneck migration; branch synchronization time exposes whether parallelism is actually helping. Compare process trees or directly-follows frequencies before and after a policy version change, and use conformance checks to detect missing pause/resume or handover events.

# 8. Reproducible Python

The following is a transparent interval-scheduling scaffold, not a validated production simulator. It samples the supplied demand and ranges, applies the three policies, accounts explicitly for calendar availability, handover, setup, coordination, sequential/parallel branches, and the one-preemption restart penalty, and repeats each scenario/policy three times. The dispatch approximation processes released cases in arrival order; a production DES should replace that loop with an event calendar that inserts newly arrived P1 work into an active queue.

```python
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
from collections import defaultdict
from datetime import datetime, timedelta
import pm4py

# Time is minutes after 08:00 on day 0. Shifts repeat every 1,440 minutes.
SERVICE = pd.DataFrame([
    ["Intake", 5, 8, 10, 14, .08],
    ["Mechanical", 18, 28, 35, 55, .15],
    ["Electrical", 22, 35, 40, 65, .18],
    ["Software", 25, 40, 45, 80, .20],
    ["Validation", 10, 18, 22, 35, .12],
], columns=["activity", "ordinary_lo", "ordinary_hi", "tail_lo", "tail_hi", "tail_prob"])

SKILL = pd.DataFrame([
    ["Ada", 1.00, 1.00, 1.35, np.nan, 1.15, 0, 480, 255, 285],
    ["Ben", 1.10, 1.00, np.nan, np.nan, 1.00, 0, 480, 240, 270],
    ["Chen", 1.10, np.nan, 1.00, 1.25, 1.10, 0, 480, 270, 300],
    ["Deepa", 1.00, 1.25, 1.00, np.nan, 1.00, 120, 600, 360, 390],
    ["Emil", 1.10, np.nan, 1.30, 1.00, 1.00, 240, 720, 480, 510],
    ["Farah", 1.00, 1.40, 1.40, 1.40, 1.20, 0, 240, np.nan, np.nan],
], columns=["resource", "Intake", "Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software",
            "Validation", "shift_lo", "shift_hi", "break_lo", "break_hi"])

DEMAND = pd.DataFrame([
    ["08-10", 7, 10, 55, 35, 30, 25, 15, 20, 10],
    ["10-12", 9, 15, 55, 30, 25, 25, 15, 25, 10],
    ["12-14", 7, 12, 58, 30, 22, 28, 18, 20, 12],
    ["14-16", 10, 18, 52, 30, 20, 30, 18, 20, 12],
    ["16-18", 6, 10, 50, 40, 20, 25, 25, 15, 15],
    ["18-20", 3, 5, 45, 50, 25, 20, 30, 10, 15],
], columns=["block", "expected_arrivals", "P1_pct", "P2_pct", "P3_pct",
            "M_pct", "E_pct", "S_pct", "ME_pct", "ES_pct"])

SCENARIOS = ["Base", "U1", "U2", "U3", "U4", "U5"]
POLICIES = ["S", "P", "H"]
TYPE_COLS = ["M_pct", "E_pct", "S_pct", "ME_pct", "ES_pct"]
PRIORITY_COLS = ["P1_pct", "P2_pct", "P3_pct"]
BRANCHES = {"M": ["Mechanical"], "E": ["Electrical"], "S": ["Software"],
            "ME": ["Mechanical", "Electrical"],
            "ES": ["Electrical", "Software"]}
FAMILY = {"Mechanical": "M", "Electrical": "E", "Software": "S"}
RANK = {"P1": 0, "P2": 1, "P3": 2}
ABSENT = {"Base": set(), "U1": set(), "U2": set(), "U3": {"Chen"},
          "U4": set(), "U5": {"Chen"}}

# Fixed home queues for S; P and H use the full qualified set.
HOME = {
    "Intake": ["Ada", "Ben"],
    "Mechanical": ["Ada", "Ben", "Deepa"],
    "Electrical": ["Chen", "Deepa", "Emil"],
    "Software": ["Chen", "Emil", "Farah"],
    "Validation": ["Ben", "Deepa", "Emil"],
}
ANCHOR = {"Intake": "Ada", "Mechanical": "Ben", "Electrical": "Deepa",
          "Software": "Emil", "Validation": "Deepa"}

def scenario_table(name):
    d = DEMAND.copy()
    if name in ("U1", "U5"):
        d.loc[:1, "expected_arrivals"] *= 1.7
        d.loc[:1, "P1_pct"] += 8
        d.loc[:1, "P2_pct"] -= 8
    if name == "U2":
        for i in [2, 3]:
            d.loc[i, "E_pct"] *= 2
            d.loc[i, "ES_pct"] *= 2
            z = d.loc[i, TYPE_COLS].sum()
            d.loc[i, TYPE_COLS] *= 100.0 / z
    return d

def speed(resource, activity):
    x = SKILL.loc[SKILL.resource.eq(resource), activity].iloc[0]
    return float(x) if pd.notna(x) else np.inf

def qualified(activity, active):
    return [r for r in SKILL.resource if r not in active and np.isfinite(speed(r, activity))]

def expected_load(name):
    d = scenario_table(name)
    tail_scale = 2 if name == "U4" else 1
    means = {}
    for _, x in SERVICE.iterrows():
        p = min(1, x.tail_prob * tail_scale)
        means[x.activity] = ((1-p)*(x.ordinary_lo+x.ordinary_hi)/2 +
                             p*(x.tail_lo+x.tail_hi)/2)
    work = defaultdict(float)
    for _, row in d.iterrows():
        n = row.expected_arrivals
        work["Mechanical"] += n*(row.M_pct+row.ME_pct)/100*means["Mechanical"]
        work["Electrical"] += n*(row.E_pct+row.ME_pct+row.ES_pct)/100*means["Electrical"]
        work["Software"] += n*(row.S_pct+row.ES_pct)/100*means["Software"]
        work["Intake"] += n*means["Intake"]
        work["Validation"] += n*means["Validation"]
    return sum(work.values()), dict(work)

def is_available(resource, t, calendar_activity, scenario):
    if resource in ABSENT[scenario]:
        return False
    day, local = int(t // 1440), t % 1440
    row = SKILL.loc[SKILL.resource.eq(resource)].iloc[0]
    if not (row.shift_lo <= local < row.shift_hi):
        return False
    if pd.notna(row.break_lo) and row.break_lo <= local < row.break_hi:
        return False
    # U5 IT outage: no Intake work from 09:30 to 10:00 on day 0.
    if scenario == "U5" and day == 0 and calendar_activity == "Intake" and 90 <= local < 120:
        return False
    return True

def emit(intervals, case_id, concept, resource, start, end, extra=None):
    if end <= start:
        return
    x = {"case:concept:name": case_id, "concept:name": concept,
         "start_timestamp": start, "time:timestamp": end,
         "org:resource": resource}
    if extra:
        x.update(extra)
    intervals.append(x)

def consume(state, resource, ready, duration, calendar_activity, label, scenario,
            intervals, case_id, extra=None):
    """Consume active calendar time, splitting around breaks/shifts/outage."""
    if duration <= 0:
        return ready
    t = max(float(ready), state["free"][resource])
    remaining = float(duration)
    segment_start = None
    last_t = None
    while remaining > 1e-8 and t < 3*1440:
        ok = is_available(resource, t + 1e-6, calendar_activity, scenario)
        if ok:
            if segment_start is None:
                segment_start = t
            step = min(.5, remaining)
            # Use half-minute resolution; split if the next point is unavailable.
            if not is_available(resource, t + step - 1e-6, calendar_activity, scenario):
                step = .1
            remaining -= step
            t += step
            last_t = t
        else:
            if segment_start is not None:
                emit(intervals, case_id, label, resource, segment_start, t, extra)
                segment_start = None
            t += .1
    if segment_start is not None:
        emit(intervals, case_id, label, resource, segment_start, t, extra)
    if remaining > 1e-8:
        raise RuntimeError("calendar horizon too short for this scaffold")
    state["free"][resource] = t
    state["busy"][resource] += duration
    return t

def choose_resource(activity, ready, case, policy, scenario, state, avoid=set()):
    candidates = [r for r in (HOME[activity] if policy == "S" else qualified(activity, ABSENT[scenario]))
                  if r not in avoid and r not in ABSENT[scenario]]
    if not candidates:
        candidates = [r for r in qualified(activity, ABSENT[scenario]) if r not in avoid]
    fam = FAMILY.get(activity)
    scored = []
    for r in candidates:
        setup = 5 if fam and state["last_diag_skill"].get(r) not in (None, fam) else 0
        finish = max(ready, state["free"][r]) + setup + 7 + (5 if fam else 0)
        # H keeps anchor capacity for P1 unless it is the only practical choice.
        reserve_penalty = 0
        if policy == "H" and case["priority"] != "P1" and ANCHOR.get(activity) == r and len(candidates) > 1:
            reserve_penalty = 18
        # For equal finish, retain the case and avoid diagnostic skill switching.
        retain_penalty = 0 if case.get("previous_resource") in (None, r) else 7
        scored.append((RANK[case["priority"]], finish + reserve_penalty + retain_penalty, r))
    scored.sort()
    return scored[0][2]

def sample_service(activity, rng, tail_scale):
    x = SERVICE.loc[SERVICE.activity.eq(activity)].iloc[0]
    tail = rng.random() < min(1, x.tail_prob * tail_scale)
    lo, hi = (x.tail_lo, x.tail_hi) if tail else (x.ordinary_lo, x.ordinary_hi)
    return rng.uniform(lo, hi), tail

def schedule_stage(case, activity, ready, previous_resource, previous_family,
                   policy, scenario, state, intervals, rng, tail_scale,
                   avoid=set(), branch_id=""):
    r = choose_resource(activity, ready, case, policy, scenario, state, avoid)
    fam = FAMILY.get(activity)
    handover = 7 if previous_resource is not None and previous_resource != r else 0
    team_handover = 5 if (handover and previous_family and fam and previous_family != fam) else 0
    setup = 5 if fam and state["last_diag_skill"].get(r) not in (None, fam) else 0
    overhead = handover + team_handover + setup
    if handover:
        ready = consume(state, r, ready, handover, activity, "handover", scenario,
                        intervals, case["id"], {"case:stage": activity})
    if team_handover:
        ready = consume(state, r, ready, team_handover, activity, "team_handover", scenario,
                        intervals, case["id"], {"case:stage": activity})
    if setup:
        ready = consume(state, r, ready, setup, activity, "setup", scenario,
                        intervals, case["id"], {"case:stage": activity})
    active = case["durations"][activity] / speed(r, activity)
    # Approximate one allowed preemption: charge 10% of remaining active work.
    # A full DES would split an existing lower-priority interval at the arrival time.
    if (case["priority"] == "P1" and policy in ("P", "H") and
            activity in FAMILY and case["preemptions"] == 0 and
            ready - case["arrival"] > 20):
        penalty = .10 * active
        ready = consume(state, r, ready, penalty, activity, "restart_penalty", scenario,
                        intervals, case["id"], {"case:stage": activity})
        case["preemptions"] += 1
    start = max(ready, state["free"][r])
    end = consume(state, r, start, active, activity, activity, scenario, intervals,
                  case["id"], {"case:stage": activity, "case:branch_id": branch_id})
    if fam:
        state["last_diag_skill"][r] = fam
    case["previous_resource"], case["previous_family"] = r, fam
    state["handover_minutes"] += overhead
    return end, r, fam

def simulate(policy, scenario, seed):
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    d = scenario_table(scenario)
    tail_scale = 2 if scenario == "U4" else 1
    cases, intervals = [], []
    base_day = datetime(2026, 1, 1, 8, 0, 0)
    cid = 0
    for i, row in d.iterrows():
        n = rng.poisson(row.expected_arrivals)
        for _ in range(n):
            pri = rng.choice(["P1", "P2", "P3"], p=row[PRIORITY_COLS].to_numpy(float)/100)
            typ = rng.choice(["M", "E", "S", "ME", "ES"], p=row[TYPE_COLS].to_numpy(float)/100)
            durations = {}
            for a in ["Intake", "Mechanical", "Electrical", "Software", "Validation"]:
                durations[a], _ = sample_service(a, rng, tail_scale)
            arrival = i*120 + rng.uniform(0, 120)
            cid += 1
            cases.append({"id": f"{scenario}-{seed}-{cid:04d}", "arrival": arrival,
                          "priority": pri, "type": typ, "durations": durations,
                          "preemptions": 0, "previous_resource": None,
                          "previous_family": None})
    # Released cases are processed in arrival order; P1 preemption is represented explicitly.
    cases.sort(key=lambda c: c["arrival"])
    state = {"free": {r: 0.0 for r in SKILL.resource},
             "busy": defaultdict(float), "last_diag_skill": {},
             "handover_minutes": 0.0}
    for c in cases:
        ready, r, fam = schedule_stage(c, "Intake", c["arrival"], None, None,
                                        policy, scenario, state, intervals, rng, tail_scale)
        diag = BRANCHES[c["type"]]
        # Parallelism is a policy decision, not a hidden speed multiplier.
        serial_est = sum(c["durations"][a] / min(speed(x, a) for x in qualified(a, ABSENT[scenario])) for a in diag)
        parallel = (len(diag) == 2 and policy != "S" and
                    (c["priority"] == "P1" or serial_est - max(c["durations"][a] for a in diag) - 8 >= 12))
        if len(diag) == 1:
            ready, r, fam = schedule_stage(c, diag[0], ready, r, fam, policy, scenario,
                                           state, intervals, rng, tail_scale, branch_id="1")
        elif parallel:
            e1, r1, f1 = schedule_stage(c, diag[0], ready, r, fam, policy, scenario,
                                         state, intervals, rng, tail_scale, branch_id="1")
            e2, r2, f2 = schedule_stage(c, diag[1], ready, r, fam, policy, scenario,
                                         state, intervals, rng, tail_scale, avoid={r1}, branch_id="2")
            ready = max(e1, e2)
            # Coordination is a real eight-minute interval before Validation.
            ready = consume(state, r1, ready, 8, "Validation", "coordination", scenario,
                            intervals, c["id"], {"case:stage": "Validation"})
            c["previous_resource"], c["previous_family"] = r1, f1
        else:
            for j, a in enumerate(diag, 1):
                ready, r, fam = schedule_stage(c, a, ready, r, fam, policy, scenario,
                                               state, intervals, rng, tail_scale, branch_id=str(j))
        ready, r, fam = schedule_stage(c, "Validation", ready, c.get("previous_resource"),
                                        c.get("previous_family"), policy, scenario,
                                        state, intervals, rng, tail_scale)
        c["completion"] = ready
        c["lead_time"] = ready - c["arrival"]
    # Max regular-day utilization; cases spilling into the next repeated shift are included by day.
    capacities = {r: float(row.shift_hi-row.shift_lo - (0 if pd.isna(row.break_lo) else row.break_hi-row.break_lo))
                  for r, row in SKILL.set_index("resource").iterrows()}
    util = {r: state["busy"][r] / capacities[r] for r in capacities}
    result = {
        "mean_delay": np.mean([c["lead_time"] for c in cases]) if cases else np.nan,
        "p95_delay": np.percentile([c["lead_time"] for c in cases], 95) if cases else np.nan,
        "P1_SLA": np.mean([c["lead_time"] <= 120 for c in cases if c["priority"] == "P1"] or [np.nan]),
        "P2_SLA": np.mean([c["lead_time"] <= 480 for c in cases if c["priority"] == "P2"] or [np.nan]),
        "P3_SLA": np.mean([c["lead_time"] <= 1440 for c in cases if c["priority"] == "P3"] or [np.nan]),
        "max_resource_utilization": max(util.values()),
        "utilization_fairness": max(util.values()) - min(util.values()),
        "handover_setup_min_per_case": state["handover_minutes"] / max(1, len(cases)),
        "cases": len(cases),
    }
    return result, intervals, cases
```

# 9. Limits and validation plan

Expected arrivals and service ranges do not identify the arrival correlation, tail correlation across activities, queue discipline actually used, resource availability outside the listed shifts, case abandonment, rework, or the exact semantics of preemption. They also do not establish whether handover/setup consumes productive capacity or merely elapsed time. The numerical ranges above therefore distinguish lower bounds and planning estimates from observed performance. In particular, no policy claim can establish 90% P2 attainment without an explicit overnight or next-shift coverage decision.

Before rollout, run a two-week backtest on timestamped cases with resource calendars reconstructed from the event log. Replay the same arrivals and sampled service distributions through S, P, and H, with common random numbers and at least 1,000 seeds per scenario. Calibrate the tail probabilities, resource multipliers, handover/setup durations, and the probability that a second branch can truly run in parallel. Report P1/P2/P3 SLA, mean and P95 end-to-end delay, stage-specific queue delay, resource utilization and its spread, preemptions, and handover/setup minutes with confidence intervals.

Then conduct a guarded pilot: use H for a randomized subset of comparable non-emergency cases while retaining the existing policy for the control subset; P1 cases remain under the reservation rule in both arms. Do not intentionally inject an outage or withhold Chen. Instead, replay U1–U5 as shadow forecasts and activate surge coverage when the trigger thresholds fire. Stop the pilot if any rolling P1 SLA estimate falls below 90%, if a P1 queue exceeds 30 minutes, if any person exceeds 90% planned utilization for two consecutive blocks, or if missing handover/pause events make the audit trail incomplete. Expand only after H improves P95 and worst-scenario P1 performance without materially increasing Validation waiting or P3 starvation.
