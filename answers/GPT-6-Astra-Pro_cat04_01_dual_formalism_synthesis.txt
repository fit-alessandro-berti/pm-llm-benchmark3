# 1. Assumptions and Interpretation

1. **Observation boundary.** I model finite, completed cases as sequences of activity-completion labels. `Register Deviation` occurs once, first; `Archive Case` occurs once, last. Silent routing steps are not recorded. Missing events, duplicate logging, cancellations, and activities outside the supplied narrative are out of scope.

2. **Risk routing.** The risk route is one exclusive choice between `Supervisor Review` and a concurrent submodel containing both `Quality Review` and `Regulatory Review`. The choice becomes available after root-cause analysis and is made once per case, outside the trial loop. Case data should select the appropriate branch externally; neither model can verify that the selected branch agrees with an unrepresented risk attribute.

3. **Optional supplier consultation.** Consultation is represented by an exclusive choice between a silent step, `tau`, and `Consult Supplier`. This choice is outside the trial loop, so consultation occurs zero or one times. The optional submodel must finish before release, including taking the silent branch when consultation is not selected. Without case data, the models cannot identify a case in which consultation was required but incorrectly omitted.

4. **Inspection outcomes.** The trial-related projection of a completed trace must have the form

   $$
   E\,I\,(J\,E\,I)^k,\qquad k\geq 0,
   $$

   where \(E\) is execution, \(I\) inspection, and \(J\) adjustment. Every inspection followed by another iteration is interpreted as failed; the terminal inspection is interpreted as passing. This is an **existential outcome abstraction**, not verification of actual results. A known passing inspection followed by adjustment requires an outcome-aware check to detect.

5. **Meaning of “may overlap.”** I interpret this as model-level concurrency: independently enabled activities may overlap, and neither completion order is imposed. Sequential schedules remain permissible. Completion-only traces cannot establish actual temporal overlap or prove that an activity did not start prematurely. Both models therefore represent an untimed activity abstraction of the finish-to-start requirements.

6. **Duplicate visible labels.** Duplicate labels are permitted in principle, but neither construction uses them: each of the 14 supplied labels has exactly one visible leaf or transition in its model hierarchy. Repeated trial events are repeated executions of loop nodes, not duplicated model nodes.

7. **Process-tree approximation policy.** The process tree is a **conservative, duplicate-free under-approximation**. It introduces a common barrier after containment and root-cause analysis. This sacrifices some early downstream execution while preserving approval–trial overlap and all modeled release prerequisites. I do not claim it is the uniquely best compact under-approximation.

8. **No additional dependencies or urgency.** Reviews do not require corrective-action design to finish, and trials do not require reviews to finish. “May start as soon as” means enabled, not compelled to start immediately. No resource, duration, scheduling, or fairness constraints are added. Completed traces have finitely many iterations; the loop does not guarantee eventual success in every execution.

# 2. Intended Dependency Specification

The following aliases abbreviate the **exact supplied labels** in expressions and witnesses:

```text
R = Register Deviation          C = Contain Material
A = Analyze Root Cause          P = Plan Verification
D = Design Corrective Action    S = Supervisor Review
Q = Quality Review              G = Regulatory Review
U = Consult Supplier            E = Execute Trial
I = Inspect Trial               J = Adjust Parameters
L = Release Material            Z = Archive Case
```

Submodels: **B** is the selected approval route; **O** is the optional supplier obligation; **T** is the complete trial loop. Precedence below means completion of the source before execution of the target, interpreted through the activity abstraction in Section 1.

| ID  | Source activity or submodel | Target activity or submodel               | Relation                               | Rationale                                                                                                 |
| --- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| D01 | R                           | C and A                                   | Precedence / initial split             | Registration precedes both initial activities and, transitively, all subsequent visible work.             |
| D02 | C                           | A                                         | Concurrency; no order                  | Containment and analysis may proceed independently.                                                       |
| D03 | C                           | P                                         | Precedence                             | Verification planning requires completed containment, not completed analysis.                             |
| D04 | A                           | D                                         | Precedence                             | Corrective-action design requires completed analysis, not completed containment.                          |
| D05 | A                           | B                                         | Precedence                             | Risk-route selection and its reviews follow analysis.                                                     |
| D06 | S                           | Concurrent submodel containing Q and G    | Exclusive choice                       | Select the low-risk route or the high-risk route, never both.                                             |
| D07 | Q                           | G                                         | Concurrency within the high-risk route | Both are mandatory when that route is selected; neither precedes the other.                               |
| D08 | A                           | O                                         | Precedence                             | A selected consultation cannot execute before analysis completes.                                         |
| D09 | tau                         | U                                         | Optional exclusive choice              | O performs either no visible consultation or exactly one consultation.                                    |
| D10 | P and D                     | T                                         | Conjunctive synchronization            | The first execution requires both work products. Approvals and consultation are not trial prerequisites.  |
| D11 | B                           | T                                         | Concurrency                            | Reviews may finish before, during, or after the trial loop.                                               |
| D12 | O                           | C, P, D, B, and T                         | No additional ordering                 | After A, consultation may progress independently of these activities, subject to release synchronization. |
| D13 | \(E_i\)                     | \(I_i\)                                   | Within-iteration sequence              | Each trial execution precedes its inspection. Independent activities may interleave between them.         |
| D14 | Failed \(I_i\)              | \(J_i\), then \(E_{i+1}\) and \(I_{i+1}\) | Loop continuation                      | Failure requires adjustment followed by another complete trial iteration.                                 |
| D15 | Passing \(I_i\)             | Exit from T                               | Exclusive loop exit                    | A passing inspection ends the trial loop; adjustment cannot follow that outcome.                          |
| D16 | T, B, and O                 | L                                         | Conjunctive synchronization            | Release requires a completed trial loop, completed selected reviews, and discharged supplier obligation.  |
| D17 | L                           | Z                                         | Precedence / finalization              | Archiving follows release and ends the case.                                                              |

These relations describe the intended behavior independently of how either model is constructed. In particular, a shared predecessor or successor does **not** impose an order between otherwise independent obligations.

# 3. PM4Py Process-Tree Model

**Compact expression.** The aliases above denote visible leaves. `->` is sequence, `+` is concurrency, `X` is exclusive choice, and `*` is a binary do/redo loop. PM4Py’s process-tree definitions give the loop the required behavior: execute the first child, then either exit or execute the second child and repeat the first. ([GitHub][1])

```text
->(
  R,
  +(C, A),
  +(
    ->(
      +(P, D),
      *(->(E, I), J)
    ),
    X(S, +(Q, G)),
    X(tau, U)
  ),
  L,
  Z
)
```

The first parallel block permits containment and analysis to overlap, but joins them before downstream work begins. The second parallel block runs three obligations independently: work-product preparation followed by the trial loop; the selected reviews; and optional consultation. Its join enforces the release prerequisites.

**Self-contained construction.** Every child is explicitly inserted into its parent’s child list and given the matching parent pointer. Every leaf is a fresh object.

```python
from typing import Optional
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator


def pt_leaf(label: Optional[str] = None) -> ProcessTree:
    return ProcessTree(label=label)


def pt_node(operator: Operator, *children: ProcessTree) -> ProcessTree:
    if operator == Operator.LOOP and len(children) != 2:
        raise ValueError("Use a binary do/redo loop.")

    parent = ProcessTree(operator=operator, children=[])
    for child in children:
        if child.parent is not None:
            raise ValueError("A child cannot be reused under another parent.")
        parent.children.append(child)
        child.parent = parent
    return parent


pt_initial = pt_node(
    Operator.PARALLEL,
    pt_leaf("Contain Material"),
    pt_leaf("Analyze Root Cause"),
)

pt_work_products = pt_node(
    Operator.PARALLEL,
    pt_leaf("Plan Verification"),
    pt_leaf("Design Corrective Action"),
)

pt_trial = pt_node(
    Operator.LOOP,
    pt_node(
        Operator.SEQUENCE,
        pt_leaf("Execute Trial"),
        pt_leaf("Inspect Trial"),
    ),
    pt_leaf("Adjust Parameters"),
)

pt_approvals = pt_node(
    Operator.XOR,
    pt_leaf("Supervisor Review"),
    pt_node(
        Operator.PARALLEL,
        pt_leaf("Quality Review"),
        pt_leaf("Regulatory Review"),
    ),
)

pt_supplier = pt_node(
    Operator.XOR,
    pt_leaf(),  # tau: supplier consultation is not selected
    pt_leaf("Consult Supplier"),
)

tree = pt_node(
    Operator.SEQUENCE,
    pt_leaf("Register Deviation"),
    pt_initial,
    pt_node(
        Operator.PARALLEL,
        pt_node(Operator.SEQUENCE, pt_work_products, pt_trial),
        pt_approvals,
        pt_supplier,
    ),
    pt_leaf("Release Material"),
    pt_leaf("Archive Case"),
)
```

**Precisely what is approximated.** The common initial barrier adds four ordering requirements absent from the intended specification: \(A\prec P\), \(C\prec D\), \(C\prec B\), and \(C\prec O\). For consultation, the last relation has a visible effect only when U is selected. Consequently, narrative requirements 3–6 are overconstrained: planning cannot proceed while analysis remains unfinished, and design, reviews, and consultation cannot proceed while containment remains unfinished. The independence of C and A themselves is retained, but the independence of their downstream work streams is not. Trial enabling still depends on both work products rather than approvals; its timing can nevertheless be delayed by the artificial upstream barrier. Approval–trial overlap, consultation–trial overlap, route exclusivity, the trial-loop pattern, and release synchronization are retained. Actual risk, supplier-selection, inspection-result, and start-time correctness remain abstracted in both models.

**Why not claim a duplicate-free exact tree?** Fix the low-risk route, omit consultation, take one trial iteration, and project onto \(\{A,P,E,S\}\). The required order is:

$$
A\prec E,\qquad P\prec E,\qquad A\prec S,
$$

with A and P, P and S, and E and S incomparable. This is an N-shaped order rather than a sequence/parallel decomposition. A duplicate-free block-structured tree cannot retain this dependency pattern exactly merely by regrouping its operators.

That is not a claim that every possible duplicate-label encoding is impossible. Duplicating activities across alternatives can recover more completion orders, but makes the model larger and can replace meaningful concurrency with alternative serialized explanations. Enumerating traces only up to a fixed loop bound would not solve the unbounded problem.

# 4. PM4Py POWL Model

POWL permits a partial order over nested submodels: an edge makes the target wait for completion of the source, while incomparable submodels may execute concurrently. This allows planning and design to remain separate nodes and the trial loop to have two incoming dependencies without also waiting for approvals. ([GitHub][2])

**Compact description.** `TC` denotes transitive closure. The listed root dependencies are generating edges, not an assertion that an unclosed relation is already a strict partial order.

```text
H = PO(nodes={Q, G}, order={})
B = X(S, H)
O = X(tau, U)

V = PO(nodes={E, I}, order={E < I})
T = *(V, J)

powl = PO(
  nodes={R, C, A, P, D, B, O, T, L, Z},
  order=TC({
    R < C, R < A,
    C < P,
    A < D, A < B, A < O,
    P < T, D < T,
    T < L, B < L, O < L,
    L < Z
  })
)
```

**Self-contained construction.** Concurrency is represented by an unordered `StrictPartialOrder`, not by passing `Operator.PARALLEL` to `OperatorPOWL`. Likewise, the trial body’s sequence is a two-node partial order.

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)

r = Transition("Register Deviation")
c = Transition("Contain Material")
a = Transition("Analyze Root Cause")
p = Transition("Plan Verification")
d = Transition("Design Corrective Action")
s = Transition("Supervisor Review")
q = Transition("Quality Review")
g = Transition("Regulatory Review")
u = Transition("Consult Supplier")
e = Transition("Execute Trial")
i = Transition("Inspect Trial")
j = Transition("Adjust Parameters")
l = Transition("Release Material")
z = Transition("Archive Case")

# High-risk route: both reviews, with no order between them.
high = StrictPartialOrder([q, g])
approvals = OperatorPOWL(Operator.XOR, [s, high])

# Optional consultation occurs at most once, outside the trial loop.
supplier = OperatorPOWL(
    Operator.XOR,
    [SilentTransition(), u],
)

# One iteration is Execute Trial followed by Inspect Trial.
trial_body = StrictPartialOrder([e, i])
trial_body.order.add_edge(e, i)

# Execute the body once, then exit or perform adjustment and repeat.
trial = OperatorPOWL(Operator.LOOP, [trial_body, j])

powl = StrictPartialOrder(
    [r, c, a, p, d, approvals, supplier, trial, l, z]
)

# Every essential cross-submodel dependency is explicit.
powl.order.add_edge(r, c)
powl.order.add_edge(r, a)
powl.order.add_edge(c, p)

powl.order.add_edge(a, d)
powl.order.add_edge(a, approvals)
powl.order.add_edge(a, supplier)

powl.order.add_edge(p, trial)
powl.order.add_edge(d, trial)

powl.order.add_edge(trial, l)
powl.order.add_edge(approvals, l)
powl.order.add_edge(supplier, l)
powl.order.add_edge(l, z)

# Warshall closure: add every implied dependency through add_edge.
# The nested orders above are already transitive.
po_nodes = list(powl.children)
for middle in po_nodes:
    for source in po_nodes:
        for target in po_nodes:
            if (
                powl.order.is_edge(source, middle)
                and powl.order.is_edge(middle, target)
            ):
                powl.order.add_edge(source, target)

# Recursively checks the root and nested partial orders.
powl.validate_partial_orders()
```

The closure step is deliberate: the inspected `BinaryRelation.add_edge` implementation stores the specified edge rather than automatically completing transitivity. POWL’s validation checks transitivity and irreflexivity, including in nested partial orders. ([GitHub][3])

**Why the unconnected pairs are intentional.** At the root, the complete set of incomparable pairs can be grouped as follows:

```text
C || {A, D, B, O}
A || P
P || {D, B, O}
D || {B, O}
B || {O, T}
O || T
```

Containment constrains planning, not design, reviews, or consultation; once A finishes, those latter obligations can proceed while C remains unfinished. Conversely, planning may proceed after C while A remains unfinished. Planning, design, reviews, and consultation have no further ordering among them beyond their individual prerequisites. Reviews and consultation can also progress independently of the trial loop because their synchronization point is release, not trial entry.

Inside H, Q and G are incomparable because both reviews may run concurrently. Inside V, E and I are ordered. The children of either XOR are **alternatives, not concurrent obligations**. Finally, “unconnected” here means incomparable after transitive closure: absence of a direct generating edge alone does not establish concurrency.

# 6. Semantic Comparison

“Exact” in this table means exact for the declared, finite, untimed, outcome-abstracted behavior—not for unobserved case attributes or activity start times.

| Requirement                             | Intended semantics                                                                  | Process tree                                               | POWL                                                       | Overconstraint or underconstraint                                                     | Business consequence                                                                                              |
| --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Two initial work streams                | C and A are independent after R                                                     | C and A are concurrent, but joined before downstream work  | C and A are concurrent with separate outgoing dependencies | Tree overconstrains downstream independence, not the order between C and A themselves | A slow initial activity can unnecessarily delay the other stream’s successors.                                    |
| Split planning/design dependencies      | C enables P; A enables D                                                            | Both P and D wait for both C and A                         | Separate C→P and A→D dependencies                          | Tree adds A→P and C→D                                                                 | Artificial waiting and false nonconformance for legitimate early work.                                            |
| Risk route                              | After A, choose S or both Q and G                                                   | Correct route alternatives, but route waits for C as well  | Correct alternatives enabled after A                       | Tree adds C→B; both abstract the risk-data guard                                      | Reviews may be delayed by containment; control-flow acceptance alone cannot verify risk classification.           |
| Approval–trial overlap                  | No ordering between B and T                                                         | Preserved within the second parallel block                 | B and T are incomparable                                   | No additional direct B–T constraint in either model                                   | Reviews need not delay trial work, although they can still delay release.                                         |
| Optional supplier consultation          | Zero or one U, after A and before L; may overlap other work                         | Correct optionality and release join, but also waits for C | Correct optionality and dependencies                       | Tree adds C→U when selected; both abstract selection data                             | Supplier work may be unnecessarily delayed; an omitted externally required consultation needs a data-aware check. |
| Trial entry                             | First E requires P and D only                                                       | Correct join before the trial loop                         | P→T and D→T directly                                       | No extra immediate trial prerequisite; tree has upstream delays                       | Neither model incorrectly makes approvals a trial-entry gate.                                                     |
| Repeat-until behavior                   | Execute–inspect; failure causes adjustment and another iteration; pass exits        | `*(->(E,I),J)`                                             | Loop over ordered E–I body with J as redo                  | Exact projected pattern; both abstract actual failure/pass guards                     | Real outcomes must govern loop continuation in an executable implementation.                                      |
| Release synchronization                 | Wait for terminal passing inspection, selected approvals, and selected consultation | Join of trial/work-products branch, B, and O               | Explicit T→L, B→L, O→L                                     | Structurally exact; “passing” remains abstract                                        | Structural completion is enforced, but actual passing status still needs verification.                            |
| Duplicate-label risk                    | Repeated trial executions are legitimate; other repetition is not introduced        | One visible leaf per supplied label                        | One visible transition per supplied label                  | No duplication-based representational ambiguity introduced                            | Repeated log events should not be confused with duplicated model elements.                                        |
| Temporal overlap and start restrictions | Independent activities may overlap; prerequisites constrain starts                  | Untimed concurrency and sequence                           | Untimed partial-order concurrency and precedence           | Both omit durations and lifecycle starts                                              | Completion-order conformance is not proof of start-time conformance or observed overlap.                          |

Let \(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}\) denote the intended visible language and let \(\mathcal B\) be the early-barrier condition: **both C and A precede every selected activity among P, D, S, Q, G, and U**. Under the stated assumptions,

$$
\boxed{
\mathcal L(\text{tree})
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}\cap\mathcal B
\;\subsetneq\;
\mathcal L(\text{powl})
=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}.
}
$$

The tree imposes the barrier and otherwise implements the same obligations. The POWL model places exactly the intended dependencies around the same route, optionality, and loop submodels. T3 and T5 establish that the tree’s inclusion is strict.

# 7. Preferred Representation and Validation Plan

**Canonical representation: POWL.** Its dependencies expose the intended business logic without introducing a synchronization barrier. The process tree is useful as a compact, explicitly restrictive alternative, but should not be used as the authoritative conformance reference unless management deliberately adopts its additional waiting requirements. Otherwise, valid traces such as T3 and T5 would be misclassified as process violations.

The [combined Python construction and regression runner](sandbox:/mnt/data/material_deviation_models.py) contains both models, hierarchy checks, accepting-net conversion, soundness checks, exact visible-trace membership tests, and sampled playout.

**1. Validate the model hierarchies and dependency relations.**
Check that process-tree parent pointers match child ownership, no node object is reused, and every supplied visible label occurs exactly once in each hierarchy. Recursively check POWL partial orders for transitivity and irreflexivity. Confirm explicitly that no approval→trial edge, trial→approval edge, or consultation→trial edge has appeared during construction or simplification.

**2. Convert both models to accepting Petri nets and check soundness.**
PM4Py’s inspected conversion function supports both ProcessTree and POWL inputs and returns a net with initial and final markings. ([GitHub][4])

```python
import pm4py

nets = {}

for name, model in (("tree", tree), ("powl", powl)):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)

    report = pm4py.check_soundness(
        net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True
    )
    is_sound = report[0] if isinstance(report, tuple) else report

    if not is_sound:
        raise RuntimeError(f"{name} failed soundness checking: {report}")

    nets[name] = (net, im, fm)
```

The inspected `check_soundness` interface returns a Boolean together with diagnostics; testing the truthiness of that tuple would be incorrect. Examine the Boolean and diagnostics, including completion and deadlock problems. A successful soundness check is evidence about the converted net, **not proof of fidelity to the narrative**, and does not mean every possible sequence of failures eventually terminates. ([GitHub][5])

**3. Run exact positive and negative trace tests.**
For each row in Section 5, search reachable states of the form `(marking, trace position)`. Permit silent transitions without consuming a visible event; permit a visible transition only when its label matches the next event. Acceptance requires consuming the entire trace and reaching the **exact final marking**. The supplied runner implements this using enabled-transition execution rather than permissive replay with inserted tokens. PM4Py exposes the necessary enabled-transition and execution operations in its Petri-net semantics module. ([GitHub][6])

Expected acceptance vectors, in table order, are:

```text
Process tree: True, True, False, True, False, False, False, False
POWL:        True, True, True,  True, True,  False, False, False
```

Extend these with targeted mutations: remove one high-risk review, repeat consultation, move consultation before analysis or after release, remove an inspection, or try to release after adjustment. Also move independent reviews and consultation across trial iterations to check that legitimate interleavings remain available. A search-state limit must produce an **undecided** result, not a rejection.

**4. Compare bounded languages and supplement with sampled playout.**
Unfold the loop to one, two, and three iterations, giving each occurrence a distinct internal identity before projecting it back to its visible label. Cover both risk routes and both consultation choices. Compare the converted-net languages against an independently implemented dependency oracle—not merely against one another.

An [independent bounded semantic checker](sandbox:/mnt/data/check_material_semantics.py) was executed for this answer. It enumerated the intended dependency language separately from a sequence/parallel expansion of the proposed tree:

* **20,952** intended visible traces across the stated bound and choices.
* **12,552** process-tree traces; every one was intended.
* The **8,400** excluded traces were exactly those violating the tree’s additional early barrier. All eight witness verdicts matched.

These are unweighted counts of abstract interleavings, not empirical frequencies or estimates of business impact.

For additional sampling, use `pm4py.play_out(net, im, fm, ...)` on each converted net. The runner requests completed traces only, disallows acceptance with leftover tokens beyond the final marking, and sets a visible-length limit. The inspected playout implementation supports these controls. Sampling should supplement, not replace, coverage of every route, optional branch, and loop count. ([GitHub][7])

**5. Audit silent behavior and repeated labels explicitly.**
Verify that the silent supplier branch discharges only O: it must not bypass approvals, trial work, registration, or archiving. Reject the empty trace. Require exact final-marking reachability through any needed trailing silent steps. Inspect reachable silent-only cycles separately from visible rework cycles.

Check label multiplicities before and after conversion, and distinguish multiple firings of a loop transition from multiple distinct transitions sharing its label. For every completed sampled trace, check:

$$
\#U\leq 1,\qquad \#E=\#I=\#J+1.
$$

These count checks supplement, but do not replace, ordering and route tests.

**Validation status and limitations.** The combined Python file was syntax-compiled, and the interfaces were checked against official PM4Py source. **PM4Py was not installed in the execution environment, so its model construction, conversion, soundness, and replay tests were not executed here.** The independent bounded enumeration was executed successfully. Neither bounded enumeration nor sampling proves unbounded language equivalence of a converter, actual inspection outcomes, case-data routing correctness, or finish-to-start compliance in completion-only logs.

# 8. Final Self-Critique

1. **The strongest exactness claim is deliberately limited.** POWL is exact for the declared visible, untimed abstraction, not the full data- and lifecycle-aware process. Both models can accept a trace inconsistent with actual inspection results, risk attributes, consultation requirements, or unobserved activity starts.

2. **The process-tree approximation favors clarity over demonstrated optimality.** The initial barrier has a precisely characterized effect, but I have not proved that this tree maximizes retained behavior among all comparably compact under-approximations. Its excluded interleavings can materially distort conformance and waiting-time analysis.

3. **Library-level verification remains outstanding.** The independent enumeration supports the intended language relation and witnesses, but does not substitute for executing the PM4Py constructions and checking their converted nets. The bounded checks also do not establish every unbounded conversion property or guarantee eventual successful termination.
