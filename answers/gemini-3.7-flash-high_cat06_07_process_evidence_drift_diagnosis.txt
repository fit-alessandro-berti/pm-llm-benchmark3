## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

### January – March 2026: Benchmark Stability
* **Process & Data Reality:** Baseline operations were steady across Variant 1 (Standard visit, ~44–45%), Variant 2 (Remote resolution, ~35–36%), and Variant 3 (Parts-dependent, ~20%). Process execution times were controlled: the critical DFG edge `Order Part -> Part Received` averaged 28–35 h, and diagnosis to `Remote Fix` averaged ~4 h. Data pipelines functioned cleanly (unknown activity share 0.2–0.3%, missing queue features 1%).
* **Predictive Metrics:** Model performance was strong and well-calibrated (AUROC 0.81–0.82, Brier 0.130–0.132, ECE 0.022–0.025). The predicted risk (0.18–0.19) matched the mature observed breach rate (0.18–0.19). Label maturity at cutoff was 100% with a median label delay of 32–34 days.

### April 2026: Schema & Pipeline Incident (Not Concept Drift)
* **Process & Data Reality:** Physical field operations were unchanged: the parts delivery edge took 34 h, and matured variant breach rates remained close to baseline (V1: 0.18, V2: 0.13, V3: 0.34). However, on 1 April, a workflow migration renamed `Initial Diagnosis Completed` to `Diagnostic Assessment Completed`. Because the feature pipeline only recognized the legacy string, predictions were either skipped, triggered on arbitrary events, or generated with corrupted event prefixes. Unknown activities spiked to 17.0%, unmapped variants to 18%, and missing queue features to 14%. Duplicate events were introduced.
* **Predictive Metrics:** The model’s discrimination degraded (AUROC fell to 0.67), calibration collapsed (ECE jumped to 0.110, Brier to 0.184), and mean predicted risk inflated to 0.28 against an actual breach rate of 0.19.
* **Diagnosis:** This was a pure **Data-Pipeline Failure**. Physical process execution and underlying concept relationships $P(Y \mid X_{\text{true}})$ were intact; the model was fed corrupted feature vectors $X_{\text{corrupted}}$.

### May 2026: Remediation & Residual Lineage Debt
* **Process & Data Reality:** On 6 May, a mapping patch restored the activity alias, reducing unknown activities to 2.0% and unmapped variants to 1%. However, derived features (such as cached counts of events before diagnosis) were not recomputed for April records, and deduplication logic was only applied to the new source after 20 April.
* **Predictive Metrics:** Metrics largely rebounded (AUROC 0.78, ECE 0.050, Brier 0.146, observed breach rate 0.18, predicted risk 0.21).
* **Diagnosis:** Pipeline recovery was successful but left minor historical technical debt in April/May logs.

### June 2026: Operational Policy Shift / Covariate Drift (Not Model Failure)
* **Process & Data Reality:** On 1 June, management instituted a new remote-routing policy, triaging more cases into Variant 2 (Remote resolution jumped from 34% to 52%; V1 dropped to 30%, V3 to 18%). Because V2 has a low breach rate (0.09 in June), the aggregate observed breach rate dropped naturally to 0.15.
* **Predictive Metrics:** AUROC remained high at 0.79, and Brier score was strong at 0.139. The model predicted a mean risk of 0.20, slightly overpredicting because the training priors reflected the historical 35% V2 mix. Crucially, within-variant breach rates were stable (V1: 0.16, V2: 0.09, V3: 0.35).
* **Diagnosis:** This was pure **Covariate Drift** driven by an intentional control-flow process change. The conditional relationship $P(Y \mid X)$ was invariant; only the marginal input distribution $P(X)$ shifted toward low-risk remote profiles.

### July 2026: Onset of Supply Chain Bottleneck
* **Process & Data Reality:** On 10 July, a major parts supplier began missing delivery targets. Median time on `Order Part -> Part Received` rose from 38–42 h to 65 h. Consequently, the matured breach rate for V3 cases climbed from 0.33–0.35 to 0.46.
* **Predictive Metrics:** Overall breach rate ticked up to 0.19, while the model predicted 0.18. AUROC dipped slightly to 0.76. Label maturity was 98%.
* **Diagnosis:** Emergence of downstream **Performance Process Drift** and localized **Concept Drift** on parts-dependent cases.

### August 2026: Severe Supplier Failure / Concept Drift Crisis
* **Process & Data Reality:** The parts supplier crisis became severe. Median `Order Part -> Part Received` duration exploded to 130 h (a nearly 4x increase over baseline). The V3 breach rate surged to 0.64 (baseline ~0.31). Furthermore, V3 case share expanded to 35%.
* **Predictive Metrics:** Total observed breach rate jumped to 0.28. Because the model predicts at `Initial Diagnosis Completed` and lacked visibility into future supplier lead-time shocks, it evaluated V3 cases using historical weights, predicting a mean risk of only 0.19. AUROC fell to 0.68, Brier score rose to 0.197, and ECE surged to 0.120.
* **Diagnosis:** Genuine **Concept Drift** combined with severe **Performance Process Drift**. The semantic risk associated with entering a parts-dependent repair at diagnosis had fundamentally altered due to an unmodeled external shock.

### September 2026: Compounded Shocks — Supplier Delay + Population Shift
* **Process & Data Reality:** Two independent disruptions intersected:
  1. *Supplier Disruption:* `Order Part -> Part Received` edge time deteriorated further to 148 h; V3 breach rate remained at 0.61.
  2. *Population Shift:* On 1 September, Partner Z was onboarded (24% of case volume). Partner Z brought a rural customer base with longer technician travel times, older client hardware, and a high reliance on parts (58% V3 routing vs 36% for standard cases).
* **Predictive Metrics:** Case volume expanded to 5,100; V3 share reached 42%. Observed breach rate on mature cases reached 0.31 (Partner Z observed breach rate was 0.45 vs 0.27 for legacy sources). The model severely underpredicted risk (0.21 predicted vs 0.31 observed), AUROC dropped to 0.66, Brier score degraded to 0.214, and ECE rose to 0.140. Label maturity dropped to 82% (median delay 42 d) as rural cases took longer to resolve.
* **Diagnosis:** **Multi-factorial deterioration**: Overlapping **Concept Drift** (supplier breakdown), **Population Shift** (Partner Z onboarding), and emerging **Label Delay**.

### October 2026: The Outcome Backlog & Right-Censoring Illusion
* **Process & Data Reality:** Operational stress continued: `Order Part -> Part Received` remained backlogged at 155 h, and Partner Z comprised 27% of volume. However, the Outcome API experienced a critical technical backlog.
* **Predictive Metrics:** Label maturity at cutoff collapsed to 55%, and median label delay rose to 52 days. The reporting summary showed an apparent observed breach rate of 0.16. This was a **right-censoring artifact**: fast-resolving cases (V2 remote fixes taking ~4 h) closed immediately and were ingested by the API, whereas long-duration cases (V3 parts cases taking 155+ h, rural repairs, and breaches) remained unclosed or queued in the API backlog. The naive AUROC of 0.64 and ECE of 0.130 reflect evaluation on an unrepresentative, heavily biased survivor cohort.
* **Diagnosis:** Critical **Label Delay / Censoring Artifact** masking ongoing operational and population drift.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

### Test 1: Label-Maturity Curves and Fixed-Vintage Backtesting
* **Methodology:** Construct longitudinal cohort maturity curves plotting cumulative case closure and breach recording percentage against days elapsed since diagnosis ($t \in [0, 90]$ days), stratified by monthly cohort, variant, and partner. Perform fixed-vintage evaluation where model metrics are computed only after cohorts achieve $\ge 95\%$ resolution, or evaluate cohorts at identical fixed time horizons (e.g., exactly 30 days post-diagnosis) using Inverse Probability of Censoring Weighting (IPCW).
* **Competing Hypotheses:**
  * *Hypothesis A (October breach drop to 0.16 represents genuine operational recovery):* Fixed-vintage breach rates at day 30 will show a uniform reduction across all variants (V1, V2, V3) and partners; maturity curves for October will match January–March trajectories.
  * *Hypothesis B (October breach drop is a right-censoring artifact from API backlog):* Maturity curves for October will show severe truncation for V3 and Partner Z cases compared to V2; IPCW-adjusted or matured-cohort evaluation will reveal that October’s true underlying breach rate remains $\ge 0.30$.

### Test 2: Feature Recomputation after Activity Normalization and Deduplication
* **Methodology:** Re-extract prediction features on raw April and May event logs after: (1) mapping `Diagnostic Assessment Completed` to `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, (2) applying global event deduplication across all sources, and (3) fully recomputing derived features (e.g., event counts prior to diagnosis, queue wait times). Re-score the frozen baseline model on the repaired feature store.
* **Competing Hypotheses:**
  * *Hypothesis A (April deterioration was pure pipeline failure):* Rescoring on recomputed features restores April AUROC from 0.67 to $\ge 0.79$, reduces ECE from 0.110 to $\le 0.040$, and brings mean predicted risk back into alignment with the 0.19 observed rate.
  * *Hypothesis B (April suffered unobserved operational/concept drift):* Rescoring on clean features fixes missingness but AUROC remains low ($< 0.72$) and ECE remains elevated ($> 0.080$).

### Test 3: Calibration and Discrimination Stratified by Variant, Source, and Month
* **Methodology:** Evaluate AUROC, Brier score, and ECE independently within 6 distinct strata: $\{\text{V1}, \text{V2}, \text{V3}\} \times \{\text{Standard}, \text{Partner Z}\}$ for each monthly cohort from January to September.
* **Competing Hypotheses:**
  * *Hypothesis A (June deterioration was concept drift):* Within-stratum calibration curves in June will show significant degradation and miscalibration across V1, V2, and V3 individually.
  * *Hypothesis B (June was pure covariate shift):* Within-stratum calibration and AUROC in June remain stable and well-calibrated (V1 AUROC $\approx 0.80$, V2 AUROC $\approx 0.78$, V3 AUROC $\approx 0.80$); aggregate breach rate reduction is fully accounted for by the shift in variant weights ($w_{\text{V2}} = 0.52$).
  * *Hypothesis C (August–September failure is isolated to V3 / supplier):* In August, V1 and V2 maintain baseline calibration and AUROC ($\ge 0.78$), whereas V3 exhibits a total calibration collapse (underpredicting breach risk by $> 30\%$).

### Test 4: Comparison of Process Variants and DFG / Performance-DFG Edges
* **Methodology:** Discover the Directly-Follows Graph (DFG) and performance-annotated DFG (median edge transit times) using PM4Py across monthly case cohorts. Track edge durations for: (a) `Order Part -> Part Received`, (b) `Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Remote Fix`, (c) `Initial Diagnosis Completed -> Dispatch Technician`, and (d) `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed`.
* **Competing Hypotheses:**
  * *Hypothesis A (August breach spike was caused by internal field workforce capacity constraints):* DFG analysis will show latency inflation concentrated on `Dispatch Technician -> Repair Completed` or `Diagnosis -> Dispatch Technician`.
  * *Hypothesis B (August breach spike was caused by external supplier failure):* Latency inflation is strictly isolated to the DFG edge `Order Part -> Part Received` (increasing from 35 h to 130–155 h), while internal execution edges remain within historical bounds.

### Test 5: Conditional Outcome Invariance Tests ($P(Y \mid \text{Path})$)
* **Methodology:** Estimate logistic regression / odds-ratio models for breach probability conditional on the realized process path: $\text{logit}(P(\text{Breach} \mid \text{Variant}, X))$. Test for structural breaks in the interaction term $\beta_{\text{V3}} \times \text{Period}_{[\text{Aug-Sep}]}$.
* **Competing Hypotheses:**
  * *Hypothesis A (No concept drift; variant mix explains all variance):* $\beta_{\text{V3} \times \text{Period}}$ is not statistically significant ($p > 0.05$); the conditional breach probability given V3 remains constant at $\sim 0.31–0.33$.
  * *Hypothesis B (True concept drift on parts path):* $\beta_{\text{V3} \times \text{Period}}$ is positive and highly significant ($p < 0.001$), reflecting a doubling of conditional breach probability from 0.32 to $> 0.60$.

### Test 6: Population Transportability and Propensity Reweighting for Partner Z
* **Methodology:** Fit a propensity score model $e(X) = P(\text{Partner Z} \mid X)$ based on available customer/case features (device age, travel distance, initial symptoms, variant routing). Apply Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) to reweight standard cases to match Partner Z's covariate distribution. Compare the reweighted breach rate and model calibration against actual Partner Z performance.
* **Competing Hypotheses:**
  * *Hypothesis A (Partner Z difference is explainable by observable covariate shift):* IPW-reweighted standard cases fully reproduce Partner Z’s 0.45 breach rate and error distribution, proving the gap is driven by known feature shifts (rural distance, device age, V3 routing).
  * *Hypothesis B (Partner Z has unmodeled structural/operational friction):* IPW-reweighted standard cases show a significantly lower breach rate ($\le 0.33$) than Partner Z (0.45), indicating partner-specific execution inefficiencies or unmeasured confounders.

### Test 7: Feature Lineage, Schema Conformance, and Missingness Audits
* **Methodology:** Run automated data contract validations across monthly feature snapshots, auditing: (1) activity vocabulary drift, (2) event timestamp monotonicity, (3) feature null rates, and (4) duplicate transaction IDs.
* **Competing Hypotheses:**
  * *Hypothesis A (August–October degradation is purely operational):* Schema conformance is 100%, missingness is $< 3\%$, and unknown activity rates are $< 1\%$, confirming that Q3 model degradation is operational/concept drift rather than an ETL defect.
  * *Hypothesis B (August–October degradation is contaminated by silent ETL errors):* Audit reveals corrupted timestamps or unmapped activities introduced during Partner Z API integration.

### Architectural Strategy: Segment-Specific vs. Variant-Specific Modeling
A monolithic, single-stage model predicting breach risk at `Initial Diagnosis Completed` is fundamentally vulnerable to downstream routing and supplier shocks. We recommend a **Two-Stage Modular Architecture**:

1. **Stage 1 (Path / Variant Classifier):** At `Initial Diagnosis Completed`, predict the probability distribution over downstream execution paths:
   $$\hat{p}_{\text{path}} = P(\text{Variant} \in \{\text{V1}, \text{V2}, \text{V3}\} \mid X_{\text{diagnosis}}, \text{Partner}, \text{Device})$$
2. **Stage 2 (Modular Conditional Breach Estimators):** Maintain specialized risk models conditional on the anticipated path:
   * **Sub-Model V1 (Standard):** Features focus on technician queue, local travel distance, and historical technician velocity.
   * **Sub-Model V2 (Remote):** Low-complexity baseline model (breach risk is consistently low, $\sim 0.09–0.11$).
   * **Sub-Model V3 (Parts-Dependent):** Explicitly ingests **dynamic external covariates**: rolling 7-day median `Order Part -> Part Received` transit time, supplier backlog depth, and part category availability.
3. **Composite Risk Score:**
   $$\hat{P}(\text{Breach}) = \sum_{k \in \{\text{V1}, \text{V2}, \text{V3}\}} P(\text{Variant} = k \mid X) \cdot P(\text{Breach} \mid \text{Variant} = k, X, Z_{\text{supplier}})$$

*Justification:* This decouples control-flow routing shifts (e.g., June V2 policy) from external supplier shocks (August V3 crisis) and population expansions (September Partner Z rural travel), preventing drift in one domain from destabilizing the entire system.

### Alert Dependency & Suppression DAG
To prevent alert storms and inappropriate automated responses, alerts must follow a strict dependency hierarchy:

```
[Data Pipeline Failure Alert] ----(Suppresses)----> [Model Retraining Trigger]
              |                                              ^
         (Suppresses)                                        |
              v                                              |
[Performance Metric Alert (AUROC/ECE)] <--(Suppresses)-- [Label Maturity Alert (<90%)]
              ^
              |
         (Augments)
              |
[DFG Performance Bottleneck Alert] ---> (Triggers Supplier Risk Override, NOT Model Retrain)
```

* **Rule 1 (Data Quality Precedence):** If `Unknown Activity Share > 1.0%` or `Feature Missingness > 5.0%`, trigger **Data Engineering P1 Alert** and freeze automated recalibration/retraining pipelines.
* **Rule 2 (Label Maturity Gate):** If `Cohort Maturity < 90%` or `Median Label Delay > 40 days`, mark downstream performance metrics (AUROC, Brier, ECE, Observed Breach Rate) as **"PROVISIONAL / CENSORED"**; automatically suppress model retraining triggers.
* **Rule 3 (Process Bottleneck Isolation):** If `Order Part -> Part Received` duration exceeds baseline by $> 50\%$, raise an **Operational Supply-Chain Alert** and activate the V3 dynamic risk heuristic override, rather than diagnosing generic model decay.

### Python Process Evidence Extraction Script

```python
"""
audit_process_evidence.py
Extracts monthly process mining evidence and data-quality metrics from field service event logs.
"""

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np
import pm4py
from datetime import datetime


def extract_monthly_process_evidence(csv_path: str) -> pd.DataFrame:
    # 1. Read raw event log with pandas
    df_raw = pd.read_csv(csv_path)

    # 2. Activity Label Normalization (Handling Schema Migration)
    activity_mapping = {
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    }
    df_raw["activity"] = df_raw["activity"].replace(activity_mapping)

    # 3. Format dataframe for PM4Py compatibility
    df_formatted = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df_raw,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
        timest_format="%Y-%m-%d %H:%M:%S"
    )

    # 4. Event Deduplication Strategy: drop exact duplicate events per case
    df_clean = df_formatted.drop_duplicates(
        subset=["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"]
    ).copy()

    # Calculate Case Start Time for Case-Start Cohorting
    case_starts = df_clean.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min().rename("case_start_time")
    df_clean = df_clean.merge(case_starts, on="case:concept:name", how="left")

    # Generate monthly cohorts:
    # - case_start_cohort: aligns with prediction vintage (used for model evaluation)
    # - event_calendar_cohort: captures calendar-month operational load/bottlenecks
    df_clean["case_start_month"] = df_clean["case_start_time"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)
    df_clean["event_calendar_month"] = df_clean["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period("M").astype(str)

    monthly_summaries = []
    cohort_months = sorted(df_clean["case_start_month"].unique())

    for month in cohort_months:
        # Filter log by Case-Start Cohort
        cohort_df = df_clean[df_clean["case_start_month"] == month].copy()
        total_cases = cohort_df["case:concept:name"].nunique()
        total_events = len(cohort_df)

        if total_cases == 0:
            continue

        # Data Quality: Unknown Activity Share & Missingness
        unknown_acts = (cohort_df["concept:name"] == "UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").sum()
        unknown_share = unknown_acts / total_events if total_events > 0 else 0.0

        # Variant Discovery via PM4Py
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort_df)
        variant_counts = {tuple(k): len(v) for k, v in variants.items()} if isinstance(list(variants.keys())[0], tuple) else {k: len(v) for k, v in variants.items()}

        # Classify baseline variants
        v1_count, v2_count, v3_count, unmapped_count = 0, 0, 0, 0
        for var_trace, count in variant_counts.items():
            trace_str = " -> ".join(var_trace) if isinstance(var_trace, (tuple, list)) else str(var_trace)
            if "Remote Fix" in trace_str:
                v2_count += count
            elif "Order Part" in trace_str and "Part Received" in trace_str:
                v3_count += count
            elif "Dispatch Technician" in trace_str and "Order Part" not in trace_str:
                v1_count += count
            else:
                unmapped_count += count

        # Performance DFG Discovery (Median Edge Durations)
        perf_dfg, start_acts, end_acts = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cohort_df)
        
        # Extract median duration for critical bottleneck edge: ('Order Part', 'Part Received')
        parts_edge = ("Order Part", "Part Received")
        parts_duration_hours = np.nan
        if parts_edge in perf_dfg:
            # PM4Py performance DFG stores duration metrics in seconds
            parts_duration_seconds = perf_dfg[parts_edge].get("median", perf_dfg[parts_edge].get("mean", np.nan))
            parts_duration_hours = parts_duration_seconds / 3600.0

        # Extract median duration for remote fix edge
        remote_edge = ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")
        remote_duration_hours = np.nan
        if remote_edge in perf_dfg:
            remote_duration_seconds = perf_dfg[remote_edge].get("median", perf_dfg[remote_edge].get("mean", np.nan))
            remote_duration_hours = remote_duration_seconds / 3600.0

        monthly_summaries.append({
            "cohort_month": month,
            "cases_started": total_cases,
            "total_events": total_events,
            "unknown_activity_share": round(unknown_share, 4),
            "v1_standard_share": round(v1_count / total_cases, 4),
            "v2_remote_share": round(v2_count / total_cases, 4),
            "v3_parts_share": round(v3_count / total_cases, 4),
            "unmapped_variant_share": round(unmapped_count / total_cases, 4),
            "order_part_to_received_median_hours": round(parts_duration_hours, 2),
            "diagnosis_to_remote_fix_median_hours": round(remote_duration_hours, 2)
        })

    summary_df = pd.DataFrame(monthly_summaries)
    return summary_df
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

### Failure Mode 1: Retraining on Immature / Right-Censored Labels (October Cohort)
* **Mechanism:** Observing an apparent breach rate drop to 0.16 and an apparent AUROC of 0.64 in October, the team treats October as a fully mature cohort and retrains the model.
* **Downstream Failure:** In reality, 45% of October cases remain unclosed or backlogged in the Outcome API. Fast-resolving non-breaches (V2 remote fixes) are massively overrepresented in the training set, while unresolved long-duration parts cases and rural breaches are right-censored. The retrained model learns artificially depressed base rates and underestimates risk across all cohorts, causing widespread missed breach warnings, SLA penalties, and customer churn.
* **Mitigation:** Enforce strict **cohort maturity gates** ($\ge 95\%$ resolution) before any cohort can enter the training dataset. For ongoing evaluation, use right-censoring survival analysis (Kaplan-Meier, Nelson-Aalen, or IPCW) rather than naive binary classification metrics.

### Failure Mode 2: Calibrating Around an Uncorrected Data-Pipeline Bug (April Cohort)
* **Mechanism:** Observing an AUROC drop to 0.67 and ECE spike to 0.110 in April, the team diagnoses "statistical model decay" and applies Platt scaling, temperature scaling, or recalibration weights to the model outputs.
* **Downstream Failure:** The underlying defect was not statistical decay, but a schema break (`Diagnostic Assessment Completed` rename), missing queue features (14%), and 17% unknown activities. Applying recalibration fits mathematical compensations to corrupted feature distributions. When the upstream pipeline bug is subsequently patched, the "recalibrated" model severely under- or over-predicts on clean data, corrupting operational triage.
* **Mitigation:** Establish strict **data-quality assertions and feature lineage gates**. Model recalibration must be blocked whenever schema conformance fails, unknown activity share exceeds 1%, or feature missingness spikes. Fix the data pipeline upstream first; recalibrate only on verified clean features.

### Failure Mode 3: Treating External Supplier Breakdown as Static Model Decay (August Cohort)
* **Mechanism:** Observing a surge in breach rate to 0.28 and model underprediction in August, the team retrains the static feature weights on August data without adding supplier lead-time features.
* **Downstream Failure:** The model permanently bakes in an inflated baseline risk coefficient for all V3 cases. When the supplier eventually resolves its bottleneck and transit times return from 130 h to 35 h, the static retrained model continues to predict high breach probabilities for all parts cases. This triggers unnecessary, costly technician overtime and false managerial escalations.
* **Mitigation:** Distinguish internal process execution from **external supply-chain performance drift**. Introduce dynamic external context features (e.g., rolling median supplier delivery time, backlog count) into the model architecture, or manage transient external bottlenecks via modular operational risk overrides rather than static feature retraining.

### Failure Mode 4: Attributing Partner Z’s Population Shift to Model Algorithmic Bias or Failure
* **Mechanism:** Observing that Partner Z exhibits a 0.45 breach rate compared to 0.27 for legacy sources in September, the team assumes the model has developed algorithmic bias or has failed completely, and attempts a global recalibration.
* **Downstream Failure:** Partner Z’s elevated breach rate is driven by objective physical factors: 58% V3 parts dependency (vs 36%), rural travel distances, and older client equipment. Global recalibration distorts predictions for standard urban customers (overpredicting their breach risk) while failing to capture the specific operational drivers of rural repairs.
* **Mitigation:** Perform **subgroup stratification and transportability analysis (IPW)** before making retraining decisions. Ingest explicit geographic and asset covariates (`travel_distance_km`, `device_age`, `partner_id`) into the model architecture or deploy a dedicated rural/partner sub-model to handle structural population differences cleanly.
