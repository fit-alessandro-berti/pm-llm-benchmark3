# 1. Executive memo

**To:** Executive Leadership Team, Chief Operating Officer  
**From:** Principal Process Mining & Operations Strategist  
**Date:** September 3, 2026  
**Subject:** Executive Decision Memo: Strategic Response to Redesign R Pilot Evidence  

### Context and Operational Dilemma
Management must decide whether to scale, roll back, or adapt Redesign R following a 6-week pilot across 9,200 fiber installations. The process-mining evidence presents a sharp operational contradiction: headline intake metrics improved significantly, yet operational control and back-end execution deteriorated. Median cycle time dropped by 3.4 days (14.2 to 10.8 days), visible unit costs fell by EUR 23 (EUR 184 to EUR 161), and cases with 3+ handovers dropped from 34% to 12%. 

However, tail cycle times exploded (p90 rose from 31.0 to 38.5 days; p95 reached 62.0 days), rework surged (second visits rose from 11% to 18%), and customer complaints increased by 60% (6.1% to 9.8%). Crucially, process-model precision collapsed from 0.79 to 0.51, and alignment audits revealed severe non-compliance: premature billing before technical activation and skipped permit checks in older buildings.

### Diagnostic Synthesis: A Bifurcated Process
Process mining demonstrates that Redesign R did not fail uniformly; rather, it created two divergent operational realities:
1. **The Fast Track (Simple Single-Family Homes, 34% volume):** Merged intake and single-coordinator ownership eliminated administrative dead-time without technical penalty, cutting median duration by 6.0 days, p90 by 8.0 days, raising First-Time-Right (FTR) by 3 percentage points, and reducing complaints.
2. **The Failure Spiral (Apartments and Older Buildings, 47% volume):** In multi-dwelling units (MDUs) and heritage structures, the predictive survey-skip rule failed catastrophically. Skipping physical surveys and permit checks dispatched technicians into unexecutable installations, triggering failed visits (up to 19%), manual activations (up to 19%), and rework loops that overwhelmed second-visit technicians (92% utilization) and saturated coordinators (93% utilization).

### Strategic Recommendation: Segmented Hybrid Scaling (Alternative E)
I advise **against** an unconstrained national rollout (Alternative A)—which would institutionalize regulatory non-compliance, inflame customer churn, and trigger ballooning rework costs—and **against** a blunt total rollback (Alternative B), which would surrender proven commercial gains in the largest customer segment.

Management should adopt **Alternative E (Segmented Hybrid Rollout)**, incorporating operational safeguards from **Alternative C** and high-value telemetry from **Alternative D**:
- **Scale Redesign R for Simple Houses:** Standardize merged intake and coordinator ownership nationally for verified low-risk single-family homes.
- **Halt Survey Skipping & Premature Billing in Complex Segments:** Immediately suspend survey skipping for apartments, older buildings, and business accounts. Reinstate mandatory physical surveys and pre-installation permit checks.
- **Enforce an Automated Compliance Firewall:** Lock billing systems in the OSS/BSS stack so that billing cannot initiate without confirmed, automated two-way optical activation.
- **Rebalance Workforce Capacity:** Relieve coordinator saturation by moving from 1-to-1 ownership to 3-person "coordinator pods" with shared queues, eliminating single-point-of-failure bottlenecks. Redeploy underutilized field surveyors (utilization dropped to 52%) to front-load site surveys for MDUs, directly unloading the saturated second-visit team (92% utilization).

### Strategic Trade-Offs
- **Speed vs. First-Time-Right:** Front-loading surveys in MDUs adds 2–3 days to initial booking but prevents 12–25 days of catastrophic tail delays and customer disputes.
- **Visible Unit Savings vs. Fully Loaded Lifecycle Cost:** Front-end savings of EUR 23 per case are an accounting illusion. Rework, second truck rolls, and dispute handling already push estimated costs to EUR 207—a figure that is understated due to truncated 4-week complaint tracking. Prevention preserves real operating margin.
- **Single-Point Accountability vs. Operational Resilience:** Dedicated coordinators cut handovers but created severe fragility during employee absences. Pod-based ownership preserves high customer accountability while restoring cross-functional buffer capacity.

Executing this hybrid model secures verified velocity gains for over a third of customer orders while immediately neutralizing compliance and margin risks across complex segments.

# 3. Explanation of metric conflicts

The pilot results exhibit five apparent paradoxes. Each is explained below using principles of process mining, queueing theory, and operational systems dynamics.

### 1. High Fitness (0.94) vs. Low Precision (0.51)
- **Mechanics:** Fitness measures the proportion of log traces replayable by the discovered process model. Precision measures whether the model restricts behavior to only valid, observed execution paths. 
- **The Conflict:** Under Baseline, the process followed strict, sequential gates (Survey -> Installation -> Activation -> Billing), yielding balanced fitness (0.87) and precision (0.79). In Redesign R, the process removed procedural constraints: field surveys were conditionally bypassed, manual activations proliferated, failed visits looped directly to second visits without surveys, and billing occurred out-of-order relative to activation.
- **Resolution:** The discovered pilot model accommodates these numerous ad-hoc paths and bypasses, achieving high replay fitness (0.94). However, because the process graph now contains unconstrained parallelisms, arbitrary skips, and loose feedback loops, it allows an immense space of unobserved, non-compliant permutations. The resulting "spaghetti" model exhibits low precision (0.51), indicating that the process has lost operational discipline and structural control.

### 2. Improved Median Cycle Time (14.2 to 10.8 days) vs. Worse Tail Duration (p90: 31.0 to 38.5 days; p95: 45.0 to 62.0 days)
- **Mechanics:** In skewed service distributions, the median reflects the high-volume unimpeded path, whereas p90 and p95 reflect compound failure states governed by queueing dynamics.
- **The Conflict:** Median cycle time improved by 3.4 days overall (and by 6.0 days in simple houses), yet extreme tail delays expanded by up to 17.0 days.
- **Resolution:** Redesign R eliminated upfront intake friction by combining address validation and appointment scheduling (observed in 38% of pilot cases) and skipping surveys for low-to-medium risk profiles. For straightforward installations, cases flowed without interruption. However, when an uninspected apartment or heritage building lacked riser access or municipal permits, the technician failed the installation (failed visits jumped from 13% to 19%). This forced the order into exception handling: coordinator triage, manual activation attempts, permit applications, and second-visit rescheduling. Because coordinator utilization hit 93% and second-visit team utilization reached 92%, Kingman’s queueing approximation (\(W_q \approx \frac{\rho}{1-\rho}\)) applied: as resource utilization \(\rho \to 1.0\), waiting time explodes asymptotically. Unresolved exception cases sat in compounding backlogs, stretching the p95 tail out to 62.0 days.

### 3. Fewer Handovers (34% to 12% with 3+ handovers) vs. Lower Resilience
- **Mechanics:** Handover reduction is a core Lean tenant designed to minimize transfer latency and clarify accountability. Organizational resilience, however, requires distributed knowledge buffers and structural redundancy.
- **The Conflict:** Redesign R concentrated end-to-end case responsibility in a single "installation coordinator," successfully slashing cases with 3+ handovers from 34% to 12%. Yet operational resilience degraded significantly, and delays between failed visits and second visits lengthened.
- **Resolution:** In the baseline model, handovers between specialized intake, surveying, field execution, and activation teams distributed work across functional pools. When an individual was absent, the team queue continued to process cases. Under Redesign R, the working-together network became strictly centralized around individual coordinators. Case context was monopolized by a single person. When a coordinator was absent, ill, or overwhelmed (utilization at 93%), their active cases stalled completely because peers lacked visibility and authority to intervene. Extreme centralization reduced handovers during normal operations but introduced single-point-of-failure fragility that paralyzed exception handling.

### 4. Lower Visible Cost (EUR 184 to EUR 161) vs. Higher Rework / Downstream Cost (EUR 198 to EUR 207, with Incomplete Estimates)
- **Mechanics:** Total operational cost is the sum of prevention costs, upfront execution costs, and failure demand (costs incurred by failing to do something right the first time).
- **The Conflict:** Direct unit costs before complaint handling fell by EUR 23 per order, yet estimated total unit cost rose by EUR 9, with complaints rising from 6.1% to 9.8%.
- **Resolution:** The EUR 23 front-end saving was an accounting illusion achieved by eliminating prevention activities: field surveys were cut from 31% to 18% of cases, and clerical intake steps were compressed. However, removing prevention triggered massive failure demand downstream. The rate of failed visits increased by 46% (13% to 19%), second visits increased by 64% (11% to 18%), and manual activations more than doubled (7% to 15%). A second technician truck roll costs 3 to 4 times more than a field survey. Furthermore, the EUR 207 estimate captured downstream complaint handling only through week 4; when full 60-day billing disputes, truck rolls, and churn costs are accounted for, the net cost increase will be substantially higher, fully negating the upfront savings.

### 5. Improvement for Simple Houses (-6d median, -8d p90, +3pp FTR) vs. Deterioration for Apartments/Old Buildings (Apartments: +12d p90, -8pp FTR; Old Buildings: +25d p90, -11pp FTR)
- **Mechanics:** Process standardization assumes operational homogeneity. In physical telecommunications infrastructure, single-family homes and multi-dwelling units (MDUs) possess entirely distinct physical and regulatory architectures.
- **The Conflict:** The exact same redesign generated stellar performance in simple houses and catastrophic degradation in apartments and older buildings.
- **Resolution:** Single-family homes feature dedicated drop cables, accessible private property boundaries, and standard optical network terminals. In this environment, a predictive algorithm accurately assesses risk, and skipping surveys removes pure administrative waiting time. In contrast, apartments and pre-1980 heritage buildings feature congested risers, locked basements, complex internal conduit routing, landlord access restrictions, and municipal right-of-way permitting. A predictive rule based on basic address data cannot detect a collapsed conduit or a missing municipal permit. Technicians dispatched without physical surveys arrived unable to work, causing an 8pp to 11pp collapse in FTR and driving complaint escalations up by 5pp to 7pp.

# 5. Value-of-information analysis

The eight proposed measurements and experiments (M1–M8) are ranked below in descending order of **Decision Value (Value of Information - VOI)**. Each evaluation details the specific uncertainty resolved, the resulting decision pivot, and why it holds its relative priority.

### Rank 1: M7 — Conformance monitoring for Billing start before Activation test and Permit check absence
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** Resolves the exact frequency, geographic distribution, and operational pathways of illegal/premature billing events and municipal permit bypasses across the network.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** Provides the evidence required to implement an automated hard lock in the BSS/OSS architecture. If premature billing occurs in >0% of cases, software controls must strictly prohibit invoice generation until two-way optical telemetry confirms service activation. If permit omissions occur in >1% of older buildings, automated permit gatekeeping must be enforced prior to technician dispatch.
- **VOI Justification:** **Highest VOI (Critical Regulatory & Commercial Control).** Premature billing represents an existential compliance and reputational hazard (fines, billing disputes, 60% complaint spike). Eliminating this requires zero operational compromise and immediately stems brand damage.

### Rank 2: M4 — Measure full downstream cost for 60 days after Billing start
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** Resolves the true lifecycle cost per order across all segments, closing the current data blindspot where complaint, revisit, and dispute costs were truncated after week 4.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** Directly determines the financial viability of predictive survey skipping. If fully loaded downstream rework and dispute costs in medium-risk apartments exceed EUR 35 per case (pushing total cost > EUR 210 vs. baseline EUR 198), the economic rationale for skipping surveys in MDUs is conclusively disproven, mandating 100% pre-installation physical surveys.
- **VOI Justification:** **Second Highest VOI (Financial Reality Check).** The business case for Redesign R rests on cost reduction. Without 60-day fully loaded costs, executive management is making multi-million-euro capital decisions based on an accounting fiction (the EUR 23 upfront saving).

### Rank 3: M6 — Validate the building-risk predictive rule against field-survey outcomes
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** Quantifies the false-negative rate of the predictive rule (predicting "low/medium risk" when physical, cabling, or riser impediments actually exist) by cross-referencing predictions against ground-truth physical survey findings.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** If the algorithm’s false-negative rate in apartments exceeds 3%, automated survey skipping for MDUs must be permanently decommissioned. If the false-negative rate in suburban single-family houses is <1%, automated skipping is formally approved for national scaling.
- **VOI Justification:** **High VOI (Algorithmic Governance).** Prevents blind technician dispatches by determining whether the predictive rule can be calibrated or must be structurally restricted to single-family dwellings.

### Rank 4: M3 — Add structured reason codes for Failed visit, Manual activation, Complaint escalation, and Second visit
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** Deconstructs the aggregate failure metrics into root causes: distinguishing between customer no-shows, missing permits, physical conduit blockages, optical signal failures, and coordinator scheduling errors.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** If failed visits in MDUs are >70% attributable to physical access and internal conduit blockages, pre-installation physical surveys are mandatory. If manual activations (which doubled to 15%) are driven by provisioning software time-outs rather than field wiring, engineering fixes are assigned to IT rather than field operations.
- **VOI Justification:** **High VOI (Root-Cause Engineering).** Process mining currently identifies *where* deviations occur; reason codes explain *why*, enabling targeted operational remediation rather than blunt guesswork.

### Rank 5: M5 — Compare coordinator ownership with shared-team ownership under coordinator absence
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** Resolves whether the severe tail delays (p95 reaching 62 days) and 93% coordinator saturation are inherent flaws of single-ownership accountability or can be resolved via pooled/pod structures.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** If cases assigned to "coordinator pods" (2–3 cross-covering coordinators) achieve identical handover reductions (12%) but reduce absence-related queue stalls by >50% and lower p90 duration by >5 days, the pod structure replaces individual ownership nationally.
- **VOI Justification:** **Moderate VOI (Organizational Design).** Solves the operational resilience paradox, allowing management to retain low handovers without creating single-point-of-failure bottlenecks.

### Rank 6: M1 — Randomized pilot of survey skipping by building-risk stratum
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** Isolates the true causal treatment effect of skipping surveys within specific building strata (e.g., modern apartments vs. pre-1980 apartments vs. rural homes), eliminating selection bias and geographic confounding.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** Identifies whether a sub-class of modern, standardized apartments can safely skip surveys. If modern MDUs achieve FTR \(\ge 85\%\) without surveys, survey skipping is re-expanded to that specific sub-stratum; otherwise, all MDUs remain subject to mandatory surveys.
- **VOI Justification:** **Moderate VOI (Incremental Optimization).** Highly valuable for long-term policy tuning, but subordinate to immediate regulatory compliance (M7), lifecycle cost validation (M4), and algorithmic auditing (M6).

### Rank 7: M8 — Collect customer experience separately for "speed to appointment" and "working service after installation"
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** De-couples customer sentiment drivers, establishing the relative NPS weight of fast scheduling versus first-time-working service.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** Confirms whether customer satisfaction is elastic to initial scheduling delays. If customer sentiment drops by 15 points due to a non-working installation but drops by only 2 points for a 3-day survey scheduling delay, management formally codifies a policy of "reliability over raw speed."
- **VOI Justification:** **Lower-Moderate VOI (Commercial Strategy Alignment).** Validates qualitative customer perceptions, reinforcing the operational decision to prioritize FTR over rapid, unverified installation.

### Rank 8: M2 — Instrument start timestamps for Installation visit, Activation test, and Manual activation
- **Target Uncertainty Resolved:** Separates pure queue waiting time (latency in backlog) from active execution service time, which are currently confounded because the log records only completion timestamps.
- **Decision Pivot & Threshold:** Identifies exact technician hands-on time to refine field labor standards and dispatch schedules.
- **VOI Justification:** **Lowest VOI (Operational Refinement).** While academically desirable for advanced process mining, coarse completion timestamps and external duration metrics already provide sufficient statistical evidence to make immediate strategic and structural decisions.

# 6. Final recommendation

### Recommended Strategic Decision
Management should immediately approve **Alternative E (Segmented Hybrid Rollout)**. Redesign R must be decoupled into distinct operational tracks based on building topology and technical risk, backed by automated compliance firewalls, restructured case ownership pods, and high-priority telemetry.

### Immediate Action (Within 48–72 Hours)
1. **Implement Automated Billing Lock:** Issue an emergency IT change request to hard-code an automated validation gate in the BSS: *no billing start event can be triggered without an automated optical signal confirmation and successful activation test event in the OSS*.
2. **Re-impose Mandatory Surveys on Complex Segments:** Freeze the predictive survey-skip rule for all Multi-Dwelling Units (apartments), buildings constructed prior to 1980, and business accounts. Reinstate mandatory physical surveys and pre-installation permit checks for these segments.
3. **Establish Coordinator Pods:** Group installation coordinators into 3-person regional "pods" sharing a common backlog view to ensure cross-coverage during absences and eliminate single-point-of-failure bottlenecks.
4. **Rebalance Field Capacity:** Issue dispatch directives redeploying underutilized field surveyors (currently at 52% utilization) to handle the reinstated MDU surveys, immediately relieving second-visit rework teams (currently at 92% utilization).

### What to Pause
- **Pause** all national scaling of Redesign R in its unsegmented form.
- **Pause** predictive survey skipping across all multi-dwelling, commercial, and heritage building categories.
- **Pause** post-billing activation checks unconditionally.
- **Pause** technician dispatches for second visits that do not possess a documented failure reason code and pre-visit coordinator review.

### What to Scale
- **Scale Nationally:** The merged Address Validation & Appointment Scheduling intake module for all verified low-risk single-family houses.
- **Scale Nationally:** Single-point-of-contact customer ownership, governed under the 3-person coordinator pod structure, for the single-family home segment.
- **Scale Nationally:** Conformance checking algorithms in the process-mining platform to monitor real-time trace deviations and compliance violations across all operational territories.

### What to Measure Next
1. **Deploy M7 & M3 Immediately:** Activate automated conformance monitoring for premature billing and missing permits; embed mandatory, structured failure reason codes into the field technician mobile application.
2. **Execute M4:** Track complete 60-day lifecycle costs (including secondary truck rolls, call center escalations, and churn) for the pilot cohorts to establish audited unit economics.
3. **Execute M6:** Perform a formal audit comparing predictive risk scores against physical survey findings in 500 apartment cases to establish algorithmic baseline precision.

### Thresholds and Evidence Justifying Broader Rollout
Broader national rollout of automated survey skipping and streamlined workflows to higher-risk segments will be justified **only** when the following quantitative thresholds are met across two consecutive monthly reporting cycles:
1. **Compliance Zero-Tolerance:** Premature billing events (Billing start before Activation test) and missing Permit checks remain at strictly **0.0%**.
2. **First-Time-Right (FTR) Recovery:** FTR across apartments and complex segments rebounds to \(\mathbf{\ge 82\%}\) (with simple houses sustaining \(\ge 85\%\)).
3. **Tail Duration Compression:** The p90 cycle time across all segments falls below **30.0 days**, and p95 falls below **42.0 days** (reversing the pilot tail explosion).
4. **Complaint Reduction:** Customer complaint rates within 30 days drop to \(\mathbf{\le 5.5\%}\) nationally.
5. **Algorithmic Reliability:** The predictive risk model demonstrates an audited false-negative rate of \(\mathbf{\le 3.0\%}\) in identifying structural/cabling impediments within multi-dwelling units.
6. **Sustainable Workload Balance:** Coordinator utilization stabilizes within the sustainable target band of **75% to 82%**, with second-visit team utilization dropping below **75%**.
7. **Net Financial Accretion:** Fully loaded 60-day cost per order (inclusive of all rework, revisit, and complaint handling) proves net accretive at \(\mathbf{\le \text{EUR } 185}\) per completed installation.
