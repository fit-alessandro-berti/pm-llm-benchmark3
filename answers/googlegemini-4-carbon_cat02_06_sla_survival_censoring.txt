# 1. SLA estimands and time definitions

## Time framework
- **Time origin ($t = 0$):** Case creation at `Submit`. All durations are measured in elapsed wall-clock hours from `Submit`.
- **SLA horizon ($\tau$):** $\tau = 72\text{ hours}$. Let $\tau_{\text{ext}} = 96\text{ hours}$ denote a bounded post-SLA window for late-resolution tracking where both cohorts retain observed risk-set support.
- **Random variables:** Let $T > 0$ denote the true elapsed time from `Submit` to the first absorbing event, and let $D \in \{\text{resolve}, \text{withdraw}, \text{reject}, \text{transfer}\}$ denote the event type under internal observation. Let $C > 0$ denote the administrative or loss-to-follow-up censoring time, so we observe $\tilde{T} = \min(T, C)$ (`duration_or_age_h`) and observed status.
- **[Assumption A1 — Immediate origin alignment]:** `Submit` marks the exact start of `duration_or_age_h` with zero unrecorded pre-submission lag. Needed because individual event timestamps are absent from the extract.

## Three operational SLA estimands
Because “probability of breaching the SLA” conflates **remaining stuck in the queue**, **exiting without operational success**, and **resolving late**, we define three distinct estimands at $\tau = 72\text{h}$:

1. **Estimand $E_1$ — Active Undisposed Queue Risk at 72h (All-Disposition Survival):**
   $$E_1(72) = S_{\text{disp}}(72) = P(T_{\text{disp}} > 72)$$
   The probability that a case has reached **no** terminal disposition by 72 hours.
   - *Best operational decision:* Queue capacity planning, staffing allocation, and live SLA-clock escalation management (measuring cases still consuming internal oversight after 72h).

2. **Estimand $E_2$ — Timely Operational Non-Resolution Risk by 72h (Complement of Resolution CIF):**
   $$E_2(72) = 1 - \text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72) = 1 - P(T \le 72 \cap D = \text{resolve}) = P(T > 72 \cup D \neq \text{resolve})$$
   The probability that a case fails to achieve operational success (`Resolve`) within 72 hours. By the law of total probability across mutually exclusive states at $72\text{h}$:
   $$E_2(72) = S_{\text{disp}}(72) + \text{CIF}_{\text{wd/rej}}(72) + \text{CIF}_{\text{trans}}(72)$$
   - *Best operational decision:* End-to-end customer/business SLA governance and anti-gaming oversight. Unlike $E_1$, $E_2$ cannot be artificially improved by prematurely rejecting, encouraging withdrawal, or transferring difficult cases before 72 hours.

3. **Estimand $E_3$ — Cumulative Incidence of Late Internal Resolution on $(72, t^*]$:**
   $$E_3(72, t^*) = \text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(t^*) - \text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72) = P(72 < T \le t^* \cap D = \text{resolve})$$
   The probability that a case breaches the 72-hour threshold *and* subsequently achieves internal `Resolve` by $t^*$ (e.g., $t^* = 96\text{h}$, or bounded at $t^* = \infty$ because the oldest Cohort B observation, `S26` at $104\text{h}$, is right-censored `open`, leaving tail mass unidentified without bounds).
   - *Best operational decision:* Triage of already-breached backlog—distinguishing recoverable late resolutions ("delayed value") from dead-end stalled work.

## Right censoring vs. competing events vs. already-observed breaches
- **Competing events (`Withdraw`, `Reject`, and internal-handoff `Transfer`):** Permanently remove a case from the internal risk set for `Resolve`. Treating them as ordinary right censoring violates survival semantics by assuming withdrawn/rejected cases would still eventually `Resolve` at the conditional hazard of active cases.
- **Pre-horizon right censoring (`open` with $\tilde{T} < 72\text{h}$, e.g., `S10`, `S15`, `S24`, `S29`, `S31`):** True uncertainty at $\tau = 72\text{h}$; we know $T > \tilde{T}$, so survival and competing-risk estimators redistribute their weight across remaining risk sets after $\tilde{T}$.
- **Post-horizon right censoring / already-observed breaches (`open` with $\tilde{T} > 72\text{h}$, i.e., `S09`, `S11`, `S25`, `S26`):** For any estimand evaluated *at* $\tau = 72\text{h}$ ($E_1(72)$ and $E_2(72)$), these cases are **not uncertain**—they were observed active at $t = 72\text{h}$ and **have already deterministically breached 72 hours** ($I(T > 72) = 1$). They are right-censored only with respect to *post-72h* event timing and ultimate disposition $D$ (affecting $E_3$).

| `open` ($> 72\text{h}$) | `S09, S11, S25, S26` ($n=4$) | Age at extract cutoff | `0` at $\tilde{T}$, **but $I(T > 72) = 1$ observed** | `0` at $\tilde{T}$ ($>72\text{h}$); known non-resolved at $72\text{h}$ | Right-censored *after* 72h horizon | Reclassify extreme dormancy (`S26` gap $72\text{h}$, `S11` gap $55\text{h}$, `S25` gap $48\text{h}$) as de facto abandoned/stalled competing state in sensitivity analysis. |

## Explicit treatment of `transferred` under three plausible assumptions
**Empirical observation:** Every `transferred` case in the dataset—`S08` (A, $68\text{h}$), `S21` (B, $26\text{h}$), and `S32` (B, $96\text{h}$)—is **Priority `P1`** and **escalated (`escalated = yes`)**, following `...Escalate>Transfer`. Treating `Transfer` casually as uninformative censoring is indefensible because transfer probability is concentrated entirely in high-severity escalated cases ($3/8 = 37.5\%$ of all `P1` cases, vs. $0/24 = 0\%$ of `P2`/`P3` cases).

1. **[Assumption T1 — Competing Internal Handoff ($\epsilon = 3$)]:**
   - *Justification:* Internally, the organization's operational control ends at `Transfer`. `Transfer` is a distinct absorbing internal outcome: `S21` ($26\text{h}$) and `S08` ($68\text{h}$) exit the internal active queue before $72\text{h}$ (so $E_1$ counts them as disposed $\le 72\text{h}$, whereas `S32` at $96\text{h}$ breached $72\text{h}$ before handoff), while $E_2$ counts all three as failing to achieve internal `Resolve` by $72\text{h}$.
2. **[Assumption T2 — Conditional MAR Right Censoring ($\epsilon = 0$ at transfer time)]:**
   - *Justification:* If the SLA measures system-wide customer resolution across internal and partner agencies, `Transfer` is loss to follow-up. Under T2, `S21` ($26\text{h}$) and `S08` ($68\text{h}$) are right-censored prior to $72\text{h}$, assuming transferred cases subsequently resolve at the same hazard as untransferred cases. *Critique:* Marginal MAR is implausible because transferred cases are exclusively `P1` escalated cases; at minimum, T2 must be stratified or IPCW-weighted by `priority` and `escalated` (**T3c**).
3. **[Assumption T3 — Informative-Censoring Extreme Bounds (T3a Pessimistic vs. T3b Optimistic)]:**
   - *Justification:* Because external outcomes for `S08` ($68\text{h}$) and `S21` ($26\text{h}$) are unobserved, non-parametric Manski-type bounds bracket true 72-hour system performance (`S32` at $96\text{h}$ already exceeded $72\text{h}$ prior to transfer in all worlds):
     - **T3a (Pessimistic / Handoff-Delay Bound):** External intake and triage add friction to already-escalated `P1` cases (especially `S08`, transferred at $68\text{h}$ with only $4\text{h}$ remaining before $72\text{h}$). Pre-72h transfers (`S08`, `S21`) are coded as failing to resolve by $72\text{h}$ and remaining unresolved past $72\text{h}$ ($T > 72$).
     - **T3b (Optimistic / Specialist Fast-Track Bound):** External specialist teams resolve pre-72h handoffs (`S08`, `S21`) within the 72h SLA ($\tilde{T} \le 72, \epsilon = 1$), while `S32` ($96\text{h}$) remains a >72h breach.

# 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased

A naive analysis restricting to the $13$ `resolved` cases (`S01–S04, S07, S13` in Cohort A; `S16–S20, S27, S28` in Cohort B) reports:
- **Cohort A ($n=6$ resolved):** $2/6 = 33.3\%$ breach rate (`S03` at $76\text{h}$, `S13` at $110\text{h}$); mean resolution time $= 54.7\text{h}$.
- **Cohort B ($n=7$ resolved):** $2/7 = 28.6\%$ breach rate (`S20` at $74\text{h}$, `S28` at $88\text{h}$); mean resolution time $= 45.6\text{h}$.

This naive comparison suggests Cohort B reduced SLA breaches and shortened cycle time by $9.1\text{h}$. Every step of that inference is severely distorted by five mechanisms:

1. **Survivor / Right-Censoring Bias from Dropping Open Cases:**
   Excluding the $9$ `open` cases (`S09, S10, S11, S15` in A; `S24, S25, S26, S29, S31` in B) discards **$4$ confirmed >72h SLA breaches** (`S09` at $92\text{h}$, `S11` at $81\text{h}$, `S25` at $79\text{h}$, `S26` at $104\text{h}$) plus $1$ transferred breach (`S32` at $96\text{h}$). Over half of all already-realized >72h breaches in the extract ($5$ of $9$ cases with $\tilde{T} > 72\text{h}$) are non-`resolved` rows! Conditioning on `observed_status == 'resolved'` systematically over-samples fast completions.

2. **Conflating Open Cases Below 72h vs. Above 72h:**
   - **Open cases $> 72\text{h}$ (`S09`[$92\text{h}$], `S11`[$81\text{h}$], `S25`[$79\text{h}$], `S26`[$104\text{h}$]):** Their 72h queue-breach status is **already realized** ($T > 72\text{h}$). Moreover, their `last_event_gap_h` values are massive—`S26` ($72\text{h}$ silent since `Review`), `S11` ($55\text{h}$ silent since `Escalate`), `S25` ($48\text{h}$ silent since `Escalate`), and `S09` ($31\text{h}$ silent since `Review`)—indicating stalled or "zombie" cases rather than newly arrived work.
   - **Open cases $< 72\text{h}$ (`S10`[$47\text{h}$, gap $19\text{h}$], `S15`[$35\text{h}$, gap $2\text{h}$], `S24`[$67\text{h}$, gap $4\text{h}$], `S29`[$51\text{h}$, gap $6\text{h}$], `S31`[$23\text{h}$, gap $3\text{h}$]):** These are active in-flight cases censored by the extract freeze prior to $72\text{h}$. However, their remaining slack differs sharply: `S31` ($23\text{h}$ age, $3\text{h}$ gap) has $49\text{h}$ of SLA runway, whereas `S24` ($67\text{h}$ age in `Review`) has only **$5\text{h}$ left** to resolve before breaching, and `S10` ($47\text{h}$ age) has been inactive for $19\text{h}$ post-escalation.

3. **Informative Censoring via `Transfer` and Dormancy:**
   Consider **Priority `P1`** across both cohorts ($8$ cases total: `S04, S08, S11` in A; `S18, S21, S25, S29, S32` in B).
   - **Naive resolved-only `P1` estimate:** Only `S04` ($29\text{h}$) and `S18` ($12\text{h}$) resolved—yielding a **naive `P1` SLA breach rate of $0.0\%$ ($0/2$) and mean duration of $20.5\text{h}$**!
   - **Actual `P1` reality:** Of the other $6$ `P1` cases, **$3$ have already breached 72h** (`S11` open at $81\text{h}$, `S25` open at $79\text{h}$, `S32` transferred at $96\text{h}$), $2$ were transferred out post-escalation (`S21` at $26\text{h}$, `S08` at $68\text{h}$), and $1$ is still open in post-escalation review (`S29` at $51\text{h}$). Dropping transfers and open cases masks catastrophic tail risk in the highest-priority tier.

4. **Competing Risks (`Withdraw` and `Reject`):**
   Seven cases exit via `rejected` (`S05`[$9\text{h}$], `S14`[$63\text{h}$], `S23`[$11\text{h}$]) or `withdrawn` (`S06`[$27\text{h}$], `S12`[$15\text{h}$], `S22`[$44\text{h}$], `S30`[$70\text{h}$]). All $7$ occur $\le 70\text{h}$, and $5$ of $7$ (`S05, S12, S14, S23, S30`) are **`P3`** ($50\%$ of all `P3` cases!). Excluding them inflates apparent `P3` resolution success; conversely, treating them as independent right censoring in a standard $1 - \text{KM}$ curve for `Resolve` overestimates cumulative resolution probability because absorbed cases can never subsequently resolve.

5. **Cohort Imbalance and Selective Policy Rollout Non-Compliance:**
   - **Priority shift:** Cohort B has a higher `P1` share ($5/17 = 29.4\%$) than Cohort A ($3/15 = 20.0\%$). Because `P1` cases carry heavy escalation, transfer, and >72h stall rates, unadjusted cohort comparisons penalize Cohort B on queue retention ($E_1$).
   - **Selective compliance & pre-rollout contamination:** In Cohort B, **$12/12$ ($100\%$) of `P1` and `P2` cases** start with the new automated triage (`Submit>AutoCheck...`), whereas **$0/5$ ($0\%$) of `P3` cases** start with `AutoCheck` (`S17` runs `Submit>Screen>AutoCheck...`, and `S20, S23, S26, S30` omit `AutoCheck` altogether—including both `P3` >72h breaches in B, `S20`[$74\text{h}$] and `S26`[$104\text{h}$]!). Meanwhile, Cohort A already had $2$ `P2` cases (`S01, S15`) using `Submit>Screen>AutoCheck`. Raw Cohort A vs. B contrasts conflate treatment effect, priority mix, and complete non-rollout on `P3`.

# 4. Statistical analysis plan

## Method comparison for this extract ($N=32$, prefix-only without intermediate timestamps)

| Method | Target Estimand | Strengths on Supplied Data | Failure Modes / Constraints on Supplied Data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kaplan-Meier (KM)** | $E_1$: $S_{\text{disp}}(t)$ (time to *any* disposition) | Exact, non-parametric, handles pre-72h open cases (`S10, S15, S24, S29, S31`) without parametric hazard assumptions; works well at $N=32$. | Biased upward ($1 - \text{KM} > \text{CIF}$) if applied to `Resolve` alone by censoring `Withdraw`/`Reject`/`Transfer`. Vulnerable to informative censoring under T2. |
| **Aalen-Johansen (AJ) / Competing-Risks CIF** | $E_2, E_3$: $\text{CIF}_k(t)$ and $S_{\text{disp}}(t)$ simultaneously | Non-parametrically partitions probability at $72\text{h}$ into `Resolve`, `Withdraw/Reject`, `Transfer`, and `Open` summing strictly to $1.0$; requires only $(\tilde{T}, \epsilon)$. | Small strata ($N=32$ split by Cohort $\times$ Priority gives $3$–$7$ cases per cell) yield wide step-function intervals; requires standardization rather than deep stratification. |
| **Cause-Specific Hazards (CSH, e.g., Cox PH)** | Instantaneous transition rate $\lambda_k(t \mid X)$ among active cases | Separates covariate associations with resolution speed vs. withdrawal/rejection/transfer speed. | With only $13$ resolutions, $7$ withdrawals/rejections, and $3$ transfers, unpenalized multivariable Cox violates events-per-variable rules; PH fails when `AutoCheck` accelerates early exits ($<20\text{h}$) while escalations stall late. |
| **Subdistribution Hazards (Fine-Gray)** | Direct covariate effect on $\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(t)$ ($E_2$) | Directly links baseline covariates (`cohort`, `priority`) to cumulative resolution incidence. | Retains prior competing exits in the risk set artificially; **cannot** validly incorporate internal time-varying covariates (`Escalate`, `Rework`) without severe interpretability and immortal-time distortion; low power at $N=32$. |
| **Restricted Mean Survival / Time Lost (RMST / RMTL)** | $\int_0^{72} S_{\text{disp}}(u)\,du$ and $\int_0^{72} \text{CIF}_k(u)\,du$ | Assumption-free summary of expected active queue hours per case within $[0, 72\text{h}]$; higher small-sample efficiency than single-point $S(72)$ when hazards cross. | Summarizes average time up to $72\text{h}$ rather than pure >72h tail breach probability; must be paired with $S_{\text{disp}}(72)$ and $\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72)$. |
| **Landmarking (at $t_L \in \{24, 48\}\text{h}$)** | Dynamic conditional risk $P(T > 72 \mid T > t_L, H(t_L))$ | Eliminates immortal-time bias when conditioning on process history accumulated by $t_L$. | **Critical data constraint:** Intermediate event timestamps are missing! For `S03` (resolved at $76\text{h}$ with `Escalate`), we do *not* know whether `Escalate` occurred before or after $t_L = 48\text{h}$! Valid only for baseline covariates or at the *current* observation point for open cases. |
| **Multi-State Model (MSM)** | Stage-to-stage transition intensities $\lambda_{ij}(t)$ | Mirrors process control-flow (`Screen`, `Review`, `Rework`, `Escalate`) and absorbs competing exits naturally. | Continuous-time Markov/semi-Markov intensities require entry/exit timestamps for every stage. Here, only discrete branching probabilities and open-case current-stage sojourn lower bounds (`last_event_gap_h`) are identifiable. |

## Recommended primary and secondary analytical strategy
- **Recommendation — Primary Approach: Priority-Standardized Aalen-Johansen Competing-Risks Estimator + All-Disposition KM / RMST($72\text{h}$) across Transfer & Censoring Bounds.**
  - *Justification:* Directly estimates both $E_1(72) = S_{\text{disp}}(72)$ and $E_2(72) = 1 - \text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72)$ (plus bounded $E_3(72, 96)$) using only observed $(\tilde{T}, \epsilon)$, respects competing risks without proportional-hazards assumptions, avoids over-parameterization at $N=32$, and adjusts for the `P1` imbalance between Cohorts A and B via direct standardization to the pooled priority weights ($P1 = 8/32$, $P2 = 14/32$, $P3 = 10/32$).
- **Recommendation — Secondary Approach: Discrete Transition-Probability Audit + Baseline/Initial-Triage Stratified Sensitivity & Current-State Open-Case Diagnostic.**
  - *Justification:* Uses exact control-flow transition frequencies to audit where Cohort B changed routing (`Submit>AutoCheck` compliance on `P1/P2` vs. `P3`), pairs penalized cause-specific hazard checks restricted strictly to baseline covariates (`cohort`, `priority`), and evaluates open-case conditional tail risk using observed last-event times $t_{\text{last}} = \tilde{T} - \text{last\_event\_gap\_h}$ without fabricating historical timestamps for closed cases.

## Summary of fragile vs. robust conclusions
- **Conclusions that reverse across reasonable specifications:** Whether Cohort B "improved SLA performance overall" reverses completely depending on (i) whether `Withdraw`/`Reject` count as timely closures ($E_1$: Cohort B worse) or failures to resolve ($E_2$: Cohort B slightly better), (ii) whether imminent-breach open case `S24` ($67\text{h}$) breaches, and (iii) whether Cohort B is evaluated intention-to-treat vs. within compliant `P2` cases.
- **Robust conclusions invariant across all analyses:** (1) `P1` tail risk is severely underestimated by completed-case reporting ($\ge 37.5\%$ confirmed >72h breaches vs. $0\%$ naive); (2) post-review escalation and long inactivity gaps (`last_event_gap_h` $\ge 19\text{h}$) dominate >72h queue persistence; and (3) Cohort B's `P3` non-compliance coincides with doubled `P3` >72h queue retention.

# 7. Operational interpretation

## Converting survival outputs into operational actions
1. **Two-trigger live alerting for open cases (combining remaining slack and stage-inactivity hazard):**
   - *SLA-Horizon Proximity Alert ($E_1$ dynamic risk):* Trigger immediate supervisor intervention when an unescalated `Review` case reaches $t = 54\text{h}$–$60\text{h}$ (`S24` at $67\text{h}$ in `Review` has only $5\text{h}$ left—waiting until $72\text{h}$ guarantees a breach).
   - *Dormancy / Stall Alert (`last_event_gap_h` threshold):* Observe that `S11`, `S25`, and `S26` all entered `Escalate` or `Review` around $26\text{h}$–$32\text{h}$—well inside the SLA—and breached *solely* because no subsequent event occurred for $48\text{h}$–$72\text{h}$. Enforce stage-specific inactivity SLAs: e.g., **max $8\text{h}$ silence in `Escalate` for `P1`** (would have caught `S11` at $34\text{h}$ and `S25` at $39\text{h}$) and **max $16\text{h}$ silence in `Review` or `P2 Escalate`** (flags `S10` at $47\text{h}$, gap $19\text{h}$, with $25\text{h}$ still left to rescue the SLA).
2. **Paired SLA governance metrics (preventing Goodhart's Law):**
   - Never govern teams on $E_1(72)$ alone or naive resolved-case duration alone. Pair **Active Queue Breach Rate $E_1(72)$** with **Timely Resolution Incidence $\text{CIF}_{\text{res}}(72) = 1 - E_2(72)$** and **Pre-72h Transfer / Rejection Audit Rates**. Otherwise, teams can hit queue-clearance targets by transferring hard `P1` cases right before $72\text{h}$ (as seen in `S08` at $68\text{h}$) or prematurely rejecting/nudging withdrawals.
3. **Why risk scores must NOT be presented as causal effects:**
   - Cases with `escalated = yes` exhibit much higher >72h breach persistence ($6/11$ escalated cases have $\tilde{T} > 72\text{h}$ or transferred at $68\text{h}$, vs. $3/21$ non-escalated cases). Presenting an escalation-based risk score as causal would falsely imply *“escalating causes SLA breaches; stop escalating to improve SLA compliance.”* In reality, escalation is a **severity marker** (confounding by indication). High predicted risk identifies **where to deploy senior capacity**, not which process step to suppress.
