# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases  

QA  
• Supporting: (A02 after A03) – matches FIFO with appointment override  
• Contradicting: (A04 waited 38 min despite prio 1) – calibration + ongoing long job explain delay  

QB  
• Supporting: Pre-emption of B01 twice for prio-1 arrivals  
• Contradicting: B03 chosen before B05 – explained by 2 min scan lag or by SPT tie-break  

QC  
• Supporting: Strict 8  14  22 min SPT sequence (C02-C03-C04)  
• Contradicting: Late selection of C05 despite prio-1 and tight deadline – indicates secondary escalation rule rather than pure SPT  

QD  
• Supporting: Each resource only picks jobs it is skilled for (e.g., D-F cannot take mechanical) and then oldest first  
• Contradicting: D-F idles 44 min to avoid mid-meeting pre-emption – requires “meeting-aware” exception not in the documentation  

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests  

1. QB, 09:48 decision  
   • Hypothesis H1: pre-emptive priority  
   • Hypothesis H2: pure SPT  
   • Prediction: H1 would serve B05 (prio 1, 14 min), H2 serves B03 (12 min) as observed  
   • Discriminating observable: precise queue-entry timestamp of B05 as seen by the dispatcher (not the scan time).  

2. QC, 10:54 decision  
   • H1: continuing SPT would pick C06 (6 min)  
   • H2: deadline-aware policy picks C05 (deadline missed) as observed  
   • Needed observable: real-time slack (deadline-minus-estimated-remaining) the dispatcher computed at 10:54.  

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations (ranked)  

1. Scan-lag (0-2 min) – highly plausible; explains QB anomaly and minor QA offsets.  
2. Resource blackout compliance (calibration, meetings) – confirmed by meta-data; shapes QA and QD sequences.  
3. Hidden resume/setup overhead – could make short jobs preferable even under FIFO; would raise QC’s apparent SPT bias.  
4. Strategic under-estimation of `est_min` by technicians – would undermine SPT diagnosis if pervasive but no evidence beyond normal noise.  

# 6. Additional event data needed  

Priority order reflects the ambiguities:  

1. Exact dispatcher-visibility time of each arrival (separate from scan).  
2. Time-stamped “decision point” events: queue evaluated, candidate set enumerated, rule applied.  
3. Remaining-time estimate at every pre-emption or resume (to test SPT and SEPT).  
4. Explicit appointment tolerance window recorded at scheduling time.  
5. Resource state change log (busy, setup, meeting, calibration) with reason codes.  
6. Policy-version tag when a queue switches from normal to SLA-recovery mode.  

# 7. Final synthesis  

• Most identifiable: QD – every observed choice is fully explained by skill feasibility plus FIFO; contradictions are logistical, not logical.  
• Least identifiable: QC – one deadline escalation gives only weak evidence for SPT vs deadline-driven rules.  
• Riskiest inference: Assuming QB follows strict pre-emptive priority; a two-minute visibility error flips the diagnosis between priority and SPT.
