## Grading rationale (strict)

The submission is unusually thorough, well-structured, and aligned with the required nine-section format. It correctly frames specialization–pooling tension, bottleneck migration, SLA/tail vs fairness/robustness objectives, and implementation risk. It is still well short of near-flawless under a hypercritical standard because of material analytical gaps, a scenario table that is not evidenced by the model, and a simulation that does not credibly implement the dynamics the prose claims to study.

### Major issues (drive most of the deduction)

1. **Scenario comparison table is not supported by computation**  
   Section 4 gives wide numeric bands for every policy×scenario cell, then Section 8 provides code that would produce different quantities. No run outputs, seeds summary, or reconciliation appear in the answer body. The table is therefore invented planning language dressed as analysis. For a process-mining / capacity task that explicitly allows simulation or approximation, this is a core failure of evidence use, not a cosmetic gap.

2. **“Simulation” is not a competent model of the stated system**  
   Cases are fully scheduled one-after-another in arrival order while mutating `free[r]`. That is sequential list scheduling, not a discrete-event model of concurrent WIP, competing queues, or mid-service preemption. Consequences:
   - A later P1 cannot truly seize an in-progress lower-priority diagnostic under the stated rule.
   - Restart penalty is faked (`10% of 50% of planned`) rather than applied to remaining active service of a victim activity.
   - Dispatch does not re-evaluate when new arrivals appear during someone else’s wait.
   - Parallel ME/ES, coordination, and team-handover interactions are only lightly approximated.  
   The text labels this a “scaffold,” but Sections 2–5 still reason as if preemption, contention, and resilience were analyzed. That is internally inconsistent.

3. **Offered-load analysis under-uses multipliers, calendars, and labor vs elapsed distinction**  
   Base diagnostic minutes vs 2,490 net roster minutes is a useful alarm, but:
   - Speed multipliers are not folded into skill-specific offered load in a clear, auditable way.
   - Parallel branches reduce elapsed time, not labor; the write-up notes this then largely drops it in bottleneck claims.
   - Break staggering, Farah’s half-day, Emil’s late window, and Validation convergence are discussed qualitatively; the numeric bridge from block demand  queueing risk is thin.
   - U2’s “+110 E and +50 S” and similar shock deltas are rough and not fully derived in-place.

4. **Preemption, handover, and setup semantics drift between prose and code**  
   Prose: P1 may preempt; victim at most once; restart = 10% of remaining active service; Intake/Validation not preempted; 7 + conditional 5 team handovers; setup on skill-family switch.  
   Code: opportunistic “reclaim 10 minutes” heuristic; restart on an assumed half residual; team +5 only partially; coordination sometimes charged to a resource’s busy time and sometimes as elapsed; specialist overflow and hybrid reservations only weakly match Section 3.  
   Operators could not audit the recommended policy against this implementation.

### Moderate issues

6. **Policy specifications are strong on paper, softer as dispatch rules**

Hybrid reservations (“virtual slot,” Chen/Emil guards, overflow at 45 minutes) are still partly conceptual: scoring terms, who holds the slot, and how projected slack is estimated in real time are underspecified for an auditable control-room rule.

   Denominator sometimes net of breaks excluding OT, while availability spans include +30 OT. Fragile-at-90% is used, but restart/handover/setup/coordination charging into utilization is not kept fully consistent. CV-of-utilization as fairness is fine; claiming tight CV bands in Section 4 without a model is not.

8. **Python checklist: meets the letter, misses the spirit**  
   Tables in memory, three policies, log fields, `format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`, handover network, stage-wait side calculation, multi-seed groupby—present.  
   Failures relative to the quality bar: concurrency/preemption fidelity; brittle PM4Py API assumptions; hacky multi-level column rename; no integrated printed numeric comparison that Section 4 relies on; mechanisms “identified” in comments while the narrative still over-claims.

9. **Smaller technical nits**
