## Evaluation

### Overall
Strong transportability analysis with correct structural diagnosis (do not copy full `+(Identity, Document)`), clear confounding of Site A’s before/after, sensible segmentation, bottleneck/tail scenarios, and a mechanism-vs-implementation pilot. Required format is largely met. Score is held well below “near flawless” by non-runnable PM4Py construction, a material logic slip in expansion criteria, and several smaller inconsistencies.

---

### Section-by-section

**1. Process-structural comparison — strong**  
Twelve+ features, right columns, and the right design consequence: originals block Document start; true parallel only on the digital path; Identity//Originals-wait for the rest. DFG use (6% overlap, Originals→Document dominance) is appropriate. Dense cells and occasional over-claim (“illegal” vs “infeasible until receipt”) are minor.

**2. Transportability graph — strong**  
Required nodes and `S_` selection structure are present; shared vs site-specific mechanisms and “same diagram ≠ transportable” are well argued. Weaknesses: `API_latency → identity_capacity` confuses latency with capacity; capacity nodes are thinly linked to `cycle_time`; graph is more a mechanism sketch than a full selection diagram for identification. Explanation length is acceptable.

**3. Evidence transfer table — very strong**  
Correctly refuses to treat −9h median as an identified effect; co-interventions (FTE, mix, API, huddles, logging) are scored with appropriately low confidences. Good mediator focus on waiting before Assessment. Minor: back-of-envelope mix contribution is asserted without transparent derivation.

**4. Effect modifiers — strong**  
Required modifiers covered; beneficial/harmful signatures and mitigations are operationally usable. Some rows blur legality vs capacity (parallel Document before originals is a hard constraint, not only a “rework risk”).

**5. Quantitative scenarios — strong**  
Five labeled scenarios; includes median↑ / p90↓ divergence; scaling by ~38% digital share and discounting A for confounders is the right logic. Issues: “optimistic / unlawful” still reports population gains as if deployable; upper bounds are somewhat hand-wavy; confidence 55 on the recommended scenario is a bit high given residual IT/capacity risk.

**6. Candidate intervention — strong conceptually, messy formally**  
Eligibility split, statutory edges, WIP/huddles, dual-owner logging, and supplement handling are the right design. The POWL/partial-order text is informal, partly contradictory (nested PO vs branch choices; Legal optional vs always-after-Assessment), and not a clean executable spec.

**7. Pilot — strong**  
Stratified RCT, T0/T1/T1p, true vs logged overlap, interference/WIP controls, stop rules, and ITT vs per-protocol/IV for mechanism vs implementation are excellent. Minor: power assumptions (SD, weekly eligible volume) are unsupported; day-clustering vs case-level randomization is underspecified.

**8. PM4Py code — weak (largest penalty)**  
Intent matches the brief (A trees → nets; B POWL with concurrency and Assessment after both checks; soundness/playout comments). Execution fidelity fails several ways:
- `check_is_workflow_net(..., fm_A_after)` is a clear copy-paste bug on the A-before net.
- `OperatorPOWL.XOR` / `OP.LOOP` as attributes is not standard PM4Py usage (`Operator` enum is the usual pattern).
- `children=[legal_loop, None]` is not a valid silent branch.
- `POWL(powl_main)` wrapping a `StrictPartialOrder` is dubious; nested reuse of the same `Transition` instances across main PO, redo PO, and legal loop is a structural hazard.
- Digital vs originals is not a real XOR branch (commented away), so the “candidate” net always enforces Originals→Document.
- `play_out` on a POWL object is unlikely to match the usual Petri-net API.
- Comments on what control-flow cannot encode are good and partly redeem the section.

Under strict grading, a required artifact that would not run cleanly and does not fully encode the stated B design is a major defect.

**9. Recommendation — strong with one serious slip**  
`conditional go`, transfer kernel (Identity under originals wait; full parallel only when Document is free), first stratum, and risks are right. **Expansion rule error:** “Expand only if … rework +1.5pp” reads as requiring a rework *increase*; it should be a bound (e.g. rework increase &lt; +1.5pp). That is a real logical flaw in the decision policy. Also “At At 62%” and slightly rosy benefit ranges.

**10. Assumption register — good**  
Eight assumptions, testable, with consequences. Formatting glitches (“17%20%+”, “intentexecution”) and a few assumptions that are slightly composite, not pure.

### Why not higher
Utmost strictness: non-executable / API-inconsistent §8, expansion-criteria contradiction, informal POWL, and several quantitative/hand-wave spots bar anything in the 8.5–10 band. The analogical and transport core is clearly above average; the answer is **very good analysis with a broken required implementation section and one decision-rule error**, not near-flawless work.

### Why not lower
It does the hard intellectual work the rubric cares about: confounded A evidence, non-transport of the −9h, statutory partial order, effect modifiers, median/tail divergence, and a learning pilot that separates mechanism from IT/implementation failure.
