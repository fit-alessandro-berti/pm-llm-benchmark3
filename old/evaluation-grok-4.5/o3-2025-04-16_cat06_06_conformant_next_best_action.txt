## Evaluation

The answer has the right *shape* for this task: six required sections, A/B correctly treated as nonconformant and ranked last, G as not-yet-enabled, F as conditional, and a parallel conformant plan preferred over pure deadline optimization. ### Critical flaws

**1. Broken timing in the top-ranked plan.**  
Deadline is 90 minutes. Second-officer review alone is 35–75 minutes, plus 5–15 to record/execute. A fallback that waits until **minute 55** then interrupts S2 has earliest finish ≈ 55+35+5 = **95 minutes** and typical finish well past 90. The fallback therefore tends to **miss P-884’s deadline and still damage P-901**—the opposite of a coherent contingency. Calling the worst conformant path “execute at 90 min” is not supported by the given durations (S4 at 65 + review 75 already ≈ 140). This is a core error in the primary recommendation.

**2. Overstated success probabilities and internal inconsistency.**  
Table benefit uses deadline-save factors ~0.55–0.80; §3 claims 0.70–0.85 for the same plan; branch 1a invents 0.25–0.35 that alternate screening both clears the match and drives risk &lt; 70. None of these are in the prompt. Given SLA-breach 0.48–0.64 under normal processing and long second-review tails, 0.70–0.85 on-time probability for the composite is optimistic special pleading. Benefit ranges €11–28k vs €14–30k also disagree.

**3. Fallacious “dominance” math for A/B (§3 / §4).**  
“Even a 90% chance of avoiding a €100k fine still yields €10k EV, worse than any conformant path” is confused and **does not establish dominance**: 0.1 × €100k = €10k &lt; €20–35k operational upside, so pure EV can still favor the unlawful shortcut. The right argument is asymmetric regulatory/non-monetary harm, enforcement uncertainty, and normative prohibition—not this calculation. The answer partly recovers with “non-monetary ≈ infinite,” but the numerical anchor undermines the conformance point it is meant to support.
