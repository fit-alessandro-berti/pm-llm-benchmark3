## Grading rationale (hypercritical)

This is a genuinely strong submission: careful arithmetic, real process semantics, disciplined refusal to equate stratified association with identification, a usable learning-oriented intervention plan, and diagnostics code that matches the brief. Under a normal standard it would land near 9+. Under the required hypercritical standard, several defects are material enough to keep it well clear of the top band.

### Major / material issues

**1. Causal graph contains a cycle (Section 2)**  
The adjacency list asserts both  
`complexity  unobserved_case_risk` and `unobserved_case_risk  complexity`.  
That is a 2-cycle. A DAG cannot contain it. The first arrow is also the wrong structural claim: latent risk should drive the coarse complexity label, not the reverse. This is not cosmetic; it is a direct error in the section whose job is to encode causal structure. Bidirectional “measurement” hand-waving does not repair a cyclic DAG.

**2. “Evidence against” in §1 does not oppose the chosen verdict**  
Verdict = `mainly confounded` (for “Orion causes longer times”).  
The paragraph labeled “strongest evidence against” cites residual confounding within complexity, fatal overlap, and wait-vs-service decomposition. Those points undermine confidence in a *beneficial* Orion reading and push toward `not identifiable`; they do not cut against “the crude harmful association is mainly confounding.” The section therefore mislabels its own counter-evidence. The later clarification that “Orion is faster” is a different, non-identified estimand is good, but it makes the 70-confidence `mainly confounded` verdict sit in tension with the author’s own identification caveats. `not identifiable` (with a strong side comment that the crude gap is mix-driven) would have been cleaner.

**3. Total / direct / mediated distinction is under-delivered where required**  
The brief requires §1 to distinguish total, direct, and mediated interpretations of a possible Orion effect. The executive text focuses on confounding of the crude total contrast and notes that the within-stratum gap lives in wait. It does not cleanly separate:
- total effect of group/routing,
- direct effect after mediators,
- effects mediated by compliance, queue, rework, skill mix.  
That work appears later (§3–5), but §1—the mandated location—is thin on this axis.

### Moderate issues

**4. Wait-share claim is overstated**  
“80–90% of every within-stratum contrast is waiting” is false for Low:  
\(|\Delta wait|/|\Delta cycle| \approx 1.61/2.14 \approx 75\%\). High is ~96%, Medium ~85%. The approximation is presented as uniform.

**5. Adjacency-list format is loose**  
Multi-target prose lines plus a selection annotation are readable but not a clean directed adjacency list as specified. Secondary to the cycle error, but still non-compliant.

**6. §1 length**  
Roughly at or slightly above the 300-word cap once the dense third paragraph is counted. Minor, but the format constraints were explicit.

**7. Small-cell inference still leaks persuasive force**  
The write-up correctly flags \(n=2\) High-Nova and \(n=2\) Low-Orion cells as anecdotal, yet repeatedly leans on “every stratum reverses” and a near-constant Orion offset as if the pattern were robust. The caveats and the rhetoric are not fully aligned.

### Minor issues

- `resource_group  unobserved_reviewer_skill` is defensible as skill-mix, but skill is more naturally a pre-treatment group attribute than a downstream effect; the graph blurs that.  
- H4’s “compressed tail” argument (Orion max 33.5 vs Nova 36.0) is very weak given sample size and case mix; listed as supporting evidence with more weight than it deserves.  
- Compliance within Orion-Medium ( 0.5 h) is correctly computed but then over-read as “essentially no penalty” while admitting the wait field bundles compliance waiting with queueing—so the null is not identified either.  
- Python block is excellent diagnostically; a few printed interpretations edge toward argument rather than pure diagnostics, but the guardrails at the end mitigate this.
