## Evaluation

The submission is ambitious, well-structured, and often conceptually strong on hybrid layering and the R5/R6 conflict. Under strict grading it is not close to a high score: the artifacts are not internally coherent, several encodings are wrong relative to PM4Py semantics, and the verdict matrix does not faithfully apply the models it defines.

### What works

- Required 10-section shape is complete; R1–R12 are covered; assumptions A1–A6 are mostly sound.
- R5 vs R6 is treated as a real policy conflict, with two resolutions, an explicit choice (A), and compliance risk stated.
- Sensible split of concerns in prose: DECLARE for many activity-order rules, skeleton for cardinalities, temporal as warnings, resources/data outside activity-only models.
- R3 → `altresponse` (vs plain `response` / `chainresponse`) is the right DECLARE instinct.
- Temporal profile units, `zeta = 2.0`, and “warning not fail” reading of R12 match the brief.
- §7 SoD / pending-clarification / R11 sketches are directionally right; missing-attribute → `not evaluated` is correct policy.
- §9–§10 are thoughtful on vacuity, duplication, support/confidence misuse, and check ordering.

These strengths raise the floor; they do not rescue correctness.

### Critical failures

**1. DECLARE vs verdict matrix (decisive internal contradiction)**  
Resolution A installs global `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`. That rule fails every accepted case with no legal opinion (T1, T2, T4, and the non-escalation part of normal accept paths). The matrix still marks those DECLARE cells **pass**. The mapping text even admits the rule is stricter than the conditional legal rule, then §8 ignores that admission. A hybrid model whose own rules and own test table disagree is not a coherent deliverable.

**2. Procedural model vs T6/T7 (and extra-log activities)**  
The process tree’s alphabet omits `Sanctions Escalation`, `Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Change Bank Details`, and `Suspend Vendor`. Traces T6/T7 contain escalation/opinion (and the narrative marks procedural **pass**). Under normal PT/Petri conformance, those events are unexplained log moves → **fail** (or require an extended model that was not given). Marking T6/T7 procedural pass is false relative to the code shown.

**3. Clarification loop: formal text, code, and tests disagree**  
- Compact form suggests looping around `Classify Risk`; code is `LOOP(tau, Request→Receive)` **after** `Classify`.  
- Spec/tests allow clarification before classification (T2, T5).  
- §8 still gives T2 procedural `pass*` and T5 procedural `pass*`. Even with the disclosed approximation, honest cells are **fail** (or “fail under tree / pass under DECLARE”), not soft passes.  
- LOOP(tau, clarif) can generate 0..* paired cycles; it still cannot generate T5’s unpaired `Request` or pre-classify placement.

**4. Log skeleton `always_after` orientation is inverted for PM4Py**  
PM4Py-style `always_after(A, B)` means: when A occurs, B occurs later. The dictionary stores pairs such as `("Close Onboarding", "Receive Onboarding Request")`, `("Sanctions Escalation", "Run Sanctions Screening")`, `("Activate Vendor", "Capture Master Data")`. Under standard semantics those assert the **opposite** of the intended backbone (and the Escalation/Screening pair actively fights R7). The prose claims mirrors of `always_before`, but the encoding is not API-correct. That makes §5 unsafe to execute.

**5. R2 claimed “external authoritative” but not implemented**  
§2 assigns conditional “exactly one approval iff accepted / none if rejected” to a data layer. §7 never implements that count/XOR check—only SoD, clarification pending, emergency, R11. Noncoexistence approximations do not enforce “exactly one” on accept paths. Gap between mapping and executable layer.

- **Empty `"absence": {}`**: noise; comment admits it is not a real at-most-one encoding.
