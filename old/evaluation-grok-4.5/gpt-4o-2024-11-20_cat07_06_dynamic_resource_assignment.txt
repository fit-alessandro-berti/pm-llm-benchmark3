### Policy algorithm (Section 2) — conceptually thin and partly incoherent
- **Skill score** `max(0, resource_skill - required_skill)` is ill-defined: the matrix has per-activity levels 0–3, not a separate numeric “required_skill” threshold. The formula does not encode “prefer 3 for security,” “≥2 for high priority,” or supervised 1–2.
- **Urgency** multiplies by `case_priority` while also branching on urgent/high as strings—type-inconsistent and underspecified.
- No **aggregate utility**, weights, or lexicographic order. Filtering → vague scores → tie-break is not an auditable decision procedure.
- Case ordering vs resource updating (after each assignment: open cases, hours, batch cap) is not specified, so the policy could not be implemented reproducibly from the text.
- Resilience/bottleneck and exception paths are slogans, not rules.
- Socio-technical content is named in constraints but not built into the decision steps (supervision capacity of Gita/Hugo, team boundaries, explainable reason codes).
