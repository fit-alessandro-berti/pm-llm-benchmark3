## Overall

The memo picks a defensible hybrid (E) and mostly respects the “don’t scale nationally / don’t full rollback” constraints, but it is thin on process-mining substance, sloppy with evidence hygiene, and weak on decision thresholds and VoI. Under a strict read it is a competent outline, not a high-quality executive decision product.

## Critical flaws

**1. Fact / interpretation / assumption collapse (Section 2)**  
The evidence table fails its core job. Several “Fact” cells are interpretations, not measurements:

- “Rising efficiency in low-risk segments” is not a fact of median −3.4 days; median is overall.
- “Increased errors post-process simplification” is causal gloss, not the FTR delta.
- “Reducing handovers streamlined ownership” is interpretation of 93% utilization.
- “Missing task leads to SLA breaches…” is causal claim in the Fact column for permit absence.

The table also invents linkage (e.g., permit absence → SLA/long tails) that the prompt does not quantify as a measured couple. Assumptions are generic (“risk trade-off generalizes”) rather than the real uncertainties (pilot vs baseline mix, incomplete attributes before week 3, cost cutoff after week 4, sparse start timestamps, apartment-heavy pilot).

**2. Material misstatement of evidence**  
“reduced p90 cycle time for complex cases (31 to 38.5 days)” is wrong directionally in wording: p90 **worsened** (rose). That is an elementary error in an executive memo and undercuts trust.

**3. Process-mining artifacts underused**  
Required elements are barely treated:

- Alignments (billing before activation; repeat install without survey; missing permit; complaint after billing) are not systematically used in the decision logic.
- Temporal profile (install→activation worse for apartments; failed→second visit delay under high utilization; billing→complaint long tail) is almost absent.
- DFG edge shifts are unused beyond vague “skipped steps.”
- Social/resource findings (survey idle 78%→52%, second-visit 66%→92%, working-together centralization, stall on coordinator absence) are reduced to one utilization line.
- Generalization (0.70→0.56) is ignored; fitness/precision get a one-liner.

Section 3’s conflict explanations are slogans, not mechanism stories grounded in those artifacts.

**4. Weak discrimination among A–E**  
C and E are not cleanly separated: the memo’s hybrid already suspends skip + post-billing activation, which is largely C + segment gating. Conditions for A/B/D are boilerplate. No serious treatment of measurement bias (pilot harder mix; incomplete segment/risk data; incomplete complaint cost) as a reason D or stronger instrumentation might dominate pure E.

**5. VoI is ranked, not decision-valued**  
M1–M8 get High/Medium labels without:

- what decision flips if the result goes each way,
- dependence (M1 vs M6 overlap),
- why M2 (start timestamps) is not higher given sparse starts and worse install→activation tails,
- why M5 is only Medium when the memo itself stresses resilience/absence risk,
- ranking order beyond a priority list.

**6. Final recommendation lacks operational thresholds**  
“If M1, M6, and M4 confirm controllable risk and cost, scale gradually” is not a gate. Missing: numeric bars (e.g., FTR, complaint rate, p90, full cost vs baseline by segment; hard stop on billing-before-activation rate; permit conformance for old buildings). “What to pause / scale / measure” is directionally right but vague on who keeps coordinator model, how simple-house eligibility is defined given incomplete attributes, and what happens in non-pilot regions meantime.

**7. Smaller quality hits**  
- “p90 … reduced … (31 to 38.5)” error.
- Executive memo lean on incomplete data coverage and selection bias—central to whether pilot metrics are even comparable.
- Does not engage estimated full cost EUR 198→207 as provisional.
- NPS, SLA mix (better simple houses / worse overall), manual activation 7%→15%, business-customer billing disputes underused.
- Wordy structure with thin analysis; decision-oriented claim is only partly earned.
