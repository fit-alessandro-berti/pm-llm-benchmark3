### Critical structural failures
The required output is a complete decision memo in six exact top-level sections. What was submitted is a truncated draft: section 1 is incomplete as a memo body, sections 2–6 are present but thin, and the whole piece reads like an outline expanded once rather than a finished executive product. That alone caps the score hard.

### Executive memo (weak)
- Decision orientation is present (hybrid, not A/B), which is directionally right.
- Trade-off is stated, but the memo is short, choppy, and not written as a management memo (no clear ask, owners, timeline, or risk framing).

### Evidence table (serious flaws)
- Separation into facts / interpretations / assumptions is attempted, but several “facts” rows smuggle causal language in the interpretation column without labeling uncertainty cleanly enough for the judge rubric.
- “Pilot model fitness 0.94, precision 0.51” is a fact; treating “allows many undesired variants” as settled interpretation without noting discovery bias/log quality is sloppy.
- Sparse start timestamps, incomplete segment attributes, incomplete cost after week 4, and pilot vs baseline case-mix differences are mentioned but not systematically carried as assumptions across rows.
- At least eight items are present—meets minimum count, not quality bar.
