## Section-by-section

### 1. Model scope and state variables — weak
Boundary and entity lists are generic. State is underspecified for a DES twin: no explicit clock/event calendar, no token/marking or activity-instance state, no resource seize/release semantics, no queue attributes beyond “FIFO or priority,” and no formal representation of related-case graphs (architect bundles, geo batches, objection clusters). Cross-case dependencies are named, not modeled as state transitions with measurable effects (bundle wait vs travel savings, objection latency, council blackouts). Applicant/third-party entities are “partially exogenous” without a clear interface to municipal resources.

### 2. Parameter-estimation plan — inaccurate / non-grounded
Invented or nonstandard PM4Py APIs are presented as fact: `split_log_to_periods`, `calculate_service_time`, `calculate_waiting_time`. Fourier seasonality is decorative without a log-derived arrival process (e.g., piecewise NHPP by month × weekday × case type). The plan does not rigorously handle single completion timestamps vs start/complete splits, calendar-aware sojourn decomposition, or censoring. Rework from unstructured text is asserted without a usable coding/proxy scheme. Dependency estimation (top architects, objection bursts from public-notice length) is plausible but incomplete (no joint arrival process, no district travel model, no missingness model for `requires_*` flags).

### 3. Assumptions — partial, some unjustified
Separation by topic is good. Failures: rework independence is strong and unmotivated; “seasonal multipliers accurate to ±5%” is false precision; HR calendars “accurate to daily granularity” ignores known reconstruction bias; objections “inferred solely from duration deviations” is noted but not treated as latent/missing (no mixture or sensitivity prior). Missing start times are “extrapolated using inferred queue delays” without stating that waiting/service are not separately identified—this skirts the constraint against collapsing them without limitation.

### 4. Experiment matrix — hard fail
Required: baseline, D1–D10 where appropriate, combined policies, **≥3 stress scenarios**, factors/levels/trade-offs. Delivered: baseline + D1–D5 + **one** stress row. **D6–D9 absent; D10 combined policy absent;** multi-factor levels missing; trade-offs are slogans (“queue reduction”) not operational contrasts (SLA by type, inspector OT, fairness, rework rate). This alone blocks a high score.

### 5. Warm-up, replications, uncertainty — thin and weak
One-year warm-up / three-year run / 25 reps is arbitrary with no steady-state rule (Welch, MSER), no initialization bias check, no paired common-random-numbers design across policies, and no explicit CI/CrI procedure. Heavy tails get “Beta-PERT or bootstrap” without truncation, mixture tails, or rare-event handling for 260d complex cases. Seeds “fixed” without CRN/stream structure. Sensitivity is one sentence.

### 6. Validation — incomplete
Face, replay, KS, bottleneck, OOS are named but shallow. **Resource-utilization validation** is not a first-class method. Log replay vs generative DES is not distinguished (replay does not validate intervention counterfactuals). No calibration targets (variant mix, p50/p90 by type, queue time series, calendar blackouts). No protocol for known biases (missing objections, flattened multi-case deps, dropped emergencies).

### 7. Decision criteria — insufficient for selection
Metrics are listed without thresholds/ranges by case type, district fairness definitions, backlog units, robustness criteria under stress, or a multi-criteria rule (e.g., constrain p90 Simple ≤ X, minimize Commercial p95 subject to util ≤ 85%).
