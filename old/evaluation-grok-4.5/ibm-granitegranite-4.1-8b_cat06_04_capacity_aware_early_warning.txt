## Evaluation

The answer follows the required six-section structure, correctly excludes I-03 on stage grounds, differentiates I-05 (suppress, no new event) from I-07 (allow, new log), reserves a slot, runs three scenarios, and surfaces fairness/feedback and monitoring topics. These are necessary but far from sufficient. Under strict grading the submission fails decisively on decision-theoretic coherence, operational correctness, and elementary accuracy. Major defects are listed below; any one of them would already cap the score well below 7; together they produce a low grade.

### 1. Decision rule — fundamentally misspecified
- The rule is a hard threshold on a lightly adjusted \(p\) (\(\tau=0.55\)) followed by the score \(S=(p_{\rm adj}-\tau)\times V\). This is exactly the single-probability-threshold approach the prompt forbids. Estimated absolute risk reduction (the supplied ranges), false-alarm burden, swarm cost, time-to-deadline, and spillover externalities never enter the ranking. Effect heterogeneity is ignored at the moment of choice.
- Calibration patches are crude and inconsistently motivated (+0.04 for SME/NNL, –0.05 for SE). The prompt gives ranges (0.04–0.09 under, 0.05–0.12 over); the answer takes the most optimistic edge and applies the same +0.04 to the explicitly high-uncertainty NNL segment.
- The “priority score” has no decision-theoretic justification, produces negative values that are then treated as hard exclusions, and cannot trade a low \(p\) / high ARR / high \(V\) case against a high \(p\) / low \(V\) case.
- Alert-persistence downgrade (–0.1) is applied arbitrarily (I-02 yes, I-09 no, I-07 “maybe”).

### 2. Capacity allocation — arithmetic errors, inverted ranks, indefensible portfolio
- Explicit calculation mistakes: I-04 \(S\) is written “–13.80” (actual –7.2); tiers are labelled opposite to the computed scores (I-01 \(S=0.54\) called “Tier 1 highest”, I-09 \(S=4.60\) called “Tier 3”).
- Resulting portfolio {I-01, I-07, I-09} + reserve is poor. Rough base-case direct net values (mid ARR \(\times V\) – cost – burden) are approximately:
  - I-04  +9 k€, I-09  +3.5 k€, I-02  +2.7 k€, I-06  +2.6 k€, I-08  +4.5 k€ (borderline), I-01  +1.7 k€, I-07  +0.8 k€.
  Selecting the low-value I-07 while discarding I-02 and I-04 violates any coherent expected-value or robust ranking once ARR, burden and value are acknowledged. The answer never computes these quantities for selection.
- I-06’s imputation flag and I-08’s extreme burden/low ARR are noted only after the fact; they do not affect the rule.
- Table is cluttered with internal contradictions (“Eligible now? No” yet “Wait”, inconsistent downgrade flags, etc.).

### 3. Expected-impact table — incomplete arithmetic, missing terms, weak adversity
- False-alarm burdens (€250 + €50 + €800) are omitted from every net-impact calculation even though the prompt’s own illustrative formula includes them.
- Spillover harm uses an arbitrary “average \(V=20\) k€”; no attempt is made to identify the actual diagnosis-stage cases that would be hurt or to bound their values.
- Adverse case still assigns every selected case a strictly positive ARR; the prompt requires that “at least one selected case receives little or no benefit”.
- Units and rounding are sloppy (€k vs raw €, “1.35 k” written as 1350, etc.). The table is therefore not auditable to the standard demanded.

### 4. Repeated-alert policy
Acceptable distinction between I-05 and I-07 and a basic cool-down, but the logic is not integrated back into the scoring rule used in Section 2, and the 30-minute window is arbitrary relative to the model’s refresh cycle.

### 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks
The discussion correctly names the main risks (value starvation, NNL uncertainty, calibration asymmetry, label bias, spillover incidence). Concrete safeguards (segment quotas, hold-out, dampened learning rate, monthly audit) are proposed. However the quotas are not applied to the portfolio actually chosen, and the section does not quantify how large the induced disparities would be under the recommended rule. Still the strongest section.

### 6. Monitoring and recalibration
Lists the right categories (operational, predictive, causal, capacity, fairness). Several proposed estimators are weak or circular (alert-persistence as IV for the swarm effect; pinball on a policy-distorted sample). Trigger thresholds and the versioned policy-log idea are reasonable but generic. No link is made to the specific calibration offsets or ARR ranges used earlier.

### Global weaknesses
- Uncertainty is acknowledged in prose yet collapsed to point adjustments and mid-point arithmetic; no robust / minimax / CVaR-style selection appears.
- Queue externalities and specialist opportunity cost are mentioned only in the impact table, never inside the decision rule that consumes the four slots.
- Non-monetary obligations (regulatory reserve, customer disruption) are treated as afterthoughts.
- Writing contains numerous typos, inconsistent notation, and self-contradictory statements that further erode operational credibility.

Taken together, the answer demonstrates awareness of the required topics but fails the core test of decision-theoretic coherence and contains multiple concrete errors that would make the policy unusable or actively harmful if deployed. Hence a score of 4.0.
