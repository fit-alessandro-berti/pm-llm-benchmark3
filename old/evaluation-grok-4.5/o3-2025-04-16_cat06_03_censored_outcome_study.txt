## Overall verdict
The answer correctly refuses reject-as-non-default labeling, conditions on activation, and sketches temporal validation and a leakage table. Under strict grading that is not enough. Cohort arithmetic is internally inconsistent and does not reconcile to 48,000; prediction-time semantics conflict with the business ask and with the code; maturity cutoffs are wrong or underspecified; several PM4Py/API usages are incorrect or unsafe; and multiple design claims are vague, contradictory, or statistically incomplete. The piece reads like a plausible outline that would fail a design review.

## Section-by-section critique

### 1. Target definition — partial credit, material flaws
**Strengths:** Activation-conditioned estimand is a defensible primary choice; reject-inference is correctly demoted; multistate alternative is named.

**Failures:**
- **Prediction time is poorly aligned with the business question.** The lender asked for warning “from the first few events of every credit application.” The answer predicts only after the first Credit Review exit, and only for cases that later activate. That is late relative to “first few events,” and the unit is activated accounts, not “every application.” The mismatch is acknowledged only weakly via the estimand, not as a deployment-boundary issue.
- **Population dates are sloppy.** “Started between 1-Jan-2024 and 15-Feb-2026” includes starts that cannot mature; later cohort rules contradict this.
- **Estimand wording is incomplete.** It conditions on activation and first-pass review, but activation is future relative to prediction time. The honest estimand is something like \(P(D_{90}\mid A=1, X_{\text{prefix}})\), with activation as a selection event after the prefix—not information “at decision time” in a causal sense. Calling it “policy environment in force at decision time” blurs prediction vs policy evaluation.
- **Horizon anchoring is correct in prose (90d after activation)** but later code/cohort cutoffs do not consistently implement label maturity relative to activation + 90 + feed delay.

### 2. Cohort construction — major failure (this alone tanks the score)
**Required:** explicit inclusion/exclusion/as-of rules and a cohort-flow table accounting for **all 48,000**.

**Failures:**
- **Arithmetic does not close.**  
  - Step 2 removes 2,700 → 45,300.  
  - Step 3 claims 25,200 ever activated.  
  - 21,600 + 3,600 = 25,200 labeled/delayed activations — OK internally.  
  - Non-activated “19,” with ellipsis and “minerals” (corruption/typo) — not a number.  
  - Rejected 9,100 + withdrawn 5,700 + transferred 2,900 + still open 1,600 = **19,300**, not 19,000 and not reconciled to 45,300 − 25,200 = 20,100.  
  - 25,200 + 19,300 = 44,500 ≠ 45,300; gap of **800** unexplained.  
  - Original omitted open cases were **5,100**; after a start-date maturity filter one cannot simply assert still-open = 1,600 without showing the flow (open among early starts vs late starts, vs activations removed, etc.).
- **Step 2 maturity filter is wrong for the chosen target.** Maturity depends on **activation time + 90d + feed delay**, not on application start after 15-Nov-2025. An application started in June 2025 and activated in December 2025 is immature; one started in November 2025 and activated the same week may still be immature—but start-date truncation is neither necessary nor sufficient. Removing “starts after 15-Nov-2025” is a crude proxy that misclassifies cases.
- **“activation 23-Oct-2025”** is incomplete/broken text; the intended cutoff is roughly 15-Feb-2026 − 90 − 35 = 12-Nov-2025 (or 23-Oct if using a different delay assumption inconsistently). **23-Oct vs 15-Nov vs code’s `CUT_OFF - 90 - 35`** are not aligned.
- **Transferred / partner missing feed** are not broken out in the 21,600. The labeled export size is taken as gospel without auditing permanently missing feeds inside it.
- **Selectively unobserved** vs **competing** vs **not yet mature** are named in places but the table does not cleanly assign every row to exactly one disposition summing to 48,000.

This section fails the explicit “account for all 48,000” bar.

### 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy — superficial, partly wrong
**Strengths:** Lists several mechanisms; does not call rejects non-default; mentions sensitivity extremes and IPCW.

**Failures:**
- **Conflates mechanisms.** “Ordinary right censoring – activations after 23-Oct-2025” is delayed label maturity / administrative censoring of the outcome after activation, not classical survival censoring of time-to-default under a hazard model. For a **binary 90-day default among mature activations**, immature activations are **excluded or held out**, not right-censored binary labels in the primary analysis as described.
- **Primary analysis abandons process/survival structure** (“binary classifier on 21,600”) while the problem emphasized censoring-aware design. That can be valid, but then IPCW metrics in §7 hang on a thin story: after dropping missing-feed accounts, what is left to weight?
- **“~2% of partner accounts” MNAR** is invented; the prompt said elevated probability of permanent missing feed, not 2%.
- **Transfer handling is hand-wavy.** Competing event at application level vs missing follow-up **after** activation are different; linkage for 60% is not operationalized (when is transfer relative to activation? can default occur post-transfer?).
- **Testability claims are thin.** MAR for feed arrival is not “partially testable” in a serious way without holdout outcome mechanisms or external audits; stating it does not make it so.
- **No clear primary strategy for competing risks** if the multistate path is only alternative—OK—but then selection-through-activation bias for deployment on pre-activation prefixes is under-discussed (see §8).

### 4. Prefix extraction — contradictory and leaky design
**Failures:**
- **Internal contradiction:** prediction time is “first Credit Review exit,” but fixed-length is “first k=5 events truncated at first Credit Review exit if earlier.” So prefixes may end **before** the review exit or **at** it; features may not include the review outcome activity, or may include Accept/Reject. Including Accept/Reject at prediction time is a **different task** (post-decision monitoring) and can leak the decision; the leakage table drops `decision_reason`/`final_limit` but not the Accept activity itself.
- **“Pad with empty activity tokens”** for short cases is a modeling hack, not a process-aware as-of rule; short rejects that never hit 5 events are outside the primary population anyway—unaddressed.
- **Time-elapsed prefix (2 days) vs Credit-Review stop rule** are both proposed without a single coherent prediction-time definition for training rows (one row per case? multiple prefixes?).
- **Document loops:** stopping at the *first* Credit Review exit means the model never sees rework loops that occur when the first exit is “Request Docs.” That may be intentional for “early” warning, but it conflicts with “first few events” and with features that count Request-Docs loops (those loops often occur *after* first review exit).
- **Activity rename table** is toy-sized relative to “several activities” renamed; duplicate drop by (case, activity, timestamp) can drop legitimate same-timestamp distinct events or keep near-duplicates with different resources.

### 5. Process-aware features — mixed; some leakage/as-of errors
**Strengths:** Categories cover control-flow, timing, rework, policy calendar.

**Failures:**
- **`officer_experience_at_event (pre-collected snapshot)`** contradicts the known risk that experience is at extraction time; §6 repairs it, §5 pretends it’s already correct.
- **Rolling 30-day default rate “before prefix time”** still needs label maturity of those reference cases; “forward-looking guard” comment is not a design. Using defaults from recently activated accounts can leak future information into features if maturity is ignored.
- **Initial risk_score** may not be the score at case start; prompt says risk_score may update at several events—needs first-as-of rule (partially in §6 only).
- **Queue features** are essentially absent (no waiting time, backlog, same-resource load at as-of time).
- Features referencing Accept path are undefined given stop-at-first-review-exit.

### 6. Leakage audit — acceptable skeleton, not strict enough
**Strengths:** Table format correct; drops final_limit, decision_reason, collection_status; flags label_available_at.

**Failures:**
- **`documents_total` “EoD-1 cutoff”** is not clearly valid: prompt says nightly snapshot may include uploads later the same calendar day than nominal prediction time. End-of-previous-day can be safe; “00:00 of pred-day” as written is ambiguous and possibly still wrong depending on snapshot semantics.
- **Does not audit Accept/Reject/Contract Signed/Account Activated activities, default_90d on event rows, source_system quirks, or partner flags.**
- **Resource experience repair** `subtract (extract − event).years` is a crude fiction (promotions, hire date, part-time, etc.) presented as adequate.
- **Keep/repair on risk_score** good idea; no mention of not using last observation carried forward past prefix.
- No audit of **outcome columns repeated on events** entering `get_prefixes` / feature tables (code later takes max default at case level from full groupby—dangerous if merged carelessly).

### 7. Validation design — conceptually right direction, practically broken
**Strengths:** Rejects random split; mentions policy and migration; wants calibration and subgroups.

**Failures:**
- **Test window 15-Oct-2025 – 23-Oct-2025** is ~8 days of starts—statistically fragile for defaults (~few dozen defaults if rates hold), especially post-migration. Not a serious backtest design.
- **Train only pre-policy through 30-Jun-2025** means the model is never trained on post-policy data while val is post-policy—good for shift stress, bad as the only scheme; no rolling/expanding walk-forward, no nested maturity-aware backtest.
- **Val ends 14-Oct-2025** but labels for late val starts may not be mature by extract date depending on activation lag—**label maturity by split is not enforced** (cases in val/test must have mature labels; split is by start time only).
- **IPCW + AUROC** mentioned without defining who is censored in the scored set after excluding missing feeds.
- **`pm4py.split_train_test`** appropriately de-emphasized, but temporal split in code uses different end bounds than §7.
- No precision-recall / cost-weighted metrics despite class imbalance (~12.5% default); no time-to-default metrics despite problem offering that formulation.

### 8. Limitations — thin but directionally OK
Mentions conditional-on-activation, selection, feedback loops. Missing: identification gap for pre-activation scoring of applications that will be rejected; calibration under policy change; transfer linkage MNAR; delayed feed vs true default timing; that early interventions change labels. Three deployment DGP changes are present but generic. Acceptable section, not strong.

### 9. Code skeleton — does not meet “concrete enough to implement” quality bar
**Fatal / serious issues:**
- **`case_tbl` activation aggregation is broken.** Using `g.agg(..., lambda x: x[g.get_group(x.name)...])` inside agg on the timestamp series is not reliable pandas; `x.name` in that lambda is not a clean case-id API; this would not robustly compute activation times.
- **`pm4py.format_dataframe`** return value is discarded; API typically returns the formatted dataframe—subsequent code may not have standard column guarantees.
- **`pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(df=analysis_log, ...)`** — wrong mental model: prefixes API is log-oriented; passing a clipped dataframe with kwargs as written is not clearly valid PM4Py usage. Even if adapted, **clipping to first Credit Review then taking prefix_length=5** double-processes inconsistently.
- **`clip_to_prediction`**: `str.contains("Credit Review")` then `.index[0]` — first match may be wrong if multiple reviews; uses index[0] on filtered index not positional first in time after sort (sort is done, but `.index[0]` is label-based first index, not necessarily earliest row after filter). Bug-prone.
- **Includes Credit Review event in prefix** → decision activity leakage risk not checked in leakage audit (`leaks_present` only checks three columns still in the frame—**dropping columns is never done**, only raises if present; after clip, final_limit etc. may still be columns filled at/after decision).
- **No removal of post-prefix column values**; leakage check is column-name presence, not as-of sanitization.
- **Temporal test mask:** `between(MIGRATION, CUT_OFF - DEFAULT_HORIZON - MAX_FEED_DELAY)` uses start-time upper bound ~2025-11-12, not §7’s 23-Oct-2025; still not activation-based maturity.
- **Never builds competing-outcome statuses** (rejected/withdrawn/transferred/open) despite §2; cohort print is only three buckets—**fails to reproduce the required cohort-flow counts**.
- **`default_90d = g["default_90d"].max()`** on all events is a brittle label join and can mis-handle boolean/NaN.
- **DFG/variants** computed but unused for features; no justified link to the model.
- **Imports** `attribute_filter`, `dataframe_utils` partially unused; comments mention lifelines but no censor indicators exported.
- Line count is in range; coherence is not.

## Cross-cutting statistical design problems
1. **Selection vs prediction time:** Scoring at first review using a model trained only on eventually activated accounts yields scores for cases that will be rejected/withdrawn with undefined primary outcome—no reject handling at scoring time, no probability-of-activation head, no explicit “score only if accept” policy rule.
2. **Business ask vs estimand:** “Every credit application” vs activated-only mature labels—handled philosophically in §1, abandoned operationally.
3. **Policy change:** Indicator feature does not replace stratified models, transportability analysis, or post-change recalibration plan.
4. **Honesty:** Several numbers (2% MNAR, 23-Oct, 2,700 start removals, 1,600 open) are asserted without derivation from the given 48,000 breakdown.

## What would be needed for a high score (not present)
- Cohort flow with identities: 48,000 = mature activated labeled + immature activated + rejected + withdrawn + transferred (±linkage) + open + any residual, with **activation-time** maturity rules.  
- Single consistent prediction time and prefix rule; no decision/post-activation attributes/activities.  
- Primary analysis matching the estimand (binary on mature activated **or** survival/competing risks with explicit censor codes)—not a mashup.  
- Walk-forward validation with **label-mature** cohorts and adequate test mass after migration/policy.  
- Runnable as-of feature pipeline with real leakage sanitization and cohort audit outputs.

Credit for not treating rejects as non-default, choosing an activation-conditional primary target, listing leakage fields, and preferring temporal splits. Heavy penalties for **broken cohort accounting**, **wrong maturity logic**, **prediction-time/prefix contradictions**, **weak/infeasible validation window**, and a **non-working, leakage-unsafe code skeleton**. Under hypercritical standards this is clearly below mid-quality professional design.
