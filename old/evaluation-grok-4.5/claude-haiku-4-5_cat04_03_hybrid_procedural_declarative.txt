# Major flaws (decisive)

**ProcessTree optionality is wrong (Section 3).**  
LOOP is used for clarification, sanctions/legal, and post-activation bank-change behaviour. In PM4Py/process-tree semantics the “do” child of a LOOP executes at least once. Consequently:
- every case is forced through at least one Request Clarification  Receive Clarification cycle,
- every high-risk case is forced through Sanctions Escalation + Obtain Legal Opinion,
- every accepted case is forced through Change Bank Details  Suspend  re-Verify.

T1, T3 and any happy-path case without clarifications or bank changes are rejected by the procedural model as written. Optional behaviour must be encoded with XOR(activity, ), not LOOP. The claim that the tree is sound and accepts the reference traces is false. The earlier informal Petri-net sketch does not rescue the executable code assigned to `procedural_model`.

**DECLARE precedence semantics are inverted for R5 (Section 4).**  
```python
"precedence": {("Obtain Legal Opinion", "Activate Vendor"): ...}
```
precedence(A, B) is violated whenever B occurs with no prior A. Low-risk and high-risk cases that never escalate (T1, T2, T4, \ldots) therefore fail DECLARE. The accompanying text claims the rule is “vacuous if Obtain Legal Opinion does not exist”; that confuses precedence with responded_existence / succession. The same unconditional pair appears in the Log Skeleton `always_before` set, producing the identical false negative.
