## Deductions (hypercritical)

**1. Material analytical miss on language support uptake (Section 1.6, echoed in 8)**  
The claim that 57% of Group B “needs language support” but only 24% traverse V4, so the rest may have received no language service, ignores the resource narrative: Group B is disproportionately assigned to 7 bilingual caseworkers. Embedded bilingual ownership can satisfy language need *without* a distinct “Language Support Contact” activity. Top-6 variants already cover ~99% of B, and only V4 contains that activity—so the 24% vs 57% gap is real in the activity log, but the unmet-need inference is overstated. The same pattern applies to C (62% accommodation requested vs 31% on V5). This is a substantive interpretive error, not a nit.

**2. Conceptual blur on what the “adjusted” cycle-time gaps mean**  
The preliminary model does *not* control for `language_preference` or `disability_accommodation_requested`. Residuals of +2.6d (B) and +4.1d (C) are therefore expected even under a fair process whenever support steps take real time. The fairness question is whether those steps are under-capacitated and unmonitored—not whether a residual remains after an adjustment set that omitted the primary legitimate drivers. Section 3 mostly gets this right (“existence legitimate; duration is a capacity decision”), but Sections 1.4 and 8 repeatedly frame the adjusted cycle-time gaps as disparities “the complexity controls do not explain,” which overstates the residual-as-unfairness case. The sentence that longer delays for rights-preserving paths are “the opposite of what a well-designed system should produce” is an overreach: longer service for real extra work is compatible with good design; unmonitored rationing is not.

**3. Occasional over-attribution**  
- “Largely traceable” / “substantially explains” Group C’s +4.1d via the 4.7d accommodation wait is directionally right but loosely quantified (only a subset of C takes that edge; the +4.1d is an average residual under a partial adjustment).  
- “Most mechanically plausible cause” for timeliness gaps (server-pool concentration) is plausible and well-argued, but stated more firmly than the observational data alone can lock down without the utilization/within-resource tests the answer itself proposes.
