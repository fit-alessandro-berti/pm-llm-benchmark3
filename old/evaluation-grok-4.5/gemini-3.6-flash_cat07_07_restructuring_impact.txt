### Major issues (score-reducing)

**1. Pilot design is organizationally incoherent (serious)**  
Randomizing 50% of North cases into a “shared-service” treatment while South keeps embedded specialists confuses *case-level routing* with *people/structure centralization*. You cannot half-centralize `N_Tech` or run a real Technical Shared Service / shared approval queue as an org form on a random half of one team without contaminating control, capacity, and role clarity. A credible pilot would phase by team/queue (e.g., East-first offload, or shared queue with retained local triage liaisons), not a pseudo-RCT on cases. This is a core failure for a question about structure  process-mining observables.

**2. PM4Py sketch is partly incorrect / incomplete (moderate–serious)**  
Allowed as sketch/pseudocode, but still judged on whether it would support the claimed analyses:
- Likely wrong waiting-time field (`waiting_time` vs PM4Py’s approximate sojourn/waiting column names from `insert_case_service_waiting_time`).
- `len(hw_net_pre)` on handover network objects is not a reliable API use.
- `filter_variants(...)` usage is broken (variant key types, filter API, then treating result like a clean case DF).
- `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` is never called despite being required in the prompt’s analysis list (“resource similarity and role discovery”).
- Assumes a non-existent `phase` column without a real split strategy on the given schema.
- Unused imports; rework logic mixes `get_rework_cases_per_activity` with a simplistic `rework_flag` rate on events rather than cases.
These are not polish nits; they weaken section 7 as an evaluation blueprint.

**3. Mechanism map is thinner than it appears (moderate)**  
The ASCII “map” is mostly five intervention rows plus prose. Required themes are *mentioned*, but several links are under-specified:
- Resilience to absence is asserted, not tied to measurable log patterns (e.g., performance conditional on resource absence, cross-cover handovers, `local_expertise_flag`).
- Learning/skill atrophy is mostly deferred to section 4 rather than mapped as a first-class structure  variant/rework/role-similarity mechanism.
- “Nominal handover reduction” vs replacement by asynchronous queue waits is discussed, but still partly accepts management’s handover story without a crisp observable contrast (active R2R handovers down, case-level waits and reassignment up).

**4. Indicator table: invented or weak operationalizations (moderate)**  
- “Silhouette score” from `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` is not a standard PM4Py output; this overclaims precision.
- CSAT disparity is largely external to the given log (team-level historic CSAT only); fine as lagging business metric, weak as “from event logs.”
- No clear leading indicator for resilience/absence cover or for fairness by `customer_segment` with the same rigor as team SLA gaps.
- Subcontracting “density ±10%” is vague without a defined baseline construction.

**5. Scenario numbers are over-precise and occasionally slippery (minor–moderate)**  
Optimistic/central/adverse read well qualitatively, but many figures are presented as forecasts without stated assumptions (arrival mix, pool size, priority discipline). Small slips (e.g., adverse early-warning “Wavelength of queue growth”; occasional blending of median vs p90 narrative) reduce trust. Adverse East SLA collapse is plausible under queue failure but should be tied more explicitly to priority rules and shared Legal/Risk saturation.

**6. Fairness and exception handling: present but uneven (minor–moderate)**  
Team fairness is repeated; `customer_segment` / language-regional accommodation is often name-checked rather than operationalized (filters, segment-specific p90, rework, handovers). Exception paths (cover, security specialty, unclear ownership after shared approval) are weaker than the ping-pong story.

**7. Minor consistency / clarity defects**  
- Management’s +15% utilization target is critiqued well late, but early mechanism text sometimes treats utilization “leveling” as a clean observable win without immediate tradeoff flags.
- Role clarity is stronger in prose than in PM observables (handover_reason, reassignment, role discovery change) until later sections.
- Slightly promotional diagram density without adding identification logic.
