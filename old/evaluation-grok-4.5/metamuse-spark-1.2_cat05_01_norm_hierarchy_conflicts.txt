## Critical substantive errors

### 1. P-501 — wrong threshold and inflated breach theory
- Amount is EUR 38,000, approved supplier. GPIP-4 A2 (≥50k) and A3 (>100k / new supplier) do **not** apply. The answer correctly notes that in the table, then still frames the case partly through GPIP integrity language and marks Finance delay as a core noncompliance story.
- SOP B2 requires Plant_Approve and Finance_Approve at ≥25k, but the SOP does **not** clearly require those approvals *before* Commit. The catalogue invents that temporal relation (“presumptive per purpose; SOP text does not state before/after, but A6 + B5 imply before”). That is an interpretive leap presented as rule content. A6 is about missing *pre-commitment* approvals where the norm requires them; it does not invent a before-commit duty for B2.
- Valid defects remain: Commit before ECD-2; SHIFT-1 without active delegation while Plant was reachable; possible B2 sequencing issues depending on interpretation. Severity “Low” and NONCOMPLIANT can be defended, but the reasoning path is sloppy.

### 2. P-502 — overconfident NONCOMPLIANT; Finance treated inconsistently
- New supplier + ECD window: A3 Compliance before commit is the cleanest hard breach. That part is sound.
- Finance at 13:05 after commit 10:15: the answer waves this away via CFO D1 while treating Compliance as fatal. That split is argued, but D1 is a non-controlled email whose authority over GPIP-4 A2 is expressly uncertain (D3/D4). Using D1 to neutralize A2 for P-502/P-507/P-508 while denying analogous overlays elsewhere is selective.

### 3. R-C3 invents a SoD constraint ECD does not state
Catalogue says joint IC auth requires “2 distinct IC members != committer/requester (for SoD).” ECD-2 C3 requires two distinct IC members; it does **not** say the committer cannot be one of them. SoD is GPIP A1 (requester/committer may not be *final business approver*). Collapsing “IC authorizer” into “final business approver” is a major unargued premise. It drives P-503’s SoD theory and is smuggled into the formal rules as if textual.

### 4. P-503 — strong result, partly on invented grounds

## Structural and methodological problems

**Hierarchy vs applications.** Section 2 correctly refuses a single total order and protects A1/A3. Section 4 then repeatedly lets CFO email and ECD “ordinary workflow” eat A2/B2 for the large emergency cases. The hierarchy is not operationalized consistently; the case table runs on a more permissive shadow hierarchy.

**JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION inflation.** Used for P-505, P-507, P-508 as a compromise bucket when the honest labels under the stated assumptions are COMPLIANT, NONCOMPLIANT, or INDETERMINATE. That blurs the required taxonomy and looks like score-splitting rather than classification discipline.

**INDETERMINATE underused.** P-505 (commit vs expiry), P-507/P-508 (status of D1 vs A2, budget-owner identity), and aspects of P-503 (scope of “critical”) are natural INDETERMINATE or dual-classification territory. The instructions explicitly allow uncertainty to be visible via that label. Avoiding it while dumping uncertainty into “JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION + low confidence” is a methodological miss.

**Formal catalogue defects.**
- R-B2 temporal “BEFORE Commit” is not in the SOP text.
- R-C3 actor constraint imports A1 without derivation.
- R-D1/R-D2 elevated to rule status nearly on par with board policy despite Section 1 calling the email low-medium weight.
- Pseudo-logic is decorative; it does not constrain the case work (e.g., `R-D1 overrides_for(finance...) unless R-A2 integrity still requires CRO A7 (uncertain)` is never resolved, yet cases resolve it in favor of override).

**Evidential discipline — uneven.**
- Good: P-502 phone/ack; P-507 PDF vs ERP; ON-44 principle stated.
- Bad: P-505 “may be 9 min fast” → explanatory fact; P-503 “no record within 4h” under-argued; burden of proof “against exception” applied harshly to cafeteria and post-expiry cases but lightly when D1 must carry six-figure Finance gaps.

**Section 5 alternatives.** Better than average: P-502 Compliance-as-onboarding, P-505 ERP-as-record, P-507/P-508 strict A2 are real reversal premises. Still, alternatives sometimes change *multiple* premises at once (P-503) or float A1 waiver via D1 in ways Section 1 already rejected.

**Section 6–7.** Remediation sensibly separates breach vs logging vs proof failure and refuses fake cures. Policy draft is coherent and includes anti-normalization. These sections are the strongest part of the submission—but they cannot rescue misclassified core cases. The recommended clause (Compliance hard gate; CFO Finance addendum repository-controlled; IC not budget owners) actually **indicts** the answer’s own JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION treatment of P-507/P-508.
