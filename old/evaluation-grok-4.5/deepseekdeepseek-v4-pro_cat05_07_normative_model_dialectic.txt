## Grading Rationale (hypercritical)

### Strengths (what keeps it from collapsing)
- **Dialectical structure is genuinely strong.** Prosecution and defense are both argued at high intensity; each side’s required concessions appear (prosecution admits model/data defects and that Request_More_Info is not misconduct; defense concedes SUP-703/SUP-708 and limits of informal practice). Not a bland split-the-difference.
- **Sections 1–5 are largely on-spec.** Stipulated vs. disputed facts, burden framing, evidence matrix columns, per-case labels from the allowed set, severity/confidence, and explicit “where I reject each side” are present and mostly traceable to N1–N5 and the case table.
- **Scope/authority handling is thoughtful** on PD-3/FD-7 expiry, intake vs. onboarding, reactivation, CRM mutability, and resource/COI rules living outside control-flow.
- **Section 6 layering** (POWL vs DECLARE/pandas vs IAM/org controls vs policy registry) is conceptually the right architecture.

### Major failures (why the score is far from high)

**1. Section 7 code is not executable and is semantically invalid (core deliverable fails)**  
- Uses `operator=OperatorPOWL.XOR` — invalid. Canonical API is `Operator.XOR` from `pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator` (as in M0). `Operator` is never imported. Runtime `AttributeError`.
- Builds a **cycle** on a `StrictPartialOrder` (`tau_info_loop_exit  tau_info_loop_entry`). SPO cannot have cycles; the graph is not a valid POWL partial order.
- **Nodes used in edges are missing from the parent node set** (e.g. `info_loop_choice`, `activation_merge`, and related structures are referenced in `order.add_edge` but omitted from `M1 = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[...])`). Invalid construction.
- XOR of four silent taus with real routes hanging off sibling edges **does not encode XOR branch semantics**. The operator children are not the actual routes; the decision construct is decorative.
- Knew `Operator.LOOP` was the right tool and still shipped a broken cycle “approximation” instead of implementing LOOP.

**2. Revised control-flow fails its own stated requirements (model semantics)**  
Required: concurrent sanctions/quality on the **standard** route; low-risk **auto** route; **emergency/provisional** route where activation may precede Quality_Review (and manual approval); Request_More_Info; reject; notify before close.

Actual M1 forces `checks_block = {Sanctions, Quality}` **before** the path XOR for every case. Therefore:
- Auto path cannot replay PD-3/SUP-702 (`… Auto_Approve, Activate, Quality_Review …`) — Quality is already required pre-decision.
- Emergency path cannot replay FD-7/SUP-704 (`… Sanctions, Activate, Quality, Manual …`) — Quality is already required pre-decision; the later “post-checks” only **duplicate** work, they do not defer the first Quality.
- Limitation text claims Layer 2 will “relax” timing; that does not fix the POWL accepting the observed emergency/pilot orders. The model still marks the authorized pilot/emergency traces non-conforming. This is not a defensible approximation; it is the wrong shape.

**3. Further model/data defects**  
- Duplicate/conflicting `Transition` identities (`emergency_sanctions` vs `sanctions`; `quality`/`manual_approval` reused across pre- and post-blocks; `activate_standard` appearing both inside routes and in a merge XOR) muddies concurrency and choice semantics.
- `Activate_Supplier` vs `Activate_Supplier_Provisional` split is fine for audit labeling, but merge/XOR wiring is incoherent.
- Silent transitions with labels; unused/half-used taus (`tau_provisional_merge`, `tau_activation_done`, etc.); assembly comments contradict the graph.
- “Executable Python code block” requirement is not met; SQL/strings as side proposals do not salvage the POWL.

**4. Analytical nits (secondary, but count under strict grading)**  
- Occasional overclaim or blur (e.g. bundling SUP-715 into PD-3 prosecution framing; “93% eligibility” rhetorical precision).
- SUP-705 as `NONCOMPLIANT (minor)` is defensible, but confidence/medium and “governance-process violation” sit slightly uncomfortably next to the matrix’s stronger defense reading without tighter proof standard application.
- Evidence matrix is comprehensive yet a few “what would reverse” cells are thin or circular.
- Resource/COI findings are correctly separated from M0, but final labels sometimes still read as if process-mining conformance and org-policy breach are the same finding without a crisp two-layer verdict line.

**5. Net assessment against the judge rubric**  
- Dialectical fairness: high  
- Evidence traceability: high–medium  
- Scope and authority: high  
- **Model semantics: low**  
- **Quality of model/data revision: low on the artifact that must run; medium on the prose architecture**
