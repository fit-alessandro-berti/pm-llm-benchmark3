### Defects that materially lower the score

1. **Classification compression / failure to produce any primary JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**  
   Every resolved case is NONCOMPLIANT except one INDETERMINATE. The instruction explicitly warns against treating all deviations as noncompliant. The author’s own premises (ECD-2 displaces PO-timing and supplier register; CFO D1 can cover Finance/operational approvals  €150k; C3 + contemporaneous evidence can validate) are sufficient to support at least one primary JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION (strongest candidates: P-507 if PDF authenticated, or P-508 under a slightly less rigid reading of D2 + D1). Keeping every case NONCOMPLIANT flattens discrimination and under-uses the exception machinery the answer itself builds. This is a methodological flaw, not mere conservatism.

2. **Over-rigid split of GPIP-4 A2 that is in tension with D1’s text**  
   D1 states joint IC authorization is “sufficient for all operational approvals up to EUR 150,000.” The answer repeatedly severs “Finance Controller” from “independent budget owner” and then treats the budget-owner element as almost never satisfied because the IC charter is silent. That reading is possible, but it is not the strongest reading of “all operational approvals,” especially when D4 only preserves “essential integrity controls” (more naturally A1 and A3). The result is a near-automatic A2 failure for every  €50k case, which drives the classification compression above. The alternatives section acknowledges softer readings but the primary line does not adequately defend why the stricter split is required.

   Amount is €24,000. B2 triggers at “at least EUR 25,000.” B2 therefore does not apply. The assessment hedges with “if B2 applies” and discusses missing Plant/Finance as if the threshold were uncertain. This is a clear, avoidable factual slip.

   Several rows list late or post-commitment acts (e.g., P-502 Finance at 13:05, various Plant/Finance after the fact) under “Satisfied rules,” then re-characterize them as deviations elsewhere.
