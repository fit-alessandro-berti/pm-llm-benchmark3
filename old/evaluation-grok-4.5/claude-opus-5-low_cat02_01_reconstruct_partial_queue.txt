## Defects (material)

### 1. Little’s Law application is internally inconsistent (serious)
Section 2 correctly states the finite-horizon area identity \(L=\lambda W\) with a **common** span. Section 5 then computes S2 as:
- \(L=1340/335=4.00\) (span 08:25–14:00),
- \(\lambda=20/320=0.0625\) (different span),
- \(W=67\), \(\lambda W=4.19\),

and attributes the 5% gap to a “boundary effect.” That gap is an artifact of mismatched denominators. With a single span, \(\lambda W=L\) holds exactly by construction of the area identity. Presenting a self-inflicted residual as empirical validation undermines the methodological care claimed in §2.

### 2. I1 busy-time / utilization arithmetic does not match stated method (serious)
Stated rule (§2): OPEN contributes elapsed time to in-window busy time.  
Reported narrative: I1 busy = 275 min (range 245–280), \(\rho\approx0.72\)–\(0.78\).

Reconciliation against the log:
- Fully observed I1 services sum to 215 min,
- central P07+P15  60  275,
- **P21 elapsed (13:30–14:00 = 30) is missing from the 275 figure.**

Including P21 yields 305 min central and a materially higher utilization. The write-up’s range upper bound (280) is also irreconcilable with max imputations plus P21. The Python busy-time loop would include P21; the narrative numbers would not — a reproducibility contradiction on a headline metric. Quality bar item “will not infer utilization from completed cases alone” / “will not discard open cases” is therefore violated in the reported figures even though the prose states the right rule.

### 3. Assumption A4 (universal +5 min changeover) is overstated
A4 is used for central imputations and effective-capacity claims. The log contains clear 0-gap handoffs (e.g., C1: P07 end 11:00  P09 start 11:00). Other gaps are +5, and some are larger. Treating +5 as a structural law, then folding it into \(\mu_S1\approx3.6/h\) and \(\mu_S2\approx2.7/h\), overstates precision. Rounding alone can manufacture apparent 0/5 gaps. Central imputations that sometimes apply +5 and sometimes 0 (P05 note) are inconsistent with the assumption as written.

### 4. Coverage and counting slip-ups
- S1 start coverage stated as “18/20”; actual observed S1 starts among started cases are 19/21 (P07, P15 missing; P21 has a start; P22 never started). Off-by-one.
- Aggregate text labels P18–P20 “fully observed unremarkable cases” while they are S2-queue-censored.
- These are small, but they erode trust in denser tables where independent verification is harder.

### 5. Imputation interval notes vs table values
P05: table gives wait [20, 50] (20) and service [0, 30] (30) — identity holds — but the note oscillates between central start 09:45 (0 changeover, service 30) and 09:50 (service 25) without committing. The same note calls short services “weakly supported” while the optimistic scenario still pushes service to 0. The optimistic column is therefore more extreme than the author’s own service-distribution argument allows, weakening the sensitivity envelope.

### 6. Secondary issues
- Effective-capacity math quietly treats changeover as hard capacity loss while busy-time utilization excludes changeover — legitimate as two views, but the dual definition is easy to misread and is not stress-tested when gaps are 0.
- WIP/Q1 construction notes that missing starts bias Q1 upward, yet §4 still reports point-ish peaks without always carrying that caveat into the headline numbers.
- Code fragility: bounds stored via `.time()` then re-parsed with `DAY + str(...)`; dead placeholder timestamp; `convert_log_to_time_intervals` behind bare `try/except`. Runnable intent is clear; polish is not.
- FIFO-inversion and P22 work-conservation hypotheses are good, but P22’s “most likely logging latency” is asserted a bit firmly given A9’s own caution about extraction artifacts.
- Rounding error model (duration error  [5, +5], s.d.  2.04) is fine for worst case; propagating it only selectively (and claiming aggregate s.d.  0.5 on means) is reasonable but not shown computationally.
