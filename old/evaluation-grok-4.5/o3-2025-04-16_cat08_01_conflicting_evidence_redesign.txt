### 2–4. Redesigns — good differentiation, inflated mechanisms

Sound target (HV wrong-part + R1/R2) and good use of under-used QC capacity and the 15–35% manual inflow caveat.  
Weaknesses: “eliminates **>90%** of R1/R2 violations” is unsupported; wording “Low-risk accessory OR Premium accessory” is redundant/confused; Premium HV **24h credit** exposure when forced onto ~96h reviewed paths is under-treated; mid-risk devices are a dump bucket.

**Redesign B (evidence-first)**  
Best segment fit to the 22% unclear/missing-evidence cohort and the 26% repeat-evidence stat; assisted channel correctly answers the digital-literacy risk.  
Weaknesses: “loop is **removed**” is absolute and unrealistic; “**removes** 34 h mean re-entrance delay” treats an edge mean as fully recoverable savings; leaves Fast-approval quality failure for complete-evidence HV cases largely untouched (acknowledged only via R1).

**Redesign C (repair-first)**  
Genuinely different logic; 14%+21%=35% HV share is correct; hold window is a concrete control idea.  
Weaknesses: “**removes** 22% complaint rate” confuses a segment-specific rate with elimination and ignores Standard HV 18%; 10% fraud sampling is arbitrary and weakly tied to R1 economics; casually “doubling” escalation board ignores stated poor scalability; repair-default vs Premium service-credit economics is noted but not designed around (loaners, parallel replace options, etc.).

All three state sacrifices — good. None is a Lean cliché list — good. Mechanism claims are repeatedly stronger than the data allow — costly under strict scoring.
