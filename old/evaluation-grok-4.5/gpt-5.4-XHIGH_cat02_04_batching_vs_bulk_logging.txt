### 4. PM4Py and pandas analysis — clear deduction
Conceptually the right experiment: embed data, build interval log, vary `merge_distance` / `min_batch_size`, compute independent overlap/equality/lag/precision/queue diagnostics, keep suspect rows.

**Material defects**
1. **Probable crash / wrong unpack of `discover_batches`.**  
   PM4Py’s `discover_batches` typically returns a **dict** keyed by `(activity, resource)` with inner batch-type maps (or a closely related structure)—not an iterable of `(key, _, details)` triples. The loop  
   `for (activity, resource), _, details in batches:`  
   is very likely wrong and would not produce the intended comparison table. This directly undermines a mandatory requirement.
2. Pre-code prose asserts definitive PM4Py category labels (“should label G1… as Simultaneous…”) as if observed; they are hypotheses. That blurs observation vs prediction.
3. Precision diagnostic (`epoch % doc_precision == 0`) is weak for 1-minute sources: almost all minute-aligned industrial stamps pass, so it barely discriminates G2/G3/G6.
4. `merge_distance=0` is an edge case; behavior is version-dependent and unvalidated.
5. No guard against empty/malformed batch results; comparison quality depends on a brittle parse.
6. Comments claim interpretation value, but the printed artifact is only one wide table—acceptable, not excellent.

The pandas side (overlap rate, same start/end maxima, adjacency chain, negative waits, zero service, suspect flags) is competent and does preserve G6/G7.
