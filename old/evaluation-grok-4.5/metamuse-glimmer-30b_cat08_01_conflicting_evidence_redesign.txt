## Critical failures (major score damage)

### 1. Broken required structure (hard requirement)
The prompt requires each redesign to use a clear substructure: flow, targets, mechanism, sacrifices. The answer uses bullets, but several “flow” descriptions are incomplete or internally inconsistent (especially A and C).

More damaging: **Section 5’s table is not a usable Pareto table**. Cells like `median for high-value, / for low-risk`, bare `nominal manual cost, true cost`, and empty-looking qualitative markers read as corrupted or placeholder text. A judge cannot recover coherent cycle-time/cost/quality signs for A–C. That alone should tank the score: the comparison section is a required deliverable, not optional polish.

`dominated?` is filled with vague `conditional` / `no` without stating *what dominates what, on which objectives, for whom*. That is not Pareto reasoning.

### 2. Invented or over-precise claims not in the prompt
Examples of overreach:
- “~5% reviewed/repair level” as if Redesign B will hit the observed repair-path complaint rate — treating a descriptive segment statistic as a guaranteed treatment effect.
- Implicit assumption that forcing Track 2 yields the 3.3%/5%/6% complaint rates without selection effects (repair-path cases are already different).
- “cuts repeated evidence requests by 30–45%” stated as mechanism success; the prompt only says a portal *could* reduce repeated requests by that range.
- Service-credit logic for Premium low-risk is used as if 24h is a hard SLA engineered into the process; the prompt says exposure is high if cycle time exceeds 24h, not that a control system exists.
- R1 is “claim above EUR 400 must have Manual **or** Fraud/risk before Fast approval.” Redesign A’s wording sometimes blurs this into broader routing folklore (“all other…”, “forced to Manual or Fraud”) without tying back to the EUR 400 threshold and without addressing that many auto-approved cases may already be under threshold.

### 3. Weak / wrong process-mining interpretation
- **Fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces** is mentioned but not interpreted. High fitness with low % fitting traces usually means many small deviations in many traces, or a few costly misalignments — the answer never uses that distinction.
- **Low precision** is quoted and then ignored operationally. A serious redesign should say what low precision implies (model allows too much behavior / policy is not enforced in discovery), and how the redesign raises precision (forbid illegal edges), not only “add a portal.”
- **Volume inconsistency risk**: DFG edges are multi-path and not a single funnel; the answer treats 2550 Fast approvals and 2300 dispatches like a clean main path without reconciling Fast approval → Quality/compliance close (350) and Fast approval → complaint (140), or the reopen re-entry loop (310 back to manual). Reopen loops mean “9.2% of cases” and edge counts are not interchangeable without care.
- **Performance DFG**: long complaint tails (72h / 96h means) are cited as quality problems, but redesigns barely target the *temporal* mechanism (wrong part selection long-tail after dispatch) except as a slogan (“quality gate”). No design chooses gate placement using p90 vs median, capacity, or Premium 24h exposure simultaneously with enough rigor.

### 4. Redesigns are not cleanly “materially different” in mechanism
A/B/C share the same kit: segment rules, evidence portal, pre-dispatch quality gate, high-value friction.  
- A = rules + portal + gate  
- B = hard split + repair-first (best differentiation)  
- C = evidence-first + gate + monitoring + escalation tweak  

B is genuinely architectural. A and C are **adjacent control-plane variants**, not three sharply different process designs. C’s “predictive reopen prevention” is almost content-free: no signals, no model inputs, no decision policy, no link to attributes (defect type, segment, replacement type).

### 5. Segment logic errors / compliance holes
- Redesign B sends “mid-risk devices and all high-value” to repair-first, but the prompt’s normative backbone is **R1 (value-triggered review)** and **R2 (evidence before dispatch except Premium low-risk accessory)** — not “repair-first for everything expensive.” Repair-first may improve quality for wrong-replacement economics (EUR 410) but is not the same as compliance. The answer conflates quality architecture with conformance architecture.
- Redesign A keeps Fast Lane for Premium low-risk accessories but also loads a 6–18h pre-dispatch gate onto “all other low-risk accessories,” which can destroy the only segment where speed clearly dominates (and can create service-credit risk if mis-applied to Premium). The sacrifice section does not own that cleanly.
- R3 (reopened claims must pass Manual eligibility before final close/denial) is mostly name-checked; none of the redesigns specify how close/denial paths after reopen are hard-gated in the workflow (beyond C’s vague “mandatory Manual eligibility”).

### 6. Resource math is hand-wavy where the prompt gave numbers
The prompt gives reviewer counts, productive hours, utilization cliff at 82%, fraud bench of 2, repair quote of 3, QC of 2, escalation scalability limits, and a warning that rule updates may raise manual inflow 15–35%.  
The answer repeats these numbers but never does even qualitative load reasoning (e.g., moving a chunk of the 2550 auto-fast cases into manual/fraud and what that does at the 82% cliff). “May overload” is generic given the data provided.

### 7. Contradiction handling is superficial
Section 1 states the fast-path contradiction adequately (this is the strongest part). Then the redesigns do not show a *decision policy* that preserves fast-path advantage where it is rational (Premium low-risk accessory; Standard low-risk with visibility) and removes it where true cost dominates (high-value wrong replacement; R1/R2 exposure).  
Instead, each redesign mostly “adds brakes,” which is the usual generic response to “fast but dirty.”

### 8. Experiment section is mediocre and partially mis-aimed
- First pilot on Standard low-risk accessory for A is defensible, but stop/go thresholds like “complaint/reopen falls 30% relative” are arbitrary and not tied to segment baselines (8% → ?), cost after rework, or compliance event rates.
- Sequencing jumps to B on Premium high-value without a clear safety monitor for service credits and compliance during the slowdown.
- “Learning real vs shifted downstream” mentions end-to-end close and reopen — good — but does not require **matched segment mix**, **policy-fit rate**, **true cost after credits/reships**, or **time-to-stable-close including reopen**, which is what this exact contradiction needs.
- No discussion of contamination from campaign-driven fraud peaks (prompt-listed), which can fake pilot failure.

### 9. Writing artifacts that read like generation errors
Phrases such as “Keeps speed for Premium low-risk accessory where service-credit exposure dominates” (exposure dominates *when slow*, not when fast), and table cells with stray `/` and empty signage, look like unresolved drafting. Under strict grading, that is not cosmetic: it makes the decision table non-actionable for an executive sponsor.

Under “utmost strictness,” it is far from near-flawless and does not deserve a high band.
