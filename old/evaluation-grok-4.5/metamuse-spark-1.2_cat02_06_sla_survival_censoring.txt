## Section-by-section

### 1. SLA estimands and time definitions — good design, damaged clarity and some wrong math language

**Strengths:** Clear origin at `Submit`, horizon 72h, right truncation at cutoff, three distinct estimands tied to alerting / governance / backlog, and a useful exemplar table for S03, S09, S06, S08. The distinction among right censoring, competing events, and already-observed breach (including open age > 72) is the right one.

**Problems:**
- Math is systematically corrupted (`S()`, `Pr(T_resolve  t  resolve`, empty `` tokens). An SLA survival answer cannot afford unreadable estimands.
- E1 is labeled “marginal” while described as net/hypothetical survival with competing events censored. That is cause-specific / net-style reasoning, not a clean marginal estimand under competing risks.
- E2’s “breach-inclusive = 1 − F_resolve(τ)” is said to include “late + competing + **censored tail**.” Administrative censoring is not a cause in the CIF partition. At τ, probability mass splits into cause-specific CIFs plus still event-free; lumping “censored tail” into breach muddies the estimand the section is supposed to define.
- S09 as “Pr(breach)=1 observed” is fine for “not resolved by 72,” but only under that definition of breach—not under “resolved after 72,” which is still unknown.

### 2. Event and censoring encoding — solid table, transfer bounds botched

**Strengths:** Status → indicator/CR-code tables; open kept as censored; transfers under censoring vs competing vs bounds; withdraw/reject alternatives. This matches the required format.

**Problems:**
- **T-C bound direction is wrong.** If transfers are “sicker” and would have breached (not resolved by 72), correcting independent censoring should **lower** \(S(72)\) (raise breach risk), not give an “upper bound on \(S(72)\).” “Best case = impute success” also does not cleanly give a “lower bound” on \(S\) without specifying the estimand (time-to-resolution vs breach indicator).
- “C-worst: encode as 1 for Pr(T_resolve>72)” is ambiguous and conflicts with later code (see §8).
- Claiming non-informative transfer “overestimates \(S\) if transfers are sicker” is the usual direction—but then the worst/best labels disagree with that sentence.

### 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased — right biases, several bad numbers

**Strengths:** Concrete IDs for survivor bias, dropped opens (including known post-72 opens), competing risks, transfers, cohort/priority imbalance, and stale vs active opens. This is the right attack on completed-case analysis.

**Problems:**
- “True \(F_{\text{resolve}}(\infty)=13/32\)” is **false** with 9 opens still unresolved. 13/32 is a lower bound on eventual resolution incidence, not the CIF.
- \(F_{\text{resolve}}(72)=9/32\) is a crude count, not “the” CIF; opens censored before 72 and competing events need risk-set weighting (AJ).
- “Proper \(1-S_{KM}(72)=1-0.64=36\%\)” and “AJ ~30%” are **asserted without a correct, transparent estimator**—then used as if established.
- **Apples-to-oranges “qualitative reversal”:** naive ~31% is \(P(T>72\mid\text{resolved})\). ~70% as \(1-F_{\text{resolve}}(72)\) mixes late resolve, withdraw, reject, transfer, and still-open mass. Showing that a different estimand is larger is not proof the same estimand reversed after debiasing. Rhetorically effective, statistically unfair.

### 4. Statistical analysis plan — appropriate menu, weak justification numerics

**Strengths:** Estimand-first framing; KM vs AJ vs CSH vs Fine-Gray vs RMST vs landmark vs multi-state; primary AJ+KM and secondary landmark/RMST is defensible for n=32 and process structure; Fine-Gray rightly downgraded; causal caution on cohort.

**Problems:**

- Little on how ties, discrete hours, or only 13 resolution events limit stratified KM/AJ by cohort×priority (many strata will be empty or single-event).
