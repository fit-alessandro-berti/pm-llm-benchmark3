# Evaluation

## Summary judgment
The submission is a strong, estimand-first process-mining survival design that largely matches the required nine-section format, uses the supplied cases concretely, treats competing risks and leakage seriously, and ships a substantial self-contained analysis script. Under a hypercritical standard it is **not** near-flawless: there is a real dual-estimand encoding inconsistency in the code, several statistical and operational imprecisions, and a few places where the prose overstates what the extract can support.

## Section-by-section

### 1. SLA estimands and time definitions — strong
Time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72h, and multiple estimands (CIF of Resolve, complement, late Resolve, still-active, cause-specific) are well defined and mapped to decisions. The four example cases (S03/S09/S06/S08) are handled carefully and distinguish “not resolved by 72” from “still active at 72.”

**Deductions:**  
- Late-resolution is written as \(F(t^*)-F(72)\) without spelling out residual right-censoring after 72 (open cases such as S09/S11/S25/S26), so the probability language is slightly cleaner than the data allow.  
- “No-resolution-by-SLA risk = \(1-F_{\mathrm{resolve}}(72)\)” is only unambiguous once the competing-risk convention is fixed; the table mostly does this, but the headline phrasing still slides between CIF-complement and a vague breach rate.

### 2. Event and censoring encoding — strong
Statusevent/competing/censor map is clear; open below vs above 72 is distinguished; transfers get three (actually four) explicit assumptions; withdrawal/rejection are not silently treated as censoring.

**Deductions:**  
- Sensitivity column is thorough but occasionally blurs *encoding* with *business interpretation*.  
- “IPCW / joint models” are named without any feasible identification strategy on n=32 and no timestamps—over-claim relative to the extract.

### 3. Why naive completed-case analysis is biased — very strong
Concrete IDs, survivor bias from old opens (S09/S11/S25/S26), below- vs above-72 opens, long gaps, competing exits, cohort/AutoCheck imbalance, and P1-only transfers are all on-point and evidence-tied.

**Deductions:** Minor. “13 resolved / 4 late” is correct; the bias narrative is slightly repetitive with §6.

### 4. Statistical analysis plan — strong
Estimand-first ordering is correct. AJ as primary for Resolve CIF, KM/RMST for any-terminal as secondary, and explicit rejection of naive KM-for-Resolve as primary are appropriate for these data. Landmarking/multi-state limitations given missing timestamps are rightly flagged.

**Deductions:**  
- Subdistribution vs cause-specific discussion is generic; with n=32 and sparse causes (3 transfers, few reworks), Fine-Gray is more ornamental than usable—should have been down-weighted harder.  
- Restricted mean is recommended without defining the upper limit or competing-risk variant (e.g., RMST of time unresolved vs time unabsorbed by Resolve).  
- No plan for variance/CI or exact small-sample behavior despite repeatedly noting n=32.

### 5. Process-aware features and stage hazards — strong
Baseline vs time-varying vs post-treatment/leakage table is excellent (`escalated`, full `rework_count`, full terminal prefixes called out). Immortal time is explained with the right mechanism. Transition skeleton from prefixes is faithful to the data.

**Deductions:**  
- “State-transition diagram” is only a textual adjacency list, not a real diagram (Mermaid was invited).  
- Some proposed features (`last_event_gap_h` as general covariate, full path variants for prediction) still need stricter “measured at landmark only” discipline than the table always enforces.  
- Stage-hazard language slightly outruns Assumption A2 (no activity times).

### 6. Sensitivity analysis — strong
Transfer × withdraw/reject × open censoring × cohort/AutoCheck matrix is the right set; qualitative reversal statements are concrete; the 9 / 18 / 5 vs 9 / 16 / 7 horizon tally matches the extract.

**Deductions:**  
- “IPCW or weighting by age/gap” and best/worst-case for opens below 72 are named without specifying weights or bounds—hand-wavy at this sample size.  
- Cohort-policy sensitivity correctly notes non-randomization but then under-develops how incomplete AutoCheck compliance should be coded (ITT calendar cohort vs as-treated AutoCheck exposure).

### 7. Operational interpretation — good
Maps outputs to expedite / capacity / transfer audit / stalled-gap alerts; rejects causal reading of cohort B; asks for calibration and priority/cohort slices.

**Deductions:**  
- “Conditional probability given current age, stage, prefix, …” is described as if a fitted dynamic model exists; the extract cannot support a well-calibrated individual alert model.  
- Fairness language is thin (no concrete metric, no base-rate handling by priority).  
- Risk of treating survival scores as ranking tools without decision thresholds or cost asymmetry is under-discussed.

### 8. Python / pseudocode — good, with a material flaw
Meets most hard requirements: in-memory table, two estimands, three transfer scenarios, open cases retained, cohort/priority stratification, sensitivity loop, PM4Py-compatible log + `get_variants` (with fallback), no fabricated timestamps, printable summaries, named fallback if survival libs missing.

**Major flaw:**  
`encode_events(..., transfer_scenario="censor")` sets `cause=0` for transfers and therefore `event_any_terminal=0`. The secondary estimand in §4—“KM / survival without **any internal terminal disposition**”—must count `Transfer` as terminal (internal observation stops). Under `transfer="censor"`, `km_survival_at` therefore **incorrectly censors transfers** for the any-terminal analysis. Transfer assumptions should affect the Resolve-specific encoding, not silently redefine “any terminal.” This is a real logical bug relative to the paper’s own estimands, not a style nit.

**Lesser code issues:**  
- `feature_summary`’s `mean_last_gap_open_only` lambda is fragile index gymnastics and easy to get wrong.  
- Custom AJ/KM are algorithmically OK for discrete times but print point estimates only—no CIs, no tie policy statement, no risk-set tables.  
- Adverse transfers use `cause=5` but never enter a dedicated “breach” subdistribution; reliance on \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{resolve}}\) is fine for “not resolved,” yet the scenario name over-promises.  
- PM4Py path uses manual `EventLog` construction (allowed as equivalent) but does not demonstrate `get_prefixes_from_log` or any time-aware prefix feature—only full-trace variants, which are leakage-prone if used as baseline predictors (the prose knows this; the code still leads with full variants).  
- No assert that resolved-by-72 counts match the hand tally in §6.

### 9. Limitations and additional data — strong
Priorities (timestamps, post-transfer outcomes, cutoff/start dates, compliance, assignment, workload, reason codes, SLA legal definition) are exactly what a stronger design needs. Explicitly refuses strong causal claims on cohort B.

**Deductions:** Light. Could have ranked “external transfer outcomes” and “activity timestamps” even more sharply above nice-to-haves.

## Score rationale (strict)
Start near ceiling for structure, competing-risk literacy, case-level evidence, leakage/immortal-time awareness, and a working multi-scenario script. Significant docks for: (1) transfer-censor breaking the secondary any-terminal estimand in code; (2) individual-alert / IPCW / Fine-Gray ambitions beyond n=32 and missing timestamps; (3) point estimates without uncertainty; (4) weak “diagram”; (5) full-prefix features still surfaced as cohort summaries without a landmark guardrail in code.

These are more than cosmetic. Under the instructed hypercritical bar (minor issues cut sharply; very high scores only if nearly flawless), the work sits in the high-good / low-excellent band, not 9+.

**Final grade: 8.3**
