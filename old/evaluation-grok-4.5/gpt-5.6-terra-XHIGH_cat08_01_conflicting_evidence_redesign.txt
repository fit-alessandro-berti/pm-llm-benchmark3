## Evaluation

### Strengths
The diagnosis is the strongest section: it correctly centers the core contradiction (fast path 31% cheaper pre-rework / only 9% after; 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen), cites well above five artifacts (frequency DFG volumes, performance DFG means/p90s, token replay 0.91 / 68% fitting, alignment move-on-log deviations, precision, temporal profile, rework stats, segment outcomes, resource limits), and refuses the false “manual is always better” conclusion. Trade-offs are explicit throughout. The three redesigns are materially different from each other, each has a clear mechanism and deliberate sacrifices, the table columns match the spec, failure modes include bottleneck migration, and the experiment sequence correctly insists on 30/60/90-day post-close windows and segment-level controls rather than first-close averages.

### Deductions (hypercritical)

**1. Redesigns are largely the three levers already handed to the author (major)**  
Resource section explicitly offers: (a) update auto-screen rules, (b) evidence portal –30–45% repeat requests, (c) lightweight pre-dispatch gate +6–18 h.  
Redesigns A/B/C are essentially those three items with segment wrappers. That is competent elaboration, not strong synthesis or non-generic redesign logic. Higher-scoring answers would have created at least one structurally different alternative (true segment-split processes, outcome-based replacement supplier contracts, predictive triage that changes work content, reopen/escalation redesign given the single 45-min board slot, etc.). Mapping 1:1 onto the prompt’s own suggestions materially caps originality.

**2. Sequencing logic is partially self-undermining**  
A is piloted first and is known to raise Manual eligibility inflow 15–35% while reviewers already cliff above 82% utilization. B (the intervention that removes avoidable evidence chasing from those same reviewers) comes second. The write-up monitors utilization but never installs a capacity buffer, evidence-complete-only entry rule, or concurrent micro-pilot of B. This is a logical gap between diagnosis and experiment design.

**3. Conceptual imprecision on R1**  
Redesign C claims the release gate “supports R1 and R2.” R1 is a pre-Fast-approval control (“above EUR 400 must have Manual or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval”). A post-approval release gate can still block shipment (helps R2 and residual risk) but does not prevent the R1 violation itself. Over-claiming.

**4. Under-used process areas left untouched**  
- Escalation board (one daily 45-min slot, 120 cases from reopen) is diagnosed as unscalable yet never redesigned.  
- Fraud/risk Monday/campaign bottleneck is noted but receives only “don’t over-trigger” advice.  
- Repair-quote part-price wait and 12.5% high-value repair rework receive only light treatment inside C.  
A top answer would have folded at least one of these into a redesign rather than leaving them as residual risks.

**5. Minor numeric/clarity nits**  
- “1,430 entries” is correct (650+780) but the later 220 from RequestFraud is not integrated into the loop diagnosis.  
- Table cost/cycle columns stay at “uncertain / material reduction expected” even where the prompt gives enough directional anchors to be tighter without claiming false precision.  
- Occasional repetition across diagnosis and redesign “sacrifices” sections adds length without new evidence.

**6. Format and constraint compliance**  
All seven required sections exist and stay inside the stated constraints (no generic Lean list, no average-CT-only optimization, no exact ROI/Pareto claims). No hard failures here.

### Summary judgment
Evidence use and contradiction handling are excellent; trade-off language is disciplined; experiment design is mostly sound. The answer is pulled down from the 9s by (a) packaging the prompt’s own three interventions as the three redesigns, (b) the A-then-B capacity contradiction, and (c) the R1/release-gate slip plus neglected escalation/fraud/repair levers. Under the mandated hypercritical standard these are more than nits; they prevent a very high score.
