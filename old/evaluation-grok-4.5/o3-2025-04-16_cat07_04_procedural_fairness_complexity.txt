### Section-by-section critique

**§1 Fairness dimensions**  
Lists the right dimensions but stays thin. “2–6 pp” blurs distinct adjusted gaps (−5.5 / −2.2 / −3.1). The V4 consistency claim is confused: “24% of Group B enter V4 although only 57% request language support—suggesting over-routing” does not follow. If 57% need language support and only 24% take V4, that is under-use of the language path relative to stated need, not over-routing—unless V4 is only one of several language-related paths. That logical error is serious in a fairness write-up centered on routing consistency. Appeal/rework and quality/accessibility are named more than analyzed.

**§2 Adjusted comparison strategy**  
Mentions matching, path comparison, resources, waiting-time decomposition, sensitivity, and subgroup checks—good skeleton—but execution is shallow. Controls omit `language_preference` and `disability_accommodation_requested`, which are central to legitimate vs. unfair routing. “Oaxaca-Blinder-style” is name-dropped without a workable process-mining operationalization. No clear protocol for separating disparity vs. discrimination vs. legitimate routing vs. uncertainty beyond buzzwords. PM4Py snippets are decorative, not an analysis design.

**§3 Path and resource disparities**  
Repeats the same V4/over-routing error. Workload arithmetic is invented and wrong: ~1,250 Group B cases (not “1,200+”), and “7 bilingual workers × 170” vs “52 × 70” is not supported by the scenario (Group A has 3,600 cases across 52 workers ≈ 69/worker if exclusive, but assignment is not exclusive; bilingual staff may handle mixed caseloads). Specialist queue waits (3.8d / 4.7d) and handover patterns are correctly flagged; Group D / V3 / risk routing is under-developed. “Justified vs concerning” is asserted more than carefully partitioned.

Mechanism quality is middling; no engagement with documentation completeness as endogenous to language barriers, or risk scores encoding history—central to the scenario’s own caveats.

**§5 Limitations**  
Adequate on risk-score and documentation bias, outcome quality, and coarse resource data. Incomplete on appeal quality (process of appeal, not only rates), missing needs beyond binary accommodation, and how controlling for endogenous covariates can launder unfairness. Acceptable but not strong.

Hits the required six themes with side effects. Trade-offs are generic (“budget cost,” “may shift delay”). No prioritization, no metrics for success, no warning against cutting support steps (constraint is only passively respected). Fine checklist, weak policy design.
