## Evaluation

The submission hits the required eight-section skeleton and shows real process-mining instincts (open-hours vs flow time, carry-over, outage ≠ steady high throughput, mix vs capacity vs demand, pooling risk). Under a strict bar, material errors in dates, units, logic, and code–text consistency keep it well below a strong grade.

### Critical flaws

**1. Regime boundaries are wrong or incoherent (Section 2)**  
- R0 is labeled “Mon–Tue” for `2026-04-06 – 2026-04-14`, which spans a full week including Wed–Fri.  
- Rw dates are inverted: “`2026-04-06/07/08/09/10` Sat–Sun” are weekdays; actual first weekend is `04-11/12`. This is not a cosmetic slip—it undermines the primary segmentation table.  
- R3 WIP “3219” is a broken typo (presumably 32–19).  
- Apr 18–19 (and how post-outage weekends attach) are not handled cleanly relative to R1/R2.

**2. Little’s Law is mishandled (Section 6 + code) — core competence miss**  
- Defensible open-hours form is \(W \approx L/\lambda\) with \(\lambda\) in cases/open-hour → flow time in **hours** (e.g. \(20/8.3 \approx 2.4\) h).  
- The write-up uses \(20/(8.3\times 12)=0.20\) “days” without a clear open-day vs calendar-day convention, boundary conditions, or WIP snapshot semantics.  
- Python does something else again: `ft_days = W/(lam*12)/24` ⇒ \(W/\bar{A}/24\), ~0.008 days for the baseline—**not** the 0.20 in the matrix. Text and code contradict each other on the central estimand.  
- Calling R0 “applicable” with high confidence oversells aggregates that omit 07:00 backlog and overnight carry-over.

**3. Code segmentation does not match the narrative**  
Classifier order sends `staff >= 7` to `HighStaff` (so 04-16 and 04-29/30 leave the story’s R1/R3), and **2026-04-17 (6.5 FTE weekday recovery) falls through to `Weekend`**. That is a logic bug, not a minor implementation detail. Outage/recovery is not segmented as claimed in Section 2.

**4. Section 4 “Simpson” point is backwards**  
Post-intervention median service (32) **exceeds** baseline (25) while WIP falls on **capacity**. Claiming pooled data might suggest “service times improved” does not follow from the quantities discussed. The WIP/throughput contrasts are directionally fine; the paradox write-up is not.

**5. Unsupported statistical claims (Section 5)**  
Fano/CV/lag-1 on four hourly vectors are fine as illustrations. Asserting “Ljung-Box … p < 0.05” with no computation in the required Python block is invented precision. The same section correctly notes four days cannot pin a stochastic model—then undercuts that caution with a definitive serial-test claim.
