### Deductions (strict)
1. **PM4Py plan incomplete relative to the prompt’s required toolkit** (0.6)  
   Prompt explicitly asks for log filtering, variant analysis, DFG **and** performance DFG, handover networks, service/waiting-time insertion, **and** declarative/log-skeleton checks. The code omits plain `discover_dfg`, `filter_variants`, `filter_case_performance`, and `discover_declare`. `filter_paths_performance` is invoked without the required performance bounds. This is a direct miss on a required section deliverable.

2. **API / technical imprecision** (0.4)  
   - `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="case")` is not the standard PM4Py pattern for case-level attributes (normally `filter_trace_attribute_values` / case filtering).  
   - `insert_case_service_waiting_time` signature and follow-on `get_service_time` usage are hand-wavy.  
   For a section whose job is an executable analysis plan, this matters.

3. **Mediation / identification soft spots** (0.4)  
   Section 2 lists sensitivity for biased covariates but does not cleanly operationalize the central fairness identification problem: support steps and documentation completeness are **mediators of procedural burden**, not pure confounders. Matching on them can absorb the very disparity under study. The answer notes the issue in limitations but does not design the comparison strategy around it (e.g., primary estimates that do **not** condition on mediators, plus separate mediation decomposition). That is a substantive gap for “assess procedural fairness after controlling for process complexity.”

4. **Minor overgeneralization of evidence** (0.3)  
   Handover burden is stated in the scenario as concentrated **in two regions**; the answer often presents it as a general Group B/C pattern. Small, but it weakens precision.

5. **Thin treatment of consistency-of-routing and appeal quality** (0.3)  
   Consistency is listed but not tested with a clear within-stratum design (same risk band + same need flag  different supervisor/fraud rates). Appeal/rework is cited numerically but never linked to a concrete PM4Py check (e.g., variant V6 performance by group, or rework loops via DFG/skeleton).

### Score rationale
Content quality and constraint compliance are high; the answer is usable and mostly correct. It is not nearly flawless: required PM4Py coverage is incomplete, identification strategy for procedural (not just outcome) fairness is under-specified, and there are avoidable technical and precision errors.
