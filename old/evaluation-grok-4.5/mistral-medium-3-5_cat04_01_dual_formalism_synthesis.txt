The submission follows the required heading structure and produces the expected sections/tables, but collapses under even moderate scrutiny on semantics, model correctness, API fidelity, internal consistency, and the explicit quality criteria (exactness vs. approximation, counter-examples, runnable code, no disguise of loss). Major defects are not cosmetic; they render both models non-faithful and largely non-executable as claimed. Details below.

# 1. Assumptions
- Duplicate-label claim (“No duplicate visible labels… All activities appear exactly once”) directly contradicts the required trial-loop behaviour and the witnesses that contain repeated Execute/Inspect. This is a foundational misunderstanding.
- Approximation policy is labelled “conservative under-approximation” yet later text and the T4 witness treat the tree as excluding valid overlap (under) while the parallel placement of approvals sometimes over-approximates; the policy is therefore incoherent.
- “Passing inspection” abstraction via control-flow is stated, but neither model realises a clean repeat-until (see §§3-4).
- Risk and supplier optionality are described as XOR, yet the concrete artefacts do not implement choice correctly.

# 2. Dependency table
Reasonably complete list of intended relations, but several rows blur choice vs. concurrency vs. synchronisation (e.g., risk route, supplier, loop exit). The table is not used consistently by the later models, so it functions more as decoration than as a specification that the implementations satisfy.

# 3. Process-Tree Model
- The compact expression is not a legal process-tree string; empty placeholders and ad-hoc loop notation make it unusable.
- Python code is not self-contained/runnable:
  - `Op.LEAF` does not exist in the stated API; leaves are `operator=None`.
  - Nodes (especially `risk_xor`, `supplier_xor`) are inserted into multiple parents; parent pointers are overwritten and the resulting object is not a tree.
  - Control-flow is scrambled: approvals/supplier appear both inside the post-analyse parallel and again inside the later “sync” parallel; the trial is sequenced after the whole main parallel, destroying the required overlap of approvals with the trial cycle and the independent start of Plan Verification / Design Corrective Action.
  - LOOP construction does not encode “repeat until pass; Adjust never after the successful Inspect”.
- The limitations paragraph admits some problems but understates them and does not match the code that was actually written. Representational loss is therefore disguised rather than defended with a clear under-/over-approximation policy.

# 4. POWL Model
- Fundamental misuse of the formalism: a flat `StrictPartialOrder` containing every activity (plus two silents) is created. No `OperatorPOWL` for XOR (risk route, optional supplier) or LOOP (trial cycle) appears anywhere. Consequently:
  - All three review activities are mandatory.
  - Both Consult and skip are mandatory.
  - Adjust is forced by an edge.
  - A cycle (InspectAdjustExecute) is added to a strict partial order, which is acyclic by definition; the model is ill-formed.
- Transition constructors are called with two identical string arguments; the API reminder shows the expected form.
- Edges for the risk choice and the loop exit are missing or wrong; the “unconnected nodes may remain concurrent” paragraph is therefore vacuous.
- The model cannot generate any of the required choice or iteration behaviours.

# 5. Witnesses
- The eight-trace table exists and tries to cover the mandated variety (low/high risk, supplier, multi-trial, PT-approximation exposure, cross-branch violation).
- Because both models are broken, the “Process-tree verdict / POWL verdict” columns are unsupported assertions.
- T4 is a reasonable attempt to expose PT weakness, yet the concrete PT does not even reach the state in which that distinction matters.
- Several “allowed” traces are impossible under the artefacts that were coded (missing choice operators, forced activities, broken nesting).

# 6. Semantic Comparison
The table repeatedly claims “Exact” for the POWL column and for several PT rows. Those claims are false given the code. Over-/under-constraint diagnoses therefore rest on fiction. Duplicate-label row contradicts §1 and the loop requirement.

# 7. Preferred Representation & Validation
- Preference for POWL is defensible in principle, but the delivered POWL does not realise any of the advantages claimed.
- Validation plan lists the right high-level steps (conversion, soundness, playout, positive/negative traces, silent/duplicate checks) yet supplies no concrete executable checks, uses non-existent API names (`pm4py.conformance_checking`), and never confronts the fact that the models cannot be converted into meaningful Petri nets in the first place.
- Limitations paragraph is generic and does not identify the fatal operator/cycle problems.

# 8. Self-Critique
Three weaknesses are listed and one is semantic, satisfying the mechanical requirement. However the critique itself is shallow: it never mentions the complete absence of OperatorPOWL, the illegal cycle in a partial order, node-reuse bugs, or the contradictory duplicate-label assumption. It therefore fails to demonstrate genuine understanding of the residual damage.

## Summary judgement (hypercritical)
Format compliance and the presence of tables give a superficial impression of completeness. Virtually every semantic obligation—choice of risk route, optional supplier, repeat-until trial, cross-branch synchronisation at Release, proper use of the two formalisms—is either incorrectly encoded or omitted. Code artefacts are non-executable and contradict both the narrative and the API reminders. Representational loss is not cleanly isolated or defended. Under the instruction to penalise even minor issues heavily and to reserve high scores for near-flawless work, the submission cannot exceed a low single-digit grade.
