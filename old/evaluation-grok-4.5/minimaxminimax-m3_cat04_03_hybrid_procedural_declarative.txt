## Critical defects (major score impact)

### 1. DECLARE `precedence` for alternative approvals is incorrect

Encoded:

- `precedence(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)`
- `precedence(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)`

In DECLARE, `precedence(A, B)` means: **if B occurs, A must already have occurred**. It is not “A is allowed before B.”

Consequences:

- **T1** (Manager only) violates `precedence(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)`.
- **T2** (Compliance only) violates `precedence(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)`.

Yet both are marked **DECLARE = pass**. The model and the matrix contradict each other.

### 2. Same bug in Log Skeleton `always_before`

Both pairs are present:

- `("Manager Approval", "Activate Vendor")`
- `("Compliance Approval", "Activate Vendor")`

`always_before(A, B)` has the same “B  A earlier” reading. Again T1 and T2 must fail Log Skeleton; the matrix says pass. R2’s XOR is not expressible by requiring *both* predecessors.

### 3. Unconditional “Opinion before Activate” breaks happy-path accepts

DECLARE:

- `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` (even at `confidence = 0.5`)

Log Skeleton:

- `always_before(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`

Whenever `Activate Vendor` occurs without a prior legal opinion—the normal low/high-risk paths in **T1/T2**—these fire as violations. Opinion is only obligatory after `Sanctions Escalation` (R4/R5), not globally. Encoding R5 as unconditional precedence/always_before is a policy-level error, not a nit.

### 4. `responded_existence` does not implement “exactly one approval”

```text
responded_existence(Activate  Manager) @ 0.5
responded_existence(Activate  Compliance) @ 0.5
```

`responded_existence(A, B)` means A  B occurs *somewhere*. Two such rules do **not** form a disjunction/XOR via confidence. Confidence is not a logical OR. R2 remains mishandled despite a long explanation claiming otherwise.

### 5. Verdict matrix inconsistent with the stated procedural alphabet

Procedural tree **excludes** `Sanctions Escalation` and `Obtain Legal Opinion`.

| Trace | Foreign activities present? | Matrix says |
|---|---|---|
| T6 | Yes | procedural fail/warning |
| T7 | Yes (same two labels) | procedural **pass** |

T7 must fail (or warn) for the same reason as T6 under that design. Treating them differently is an internal contradiction.

### 6. T7 vs Log Skeleton `directly_follows`

Skeleton includes `directly_follows(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval)`.

T7 order: EDD@8  …  Obtain Legal Opinion@40  Compliance@42.  
Opinion sits between EDD and Compliance  **directly_follows fails**. Matrix marks Log Skeleton **pass**. Wrong.

### 7. T5 procedural cell is incoherent

The tree’s clarification fragment is `LOOP(SEQ(Request, Receive), )` under an optional XOR. T5 is `Request` with **no** `Receive` before outcomes. That is not a fit for `SEQ(Request, Receive)`. The matrix still puts Procedural = **pass**, while the parenthetical text admits the `do`-child needs Receive. Fail/pass should be decisive; here it is muddled.
