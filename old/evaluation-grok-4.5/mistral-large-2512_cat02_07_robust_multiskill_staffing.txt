**3.0**

The submission follows the required 9-heading skeleton and touches every mandated topic, but it is riddled with material analytical errors, internal contradictions, inverted capacity logic, non-executable code, and invented numbers that do not follow from the supplied evidence. Under a hypercritical standard these defects are disqualifying.

**Section 1.**  
Assumptions are labelled and two objective formulations are supplied. Minor credit. Parallelism rule and preemption threshold are arbitrary and never used consistently later.

**Section 2 (fatal).**  
Offered-load arithmetic treats a speed multiplier >1 as *additional* capacity (`sum(multipliers)*minutes`). This is dimensionally inverted: a 1.35 multiplier means the resource is slower and contributes *less* effective capacity. Consequently every utilisation figure is wrong.  
Key resources are silently dropped (Ada’s Electrical 1.35, Farah’s Mechanical 1.40, etc.). Breaks and partial-shift overlaps are ignored. U2 renormalisation contains an arithmetic slip (2×28/140  43.8). Calculated utilisations stay <50 % in base yet later tables claim 85-100 % and multi-hour delays—an irreconcilable contradiction. No proper skill-time matrix, no bounds, no migration analysis under the five shocks. The section fails the “show enough work to audit” requirement.

**Section 3.**  
Three policies are described with eligibility and dispatch sketches. Acceptable on paper, but the descriptions are not tight enough to be implemented unambiguously and are not the policies actually coded in Section 8.

**Section 4.**  
The policy-by-scenario table is pure fabrication. Ranges are not derived from the (already broken) load calculations nor from any simulation output. Several required columns are missing or blank in the shock rows; uncertainty is only cosmetic. Because the underlying capacity model is inverted, the relative ordering of policies cannot be trusted.

**Section 5 (logical collapse).**  
The authors’ own table shows full pooling dominating hybrid on every delay, SLA and resilience cell. They nevertheless recommend hybrid as “robust.” No quantitative trade-off or objective-function evaluation justifies the choice. Fallback triggers are stated but never linked to the numbers in the table. The recommendation therefore fails the “dominates or best trade-off under your chosen objective” test.

**Section 6.**  
Secondary-effect discussion is the strongest part—covers gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, preemption thrashing, Validation migration, fairness and interpretability with plausible mitigations. Still only partial credit because none of the risks are quantified or tied back to the earlier (incorrect) numbers.

**Section 7.**  
Attribute list is largely complete. Several PM4Py calls are non-existent or mis-specified (`get_waiting_time`, `discover_bottleneck_net`, `get_resource_utilization`, bare `view_` calls). The monitoring design is therefore only aspirational.

**Section 8 (catastrophic).**  
- Syntax error in U4 dictionary literal.  
- Discrete-event engine never checks whether a resource is already busy; overlapping assignments are possible.  
- No parallel-branch logic whatsoever despite being required.  
- Preemption, handover and setup are incompletely or incorrectly inserted into timestamps.  
- Service times are re-sampled randomly for utilisation statistics, destroying reproducibility.  
- Event log emits null `start_timestamp`s, mixes event types into `concept:name`, and omits most mandated attributes.  
- Single seed, no uncertainty bands.  
- Many mechanisms required by the prompt are either absent or hidden inside broken control flow; the code does not identify the omissions.  
The block therefore satisfies almost none of the eight explicit Python requirements and cannot be executed to produce the claimed comparison tables.

**Section 9.**  
Limits and pilot/back-test sketch are reasonable and protect P1 traffic. Minor credit only.

**Global defects against the quality bar and general rules**  
- Specialisation-vs-pooling tension is asserted but never quantified with correct loads or speed-adjusted capacities.  
- Time-varying shifts, breaks and multipliers are mishandled.  
- Numbers in the comparison table have no audit trail from the evidence.  
- Internal consistency is repeatedly violated (load vs delay, table vs recommendation, policy description vs code).  
- Python is neither a working DES, a correct capacity approximation, nor a clean optimisation scaffold.  
- Uncertainty is treated only by writing ranges; no bounds, sensitivity or multi-seed results appear.

Collectively these are not “minor issues.” They destroy analytical credibility, reproducibility and decision value. A score above 3 would require overlooking inverted capacity arithmetic, a self-contradictory recommendation, and non-runnable code that fails the majority of the explicit Python checklist. Hence 3.0.
