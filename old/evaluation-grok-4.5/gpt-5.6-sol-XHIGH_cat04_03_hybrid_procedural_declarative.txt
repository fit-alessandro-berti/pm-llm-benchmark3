## Section-by-section critique

### 1. Policy Interpretation — solid, not perfect
- Two R5/R6 resolutions, a chosen default (strict R5), and real compliance risk: good.
- Assumptions are mostly defensible (completed traces, accepted = has Activate, serialized clarification, no risk attribute).
- **Nit:** Assumption 9 silently strengthens R11 (Suspend before payment). Disclosed, but the strengthening is easy to miss later when scoring “procedural pass.”
- **Nit:** Assumption 8 (post-payment out of scope) is reasonable but never stress-tested against R11’s “eventually” wording for Suspend.

### 2. Mapping table — good coverage, uneven precision
- All of R1–R12 appear; authoritative layer is often named.
- **Issue:** R2 admits DECLARE cannot express “exactly one of {Manager, Compliance} on accept,” then still leans on pairwise `noncoexistence` as if it were close enough. The gap is real: absence of both approvals on an Activate trace is only caught procedurally.
- **Issue:** R5’s DECLARE encoding via `nonsuccession(Activate, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is a **conservative over-approximation** (blocks any post-activation opinion, even unsolicited). Stated later, under-emphasized in the table’s “exact encoding” column.
- **Issue:** R10 claim that DECLARE has “no unambiguous at-most-once” is slightly overstated (dialects / patterns exist); minor.

### 3. Procedural backbone — ambitious, mostly coherent, hard to certify
- Accepting Petri net is an appropriate choice for orthogonal clarification + sanctions state.
- Main happy paths (low, high, reject), strict R5 gate, and serialized clarification/escalation are credible.
- Concurrency of the three checks and XOR after Classify are correct in structure.
- **Problems:**
  1. **Over-long hand-built net with duplicate-labelled transitions** (`Close Onboarding` ×2, `Verify Bank Account` ×2). Plausible, but easy to get wrong; no soundness argument, reachability sketch, or invariant check. Under strict review this is a liability.
  2. **R11 modeled strictly stronger than the text** (both Suspend and re-Verify before payment; cycles serialized). Disclosed once, then treated as “the” procedural truth in the matrix.
  3. **`directly`-style closure and “payment required after every Activate”** are baked in; aligns with backbone prose but not with every cross-cutting reading of “eventually.”
  4. **No executable alternate net for Resolution B** — Section 8 still marks “T6 — R6 exception / Procedural = pass.” That pass is **asserted, not shown**.
  5. Risk route is purely structural (no `risk_level`); acknowledged, but then R2 “exactly one approval in an accepted case” is only half-enforced relative to classification intent.
  6. Escalation after Classify / during EDD is allowed; requirements don’t forbid it, but interaction with `p_case_open` / close-on-reject is subtle and under-explained.

Not a failed model; not a verified one either.

### 4. DECLARE — valid-looking, with real holes
- Format (`support`/`confidence`, unary keys, binary tuples) matches the prompt.
- Useful distinctions (response vs precedence vs altresponse vs noncoexistence vs nonsuccession) are explained.
- **Flaws:**
  1. **No encoding that Activate is preceded by (Manager XOR Compliance).** Authoritative deferral to the net is fine only if the matrix never treats DECLARE as sufficient for accept-path validity; it sometimes reads that way.
  2. **`exactly_one` on Capture / Screening / Classify** is a strong policy choice not in R1–R12; rework/re-screen would false-fail.
  3. **`nonsuccession(Activate, Legal Opinion)`** over-fires on non-escalated post-activation opinions (admitted only in prose).
  4. **Clarification:** `altresponse` does **not** enforce “before final outcome”; they correctly add a custom monitor in §7, but DECLARE alone is over-credited in places.
  5. R10 at-most-once left entirely to the skeleton/net — fine, but then DECLARE is less “defense in depth” than claimed for cardinality.

### 5. Log skeleton — mostly careful, incomplete, one sharp edge
- All six keys present; orientations documented; deliberate non-encoding of strict R5 (so T6 can pass skeleton while failing the net) is a good design call.
- **Flaws:**
  1. **`directly_follows: {("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding")}`** is stricter than the scenario (“follows,” not “immediately follows”). Unnecessary false-fail risk.
  2. **Missing obvious `always_before` for Reject  Classify** (and similar), while other route constraints are present — uneven.
  3. **`always_after` / equivalence are weaker than alternate/paired cycles** under multiple clarifications or bank changes; noted in §9 but still used as hard checks in §8.
  4. `activ_freq` only on a subset; fine if defended, but then overlap with the net’s cardinality is messy.

### 6. Temporal profile — correct numbers, fine framing
- Seconds conversion and `zeta = 2.0` are correct.
- T7 \(z \approx 3.67\) warning and payment \(|z| \approx 1.94\) pass are calculated correctly.
- Warnings-only matches R12.
- **Nit:** “0–bound” intervals are informal; PM4Py flags both tails via \(|z|\). Harmless if consistent.

### 7. Resource/data layer — good intent, API hazard
- Missing attributes  indeterminate/manual review (not silent pass): correct.
- Custom SoD + clarification monitor + escalation policy checker: right place for R9/R6/R3 gaps.
- **Flaws:**
  1. **`pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`** usage is underspecified. In PM4Py this filter **keeps cases that satisfy** four-eyes (different resources), it does not return violators. Naming (`*_candidates`) and lack of complement/logic make this easy to invert in deployment.
  2. R9a with **multiple approvals**  `not evaluated` is OK, but interaction with already-failed R2 could be cleaner.
  3. R6 exception path requires several invented case attributes; fine, but T6 “policy-dependent” then depends on join rules not fully standardized in §10.
  4. Clarification monitor is necessary and correct; reinforces that DECLARE `altresponse` was never enough.
