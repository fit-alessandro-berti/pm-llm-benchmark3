## Strict critique

The answer has acceptable high-level structure and correctly refuses to treat rejects/withdrawals/open cases as non-defaults in the primary path. It recognizes activation-induced selection, discusses competing risks, demands temporal splits around the policy and migration breaks, and supplies a leakage table covering the required fields. These keep it from collapsing into a 1–2. However, under hypercritical review the submission contains multiple fatal or near-fatal methodological errors, internal contradictions, and direct failures to meet the stated requirements. A nearly flawless answer would be required for anything  8; this is far from it.

### Section-by-section failures

**1. Target definition**  
Primary choice (time-to-default after activation with right censoring) is defensible and the estimand is stated.  
However:  
- Prediction time is left vague (“after the first few events \ldots e.g. after Credit Review”) and later contradicted.  
- The business question asks about “every credit application”; the chosen population silently discards the majority without a clear operational mapping.  
- No precise definition of the time origin, the censoring time random variable, or how delayed external feeds (7–35 days) enter the likelihood.  
Alternative competing-risk target is fine but under-developed.

**2. Cohort construction**  
Arithmetic reaches 48 000, which is good.  
Fatal softness:  
- The “primary analysis cohort” is defined as the 21 600 fully labeled cases, yet a survival analysis must retain the 3 600 immature activated cases as censored observations. The table and the later text disagree.  
- Pre-/post-policy rows are approximate guesses, not derived, and are not a proper flow.  
- As-of rules are incomplete (no explicit handling of the exact moment `label_available_at` becomes known, no rule for same-day document snapshots, no rule for transfers that later re-appear).  
- Prediction time is declared to be “the first event after Credit Review,” which already excludes true early prefixes for the majority of applications.

**3. Censoring and missing-label strategy**  
Distinctions among right censoring, delayed labels, activation selection, transfers and permanent missingness are named.  
Critical errors:  
- Ordinary right-censoring is incorrectly described as “censored at 90 days” for cases whose labels have simply not matured yet.  
- Primary strategy still drops permanently-missing partner feeds; the missingness mechanism is not MAR and is acknowledged as untestable, yet no inverse-probability or joint model is proposed.  
- One sensitivity analysis re-introduces the forbidden “treat never-activated as non-default” under a counterfactual that cannot be identified from the data.  
- Testability claims are overstated (policy-change effect on the outcome regression is not “only selection”).

**4. Prefix extraction (severe)**  
This section is conceptually inverted:  
- Prediction time is set to the Credit-Review decision, then the “prefix” is defined as the next 3 events or the next 7 days *after* that timestamp.  
- That is future information relative to the declared prediction moment. Standard PPM prefixes are the history *up to* the prediction point.  
- The text simultaneously claims “without looking into the future” and then explicitly looks forward.  
- Short-case, loop and renaming handling are superficial; no formal guarantee that post-activation or post-outcome events are excluded when prediction time itself sits after Accept.  
This single design choice undermines leakage prevention and as-of semantics for the entire study.

**5. Process-aware features**  
Lists are reasonable and the static / prefix / historical distinction appears.  
Gaps: no concrete definition of queue-length or resource experience *at event time*, no guard against the nightly `documents_total` snapshot, and several features (final_limit, decision_reason) sit on the wrong side of the chosen prediction time.

**6. Leakage audit**  
Table format is correct and most required rows are present.  
Problems:  
- `final_limit` and `decision_reason` receive blanket “drop/keep” rules that ignore the fact that the authors’ own prediction time is *after* the credit decision.  
- No row for the inverted prefix construction itself.  
- `label_available_at` / `data_extract_time` correctly dropped as features, yet the cohort logic never actually uses them to enforce maturity.  
- Resource experience “drop or repair” is left unresolved.

**7. Validation design**  
Temporal blocking around 1 Jul and 15 Oct is the right instinct; random-split warning is correct.  
Fatal flaws:  
- “Test period 1 Jan 2026 – 15 Feb 2026” lies outside the event observation window; almost no case *starts* exist.  
- Train stops at the policy change, so the model never sees post-policy data; a pure pre-policy train + post-policy test is a domain-shift experiment, not a usable early-warning system.  
- Label maturity is not enforced inside each fold; late 2025 activations are still incomplete on 15 Feb 2026.  
- Metrics list C-index and Brier yet the implementation (below) never builds a survival response.  
- No calibration or subgroup plan that respects the competing-risk alternative.

**8. Limitations**  
Acceptable. Three deployment-shift examples are present.  
Still too weak on the fundamental point: the estimand is a selected, policy-dependent association among activated accounts only; any language that could be read as “default risk of an application” is over-claim.

**9. PM4Py-oriented skeleton (severe)**  
Required elements are missing or contradicted:  
- `pm4py.format_dataframe` is never called.  
- `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is never called; a hand-rolled, forward-looking extractor is used instead.  
- No justified use of `discover_dfg`, `get_variants`, `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` or `insert_case_service_waiting_time`.  
- Target construction sets `censored_activated  0`, directly violating the primary survival target and introducing classic censoring bias.  
- No `time` / `event` columns are emitted for a later survival or competing-risk model (only a comment).  
- Prediction-time / prefix logic reproduces the future-leakage error of Section 4.  
- Temporal split is by start time but does not filter on label maturity or activation status inside folds.  
- Leakage audit is a stub that only checks column presence.  
- Inefficient Python loops, incomplete activity mapping, buggy terminal-event logic, unused sklearn import.  
Approx. length is reached, yet the code is not a credible implementation skeleton of the design claimed in Sections 1–8.

### Summary judgment
The submission demonstrates awareness of the central statistical traps (selection through activation, no casual non-default labeling, temporal drift) and organises the nine required headings. Those merits are erased by:  
1. inverted prefix semantics that look into the future,  
2. treatment of censored observations as non-events,  
3. failure to exercise the mandated PM4Py APIs,  
4. incoherent prediction-time definition versus the business request, and  
5. an invalid test window and non-operational survival response.  

Under the instructed hypercritical standard these are not minor polish issues; they are disqualifying for any score in the upper half of the scale. Hence 4.0.
