### Overall
The answer hits all seven required sections, uses the timeline/DFG/conformance material in a coherent narrative, and makes several sound keep/modify/retire calls (especially retiring **C** and **G**, keeping **A** only with guardrails). **2. Adaptation-pattern table — largest failure**  
Column design matches the brief and many dispositions are defensible, but multiple cells present **fabricated precision as fact**:

- **F**: “0 CS diversion,” “sample QC 98%,” “1.3% cold-chain” — none given.

- **L**: “no regulator finding yet” — invented.

Under a groundedness standard, inventing audit-grade numbers is a serious flaw, not polish. Rationale quality is mixed: retire C/G and modify D/H/F are good; treating I as clean “KEEP” understates pharmacy harm; L as routine KEEP understates that remediation is debt, not control.

The mission-vs-immutable-controls framing is strong, and C/G as concealed noncompliance is correct. Problems:
- Listing **K** and especially **L** as “true resilience” blurs operational resilience with **post-hoc documentary cleanup**. The prompt explicitly says after-the-fact audit remediation ≠ real-time compliance; L is necessary recovery work and a lagging risk signal, not resilience during the disruption.

**5. Resilience indicators — good, not excellent**  
Leading/lagging split covers throughput protection, control completeness, backlog age, rework, and fairness.
