### Strengths
The answer hits all eight required sections with substantial, on-topic content. It correctly rejects a pure independent-case DES, treats objections as latent/unobserved, separates service vs. waiting without collapsing them, incorporates resource calendars and non-stationary demand, and avoids optimality claims. Cross-case mechanisms (architect bursts, district inspection bundles, objection clusters) are present in scope and assumptions. ### Major / moderate deductions
1. **Missing required placeholder for arrival generation**  
   The prompt explicitly requires placeholder classes *or functions* for arrival generation, routing, resources/calendars, and experiment execution. `ResourceCalendar`, `Router`, `SimulationEngine`, and `ExperimentRunner` exist; there is no `ArrivalGenerator` (or equivalent function). Arrivals are only a constructor argument. This is a direct miss against the required skeleton.

2. **Decision-drafting AND-join / “wait for all applicable reviews” under-specified**  
   The process context and known rules require Decision drafting to wait for Planning, Fire, Environmental, Public-notice, and Inspection results that apply. State variables list `current_activity` / history but do not clearly define join tokens, pending-result sets, or synchronization state. Parallel `+()` in the tree is not enough for a credible digital twin of this process.

3. **D2 data gap handled weakly**  
   “No prior violations” is not in the log and is not given a clear imputation / sensitivity assumption in §3, unlike objections and missing starts. Implementing D2 without that is under-specified.

4. **Parameter estimation remains high-level**  
   “Fit log-normal/gamma to median/p90” and “group by architect_id” are stated, but there is little concrete plan (e.g., method-of-moments vs MLE, how to turn 31% top-20 architect share into a batch process, how to calibrate bundle probability and travel reduction, how to map HR calendars into availability functions). For a simulation *design* answer this is a noticeable thinness.

5. **Experiment matrix effect sizes presented as facts**  
   e.g. D1 “reduces missing-doc loops by 40%”, D3 “travel reduction 30%”, D9 “46d  20d” are design *assumptions* for intervention levers, not estimated quantities. They are not consistently labeled as such, which blurs calibration vs. scenario design.
