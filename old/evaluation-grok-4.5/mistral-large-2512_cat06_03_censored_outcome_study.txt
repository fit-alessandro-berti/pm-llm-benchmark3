The answer follows the nine-section structure and avoids the worst failure modes (it does not casually relabel rejects/withdrawals/open cases as non-default, it flags several leakage sources, and it rejects a pure random split). It is still far from a statistically defensible or carefully engineered study design. Multiple material errors in cohort arithmetic, estimand timing, censoring mechanics, and the code skeleton compound; under strict grading these are disqualifying for a high score.

### 1. Target definition
Primary choice (binary/survival default | eventual activation, predicted at first Credit Review) is a recognized option and the text correctly refuses to treat non-activated cases as non-defaults. However:
- The estimand is explicitly conditioned on a post-prediction event (activation). At the stated prediction time the model therefore cannot be applied to “every credit application” without an additional activation model or strong selection assumptions that are never formalized.
- Horizon is defined from activation, not from prediction time; the operational early-warning claim is thereby weakened and never reconciled.
- The alternative (competing risks) is described only superficially and then dismissed largely for “complexity/communication,” not for statistical reasons.
- No clear statement of the causal/counterfactual status of the estimand appears.

### 2. Cohort construction
Fatal arithmetic and accounting errors:
- Invents an unsubstantiated “No Credit Review = 3 600” exclusion.
- Then lists the original business aggregates (9 100 + 5 700 + 2 900 + 5 100 + 21 600 + 3 600 = 48 000) as sub-categories of the remaining 44 400, which is impossible.
- Primary-analysis cohort declaration (25 200) therefore rests on inconsistent counts.
- Linkage-table coverage (~60 %) and partner-originated missing-feed risk are mentioned but never turned into explicit inclusion/exclusion or weighting rules.
- As-of/maturity cut-offs mix end-time with activation-time and are applied inconsistently later.

A cohort-flow table that does not reconcile to the given 48 000 is a core failure of the required deliverable.

### 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy
Distinguishes several mechanisms (good) but then mishandles them:
- States that immature activated cases are “right-censored at prediction time.” For a time-to-default-from-activation analysis the censoring time must be measured from activation; censoring at the earlier prediction time is incorrect.
- IPCW is proposed only after restricting to the activated subpopulation, so it cannot correct activation selection.
- Permanently missing partner feeds and partial transfer linkage are acknowledged as MNAR yet receive no concrete primary-analysis treatment (only vague sensitivity bullets).
- Testability claims are generic; no design-specific partial tests (e.g., linked vs unlinked transfers, pre- vs post-migration label delay) are defined.

### 4. Prefix extraction
Conceptually acceptable (fixed-count, elapsed-time, and first-Credit-Review prefixes; rename mapping; duplicate drop). Weaknesses:
- Primary prediction time (first Credit Review) is not cleanly reconciled with the “first 3/5/7 events after start” options.
- Short-case padding and document-loop handling are asserted without concrete rules that preserve as-of semantics.
- No explicit guard against same-day snapshot leakage inside the prefix generator itself.

### 5. Process-aware features
Reasonable catalogue (control-flow, timing, rework, calendar/policy indicators). However:
- Resource experience is listed while the known extraction-time contamination is only foot-noted.
- Queue and historical-reference features are proposed without stating the separate data sources or as-of joins required.
- No distinction is made between features that are valid only conditional on reaching Credit Review versus features usable for all applications.

### 6. Leakage audit

Minor omissions: no row for `label_available_at` interaction with maturity filtering, weak treatment of same-day `documents_total`, and no audit of the invented activity-name mapping.

### 7. Validation design
Temporal split by case start is the right instinct and the critique of random splits is solid. Defects:

- Maturity filtering is applied only to val/test while train is allowed censored cases, but the survival-versus-binary metric set is never aligned to a single primary analysis.
- Calibration and subgroup plans are listed but not tied to the policy-change or missing-feed mechanisms.

### 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary
One of the stronger sections: clearly separates predictive association from counterfactual default risk and lists credible deployment feedback loops. Still under-states the central limitation that the primary estimand is undefined for the majority of applications that never activate.

### 9. PM4Py-oriented skeleton
The code attempts many required pieces (format_dataframe, temporal split, prefix call, leakage check, cohort counts) but contains numerous concrete defects that would prevent correct execution or correct statistics:
- `label_mature` uses case end-time rather than activation time + 90 + delay.
- Outcome logic assigns “non_default” to immature activated cases.
- Temporal-split indexing (`val_cases[case_info.loc[val_cases.index \ldots]]`) is broken.
- `case_info.loc[x, \ldots]` policy-period lookup assumes an index that was never set.
- Several PM4Py calls are non-existent or mis-parameterized (`get_variants_sorted`, fragile `get_prefixes_from_log` usage, DFG computed but unused).
- No guard that post-prediction columns are dropped before feature materialization beyond a late filter.
- Comments promising survival/competing-risk hooks are present but the data structures needed for them (activation timestamps, censoring times, competing-event indicators) are never built.

Because the skeleton is offered as the concrete implementation of the design, these bugs are not cosmetic.

### Summary judgment
The response demonstrates awareness of selective labels and leakage, yet fails basic cohort reconciliation, conditions the primary estimand on future activation without an operational path, mis-specifies censoring times, and supplies non-working code for the central data-engineering steps. Under the instructed hypercritical standard these are major, not minor, defects. The work is therefore only partially successful and receives a 4.0.
