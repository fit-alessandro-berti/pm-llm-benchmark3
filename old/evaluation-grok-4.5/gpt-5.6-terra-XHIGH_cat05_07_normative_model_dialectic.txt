## Evaluation

This is a strong, professional conformance review: correct required headings, genuine steelmanning of both sides, traceable case reasoning, and a serious layered redesign. ### What is genuinely strong

### Material defects (score drivers)

**1. Internal inconsistency: burden of proof vs SUP-702 (serious)**  
§1 places the burden on operations to prove authority to derogate, predicates, and safeguards; PD-3’s power to waive CS-9 is explicitly **unproved**, and automated master-data validation is **unproved**. Under that standard, SUP-702 is not a justified exception. Classifying it **MIXED** because it “fits pilot predicates” reintroduces the split-the-difference move the prompt forbids. Consistent options: **NONCOMPLIANT** (failed exception burden) or a narrow indeterminate on *authority evidence only*—not a soft middle that half-credits an unproven waiver. This undercuts §1’s own method.

**2. Classification hygiene slips**  
- **SUP-711 as COMPLIANT** is too clean. The observed log is defective; the defensible primary label is data-quality (with business conformance *after* deterministic dedupe), not straight COMPLIANT.  
- **SUP-715 as NONCOMPLIANT** over-reads the record: materials establish missing **disclosure**, not that the approval is void or that the relationship is banned. Better as policy/control failure with modest confidence, or MIXED only if dual control-flow vs COI tracks are both first-class—here control-flow is fine.  
- **SUP-704 at Medium-High** while cumulative spend before retrospective approval is still missing is slightly hot given FD-7’s cap is load-bearing.  
- Allowed labels are used, but **MIXED** on 702 functions as residual uncertainty dumping, contrary to “do not use mixed as a substitute for analysis.”

**3. M0/CS-9 semantics: sharp then under-used**  
Correct that M0 forces `Quality_Review` before *all* XOR exits (including reject) while CS-9 ties quality to activation. That insight should have disciplined 709 and the revised standard-reject path more tightly; the write-up still oscillates between “M0 over-restrictive” and keeping quality-before-reject on the standard branch without a crisp normative choice.

**4. POWL / code: real technical and fidelity issues**  
- **Same activity, many `Transition` instances** across XOR arms is a common pattern but fragile for conformance semantics unless `route_id` is mandatory everywhere; the prose says this, the model does not enforce it.  
- **`Request_More_Info` only on the standard arm** (and only as concurrent optional LOOP) is a weak approximation: no redo of quality after new evidence, no unresolved-request guard, absent on auto/emergency/reactivation where the same operational behavior appears in aggregates (47 cases). Stating “awkward in POWL” once does not fix under-coverage.  
- **LOOP(do=requestevidence, redo=) + XOR(, loop)** is a defensible 0..* sketch, but it does not implement “repeatable until decision” against the checks in a controlled order; calling checksinfo “interleaving” overclaims what the partial order guarantees.  
- **Emergency path** collapses daily monitoring into one abstract event—acceptable only with hard data rules; those rules are listed narratively, not as an operable companion spec (no schemas/pseudocode checks).  
- **SilentTransition / Operator.LOOP** assumptions are version-sensitive; “executable” is asserted without a minimal build sanity pattern. Not fatal, but overconfident.  
- Withdrawal/lead/reactivation arms are structurally present but **eligibility to enter an arm is entirely outside the model** with no DECLARE/log-skeleton companion fragment—§6 name-drops DECLARE then never specifies a single constraint (e.g., `Activate`  `Sanctions` succeeded; `Auto_Approve`  pilot window; `Notify` before `Close` with CRM join).
