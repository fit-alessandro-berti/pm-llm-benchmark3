## Grading rationale (hypercritical)

### What is strong
Sections 1–8 are substantively aligned with the brief: activation is treated as selective observation (not free non-default labels); primary estimand is conditional on activation; multi-state alternative is clearly a different question; cohort flow accounts for all 48,000 with the right partition; censoring types are separated; leakage table covers the mandated fields; validation is temporal and explains why random splits fail; limitations and deployment feedback loops are credible. This is clearly above a weak “train on 21,600 and call the rest 0” design.

### Major deductions

**Section 9 fails hard requirements and has concrete defects (largest hit)**  
- The brief requires demonstrating `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`. The skeleton never calls it; it only hand-rolls a loop and waves at the API in comments. That is a direct miss on a named required element.  
- `mature_label` uses `case_agg["default_90d"]` but `default_90d` is never aggregated/merged into `case_agg`—the maturity engine is broken as written.  
- `open_flag` logic is nonsensical (`"Account Activated" not in s.name`). Residual open count printing will not match the Section 2 table.  
- No coherent case-level feature matrix joined to the target for the 21,600; prefixes are concatenated but labels, splits, and model-ready rows are not actually produced.  
- `documents_total` and corrected resource experience are audited in prose and then absent from the implementation path.  
- `partner_origin` is faked from `case:region`.  
These are not polish issues; they make the “concrete enough to implement” skeleton unreliable.

**Section 2 maturity rules are internally inconsistent**  
Text mixes “activation  15 Nov 2025” (90d window only), “~12 Oct 2025” (90d + 35d feed), and code’s `extract_date - (90+35)`. As-of rules for delayed feed vs ordinary right-censoring blur in places. Minor but real for a study judged on as-of correctness.

**Prefix rule ambiguity (Section 4)**  
Fixed-count vs 72h elapsed is described as “whichever first,” but boundary semantics when activation, short cases, and document loops interact are not fully pinned down (e.g., exact tie-break when the 3rd event is after 72h and is `Request Documents`). Usable, not airtight.

**Primary target vs business wording**  
Choosing conditional-on-activation is defensible and matches the brief’s warning against fake non-defaults, but the write-up still under-specifies how an “every application / first few events” product would gate or abstain pre-activation in deployment (Section 8 helps, but the operational contract is thinner than the estimand section implies).

**Secondary nits**  
- Multi-state alternative is conceptually good but thinner on prediction time, horizon alignment, and evaluation metrics than the primary.  
- DFG/variants/`extract_temporal_features_dataframe` are used on prefixes (good) but never clearly frozen to training-only reference data for test application (leakage-by-reference risk).  
- Sensitivity analyses are named more than operationalized (weights, linkage join keys, testable diagnostics).

### Why not lower
It does **not** commit the brief’s disqualifying errors (mislabel all omitted cases as non-default; train only on 21,600 with no cohort accounting; use post-outcome fields; primary random split). Conceptual design quality is high.

### Why not higher
Under the instructed strictness—minor inaccuracies and requirement misses must cut the score sharply—the broken/incomplete PM4Py skeleton, missing required prefix API demonstration, and maturity/as-of inconsistencies cap the grade well below “nearly flawless.” A top score needed executable cohortprefixno-leakagetemporal-split wiring that matches the prose.
