### Section 1 – Fundamental estimand errors (severe)
- The primary estimand “P72” is defined as the binary “1 if duration_or_age_h > 72 **or case is still open at cutoff**”. This treats every right-censored observation, including those with age  72 (S15=35 h, S31=23 h, S24=67 h, \ldots), as an already-observed breach. That is statistically incoherent and operationally false; an open case below the horizon is still at risk and must be censored, not scored as a failure. The text never corrects or qualifies this.
- Contribution rules contradict the definition and ordinary competing-risk logic:
  - S05 (withdrawn @ 27 h) is scored 0 for “not resolved by 72”. A withdrawal is a non-resolution; under any “P(not resolved by 72)” it must contribute 1 (or be handled via 1 – CIF_resolve).
  - S09 (open @ 92 h) is scored 1, which happens to be correct for the binary, but the same rule incorrectly scores short opens as 1.
  - CIF72 is described as “probability of resolving *after* 72 h” yet S09 is said to “contribute to CIF72 at 72 h (still open, treated as unresolved)” – nonsense for a cumulative incidence of the resolution event.
- Already-observed breaches (resolved > 72 or open > 72), administrative censoring (< 72), and competing terminal events are never cleanly separated. The parenthetical dismissal of the “no terminal disposition at 72 h” estimand is confused.

These are not minor wording issues; they reverse the meaning of the target quantities the entire benchmark asks to define first.
