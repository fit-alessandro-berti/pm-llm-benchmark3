## Section-by-section critique

### 1. Decision rule — strong frame, weak closure
**What works.** Gate A (stage eligibility) is exactly right and is the cleanest disqualification of I-03 in the required spirit. Gate C (material evidence change) and Gate D (imputation distrust) are operationally realistic. Segment bias corrections are applied in the right direction with widened intervals. The explicit refusal to treat supplied effect ranges as known causal effects, and the use of a lower-third planning point plus an attributability factor, are appropriate. Escalating marginal externality is a sophisticated and correct response to convex congestion.

**Fatal coherence problems.**
- **Gate B is self-contradictory in application.** The rule states cases with  10 h runway “are deferred.” I-04 has exactly 10.0 h and is selected for immediate intervention; I-08 has 8.0 h and is deferred. The gate is either binding or it is not; using it as optional flavour text while presenting it as a hard gate destroys rule integrity.
- **The priority score is not the thing that allocates.** Score = ·V  450  B_fa  S_marginal is stated as the funding rule (“funded only if its score stays positive at its own marginal slot price”). Computed pre-externality scores rank roughly I-04 (6790)  I-08 (2550) > I-02 (2000) > I-01 (1379)  I-09-discounted (1342) > …. The selected set is {I-04, I-02, I-09}. I-08 is openly acknowledged as the best *remaining* candidate after the three chosen slots, which means two lower-scoring cases were elevated over a higher-scoring one without a formal, pre-registered adjustment (urgency multiplier, option-value term, or hard runway constraint) appearing in the decision rule. Tie-break language (“shorter runway”) cannot carry a 2550 vs 1342 gap.
- **p\* is computed carefully and then largely unused.** Calibration produces decision probabilities, but the formal score never multiplies by or thresholds on p\*. Calibration theatre without a mechanical role in ranking is decoration.
- **NNLS 0.6 effect shrink and imputation 0.5–0.7 factors are judgement calls presented as if they were data.** Acceptable only if flagged as priors; they are not.
- Time cut-offs (2.0 h / 2.5 h), |p|  0.10, cool-down length, and the €15k untreated average are all free parameters. No sensitivity is shown.

### 2. Capacity allocation — complete table, indefensible portfolio
The table format meets the spec. I-03, I-05, and I-07 are handled in the spirit of the quality criteria. Reserve-one is consistent with both management preference and the fourth-slot externality math.

**Selection errors.**
- **I-04 at 10 h should not clear a deferral gate the author wrote.** Committing a same-day slot to a 10 h Strategic Enterprise case while a regulatory arrival is explicitly possible, and while I-02/I-09 have 2.5–3 h runways, needs a tighter justification than “highest EV.” Option value of waiting is mentioned rhetorically and then ignored.
- **I-08 exclusion is special pleading.** The conservative  already sits at the lower third; FA burden is already subtracted; the lower-bound “value-destroying” argument was already priced in. Re-using those facts to demote I-08 below I-02/I-09 double-counts downside and reveals that the author does not trust their own score.
- **I-09 as Tier 1 is an unmodelled VOI claim.** Learning value for a sparse segment is a legitimate consideration; it is not in the stated score. Listing “€1,342 discounted, €3,070 undiscounted — Tier 1” is having it both ways.
- **I-07 alternative instrument is good operationally** but sits outside the swarm policy; the answer never states how cheap-instrument capacity is governed or whether it consumes senior time that also has opportunity cost.
- Marginal externality schedule (€1,500/1,800/2,200/3,000) is invented without derivation from the 0.01–0.03 and 8–20 min ranges. Auditable arithmetic was required; this is not fully auditable.

### 3. Expected-impact table — right shape, inconsistent inputs
Three scenarios, rough arithmetic, and an honest adverse case that goes net-negative are all strengths. The spillover caveat (untreated queue may be high-value) is exactly the right humility.

**Problems.**
- **Base scenario silently drops the NNLS 0.6 factor** used in allocation. If planning used discounted effect, the base impact row should too. Using full midpoint 0.24 for I-09 in the table while using 0.216 × 0.6 in the decision is an apples-to-oranges move that inflates base net benefit.
- Optimistic “Confidence: Low” confuses scenario plausibility with estimate confidence; the column becomes hard to interpret.
- FA burden is charged on an ad-hoc declining schedule (~€1k / ~€2k / €3.4k) with no link to alert precision or historical false-positive rates.
- Direct breaches avoided are summed as if independent; with shared specialists and shared queue this is optimistic.
- Spillover valuation still rests on an assumed €15k average and a fixed “six untreated cases” even though the live board shows fewer diagnosis-stage cases. The author notes the weakness and then does nothing to tighten it for *this* portfolio.

### 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy — strongest section
Cool-down, four-part evidence rule, escalation to instrument review rather than louder swarming, re-entry without penalty, and 1.5× price on repeat swarms are all coherent and deployable. The I-05 vs I-07 contrast is precise, correctly refuses clock-driven repetition, and correctly refuses to swarm I-07 for runway/value reasons while still extracting value from the new log. This section meets the quality bar.

Minor nits: 2-hour cool-down and |p|  0.10 are unmotivated constants; time-decay exclusion needs a concrete test for “driven by new features rather than shrinking deadline” or it will be gamed or ignored.

### 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks — comprehensive, partly unintegrated
All five required themes are covered with concrete mechanisms (25% SME floor, V-cap at €40k, cheap-instrument tier, NNLS quota + mechanism coding, 60-day expiry on calibration offsets, treatment feature + untreated-only training, mid-band randomised holdout, segment spillover ledger, compensating priority bonus). This is well above typical answers.

**Integration failures.**
- V-cap at €40k is proposed here but was **not** applied in §1–2. Had it been, I-04 and especially I-08 would have reordered. A safeguard that would have changed today’s slate cannot be left as future work without comment.
- Randomised holdout is the right causal strategy; parking it only in fairness (rather than in the decision rule and monitoring as a first-class capacity tax) underweights it.
- “At least 25% of slots to SME/low-value” is stated without reconciling it against today’s slate (0/3 selected are SME). Acknowledge the violation or apply it.
- Risk of spillover landing on untreated low-value segments is correctly named; the live allocation (heavy SE + NNLS) is not checked against that risk in real time.

### 6. Monitoring and recalibration — thorough, slightly generic
Operational, predictive (untreated-only), causal (holdout > capacity exhaustion > RDD > matching), spillover, fairness, drift, and change-control cadence are all present. Quarterly instrument review is the right meta-question. Expiry of calibration offsets and “retain previous rule for counterfactual tracking” are strong.

**Gaps.**
- No explicit stopping rule tied to the adverse scenario (when do we suspend swarms if realised net impact tracks the €3k to €6k world?).
- No pre-registered metric for “Gate B deferral compliance” or “score-vs-tier concordance” — ironic given those were the failure modes today.
- Causal estimation plan does not say how contamination from the “cheap alternative instruments” (senior reads, deadline extensions) will be handled.
- Sample-size triggers (“ 50 NNLS”, “40+ treated”) are reasonable but unsupported.

## Cross-cutting logical flaws (main score drivers)
1. **Rule–allocation inconsistency** on I-04 (deferral gate), I-08 (score rank), and I-09 (tier inflation). Decision-theoretic coherence was an explicit grading criterion; this is the largest single defect.
2. **Double use of downside** on I-08 (conservative  already encodes lower-bound risk; narrative re-uses it to override rank).
3. **Unmodelled objectives** (VOI for NNLS, pure urgency) smuggled in at allocation time rather than written into the score.
4. **Scenario table parameters  planning parameters** (NNLS discount).
5. **Fairness instruments that would change today’s decision are not applied today.**
6. **Ornamental calibration:** p\* adjustments do not enter the funding formula.
7. **Free parameters without sensitivity** (externality schedule, time gates, cool-down, untreated €15k).

- Structural refusal of I-03 despite p_now = 0.82 and €40k.
- Evidence-based split of I-05 (suppress) vs I-07 (new log, wrong instrument).
- Explicit spillover pricing and a net-negative adverse scenario.
- Treatment-as-feature + untreated-only training + mid-band randomisation (correct causal posture).
- Concrete fairness floors/quotas/ledgers rather than principles.
- Reserve slot justified by both policy preference *and* marginal math.
- Clear writing and full coverage of the six required sections.
