### Overall

### Critical flaws (score drivers)

**1. First-year four-module budget is asserted and then broken (major).**  
Constraints allow **at most four modules in year 1**. Section 7 recommends exactly four: A5, A4, A6, A12 and claims the budget “is respected.” Section 6 then schedules **A5, A2, A4, A6, A12, A7, A3, A9, A10** inside months 0–12, plus A1/A8/A11 only deferred. That is not a phased subset of four; it is a different, larger program. Calling A2 a Phase-1 deploy while excluding it from the recommended four makes the portfolio definition non-operational. This is a direct failure on a hard constraint the answer itself cites.

**2. Recommended portfolio never gets its own process-level effects (major for required structure).**  
Section 4 analyzes Q, B, and C only. The chosen set is **B with A7 swapped for A12** (Hybrid D). Effects of dropping valuation triage and adding the control workbench—on cycle time, the 58h sync, provisional signing, four-eyes, and analyst load—are never stated as a unit. Judges cannot verify the recommended portfolio against the required effect dimensions without stitching fragments and guessing.

**3. Deep-integration accounting is inconsistent.**  
The answer says only two deep integrations in six months, labels A6 and A12 as those two, then still brings **A3 and A9** live in months 9–12 (still year 1) without reconciling “two deep ever” vs “two deep in H1” vs module count. A4 is waved through as “light/medium” to protect the story; that is convenient, not evidenced. A12 is deep in one place and used to satisfy compliance while A3 (also deep, high FN risk) is both deferred and then deployed in-year.

**4. Presentation corruption undermines precision claims.**  
Repeated stripped operators and broken quantities (“33002800”, “36507300”, “1188011680”, “load  (A4)”, “short-term , then ”, “KYC wait , Contract…”) make several “expected effects” lines unreadable. For a deliverable that sells quantitative tail reductions (−25–35% cycle time, cut mismatch wait to ~10–12h, remediation 8.5%→3–4%), unreadable arithmetic is not a cosmetic issue.

**5. Over-precise impact numbers without method.**  
Figures such as −25–35% Equipment-finance cycle time, ~30–40% of the 20h normalize wait, A3 auto-clearing “~60% low-risk,” and remediation “~3–4%” are presented as expected effects with little scenario logic (mix of Micro/SME/High-exposure, concurrency, committee weekly cadence). Under strict grading, invented precision counts against portfolio reasoning quality.
