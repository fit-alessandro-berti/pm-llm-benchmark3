## Critical failures

### 1. Fabricated / inconsistent numbers (major)

Utilization and queue figures are asserted as if measured, but they are not in the data and often break capacity math:

- **S-1**: Pre-analytical “118%”, prep “125%”, machines “133%”, medical validation “140%”, queues “>20h / >30h” — none are derived from the peak metrics (pre-analytical peak is already 96% / 9.5h median; medical validators 93% / 22h). A +45% volume and mix shift is never reconciled to FTE or station rates.
- **S-2**: M2 at “170%” with M1 down is hand-waved; recovery “whiplash” to tech val 155% then medical 120% is narrative, not tied to DFG path volumes.
- **S-3**: Medical validation “170% / 48h” and tech val rework “>15% because technologists redo borderline results” is speculative behavioral claim with no log support.
- **S-4**: “every 1% increase in wave amplitude adds 0.7% to next-day Urgent cycle time” is an unsupported elasticity claim.
- **R-A**: “cuts recollection 760→300 normal; 780→350 peak” and “~7% assay time / 0.8 FTE” are pure invention. Peak recollection demand is *higher* relative to volume (780/9730 ≈ 8.0% vs 760/18120 ≈ 4.2%); a 2h triage does not automatically halve loops without rejection/false-positive analysis.
- Trade-off table percentages (−8%, −20%, SLA met, etc.) are almost entirely unearned.

Hypercritical standard: once the answer pretends precision it does not have, credibility of the whole redesign drops sharply.

### 2. Misuse of candidate interventions and conflict hints

- **I4** is “cross-train two technical validators to do **accession correction**.” **R-C / P-3** then have those same people flip to **medical validation** (“100% on Medical validation”). That is not I4, not justified by the 4-week tech-val cross-train note, and invents clinical privilege expansion. Medical validation is a different licensed skill pool in the problem.
- **I1** (strict Urgent-first) is mostly avoided in spirit via R-D, which is good, but the package never seriously stress-tests starvation of Routine/Complex under P-1’s “M1 reserve 25%→40%” plus micro-batches of 6.
- **I2** (batch 12→24) is largely absent; good given conflict hints, but the answer never explains rejection of a manager candidate that the brief flags as important.
- **I3** modified to “25% of M1 runtime reserved” is reasonable, but “M2 keeps mixed work to balance repeat risk” undersells the given fact that **Repeat-after-failure clusters after night shifts and M2 usage**. Dedicating or loading M2 without a repeat-mitigation control is a material omission.
- **I9** ignored without comment; **I10** used only in failure protocols — acceptable, but partner 12h transport vs Urgent 18h SLA is barely treated (P-2 ships Complex overflow; Urgent penalty risk under assay outage is weak).

### 3. Scenario set is thin relative to the brief

- S-3’s “elective work postponed elsewhere → Complex 18%” is an unmotivated side assumption.

### 4. Bottleneck-migration analysis: structure yes, evidence no

Strengths: ordered cascades (upstream WIP → release surge → downstream validation) and the S-2 catch-up whiplash idea match good process thinking.

Weaknesses:

- Barely uses **frequency DFG** path counts (e.g., peak tech→medical 3420 vs tech→release 4050; hold for specialist 610; batch wait 1180; repeat assay 1260). Migration claims should cite these edges.
- Peak **performance DFG** edges (hold→medical 18/48h, tech→medical 8/24h, batch wait→assay 9/22h, recollection→receive 42/60h) are almost unused as quantitative anchors.
- Variant facts underused: Urgent batch bypass falls 74%→58% in peak (fragmentation vs priority trade-off); Complex 3.1× medical / 2.4× specialist; recollection consumes clerk capacity explaining errors.
- “Relieving any of the first three moves bottleneck to medical validation in two shifts” is a slogan, not a migration chain tied to who exits which path.

## Why not higher (hypercritical)

A strong answer would: (1) anchor every scenario to peak DFG frequencies and performance edges; (2) keep medical vs technical vs accession skills distinct; (3) quantify with transparent, approximate arithmetic from given utilizations/queues rather than fake 133%/170%; (4) show explicit losers under each policy (Routine p90, Complex specialist path, rework from M2); (5) resolve I1–I10 conflicts with clear on/off design; (6) produce a readable trade-off table with honest negative cells.

This answer performs like a polished template filled with unsupported precision and one disqualifying resource-role error (tech validators as medical validators). Under “utmost strictness,” that caps the score in the low-to-mid range.
