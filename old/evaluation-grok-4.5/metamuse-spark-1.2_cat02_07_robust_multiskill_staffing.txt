### Critical failures in capacity analysis (§2)
Daily demand is stated as “42 cases.” Summing expected arrivals gives \(7+9+7+10+6+3 = 42\), fine. Diagnostic loads (Mech 476, Elec 797, Soft 494) are presented as if derived, but the work is not shown at activity-mix granularity in a way that can be audited end-to-end, and several capacity numbers are wrong or internally inconsistent.

Examples of hard errors:
- **Software capacity 10–12:** The text correctly notes Deepa has no Software, then still writes a confused “197+0” path while using Soft cap ~197. Farah’s Software multiplier is 1.40, so 120 min availability is \(120/1.40 \approx 86\) base-minutes, not a full 120. Chen: \(120/1.25 = 96\). Soft cap ≈ 182, not ~197. Small, but symptomatic.
- **Mechanical capacity 16–18 = 96:** Only Deepa remains for Mechanical. Available minutes ≈ 120 − break overlap; Deepa’s break is 14:00–14:30, so 16–18 is full 120 min. Cap = \(120/1.25 = 96\). OK. But **18–20 Mech cap = 0** is correct only if overtime is excluded; the narrative then treats evening ME/M work as structural failure without folding the stated 30-minute overtime consistently into the capacity table.
- **Intake daily capacity 2919 vs load 291:** With six people and long shifts this order of magnitude is plausible, but **block Intake caps (~345, 465, 620…)** are not derived transparently from who is on shift and their multipliers. Several look like rough guesses.
- **U2 “renormalized” loads:** Claiming Elec 12–14 becomes ~190–210 (+50%) after *doubling E and ES and renormalizing* is not a careful bound. Doubling E+ES then renormalizing does **not** double Electrical offered load; it increases E’s share while shrinking the total probability mass of other types. The stated “+45–50%” is directionally muddy and not shown with algebra.
- **U3 capacity removals:** “Elec −450, Soft −360, Val −409, Intake −409” treats Chen as 450 base-minutes of every skill at multiplier 1.0. Chen’s Software multiplier is **1.25**, so Software base-capacity removed is \(450/1.25 = 360\) (that part matches), Electrical at 1.00 removes 450 (OK), but Validation at 1.10 removes \(450/1.10 \approx 409\) only if the whole shift is Validation-eligible capacity counted in full in every skill’s pool simultaneously—an accounting double-count if the same 450 minutes are summed across skills as if additive system capacity. The daily skill caps are not mutually exclusive resource pools; presenting them as independent “caps” overstates total system capacity and understates contention.

The section correctly stresses **time-varying** capacity and the 16–18 Mechanical single-server problem, and U5’s Intake outage narrative is directionally sound. Those insights are real. They do not redeem the false precision of the load/cap tables.

### Scenario table and false empiricism (§4)
The comparison table is the heart of the grade. It reports tight ranges (e.g., HYBRID U5 P1_SLA 65–78%, SPECIAL base mean 85–110) attributed to “100 replications” / scaffold estimates with “±15–25% uncertainty.”

Problems:
1. **The Python scaffold is not a faithful DES** of the policies described in §3. It is a greedy per-case sequential assigner with broken parallel handling, almost no real preemption, weak break logic, and `random.poissonvariate` / gauss hacks. It cannot underwrite the table’s numeric ranges.
2. **§4 cites “100 replications”; §8 runs `n_rep=20`.** Internal inconsistency.
3. **Policy ranking is largely preconceived.** HYBRID wins nearly every robustness cell; POOL wins means; SPECIAL loses. That may be qualitatively defensible, but presenting it as measurement is misleading.
4. **P2_SLA discrimination is weak by the author’s own A8**, yet the table still prints fine-grained 96–99% vs 97–99% distinctions that the model cannot support.
5. **max_util and fairness** depend on utilization definitions that mix busy time without consistent treatment of handover/setup/overtime/breaks—then are reported to two digits.

Under strict grading, inventing operationally decisive metrics from a non-validating scaffold is worse than giving qualitative ordinal comparisons with explicit “not simulated” labels. The prompt allows estimates and bounds; it does not allow dressing scaffolding noise as scenario science.

### Python (§8) — decisive downgrade

What is delivered:
- Tables encoded: yes.
- Mechanisms “mentioned”: yes, with constants.
- **Faithful implementation: no.** Parallel ME/ES is approximated badly; second branch timing is contorted; preemption is essentially stubbed; breaks/overtime are crude; arrival Poisson path is buggy; U2 renormalization exists but load accounting in prose doesn’t match; HYBRID reserve is a thin filter, not “1 slot reserved per skill.”
- PM4Py calls are in a broad `try/except` that prints errors and continues—i.e., analysis is optional theater.
- Comments admit omitted DES mechanisms, which is good honesty, but then §4 still treats outputs as evidence.

A “well-specified scaffold” is allowed only if omitted mechanisms are identified **and** results are not over-claimed. Here omissions are identified in §8 and then ignored in §4–§5. That is a integrity problem in the analysis, not a coding style issue.
