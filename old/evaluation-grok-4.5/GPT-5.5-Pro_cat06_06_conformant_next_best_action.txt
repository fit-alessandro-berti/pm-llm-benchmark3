### Defects that materially lower the score

**1. Internal inconsistency on the top-ranked plan (serious for a rank-1 cell)**  
The table’s “Earliest plausible continuation” for Rank 1 is strictly sequential:

`… -> updated Analyst Review by R17 -> Alternate Screening Result -> Second Officer Review`

while the plan prose and §2 claim alternate screening runs *in parallel* with review preparation. That is not a cosmetic wording gap: if the second officer waits for the alternate screen, D loses much of the speed rationale that justified ranking it above pure C. The primary artifact (the ranked table) therefore undercuts the stated recommendation.

**2. S2/S4 switching rule is not operationalized**  
“Use S4 only if a firm near-term start is confirmed, otherwise S2” is left without a time box. Learning S4’s ETA is itself a timed action under a 90-minute hard deadline. Without an explicit rule (e.g., “if no confirmed S4 start 35–40 min within 5–10 minutes, commit to S2”), Rank 1 is not an executable policy; it is a preference statement. §5 discusses the 35 vs 65 counterfactuals well but does not close this control-loop gap.

**3. Wording error on timing**  
Rank 1 benefit text: “execute in **40–90 min after approval**.” The 40–90 minute window already *includes* review + recording + execution. “After approval” is wrong and inflates apparent slack.

**4. Rank-3 placement of F is weakly supported**  
F is labeled `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS` with only low-medium confidence, and the prompt never establishes that the rejection evidence threshold is met. Given P(ultimate approval | conformant review) = 0.55–0.75 and false-rejection harm up to €80k, putting conditional F above clearly conformant E (and as the third-best overall move) is aggressive. A stricter answer would rank “verify rejection authority/threshold” as a check, not park F at #3 while the enabling condition is unknown. The answer’s own hedges in §4 are better than its table rank.

**5. Process-trace idealization after a “clearing” re-screen**  
§2 asserts that if alternate screening resolves the fuzzy match and risk falls below 70, “second-review requirement may no longer be triggered.” That tracks the letter of rule 6, but the case has already left the Auto-Clear corridor (risk was 7872, analyst path taken, evidence cycle run). The answer should have been tighter about what *valid approval path* remains (analyst-only approve vs. still needing a documented disposition standard) rather than implying the control burden lightly lifts. Minor, but it is a conformance-edge softness.

Even in the corrected §2 tree, it is unclear whether S2/S4 begins reviewing *current* evidence immediately and folds in alternate-screen results only if they arrive in time, or waits.
