## Grading rationale (hypercritical)

The answer is long, structured exactly as required, and clearly stronger than a naïve “rank by \(p_\text{now}\)” baseline. It correctly refuses I-03, distinguishes I-05 from I-07, applies segment calibration adjustments in the right direction, discusses spillovers, and supplies extensive fairness/monitoring content. Those strengths prevent a collapse into the bottom half of the scale. However, under a strict decision-theoretic and operational standard the submission contains a central conceptual error, material allocation mistakes, internal inconsistencies, and several secondary flaws that collectively cap the score well below the excellent range.

### Critical failure: decision-theoretic incoherence (Sections 1–3)

The problem explicitly supplies the approximate objective  
\[
\text{expected direct benefit} \approx (\text{absolute risk reduction}) \times (\text{value of avoiding breach}) - \text{swarm cost} - \text{false-alarm burden}.
\]
Absolute risk reduction is already an absolute probability decrement; it must **not** be further multiplied by \(p_\text{now}\).

The answer’s priority score and all economic-benefit arithmetic do exactly that:
\[
(\text{adj\_}p) \times (\text{Value} - \text{FA burden}) \times (\text{midpoint effect}).
\]
Consequences:
- High-value, moderate-\(p\) cases (especially I-08) are systematically under-valued. Correct midpoint arithmetic on I-08 is roughly \(0.10\times 100{,}000 - 450 - 5{,}000 = +4{,}550\); the answer reports a large negative and discards it.
- “Direct breaches avoided” is sometimes treated as the sum of absolute effects (correct) and sometimes entangled with \(p\times\text{effect}\) (incorrect), producing inconsistent rows in the impact table.
- Calibration adjustments are applied to \(p\) and then multiplied into benefit, double-penalizing or double-rewarding segments.

This is not a minor notational slip; it is the core quantity the policy is supposed to optimize. Decision-theoretic coherence is therefore substantially compromised.

### Capacity allocation errors (Section 2)

- Four slots exist; management preference is to hold one in reserve \(\Rightarrow\) three usable slots. The answer intervenes on only two cases (I-01, I-04) and leaves a slot idle. I-02 (high value, Diagnosis Started, second alert, still inside the effective window) is an obvious third candidate that is merely placed on a “wait” list.
- Priority “tiers” are asserted narratively; the elaborate formula defined in Section 1 is never numerically evaluated for the ten cases. The table therefore cannot be audited against the stated rule.
- Spillover proxy inside the score uses the **treated** case’s own value—an incorrect externality term.
- Stage multiplier (1.2 for Diagnosis Started) is directionally debatable: the intervention window is closing, so urgency rises but expected effect may fall; the answer does not resolve the tension.
- I-06 and I-09 handling (data escalation / manual review) is reasonable; I-05 suppression is correct. These local strengths do not offset the global under-allocation and the flawed ranking engine.

### Expected-impact table (Section 3)

- Continues the \(p\times\text{effect}\) error, so the “Direct economic benefit” column is not credible.
- Intervention cost accounting is inconsistent (sometimes €450 is omitted from the per-case benefit line and only appears as a portfolio total).

Minor operational quibble: 90 min / 60 min windows are reasonable but not stress-tested against the 30-minute model refresh and the 1.5 h deadline on I-07.

Some metrics are unrealistically precise given the stated data-quality problems; the plan is slightly over-engineered relative to “operational realism.”

### Additional deductibles

- Observational \(p\) is repeatedly treated as if it can be plugged directly into an interventional value-of-information calculation after only a constant shift; the problem warns that \(p\) is not the probability under intervention.
- Effect ranges are acknowledged as uncertain yet mid-points are inserted into a point-score formula with little robustification (no regret, no lower-partial-moment, no explicit minimax tier).
- Verbose repetition and an extra summary table do not add decision value.
- Minor arithmetic and wording slips (e.g., “FalsAlarmBurden”, sign errors in places, “Tier 1.5”) reduce polish.

### Net assessment

The submission demonstrates genuine engagement with portfolio externalities, stage eligibility, repeated-alert logic, fairness, and monitoring—the dimensions that separate strong from weak answers. It is therefore well above a failing or purely heuristic response. At the same time, the central expected-value formula is wrong, capacity is left idle, I-08 is mis-ranked, priority scores are not computed, and confidence is overstated. Under a hypercritical standard that treats decision-theoretic coherence and operational tightness as non-negotiable, these are major defects. The result is a solid-but-flawed policy document, not a near-flawless one.
