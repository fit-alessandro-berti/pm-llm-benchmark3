## Grading rationale (hypercritical)

### What works
Sections 1–5 are substantively strong: clear stipulated/disputed split, usable burden-of-proof rules, genuinely adversarial briefs, a complete case matrix, and case-level calls that mostly avoid lazy “split the difference.” Severe cases (SUP-703, SUP-708) are treated as serious; pilot/emergency overlays and data defects are not ignored. Section 6’s layered architecture (POWL vs DECLARE-style vs data vs org controls) is directionally right.

### Major failures (drive the score down hard)

**1. Section 7 POWL misses mandatory semantics (critical)**  
Required minimums include an **emergency/provisional route** and behavior consistent with pilot/emergency overlays. The constructed POWL does **not** contain a provisional path where `Activate_Supplier` can precede `Quality_Review` / manual approval. Everything is forced through:

`Register  checks(SanctionsQuality)  request?  decision  notify  close`

That **cannot** express PD-3 (quality up to +2 days *after* activation) or FD-7 (provisional activation before quality/manual approval, sanctions-before-activate only). Emergency is deferred to tables/constraints while the control-flow model remains standard-path-only. That is a direct miss of the stated modeling requirement, not a minor omission.

**2. Ill-formed / unsafe POWL structure**  
`activate` is a **shared `Transition` instance** inside two XOR children (`approved_manual` and `approved_auto`). Reusing the same node object across alternative branches is a classic POWL mistake and undermines executability/semantics. A defensible model needs distinct activation nodes or a single activation *after* a resolved approval choice—not the same node owned by two alternatives.

**3. `Request_More_Info` approximation is weaker than claimed**  
An XOR of one `Request_More_Info` vs skip is not a repeatable loop; SUP-707 has *two* requests. The write-up admits POWL loop limits (good) but then oversells the approximation. A bounded XOR-unrolling (0/1/2) or an explicit loop operator pattern was available and not implemented cleanly. Dead/duplicate constructions (`request_optional` vs later `request_or_no`) add mess.

**4. “One executable Python code block” violated**  
Two separate Python blocks appear; the second is mostly empty schemas/`print` scaffolding. Much of the block is non-model noise (empty DataFrames, banners) rather than a tight executable revised POWL plus compact overlay tables. `SilentTransition(label=...)` is at best API-fragile.

**5. Prosecution brief under-delivers a required concession**  
Instructions: prosecution **must** acknowledge genuine model/data defects. The brief mostly weaponizes “obsolescence is no excuse” and only thinly concedes logging/model gaps. Defense’s required concession on real risk is better (SUP-703/708/post-expiry), so dialectical balance is uneven against the spec.

### Moderate issues
- **Overclaim:** Notify_Requester treated as CS-9-mandatory; N1 text given does not clearly mandate notification—only M0 does.  
- **Embellishment:** “Email evidence” of imminent permanent amendment for SUP-714 is stronger than source wording.  
- **Sample-rate rhetoric:** “~27% compliant of 15” treats a curated edge-case set like an operational compliance rate—sloppy system-level framing.  
- **Inconsistency:** notification missingness 27% vs 29%; severity/confidence sometimes asserted while still “pending” decisive facts.  
- **Section 5 bloat:** long restatement of the matrix without tightening judgments; a few classifications are reasonable but under-argued relative to authority (e.g., SUP-713 “COMPLIANT” despite post-hoc mapping).  
- **Reactivation labels** proliferate parallel alphabets instead of one scoped model with silent/case-type guards—workable as architecture, weak as a single normative POWL.

### Minor issues
- Repetition across §§2–5; matrix cells occasionally argue both ways without a crisp dominant defect type.  
- Resource/COI cases correctly pulled out of pure control-flow, but M0-vs-CS-9 “compliance” language occasionally blurs model conformance vs policy breach.  
- Aggregate pilot stats used well; less discipline on what remains **unproved** (transaction timestamps for SUP-705/708).

### Score calibration
Analytical core is roughly high-6 / low-7 work. The deliverable is **not** near-flawless: missing emergency route in POWL, wrong forced quality-before-activate ordering for authorized overlays, shared-node construction error, dual code blocks, and weak prosecution concession on model/data defects are disqualifying for anything in the 8–10 band under the stated strict standard. Remaining clarity and matrix quality prevent a collapse into the mid-5s.
