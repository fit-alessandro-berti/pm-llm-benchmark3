**6.5**

The answer is structurally complete and legally careful on the points that matter most: A and B are ranked last and cannot be rescued by a favorable forecast; screening, SAR, and self-certification are not treated as substitutes for independent second-officer review; G is correctly left as an unenabled exception; and P-901 is at least acknowledged. That is the skeleton of a strong solution. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading there are material ranking errors, an internal contradiction on the C/D tradeoff, missing arithmetic, and several places where the write-up is smoother than the analysis.

**Ranking and plan design.** The single best feasible plan is not in the table. Alternate screening takes 15–30 minutes; S2 review takes 35–75 minutes. Those intervals are compatible. Starting the vendor call immediately while sending the file to S2 is almost strictly better than pure C: it uses one scarce API call, can arrive before S2 decides, and does not postpone the mandatory review. Rank 1 is pure C. Rank 2 waits on S4 and only falls back to S2. Section 6 then quietly adds concurrent screening “only if it can complete concurrently,” which, given the durations, it can. The official ranking and the final recommendation disagree. That is not a presentation nit. The task explicitly allows composite plans and asks for sequence, concurrency, and fallback to be explicit.

F is also mis-ranked. The prompt’s Action F is reject now. The answer rewrites it as “reject only if an authorized person confirms the threshold; otherwise go to second review.” That rewrite is legally responsible and then gets credited as a third-best action. The rewritten object is a decision procedure that collapses into C/D whenever the missing predicate fails, which is the situation the prompt already flags as uncertain. Unconditional F, given a 0.55–0.75 observational approval rate and a €15k–€80k false-rejection band, should sit below any live second-review plan and arguably below E. Parking a sanitized F at rank 3 inflates it.

**The S4 sensitivity claim is the clearest logical error.** Section 5 says that if S4 is confirmed in 35 minutes, D “becomes approximately tied with, and may move above, C.” The arithmetic does not support that. Mid-to-upper review plus recording is 40–90 minutes after the reviewer starts. S4 at t=35 yields a 75–125 minute envelope against a 90-minute deadline. C remains the only conformant path with a realistic chance of finishing inside the window. D at 35 minutes still likely misses P-884’s deadline. D can overtake C only if P-901’s expected interruption cost exceeds P-884’s timing benefit. That comparison is never made.

The materials for it exist and are unused. P-884’s timing value is €20k–€35k. P-901’s breach increment is 0.12–0.28. If P-901’s deadline loss were in the same band, the implied spillover is on the order of a few thousand to about €10k — smaller than P-884’s timing stake, not larger. P-901’s loss-given-breach is unknown, so the ranking of C over D is under-justified either way. Asserting both “C first” and “D may pass C at 35 minutes” without an expected-cost argument is a hole, not a nuance.

**C is oversold as a deadline save, and the P-901 cost is misstated.** C’s own envelope is 40–90 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. The upper tail meets or misses the deadline. The 45-minute interruption figure is treated as fixed even though review is 35–75 minutes. If S2 takes 75 minutes, P-901 is blocked longer than 45 minutes and the 0.12–0.28 breach increment is a lower bound. The joint failure mode — P-884 still late and P-901 damaged — is not in the trees or the harm column. That tail is exactly why a time-boxed S2 pull, or screening-first with a hard interrupt threshold, is more robust than “interrupt now.”

**Counterfactuals are traces, not the analysis that was asked for.** Section 2 supplies compact trees for all seven actions, which is more than the “at least five” floor. They do not systematically separate what the process would permit, the likely continuation, P-884 benefit, P-884 harm, spillover, compliance/non-monetary risk, and intervention uncertainty. Uncertainties are appended as comments. The C tree has no deadline-miss branch, no longer-than-45-minute interruption branch, and no “S2 asks for more evidence” branch. The D tree correctly keeps second review after a weaker match, but it does not branch on Rule 6’s disjunction: an unresolved match *or* risk ≥ 70. A cleared match with risk still at 72 still requires a second officer. A cleared match *and* a refreshed score below 70 might not. That distinction is the only way re-screening can change the mandatory path, and it is blurred.

**Case facts are under-used.** The 12-minute-old score of 72 sits two points above the second-review threshold; refreshing it cannot retire Rule 6 while the 0.46 match remains unresolved. Section 6 still elevates a parallel score refresh. The 0.46 confidence is never interpreted. It is a gray-zone hit, which is an argument against rushing F and a concrete reason to spend one API call, not a decoration. The observational SLA range 0.48–0.64 is barely used as a baseline against which C’s residual breach probability could be judged. Transportability is stated well in Section 3 and then not applied to the claim that C “preserves” the €20k–€35k band.

**Conformance labeling is slightly too clean.** C is tagged `CONFORMANT NOW`. The prompt’s own constraint language is that C is conformant *if* S2 independently reviews the evidence. That is the `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS` label. Using the stronger label assumes the independence condition. G, A, B, D, and E are labeled correctly, and Section 4’s rule mapping is the strongest part of the paper: Rule 6/7/9 on A and B, screening as non-substitutive, SAR as non-authorizing and non-disclosable, deadline pressure as non-incident, and the explicit statement that even a 99% approval forecast cannot waive four-eyes. That section is why the score is not lower.

**Other strict deductions.** Benefit/harm table cells are narratives, not ranges. “High” confidence on A and B is ambiguous (confidence the action is good vs. confidence it is correctly condemned). Rule 3 auto-clear is omitted, which is obvious but part of a complete constraint map. R17’s post-validation Analyst Review — the next step in the stated evidence cycle — is skipped without comment; the prompt’s own note on C mostly licenses this, so it is a small clarity miss rather than a fatal one.

Net: the answer knows the compliance hierarchy and does not make the usual catastrophic mistake of optimizing P-884’s deadline with an unlawful shortcut. It still mis-ranks the live operational choice, contradicts itself on S4-at-35, fails to price the spillover it treats as decisive, and leaves the required counterfactual dimensions thinner than the rubric asked. That is a clearly incomplete expert answer, not a near-flawless one.
