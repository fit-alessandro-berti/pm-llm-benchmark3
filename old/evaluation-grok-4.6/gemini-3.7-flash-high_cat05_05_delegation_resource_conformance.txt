The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly event-time aware, but it is not close to flawless. Several classifications overstate what the evidence can support, a material delegation-scope issue is missed, and the PM4Py checks are not reliable as written.

**Delegation scope is under-analyzed.** D-QM-01 authorizes QM approval for **production-equipment** change controls only. **Identity cases are over-decided.** The prompt says there is no single canonical answer for ambiguous identity evidence. The answer repeatedly converts ambiguity into a finding:

- **CHG-506** is labeled `UNAUTHORIZED` / credential impersonation / “fraudulently utilized,” **High** confidence. Ben’s denial plus an office IP is not proof of a third-party actor. Benign alternatives (he stayed, clock skew, delayed commit) are listed and then effectively discarded. Primary label should have been `INDETERMINATE` or `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM`.
- **CHG-504** is `AUTHORIZED` and credential sharing is described as “refuted” by Asha’s statement and VPN telemetry. The brief says security **cannot** exclude credential sharing. Asha retains QM authority if she acted, but actor identity is not settled.

- **CHG-509** asserts D-QM-02 is “fully valid” because the principal was absent. Asha’s recorded leave ends 2026-08-14; 15 Aug is a weekend, not recorded leave. Weekend ≠ formal absence without argument.
