**6.4**

The answer is unusually complete and hits every named quality bar (I-03 is not swarmed, I-05 is not treated like I-07, spillovers are priced, calibration is segmented, labels are treated as endogenous). It is not nearly flawless. Several mistakes are load-bearing: they change who gets a slot, when, and why.

## Decision rule: coherent on paper, not the rule they run

The five-layer checklist is deployable and correctly refuses a probability threshold. Stage, repeat, and imputation gates are the right hard constraints. Treating supplied ARR bands as priors rather than causal effects is explicit and repeated.

The rule then breaks against itself.

Tier A requires `NDE_low > 0`, `NDE_mid > MSC`, **and** `T ≤ 4 h` or else waiting would forfeit eligibility. I-04 has 10 h in Triage. Under that rule it is Tier B. It is labeled Tier A and given slot 3 anyway. The long horizon is used both as a reason to wait for information (the written rule) and as a reason it can be queued later but still committed (the allocation). Those are different policies.

`ARR_adj = ARR × min(1, p_adj_mid / p_now)` is not implied by outcome miscalibration. A level bias in `p_now` after a Strategic Enterprise workflow redesign does not scale the swarm’s absolute effect one-for-one. Applying the haircut on top of the already-supplied ARR range double-penalizes I-02, I-04, and I-08. The conservatism is stated; the identification is not.

`NDE = ARR×V − 450 − (1−p)×FA` is more careful than the prompt’s unweighted FA term, but it is the wrong probability. If the burden is “swarmed and no breach,” the weight is `1 − (p − ARR)`, which is larger for high-ARR cases. The answer never flags this. Using `(1−p)` systematically favors already-likely breaches.

MSC ≈ €2,400 is treated as a known hurdle. The six spillover cases are not in the table; `V̄ ∈ €15k–€25k` is an assumption. If the untreated diagnosis pool contains I-08-class value, I-02 fails the hurdle the whole ranking rests on. MSC is also computed as `6 × s × V̄` as if delay cost were only extra breach probability times contract value, ignoring the stated 8–20 minutes of aggregate delay as a separate operational loss.

Several numbers that actually decide cases are free parameters without sensitivity: 50% haircut for `T < 2 h`, `Δp_adj ≥ 0.10`, `T ≤ 4 h`, NNLS ARR stretch `[0.7× lower, 1.1× upper]`, Public Sector `±0.03`. The NNLS stretch is also asymmetric and looks tuned so I-09 still clears `NDE_low > 0`.

## Allocation: I-03 is right; the live portfolio is not

I-03 is handled correctly, including a non-swarm path. I-05 is suppressed. I-07 is re-opened because of the log, then refused a slot. That trio is the strongest part of the paper.

I-08’s lower bound is wrong. With the answer’s own inputs (`ARR_adj ≈ 0.04`, `V = €100k`, `p_adj` mid `0.345`, `FA = €5k`), `NDE_low ≈ +€0.3k`, not `−€0.2k`. The negative sign is then used to “fail the robust gate.” The wait decision is still reasonable (long `T`, huge FA, small ARR), but the stated reason is an arithmetic error.

The serious operational failure is I-06 versus I-04.

I-06 is Diagnosis Started, 4.0 h left, Public Sector, jump 0.20→0.52, data unverified. The answer correctly withholds the slot for a ≤30-minute check. It then makes that case “first claimant of the reserve release” at **15:00**. At 15:00 I-06 has **1.0 h** left. That is the feasibility knife-edge the same document just defined; a 45-minute swarm leaves no resolution/validation time. The I-06 row says “4 h gives room to verify and still swarm.” The portfolio paragraph parks it three hours later. Both cannot be true.

Meanwhile I-04 (Triage, 10 h) is locked as slot 3 at 13:30. Under the answer’s own time-criticality-first order, a verified I-06 at 12:30 (Diagnosis Started, ~3.5 h) outranks I-04. Verification is supposed to finish before I-02’s 12:45 window, so a reshuffle was available and is not specified. The high-value long-horizon case is committed; the short-horizon diagnosis-stage case is attached to a reserve that arrives too late to use. That is the opposite of the written sequencing rule.

I-02 is committed on a knife-edge (`NDE_mid €2.5k` vs MSC `€2.4k`) under the SE overprediction the answer itself emphasizes, and is described as “negative under adverse.” Using it as slot 2 before I-06 is resolved is fragile.

I-09 as slot 1 is a defensible fairness/time-criticality choice and a weak causal choice. The document also calls NNLS swarms an “exploration bonus.” Measuring effects on a case with 2.5 h to SLA is not a free experiment; it is using the customer as the instrument. Section 6 then randomizes only borderline cases, so the segment they claim they will learn from is not the segment in the RCT. Those two designs do not fit together.

Public Sector “duty tier” is advertised as a non-monetary override and then not applied to I-01 or I-06. The only duty use is the unlisted regulatory reserve. Fine as management preference; not fine as a rule that was said to change ranking.

The 14:15–15:00 gap is unspecified. Three sequential 45-minute swarms end 14:15; the reserve “until 15:00” either idles both seniors or pulls them off the queue a second time. That second pull is an extra spillover the impact table does not price.

## Impact table: auditable, then over-interpreted

Optimistic / Base / Adverse arithmetic is mostly reconstructable and the base case honestly crosses zero at high spillover. That is the right epistemic posture.

Gaps: optimistic `V̄` is €15–20k, base is €15–25k; I-04 midpoint ARR flips between 0.155 and 0.16; “upper half” for I-09 is written 0.25–0.33 rather than 0.23–0.33. Small, but this section exists to be audited.

The extra “base + I-06 at 15:00” row is the same infeasible plan. Reporting a fourth slot as “+€0.4k marginal” is meaningless if the case is no longer treatable at the release time.

Linear spillover `3 × 6 × s` ignores congestion convexity except in Adverse. The prompt flags “strong dependence on congestion.” Adverse `s ≈ 0.03–0.05` is invented (base stated range is 0.01–0.03). Allowable as a stress, not as if it were in the brief.

No alternative three-case portfolios are scored. For a portfolio problem with ten lines, greedy-plus-hurdle is not enough to show I-09+I-02+I-04 dominates I-09+I-06+I-04 or two slots plus option value on I-04.

## Temporal policy: I-05 vs I-07 is the high point

Material-change, visible suppression, no accumulation of stale alerts, and a distinct non-slot action for I-07 are exactly what the brief asks for. Cool-down, re-entry, and exception logging are operationally real.

Minor: I-05 is Tier C in the table and “Tier C/B” in §4. Triggers for I-01/I-08/I-10 are reasonable but untested against the 0.10 `Δp` rule (a slow grind of +0.09 never re-enters).

## Fairness: concrete safeguards, several of them unused

The five required risks are all named with operational controls, not slogans. Treatment flags, untreated-only calibration, booking spillover to the segments that ate the delay, and a two-swarm congestion cap are the right shape.

The 25% below-median quota is proposed and then missed today (0/3; I-09 is above the eligible-set median) with no note that the daily book is in violation. The €40k value cap is illustrated and **not applied**; I-04 is still chosen on uncapped €60k. Safeguards that do not bind on the worked example are decorative.

“Uncertainty as width, not a downward shift” is a good anti-discrimination stance. Combined with slot 1 plus an exploration bonus, it over-corrects from “don’t starve NNLS” to “spend the scarcest resource where the effect is least known.”

Junior backfill is asserted, not costed, and would change both MSC and the adverse delay band if it were real.

## Monitoring: broad, partly fantasy

The menu (operational, predictive, causal, capacity, fairness, policy-versioned drift, pre-registered rule changes, four-week shadow) is the right menu.

Power is not faced. Four slots a day and a 20% withhold among *borderline* cases will not deliver “per-segment ARR CI within ±0.05” on a quarterly cadence, and will not estimate NNLS at all if NNLS cases are always time-critical treated. RD around a composite hurdle that also includes stage, `T`, duty tier, and manager override is not a clean cutoff. “100 unlabeled-untreated cases per segment” will not arrive on the same clock for NNLS and Public Sector.

“Change the threshold” in §6 sits oddly with §1, which claimed not to use a threshold. The live rule is a hurdle on `NDE_mid − MSC` plus discretionary gates; that is what would have to be locked and shadowed.

## Net

This is a strong exam answer with the skeleton of a real policy: eligibility ≠ predicted probability, repeats need new evidence, untreated cases are in the objective, I-03 is a different lever. Strictly graded, it is not a reliable policy memo. The written Tier A rule is not the rule used for I-04; I-08’s robust-gate failure is a bad number; I-06 is assigned to a clock at which the same document says the swarm no longer fits; I-02 is committed on a €100 expected-value margin under the segment bias the author just installed; and the fairness/learning apparatus is richer on the page than in the 12:00 book. Those are decision errors, not polish issues.
