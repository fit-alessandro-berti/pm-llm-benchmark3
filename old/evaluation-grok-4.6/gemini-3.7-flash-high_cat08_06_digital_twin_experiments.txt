5.3

The submission looks complete and professionally structured, but it is not reliable enough to advise a municipality. It repeatedly presents invented numbers as if they were estimated from the log, mis-applies the given demand multipliers, fits distributions that do not match the stated medians and percentiles, and then ships a simulator whose control flow and calendars contradict both the process tree and the design text.

**Required format and coverage.** All eight sections exist and stay on topic. That is the main strength. The design is not a naive independent-case replay: non-stationary arrivals, resource calendars, rework, latent objections, and cross-case coupling are at least named. The code avoids `pm4py.read_csv` and uses `pm4py.format_dataframe`. Those are necessary, not sufficient.

**Process-mining grounding is weaker than it appears.** The skeleton never calls `pm4py.parse_process_tree` and never explains why the simplified tree string cannot be parsed. `parse_and_validate_process_tree()` builds a tree by hand and is never invoked. Case arrival average is not computed. Inter-event times are stored as `transition_duration_hours`; service and waiting are not separated in the log, and the code does not state that limitation at the point of computation. `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` is not a stable public helper in the usual PM4Py surface (`pm4py.get_variants` is). Branching probabilities, loop probabilities, and parametric fits are written as if they were mined from 31,200 cases. Only a two-case excerpt is available. Treating those figures as empirical results is a grounding failure.

**The quantitative core does not add up.** Monday is 2.2× Wednesday, not 2.2× every other day. Peak months are 1.7× January–February, not 1.7× the annual mean. Applying both multipliers to a 57 cases/day average overstates peaks and mis-specifies the rest of the week, including weekends that the excerpt already shows are used. The 0.42 missing-document probability is the share of *simple* cases with missing docs (`19/45`), then applied to every case type. Completeness `LogNormal(3.22, 0.55)` has p90 ≈ 51 minutes, not 90. Planning, fire, and legal fits have the same error: σ is too small to hit the given p90. `random.weibullvariate(5.2, 0.85)` swaps shape and scale relative to the text and yields a median far below 4 hours. A digital twin whose sampling distributions do not recover the calibration targets cannot be used for intervention ranking.

**Control flow and calendars are inconsistent.** The process tree places public notice *after* the parallel reviews and *before* inspection. The engine runs notice concurrently with planning/fire/environment, which shortens the critical path and contaminates D5 and D9. Completeness rework, inspection rework, planning rework, legal rework, and appeals are flattened into coin flips. `ResourceCalendarManager.get_effective_capacity` is dead code: SimPy resources keep fixed capacity, so Monday clerk diversion, 1.8 environmental FTE, council-week legal shutdown, and the peak inspector are not actually binding. Cases can seize a resource off-shift and hold it until Monday. Inspectors are created with `fte + 2` slots in every scenario. District batch flushing uses `case.submit_time` rather than time of entry to the inspection buffer, so long cases flush immediately.

**Cross-case dependence is advertised, then dropped.** The writeup specifies a Hawkes architect process, spatial objection contagion, and related-case bundling. Arrivals are an independent modulated Poisson with a random `architect_id`. Contagion is absent. D6’s aging reserved planner is a flag with no dispatcher. D7 is specified as appointment slots and implemented as a Monday quota. D2 ignores prior violations. D1’s extra pre-submission delay appears in the experiment table and not in the engine. The experiment runner executes one replication, one seed, and only a subset of D1–D10/stress cells, despite a 35-replication CRN plan.

**Assumptions and missing data are overclaimed.** Inferred wait/service splits are not “unbiased.” Notice duration is not an “exact proxy” for unobserved objections; the mixture-model idea is right, the wording is not. Fabricated baselines (38.5d median, 68.2% simple SLA, 94.2% inspector utilization) are reported as facts. Decision targets are physically implausible: a 14-day statutory notice, 18% new-residential fire+notice cases with median 48d, and commercial/complex tails at 130–260d cannot support a system median ≤22d, p90 ≤48d, or 98% of simple cases inside 15 days.

**Experimentation and uncertainty are better on paper than in substance.** Welch warmup, multi-year horizon, CRN streams, BCa intervals, and Sobol on latent objection rate and the wait/service split are the right checklist. They are not connected to implementable estimators. A 3% relative half-width on the p90 of a heavy-tailed cycle time is not something N=35 will deliver. KS tests at α=0.05 on tens of thousands of cases will reject almost any calibrated model; that is not a serious validation gate. Wasserstein <2 days, R²≥0.96, and MAPE≤4.5% are arbitrary precision theater.

**What remains salvageable.** The entity/state list, fork-join reviews, latent objection mixture, calendar table, D3 travel-versus-wait trade-off, D4 peak capacity, combined D10, and three stress scenarios are the correct *kind* of design. The answer does not claim a simulated optimum. That is not enough. As written, the twin would rank policies from a mis-scaled arrival process, the wrong critical path, unused calendars, and distributions that miss the empirical tails. Under strict grading, that is a mid-fail, not a near-miss.
