**4.4**

The answer has the right skeleton and a few sound instincts, but it is not a reliable compliance assessment. It repeatedly contradicts its own premises, misapplies time and thresholds, and clears or half-clears purchases that its stated method would have to fail.

**Consistency is the central failure.** Section 1 says board policy outranks silent overlays, ambiguities are resolved against waiver of core controls, and later approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment control. The hierarchy then ranks A1/A3 (and, in places, A2) as non-overridable. The rule catalogue and pseudo-logic immediately disagree: R2’s exception column treats ECD-2 C2 as a Compliance defeater, while the pseudo-logic says C2 does **not** waive Compliance; Finance is “normally” required unless ECD-2 or the CFO email overrides it, after the hierarchy said ECD-2 probably cannot override A2. The case table then waives or treats as cured the same controls the method refused to waive. Section 7 reverses the case holdings again (Finance/Compliance/SoD cannot be waived without the CRO). That is not issue-specific hierarchy. It is three incompatible theories presented as one.

**Time is mishandled in the first case, which is disqualifying on this rubric.** P-501’s commitment is 08:50 on 14 July. ECD-2 starts at 09:00. ON-44 makes ERP stamps late, not early, so the real act is even more clearly pre-directive. The answer still scores commit-before-PO as “R4 allowed under ECD-2,” lists ECD-2-era evidence (“supplier ack 10:15”) that belongs to P-502, and calls the file COMPLIANT with **high** confidence. That is a category error, not a close call. B4 is also misread: substitution requires an **absent** Plant Manager **and** active delegation. A reachable, non-delegating Plant Manager is a failed exception condition, not a “logging defect.” Post-commitment Plant/Finance approvals are exactly what A6 says may mitigate and do not automatically cure. The answer’s own R6 requires those approvals **before** commitment, then treats after-the-fact entries as satisfying R6. P-501 should have been NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE. COMPLIANT / High is indefensible on the answer’s own rules.

**Several other classifications do not follow from the stated interpretation.**

- **P-502 (JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION):** New supplier, EUR 62k, 14 July — so A3 and A2 both attach **before** commitment, and the CFO email does not yet exist. Compliance at 18:30 and Finance at 13:05 are late. The table lists R2 as **satisfied** even though R2 is a pre-commitment rule and the answer elsewhere says A3 is not waived. If A3 survives ECD-2, this is NONCOMPLIANT with possible mitigation, not a justified exception. If C4’s “supplier-onboarding” swallows A3, the hierarchy had to say so. It said the opposite.
- **P-503:** NONCOMPLIANT is the right primary call, but the analysis misses the cleanest GPIP defect: EUR 140k requires Compliance **before** commitment (A3). The deviations column never names it. The same row treats ECD-2 timing rules as satisfied while asserting the backup-line purchase is outside ECD-2 scope. That is internally incoherent.
- **P-504:** Out-of-scope NONCOMPLIANT is correct. Applying Plant/Shift-Lead rules to EUR 24k is not; B2’s floor is EUR 25k.
- **P-505:** Using the 17:59:31 supplier ack over the 18:06 ERP stamp is the one piece of real evidential discipline in the paper. The controlling interpretation is still wrong: A3 is not triggered (EUR 88k, approved supplier). The live issue is A2/B2 Finance before commitment, plus whether D1 still covers a minute-before-expiry commit. Calling it COMPLIANT without deciding A2 is hand-waving.
- **P-506:** NONCOMPLIANT after expiry is sound. The verbal-extension point is handled properly.
- **P-507:** JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is only as strong as a contested reading of D1. Medium confidence is fair; §6 then undoes the better evidence move by treating ERP 14:30 as a late-authorization defect after the case table relied on the 12:40 signed PDF, which is inside C3’s four-hour window.
- **P-508:** D1’s sufficiency cap is EUR 150k. This purchase is EUR 155k. D2 adds Legal “as well”; it does not clearly extend the operational-approval waiver. High-confidence JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION suppresses that ceiling. An “as well” reading is arguable; High confidence is not.

**Process awareness is uneven.** The answer sees A1, A5/A6, C4’s limited subject-matter, D3’s limited CFO writ, and the CRO gap. It does not use A4 (emergency waives tendering only) as the strongest anti-silent-waiver clause. It never decides whether Incident Command members are “budget owners” even though A2 is a two-part control and the facts say the charter does not make them so. “Independent budget owner” therefore appears and disappears by case. R3 collapses B2 and B4 into “Plant **or** Shift Lead,” which is not the SOP.

**Section 5 is weaker than it looks.** The P-501 alternative is not an alternative; it is the better primary and should have flipped the row under the stated burden of proof. The P-508 alternative gets A1 backwards: A1 bars the requester/committer from being the **final business approver**. BUY-8 committed and was **not** an IC authorizer; that is A1 compliance, not an A1 breach. Inventing a High-credibility flip from that premise is a legal error.

**Section 7 does not resolve the problem it was asked to resolve.** It ANDs A3’s triggers (“new suppliers >EUR 100k”), drops the comparator on the EUR 150k sentence, and forbids Finance waiver without the CRO after the case table allowed exactly that waiver. The 72-hour sunset is an unargued new policy, not a clarification of these sources.

**What remains creditworthy:** required headings; an attempt at issue-specific weights rather than a single stack; explicit facts/assumptions; some valid calls (P-503 SoD, P-504 scope, P-506 expiry, P-505 contemporaneous evidence); and a policy draft that at least names expiry, recording windows, and emergency normalization. That is a mid-structured exam answer with several case-dispositive mistakes. Under a strict reading of this rubric it is far from nearly flawless.
