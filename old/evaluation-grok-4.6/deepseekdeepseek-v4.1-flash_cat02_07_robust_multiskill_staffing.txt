**6.0**

The write-up is complete on format, does real offered-load arithmetic, and does not pick a winner from base-case mean delay alone. It is not close to flawless. Several results are asserted rather than derived, and the policies in §3 are not the policies in the code.

**What is actually solid**

- Mixture means, block workloads, shift/break clock capacity (2490), and the 16:00–18:00 software pinch (≈92.4 vs Emil ≈90) check out.
- Multipliers are treated as slowness, not speed. Parallel ME/ES is correctly treated as extra resource work plus an 8-minute coordination delay, not as free capacity.
- U1/U3/U5 bottleneck migration is the right story: morning surge, Chen as the electrical/software hinge, Farah-only software before noon under U5.
- Four policies are specified in operational terms; robustness is defined across U1–U5; fallback triggers and “when another policy is better” are present.
- §6–§7 and §9 match the prompt (gaming, P3 starvation, preemption churn, Validation as the migrated queue, event attributes, a P1-safe pilot).
- The Python block is a real minute-step simulator with encoded tables, three policies plus a fourth, handover/setup/preemption/parallel flags, seeds, an interval log, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, and a separate skill-level wait table.

**Material problems**

1. **§4 is not computed.** The policy×scenario grid is a monotonic ranking (Robust wins almost every cell) with wide ranges (±30–50% delay, ±5–15 pp SLA). It is not tied to the §2 loads (no Kingman/queueing bound, no wait-from-overload identity) and not taken from the simulator that §8 then runs. Under a 109%+ offered-load day, base Robust P1 SLA of 94–98% and mean delay 30–60 min are guesses, and they can easily contradict the code.

2. **Policy text ≠ code (this is the largest process-mining failure).**  
   - B/C/D promise SERPT and handover-aware resource choice; the code assigns the first idle resource in `resource_df` order to FIFO-within-priority work. Ada is structurally preferred.  
   - Parallelization thresholds (10/15/20 min, critical-path test) are not implemented: non-strict is always parallel, strict is always sequential.  
   - Strict is specified with P1 preemption; the code disables preemption for `strict`.  
   - Overtime is always in the availability denominator (`end+30`), so utilization is not “planned utilization” as defined in the prompt.

3. **SLA metrics from the sim are biased.** Horizon is 1440 min from 08:00; shifts do not repeat the next morning; unfinished cases are stamped complete at `max_min`. Late arrivals that never finish look *better* than early ones. P3’s 24-hour target is not actually tested. Three seeds do not rescue that.

4. **Effective skill×block capacity is thin.** Total standard minutes vs clock is a useful bound, but in-block ratios (e.g. 16:00–18:00 at 187%) treat work as trapped in the arrival window. Skill-specific capacity as clock/multiplier is only sketched (one software check), so U2 electrical and U4 tail claims stay qualitative.

5. **Internal unclarity left in the final text.** Policy D’s priority line still contains “otherwise P3 before slackless P2? No: …”. O1’s `p95_delay_penalty` and O2’s regret metric are not fully specified. Fairness as `1-(max_util-min_util)` rates equal overload as “fair.” U2 is handled only under renormalized mix; the doubled-count alternative is named, not computed.

6. **PM4Py demonstration is hedged.** `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `get_service_time` / `discover_handover_of_work_network` sit in `try/except` with unspecified signatures, so the required post-analysis may print a skip instead of a result. Case-level PM4Py waiting vs stage-level `ready→start` is the right split when it runs.

**Net:** Competent control-flow/capacity reasoning and a serious scaffold, undermined by an unevidenced comparison table, dispatch rules that were not implemented, and SLA numbers that the simulator cannot support. That is a mid-score answer, not a high one.
