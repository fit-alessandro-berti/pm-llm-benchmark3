The submission is a strong, internally consistent OCEL construction with a coherent source-direct policy, complete mandatory columns, referential checks, and generally careful analysis. It is not near-flawless under a strict reading.

**What holds up.** Object types, event inventory, payment amounts as event attributes, timezone-aware timestamps, E2O activity/timestamp projection via merge, and the stated O2O facts are all in place. The decision not to mark M2 as `completes_settlement` is the correct conservative choice. Validation covers the required integrity properties. Flattening predictions for Order / Package / Delivery / Invoice match the chosen E2O policy, including copied shared events (E06, E07, E11) and the weakness of Payment flattening. The Petri-net discussion correctly refuses a unique interpretation. Required APIs, commented viz, and the eight named failure modes are present.

**Material unclarities.**

1. **OTG is described as an E2O co-participation graph.** Order–Package via E07 and “no Order–Delivery edge under this E2O policy” treat OTG as event-derived type interaction. If `discover_otg` is the O2O type schema (the usual OCEL 2.0 reading), the graph is Order–Item, Package–Item, Delivery–Package, Invoice–Order, Payment–Invoice — and Order–Package is *absent*. That collides with the ET–OT / interactions discussion and is the largest conceptual slip.

2. **Activity-level audit overstates Pack Package.** The table says Pack Package touches Order, Item, and Package. Only E07 touches Order; E08 does not. The later E08 paragraph repairs this, but the compact audit is still wrong as stated.

3. **Section 1 does not actually explain the trade-off it is required to state.** It records the policy and the provenance split. The real trade-off (shorter honest traces vs. end-to-end operational visibility, frequency inflation, false synchronization) is deferred to §6.

- Qualifier morphology is uneven (`created_order`, `receiving_order`, `dispatch_delivery` vs. past-participle roles). Usable, not “consistent vocabulary” in a strict sense.
- Order omitted-event list cites E08/E10/E14 and then waves at “dispatches, confirmations, and payments,” leaving E09 and E13 implicit.

- OC-DFG writeup mixes type-level frequencies with instance-level storytelling (P1, O100, O200) without saying what the OC-DFG actually stores.

High craft, one real graph-semantics confusion, and several imprecise tables/claims. That is very good work, not a 9+.
