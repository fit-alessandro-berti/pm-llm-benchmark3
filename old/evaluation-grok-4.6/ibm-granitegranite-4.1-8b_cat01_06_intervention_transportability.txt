**4.2**

The high-level instinct is right — do not copy Site A’s blanket parallelization, segment on statutory prerequisites, treat Site A’s before/after as confounded, and recommend a conditional, digital-first pilot. That is not enough. Under a strict rubric the answer is repeatedly inaccurate, incomplete against its own spec, and causally sloppy. It reads like a plausible memo that would fail an expert audit.

**Format and coverage.** The ten headings exist, but required content is missing or renamed. Section 1 collapses case mix and tail, never isolates loops as a first-class feature, and omits Site A’s six-open-task WIP rule. Section 3 drops high-complexity mix (48%→40%), p90, waiting-before-assessment, the marketing/mix shift, and huddles as standalone co-interventions — exactly the evidence that blocks treating the nine-hour drop as a treatment effect. Section 2 does not keep the mandated node set (`cycle_time`, `appeal` become other names; several capacity nodes are dead ends). Section 5 never supplies the required scenario in which the average improves while the tail worsens.

**Process-structure errors.** Site B’s document channel is inverted in the comparison table (“legacy analog for 38%”); later sections use 38% digital. That is a basic reading failure. Site B already starts Identity Check and Originals Receipt after Register (DFG: 2,050 and 1,860); the answer treats B as simply sequential and never uses the DFG. The candidate POWL then breaks the stated semantics: “IdentityCheck <: Assessment (requires DocumentCheck completion only for digital)” is false — Assessment always waits for both checks. Legal review is over-excluded from verification parallelization even though it is downstream of Assessment. Supplement is described as retriggering the entire parallel block, including identity, which the DFG does not support.

**Causal transport.** The graph is not a transport graph. `treatment_parallelization → digital_documents` is backwards. `originals_required → legal_capacity` is invented. The write-up then calls Site A’s registry API upgrade a *shared transferable mechanism*. It is a co-intervention, and Site B does not have it. Overlap 8%→71% is correctly flagged as timestamp-contaminated, then still used as if it measured true concurrency. The nine-hour median cut is verbally disclaimed and then recycled as an ~8h transferable effect (confidence 55–60). Mix shift, +2 FTE, huddles, and API latency are not partialled out. Quantitative mapping from B’s own numbers — sequential identity+document ≈ 11.5h vs `max(3.4, 8.1)`, 18h originals delay, 3 assessors, 2 legal, 360/1,360 supplement — is almost unused.

**Scenarios and modifiers.** Optimistic and “digital-only” are duplicates. Bottleneck-shift and legal-congestion apply the treatment to 100% after arguing eligibility is 38%. Confidence 10 on the IT-lock scenario is theater, not analysis. Several modifier rows invent extra interventions (parallel assessment, hire/cross-train two identity reviewers) or claim parallel verification will raise legal supplement rates without a mechanism.

**Pilot.** Case-level randomization on a four-person identity pool is a SUTVA problem the design does not control. “12 weeks (2×6-month pre/post windows)” is internally incoherent. Power is pinned to the confounded 8h effect. The mechanism-vs-implementation paragraph has the distinction backwards: unreliable timestamps are measurement failure; an IT lock that prevents overlap is implementation failure; mechanism failure is true overlap without shorter waits (assessment/legal/originals dominate).

**Section 8 is a hard fail.** Site A’s *before* tree omits `Assessment`. The code ignores the required `convert_to_petri_net` path and fabricates APIs (`PartialOrderWorkflowNet`, `add_conditional_edge`, `convert_to_workflow_net(..., technical_discipline="business")`, `convert_powl_to_workflow_net`, a nonsensical `play_out`). Real PM4Py POWL is `Transition` / `OperatorPOWL` / `StrictPartialOrder`. The Site B net makes Legal Review, supplement, Approve, Reject, and Notify concurrent or always-on rather than XOR/LOOP, so it is not a sound encoding of the proposed intervention. Comments skip case locks.

**Recommendation.** `conditional go` is defensible, but it inherits the 8h overclaim, the unnecessary legal exclusion, and an invented staffing co-intervention — repeating Site A’s confounding. The assumption register is complete in form and weak in substance (API replicability, extra reviewers, “majority of digital claims”).

A strong answer would keep Assessment in both A trees, refuse to identify A’s Δ, bound B’s digital-path saving at a few hours of wait/service, keep originals and legal loops mandatory, encode B as a real partial order with an optional legal/supplement loop, and design a stratum-level pilot that can tell overlap-without-gain from lock-without-overlap. This answer gestures at that and then gets the structure, the facts, the graph, and the model wrong.
