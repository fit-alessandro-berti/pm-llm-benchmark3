**6.0**

The write-up is complete, on-format, and mostly the right kind of analysis: regimes, pooling distortions, burstiness, Little’s Law limits, and a runnable single script. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has several factual errors, one serious units inconsistency, and a few unsupported claims.

**What holds up**
- Estimands and threats in §1 are largely correct: opening-hours convention, arrivals ≠ same-day completions, median service ≠ flow time, carry-over, snapshot WIP, mix-shift ambiguity.
- Primary cut (baseline weekdays / outage-recovery / high-mix weekdays / intervention weekdays / weekends) is defensible, and the alternative (WIP per FTE) is a genuinely different operational story with a fair strength/weakness comparison.
- Hourly Fano/CV arithmetic for the four representative days is correct, and the “four days ≠ a stochastic model” caveat is right.
- Little’s Law verdicts are directionally right: outage and recovery are not defensible; other blocks are at most approximate. Median service is not used as \(W\).
- §7’s pooled-but-risky policy (uniform +10% FTE) is a real Simpson-style staffing mistake.
- The Python block meets the mechanical spec: in-memory tables, pooled vs regime summaries, two dispersion stats plus lag-1, a labeled heuristic change point, Little’s Law only on chosen windows, PM4Py mentioned only as comments on a real event log.

**Material defects**

1. **Little’s Law units are internally inconsistent (largest technical miss).**  
   §1 assumes `mean_wip` is an opening-hours snapshot average and defines the day as 12 h / 720 min. §6 then sets \(W = L/\lambda \times 1440\). That is coherent only if \(L\) is a 24-hour time average. Overnight WIP is the carry-over backlog, not the daytime mean, and there is no service while closed. Using 1440 roughly doubles implied sojourn versus an open-hours conversion (\(L/\lambda \times 720\) ≈ 146 min on baseline vs the reported 291). The task explicitly required window, units, and boundary conditions. Naming 1440 is not an explanation.

2. **Several numbers in the prose are wrong or uncomputed.**  
   High-mix weekdays are not 6.0 FTE: 2026-04-24 is 5.5, so mean staff is 5.93 and the table’s “staff 6.0” is false (the script would print 5.93). Weekend `complex_pct` is 253/8 = 31.6, not 29.1; pooled complex is 32.9%, not 32.2%. Intervention implied \(W\) is 463 min, not 466. “Interquartile” ranges in §4 are not IQRs (baseline weekday arrivals IQR is about 96–104, not 98–105).

3. **Unsupported / false process claims.**  
   Post-mix weekday arrivals do **not** “increase Mon–Fri” (Apr 20–24: 108, 115, 119, 111, 106). Lag-1 “near zero in baseline” is not computed; the code only prints one overall autocorrelation. The “Simpson-type reversal” is asserted, not shown; what is shown is a mixture of levels, not a sign flip.

4. **Outage/recovery handling is better in prose than in the primary table.**  
   The quality bar says not to treat recovery as a normal high-throughput regime. §2 still folds 15–17 into one row with mean completions 99.7, which averages a blocked day (58) with flush days (129, 112). Weekends are also left unsplit despite the same mix break the authors use to split weekdays (p90 ~56 vs ~81; weekend FTE also rises to 3.0 after the intervention).

5. **Presentation shortcuts.**  
   Observation / estimate / hypothesis labels are mostly dropped. Two Little’s matrix cells are literally `~ ~`. Confidence is stated but not tied to sample size (n=3 intervention weekdays) as clearly as the uncertainty deserves.

Net: a competent, complete benchmark response with real queueing structure, undermined by a factor-of-two sojourn convention error and enough broken or invented numbers that it cannot be scored as nearly correct.
