**6.4**

The answer follows the required eight-heading structure, respects the scenario constraints (raw gaps ≠ discrimination; case mix ≠ automatic justification; risk scores are not treated as neutral; support steps are not to be removed), and ends with appropriately calibrated language. Those are real strengths. Under a strict reading, however, it is not close to flawless: a central quantitative inference is inverted, at least one mechanism is invented, resource arithmetic is sloppy, the required disparity/discrimination/routing/uncertainty distinction is only implied, and the PM4Py plan would not run as written.

**Section 1.** Coverage of the eight dimensions is complete and mostly well prioritized. The distinction between raw parity and adjusted residuals is handled correctly, and the point that burden concentrates in rights-preserving queues is the right fairness insight. The routing discussion is wrong. V4 is 24% of Group B while 57% of Group B needs language support. That cannot suggest over-routing; if anything it suggests under-provision, path fragmentation, or that language-support cases sit in variants that were not tabulated. Writing “although only 57%” treats the larger number as if it were the smaller one. Compacted phrases such as “riskfraud review” and “languagesupport” also make the consistency discussion harder to parse than it should be.

**Section 2.** Matching, variant logits, resource assignment tests, waiting-time decomposition, covariate-omission sensitivity, and region/channel splits are the right toolkit. Oaxaca–Blinder-style decomposition of extra activities vs. same-activity slowness vs. specialist queues is genuinely useful. The prompt, however, asked for a strategy that *distinguishes* disparity, discrimination, legitimate routing, and uncertainty. Those four objects are never operationalized. The plan also never decides whether `language_preference` and `disability_accommodation_requested` are controls, stratifiers, or mediators—the key design choice when the hypothesis is that rights-preserving steps create residual burden. Residual gaps are treated as point facts; no uncertainty, significance, or power discussion appears (Group C’s −2.2 pp is especially fragile).

**Section 3.** Several cited shares and waits are accurate, and specialist queues plus regional handovers are the right hotspots. The same inverted V4 claim is repeated and becomes a pillar of the section. The caseload comparison is also unreliable: Group B is “disproportionately assigned to 7 bilingual caseworkers *and* 3 overloaded regional officers,” not 1,200+ cases on seven people at 170 each. The 52×70 figure treats Group A’s 3,600 cases as if those workers had no B/C/D work. Claiming that the 3.8 d and 4.7 d specialist waits “explain most” of B/C extra cycle time ignores that those waits apply only to the subset on V4/V5 (roughly 0.24×3.8 ≈ 0.9 d and 0.31×4.7 ≈ 1.5 d) and that the +2.6 / +4.1 figures are already adjusted. The largest path contrast—V1 42% vs 19% (happy-path access) and V3 9% vs 28% for Group D—is barely used.

**Section 4.** Four-plus mechanisms with a legitimate/concerning split is the right shape. Mechanisms 1–3 and 6 are plausible. Mechanism 4 is not supported: there is no evidence that Group B cases with majority-language preference are sent to Language Support Contact. That is stereotyping inferred from an inverted percentage comparison. Mechanism 5 (unfamiliar document formats triggering supervisor review) is admissible as speculation but is presented with more specificity than the log can bear.

**Section 5.** This is the strongest section. Historical bias in `risk_score_band`, collider/masking risk from `documentation_completeness`, daily resource granularity, missing dignity/explanation quality, and coarse accommodation coding are all on target. “Missing applicant needs” is only partly developed (SES/digital literacy appear; language *proficiency* vs preference, informal accommodations, and explanation quality at notification do not).

**Section 6.** All six mandated options appear, none delete support steps, and each has a trade-off. The trade-offs are thin. SLA targets without capacity invite gaming and rushed support; capacity without demand management is more than a utilization risk; explanation templates can become boilerplate that does not reduce appeals. Those second-order effects are the ones that matter in this process and are underspecified.

**Section 7.** Conceptually it touches variants, performance DFG, waiting-time insertion, handover networks, roles, and a log-skeleton check. As executable PM4Py it is incorrect. `format_dataframe` does not take a column list; it is also the wrong next step after `xes_importer`. DataFrame and EventLog APIs are mixed. `filter_variants(..., list(variants.keys())[:10])` does not select the ten most frequent variants. Group-wise variant shares, between-group DFGs, `filter_paths_performance`, `get_all_case_durations`, `discover_working_together_network`, and `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` are absent or named only in comments. The plan therefore does not actually show how to *compare* groups on the fairness quantities defined in sections 1–3.

**Section 8.** The conclusion is the most disciplined part of the essay: residual burden for B and C, capacity bottlenecks in protective steps, risk-based routing for D that cannot be treated as cleanly legitimate, and a refusal to convert that into a finding of discrimination. “Procedurally concerning” is better than a false binary. It is slightly overconfident that bottlenecks “largely” explain the gaps, and it underweights Group D’s residual appeal disparity (+5.7 pp) given the instruction not to treat the risk band as neutral.

**Net.** A competent, well-structured policy analysis with a serious local error (V4 over-routing), an unsupported mechanism, loose resource math, an incomplete causal taxonomy in the methods section, and non-runnable PM4Py. That is clearly above a failing or generic answer, and clearly below a near-flawless one.
