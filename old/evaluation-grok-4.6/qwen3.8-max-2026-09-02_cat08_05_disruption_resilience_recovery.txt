**7.4**

The final write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly faithful to the case. It hits all seven required sections, uses T0–T4 metrics, DFG frequencies, and conformance/temporal findings, and it correctly refuses to treat every deviation as failure, throughput as resilience, or after-the-fact audit work as real-time control. Those are real strengths. Under a strict reading, they are not enough for a high score.

**Section 3 is the main analytical failure.** Hospital-critical fast lane (A), deferral (I), and backlog prioritization (J) are listed as true resilience *and* as ambiguous. Audit remediation (L) is likewise both a recovery-resilience mechanism and an ambiguous/non-equivalent control. That is not nuance; it is an unresolved classification. A reader cannot tell what the author actually decided. Temporary release (F) is treated more generously than the cold-chain documentation gap warrants, while B is marked “Keep” even though the rationale is a redesign (preapproved matrix, time-boxing, documentation). The keep/modify/retire column therefore drifts.

**Several inferences are over-precise.** The 2,600 phone PODs versus 3,250 dispatch-without-scan events are read as incomplete evidence capture; the DFG is incomplete in several places (unbalanced node frequencies), so in-flight, cross-phase, or unrecorded paths are equally plausible. The 7.8% cancellation rate is placed next to the 61% pharmacy fill rate as if it were a pharmacy-specific result; it is an overall rate. Those are small but real accuracy slips.

**The recovery model is usable but looser than it looks.** Required elements are present (triggers, triage, minimum controls, evidence, backlog, reconciliation). Many thresholds (30 minutes, 2 hours, 12/24-hour upload, 10-day SLA, fitness 0.95) are generic policy inventions, not derived from the observed p95 backlog delay, variable dispatch-to-POD lag, or the 2,300-case audit tail. Notation is announced and then barely used; the later reconciliation tree is a different scheme. Return-to-normal at fitness 0.95 is slightly below the 0.96 baseline with no comment. Fairness “service floors” stay vague compared with the rest of the control design.

**What still works:** T0–T4 risk posture is temporally coherent; C and G are correctly treated as concealed noncompliance rather than clever workarounds; hidden T4 workload (1,400 reconciliations, 1,200 POD remediations, 2,300 audit cases) is not washed out by recovering cycle times; the management paragraph is actually decision-oriented. Those points keep the score well above a middling answer. The overlapping labels, a few over-read frequencies, and a recovery design that is comprehensive more than tightly evidenced keep it well below “nearly flawless.”
