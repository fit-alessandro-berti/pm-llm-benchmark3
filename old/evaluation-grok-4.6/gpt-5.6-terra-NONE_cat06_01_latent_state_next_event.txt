**6.5**

The answer is process-aware, structurally complete, and clearly better than a DFG-copying baseline. It is not close to flawless. Several predictions contradict the answer’s own control-flow premises, and a few probability/enabled-state choices are poorly disciplined.

**What is actually strong**

C6-A vs C6-B is handled correctly: same visible prefix, different obligations. High severity, injury, common-lot/multi-device structure, and a reportability-oriented triage resource are not treated as interchangeable with a low-severity, single-device, high-similarity duplicate. The cross-case note on this point is the best part of the submission.

The answer generally respects the observation model. Missing events are not treated as proof of missing work, especially in C6-D. Alternatives are retained. Supporting/contradicting/unknowns are separated rather than collapsed into advocacy. DFG counts are treated as observational, not normative. Fast Close is correctly withheld once cases are already inside an evidence cycle. Supplier Analysis is required from related same-lot structure (C6-A/E) and not invented for different-lot repeated malfunction (C6-C).

**Material process errors**

The worst flaw is repeated. In both C6-C and C6-D, rank 3 is `Authority Notification` as the *next logged* event, with intervals up to 0.20–0.22. The answer itself states that notification requires a reportable regulatory conclusion **and disposition**, and that batches go out at 16:00. That is not a next-event forecast; it is a skipped-prefix forecast. At 14:00, with Regulatory Assessment still unseen, a long risk-prioritized queue, and notification gated on later milestones, this is not a coherent top-3 successor. `Close — No Safety Issue`, `Corrective Action Opened`, further technical work (C6-C’s unconfirmed root cause), or—especially in C6-D—a *delayed evidence-cycle completion appearing in the feed* are all more faithful to “next logged.”

C6-D’s observation caveat is better than its predictions. Migration delay is the distinctive fact of that case, yet none of the three forecasts is a late-arriving evidence or review event. The worklist snapshot is used well for Regulatory Assessment; the 8–15% / ≤6 h feed model is mostly left in the prose.

**Weaker probability and enabled-state discipline**

C6-A’s rank-2/3 mass on `Technical Investigation Completed` and `Regulatory Assessment Completed` is too high for an immediate next log event. Evidence arrived at 12:10; it is 14:00; review is the normative successor after `Evidence Received`; technical median times are ~8.5–9 h; regulatory completion is 2–14 h after assignment; one of three engineers is out. Parallel start at triage is possible, and high-severity overtake is real, but 0.18–0.36 and 0.12–0.29 as *next* completions imply a large chance of leaping over review. That is an overread of concurrency.

`process_enabled` is inconsistent in a way that matters. C6-A marks investigation *completions* enabled before review. C6-B marks investigation not enabled. C6-C/D mark disposition not enabled while floating S5 as an alternative. C6-E marks `Customer Evidence Requested` not enabled (correct) but `Supplier Analysis Completed` enabled 40 minutes after the second receipt, despite a 6–36 h cycle. The write-up understands the distinction; the flags do not apply it cleanly.

C6-B’s alternative `S5 DISPOSITION_READY` is weakly supported on a normal feed with no review completion. “Completed outside the visible prefix” is exactly the inference the observation model says not to make casually.

C6-E’s rank-3 supplier completion has the right obligation logic and the wrong next-event timing, only partly rescued by a low interval.

**Smaller defects that still count**

Queue data are mentioned more than used. Three live cases are in or entering a 4-deep / 2-reviewer evidence-review queue; elapsed times versus 1–5 h “after queue entry” and the 16:00 cutoff are not worked through. Completeness 0.86 in C6-A makes another evidence request a live competitor that never enters the top 3, while slower investigation completions do. Several intervals (0.42–0.65, 0.18–0.36) still look more precise than the evidence warrants. Residual alternatives such as Product Hold/Recall on C6-A, or a second technical pass on C6-C, are left almost entirely to the unknown lists.

**Bottom line**

This is a competent expert draft with real semantic control, not a shallow frequency dump. Hypercritically, the self-contradictory Authority Notification ranks, the underuse of C6-D’s migration mechanism *inside the forecasts*, and the inflated near-term investigation-completion mass keep it well short of excellent.
