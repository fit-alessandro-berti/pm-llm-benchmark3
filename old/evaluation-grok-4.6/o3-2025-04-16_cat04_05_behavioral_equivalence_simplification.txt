**5.5**

The A-vs-B non-equivalence story is mostly competent, but the answer is not close to flawless. It misdefines the P1 observation, reports structural numbers that cannot be right, never actually derives the quantities it prints, and then ships a “P2-preserving” simplification that is not P2-preserving. Under a strict reading those are core failures, not polish issues.

## What is actually correct

The central semantic contrast is right: in A, `Check Revision` is inside the loop body and is synchronized with each `Analyze Revision` before the redo/`Close Case`; in B it is a single parallel companion of the whole loop. From that, the P1 and P3 verdicts (`not equivalent`) follow, and the four traces in §4 are valid witnesses.

- `A-1` / `A-2` require one check per analysis attempt. B cannot produce them.
- `B-1` / `B-2` put `Rework Case` between analyses with only a late (or otherwise unpaired) check. A cannot produce them, because the AND in the do-part must finish before the loop can take `Rework Case`.

Loop-do-once, optional `Approve Revision` via `X(..., tau)`, and the fact that the skip branch lets them hide `tau` without invalidating those particular witnesses are all handled. P2 as a collapsing abstraction is the right idea, and “bounded ≠ unbounded proof” is stated clearly.

That is enough to show the author understood the models. It is not enough for a high score.

## §1 Equivalence notions — several definitions are wrong

Visible-trace equivalence after hiding `tau`, and bounded P3 equivalence, are fine. The other two are not.

**P1 / OC is not the business rule that was given.** P1 requires that every analysis have its own check *before the next `Rework Case` or `Close Case`*. It does not require the check to occur *later than* that analysis. In A, `Analyze Revision` and `Check Revision` are in an AND, so `Open, Check, Analyze, Close` is a legal A-trace and is P1-compliant. The written OC rule 1 rejects it. The observation boundary is therefore stricter than P1 and silently excludes part of A’s language.

OC-equivalence is then defined as “same set of OC-legal traces.” That is a filter, not an equivalence of the two systems under P1. B’s non-compliant traces would be discarded rather than counted as distinguishing behaviour. Rule 3 (every rework is followed by another analysis) is true of *both* languages and does not discriminate. Rule 4 (“all other visible behaviour may occur arbitrarily often”) is false: `Approve Revision` is at most once per iteration in A, and `Check Revision` is exactly once in B.

**P2 / DB is the wrong type.** P2 induces a *set* of dashboard observations `(#Analyze, hasRework, hasCheck)` (plus the fixed open/close frame). Calling this a *multiset* of tuples imports frequencies that P2 never defined. Without a stochastic model, multiset equality is meaningless. The illustrative pair `Open, Analyze, Analyze, Close` vs `Open, Analyze, Close, Analyze` is also a bad example: the second is not a complete trace of either model.

Occurrences distinguished by position is the right convention; the surrounding text is sloppy.

## §2 Structural comparison — mostly right, incomplete on placement

The table gets loop scope, min/max checks, optional approval, and the AND-join before `Close Case` right. Two gaps matter.

- “Both allow very early check” is true but weak. The decisive placement facts are: in A a check cannot straddle `Rework Case` (checkᵢ is before reworkᵢ; checkᵢ₊₁ is after it); in B the single check is concurrent with *Analyze, Approve, and Rework*, so it may sit before the first analysis, between rework and the next analysis, or after the last analysis. That is the P1 defect, and it is only half-stated.
- “A guarantees a 1-to-1 relation” is true for *counts*, not for *order*. Inside an iteration the AND freely interleaves Analyze and Check.

## §4 Counterexamples — valid traces, wrong observation story

Four complete traces, both inclusion directions, at least one with two analyses: that part meets the spec.

The preamble “( and `Approve Revision` are already hidden.)” is a real mistake. P3 keeps every visible label; P1 keeps approvals and treats their placement as relevant. Choosing the `tau` branch is legitimate, so the listed traces remain *legal* witnesses, but the author presents Approve-hiding as part of the comparison rather than as a w.l.o.g. restriction. There is no witness that actually uses `Approve Revision`, even though P1 asked for that dimension. Cheap extra witnesses they should have given:

- A-only: `Open, Analyze, Check, Approve, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close`
- B-only: `Open, Analyze, Approve, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close`

Optional approve/`tau` therefore “do not affect any witness” is true only because they refused to write a witness where they would.

## §5 Code — right algorithm, probably not executable, not executed

The exploration strategy is the one the prompt asked for: convert both trees, check soundness, BFS over markings, silent transitions free, visible length and `#Analyze` bounded, accept only `mark == fm`. The exhaustiveness argument is essentially right.

It still fails the section:

1. `pm4py.execute_transition(net, new_mark, t)` is not in the stated PM4Py surface. Firing is `pm4py.objects.petri_net.semantics.execute(...)` (returns a new marking; it does not mutate a `.copy()`).
2. Unused imports (`replay`, `pt_converter`, `defaultdict`) show the code was not cleaned or run.
3. `check_soundness` is called and ignored.
4. No printed cardinalities, no unique traces. The prompt asked for a program that *prints* the symmetric difference. This is a sketch.
5. `label is None` is the usual silent encoding, but it is never checked against the converted nets.

So: conceptually exhaustive BFS, not a self-contained demonstration.

## §6 Footprints / stochastic — conclusion OK, evidence thin

“Don’t invent probabilities” and “low EMD ≠ trace equivalence” are correct. Everything else is hand-waving.

`discover_footprints` on these two trees is *not* obviously the same. In B, `Check Revision` is concurrent with `Rework Case`; in A they are sequential around the loop edge. Even if a log-style footprint collapses `Check→Rework` and `Rework→Check` to `||` in both models, that is a reason to *run* the API and say what it returned. They assert “same directly-follows relations between most labels,” provide no code (the prompt said to provide it where plausible), and then say footprints forget counts. Counts are one failure mode; concurrency of Check with Rework is another, and they missed it.

## §8 Simplified model — the constructive part is wrong

Targeting P2 is allowed. The tree is not a P2 abstraction of either original.

```text
->('Open Case', *('Analyze Revision', X('Rework Case', tau)), 'Check Revision', 'Close Case')
```

Standard binary loop: do `Analyze Revision` once; then exit, *or* execute the redo and do `Analyze Revision` again. The redo is `X('Rework Case', tau)`. Taking `tau` yields

`Open, Analyze, Analyze, Check, Close`

and, more generally, any `#Analyze ≥ 2` with `hasRework = 0`. Both originals forbid that: the only redo label is `Rework Case`, so `#Analyze = 1 + #Rework`. P2 still records *both* `#Analyze` and whether rework occurred. The dashboard tuple set of the simplified model is a strict superset of the originals. The preservation table’s “#Analyze exact” and “hasRework exact” rows are false.

This is the standard loop mistake: the operator already makes the redo optional. Wrapping the redo in `X(Rework, tau)` does not “make rework optional after each analysis”; it makes *another analysis possible without rework*.

A P2-faithful compact tree is

```text
->('Open Case', *('Analyze Revision', 'Rework Case'), 'Check Revision', 'Close Case')
```

(nine nodes if you drop Approve, no extra `tau`). Check-at-the-end is a legitimate P2 sequentialization of interleaving. The extra XOR is not.

Further problems in §8:

- Negative trace `Open, Check, Analyze, Close` is in *both* originals and is P2-identical to `Open, Analyze, Check, Close`. Treating it as a regression failure means they are enforcing an order P2 was defined to forget.
- `Open, Analyze, Close` is a fair negative (missing check).
- They never reject the spurious `Open, Analyze, Analyze, Check, Close`.
- “9 nodes, depth 3, substantially smaller than both originals” is only true if you compare to the real 12/12 trees, not to their 15/13 table.
- Soundness is “verified” with no check in the snippet.

A single simplification cannot serve P1 and P2; they imply that, but the model they offer does not even serve P2.

## §9 Recommendation — right on P1, wrong on replacement

Keep A for P1: yes. Do not treat P3 as a proof: yes. Replace either original by this simplified model for P2: **no**, because the simplified language is not the P2 image of A or B.

## Net assessment

The answer looks complete because it follows the heading template and writes in the register of a methods paper. Graded on whether every conclusion is supported and whether the proposed simplification preserves what it claims, it is not.

Serious defects: wrong P1 definition; P2 defined as a multiset; Approve wrongly treated as hidden for P3; BFS code that does not match the PM4Py API and was not run; footprint claims without running `discover_footprints`; invented 15/13/5 node-leaf counts; a simplified loop that admits analyze-without-rework and therefore fails the view it was built for.

What remains is a correct informal argument that A and B are not trace-equivalent, with four good counterexamples. That is a mid-range result, not an 8–10.
