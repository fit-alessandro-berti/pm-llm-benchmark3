**2.4**

The answer has the right section headings in spirit and repeats some of the problem’s vocabulary (selection, temporal split, untestable counterfactuals). That is not enough. It fails the actual design problem: early prediction under selective observation. Several choices match the prompt’s own definition of a weak answer.

## 1. Target definition — prediction time is inverted

The business question is an **early-warning** model from the **first few events**. The answer sets prediction time to **90 days after Account Activated**. That is after the outcome window has closed. It is not a monitoring design; it is almost a post-hoc description of a realized label.

The rest of the estimand is internally inconsistent. Population is “all 48,000 applications,” the target is default only among activated accounts, and the estimand is an “average probability … conditional on process history” that never states \(P(Y=1 \mid A=1, H_t)\) versus a counterfactual \(P(Y^{a=1}=1 \mid H_t)\). “Aligns with available labeled data” is exactly the convenient activated-only cohort the prompt warns against.

The alternative is a slogan (“default for all applications”) with no unit, time origin, handling of undefined outcomes, or competing-risk semantics. The suggested formulations (multi-state, censored time-to-default, labeled counterfactual) are not actually specified.

## 2. Cohort construction — arithmetic and logic collapse

Inclusion and exclusion contradict each other. Withdrawn/transferred are both “included as censored” and “excluded because omitted from the labeled export.” Including cases with a `Default Recorded` event **selects on the outcome**. Restricting to `Contract Signed` is not the same as activation, and it is not an as-of rule.

The flow table does not account for 48,000 cases and mislabels the known counts:

- 18,900 **non-defaults** are placed under “Labeled (Default).”
- 9,100 rejected cases disappear.
- 3,600 immature activated accounts are counted twice (censored and missing).
- 2,900 transfers are counted as both competing and missing.
- “Active” contains only the 2,700 defaults.

There is no as-of / snapshot date logic, no maturity cutoff (`activation + 90d + feed delay ≤ extract`), and no treatment of the 60% transfer linkage table.

## 3. Censoring — the primary mechanism is defined backwards

Completed 90-day non-defaults are **observed zeros**, not right-censored. Right censoring applies to immature activations and delayed feeds, not to accounts that survived the horizon. “Use the available label” ignores `label_available_at` as an information-time constraint.

The required distinctions are not operationalized: delayed labels vs ordinary censoring vs activation selection vs transfer-as-competing-event vs transfer-as-missing-follow-up vs permanently missing partner feeds (likely MNAR). No primary estimator is named (binary among mature activations, IPCW, Fine–Gray, discrete-time hazard, Heckman/reject-inference, etc.).

The sensitivity analysis **assigns default risk 0 to rejected cases**, which the prompt forbids. Calling “the relationship between activation and default” testable is false from these data: \(Y\) is undefined or unobserved when \(A=0\).

## 4. Prefix extraction — late prefixes, extra selection

Prefixes are taken as the **last** \(n\) events / last 30 days **before activation**, or time since Credit Review. That is late-process, post-review information, not “first few events,” and it leaks decision-adjacent state.

Discarding traces with \(<5\) events adds another selected cohort (quick rejects/withdrawals). Collapsing document loops into `documents_total` adopts a feature the prompt flags for **same-day future uploads**. Duplicate events from the 15 Oct 2025 migration are ignored. There is no rule that every event in the prefix satisfies `timestamp ≤ prediction_time` and that later events are dropped from the log **before** featurization.

## 5. Features — outcomes used as covariates

`time_to_default` is the target, not a feature. `time_to_activation` and `time_to_decision` are not available at an early prefix. `risk_score` is treated as static even though it is updated. Resource experience is taken “as of activation,” but the warehouse stores experience **at extraction time**. Queue length is listed with no as-of reconstruction from contemporaneous cases.

Control-flow, rework counts, waiting/sojourn times, and historical-reference features are mostly missing. The required distinction (static vs prefix-derived vs reference-data) is not maintained.

## 6. Leakage audit — incomplete and often wrong

Required items are thin or absent. Future events (Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections Contact, Default Recorded) are not in the table. `documents_total` is called “low” risk and kept; the same-calendar-day snapshot leak is ignored. `decision_reason` is kept even though it is populated at Accept/Reject. `collection_status` “before activation” is incoherent (collections occur after activation). `label_available_at` is treated as a keepable feature rather than a label-maturity gate. Resource experience is kept without repair.

`final_limit` is dropped for the wrong reason (“may change after activation”); it is a **post-decision** field.

## 7. Validation — reasonable prose, then the forbidden split

A start-time temporal split, policy-era slices, calibration, and subgroup checks are the right *words*. They are not made operational (no maturity filter on the test window, no explicit pre-/post-1 Jul 2025 and post-15 Oct 2025 backtests, no handling of labels that arrive 7–35 days late).

Cohen’s kappa is a weak primary metric here; there is no proper scoring rule (Brier, log loss), no calibration slope/intercept plan, and no competing-risk metric despite earlier censoring talk.

The code then implements **`pm4py.split_train_test` with `random_state=42` as the actual split**, which the prompt says must not be the primary design. Two sections are numbered “7”; **section 8 is missing**.

## Limitations — too shallow for the decision boundary

The text does not cleanly separate (i) association in the activated subpopulation, (ii) selection into activation, and (iii) counterfactual default under a different policy. Deployment effects are generic (policy, migration, “labels decrease”). Missing the first-order feedback loops: the model changes who is accepted; collections/interventions change realized default; transfer/partner routing changes missingness.

## 9. Code — fabricated APIs and a leaked pipeline

The skeleton violates the implementation contract:

- Never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`.
- Invents `pm4py.algo.process_models.temporal_features` and `pm4py.algo.process_models.prefixes`.
- Misuses discovery APIs (`discover_dfg.discover_dfg(...)`, `get_variants.get_variants(pdg, ...)`).
- Builds DFG, variants, and temporal features on the **full future-leaking** event table.
- Aggregates `default_90d`, `time_to_default`, and max `documents_total` into the feature table.
- No configurable cutoff, no activation/terminal/censoring/maturity case table, no cohort counts that reconcile 48,000.
- No drop of post-prefix columns/events.
- Primary split is random, not temporal.
- Prefix length 30 is not “first few events.”
- Comments admit survival/competing-risk work is absent; they do not mark a real hook.

## Overall

This is not a near-miss. It answers a different problem (score activated accounts after the fact), uses the biased 21,600-row export, corrupts the cohort arithmetic, inverts censoring, leaks the outcome into features, and ships a non-runnable PM4Py sketch whose only implemented validation is a random split. Residual credit is only for naming selection and for dropping `final_limit` / `collection_status` in the audit table. Under a strict reading, that is a low-2s design, not a serious study protocol.
