**5.0**

The write-up has the right skeleton—max-not-sum, calendar pauses, overlapping path bands, dependence from the sensor gap, and ranges instead of a fake point forecast—but the numerical engine is unreliable, and those errors move the central forecast by about one to two days.

The most damaging mistake is the QA arrival model. Arrivals are specified only in QA opening hours. From Friday 15:30 to a Monday-morning join that is about 10–11 open hours, so roughly 3–4 expected arrivals, not \(64.5 \times 0.35 \approx 21\). The decomposition also adds those arrivals to the current five batches and never subtracts Friday-evening or Saturday throughput. The resulting “P50 wait ≈ 2 days, P80 ≈ 3 days” is an artifact. A defensible Monday join queue is a handful of batches in front of two reviewers, i.e. hours to maybe one working day, not mid-week completion as the default. That single error infects S1–S4, the P50/P80 clocks, and the ranking of “future QA queue” as a top driver.

Chemistry is also mis-specified. Remaining work is 0.4–0.9 h and 1.2–2.0 h, so the first analyst frees about 15:54–16:24 and the second about 16:42–17:30—not “both done by 17:00 / 17:54.” With one urgent plus three ordinary jobs waiting, FIFO (the 55% regime) essentially precludes a Friday start. Even under risk-priority, the urgent sample still goes first; B-417 starting by 16:30 with probability 0.55 and by 18:00 with probability 0.97 is not defensible. Same-day finishes at 18:30–20:30 violate the 18:00 close. S2’s “rerun starts before 15:00 (rare)” is not rare: it is impossible at 15:30, because the first chemistry service has not begun.

The on-call probability is inverted in the S1 decomposition (\(p=0.35\) no authorization, \(p=0.65\) authorized) even though the ledger and §5 quote the correct 0.25–0.45 band. More importantly, weekend authorization often does **not** move the join: under the likely FIFO path, chemistry is still running Monday morning, so a Saturday readout sits idle at the parallel max. Treating on-call as the #1 P50 shifter therefore overstates a calendar effect that is frequently masked by the chemistry queue.

Internal arithmetic does not add up. Wednesday 22 September is the wrong date (Wednesday is the 23rd). The S1 table (Tue–Wed), the S1 prose (Wed 22–Thu 23), and §4 (Tue 13:00–Thu 17:00) disagree. Stated remaining hours disagree with the clocks (Tue 22 13:00 is ~93 h, not ~72 h; Mon 28 12:00 is ~217 h, not ~195 h). The triangular mean is \((1.5+2.5+4.5)/3=2.83\), not 2.67. Midpoint probabilities are used as if they were least-biased without carrying the stated ranges through.

What remains creditworthy is the qualitative design: six required headings, four-plus overlapping scenarios, explicit max-vs-sum, weekend pause logic in the abstract, excursion-induced positive dependence, a rejection tail that is not treated as the mode, and a next-event section with signed updates. Those keep the answer out of the lower band. They do not offset a systematically late forecast, inverted staffing odds, an illegal Friday chemistry close, and a queue analysis that never uses the calendar it cites. Under a strict reading this is a competent template filled with broken arithmetic, not a near-flawless forecast.
