**4.5**

The answer is complete in outline and readable, but the core deliverable — a feasible, constraint-respecting, internally consistent assignment — is unreliable. Under a strict reading it is a well-formatted policy memo built on several disqualifying mistakes.

**Format and framing.** The seven required headings are present and the piece does not optimize solely for throughput. That is the high-water mark. Section 1 already blurs categories: objectives are labeled “soft constraints,” and continuity (a preference: “prefer… unless”) is listed as a hard constraint. The 20% training rule is stated as an objective rather than a conditional quota. These are not cosmetic naming issues; they make the later scoring and the assignment table hard to audit against the spec.

**Policy design.** The algorithm is a weighted score over a case-sorted loop, which is a reasonable sketch, but two required components are missing or inert. SLA urgency is given 40% of the score and then computed only from case attributes, so it does not distinguish candidates for a given case; after the sort, that weight is wasted. Resilience / bottleneck avoidance is required in the prompt and never appears in `calculate_score`. Hours remaining are decremented but never used as a filter. Supervision is not a first-class object (no supervised-pair type, no supervisor capacity). The 20% training target is a 5% bonus, which cannot enforce the quota. Tie-breaking and exception handling exist only in prose. Batching of Quality Check is a stated hard rule and is absent from the procedure.

**Section 3 contradicts both the data and the author’s own rules.** This is the decisive failure.

- Overtime: the policy forbids loading `overtime_risk=high` except when the case is urgent and no safer alternative exists. The table still gives R2_Bruno two non-urgent cases (C004, C010), R4_David a normal rework (C014) despite 14 open cases and 3.0h left, and R9_Iris a low-priority trainee-suitable check (C012). Section 4 then claims the policy “avoids overloading” Bruno and David. That is a direct falsehood, not a trade-off.
- Security: C015 is given to R10_Jonas (Security = 0) under Hugo. The hard rule allows skill 3, or skill 1–2 under Gita/Hugo — not skill 0. Eva is also 0. The queue note naming Eva/Jonas conflicts with the matrix; a competent policy would flag that conflict and use Celine (1), David (2), or Iris (1). Treating the note as a waiver of “0 = not allowed” is a compliance error.
- Training flags are mostly wrong. C006 is Quality Check for Jonas, whose training priority is security. C009 is Diagnosis for Eva, whose priority is security. C010 is Approval for Bruno (already skill 2; his priority is quality). Three of five “Yes” training rows do not match the matrix. The one genuine trainee diagnosis case they do make, C003, is the worst possible choice: owner David is Diagnosis 3, the case is already a repeated loop with 2 reassignments (evidence: >2 reassignments → 1.8× rework), and C009 is explicitly marked as the training opportunity. Celine (Diagnosis 3, 6 cases, low OT) is ignored.
- Continuity is applied inconsistently. C004 is taken from Amal (Eligibility 3, current owner, routine) and given to high-OT Bruno, then Amal is handed a new eligibility case (C013). That is a pointless handover. C006/C012 are swapped between Iris and Jonas for no stated continuity or batching reason, and the training rationale attached to C006 is false.
- Capacity is not checked. Farid receives two urgent cases (C002 SLA 2h, C011 SLA 4h) with 6.5h left, and is also named as Bruno’s supervisor on C010. Eva receives C005 (high), C016 (high, keep), and C009 (unnecessary). Hugo does urgent C001 (3h left, complexity 0.88) plus an illegal supervised training on C015.

Several individual rows are sensible (C001 Hugo, C002 Farid, C005 Eva, C007 Celine, C008 Gita, C016 Eva). That is not enough when the batch as a whole violates the overtime rule, the Security skill rule, and the author’s own scoring function (which would have kept C004 with Amal and would not have preferred Amal-skill-1 over David/Celine on C003).

**Comparison and explainability.** The comparison table is the usual “ours wins on six of seven axes” scorecard. The prompt asked not to claim a single obvious optimum and to reason about trade-offs. The prose admits a throughput sacrifice, but the table and the false OT claim make the policy look strictly dominant. There is no scenario analysis (Hugo absent, two Security reviews colliding, Farid’s two urgents) and no attempt to actually run first-available or specialist-only on this snapshot, so the comparison is asserted rather than computed. The worker note is the strongest section: tone is non-punitive and the six principles are intelligible. It is also generic. It never explains the contested moves (why Bruno received more work, why a looping diagnosis went to a trainee, why Jonas is on Security), so it would not survive a shop-floor challenge to *this* roster.

**Audit and PM4Py.** The indicator list matches the required topics and is the second-best section. Several operationalizations are wrong or hand-wavy: `filter_event_attribute_values(..., [">1"])` does not accept comparison operators; `filter_case_performance(log, "SLA_missed")` and `filter_case_performance(..., "SLA_remaining < 4h")` are not the PM4Py API (it takes numeric min/max duration); Gini workload from `discover_working_together_network` is a non sequitur; `get_service_time(..., activity=...)` is not used with a supported signature. The sketch never calls `discover_performance_dfg`, `discover_batches`, or `get_variants`, which the prompt asked to mention. Training-share code treats `training_priority` as a log column; it is a resource attribute and must be joined. There is audit-only code, no learning from handover cost, skill-mismatch service times, or rework after assignment — despite the prompt’s “learn or audit.”

**Net.** This reads like a competent template filled without a close pass over the matrix, the hard rules, or the author’s own algorithm. Completeness and tone keep it out of the bottom third; the illegal Security assignment, repeated OT violations, false training labels, and the Section 4 / Section 3 contradiction make a high score indefensible.
