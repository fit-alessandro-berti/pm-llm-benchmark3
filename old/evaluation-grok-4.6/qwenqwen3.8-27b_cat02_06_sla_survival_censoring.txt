**7.2**

The final write-up is a strong SLA survival design: estimands are defined before estimators, time origin/horizon are clear, open cases are kept, withdrawal is not treated as ordinary censoring, and escalation/rework are correctly barred as baseline predictors. Concrete case IDs are used well, and the required nine-section shape is followed.

It is not close to flawless.

The multi-state diagram omits an observed transition: S27 is `Review>Rework>Resolve`, but the diagram only allows `Rework → Review`. That is a process-mining error, not a wording quibble.

The third transfer rule is weaker than it looks. For both primary at-72 estimands, “hybrid failure” is numerically the same as “competing terminal.” Only late-resolution CIF can change, so the advertised three-way sensitivity is thinner than claimed.

Section 8 meets the checklist on paper, but the PM4Py path is speculative (`pm4py.OOP.EventLog` / constructor guessing, then a silent manual fallback). If that API is absent, the required control-flow step never actually runs.

Reported CIF/KM shifts (~0.30→0.31, ~0.40→0.44) have no interval, SE, or even a plain statement that a 1pp move on n=32 is indistinguishable. “Cause-specific cumulative incidence” also blurs cause-specific hazards with Aalen–Johansen CIF.

Everything else—case-level encoding, completed-case bias, immortal time, method choice for this extract, operational non-causal reading, and the missing-data list—is competent and internally consistent. The deductions above are why this stays well below a top-band score.
