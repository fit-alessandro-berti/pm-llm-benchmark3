The final write-up is complete, dialectically serious, and mostly well-judged at case level, but it is not nearly flawless. A required POWL revision is semantically wrong on the happy path, several mandated defense points are thin or missing, and a number of statements are garbled or slightly over-claimed.

**What is strong.** All seven required headings are present and used for real work rather than padding. Section 1 separates stipulated facts, disputes, and unproved items, and the burden-of-proof rule (exception-invoker proves authority/window/eligibility; log omission is not non-occurrence) is the right standard for this file. The two briefs do not collapse into a bland compromise: the prosecution is right that PD-3/FD-7 expiry and CS-9 still bite, and the defense is right that M0 cannot carry RMI, overlays, reactivation, rejected leads, or CRM missingness. **Major model-semantics error.** In the revised POWL, the standard route puts  
`OperatorPOWL(LOOP, [Request_More_Info, SilentTransition()])`  
in parallel with Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review. Process-tree/POWL LOOP executes the first child at least once. That makes Request_More_Info **mandatory** on every standard case. Section 6 even calls the loop “optional,” so the text and the code disagree. Canonical CS-9 traces such as SUP-701 have no RMI and would **not** fit `M_revised`. The defensible encoding is `LOOP(tau, Request_More_Info)` or `XOR(tau, LOOP(...))`. The commentary discusses interleaving limits and never states this optionality bug, which is exactly the “hiding the limitation” failure the prompt forbids.

**Other material defects.**
- The defense brief never actually treats **concurrency**: M0 already allows unordered Sanctions_Check ∥ Quality_Review, so order among checks is not a deviation. That was an explicit defense requirement.
- Auto and emergency XOR arms have no reject path; an eligible case that should be refused is inexpressible. That limitation is not declared.
- The evidence matrix for SUP-704 lists “retrospective approval record” as missing even though Manual_Procurement_Approval is in the trace the next business day.
- Operators and relations were stripped in the prose (`requester  manual approver`, `Quality  2 business days`, `spend  EUR 20,000`, arrow-free route sketches). Those lines are unclear as written.
- SUP-715 as NONCOMPLIANT is a stretch: CS-9 does not bar reviewer/approver spouses; the record only shows a missing disclosure. MIXED or an org-control finding with lower confidence would have been tighter. SUP-713’s mapping was approved *after* the case; calling it clean equivalence slightly overstates the file.

**What keeps it well above a middling score.** Case-level calls on authority, expiry, and high-risk bypasses are traceable and mostly right; the prosecution admits M0/CRM defects; the defense admits SUP-703/708/712/714; the overlay table and event schema are usable; the code is otherwise a coherent four-route POWL with a shared notify→close tail and no external I/O.

A near-flawless answer would have encoded optional RMI correctly, stated remaining POWL limits honestly, addressed concurrency in the defense, and kept the CS-9 resource/amount/time predicates readable. This is a strong review with a broken standard-route model.
