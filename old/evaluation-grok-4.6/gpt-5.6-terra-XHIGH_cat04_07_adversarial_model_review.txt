## What is actually strong

Process-tree reading of Model S (`+` of four `X(a, τ)` nodes ⇒ every review optional and unordered) is correct, and the observation that S is *selectively* permissive — it swallows skipped controls but cannot represent `Correct Dossier` — is the right diagnosis. Format compliance is complete: the eleven required headings are present, the metric table has the required columns, and no fabricated numeric results appear.

## Concrete defects

**1. `model_third` is not the model the audit describes.**  
The assigned POWL is a clean first-pass net: mandatory `Credit`/`Technical`, optional `Supplier`/`Expedite`, XOR approval. P3, P6, P7 diagnostics, and the tri-state verdict live only in `validate_third`. Section 8 nevertheless scores the “Third alternative” as enforcing P3/P6/B6/D1/D3. A reader who converts `model_third` and runs alignments will not get those verdicts. The task allowed a hybrid, but the write-up does not keep the executable object and the claimed language apart. There is also no single `accept(trace)` that intersects POWL membership with the checker, so the two layers can drift.

**2. Formal rework text contradicts the checker.**  
The compact specification says affected checks *may* repeat; the later bullet and the implementation *require* every named check to reoccur (`pending_rechecks` must be empty). That is a real semantic bug in the spec, not wording noise.

**3. Section 8 over-claims B6 and D1.**  
`validate_third([R,C,T,Correct,T,M,L], correction_effects={3:{T}})` returns **indeterminate** when `source_type` is omitted (`Supplier` absent). The table says “Accepts.” B1/B2 carefully add the internal/unknown-source caveat; B6/D1 do not. Same function, inconsistent reading.

**4. Sidecar silently tightens P5.**  
“Exactly one approval before release” is rewritten as “approval is the penultimate event.” Anything logged between approval and release — even a harmless completion/archive event — is invalid. That is not in P1/P5.

**5. One anti-S trace muddies a real violation with a legal pattern.**  
`R, C, S, E, T, Auto, L` is forbidden because Expedite precedes Technical. The same row treats “Supplier before a mandatory check” as a defect. P3 does not order Supplier against the checks; P4 explicitly allows Expedite on either side of Supplier. Section 7 later refuses to invent a Supplier-after-checks rule. Section 3 does not follow that discipline.

**6. Optional Supplier in the net recreates a milder S pathology, and projection metrics can be gamed.**  
Expedite *is* optional. Supplier is only optional because the attribute is missing. Putting `XOR(τ, Supplier)` inside `model_third` is exactly the move the prompt warned against, even if the checker later patches it. Token generalization and alignment fitness on that net will still reward “Supplier can vanish.” The proposed fix — validate, project away corrections/rechecks, then score the backbone — will inflate fitness/precision unless those figures are never presented as raw-log conformance. Section 9 asks for two report lines; it does not state that projected-log fitness is not a compliance metric.

**7. The checker is an ad-hoc script, not a maintained constraint layer, and it is untested in the answer.**  
Existence, precedence, and exactly-one approval are ordinary DECLARE (or a data Petri net for `source_type`). Only targeted rework needs custom code. There are no assertions that B1–B8, D1–D3, or the “must vs may” rework rule evaluate as claimed. For a checker this branched, that is a completeness failure, not a nicety.

**8. Smaller nits.**  
Immediate-approval also rejects a second Register/Release only in the slice `labels[1:-2]`, which is fine, but unknown activities after approval never get a tailored reason. Internal cases *with* Supplier are accepted without comment. Credit ∥ Technical is slightly stronger than “either order.” The alignment-cost skeleton is comments around a version-dependent API. “Unmeasured soundness” is a review finding, not a pathology of the reported numbers. None of these would matter if items 1–6 were clean.

## Probe and C-branch checking

I re-derived S and C from the trees. B1–B8 vs S/C in §5 are correct. C has no `R,T,C,Auto,L`, no `…,E,S,…`, no Auto branch with Supplier/Expedite/rework, and only one correction spine (`C,T,Correct,C,T,Manager`). Those claims hold. No credit is lost there.

## Third alternative, net of the bugs

The *idea* is the right family: structural backbone for P1/P2/P4/P5, data guards for P3/P6, refuse to invent attributes, escalate instead of optionalizing Credit/Technical. That is safer than S and more maintainable than C. The execution is a policy engine taped to a permissive POWL, with a spec that disagrees with its own code and an audit table that describes the tape, not the net.

## Scoring

A nearly flawless answer would have kept `model_third`’s language aligned with the audit (or labelled the hybrid at every row), used attribute-conditioned nets or a non-optional Supplier plus `indeterminate` routing instead of `XOR(τ, Supplier)`, made rework obligation unambiguous, unit-tested the probes, and treated projected fitness as radioactive. This is a strong review with a leaky artefact. Under a strict reading that is a 7.3, not a high 8 or 9.
