The answer is structurally complete, operationally serious, and correctly refuses a naïve copy of Site A. It still falls well short of near-flawless: several quantitative and causal claims are sloppy or internally inconsistent, and those errors matter for the exact criteria this task is graded on.

**What is strong.** The core transport argument is right. Site A’s 9-hour median move is treated as a confounded bundle (mix shift, +2 FTE, API upgrade, huddles, better stamps), not as an identified treatment effect. The intervention is redesigned around partial order, not around a copied AND-split: Document Check is not released before Originals Receipt when originals are required; Identity is overlapped with the originals wait; legal/supplement structure is left statutory. Co-interventions are separated rather than laundered into “parallelization.” The bottleneck-shift scenario (median down, p90 up), the IT-lock placebo scenario, and the intermediate-variable logic (true overlap vs. cycle time) are the right causal distinctions. The POWL sketch and the comments on what an untimed net cannot encode (data-dependent legal/originals rules, 4/7/3/2 pools, the case lock, durations) match the modeling brief.

**Process table and graph.** Section 1 covers the required features and the right design consequences, but the columns are messy: after-period numbers are dumped into the before cell (rework, tail), and huddles sit under “same” queue discipline even though they are a post-change co-intervention. The adjacency list includes every mandated node and the `S_` selection pattern, and the 180–280 word gloss is on-mechanism. The graph itself is not a careful structural causal model. `API_latency → true_overlap` is the wrong arrow: latency changes identity service time, not whether intervals can overlap. Capacity nodes are wired into `true_overlap` rather than into waiting and cycle time. `originals_required` affects cycle time through a long exogenous delay even when it does not change overlap; that path is missing. Those are not cosmetic graph-drawing choices. They are the modifiers the rest of the memo depends on.

**Evidence and modifiers.** Section 3 is the best part of the memo: low confidences, mix and FTE treated as non-transferable, huddles scored as portable only if transferred as themselves. Section 4 hits every required failure mode. The digital-row “harmful signature: none specific” is too clean; that stratum still shares the three-assessor queue and the four-person identity pool.

**Quantitative scenarios.** The service-overlap ceiling (`max(3.4, 8.1)` vs. the sum) is the right first-order bound and is correctly used to reject a −7 to −9 hour copy of A. Then the arithmetic gets loose. Digital × non-legal is written as ~25–38% without a joint-distribution argument; under independence it is about 23%, and the same range is later reused for “digital, mostly non-legal.” The 2–4 hour residual ATE at A is asserted, not decomposed. More seriously, wave-1 power assumes an IQR of about 10 hours. That cannot be reconciled with Site B’s median 44 and p90 110 (or Site A’s 41/96). A 2-hour median contrast with ~200 cases per arm is therefore not shown to be detectable, and the expansion trigger (≥2 hours in the first stratum) sits on the floor of the predicted range. The 3.4-hour figure is also a *service* ceiling; if “waiting before Assessment” includes inter-check queueing, the sojourn gain can exceed that ceiling, which the scenario table never keeps straight.

**Pilot and recommendation.** The pilot design is the right shape: strata as modifiers, three treatment versions, overlap as a manipulation check, shared-pool interference, stop rules tied to Section 4. Case-level randomization with four identity and three assessor FTEs makes SUTVA failure the default, not a tail risk; monitoring plus a 15% tripwire is weaker than the problem the memo itself states. The mechanism-vs-implementation paragraph is good and may slightly overrun the 220-word cap. `conditional go` is the only defensible call; the “what not to copy” paragraph is exact.

**PM4Py.** The code follows the required construction path: `parse_process_tree` for A before/after, Petri conversion, a nested POWL with `StrictPartialOrder` plus optional originals and a `LOOP` legal/supplement branch, explicit edges so Assessment waits for both checks, soundness/workflow checks, playout, and an honest non-encoding comment. `LOOP(Legal Review, Request Supplement → doc redo → assessment redo)` is the right control-flow reading of rule 7. Residual fidelity gaps: XOR-skip makes lawful digital skips indistinguishable from unlawful originals skips (noted, not fixed); `play_out` will emit silent labels as `None`; `check_soundness(...)[0]` assumes a tuple API. These are not fatal, but they keep the model from being “nearly flawless.”

Net: this is a competent transport memo with the right refusal, the right segmentation, and a usable pilot skeleton. It is not a 9–10. Wrong arrows in the transport graph, an IQR that contradicts the tail statistics, and elastic eligibility/effect numbers are enough, under a strict bar, to hold the grade at 7.0.
