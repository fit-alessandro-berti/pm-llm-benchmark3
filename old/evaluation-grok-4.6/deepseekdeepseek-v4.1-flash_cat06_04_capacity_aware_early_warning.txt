**7.2**

The answer is a complete, on-format policy that hits the three “strong answer” traps: I-03 is refused despite \(p=0.82\), I-05 is suppressed while I-07 is allowed to re-enter on new evidence, and untreated-case spillover is in the allocation and the impact table. Arithmetic for lower-bound nets and the three scenarios is auditable and mostly correct. That is a solid piece of work, not a near-flawless one.

**Decision-theoretic gaps**

The largest hole is that case ranking is done on *pre-spillover* lower-bound net, then three slots are committed anyway. Under the answer’s own spillover model, a slot costs about €0.9k–€1.8k (optimistic), €1.8k–€3.6k (base), and much more in adverse. I-02’s lower-bound net is ~€950, so it does not cover base spillover and is only roughly break-even at the midpoint (€2.7k vs ~€2.4k per slot at \(6 \times 0.02 \times €20k\)). Naming I-02 as a selected intervention *now*, while the rule also has a post-hoc congestion gate, is internally inconsistent. A coherent robust policy would either stop at I-04 and I-09, or mark I-02 as sequential/conditional on measured queue delay.

Strategic cases are penalized twice: \(p_{\text{adj}} = p_{\text{now}} - 0.085\) *and* “use the lower half of effect ranges.” Segment miscalibration of *observational* breach probability is not evidence that the supplied *effect* ranges are overstated. That stacks conservatism in a way the problem does not support, and it quietly tilts the portfolio against Strategic cases except the very highest-value one.

The first-alert thresholds (\(p_{\text{adj}} \ge 0.45\), or \(\ge 0.35\) with high value) sit beside an expected-value score without being derived from it. I-04’s adjusted probability is ~0.395, so it fails the headline threshold and is still the top pick. The probability cutoffs are therefore not actually doing the work the rule claims.

**Operational realism**

Two specialists deliver 45-minute swarms. Three committed swarms plus coordination is a sequencing problem: I-09 has 2.5 h to the SLA. Parallel vs serial staffing, specialist interruption of ordinary diagnosis, and whether I-09 can actually be reached in time are never treated. Spillover is converted into money; the physical capacity constraint is not.

The €20k average value for the six unlisted diagnosis-stage cases is a necessary placeholder and is disclosed. The adverse spillover top end of €24k is not: \(3 \times 6 \times 0.03 \times €30k = €16.2k\). The extra jump to €24k is an unstated congestion multiplier.

False-alarm burden is subtracted in full in every scenario, matching the prompt’s schematic formula but not a proper expectation (burden should scale with P(unnecessary intervention)). Non-monetary obligations (public-sector duty, regulatory option) appear only as a reserve preference, not in the score.

**I-07 / fairness tension**

I-07 is handled correctly as *re-entry*, then parked as Tier C on EV. That is consistent with the score and weak on the rest of the brief: SME underprediction, 1.5 h to SLA, and a new log. The fairness write-up proposes a low-value set-aside “per 4-slot block,” then does not apply it: the reserve is held for a possible regulatory incident, not for I-07/I-01. Safeguards that are not used on the live portfolio are decorative.

**Impact table and causality**

Optimistic/base/adverse arithmetic for I-04 + I-09 + I-02 is internally consistent (0.69–0.80 / 0.58 / 0.26–0.36 breaches; nets ~€14–21k / €4.4–9.8k / −€6.7k to −€23k after cost and false-alarm). The table still treats the supplied effect ranges as if they were usable causal effects, with only “plausibly” and a confidence column as hedge. The brief forbids that slide. There is no sensitivity to “the model’s reason for alerting was wrong” beyond zeroing one case in adverse.

**What remains good**

Eligibility by stage, repeat-swarm ban, I-06 held for imputation checks, I-08 refused on a negative lower bound despite a large midpoint EV, one slot reserved, I-05 vs I-07 spelled out, and a monitoring plan that covers calibration, IPTW/doubly robust effects, treatment logging, drift, capacity gates, and segment fairness. Those are the right ingredients. They are not assembled tightly enough—especially around internalizing spillover into the *third* slot and not rewriting probability calibration as an effect haircut—to sit in the high band under a strict reading.
