**4.2**

The answer has the right *shape* (six required sections, I-03 excluded, I-05 vs I-07 distinguished, spillover mentioned) and is therefore not a collapse-to-`p_now` failure. Under a strict reading it is still not a coherent decision policy. Several errors would cause the wrong cases to be treated and would reverse the sign of the claimed net impact.

### Critical decision errors

**Repeated *alerts* are treated as if they were repeated *swarms*.** The brief forbids a second swarm unless a materially new event occurred. I-02 and I-09 are second *alerts with no prior swarm* — i.e., the first chance to use the intervention. Marking them ineligible (Tier 3 / “do not intervene now”) is a rule-level mistake. I-05 is the case that should be suppressed; I-02 (Diagnosis Started, 3.0 h, €25k, ARR 0.08–0.22) is among the most time-aligned candidates in the book.

**I-04 is parked as “Wait” because 10 h remain.** That inverts the technology. The swarm is worthless after Diagnosis Complete, so a high-value case still in Triage is in the *open* window, not a reason to delay. Midpoint EV is on the order of \(0.19 \times 60{,}000 - 450 - 2{,}000 \approx €9{,}000\) even before calibration haircuts — the largest direct stake in the portfolio. Sending I-07 (€5k) and I-01 (€9k) instead is not a conservative reading of uncertainty; it is a ranking error.

**I-06 is internally contradictory.** Section 1 says imputed/unclear data (explicitly citing I-06) should default to *suppressed*. Section 2 then puts I-06 in the selected intervene set as Tier 1. The brief already flags that the 0.20→0.52 jump may be an imputation artifact. Selecting it as a firm “Intervene” without a verify-or-hold step is both inconsistent and reckless.

**Time-to-SLA is used as a hard tier cut (≤4 h) without residual-process logic.** I-07 is still in *Triage* with 1.5 h left. A 45-minute swarm, even if it works, leaves Diagnosis Complete → Resolution → Validation in a window that may already be too short. The supplied ARR range can be used, but operational realism required saying so. Two specialists run *one* swarm together; four slots from 12:00 are three hours of sequential swarming, which collides with the 15:00 reserve preference. That capacity geometry is never analyzed.

### Section 3 is not auditable — and the arithmetic is wrong

The required impact table is the place the answer claims to show its work. Two quantitative failures dominate.

1. **“Direct breaches plausibly avoided” of 2–3 / 1–2 / 0–1.** Avoided breaches are the *sum of ARRs*, not the count of treated cases. Optimistic sum of upper bounds is \(0.35+0.30+0.32 = 0.97\) expected breaches, not 2–3. Base ≈ 0.75, adverse ≈ 0.53. Off by a factor of two to three, and it treats a probabilistic reduction as if each case were a Bernoulli that “avoids a breach.”

2. **Spillover harm is off by 10×.** Written formula: \(3 \times 6 \times €40\mathrm{k} \times 0.01 \approx 720\). The product is **€7,200**, not €720 (they multiplied by 40 instead of 40,000). Base and adverse are likewise €10,800 and €21,600, not €1,080 / €2,160. With the corrected spillover, **base net impact for this portfolio is negative** (\(\approx 7{,}015 - 1{,}350 - 10{,}800 < 0\)), and adverse is largely so. The interpretation “likely net positive (~4–8k)” is an artifact of the units error. That is a decision-theoretic failure, not a rounding issue: the table is exactly the artifact that was supposed to discipline slot use under externalities.

Further, false-alarm burden (€250+€300+€50) is omitted from the suggested EV identity; untreated “€40k average” is an average over *alerted* Strategic values, not the six anonymous diagnosis-queue cases in the spillover model; and three slots on the *same* six cases should be modeled as stacked \(\Delta p\) on those six, with a note that the 6 may not be the other table rows.

### Weaker but still material flaws

- **Calibration is applied to the wrong object.** SME underprediction and Strategic overprediction are about *observational \(p\)*, not ARR. Shifting ARR midpoints by an arbitrary \(\pm 0.02\) (wrong magnitude vs 0.04–0.09 / 0.05–0.12, wrong target) is not “robustifying probabilities.”
- **NLLS safeguard “require two senior specialists (not one)”** restates the intervention (already two seniors). It is not a safeguard.
- **Fairness write-up fights the wrong war.** It worries that value-based ranking starves small cases; the chosen portfolio starves the large ones (I-04, cautiously I-08) while spending scarce delay-imposing slots on €5k–€9k cases. Public-sector non-monetary duty is named barely or not at all.
- **Causal-effect plan is a naive treated-vs-untreated gap.** Swarms are assigned to high-risk cases; that comparison is confounded. The pause rule “untreated breach rate > 0.02” is set at the *expected* spillover, so it would fire when the model is working as advertised.
- I-01 is labeled Wait in the table and then included in the “selected intervene” set; I-04, also Tier 2 and far higher EV, is not. Own tiers are not applied.

### What is actually good (and why this is not a 1–3)

I-03 is correctly refused. I-05 (no new event) is not treated like I-07 (new log). Eligibility is not a single \(p\) threshold. ARR is not equated to \(p_\mathrm{now}\). Segment calibration, feedback-loop label leakage, and a multi-channel monitoring plan are present. Reserve-one-slot is framed as a preference. Those are the brief’s “strong answer” checkpoints, and they are why the score is not in the collapse range.

They do not offset: wrong eligibility for I-02/I-09, inverted timing on I-04, contradictory I-06, and a 10× spillover error that flips the portfolio from “net positive” to “net harmful” under the answer’s own model. Strictly, that is not a deployable early-warning policy.
