**7.3**

The final write-up (sections 1–7) is complete, well structured, and largely on-brief: it stays inside the required headings, treats service/quality/resilience as hard constraints, distinguishes justified vs slack-driven air/van use, and keeps recommendations tied to segments, DFG edges, and the given policies. It is not near-flawless. Several grounding errors and a few logical slips are enough, under a strict reading, to keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is done well.** The system boundary is an operational life-cycle cut that matches the log (energy, scrap, packaging, outbound transport, obsolescence) and is honest about what is unevidenced (inbound, returns legs, grid timing, downtime-avoided impact). The indicator set is usable and attribute-native, not generic ESG language. Interventions cover all twelve candidates with target path/segment, mechanism, side-effects, and monitoring evidence. The effects table uses prompt-supported ranges (air 4–6×, consolidation 35–55%, CNC_A 2.8→1.3 kWh/item, heat-treat 1.7× lateness, 6% obsolescence) rather than invented LCA point estimates. Rebound coverage is the strongest part: batching→express rescue, consolidation→air upgrade, packaging→returns, inspection shortcuts→escaped defects, obsolescence write-off, slack-consumption/customer behaviour, CNC_A saturation, exception-log gaming, and out-of-boundary displacement. The package is sequenced (governance and SP1 air misuse first; production core; then consolidation/lanes/promise dates) and correctly refuses a blanket air/van ban.

**Grounding inaccuracies.** These are the main score cuts.

1. Packaging-rework arithmetic is wrong. Inflows are 260 + 360 = **620** (`Package standard/reinforced → Rework packaging`); outflows are 420 + 130 = **550**. The text calls this “550 loops” and then lists the 260/360 edges. That is a DFG error, repeated in the table.

2. Emergency production is mis-attributed. The 760 `Availability check → Emergency production` cases sit with the Critical-downtime path, not Made-to-order Regular. O10 and the effects table treat “fewer 760 emergency starts” as a Regular-MTO promise-negotiation win. That is a process-path mistake.

3. Air volumes are conflated. SP1 violations (~22% of Standard-stock Service-contract ≈ 420 orders) are presented as if they were the 620 `Package standard → Ship air` cases. Those sets are not the same: standard-pack air also includes Regular stock, and Service-contract air can use other pack types. The ~15 kgCO2e/order saving is the **stock-air vs stock-ground path** gap (18.5 vs 3.6), then applied as if it were the SS-SC segment lever (segment averages 7.8 vs 4.2). Path-level deltas and segment-level interventions are mixed.

4. Van intensity is similarly mixed: section 1 treats 38 kgCO2e (Critical-downtime **segment** average) as the van factor; the van **path** is 52. Partial-shipment “second legs of 1,010” also overstates: 790 go straight to pack, 220 to backorder, 160 re-pick, 60 cancel.

**Other defects.** O12’s energy/transport/material cells are marked as physical “no change / enabling,” which blurs intervention effects with governance. Several table cells stack so many caveats that direction is hard to read. A few monitoring statements are looser than the DFG would allow (e.g. treating the 620 air edge as the SP1 KPI). None of this sinks the recommendation, but it is not the precision the prompt asked for.

**Verdict.** Strong multi-criteria redesign, good rebound treatment, correct refusal to greenwash service-critical vans and air. Marked down for DFG arithmetic, wrong path-to-lever mapping on emergency production, and repeated conflation of path averages with segment/intervention volumes. Those are exactly the grounding failures a strict judge should penalize.
