**6.5**

The write-up is ambitious, well structured, and often genuinely competent: it separates flow counts from snapshots, refuses to treat recovery throughput as capacity, keeps Little’s Law off unbalanced windows, and correctly treats raw hourly Fano factors as confounded by the diurnal profile. Those strengths do not offset repeated quantitative errors, a few broken inferences, and over-confidence that the same document elsewhere says is unwarranted.

**Material calculation and consistency failures**

Pooled arrival intensity is stated as \(7.50\) per opening hour (threat 5, §4, Little’s Law matrix). The table sums to \(2619\) arrivals over \(28 \times 12\) hours, so \(\lambda \approx 7.80/\mathrm{h}\). Completions total \(2509\), so \(X \approx 7.47/\mathrm{h}\), not \(7.17\). The paired claims \(L/\lambda = 3.98\,\mathrm{h}\) and \(L/X = 4.16\,\mathrm{h}\) are therefore wrong (about \(3.83\) and \(4.00\)). The same section says weekends are \(14\%\) of arrivals, which *is* \(359/2619\), so the author had the right totals and still published the wrong rates. The Python would not reproduce the prose.

Several other figures are simply off: \(04\textrm{-}16\) is \(129/(7\times 12)=1.536\) completions per FTE-hour, not \(1.49\); mean staffing is \(\approx 5.14\) FTE, not \(\sim 5.4\); weekend median WIP is \(17.5\), not \(19\); weekend capacity share is \(\sim 15\%\), not \(\sim 12\%\). None of these is cosmetic once the text uses them as if computed.

**Logical errors**

“WIP recovered \(\sim 5\times\) faster than the backlog account” is false. From \(04\textrm{-}28\) to \(05\textrm{-}01\), \(\mathrm{mean\_wip}\) falls \(50\to 32\) (\(-18\)) and \(\mathrm{cum\_net}\) falls \(128\to 110\) (\(-18\)). Same decrement. The \(5\times\) is invented.

§4 says the pooled arrival median of \(104\) “sits in a gap between two modes.” It does not. Weekends are \(36\)–\(53\), weekdays \(92\)–\(121\); \(104\) is inside the weekday cluster. The *mean* is nearer the gap; the argument mixes the two.

D1 is declared unstable at \(\rho\in[1.11,1.17]\) with “high” confidence while realised busy fractions of \(0.98\)–\(1.04\) are waved through as “near-saturation.” Busy fraction \(>1\) means the imputed \(E[S]\) and/or \(C\) are inconsistent with the clock. That should shrink the service-time bracket and pull \(\rho\) down, or force an explicit capacity rewrite. The cleaner, distribution-free point—seven consecutive weekdays with arrivals \(>\) completions—is already in the net-flow column and did not need an over-identified \(\rho>1\).

Threat 3 (cum_net peak \(+128\) vs peak WIP \(52\)) is correctly identified as the largest data defect, then under-used. Most of the \(+94\) D1 imbalance never appears in snapshots. That is compatible with abandonment, transfer, or a WIP definition that excludes parked work, which would change the staffing story from “integrating physical queue” to “lost or hidden demand.” Calling A1 Little’s Law **applicable** after saying no level claim is safe is an unresolved contradiction.

**Smaller but real issues**

Effective capacity \(0.80\)–\(0.90\) of FTE is an unlabeled assumption, then used to call every \(\rho\) a lower bound. Lognormal/Weibull means are not bounds on \(E[S]\). “Weekday hours run \(8.3\)–\(9.6/\mathrm{h}\)” confuses daily mean intensity with intra-day rates. \(n_{\mathrm{eff}}\) is “five or six” in one place and \(\approx 7\) from the author’s own AR(1) formula in another. The \(04\textrm{-}21\) peak-hour vs \(8.4/\mathrm{h}\) ceiling check is partly circular because \(8.4\) comes from that day’s own median/p90. In the code, `d.date` is datetime and hourly dates stay strings; the `peak_08_10` audit join is not clearly reproducible.

**What remains strong**

Definitions and units are careful; median service is never sold as flow time; outage and recovery are isolated; the \(09\textrm{:}00\)–\(11\textrm{:}00\) vs labelled \(08\textrm{:}00\)–\(10\textrm{:}00\) audit is sharp; detrended hourly dispersion and the batch-like \(04\textrm{-}21\) profile are the right analysis; the change-point scan is caveated; the load-state alternative is a real rival, not a relabeling; the pooled-staffing policy is a fair Simpson/composition trap. Format and the single Python block match the spec.

That is a strong essay with a broken quantitative spine. Under a strict reading it is not near-flawless, and the errors are concentrated in the numbers the rest of the argument leans on.
