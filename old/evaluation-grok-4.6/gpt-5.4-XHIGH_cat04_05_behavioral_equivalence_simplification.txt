The technical core is unusually strong: loop semantics are handled correctly, the counterexamples are valid in both directions, the P3 cardinalities 76 / 85 / 4 are exactly right, and the P2 abstraction is the right coarsening. Under a hypercritical rubric that still is not enough for a high score. The write-up repeatedly treats informal wording as if it were a definition, asserts computed facts it does not show, and leaves several formal and implementation edges loose.

## What is actually correct

Visible-trace, P3, and P2 notions are usable. Labels are distinguished by occurrence position, not transition identity; P2 then coarsens checks and rework to presence and keeps only the analyze count. That matches the stated dashboard.

The structural contrast is the right one: A ties one check to every loop iteration; B places a single check in parallel with the whole loop. The four witnesses are genuine:

- A1 / A2 need two checks, which B cannot produce.
- B1 puts `Check Revision` after `Approve Revision`, which A cannot produce because approval sits after the `+(Analyze, Check)` join.
- B2 exits the first attempt with `Rework Case` before any check, which A cannot produce.

Optional `X(Approve, tau)` is treated correctly: hiding `tau` does not hide a missing check, and it does create extra B-only interleavings such as B1. The P3 logic in the verdict table is also right: a concrete bounded counterexample *proves* non-equivalence; only a failed search would be inconclusive.

The simplified P2 tree is simpler than both originals (7 nodes, depth 3, no silent leaf) and its α_P2 image matches A and B. The preservation table honestly marks P1 as lost. Section 9 correctly refuses a single replacement for all three views.

## Formal gaps that should have been cheaper to close

**P1 is not a real equivalence notion.** “Same complete visible behaviors under that compliance interpretation” is circular. The attempt-splitting function is never written down, so it is unclear whether P1-equivalence is (i) visible-trace equality, (ii) equality of traces that pass a compliance filter, or (iii) mutual satisfaction of a safety property. In (i) P1 collapses to ≡vis. In (ii) one must say how to segment a B-trace when the single check sits *on* a `Rework Case` boundary. The verdict “not equivalent” is still right, but the notion does not “guide a comparison” the way the prompt required.

**The A-row on check placement overclaims.** “It must occur before any Approve Revision, before Rework Case, and before Close Case” is false globally: the check of iteration 2 occurs after the approval of iteration 1, and a non-final attempt ends at `Rework Case`, not `Close Case`. The qualifier “within each revision attempt” only partially rescues this. A formal statement would have been: in iteration *i*, `{Analyze, Check}` both complete before that iteration’s optional approve and before the following `Rework`/`Close`.

**α_P2 is slightly more brutal than P2’s text.** P2 still observes the Analyze/Rework skeleton; it only hides Analyze/Check *interleaving*. After hiding approve and deleting checks, that skeleton is forced to `Analyze (Rework Analyze)*` in both models, so the 5-tuple happens to be enough. That justification is missing. The equivalence claim is therefore true but under-argued.

## Code and “derived” numbers

The BFS is the right algorithm and, *for these two nets*, it will accept every P3-complete trace, because every complete trace ends with visible `Close Case` and that firing is the final marking. It is still not the general procedure the comments advertise. Once `len(trace) == 12` the search refuses further silent moves. Any conversion in which a silent loop-exit or AND-join remains after the last visible event would drop those traces. That case is not discussed.

`check_soundness(...)[0]` assumes a tuple return; several pm4py versions return a bare bool. `marking_signature` keys on `place.name`, which is safe only if the converter’s names are unique. Section 6 is not self-contained (`tree_a` / `net_a` are assumed) and is never executed, so footprints and EMD contribute no evidence.

The simplicity scores `0.8181818182` and `0.7777777778`, the claim that “A yields a larger net than B”, and the simplified model’s `1.0` are presented as derived. No place/transition/arc counts appear. Node count, depth, and leaf counts *are* independently checkable and are correct (12/12, 5 vs 6, 6+1, 0 duplicates). The Petri-net figures are not. Reporting exact floats without the underlying net statistics is exactly what the prompt told the author not to do unless they were derived in the answer.

The 76/85/4 figures are combinatorially correct (A: 4 + 16 + 56; B: 5 + 20 + 60; intersection is exactly the four `n = 1` traces that are not `Analyze, Approve, Check`). The answer never shows that counting argument. It attributes the numbers to the BFS. A reviewer cannot see an independent check, and the BFS as published is one silent-edge case away from disagreeing with those numbers.

## Simplification choice

P2 is the *only* view under which A and B coincide, so it is the easy target. The prompt allows that, but “most appropriate” is argued only as “the view that makes them the same.” Operational compliance is the view that actually distinguishes the models, and it is the view a simpler model could still have preserved, for example

```text
->('Open Case', * (->('Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', X('Approve Revision', tau)), 'Rework Case'), 'Close Case')
```

That would have been a semantics-preserving simplification of A under a tightened P1 (analyze then check). The author notes the tightening in §9 and then does not offer the tree. Choosing the weakest view and calling the result “the” simplified model is defensible, not impressive.

Regression/negative traces for the P2 tree are valid but shallow: they only test the loop-plus-mandatory-check skeleton, not the abstraction boundary (e.g. that a mid-sequence check is *intentionally* outside the observation).

## Net verdict

Nothing in the required nine-section shape is missing, and the hard semantic facts are right. The answer still falls well short of “nearly flawless”: P1 is underspecified, one structural cell is sloppy, the bounded explorer is more special-case than advertised, several numeric claims are unshown, and the proposed simplification preserves the view that did not need preserving. Those are individually small; together they are enough to keep the score out of the high band.
