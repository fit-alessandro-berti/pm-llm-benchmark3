**6.2**

The headline timeline is largely right: April is a pipeline/schema break, June is a mix/routing change, August–September is overlapping process/concept/population pressure, and October is not a clean performance month. That is the core of the assignment, and the six-section scaffold is complete. Under a strict reading, that is not enough. The answer repeatedly blurs the drift taxonomy it is supposed to separate, invents numbers, misstates bias direction, and fails the only fully checkable deliverable (the PM4Py sketch).

**The central taxonomy is muddled.** The model scores immediately after diagnosis. Later `Order Part → Part Received` time is not a prefix feature. A supplier shock after that prefix changes \(P(Y \mid X)\) even if the DFG path is unchanged. That is concept drift *caused by* process drift. The answer instead writes that “if pure process drift (longer wait), the model should adapt,” and in July it claims the model is “capturing the supplier-delay signal correctly (via wait-time features).” Those wait times are not available at the stated prediction point. The same confusion reappears in Test 5: a stable coefficient of post-diagnosis wait on breach is taken as “covariate drift, not concept drift.” For the deployed prefix model, stable \(P(Y \mid \text{wait})\) plus a large shift in wait is exactly a change in \(P(Y \mid X_{\text{diagnosis}})\). Covariate drift is also the weakest row: it is parked on a hypothesized lagged queue feature in August, while the clearer \(P(X)\) stories (June mix, Partner Z profile, April’s corrupted prefix features) are under-used.

**Several empirical claims are wrong or fabricated.** September’s 0.31 is called “plausibly inflated” because unresolved cases leave the denominator. The audit says missing labels disproportionately hit long paths; that **deflates** the observed rate, which is also how the October inversion is later (correctly) read. August’s V3 move from 0.46 to 0.64 is a 39% relative increase, not “76%.” October’s 0.16 does not “match January” (0.18). Partner Z labels are said to mature in “single-digit days”; the prompt only says they mature more slowly. The 70%/20% unresolved split, the 0.08 residual threshold, and the 20pp/16pp wait-vs-residual split are invented and then treated as if they were evidence. April is listed as “clean pipeline” training data in one place and as unsafe until recomputed in another. The hold-out plan trains on January–July and then validates on January–February.

**Calibration language is sloppy.** Predicted 0.19 vs observed 0.28 is systematic underestimation of risk, not being “underconfident in bad outcomes.” Test 3’s “overconfident” reading of the same pattern repeats the error. June as “partial” pipeline failure is weakly supported (unknown activity 1%, missing queue 2%). The table is also careless on its face: `3552%`, `223542%`, `65148 h`, `34384252 d` are unreadable without reconstructing the missing arrows.

**Section 5’s code misses the spec it was graded on.** It never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `pm4py.filter_time_range`, or `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`. It never builds both an event-calendar cohort and a case-start cohort with different interpretations. The supposed case-start cohort first slices events to the calendar month, then takes the first remaining timestamp as start, so multi-month cases are truncated and starts can be mis-dated. Dedupe uses an invalid `subset` expression; `eval` on variant keys is unsafe; `discover_performance_dfg` is computed and ignored; a 30-day maturity cutoff is too short given 33–52 day delays; outcome is taken from an arbitrary first-event row. Alert-suppression design in the prose is one of the better parts of the answer. The missing first-class intervention/deployment monitor is a smaller miss next to the code.

**What still works.** The month-by-month narrative refuses a single-cause story. April’s prediction–label gap with a stable 0.19 breach rate is read correctly. June’s V2 0.09 rate is used to reject concept drift. Partner Z is correctly kept out of the August explanation. October is not treated as comparable to mature months. Tests usually state two hypotheses. The staged “do not train on October / do not calibrate April / do not retrain June” decisions are proportionate. The six risk modes are the right failure modes, even when the surrounding numbers are unreliable.

The piece looks like a strong audit until you check definitions, arithmetic, and the required code. Those are not polish issues on this rubric.
