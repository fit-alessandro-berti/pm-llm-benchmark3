**6.3**

The final nine-section write-up is a serious design, not a benchmark-chasing shortcut. It does the hard things the prompt is grading for: default is not defined for rejected/withdrawn/open cases; the 21,600-row export is treated as a selected, matured slice rather than the population; validation is temporal; leakage is discussed as a first-class problem. It is not nearly flawless. Several errors sit at the center of what the task asked to get right.

**What is actually strong.** The primary estimand (90-day default after activation, right-censored, among accounts that activate under the observed policy) is operationally and statistically coherent, and it is labeled as conditional rather than sold as “default for every application.” The alternatives (multistate routing; explicitly assumption-heavy counterfactual) answer different questions. The 48,000-row flow arithmetic is correct (9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 5,100 + 25,200). Rejected/withdrawn/transferred/open cases are competing or undefined outcomes, not fake negatives. The five missingness mechanisms are separated, with a real testability ladder and Manski-style sensitivity for partner feeds. The leakage table covers every mandated field with keep/repair/drop rules that are mostly right (`final_limit` / `decision_reason` / `collection_status` / labels / future events). Random splits are correctly rejected for policy mixing, migration duplicates, sibling checkpoints, and immature labels. Limitations distinguish association, selection, and counterfactuals, and the deployment feedback loops are real.

**Target and cohort (real gaps, not nits).** Conditioning the study cohort on *eventual* activation uses post-prediction information to choose who is in the sample. That is allowed for this estimand, but the business ask is an early warning on *every* application. The text flags the mismatch; it does not give a deployable joint rule (activation model × conditional default) beyond “declare the extra assumption.” The 125-day sentinel (90+35) ignores `label_available_at`, a field that exists specifically to time delayed feeds. Administrative censoring is called independent “by design,” but remaining follow-up is a function of start date, and start date is entangled with the 1 July 2025 policy break—so independence holds only after conditioning on era/start time, which is not stated cleanly. Permanent missingness among mature activations is discussed, but the given counts already exhaust 25,200, so that mechanism is not identified in this snapshot unless the export/log differ.

**Validation design is the main statistical flaw.** Train through 30 June 2025 is a defensible pre-policy fit. Holdout as starts 1 Oct–31 Dec 2025 is not. Extract date is 15 Feb 2026; activation + 90 + 35 days is mature only for activations through ~13 Oct 2025. Almost no October–December *starts* can be headline-evaluated. The write-up notices the matured post-policy sample is “thin,” then still parks headline metrics on that empty-ish slice and waves at “shadow mode.” The window that can actually carry post-policy *and* mature labels is roughly July–early October starts—the calibration fold—not the holdout. Intercept-only recalibration is also too weak if the policy change altered the prefix–outcome map, which the prompt says it did.

**Section 9 does not implement the as-of prefix discipline the prose requires.** This is the largest implementation failure.

- `features_until(pd.Timestamp("2025-05-31"))` is a **global calendar cut**, not a per-case prediction time (first Credit Review, k-th event, or start+Δ). A case that started in January 2024 contributes its **full** history through May 2025—including Contract Signed, Account Activated, collections, and default. That is exactly the leakage the leakage-audit section claims to prevent.
- `f[f["n_events"] == k_anchor]` after that aggregation keeps cases whose *entire history up to the global cut* has length k. It does not extract length-k prefixes.
- `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is called and then discarded; the feature table is a separate groupby.
- The “blacklist” check is inverted: `NEVER_FEATURES.intersection(ev.columns)` is non-empty by schema (`default_90d`, `collection_status`, `final_limit`, …), so the assert fires on any faithful CSV. Banned columns are never dropped; the check is not “are they in the feature table?”
- `demo = convert_to_event_log(tr_ev.head(20000))` samples **events**, not cases, so traces are arbitrarily truncated.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is fed an EventLog; the usual API expects a dataframe.
- `utc=True` followed by `.dt.tz_localize(None)` is the wrong tz teardown (needs `tz_convert(None)` on a tz-aware series).
- Crude incidence uses the whole fold if any row is eval-eligible, not the eligible subset, and mixes non-activated rows into a default rate.
- `documents_total` as-of repair is described in the table and absent from the code.

A skeleton is allowed to be incomplete. It is not allowed to contradict the study’s own prefix and leakage rules while presenting itself as the audit pipeline.

**Smaller issues that still count under a strict bar.** Resource “experience” is repaired with historical volume, which is not tenure. Duplicate detection misses same-activity duplicates with jittered timestamps (the failure mode a migration actually produces). Service/waiting features are unjustified if the log has no start/complete lifecycle. Policy-era is smuggled in as a static feature without saying whether the model is allowed era-specific intercepts. Four DGP-change mechanisms are listed after “Include at least three.” Prose on combining scores is careful; the code never builds an activation model or a competing-risk table, only comments.

**Net.** Conceptually this is well above a weak answer (no fake non-defaults, no “just fit the 21,600,” no random split as the protocol). It is well below a high score because the holdout cannot mature, `label_available_at` is unused, and the PM4Py pipeline would leak post-prediction events into X. Those are not editorial nits; they are the assignment’s grading axes.
