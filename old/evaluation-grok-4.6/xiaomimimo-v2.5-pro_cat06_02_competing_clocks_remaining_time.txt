**4.6**

The write-up has the right skeleton—six required sections, `max` rather than a sum at the join, path envelopes, calendar pause for chemistry, and a dependence discussion—but the actual forecast is not reliable enough for a high score.

The remaining-time numbers in §4 are wrong by about a full day. Friday 15:30 → Monday 13:00 is **69.5 h**, not 45.5 h; Monday late afternoon is ~73–75 h, not 48–50 h; Tuesday 09:00 is ~89.5 h, not 65.5 h. The clock dates can still be read off the stage logic, but the required hour ranges are systematically short. That is a basic temporal-arithmetic failure in the headline forecast.

QA calendar treatment is also wrong, and it contaminates the join and the “smooth” scenario. QA is open Friday 15:30–20:00 (one reviewer) and Saturday 09:00–13:00 (one reviewer): about 8.5 reviewer-hours before Monday. Five batches at a 1.5 h median is about 7.5 h of work, so the *current* queue is likely mostly cleared over Friday evening and Saturday. New work arrives during those same opening hours at 0.35/h. Treating the Monday queue as “the same five batches,” and asserting weekend arrivals are “zero or negligible,” contradicts the prompt and fails the required future-join queue analysis. Scenario 1’s dominant clock (“QA backlog until ~11:45”) and the Monday 13:00 earliest time are artifacts of that error.

Parallel-clock dominance is overstated. On the no-rework path, micro readout is Monday ~08:30–09:30 unless weekend on-call already finished it; chemistry under risk-priority is roughly Monday 08:30–11:30. Those intervals overlap. Declaring chemistry “the slower clock” and calling on-call “moot” erases the micro/staffing floor that should govern the smooth path. On-call only moves terminal time a little if chemistry is late, but it is not irrelevant when chemistry is early.

Quantile construction does not match the author’s own path table. Documentation queries (0.15–0.30) and investigations are more probable than a chemistry rerun that crosses 18:00, so a P80-like band ending Tuesday 09:00 is too tight. The P90/severe band is then loaded onto laboratory investigations (a few percent after mixing rates), which is closer to a far tail than P90. “Triples” for excursion-driven failure rates is an unexplained knob.

Smaller issues: §6 first frames Analyst B choosing B-417 versus Urgent, then describes the more coherent A-takes-B-417 story; contamination +20–30 h is collapsed to “Tue ~09:00”; rejection is mentioned only in passing. Structure and uncertainty typing are competent. The calendar, queue-at-join, remaining-hour, and bottleneck errors are not.
