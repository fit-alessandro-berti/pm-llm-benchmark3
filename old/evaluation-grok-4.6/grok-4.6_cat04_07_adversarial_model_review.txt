**8.5**

The answer is a strong governance review: it separates use cases, refuses a single score, reasons from language membership, designs a genuinely better third model, and writes a real dissent. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, the remaining defects are concentrated in technical wording, incomplete artifacts, and a few overclaims.

## What holds up

Use-case separation in §1 is the right frame. The metric table does not average scores and correctly treats the 80/20 numbers as unreproducible indications. Probe membership for B1–B8 is right on both submitted expressions. Pathologies are ranked by decision impact rather than listed. The third backbone (`RD · (CC ∥ TR) · (CD · recheck)* · (SR? ∥ ER?) · (MA | AA) · RO`) is safer than S and more maintainable than C. P3/P6 are over-approximated only at the missing attributes, not by optionalizing mandatory checks. A1–A3 distinguish the three models. §11 is a serious conservative rebuttal, not a token objection.

## Defects that block a top score

**1. Central fitness claim is slightly wrong.** Skipping Credit Check / Technical Review on S is not “near-perfect” replay. With `X(activity, tau)`, those skips are typically **cost-0 silent model moves**, so B3-class traces have **fitness 1.0** and sit inside the 0.992, not in the 0.8% residual. The residual is the alphabet hole (Correct Dossier, repeats). The pathology is therefore worse than the wording: the violations Compliance cares about are scored as healthy. The answer understands the mechanism elsewhere; the wording still understates the camouflage.

**2. Spec/code mismatch on P6.** `declare_hooks` promises `next_recheck_set_equals_attribute`. `posthoc_policy_flags` only flags a missing `affected_checks` attribute. When the attribute exists, the wrong recheck arm is not checked. That is the exact decision F6 asked to make transparent.

**3. Validation skeleton does not implement the ship-blocker.** §9 and §10 reject a compliance model if a must-reject probe is in the language. `evaluate_candidate` never computes language membership or negative-suite precision; those checks are comments. Cost-sensitivity and play-out are described, not sketched. The prose is stronger than the artifact the board was told to run.

**4. Third-model sequencing is tighter than P1–P7 and is under-sold.** Repairs are locked between the initial AND and the SR/ER block. `RD, CC, TR, SR, CD, TR, MA, RO` (supplier review surfaces a dossier error) is rejected. §7.5 mentions SR “during an open correction,” not SR-then-correct, which is the operationally obvious case. The choice is defensible; calling the gap undocumented is not a full defense.

**5. A3 is over-read as policy.** P3 constrains *whether* Supplier Review occurs, not that it cannot precede the mandatory checks. Rejecting A3 is a frequent-variant convention shared with C, not a written rule. The later hedge (“if Operations later proves…”) should have been the claim, not the afterthought.

**6. C’s 0.973 training fitness is over-interpreted.** “Already drops some training variants” is likely, not shown. Incomplete cases, start/end cuts, or duplicate-label alignment effects could produce the same number. The XOR-of-eight expression already proves incompleteness; the 0.973 is treated as confirmation it cannot provide.

**7. Smaller unclarities.** S is called “flower-like”; it is a skippable parallel block, not a flower (no arbitrary repeats). “Detective-blocking” blurs alert vs hard stop. The third model reintroduces silent transitions and cloned check labels—the same mechanisms §6 criticizes—without saying how the board should read *its* fitness/precision. `pm4py` is imported and unused. Equivalence of the POWL object and the commented tree string is asserted, not checked.

## Net

The recommendation is still the right one: S is an exploration map, C is a variant catalog, neither is a control, and the third model is the only candidate that encodes P2/P4/P5/P7 without memorizing eight movies. The misses are not inverted membership or metric worship. They are the difference between an excellent dossier and a board-ready one: one wrong fitness implication, one unimplemented rule, one unevaluated ship-blocker, and two places where modeling convention is sold as policy.
