4.6

The answer follows the required heading structure and, at a high level, avoids the two dumbest failure modes (treating every same-user case as an automatic objective failure, and treating every distinct-user case as an automatic pass). That is the main reason it is not substantially lower. Under a strict reading, however, it is not close to a high score: it contains multiple factual misreads of the case file, internally contradictory control theory, an incomplete sufficiency test, and a Section 7 implementation that does not do what was asked and would not run.

**Section 1 is too coarse and mis-assigns control roles.**  
FEP-1 is decomposed into five plausible threat classes, but the prevent/detect/correct mapping is sloppy. MG-4 is labeled detective/corrective for unauthorized transfer as if timing did not matter; before release or before settlement it is primarily preventive of irrevocable loss. AV-2’s coverage is understated (operator limit, ownership, 7-day bank-change, PO match) and overstated (a pass is treated as if it addressed “unauthorized/erroneous transfer” generally, despite the brief’s explicit statement that AV-2 does not evidence receipt of goods, commercial reasonableness, or who controlled a compromised account). ED-1 is said to have “no coverage” of collusion because it “relies on AV-2”; that is wrong. ED-1’s independent MG-4 condition is the main anti-collusion element in the emergency path. RA-7 is called corrective for bank compromise without the brief’s timing caveat: it is detective, and only partly corrective inside the recall window. Shared-service-account independence, rubber-stamped MG-4, and override-as-coverage-failure are missing from the decomposition even though they are central to later cases.

**Section 2 does not deliver the requested test.**  
The prompt asked for four sufficiency grades: fully equivalent, partially equivalent, merely mitigating, and ineffective. “Merely mitigating” is omitted. The fully-equivalent checklist is a list of words (timing, independence, coverage…) rather than an operable test: it never states which combination of conditions is necessary versus sufficient, nor how to score a package that is timely and independent but narrower than FEP-1. The claim that AP-207 is the exemplar of partial equivalence because “MG-4 is preventive but late” is confused: in AP-207 the manager acted after release and *before* settlement, which is the stronger of the two post-release timings. The “same expected loss” discussion is a one-sentence non-explanation. The point that needed to be made is that equal expected monetary loss is not equivalent control: packages can share EL while differing in residual threat mix, independence, auditability, deterrence, overrideability, and recoverability. That argument is not made.

**Section 3 gets the directional call on most cases and then undermines it with control-role errors.**

- **AP-201 — OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET** is a defensible “technically nonconforming but tolerable” call (low amount, independent manager before settlement, clean vendor). It is then justified with a false statement: “Preventive controls: None.” AV-2 fully passed. The manager action at 08:30 also sat *before* the 10:00 settlement, so there was still a chance to stop irrevocability; calling corrective capacity “None (settled)” erases that window. “Missing evidence: Emergency token absence” is not missing; the case file states there was no token. Residual risk is framed as “no fraud evidence” rather than as a remaining same-user convenience path.
- **AP-202 — OBJECTIVE_FAILED** is correct, but AV-2 is described as having “blocked” the payment. It emitted a *warning*. The operator then used a manager override credential. Those are different control failures (warning + stolen/shared override vs. a block that was forced through). Independence, amount, new vendor, same-day bank change, post-recall audit, and unrecovered loss are handled better than the AV-2 wording.
- **AP-203 — OBJECTIVE_MET** is reasonable and closest to a clean ED-1 package. “Missing evidence: No collusion risk” is not missing evidence.
- **AP-204 — OBJECTIVE_FAILED** is arguable because a fabricated receipt is a realized objective failure, but the write-up is overconfident. The brief says the pair “deny collusion,” that they may be a dedicated two-person cell, and that organizational design documents are missing. High confidence plus “collusion likely” oversells the evidence. AV-2 is listed as a relevant preventive; AV-2 does not test receipt authenticity, so a clean pass is almost orthogonal to the defect. The log also has **no MG-4 at all**; that absence is not called out. Severity “Medium” on a €220k fabricated-receipt payment after the recall window is soft.
- **AP-205 — LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION** is correct.
- **AP-206 — OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET** is a fair indeterminate-leaning call, but ED-1 is treated as a preventive control that merely had a “late token.” Condition 1 is binary: the token must be active *before* Prepare_Payment. On the recorded times, ED-1 is not met. The residual-risk story should be “emergency path used without a satisfied permission,” not “ED-1 almost worked.”
- **AP-207 — OBJECTIVE_MET** matches the brief’s “plausible ED-1 use,” but it is scored identically to AP-203. Pre-release approval with documentary review is stronger than post-release ratification. That distinction is discarded.
- **AP-208 — OBJECTIVE_FAILED** is correct and correctly does *not* treat a later “legitimate payment” finding as conformance. Good. The missing-evidence cell (“Bank-change audit”) is vaguer than the actual gap: no reliable bank-ownership evidence *at release time*, plus an unsigned checklist and AV-2 down.
- **AP-209 — OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET** is the right refusal to equate form with objective, but “same as AP-204” is too strong: AP-204 has a fabricated receipt, AP-209 does not. Again there is no manager event in the log; later the code invents `before_release` for this case.

Independence, recoverability, and case risk are used, but not consistently. Several cells equate “no defect found” with low residual process risk, and “a control occurred” with “the control addressed the relevant threat.” That is exactly what the prompt said not to do.

**Section 4 contradicts itself on the question it was required to answer.**  
The brief asked whether manager action after Release_Payment but before settlement is preventive, detective, or hybrid. The table asserts both: one row calls all pre-settlement MG-4 preventive; the next calls post-release MG-4 detective/corrective and cites AP-201. There is no chosen semantics, only collision. The better account is hybrid: detective of the release decision, still preventive of irrevocable settlement if the manager can stop or recall before the bank cuts off. RA-7 is flattened to “after settlement,” which is false for the general control (and the 24-hour recall fact is what makes early RA-7 partly corrective). ED-1 is called a preventive control that “combines” RA-7; RA-7 is a post-hoc condition of a permission, not a pre-release preventer.

**Section 5 is thin and over-certain.**  
Five cases meets the minimum. Counterfactuals are stated as if an independent second releaser “would” detect the bank change, validate receipts, or enforce ED-1 timing. AV-2 had already flagged AP-202’s bank change; the break was override plus a conflicted supervisor, not non-detection. An independent releaser is not a guarantee of receipt inspection (AP-204) and would not resolve a logging-delay hypothesis (AP-206). Qualitative High/Medium/Low ranges are used, which is good; invented precision is avoided, also good. What remains exposed is mostly restated as the original threat rather than the residual after the actual package.

**Section 6 invents thresholds and blurs alert versus proof.**  
Same-user >5%, override >2%, pair >3 times in 30 days, and similar cutoffs are arbitrary and, for a dedicated payment cell, would either alarm constantly or be gamed. The required distinction between alert and proof is attempted and then broken: the key sentence is missing the negation (“Alerts … proof”), and the table treats bank-change proximity as “account compromise” and late MG-4 as misconduct. Those are risk indicators, not proof. Shared-workstation and repeated-pair monitoring is directionally right; the operationalization is not.

**Section 7 is a miss on a fully specified artifact.**  
Required: full embedded CSV via `io.StringIO`, UTC parse, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, "Prepare_Payment", "Release_Payment")`, separate identification of blocked attempts, join to constructed case facts, flags for emergency eligibility / AV coverage / manager timing / audit timing / bank change / repeated pairs, and an explicit “evidence not verdict” label.

What was delivered: a truncated CSV (AP-206–AP-209 omitted, marked “simplified”), unused XES import, no `pm4py.format_dataframe`, a nonstandard and likely invalid `log_filter.filter_four_eyes_principle` path, a malformed `log_converter.apply(..., parameters={"parameters": {"format": "df"}})`, datetime parsing without a UTC guarantee, an invented `filter_trace_attributes` call, DataFrame-style access on what would be an EventLog, a broken double merge, and an `emergency_eligible` rule that ignores vendor age, bank-change, AV-2 operational-without-override, token-before-prepare, and one-business-day audit. Case-facts then fabricate manager timing of `before_release` for AP-204 and AP-209, where no manager activity exists, and treat AP-206’s late token as `emergency_token=True` for eligibility. The version caveat on four-eyes polarity is the only solid engineering note. As written, this is not executable checking code and cannot be the preliminary evidence layer the brief asked for.

**Net.**  
The skeleton of a control-objective review is present, and the headline classifications for AP-202, AP-203, AP-205, AP-207, AP-208, and the form-versus-objective split on the u12/u13 pair are in the right neighborhood. That is not enough. The analysis repeatedly misstates what AV-2 did, applies ED-1 where its first condition fails, cannot keep preventive/detective/hybrid timing straight, drops a required sufficiency category, and replaces the mandated PM4Py reconstruction with non-running, incomplete sample code. Those are not nits; they are failures on the exact dimensions the judge was told to score.
