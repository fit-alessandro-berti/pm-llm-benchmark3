**6.2**

The write-up follows the required seven-heading format, reasons from decision times rather than start-order folklore, labels assumptions, and is strongest on QA (FIFO plus appointment insertion) and QD (skill routing plus per-resource FIFO and availability). Those parts, plus sections 5–7, are competent process-mining work. Under a strict reading it is not close to excellent.

**Eligible-set reconstruction — the core task — is incomplete.**  
QC is the worst case. At 10:10 the table lists only C01 and C02; C03 (P2, 10:05) and C04 (10:07) are already waiting. That produces a false priority prediction (“tie (3)”). The opening choice is C02, which **rejects FIFO (C01) and priority (C03)** and matches SPT. Missing C03 hides the single best identification test on that queue. At 10:18, C04 and C05 are omitted. At 10:32, C06 (arr 10:25, est 6) is omitted; SPT would have picked C06, not C04. QA at 08:30 likewise drops A05 (arr 08:21). The task asked for plausibly waiting jobs at each start; those omissions are not cosmetic.

**The section 1 “contradicting episode” column is misused.**  
For QA, QC, and QD the cited “contradictions” are mostly evidence against *priority*, not against the stated primary (FIFO / skill+FIFO). A04 after A02 *supports* FIFO. C05 after C04 *supports* FIFO-with-C01-deferred. D08 after D04 *supports* per-resource FIFO. That is a conceptual error in the required table, not a wording slip.

**QC identifiability is underplayed, then over-sold.**  
C02→C03→C04 is *both* FIFO among non-C01 jobs *and* SPT (ests 8, 14, 22 arrived in that order). The answer mentions SPT in cells but still treats “FIFO + defer oldest” as the main story without stating that the first three jobs cannot separate FIFO from SEPT. The global start order C02–C06–C01–C07 does fit “FIFO except C01 last among the opening backlog,” so the *label* is defensible; the *identification argument* is not.

**QB is directionally right and over-simplified.**  
Preemption of B01 by B02, resume at 09:31 (over P2 B03), reassignment to B03 at 09:48, then B05 (P1) at 10:00 is a real hybrid. Calling the primary “FIFO” is a stretch: B05 jumping B04 is not FIFO, and resuming B01 at 10:14 over B06 (P2, arr 10:05) and B07 (appointment 10:30) is a first-class decision that never appears in the QB table. Scan-delay for B05 at 09:48 is allowed by the prompt but is used as a patch rather than carried as residual uncertainty. Confidence 60 is fair; the narrative is not.

**QD misses a work-conservation hole.**  
D-F is idle 11:36–12:00 while D10 (P1, electrical, est 12) is waiting and D-F can do electrical. Delaying D05 until 12:20 to avoid the meeting is well argued; not explaining why D-F does not take D10 in the pre-meeting window is not. D-X also idles 11:20–11:30 for D06’s appointment while D08 (P1, mechanical) is waiting. Appointment over priority is a feature, but it should have been a named contradicting episode for any claim that skill+FIFO fully accounts for assignment. Confidence 90 is too high.

**Smaller factual/language problems.**  
QA service order is *not* “arrival order exactly” (A03 jumps A02). The QA identifiability cell says priority delays are “fully explained by FIFO,” which is successful identification, not an identifiability problem. Section 4’s QC test contrasts only FIFO vs priority/deadline and again ignores C06/SPT. No invented rows, but several decision sets are not the sets the data imply.

**What still holds.**  
Assumptions A1–A2 are properly flagged. Preemption vs non-preemption is handled. Appointment is treated as exact-time on A03/D06 and soft/late on A06/B07. Exceptions in §5 are ranked and diagnostically relevant. The collection plan in §6 maps onto the actual ambiguities. The synthesis that priority is the most dangerous claim to read off these traces is correct.

The paper looks like a solid analyst draft with a broken QC decision log and a table column that does not mean what it is supposed to mean. That is well above a shallow label-the-queue answer and well below a nearly flawless reconstruction.
