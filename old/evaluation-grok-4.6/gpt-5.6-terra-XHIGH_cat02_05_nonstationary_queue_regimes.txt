**8.3**

This is a strong, internally consistent process-mining analysis with careful estimand definitions, mostly accurate arithmetic, and the right refusal to treat recovery or the pooled 28-day window as a stationary queue. It is not near-flawless. Several completeness and inference gaps keep it well below a top score under a strict reading.

**What holds up.** Opening-hours conventions, the \(A\) vs \(C\) distinction, and the refusal to treat median/p90 service as mean flow time are all correct. The +110 net imbalance and the Apr 24/25 stock-flow contradiction are real and well framed as assumption failures rather than a point estimate of backlog. Regime totals I recomputed (early weekday 701/697, high-mix weekdays 798/704, recovery 211/241, \(q_{50}\) screens, Fano/CV, Little’s ratios) match the prose. Outage and recovery are not treated as ordinary high-throughput samples. Little’s Law is denied an `applicable` verdict everywhere, with units and clock stated. The Simpson-style FTE–WIP reversal and the weekday/weekend mixture artifact in daily Fano are the right pooling critiques. The Python is self-contained and matches the required checklist.

**Material analytical gaps.**

1. **The Apr 20 “break” is over-sharpened.** Apr 15–17 already show rising `complex_pct` (25–27), longer service (31/68, 33/72), and high WIP (47, 52) before the 41% jump. A two-step, outage-triggered, or coding-change story is left largely uncompared with the clean low-mix / high-mix cut. That matters because the primary segmentation treats 15–17 as pure disruption/recovery and 20+ as a new demand/mix regime.

2. **The `peak_08_10_pct` vs hourly-vector conflict is discovered and then under-used.** The mismatch is genuine (e.g. 2026-04-07: 16.8% vs 31%; 2026-04-21: vector 08–10 is 11/115, not 47%). Two interpretations follow: the vectors are true clock hours and the peak field is wrong, or the vectors are stylized “representative” shapes scaled to daily totals. Burstiness claims and the 10:00/13:00 staffing recommendation lean on the vectors as if (1) were settled. The operational section only says to reconcile before an 08–10 rule; it does not carry the ambiguity into the Fano table or the Apr 21 “58 arrivals in two hours” claim.

3. **Capacity pressure is one-sided.** \(q_{50}\) uses arrivals × daily median. In backlog-building high-mix weekdays that is an offered-load screen (~0.94), not a handled-load screen (~0.83 if completions are used). The text warns that \(q_{50}\) is not utilization, but never shows that sensitivity. In a recovery or intervention window the same choice tilts the story.

4. **Small-n regimes are reported to one decimal as if stable.** Three post-start weekdays and two weekends get 115.0, 121.0, 37.0, \(q_{50}=0.76\) with no min–max or day-level range in the main table. Confidence labels help; they do not replace the “use ranges when means are unsupported” requirement.

**Smaller defects.** “At least the initial stock plus 88” is exactly \(I_0+88\) under the stated conservation assumptions. Little’s ratios are computed in code for windows the matrix calls `not defensible` (comments save this from being a contradiction, not from being messy). Lag-1 correlations are left as “about 0.41 / 0.61” in prose despite exact code. Pre-period completions already exceed arrivals on several early days, which is unused evidence that \(I_0>0\) and that the Apr 25 WIP contradiction is even sharper. No explicit bound on opening stock is attempted from day-1 snapshots.

**Format and competence.** All eight headings are present and on-task. Assumptions are labeled. The alternative factorial cut is a real alternative, not a relabeling. PM4Py remarks correctly refuse to treat the aggregates as an event log. None of that offsets the inference gaps above.

The piece would be in the high 9s if it had treated the week-2 creep and the peak/vector conflict as first-class uncertainty, shown arrival- vs completion-based load screens, and reported ranges for \(n\le3\) cells. As written it is excellent technical work with avoidable over-precision and two unresolved identification issues.
