**6.6**

The write-up is a competent process-mining diagnosis: it uses eligibility sets rather than start order, labels assumptions, separates observation from hypothesis, and follows the seven-heading template. Under a normal reading it would score higher. Under strict grading the factual errors, overclaims, and missed competing models are enough to keep it well below “nearly flawless.”

**What is actually strong**

- QA is the best-identified queue in the essay, and the core call is right: non-preemptive FIFO with an appointment override, not priority or SPT. A02 beating A04 at 08:32, A05 beating A08 at 09:05, and A07 beating A08 at 09:32 are the right discriminating episodes. Non-preemption is correctly read from A06 starting late rather than cutting A05.
- QD’s skill split, D05 waiting for D-F, D-X idling for D06, and FIFO-within-skill at D-M 11:25 / D-X 11:48 are the right positive tests. Priority and SPT are correctly rejected there.
- In-sample `est_min` matching realized duration on every job is noticed and used to down-rank noisy-estimate stories. That is the correct falsification move.
- Uncertainty handling is generally honest: QA 78, QB 55, QC 62, and the identifiability column names real observational equivalences (C05 as both P1 and earliest deadline; first QB preemption also compatible with SRPT; skill masking policy in QD).
- Section 6 is tailored rather than generic (09:46–09:49, 10:30–10:33, eligibility snapshots, remaining-time at preemption).

**Factual and logical defects**

1. **Wrong remaining time at QB 09:31.** B01 has used 14 minutes (09:02–09:16); remaining is **31**, not “~14 residue.” SPT still picks B03, so the *ranking* survives, but the table is numerically false. Remaining 14 is the *later* 10:14 state. That is a basic reconstruction error in the section that is supposed to be the evidential spine.

2. **Primary QB model is the worse of two priority stories.** Treating the rule as 1>2>3 preemptive priority forces exceptions at 09:31 (B01 over B03), 10:14 (B01 over B06), and 10:28 (B04 over B06). A two-class rule — preemptive **P1 vs everyone else**, FIFO inside the non-P1 class — predicts those three choices and leaves 09:48 as the one documented “dispatcher reassignment.” P2 never cleanly beats a waiting P3 except at that logged exception and at 10:36, which is also FIFO. The essay’s “resume affinity / work-drain” secondary is adjacent to this model but never stated as the better primary. Confidence 55 papers over a model-selection miss, it does not fix it.

3. **09:48 eligibility is sloppy.** B04 arrived at 09:35; it cannot be scan-invisible. Writing “B04 (e8) or B03 if B04 invisible” is incorrect. B05 at 09:46 vs a 09:48 start uses the **entire** two-minute scan allowance. That is allowed by the prompt, but it is a boundary save, not independent support for priority. SPT at that instant predicts B04, not a “tie.”

4. **“Only SPT predicts C03” overclaims uniqueness.** Among FIFO / priority / raw EDD, yes. Among defensible close cousins, no: at 10:18 C05 is already infeasible (40 min work, 32 min to deadline), so EDD-among-feasible and least-slack-among-feasible also pick C03. 10:10 C02 is the actually unique SPT episode versus those competitors. The essay treats 10:10, 10:18, and 11:34 as equally diagnostic.

5. **C05 was infeasible at arrival, not merely “at risk when selected.”** Arrived 10:11, deadline 10:50, `est_min` 40 → 10:51 even if started immediately. Deferring C05 is ordinary triage of a doomed job, not an SLA-save. Promoting it at 10:54 is promotion of an already-tardy P1 job. That distinction matters for the “policy changes when an SLA becomes endangered” clause and is not drawn.

6. **QC 10:32 is under-interpreted.** C04 is a real violation of live SPT. The frozen-initial-sequence reading is better than the essay allows: initial SPT order is C02(8), C03(14), C04(22), C01(36), C05(40); observed prefix is exactly C02–C03–C04, with C06 (arrived 10:25, mid-C03) not allowed to break the committed next job. The text dismisses “preserve order” in one clause. Moore–Hodgson is then invoked in §4 in a way that does not actually predict C04 (after dropping infeasible C05, EDD on the rest is C01). Naming a theorem that does not select the observed job is worse than saying “unexplained.”

7. **D-F idle 11:36–12:00 is not explained by D05 reservation.** D10 is electrical, 12 minutes, and would finish before the 12:00 meeting; D05 cannot start until 12:20 anyway. Inserting D10 does not delay the only-qualified job. Cross-server “already assigned to D-X” is possible but is an extra coordination assumption, not a consequence of skill + FIFO + appointment. Confidence **88** is too high given a 24-minute work-conservation hole on a P1 job.

8. **QA “appointment reservation” overstates the evidence.** A03 starts when A01 ends at 08:20; there is no idle-to-wait. A06 does not preempt and does not induce idle. What is identified is a non-preemptive appointment jump at/after the appointment epoch, with FIFO the rest of the time. QD *does* show reservation idling; QA does not. The identifiability note (no idle test) contradicts the primary-discipline wording.

**Smaller issues that still cost**

- Priority failures on QA omit 09:20 (A06 P2 over A08 P1).
- First-job 2-minute starts (A01, B01) are lumped with scan delay; they are equally consistent with a fixed setup lag.
- §5 item 4 treats D-F idling as support for look-ahead routing; that is circular if look-ahead is what needs to be shown.
- “Most dangerous to infer” is operationally sensible for pure SPT on QC and aggressive preemption on QB, but the more direct danger on QD — shipping FIFO without the skill matrix — is missing.
- Several table cells hedge with stacked alternatives (“B07 / B04”, “D08/D10”) without stating which information set is being assumed. That blurs the decision-set discipline the rest of the essay is aiming for.

**Scoring rationale**

Format, decision-set method, assumption hygiene, and the QA/QD cores are clearly above a median answer. Strictly, that is not enough. A remaining-time error in a reconstructed decision table, an over-unique SPT claim, a missed P1-only model that removes most of the QB “exceptions,” a misapplied Moore–Hodgson aside, and a high-confidence QD label sitting on an unexplained idle are not nits. They are exactly the failure modes the prompt warned against: forcing a named discipline, then spending exceptions to protect it.

A nearly flawless script would have put **P1-preemptive + FIFO otherwise** on the table for QB, treated C05 as infeasible from \(t=0\), kept C04 as either freeze or unexplained rather than a named algorithm that does not predict it, and dropped QD confidence until D10/D-F idle had a constraint that actually forbids the insert. This answer does not.
