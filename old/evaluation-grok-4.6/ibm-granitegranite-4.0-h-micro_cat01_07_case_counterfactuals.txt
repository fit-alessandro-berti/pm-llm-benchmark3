The answer has the requested skeleton and occasionally names the right risks (C1 reservation, assessment congestion, K-337 as a policy change). Almost every technically load-bearing claim is wrong, underspecified, or incompatible with the normative rules. That is not a near-miss; it is a formatted paraphrase of the prompt with invented mechanics.

**Feasibility model.** The partial order is not a model of these four cases. `Triage → Identity ∧ Document` is false whenever originals are required. `Identity ∧ Document → Assessment ∧ (Originals Receipt | Compliance Review)` inverts the originals constraint and treats compliance as an alternative to assessment rather than a mandatory successor on high-risk/high-value work. `Assessment → Request More Info ∧ Decision` makes an optional exception path obligatory and jointly enabled with decision, which the rework rule forbids. The prompt said not to force all cases into one sequence; this does exactly that, and incorrectly. Conditional constraints are a bullet restatement of the given rules, not a second fenced constraint set. Intervention typing is confused: suppressing the second RMI is listed as both routing and policy; K-104 is called a timing change and then, later, quietly rewritten as a D2 reroute.

**Case table — fatal path errors.** K-104’s specified intervention is an early S1 packet plus a provisional C1 slot. The counterfactual path assigns **Document Check (D2)** and inserts a “provisional Compliance Review” as if it occupied the final-CR slot. That is not a minimal change; it is a different intervention, and a bad one (D1 is the specialist for complex corporate files). Final CR after final assessment is mandatory; a preliminary screen does not replace it. K-219’s “factual mechanism” describes supplier-total inconsistencies and rework — that is K-337. K-219’s factual document check was accurate with no rework. K-337’s path goes `Assessment 1 → Decision (with conditions)` and drops RMI1, DC2, Assessment 2, **and Compliance Review**. High-risk and high-value still require CR after the latest assessment and before decision. The four-eyes rule is irrelevant if compliance is omitted. K-411’s path is the only one that is roughly legal, and even there concurrency is asserted rather than timed against the Tuesday queues.

**Temporal magnitudes are not uncertain; they are invented.** Factual cycles are on the order of 1.5–8 calendar days. Reported effects of “±1–2 hours” and best/base/worst totals of 14–22 hours are an order of magnitude off and do not come from snapshot queues, service-time bands, overnight waits, or the weekend external loop. Base case is repeatedly defined as the factual execution, which is not a counterfactual. Worst case for K-337 claims cycle time *increases* after deleting a loop. There is no walk from “earlier readiness” to “earlier Decision/Notify”: reserved C1 does not finish Document Check (K-104); D2 starting at originals is not a one-hour object (K-219); deleting Fri→Mon RMI2 is days, not hours (K-337). Queue forecasts are treated as decoration, then replaced with round numbers.

**Derivations do not derive.** K-104 claims C1 reservation accelerates documents. K-219 keeps citing supplier inconsistency. K-337 both “does not alter final compliance” and “eliminates … compliance review.” Evidence-that-would-reverse paragraphs restated hoped-for outcomes (“if it goes well, side effects are minimized”) instead of identifying observables that would flip the sign (preempted C1 slot, D2 handwriting fail → 8–36 h rework, Assessment-2 file still non-decisionable, workaround timestamps batched so concurrency is fictitious). Service-time ranges from the brief are unused.

**Capacity section fails the brief.** K-104 does not use an originals-receipt queue; the scarce object is singleton C1, and the stated complication is delay of *other urgent compliance cases* — missing from the table. K-219 should move load from D1 to D2, not “none/none,” and it is not a high-value case. K-337 at scale *releases* D1/A1/C1 and weekend-bound external wait; “none/none” plus “direct effect on decision policy” is not a capacity analysis. The 100-case write-up is far under the 250–400 word requirement and does not actually analyze reservation of C1, D1↔D2 substitution under skill mismatch, induced vs avoided rework, assessment-queue migration, weekend/external compounding, or timestamped vs true concurrency. Bottleneck migration is named, not shown.

**Robustness, pilots, synthesis, assumptions.** Ranking K-337 last on quality grounds is the one defensible comparative call, then it is undermined by calling K-219’s benefit “reduces rework” (the intervention *raises* rework risk) and K-411’s benefit “significant cycle-time reduction” after the same author scored it ±1 hour. Reversibility of K-411 is not “low because timestamps.” K-337’s information value is not low. The K-104 pilot still assigns D2. “Quality outcome” rows describe queues. Eligibility/assignment/sample logic are slogans, not a design. Synthesis is under-length and claims K-411 is least transportable to policy; the non-transportable objects are C1 slot reservation and a binding decision-policy change under interference. The eight “assumptions” are unlabeled or mislabeled restatements of given rules (items 7–8 have no type; four-eyes is a constraint, not an assumption). None of the actual modeling bets (packet completeness, D2 handwriting fail rate, C1 non-preemption, Assessment-2 sufficiency, fixed background queues) are audited.

**Net.** Required headings exist; mandatory control-flow, minimal-change, and queue-consistent timing do not. An evaluator looking for feasible counterfactuals, calibrated ranges, and interference would have to rebuild the analysis from the case tables.
