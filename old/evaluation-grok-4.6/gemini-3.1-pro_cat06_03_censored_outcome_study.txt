**5.2**

The write-up avoids the worst failure modes (treating omitted cases as non-default, fitting only the 21,600 labeled rows without qualification, using a random split, or treating post-decision fields as early features). It is nevertheless not close to a clean design. Several errors are material, not cosmetic, and they compound.

**Target.** The primary estimand is conceptually split. A competing-state outcome on *all* applications is reasonable, but the horizon is defined as “90 days after the final credit decision (or 90 days post-activation).” That starts the clock on a future event that the early-prefix model has not observed. Reject / withdraw / transfer occur at or near decision time; default is defined 90 days after activation. These are different time origins and different state spaces glued together. Prediction time is also left as “\(k\) events *or* elapsed time \(t\)” rather than one operational as-of rule. Open cases are in the population statement but not in the state list; they only appear later as censoring. The alternative (default | activation) is described more honestly than the primary, including reject-inference selection, but then Section 8 talks as if the fitted model *is* that selected-activation estimand. That contradiction means the paper never stably owns one question.

**Cohort.** The 48,000 arithmetic is correct, and rejected / withdrawn are not relabeled as non-default. Treatments are still sloppy. All 5,100 still-open cases are called “not yet mature” right-censoring; long-pending 2024 cases are not the same mechanism as late 2025 applications. Transfers are simultaneously a competing outcome and missing follow-up, with no rule for the 60% linkage table. Rejected rows are tagged “selectively unobserved default” *and* competing events, which undoes the primary target. The maturity cutoff (extract − 125 days ≈ 13 Oct 2025) is the right *length* for activation + 90 + delayed feed, but it is applied to **case start**, not activation / horizon start. A Jan-2024 case that activates in Jan 2026 is immature; a case that starts on 1 Oct 2025 and activates on 10 Oct 2025 can be mature. The as-of rule is therefore wrong.

**Censoring.** Delayed labels and partner missingness are handled better than in a naive classification setup, and MNAR for counterfactual default of rejects is correctly called untestable. The proposed “sensitivity analysis” is not a sensitivity of the primary competing-risks model; it is a different estimand (Heckman / reject inference). There is no planned sensitivity for informative transfer, unlinked 40%, partner missingness, or long-open cases. Delayed labels called MCAR given time are at best MAR; the terminology is loose. Permanently missing partner feeds are called right-censored while also flagged as possibly informative, with no identification strategy.

**Prefixes / features.** Fixed-count and elapsed-time prefixes are named, and renaming is acknowledged. Short cases are “exclude *or* pad,” which is not a design. Excluding cases that ended before \(k\) selects on survival to the prediction point and is not stated as an at-risk-set rule. Duplicate migration events are ignored. Process features cover the required families only thinly (last activity, a rework count, month, a verbal queue replay). No as-of definition of who is in the queue, no distinction between waiting and sojourn, no handling of `risk_score` as a prefix attribute in this section.

**Leakage table.** This is the strongest part: `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, `risk_score`, resource experience, and label timing are all present with keep/repair/drop. Even here, `final_limit` is said to “reveal the outcome” (it reveals the decision, not default), and `label_available_at` is said to “perfectly predict maturity” rather than leak activation-plus-label existence. The code then **drops** `documents_total` instead of repairing it, so the audit and the pipeline disagree.

**Validation.** A random split is correctly rejected. The implemented split is not a serious backtest. Training stops at the 1 Jul 2025 policy change, so the model never sees the regime that would be deployed. Validation is Jul–Sep 2025. The test set is starts from **1–13 Oct 2025**, labeled “post-migration.” Migration is **15 Oct 2025**. The test window is pre-migration, about two weeks long, and after a 125-day maturity filter almost no post-migration case can have a matured `default_90d` before 15 Feb 2026. That constraint is the central temporal fact of the snapshot and is missed. There is no rolling origin, no gap that matches label delay, no maturity filter on train/val, and no plan for scoring censored test cases. Metrics (competing-risk Brier, cause-specific time-dependent AUC, calibration, channel/region slices) are appropriate *if* the target and split were coherent.

**Limitations.** Performativity, gaming, and policy drift are real deployment effects. The section then states the study only learns “who defaults among those we currently approve,” which is the *alternative* target, not the multi-state primary. Counterfactual default is correctly declared out of scope, but the decision-use boundary is therefore written for the wrong estimand.

**Code.** Roughly the right length and it does parse timestamps, `format_dataframe`, and a start-time split. It does not meet the stated skeleton contract: no `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`; no activation time; no as-of event cutoff; no proper censoring / competing-state flags; no cohort-flow counts for the 48,000; maturity on start time; test slice both tiny and mislabeled; leakage columns dropped rather than repaired; future activities filtered *after* taking the first three events, which silently shortens prefixes; `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` used without an as-of justification; naive vs UTC timestamp comparisons; survival/competing-risk hooks are one trailing comment. Invented rename keys are acceptable as placeholders; fabricating a clean pipeline around a wrong maturity rule is not.

Net: honest about undefined default without activation, and the leakage list is usable, but the primary estimand, maturity rule, and temporal test are internally inconsistent. Under a strict reading that is a failed study design with a competent outline, not a near-miss.
