## Conceptual failures on the quantities that matter

**Double counting.** `join_ready` *is* the last completion, so the interval “between the last branch completion and `join_ready`” has length **0**. Summing `sync_i` does not double-count that phantom interval. The identity they needed is:

`queue_i + service_i + sync_i = join_ready − fork_release` for **every** `i`.

Those three paths are parallel; summing them **triple-counts** fork–join elapsed time. Elapsed fork–join time was a required component and is never defined as `max_i(queue_i+service_i)`.

**Mean sync wait.** Reporting A = 0, C = 0, B = 15 is impossible under their own definition: a branch that is non-critical in 75% of cases cannot have mean sync wait 0 unless all three branches always finish together. Correct means are about **A 11.0, B 4.3, C 30.4**. They inverted the meaning of synchronization wait (idle *after* completion) and therefore inverted C’s role.

**Slack / second-longest branch.** A 20% A-service cut is 10 min, but F01 slack of A over B is only **5 min** (sojourns 50 vs 45). F10 is not A-critical at all (slack of B over A is 5). The writeup treats a 10 min A cut as a 10 min `join_ready` cut on {F01, F10, F11}. That is exactly the mistake the prompt forbade: speeding a branch only helps up to the next-longest sojourn.

**B-queue −50%.** Queues are not a constant 25 min, so the cut is not a uniform 12.5 min. On F10 the cut exceeds slack and saves 5, not 10. F08 is not B-critical, so it is a zero-benefit case they counted as a win.

**Masking.** The prompt required asking whether F-station congestion eats branch gains. The writeup assumes Δ`join_ready` = Δ flow time, then separately claims every case benefits from cutting downstream wait. F1/F2 occupancy does **not** explain the waits (F1 is idle 09:28–10:00 while F03 waits until 10:00; F2 is free at F02’s `join_ready` 09:25). Downstream is not shown to be a FCFS multi-server queue, so the 1:1 translation is an unlabeled, likely false, assumption.
