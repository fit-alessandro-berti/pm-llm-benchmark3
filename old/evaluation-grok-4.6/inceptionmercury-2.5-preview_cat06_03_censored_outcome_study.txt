The answer has the right section skeleton and avoids the crudest failure (coding rejected/withdrawn/open as non-default). It is not a defensible study design. Multiple required distinctions are wrong or missing, the prediction-time choice contradicts the business question, the validation split is largely unlabeled, and the implementation skeleton would not run and reintroduces leakage.

## 1. Target definition

The conditional-on-activation 90-day default estimand is a legitimate primary target, but it is specified inconsistently and operationally late.

- The lender asked for an **early warning from the first few events of every application**. Predicting “after the final Credit Review but before Contract Signed” is a post-decision booked-loan screen, not that product. At that point Accept is already known, `decision_reason`/`final_limit` are in play, and rejected paths are already out of scope.
- **Population and estimand do not match.** The population is matured activated accounts; the estimand is \(P(Y\le 90\text{d}\mid\text{Activated},\text{prefix})\). Immature activations are in the estimand and out of the population, with no maturity/censoring statement in the target itself.
- Horizon is anchored at activation, while prediction is earlier, so time from call to outcome is **90 days plus a random origination delay**. That is never stated.
- The 7–35 day **delayed outcome feed** is absent from the target/maturity definition even though `label_available_at` exists.
- The required “credible alternative” is the **indefensible convenience target** (rejected as non-default). Competing risks, multi-state outcomes, censored time-to-default, or an explicitly assumption-laden counterfactual were asked for. The explanation of that alternative is also false: coding rejects as \(Y=0\) assumes default probability 0, not “the same rate as approved.”

## 2. Cohort construction

The 48,000 arithmetic is correct and omitted cases are not labeled 0. The flow still misclassifies mechanisms the prompt told you to separate.

- **Open applications (5,100) are not right-censored 90-day defaults.** They were never activated; the primary outcome is undefined, not truncated survival. Treating them as censored is the exact “undefined vs censored” error the quality bar flags.
- The 3,600 “immature” rows mix **calendar-time not yet 90 days after activation** with **delayed feeds**. Those are different missingness processes.
- Transfers are one bucket (“partially missing”) with no split of the 60% linkable vs 40% lost, and no row for **partner-originated activated accounts with permanently missing feeds**.
- Inclusion is “all starts in 2024–2025,” which is the raw extract, not the analysis cohort for a target that conditions on activation and on having reached the chosen prediction event. There is no exclusion for cases that never hit that Credit Review landmark.
- As-of rules ignore `label_available_at` and the delayed feed.

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

Required categories are incomplete and the primary strategy contradicts the target.

- **Delayed labels** and **permanently missing partner feeds** are not treated as distinct mechanisms.
- Open cases are again described as ordinary right censoring (“survived past the prediction point but not the 90-day horizon”). That statement is false for never-activated files.
- Primary analysis oscillates between **IPW to all applicants** (different estimand) and “survival or classification on activated cases only” (two different likelihoods, no choice).
- Sensitivity analysis that “assumes a proportion of transferred or open cases default” does not match a target defined only under activation, and “a proportion” is unspecified.
- Linked-transfer MAR is **not** testable from the linkage rate. Linkage rate is a missingness frequency; equality of outcomes for unlinked transfers is untestable without those outcomes. Observables of linked vs unlinked are at most a partial check.

## 4. Prefix extraction

Both prefix styles are named, then specified too loosely to be as-of safe.

- Prediction time is “the Credit Review outcome event” despite **repeated Credit Review loops**. First vs last review is undefined.
- Fixed-k prefixes after filtering to the decision timestamp are not the usual first-k early-warning prefixes; they can include the decision itself.
- Padding short cases with “Case Terminated” **encodes the competing terminal outcome into the prefix**.
- Collapsing “same activity within 1 hour” can delete legitimate document-loop rework; migration duplicates and rework are not the same.
- Elapsed-time prefixes have no \(\delta\), no rule for cases that already terminated, and no prediction timestamp definition.

## 5. Process-aware features

The taxonomy is thin and partly ill-posed.

- “Number of unique variants seen” in a single prefix is not a meaningful control-flow feature (a prefix is one path). Unique activities or loop counts would be.
- Queueing is essentially `org:resource` ID; no backlog, waiting time, or concurrent-workload features.
- Rework is a single boolean; cycle count and time-in-rework are missing.
- Historical reference data is mentioned only for HR experience. No as-of risk-score path, no prior-cohort rates, no volume/calendar controls beyond one policy dummy.

## 6. Leakage audit

The table exists and hits most named fields, but several decisions are wrong given *this* design.

- `default_90d` is not a feature. **“Keep” in a leakage table is target leakage** unless it is clearly excluded from \(X\). `label_available_at` was the requested element.
- `documents_total`: availability rule describes a repair (count events \(\le\) prediction time), action is **Drop**. Inconsistent.
- With prediction after final Credit Review, `decision_reason` and `final_limit` are available and highly post-decision. “Repair if post-decision” quietly changes the task into using the underwriting decision as a feature.
- No audit of `source_system` / partner missingness, `data_extract_time`, or `label_available_at` as a feature.

## 7. Validation design

Temporal splitting is the right *idea* and the random-split warning is correct. The actual split is not usable.

- Extract date is 15 Feb 2026. Test starts **15 Oct–31 Dec 2025**. Outcome needs activation delay + 90 days + 7–35 day feed. A large majority of test (and many validation) labels are **not mature**. There is no `activation + 90d + feed \le cutoff` (or `label_available_at \le cutoff`) rule.
- No rolling/expanding **backtest**. Train is entirely pre-policy; the model never sees the regime it is tested in, and that choice is not framed as a shift stress test vs a deployable scheme.
- **Subgroup performance is required and missing** (channel, region, amount, partner-origin).
- Metrics: class imbalance (~12.5% among labeled activations) is ignored (no PR-AUC/utility); survival vs binary is still unchosen; “Brier requires recalibration for censored data” is not an estimator (IPCW Brier, etc.). The study was supposed to be uncertainty-aware; there is no calibration-by-time, interval, or selective-prediction protocol.

## 8. Limitations and decision-use boundary

The rejected-applicant counterfactual boundary is stated. The rest is sloppy.

- Claims “uncertainty quantification” that was never designed.
- Predictive association vs **selection into activation** vs counterfactual risk is not actually separated for the primary estimand.
- Deployment shift (1) is directionally wrong: tightening approvals makes the **booked** cohort safer/different, not “training riskier vs served riskier.” The performative loop (model changes who is activated, which changes the conditional outcome law) is the point, and it is garbled.

## 9. Implementation skeleton

This section is far below the spec (~80–160 lines, concrete, PM4Py-correct, as-of safe).

- `pm4py.format_dataframe(..., activity=..., time=...)` is the wrong API (`activity_key`, `timestamp_key`).
- Required `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is **not used**.
- Case table is only min/max timestamp. `get_cohort` reads `status` and `label_available_at`, which were never built; **KeyError**. Status is not in the schema and should be derived from activities (Activated / Rejected / …).
- Prefix cut is `end_time` (last event in the case), i.e. Contract Signed, Account Activated, collections, default. That is the opposite of a prefix and of “prevent post-prefix columns/events.”
- `audit_leakage(df, "end_time")` looks for a column that does not exist on the event frame.
- `print(casesorts)` is a NameError.
- Post-decision columns (`final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, `default_90d`) are never dropped from \(X\).
- No configurable maturity cutoff, no survival/competing-risk hook comments of substance, no activity-name normalization, no demonstration that features are computed only on the prefix.

**Net:** conceptually aware of selection, still statistically incorrect on censoring vs undefined outcomes, operationally misaligned on prediction time, invalid on temporal label maturity, and the code both fails and leaks. That is well below a rigorous design, even though it is not the naive 21,600-row random-split baseline.
