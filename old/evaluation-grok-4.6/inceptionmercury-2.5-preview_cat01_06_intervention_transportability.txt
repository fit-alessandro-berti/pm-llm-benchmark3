**4.2**

The answer has the right headline (do not copy Site A wholesale; restrict concurrency; `conditional go`) and mostly follows the required skeleton. Under strict grading that is not enough. Core transport, measurement, and model-fidelity claims are repeatedly imprecise, internally inconsistent, or technically invalid.

### Format and high-level call
Headings, most table schemas, a bottleneck scenario with median down / p90 up, and rejection of “create both tasks after Register for every case” are present. That prevents a collapsing score. It does not offset broken causal structure, invented process-mining APIs, and a pilot that cannot be run as specified.

### Process structure (Section 1)
Several cells are factually wrong or too coarse to support transport.

- Site A **before** was 48% high-complexity, the same as Site B. Labeling A as “simpler” erases the actual mix shift (48% → 40%) and the marketing co-intervention.
- “38% digital required” is backwards: 62% require originals; 38% do not. Digital channel and statutory originals are not the same variable.
- Site A’s six-open-task WIP rule is omitted, so queue discipline is not actually compared.
- Site B is not merely “conditional parallelism.” Identity and originals receipt already may start after Register; Document Check is blocked by originals when required. The binding constraint is a **partial order plus a data-dependent enablement**, not a generic concurrency flag.
- DFG counts are unused. Register→Identity (2,050), Register→Originals (1,860), Originals→Document (2,108), and Identity→Document (1,160) already show that identity often runs during the originals wait. For the 62% originals stratum, copying A’s “start Document Check at Register” is both unlawful **and** mostly pointless: identity (3.4h) is typically hidden inside the 18h originals delay.

### Transport graph (Section 2)
The required node set is listed, then not used as a causal graph.

- The central arrow `treatment_parallelization → true_overlap` is missing.
- `rework` and `appeal` appear in the node list with **no incident edges**.
- `originals_required → !true_overlap` is not an adjacency-list edge.
- `role_specialization → IT_case_lock` and `case_complexity → legal_capacity` are not mechanisms.
- Capacity nodes (`identity_capacity`, `document_capacity`, `assessment_capacity`, `legal_capacity`) and `queue_huddles` are sinks.
- Selection nodes `S_*` are attached, but site differences in **pools (4 / 7 / 3 / 2)**, lock workarounds, and legal-supplement loops never enter the outcome paths.

The prose correctly says a similar control-flow diagram is not transportable. The graph does not encode that claim.

### Evidence transfer (Section 3)
Co-interventions are noticed, and the 9h median drop is not fully treated as an ATE. Interpretation is still too causal.

- Median cycle time as “structural reduction” (confidence 40) overstates identification. A also added two document reviewers, upgraded the identity API, changed mix, added huddles, and improved timestamps, with no negative-control process.
- Waiting-before-assessment at confidence **70** is too high for a confounded before/after overlap metric that “may partly reflect improved timestamp recording.”
- Mix shift, extra FTEs, and API latency are partly collapsed rather than carried as separate transport rows.
- Site B’s 4 identity / 3 assessor / 2 legal FTEs are not used to bound what could transfer.

### Modifiers and scenarios (Sections 4–5)
Required modifier names are present; several interactions are false or operationally illegal.

- “Cap legal volume” is not a mitigation for a statutory 40% legal-review class.
- Digital claims with harmful signature “none” ignores IT lock, assessment congestion, and shared-pool interference.
- Section 5 **does not show the required mapping from A’s evidence and B’s constraints to ranges**.
- Eligible share flips between 30% and 38% without definition of subgroup vs population median.
- Optimistic −8h (or the extra 100%-of-B −10h scenario) smuggles A’s unidentified 9h reduction into B. For digital cases the structural ceiling is on the order of overlapping identity service (~3.4h) plus some queueing, further reduced by specialization and locks—not 8–10h for 100% of volume.
- Legal/rework “quality up” while cycle time worsens is an unargued quality claim.

### Intervention and POWL (Section 6)
Eligibility restriction to non-originals cases is the right statutory move, then the spec undoes it by putting `OriginalsReceipt` back on the happy path and using `{LegalReview, Approve, Reject}` as if they were a well-defined exclusive choice.

The partial-order set is not a partial order: `DocumentCheck → Assessment → LegalReview → RequestSupplement → DocumentCheck` is a **cycle**. Exclusive decisions are written as sibling successors, which in a strict partial order means **all** of legal, approve, and reject must occur. Assessment-after-supplement (Site B rule 7) is omitted. This is not a small notation issue; it misstates the candidate process.

### Pilot (Section 7)
This section fails its own causal purpose.

- Stop rule **rework > 15%** with baseline **17%** would trip immediately.
- Unit is case ID while randomization is “stratified cluster,” with only 4 identity and 3 assessor FTEs. Shared queues violate SUTVA; “queue separation” is not a credible safeguard at this scale.
- 3 months cannot observe 90-day appeals.
- Restricting to digital cases tests **eligibility**, not the difference between mechanism failure (true overlap achieved, wait does not fall because of downstream bottlenecks) and implementation failure (lock, back-entry, tasks never concurrent). No fidelity-first or CACE/ITT split is specified.
- “Minimum 500 cases” is asserted, not derived from B’s ~38% eligible flow.

### PM4Py (Section 8) — disqualifying for model fidelity
The prompt required constructors consistent with PM4Py POWL (`StrictPartialOrder`, `OperatorPOWL`, `Transition`, process-tree strings such as `->(...)`, `+(...)`, `X(...)`). The code invents `ProcessOWL`, `Node`, and `Arc`; parses nonexistent `Sequence` / `Or` / `Parallel` trees; never unpacks `(net, im, fm)`; imports `play_out` from the wrong place; encodes XOR as concurrent arcs; and feeds a cyclic graph to a strict partial order. Comments about what the model cannot encode are the only sound part. This does not demonstrate that Assessment waits for both checks in a sound workflow net.

### Recommendation and assumptions (Sections 9–10)
`conditional go` with digital-first entry is reasonable. Expected effect sizes, capacity rebalancing (4/7/3/2), and explicit expand / modify / kill thresholds are thin. Assumption 7 (do not change timestamp logging) contradicts the intervention’s “enforce real-time logging,” which is exactly the co-intervention that contaminated A’s overlap metric. Supplement-rate stability is a hypothesis to estimate, not a maintained assumption.

**Net:** directionally correct analogical caution, but not grounded in B’s partial order, DFG, or capacities; A’s before/after still leaks into numeric claims; the pilot is infeasible as written; the process models are unsound. That is far from near-flawless.
