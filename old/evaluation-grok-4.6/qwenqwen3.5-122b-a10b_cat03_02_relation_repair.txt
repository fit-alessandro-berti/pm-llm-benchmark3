**5.8**

The write-up has the right skeleton and is strongest on the genuinely ambiguous financial and confirmation questions. It is not close to a high score under a strict reading.

**What is actually good.** Sections 1–8 are present. Amount equality is treated as evidence, not proof. `ev11` is not forced onto a single delivery. Bundle A vs B is a real interpretive split (minimum change vs standard billing/settlement), not a list of disconnected edges. Unresolved items are named. Several over-repair risks are dataset-specific rather than generic. The selected JSON is consistent with the conservative bundle.

**Material misses.** The most clearly indicated gap is never repaired as E2O: `ev08` is item-only (`IT-B1`) with `scanner_ref=PK8-2`, and domain rule 3 is written for exactly this case. The answer adds only `PK-8 packs IT-B1` and then writes as if `ev08` already relates to `PK-8`. That conflates an event attribute with an E2O fact. The obvious parallel O2O `DEL-Y carries PK-8` (mirroring `DEL-X carries PK-7`) is also absent, even as an explicit non-addition. The table therefore fails the required “additions and tempting non-additions” job: missing rows include `ev08`–`PK-8`, `ev12`–`ORD-B`, `ev14`–`INV-44`, and `ev11`–`ORD-B` (both orders are `C17`).

**Concrete errors.** Qualifier direction is inverted (“Package carries Delivery”), which violates the stated O2O rule. “Consequence if wrong” for R01 describes the cost of *not* adding the edge. `Create Order` “normally one order” is upgraded to a hard invariant. Under Bundle A, `ORD-A` is not “Paid” via `ev13`: `PAY-1` is 100 against a 230 invoice and a 130 order. The claim that Bundle B gives `ORD-B` a full invoice/payment lifecycle in flattenings is wrong: O2O `bills`/`settles` does not put `ev12`/`ev14` on the Order flattening unless those E2O links are added, and they are not. `ORD-B` does not even have a Pack-Item E2O, so saying its path “terminates at Pack Item” is sloppy.

**Weaker audit and tooling.** Dual E2O/O2O support is discussed in the abstract and then ignored in the actual repair (high-confidence O2O from a scanner string, no matching E2O). Code copies tables and flattens Order/Delivery, but it never does the required duplicate check, and column names drift (`event`/`object` vs `event_id`/`object_id` vs `ocel:eid`). Downstream discussion is directionally right and too generic on concrete OC-DFG/OTG fragments.

Competent abductive framing; too many omitted relations, a few inverted semantics, and flattening/lifecycle mistakes to score higher.
