The submission is a strong, format-compliant audit: POWL is treated as authority, LOOP is interpreted as body-then-`(redo; body)*` rather than `body*`, the N-shaped poset is identified correctly, no POWL-to-BPMN shortcut is invented, markings are not coerced from `None`/empty truthiness, and the independent visible language (45 words through three iterations) matches the stated partial order. Those are the hard parts, and they are right.

It is not near-flawless. The following defects keep the score well below 9–10.

**The audit is prospective, not performed.** Section 4 fills every cell with `expected preserved` / `ambiguous` and states that nothing is `validated preserved` because PM4Py was never run. The task asked to catch **and interpret** conversion exceptions. P2 is described as a legitimate structural failure, but no exception is obtained, classified, or used to update the table. A conversion returning an object is correctly refused as proof of fidelity; an unexecuted pipeline is also not an audit.

**Two requested validators are present only as dead code.** `convert_to_reachability_graph` and playout are gated behind `RUN_REACHABILITY = False` and `RUN_PLAYOUT = False`. On this model that choice is not well motivated: the control-flow state space is small, and the prompt asked to use those APIs where defensible. Footprint comparison is similarly hollow: it runs only if the result is a `dict` with a fixed key set. PM4Py often returns a footprints object, so the comparison can skip silently while still logging `"recorded"`.

**Silent-transition handling is brittle.** The explorer hides only `label is None`. The prose even insists that `""` is not tau. PM4Py nets and BPMN round-trips frequently use `None`, `"tau"`, or empty labels for routing. If that happens, skip/loop taus become visible letters, the 45-word baseline will not match, and the mismatch can be misread as a semantic loss rather than an observation-map error.

**Several table cells overclaim relative to the surrounding prose.** I6 packages “no order edge” with “true concurrency”; words can support the first and cannot support the second. Petri→BPMN is marked `expected preserved` for I2, I5, I6, and I9 while the discussion correctly warns that AND/XOR joins, skip completion, and start/end regeneration can change token behavior. Those cells should be `ambiguous`. I2’s negative witness also mixes “rejected required interleaving” with “accepted missing evidence,” which blurs the witness discipline the preamble tries to set.

**A few operational nits in the otherwise careful code.** `boolean_verdict` assumes any non-empty tuple is `(bool, …)`. `semantics.execute` is not given a defensive marking copy (PM4Py usually copies, but this is version-sensitive). Rejection criterion 4 is easy to misread as requiring evidence before assessment as an *order*, rather than as a required *interleaving*. None of these is fatal; together they are sloppy relative to the rest of the artifact.

What remains is high quality: exact final-marking completion, `(marking, prefix)` visited keys, explicit incompleteness on resource cuts, skip-as-token-bearing, binary-loop counterexamples, layout ≠ semantics, POWL-as-canonical with BPMN as annotated derivative, and residual limits that do not pretend bounded word equality is bisimulation. Those merits justify a high score. The unrun audit, disabled APIs, silent-label assumption, and optimistic preservation cells are enough, under a strict rubric, to keep it out of the top band.
