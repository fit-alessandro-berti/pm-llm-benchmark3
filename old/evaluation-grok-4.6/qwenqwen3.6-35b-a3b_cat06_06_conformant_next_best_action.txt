**6.2**

The write-up has the required six-section shape, uses the mandated conformance labels, correctly buries A and B, and does not treat rescreening, SAR, or self-certification as a substitute for independent second-officer review. That is the floor for a competent answer, not a high score.

The ranking is built on a false operational claim. Action C is **not** a reliable or deterministic fit to the 90-minute deadline. Review is 35–75 minutes **plus** 5–15 minutes to record and execute (40–90 minutes). The upper end consumes the entire window; “reliably fits” / “deterministic fit” in §§1, 5, and 6 is simply wrong, and it is the reason C is placed first. The 45-minute P-901 interrupt used for spillover is also inconsistent with a 75-minute review.

Section 5’s S4 arithmetic is internally contradictory. Sequential “screen 15–30 + wait 50 ⇒ 10–25 minutes left for review” is not the same as a parallel screen-during-wait plan, which would leave ~40 minutes if S4 arrives at 50. The 50-minute rerank threshold is invented and then applied as if it were a sharp policy. Even S4 at 35 minutes does not guarantee completion: 35 + 35–75 + 5–15 can still miss.

The top plan should have been an **explicit parallel composite** (start D now, wait on S4, hold C as a contingent interrupt only if S4 slips). The rubric asks for parallelism and P-901 accounting. Those appear as commentary, not as the recommended policy. Interrupting another high-value 70-minute case as the *primary* move, while S4 may arrive in 35 minutes, is weakly justified.

Other material gaps:
- Prefix ends at **Evidence Validated**. Rule 5 returns to Analyst Review before the cycle is closed. Jumping straight to S2 (C) or rejecting now (F) without that step is not analyzed.
- Rule 6 requires second-officer review before **Approve / Execute**, not clearly before Reject. F’s condition is therefore mis-specified.
- Rule 8’s hold/SAR branch and Rule 11 (no customer disclosure) are essentially absent.
- Risk 72 is 12 minutes stale and only 2 points above the mandatory-review cutoff; D’s ability to **change the Rule 6 conditions** (resolve the 0.46 match and/or push the score below 70) is not treated as a first-class branch.
- Continuations invent point probabilities (~0.6, ~0.4, ~0.7, ~0.3, ~0.5) that the case does not give, while collapsing the only given range (0.55–0.75).
- “Strict dominance” is used for C vs D vs F, where the tradeoffs are not dominance relations.
- G is ranked above a fully conformant E without stating that G is only a parallel inquiry until both exception conditions exist.
- D that already includes second-officer review is labeled `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`; the real conditionals are F and G.
- Observational-vs-interventional caution is stated, then ignored when treating C’s speed and S2’s quality as transportable.

A/B last, G not treated as live authority, and the constraint map in §4 are the strongest parts. The deadline math, the S4 numbers, the missed mid-cycle state, and the failure to lead with a parallel S4-preserving plan keep this well short of a high mark under a strict reading.
