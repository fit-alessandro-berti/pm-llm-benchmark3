**6.2**

The answer is structurally complete, process-aware, and often careful about time-of-act analysis, A6 non-cure, issue-specific authority, and contemporaneous evidence. It is not nearly flawless. Several classifications rest on smuggled rules, uneven application of the author’s own method, or overconfidence the materials do not support.

**Method vs. application.** Section 1 is the strongest part: facts and assumptions are separated, ECD-2 is treated as an operational overlay rather than an A7 exception, D1 is denied retroactivity, and exception-invokers bear the burden. That method is then applied inconsistently. The author never uses COMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE as a primary label despite calling P-508 a “coin-flip,” listing missing SoD identities on P-507, and leaving the A2 budget-owner leg unresolved on every ≥ EUR 50,000 overlay case. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is used as a catch-all for “followed the incident path,” which collapses the distinction the author drew between a valid overlay (compliance with the rules then in force) and a true exception to a still-binding rule.

**P-501 is the weakest assessment.** NONCOMPLIANT is grounded in an “unauthorized committer” reading of B4 that the text does not clearly support. B4 allows commitment before the PO in a genuine emergency and allows a Shift Lead to stand in for an *absent* Plant Manager if *actively delegated*. SHIFT-1 committed; Plant_Approve still occurs at 15:10 by an unnamed person. That is not the same as SHIFT-1 occupying the Plant Manager approval slot. Amount is below A2/A3; the supplier is approved; the stoppage prediction meets B4; the emergency form is inside 24 hours. The live B-side issue is B2/B5 (Plant/Finance not waived), which the author calls “tolerable” here while treating post-commit Plant approval as a real deviation on P-506. Same rule family, opposite weight. Severity “low-moderate” and a secondary “partially justified” gloss do not repair a poorly specified primary breach.

The Section 5 alternative that the 08:50 commit might really have been after 09:00 is inconsistent with ON-44. The outage note says an ERP stamp may be up to 12 minutes *later* than the real act, so a real P-501 commit is 08:38–08:50, still before ECD-2. The alternative invents drift in the opposite direction.

**A2 is systematically softened.** The author ranks A2 as a high, board-level pre-commitment control, records that IC members are not budget owners, and admits D3/D4 do not authorize a SoD or integrity waiver. Then P-505/P-507/P-508 are allowed to pass without an independent budget-owner approval because the gap is “residual uncertainty rather than an automatic breach.” That is a quiet rewrite of A2’s dual requirement. If D1 is only a Finance-routing interpretation, the budget-owner leg is unmet and those cases cannot be clean JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. If D1’s “all operational approvals” swallows the budget-owner leg, that premise should be stated and defended, not parked in a footnote while confidence stays high.

**P-507 overclaims.** Integrity sequencing is the best in the set (Compliance → IC auth → commit; PDF at 12:40 beats a 14:30 ERP row). High confidence is still unwarranted. The author made SoD material (R1/R10: an authorizer who is requester/committer hollows C3) and then listed “confirmation neither IC authorizer was requester/committer” as missing evidence. Under the author’s own burden rule, that is INDETERMINATE or at most medium-confidence, not a high-confidence justified exception. Citing ON-44 for the 12:40 PDF is also sloppy: ON-44 is the 14 July ERP outage, not a later document-system queue.

**P-505 timing is better than the label.** Preferring the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment over an 18:06 ERP stamp is the right evidential move (case-specific clock claim, not ON-44’s 12-minute rule). Calling a *missing* Create_PO a mere logging defect is not. C1 permits commitment to *precede* Create_PO; it does not authorize omitting the PO. Incomplete execution is not the same as a late timestamp for a completed act.

**P-508 is argued both ways.** The “as well” reading of D2 is textually available and should remain available. Presenting JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION as primary while calling the opposite reading a coin-flip, and adding secondary INDETERMINATE, is having the classification both ways. The honest primary under the author’s uncertainty rule is INDETERMINATE.

**P-502, P-503, P-504, P-506 are mostly sound**, and they carry the piece. New-supplier A3 and pre-D1 A2 on P-502 are applied at commit time, not cured by 18:30 Compliance. P-504 is correctly treated as out-of-scope emergency normalization. P-506 correctly refuses an unevidenced verbal extension. P-503 is noncompliant on SoD and missing Compliance even if Operations’ broad “critical” reading is granted.

Two overstatements still matter. “Fails on every branch” on P-503 ignores that D1 was already in force and could cover Finance *if* the purchase were in ECD-2 scope. The offered alternative that P-503 becomes INDETERMINATE requires C3 to displace A1; the author then admits that premise is weak and that SoD still kills the case. An alternative that does not actually change the classification is not a strongest alternative.

**R10 smuggles law.** C3 requires two distinct IC members before commitment, recorded within four hours. It does not say an authorizer cannot be the committer. That constraint belongs to A1, and only if IC authorization is the “final business approver.” Building it into C3 makes P-503 look like a C3 failure rather than an A1 failure plus a purpose argument. The purpose argument is fair; the formalization is not.

**Smaller defects.** D1 is scoped by the author to ECD-2 subject-matter even though the email’s operative sentence is temporal (“during the ECD-2 window”)—harmless for P-504 (no IC auth) but unexamined. Section 7’s “set number of times per quarter” is an unfinished safeguard. Extra Compliance on P-505 is listed as a satisfied rule (“R3 exceeded”), which blurs mandatory and gratuitous acts.

The hierarchy table, the defeasible rules, the A6/ON-44 discipline on P-502/P-505/P-507, and the four-way split in Section 6 are genuinely good. The grade is held down because the task is classification under a stated method, and the method is not applied with equal strictness to the hard, high-value, or last-minute cases.
