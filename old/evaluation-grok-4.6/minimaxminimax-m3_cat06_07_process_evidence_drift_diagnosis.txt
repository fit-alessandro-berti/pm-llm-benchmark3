**7.2**

The final six-section writeup is a strong audit on the main interpretive task and a clearly weaker piece of technical execution. It earns credit for the hard part of the question, then loses a good deal of it to API errors, sloppy metrics language, and a containment plan that does not match the live failure.

**What is actually strong.** The periodization the rubric cares about is correct and well argued. April is treated as a schema/pipeline break (unknown activity 17%, missing queue 14%, 18% unmapped, prefixes built on the wrong event), not as concept drift. June is a real V2 routing/mix shift that need not be concept drift, because per-variant breach rates stay roughly flat. August–September are overlapping process, concept, and population effects (Order Part → Part Received 130–148 h, V3 breach 0.64/0.61, Partner Z 24%). October is dominated by immature labels and an API backlog that censors long V3 paths, so the 0.16 observed rate is not comparable to mature months. The April-vs-August calibration *direction* (over-prediction vs under-prediction) is the right wedge between pipeline artifact and real risk shift. Falsifiers are stated. The required tests are all present and paired with competing expected results. Section 6 is the best section: retraining on October, calibrating around April, ignoring supplier-driven V3 change, and calling Partner Z “unfair” without stratified, matured evidence are all real failure modes with proportionate mitigations.

**Diagnosis and timeline issues (moderate).** “ECE turns negative” is wrong; ECE is unsigned. The quantity that flipped sign is bias (mean prediction − observed rate). June’s ECE of 0.060 is more than double the 0.025 baseline and sits next to 0.20 predicted vs 0.15 observed; calling AUROC/ECE “within baseline tolerance” overstates how well the model absorbed the mix shift. April per-variant rates *do* tick up (V1 0.18, V2 0.13, V3 0.34); “do not diverge” is too clean. Putting July in the label-delay row is a stretch at 98% maturity. The process-drift row also dumps the Order Part wait-time explosion into “process drift,” which blurs structural mix change and performance-DFG change that, at prediction time (immediately after diagnosis), is concept drift. These are not fatal, but they are exactly the kind of imprecision this question is testing.

**Section 4 is misaligned with “what to do now.”** Rolling back to a “March-validated artifact” is confused. Nothing in the record says a new model was deployed; the original model is already what is failing on V3. Immediate containment for April is feature quarantine and rebuild, not a model swap. Recalibrating only on January–June then waiting 1–2 months for a V3 model leaves the active under-prediction (0.19 vs 0.28 in August, 0.21 vs 0.31 in September) unaddressed. Mature August labels (93%) already support a variant-specific intercept or isotonic fix. Month safety labels (Jan–Mar safe, April unusable, October frozen, May–June only after repair) are better than the staged actions that sit around them.

**Section 5 architecture is sound; the code is not.** Alert dependencies are the right idea: data-quality suppresses drift/calibration alarms; label-maturity blocks retraining. The sketch then fails several requirements it was explicitly asked to get right.

- `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode="traces")` is not a valid mode and is not a case-start cohort. Standard modes are `events`, `traces_contained`, and `traces_intersecting`. Case-start is “first event in month,” which this API does not compute. The comments describe the distinction correctly; the implementation does not.
- Every month is cut at the 28th. That drops 29–31 from seven months, including October. An audit of monthly process evidence cannot do that.
- `discover_performance_dfg` is not unpacked, while `discover_dfg` is. `len(perf_dfg)` is then meaningless if the return is a tuple. The one edge the whole case hinges on is never extracted.
- `max(variants.values()) / n_cases` assumes counts; current `get_variants` often maps variants to case lists.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is applied to a diagnosis-only slice, which is not how that helper is meant to be used.
- The monthly summary omits unknown-activity share, duplicate rate, and Order Part → Part Received time — the actual audit signals.

Those are not cosmetic nits in a section whose job was a faithful, non-invented PM4Py audit sketch.

**Net.** This is a strong drift narrative with real technical and operational defects. It is far above a one-label or “October is fine” answer, and it is not close to flawless. 7.2 reflects that split.
