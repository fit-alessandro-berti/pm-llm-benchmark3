**7.2**

The final write-up (sections 1–7 only) is complete, process-grounded, and multi-criteria. It does not pick a lowest-emission design, distinguishes justified vs unjustified air/van use, and treats rework, returns, obsolescence, and resilience as first-class constraints. It is not nearly flawless.

**What holds up**

- Boundary is appropriate: order-to-delivery energy, transport, packaging mass, scrap, rework, and inferred obsolescence in; upstream materials, use-phase, and full packaging LCA out. Uncertainty about flattened orders, empty-return accounting, and low model precision is stated.
- Indicators map to logged attributes and paths, not generic ESG metrics.
- Eleven interventions (above the eight-option floor) name target variants, mechanisms, service/quality/resilience effects, and monitorable event-log signals. SP1 slack, CNC_A vs CNC_B rework (9% vs 18%), batch energy (2.8 → 1.3 kWh/item), heat-treatment 1.7× lateness, air 4–6× ground, consolidation 35–55%, Q1/reinforced-pack trade-off, and the 8% dedicated-van critical path are used correctly.
- Rebound section covers the required six themes plus two extras (shared-lane concentration, partial-shipment cancellation waste). Segment creep and last-minute expedite rebound are the strongest points.
- The package is coherent: slack-based routing first, capped batching and CNC_A shift second, packaging compatibility rather than material stripping, dedicated vans reserved for true downtime, CNC_B and air kept as fallbacks.
- Governance uses the actual conformance baselines (SP1 22%, SP2 31%) and names rollback if critical/service-contract p90 or lateness worsens.

**Deductions (strict)**

1. **Section 4 is the weakest required artifact.** Many cells are “↓/↑ slight if X,” so direction is not a decision-usable estimate. “Small/mod” is never defined in the final section. The resilience column mixes polarity: sometimes ↓ means less risk (good), sometimes the cell narrates “capacity protected” under a *risk* heading. That is a logical presentation error, not a quibble.

2. **I5 overstates CNC_A energy superiority.** The log shows CNC_A *batch-size* energy improvement and a lower rework rate; CNC_B’s own kWh/item is not given. The 16 vs 32 kWh path contrast is dominated by rework loops, not a proven machine-efficiency gap at equal batch size. Treating a CNC_B→CNC_A shift as a reliable energy win is slightly overclaimed.

3. **I4’s table contradicts its own design.** The intervention *includes* waiting caps, yet material/scrap is still marked up for obsolescence “if uncapped.” Residual risk after caps should be the scored effect.

4. **Redundancy blurs option analysis.** I1/I2 and I8/I9 largely implement the same mechanisms. S12 is recommended but never analyzed in section 3. Two obsolescence rebound items (batching vs longer promises) repeat one mechanism.

5. **A few effects stay underspecified relative to the evidence.** I10’s cycle-time cell is “↑ quoted lead time,” which is a commercial change, not a process-time effect. I11’s availability-check change is not specified enough to know whether it holds stock (hurts some customers) or improves allocation (helps). Validation “kill criteria” are only “worse than baseline,” with no stated tolerance or which segment is binding.

6. **Minor unclarity, not error:** cancelled-order sunk impact is in rebound item 8 but not clearly inside the section 1 boundary; empty dedicated-van miles are admitted as only partly visible, then I8 is still scored as a moderate transport win.

No major factual inversion of the DFG, path table, or policies. The failures are ambiguity, polarity, and a few causal stretches—enough, under a hypercritical bar, to keep this well below a high-9.
