The write-up is complete and follows the required seven-heading structure, but it is not close to a high score. Several assignment and audit claims are factually wrong, and those errors collapse the policy’s claimed constraint satisfaction.

**Fatal assignment error.** C010 is given to R8_Hugo as “Skill 3.” R8’s Approval score is **0** (not allowed). Final Approval may only be done by skill 2–3. The table invents a competence that is not in the matrix. That single mistake also manufactures the second “training” assignment used to claim a 22% training share. With C010 removed, only C009 is a real training match (1/9 ≈ 11%), so the 20% rule is not actually met. R8’s training priority being Approval does not override skill 0.

**C001 is the wrong continuity call.** Security Review, urgent, 3h SLA, complexity 0.88, notes “needs specialist.” R3 is skill 1. The same answer cites evidence that keeping a weak-skilled owner adds a median 3.3 hours of service time—already longer than the remaining SLA. Continuity/handover preference is not a license to miss an urgent enterprise SLA. A specialist (R8, then R7) is the defensible choice; supervised skill-1 work is not.

**R3 is used as a dump.** C001 (supervised urgent security), C003, C007, and C014 put R3 at the batch cap of 4 while that person already has 6 open cases and 6.0h left. C007 as owner/rework specialist is reasonable; stacking a supervised Security Review plus two more diagnosis/rework cases on the same person is not, and hours remaining are never checked against complexity.

**Baseline comparison is incorrect.** First-available routing ignores specialization *except skill 0*. The answer claims FA would send C001 to R6 (Security 0). It would not. Eligible FA candidates are those with skill ≥ 1; R3 has the fewest open cases among them. That error undercuts the comparison section.

**Policy text is incomplete and sloppy.** Required scoring themes are named, but `score_supervised`, `relax_constraints`, weights, and Quality-batching rules are not specified. “Diminishing returns for >3” is meaningless because the skill scale tops at 3. Approval “prepare but not finalize” is only partly encoded. Resilience is a slogan (`team_capacity_bonus`) rather than a rule. Tie-breaks exist, but exception handling is a stub.

Handover network, resource similarity, batches, service/waiting time, and performance DFG are mentioned in the right neighborhood, which is not enough to offset the wrong calls.

**Net.** This reads like a competent template with a broken skill lookup, one high-risk SLA assignment, an overloaded resource, a false training KPI, and a misstated baseline. Under a strict reading those are not polish issues; they are the core of sections 3, 4, and 7. A high score would require legal eligibility on every row, hours/SLA that survive the historical evidence the author themselves quote, and PM4Py used as specified.
