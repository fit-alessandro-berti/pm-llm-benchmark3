## Fatal semantic error

In standard process-tree notation (and in PM4Py), `+` is **parallel composition**, `*` is the **binary loop**, `X` is XOR, and `->` is sequence. The problem statement also says this in plain language: Model A puts the check **inside** the revision loop; Model B runs **one** check **concurrently with the entire loop**.

Model A is therefore:

- `Open` then loop then `Close`
- each iteration: **`Analyze || Check`**, then optional `Approve` (or `tau`)
- redo child: `Rework`

Model B is:

- `Open` then **`Check || (loop of Analyze / optional Approve, redo Rework)`** then `Close`
- **exactly one** `Check Revision` for the whole case
- that check may interleave with any Analyze, Approve, or Rework; the AND-join only forces it to finish before `Close`

The graded answer rewrites `+` as a loop whose first child is `Check`, then treats both models as sequential `Analyze → Check` vs `Check → Analyze` with a check **per iteration**. That is not either model. It also contradicts the English labels in the prompt.

Consequences:

- Model B does **not** repeat `Check` after `Rework`.
- Neither model is forced to start with `Analyze` or with `Check` after `Open`; both can emit either first because of concurrency (A: Analyze∥Check in each iteration; B: Check∥whole loop).
- Model A’s minimum/maximum check counts equal the number of loop iterations (≥1, unbounded). Model B’s check count is **exactly 1**.
- “First visible event after Open” is not a distinguishing feature.

Almost every row of §2 is therefore false.

## Counterexamples are invalid

Required: ≥2 traces in A\B and ≥2 in B\A, at least one with two analyses.

Given A1/B1 (`Open, Analyze, Check, Close` vs `Open, Check, Analyze, Close`) are in **both** languages (A: parallel pair; B: parallel Check with the loop). A2/B2 assume a second Check after Rework in B; B does not generate a second Check. So the table does not establish either inclusion failure.

Valid A\B examples (among others):

- `Open, Analyze, Check, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close` (two checks)
- `Open, Check, Analyze, Approve, Rework, Check, Analyze, Close`

Valid B\A examples:

- `Open, Analyze, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close` (Rework before any Check)
- `Open, Check, Analyze, Rework, Analyze, Close` (one Check, two Analyses)
- `Open, Analyze, Approve, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close`

Optional Approve and hidden `tau` matter: they add interleavings but do not create a second Check in B, and they do not remove the mandatory per-iteration Check in A. The answer never states this.
