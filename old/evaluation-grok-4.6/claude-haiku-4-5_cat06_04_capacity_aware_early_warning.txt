The answer has the right qualitative skeleton and would beat a naive `p_now` ranker. It is not close to a high score. The quantitative core is wrong, several numbers do not survive a desk check, and the live allocation contradicts the rule that was just written down.

## Core decision-theoretic failure

The suggested object is

`ARR × value − swarm cost − false-alarm burden`.

The deployed score is

`(adj_p_now) × (Value − FalseAlarmBurden) × (midpoint ARR) / (Cost + spillover proxy)`.

Multiplying an already-absolute risk reduction by `p_now` double-counts probability. False-alarm burden is then subtracted in the numerator *and* added into `Cost`. Spillover is proxied by `0.02 × this case’s own value`, which prices the externality on the wrong object. The formula is also never evaluated: Section 2 assigns “TIER 1 / 1.5 / 2” by narrative. A deployable rule that cannot be audited on the live book is not deployable.

That error is not cosmetic. Correct midpoint direct EV is roughly:

| Case | Correct midpoint net | Answer’s figure | Effect |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| I-08 | `0.10×100000 − 5450 = +€4,550` (range about −€450 to +€9,550) | **−€1,950** | Sign flip; strongest-value case discarded as “not economical” |
| I-04 | `0.19×60000 − 2450 = +€8,950` | ~€2,120 | Understated ~4× |
| I-01 | `0.27×9000 − 700 = +€1,730` | mixed | Understated |
| I-02 | `0.15×25000 − 1050 = +€2,700` | €2,700 | Accidentally used the *correct* formula |
| I-09 | `0.24×20000 − 1250 = +€3,550` | “looks good but reject” | Numerically strong, then dropped |

I-02 is computed as `value × ARR − cost` (correct). I-01/I-04/I-07/I-08/I-10 are computed as `p × ARR × value − cost` (incorrect). The book is ranked with two different objective functions.

I-08 can still be deferred on option value, weak ARR, and a €5,000 disruption cost. Claiming a firmly negative EV is false.

## Arithmetic that does not add up

Even inside the wrong formula, the impact table is sloppy.

- Base I-01: `0.61 × 9000 × 0.27 − 250 = 1,232`, written as **€1,432**.
- Optimistic I-01: text uses 0.32, implied arithmetic is ~0.35.
- Adverse I-04: `0.40 × 60000 × 0.10 − 2000 = +€400`, written as **−€400**. That sign error is then used to call I-04 a “deadweight loss.”
- I-04 base benefit subtracts €2,000 in Section 3 and €2,450 in Section 2.
- “Direct breaches avoided” (optimistic 1.0–1.3, base 0.6–0.9) does not match the ARRs used in the same rows (`0.32+0.25 = 0.57`, `0.27+0.19 = 0.46`, or `p×ARR ≈ 0.3`). The lead column is unmoored from the money column.

Optimistic / Base / Adverse are given **60–70%, 75–85%, and 40–55% credence**. Those cannot be credences over mutually exclusive worlds. Calling Base both the decision case and the highest-probability case while also giving Optimistic majority-ish credence is incoherent.

Optimistic spillover is allowed to become *positive* (“knowledge flow”). That contradicts the stated externality model.

## Allocation is operationally inconsistent with the rule

Eligibility calls are the strongest part of the memo: I-03 is correctly refused; I-05 is suppressed; I-07 is treated as evidence-changed; I-06 is held for imputation; NNL is not trusted at face value. Those are the items the rubric cares about, and they are right.

The slotting is not.

- Section 1: 4 slots, reserve 1, **3 for the live book**.
- Section 2: intervene on **two** cases (I-01, I-04), reserve one, leave the fourth idle and park I-02/I-07 as “WAIT.”
- Own stage weight *boosts* Diagnosis Started, then every Diagnosis Started case is deferred while two long-horizon Triage cases are taken.
- I-07 has **1.5 h** to SLA and is only useful before Diagnosis Complete. “WAIT because the window is closing” is the opposite of a closing-window policy. If the swarm is worth doing, it is worth doing at 12:00; if not, say do-not-intervene. Wait is a third, incoherent act.
- I-02 is already in Diagnosis Started with 3 h left and a clean second-alert / no-prior-swarm history. After calibration it is still a serious candidate. Parking it for a regulatory maybe, while spending a slot on I-04 (10 h, Strategic Enterprise, known overprediction, €2,000 false-alarm load, adverse case negative under their own—wrong—math), is not robust.
- Section 5 invents a “1 of every 4 slots for the lowest-value quartile” safeguard and does not apply it today. I-07 (€5k) is the natural test of that rule; it is left waiting.

Reserving one slot is a defensible preference. Reserving one, using two, and idling the fourth while time-critical eligible cases sit in the queue is not a scarce-capacity policy. It is leftover inventory.

## Uncertainty is announced, then ignored in the ranking

Calibration signs are correct (SME +, Strategic −, Public 0, NNL wide). Midpoint offsets are then treated as known. Effect ranges are treated as usable causal midpoints in a point score, with a later disclaimer that they are not. That is having it both ways.

I-04 is the clear stress case: overpredicted segment, long deadline, fat false-alarm cost, adverse net fragile. A robust rule would require a second look or a smaller claim, not “TIER 1.5, Slot 2.” I-09’s manual gate is reasonable; converting that gate into a hard do-not-intervene with 2.5 h left and no named reviewer SLA is how the case ages out of eligibility.

Format: “exactly six sections.” A summary table and an eight-point recap are extra.

A high score needs a coherent object, arithmetic that a reviewer can reproduce, a portfolio that uses scarce slots on open intervention windows, and scenario weights that add up. This memo fails all four. The impressive length conceals a broken objective, inconsistent EV math, a sign error that drives the adverse story, and an allocation that will not survive the next 90 minutes of clock time.
