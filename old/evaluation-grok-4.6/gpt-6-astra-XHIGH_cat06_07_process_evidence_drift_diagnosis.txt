8.8

This is a strong, internally consistent audit that hits the intended diagnoses: April is a schema/pipeline incident, June is a routing/mix change that need not be concept drift, August–September stack process, concept, and population effects, and October is not a comparable mature cohort. The table, timeline, tests, training gates, alert dependencies, and failure-mode analysis are all pointed at the right mechanisms. It is not flawless.

**What is actually strong.** The taxonomy is used precisely rather than as labels to slap on whole months. Corrupted April prefixes are treated as measurement failure, not operational covariate drift; June’s V2 jump is tied to stable remote-fix times and a lower-risk mix; supplier delay is split into process evidence (edge times) versus concept drift in the deployed feature space (mature V3 rates 0.35 → 0.64 while scores stay ~0.19). Falsifiers are stated, not just supporting evidence. October’s 0.16 is correctly refused as recovery, including the 0.088–0.538 bound and the long-path backlog. Training advice is proportionate: repair before calibrate, do not apply one intercept to June overprediction and August underprediction, and do not deploy path-specific models from eventual variants. The PM4Py sketch uses real APIs, keeps calendar vs case-start interpretations distinct, deduplicates by event identity rather than activity label, and refuses an invented drift detector.

**Strict issues (these keep it below 9).**

1. **Silent empty-calendar bug.** `filter_time_range(...)` is applied first, then the half-open `loc` is applied to that result, not to `df`. If PM4Py drops timezone-aware timestamps, uses a different endpoint rule, or returns too little, the month’s event-calendar view becomes empty and the `loc` cannot recover it. The comment claims independence from library endpoint rules; the code does not implement that.

2. **August V3 share is under-explained.** V3 goes 22% → 35% in August while Partner Z is still 0%. Supplier lateness explains `Order Part → Part Received` times and within-V3 breach rates; it does not by itself explain more cases taking the parts path. Seasonality, conversion from V1/V2, or variant-assignment artifacts are not seriously considered. September’s further rise is better grounded in Partner Z.

3. **Partner Z bias direction is left vague.** The text correctly refuses 0.45 vs 0.27 as a fairness finding, but does not say the likely direction: slower maturation plus a higher V3 share means currently observed Partner Z (and September aggregate) rates are more likely understated than overstated if unlabeled cases are long-path breaches. October gets this treatment; September does not.

4. **`extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is cargo-culted.** It is required, and the leakage warning is right, but calling it on one-month clipped event windows with `grouper_freq="M"` and `start_timestamp_key=time:timestamp` is degenerate and possibly the wrong start-time column. That is compliance theater, not process evidence.

5. **Serialization / API nits in a sketch that claims to be auditable.** Returned DFG Counters, native performance DFGs, and a Series of diagnosis counts are not cleanly serializable despite the serialization remark. `read_csv(..., dtype={"event_id": "string"})` assumes an optional column. `"Compare calendar workload with calendar workload"` is a wording slip.

6. **Covariate-drift row is slightly over-inclusive.** April–May “recorded inputs” are pipeline corruption; Partner Z attributes are omitted from X and belong primarily in population → concept-in-the-model’s-X. The prose knows this; the “months implicated” cell still blurs it.

7. **The code is not concise.** It is careful, but it is a small library, not a sketch. That would be fine if the `filter_time_range` composition were safe.

No major mis-diagnosis, no invented PM4Py detector, no treatment of October as a real recovery, and remediation is staged rather than “retrain the model.” Remaining defects are robustness, a missed mix-share mechanism in August, and a few unclarities—not a wrong story.
