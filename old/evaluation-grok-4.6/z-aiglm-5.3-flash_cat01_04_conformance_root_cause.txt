The analysis is substantively strong on the dimensions the prompt actually scores: it treats the June–July collapse as a stacked measurement problem, uses the staged fitness ladder (32% → 58% → 81% → 89%) as causal evidence rather than decoration, keeps model obsolescence distinct from logging defects, refuses to indict employees from log-moves, and treats the optional sign-off tree as an over-permissive screen rather than a policy proof. Format compliance is high: all eight required headings, table schemas, six hypotheses, four diagnostic phases, six governance rules, and the required PM4Py entry points are present.

It is not nearly flawless.

**Causal arithmetic is sloppy in the required explanation.** Fitting-trace rate fell 93 → 32 (61 pp). Label map + technical-event removal + source ordering recover 32 → 89 (57 pp), leaving a real residual (`Manual Override`, missing checks). Calling this “roughly 61 alignment points out of the 61-point Jun-Jul drop” conflates fitness percentage points with alignment cost (0.08 → 0.74) and over-attributes the drop. “Every June trace can carry up to three sensor mutations” is also too coarse: `Queue Routed` is 530/620, reversals are 70 traces, and 30 traces still say `Classify`.

**The alignment-move summarizer is wrong.** Sync moves `(A, A)` are counted as log-moves; model-moves `('>>', A)` are skipped because `'>>'` is a truthy string. Printed “dominant log/model moves” would therefore be garbage even if `fitness_alignments` itself is fine. That is a real defect in a section whose job is alignment diagnostics, not a cosmetic nit.

**Other code/practical issues.** `precision_alignments` is invoked on every tier of every period (~14 times on ~600-trace logs), far beyond the requested August–September comparison and a realistic hang risk. Source-sequence “repair” is a no-op on these aggregates (order is baked into variant strings); that limitation is disclosed, so it is not a hidden error, but it means the code cannot actually demonstrate the +8 pp step it narrates. Perfect-trace detection depends on a `fitness` key that diagnostics often lack.

**Evidence-ledger noise.** E14 is a theoretical precision warning, not evidence. Several “weakens” cells are empty or circular. The adjacency list is conceptually good (especially the missing `policy_change → normative_model` edge) but poorly presented: directed edges are not reliably marked, and labels collapse (`ClassifyRisk Screen`).

**Ranking quibble.** Treating H1 at 67 as causal responsibility *for the measured collapse* is high relative to a change that mainly explains the August residual after artifacts are removed. The rank order is still defensible; the number is inflated.

What remains genuinely good: E17 (unchecked share 14.7% → 11.1%) as a check on an “escalating bypass” story; dual-use of stable KPIs (neither prove data error nor exonerate conduct); high-value omission as a hard limit on policy claims; a four-phase program that isolates sensors before models before conduct; and governance rules that map 1:1 onto the failure modes in the chronology. Those are why this is well above a middling answer, and why it still cannot be scored as near-flawless.
