**3.3**

The submission has the required nine headings and the right vocabulary (offered load, reservation, preemption penalty, handover/setup, bottleneck migration, SLA vs fairness). Under a strict reading of the spec, that is almost all that works. The quantitative core is unauditable, internally contradictory, incomplete, and in Section 8 largely fabricated.

**Section 1.** Demand/service assumptions are only half-specified. Uniform draws from the given ranges are a legitimate modeling choice, but arrivals (deterministic expectations vs a point process), independence across activities, overtime, breaks, and how unfinished work at shift end is treated are never stated. Objective A is garbled (“1P1 attainment”) and does not define P1/P2/P3 weights. Objective B drops the comparison operators, so “maxmin utilization … 0.15” and “planned utilization 0.90” are not constraints. Decision variables (reservation fraction, parallelization threshold, preemption rule, overflow trigger) are not listed as variables.

**Section 2.** This is the first hard analytical failure. The claim that mechanical offered load peaks at ~210 person-minutes in 14:00–16:00 does not follow from the tables. Mid-range, tail-weighted mechanical work for that block is on the order of 100–125 person-minutes; electrical work is the ~200 person-minute peak. Either the skill was mislabeled or ME work was double-counted without saying so. There is no block × skill table, no conversion of shifts/breaks/Farah’s half-day/Emil’s late start into available minutes, and no speed-adjusted capacity vs load comparison. U2 and U4 get a sentence each. The prompt required enough work to audit the logic; a reader cannot reproduce the 95% utilization claims.

**Section 3.** Three named policies exist, which meets the minimum. They are not implementable as written. Strict specialization never says what happens when no “≤ 1.10” specialist is on shift (Farah and Deepa are largely ineligible). Hybrid “30% reserved capacity” is not an operational dispatch rule (calendar holds, virtual tokens, or head-of-line blocking?). P2 vs P3 ranking, overtime, setup-aware sequencing, and preemption under S and H are missing. Extra rules later appear in §6 (20% reservation floor, 12-minute parallelization test) that are not in the policy definitions.

**Section 4.** Incomplete and inconsistent. U2 and U4 are absent; S and P are missing for U3/U5. The required coverage is therefore not met. Ranges are presented as if estimated, but no seed, replication, or bounding method is given—so they are invented observations, which the prompt forbids. Utilization fairness is undefined, so 0.09 vs 0.22 cannot be interpreted. “None” as a failure mode is not a serious risk assessment. Worst: these numbers are then used as if they were results.

**Section 5.** The recommendation is false on the author’s own table. H is said to keep P1 SLA ≥ 0.91 in all five shocks and max utilization ≤ 0.94; the table has H/U5 P1 SLA 0.82–0.87, H/U3 overlapping 0.88–0.92, and H/U5 max util 0.96. Switching *to* full pooling when P1 arrivals exceed 1.5× also contradicts §4, where H already beats P on U1 (the 1.7× morning surge). Robustness is asserted, not shown. U2 and U4—the shocks that move the electrical/software bottleneck and inflate tails—are ignored in the decision.

**Section 6.** Partial. P3 starvation, skill erosion, and interpretability are mentioned. Required topics are skipped or waved at: gaming of estimates is absent; Validation as a new bottleneck is not analyzed; fairness is not treated as an implementation risk beyond a slogan. New policy parameters are introduced here instead of in §3.

**Section 7.** Attribute list matches the prompt and is the strongest section. It is still thin: no schema, no clock definition (arrival vs intake-complete vs first-touch), no unit of the SLA clock. `discover_bottlenecks` is not a standard PM4Py API and is not an acceptable substitute for specifying DFG performance, waiting-time filtering, or resource profiles.

**Section 8.** This is a failing deliverable, not a “scaffold.” Tables are replaced by `...`. The simulator is comments plus 12 identical Ada/Intake rows and hardcoded metrics. `seeds=3` is a no-op because the seed is fixed inside the function. `get_service_time` is never called. Service/waiting outputs are not labeled or decomposed by stage. U2 and U4 are not run. Handover, setup, parallel/sequential branches, and the 10% restart penalty are not implemented. Claiming a “full 300-line implementation follows” while shipping a stub is non-reproducible and violates every numbered requirement in this section.

**Section 9.** Directionally reasonable (expected counts cannot identify preemption × parallelism interactions), but the pilot is underspecified: one shift, no power calculation, fallback to S “for that block only” is operationally incoherent, and P1 protection during the experiment is a phrase rather than a design.

**Overall.** Format compliance and some process-mining literacy cannot offset a wrong bottleneck, a recommendation that contradicts the comparison table, missing scenarios, and a fake Python block. The specialization-vs-pooling tension is named, not computed. Speed multipliers and time-varying shifts are scenery. Under the stated bar—coherent calculations, honest uncertainty, implementable rules, working code—this is well below a competent pass.
