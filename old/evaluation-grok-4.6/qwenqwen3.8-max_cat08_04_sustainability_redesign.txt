**7.5**

The final write-up hits the required structure and most of the hard constraints: seven named sections, a real system boundary, an indicator set tied to log attributes, twelve interventions with the four mandated fields, a direction/range table using the given columns, more than six rebound/displacement risks, a package rather than isolated green ideas, and a validation plan that includes SP1/SP2/Q1, segment service, and exception governance. It also correctly refuses blanket lowest-emission choices and distinguishes air/van by slack and criticality.

It is not nearly flawless.

The biggest analytical weakness is loose generalization from path metrics to segments. Stock→air at 18.5 kgCO2e and stock→ground at 3.6 kgCO2e are variant numbers, not Standard-stock Service-contract averages (7.8). Using them as if they are the intervention effect overstates the typical gain. The Critical→stock/ground exception path (3% share, 9% late, much lower emissions) is the prompt’s clearest warning against forcing the green option on downtime work, and it is barely used.

There is an unresolved design contradiction. Service-contract Standard-stock already has an 18h median and 41h p90. A 12–24h consolidation wait is unlikely to keep an 85% on-time probability for much of that segment, yet the package still offers consolidated truck to Service-contract when “slack exists” without showing that such slack is common. MTO Service-contract (13% late) is likewise folded into a batched “planned lane” even though heat-treatment waits already raise late risk 1.7×. The 85% rule is asserted more than demonstrated.

The effects table is comprehensive but often hedged into near-vacuity (“↓ or small ↑”, “↑ or ↓ if…”), so it records uncertainty more than estimated direction. Batching caps are never operationalized against `promised_lead_time_hours` or observed p90s. The indicator set is broader than “concise.” Lifecycle reasoning is mostly gate-to-gate; customer downtime as a displaced impact is treated as a service KPI, not as a competing environmental/system cost.

These are not formatting misses. They are exactly the multi-criteria and path-grounding gaps the rubric penalizes. The answer is strong and usable; under a hypercritical bar it is clearly short of excellent.
