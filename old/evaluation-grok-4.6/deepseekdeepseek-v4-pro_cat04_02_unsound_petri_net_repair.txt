**6.4**

The final write-up has the right disease and the right cheapest cure, but it is not close to flawless under the stated rubric. The main defect is identified correctly, Repair A is the genuine 1-arc fix, the repaired net matches that edit, and sections 7–8 are honest about soundness vs. intent. The score is held down by missed required marking work, a non-minimal second repair, and diagnostic code that would not run.

**What is actually strong.** The net is correctly treated as a structural workflow net that should not be expected to be sound. The principal hole is the one-input silent forwarder `t_join_waived`, which does not consume `p_analysis_done`. The business consequence (authorization without a completed analysis, leftover tokens, exact `fm` not reached) is right. The marking table for `t_register → t_split → t_analyze → t_waive_review → t_join_waived → t_authorize` is a legal derivation and does distinguish “a token is in `sink`” from “`fm` has been reached.” Repair A (`p_analysis_done → t_join_waived`) is the unique cheapest policy-preserving fix under the stated cost model. The standalone repaired net is complete and consistent with that edit.

**Section 3 fails an explicit requirement.** The prompt asked to show whether `t_authorize` can occur *before or after* `t_analyze`. The only sequence given fires analysis first. The illegal order is never derived:

`t_register → t_split → t_waive_review → t_join_waived → t_authorize`

which yields `{sink:1, p_analysis:1}`, leaves `t_analyze` enabled after “completion,” and is the clearer violation of mandatory analysis. The step-5 gloss “authorization possible without analysis token being consumed” is false for the sequence they actually fired: `t_analyze` already consumed `p_analysis`. What remains is an unconsumed *completion* token. Calling the standard path “partial deadlock” is also the wrong term; that path is the well-behaved one.

**The second repair is valid but not minimal.** A and C are structurally distinct and both keep analysis mandatory, which satisfies the letter of “semantically *or* structurally.” C is not a minimal member of the “rewire waiver into the normal join” family. Adding `p_waived → t_join_normal` and deleting `t_join_waived` (plus its two arcs) is the same policy with a strictly lower edit cost than introducing a silent hop through `p_review_done`. The cost model also double-counts a deleted transition and its incident arcs; that is allowed if defined, but it inflates C and makes the minimality claim look neater than it is. Skipping a clearly labeled optional-analysis policy variant is not fatal, but it leaves the two rows closer than the prompt’s example families.

**Section 6 is not executable as written.** `semantics.is_enabled(t, [], marking)` treats the transition as absent from the net and will always fail the enabledness check; `semantics.execute(t, marking)` omits the net. The helper therefore cannot replay the counterexample. The repaired-net script only replays the *successful* waiver path after analysis and never shows that premature `t_join_waived` / `t_authorize` is now disabled. The self-contradictory comments (“NOT enabled… actually it IS enabled”) belong in scratch work, not in the delivered artifact. Reconstructing `net` instead of assuming `net`, `im`, `fm` exist is a smaller spec miss.

**Smaller nits.** The diagnosis table never really discharges “every node lies on a source-to-sink path” as a row-level claim (it is only asserted in the verdict). Table arrows are stripped, so several edits read as juxtaposition rather than directed arcs. None of these would matter if the marking argument and the diagnostic helper were tight; together with those gaps they keep the answer well below the “nearly flawless” band.
