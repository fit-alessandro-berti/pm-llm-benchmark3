**6.8**

The answer is conceptually strong and format-compliant, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict standard the quantitative core is too sloppy, several promised analyses are not actually delivered, and a few interpretive steps overreach the identification the author otherwise describes carefully.

**What is done well.** The write-up recognizes the right scientific problem: endogenous, staggered, incomplete adoption bundled with routing, staffing, case-mix, and a shared fraud desk. Section 1 stays descriptive. The estimand table is the high point—ITT availability, intensity, case-level use, median vs tail, rework, escalation, CSAT, dynamics, and transportability are separated, and interference/treatment-version issues are named. The mechanism table is process-literate (substitution vs capacity, tail amplification, denominator change in rework, survey selection). Median and p90 are never collapsed. The rollout choice is the correct one: expand only inside a design that creates a control and a saturation contrast. The claim ladder is mostly well ordered.

**Section 5 tables fail as evidence.** This is the largest defect. Pre/post and DiD cells are concatenated and unsigned: `4.038.0`, `48.9048.50 (0.40)`, Gamma median `8.45` / `4.28` / `4.18`. Minus signs and separators are missing throughout the section that is supposed to discipline the rest of the memo. As printed, several rows fail internal arithmetic. Adopter CSAT `+0.002` and control `0.084` cannot produce DiD `+0.087` unless the control change is negative; the same pattern appears in the Gamma cycle-time rows and the sensitivity envelope (`8.45 to +0.73`, `9.5 to +0.8`). A reader using the tables alone cannot recover direction. The surrounding prose often implies the intended signs, and reconstructed arithmetic is largely correct, but that does not rescue a results section whose primary artifacts are unreadable. That is a serious quantitative-discipline failure, not a cosmetic one.

**Identification rhetoric outruns the analysis.** The primary strategy is a cohort-specific group-time event study that refuses already-treated controls and lists leads, placebos, leave-one-out, and never-treated-only checks. Almost none of that is computed. What is shown is two coarse 2×2 DiDs plus raw pre/post means. The April DiD uses only Delta/Epsilon, discarding Gamma as a not-yet-treated spring control even though Section 3 said Gamma would play that role. The Python model is a pooled TWFE event study—the estimator the text warns against—and it is run only on the median. Diagnostics advertised as identifying (event-time leads, placebo dates, donor-drop, wild cluster) are absent. A prospective RCT is listed as a strategy for these data; it is a future design, not an identification strategy for the panel.

**Several numerical and interpretive slips remain after restoring signs.** Windows are not held fixed: Gamma’s raw contrast uses Jan–Jun while the DiD uses May–Jun, so the two Gamma numbers are not the same estimand. “Conditional DiD” in Section 7 is just an unadjusted never-treated 2×2; nothing was conditioned on. Load per FTE is the obvious time-varying confounder (Alpha rises from ~30 to ~43 arrivals/FTE) and is mentioned qualitatively, then ignored in the evidence tables. August volume falls in every branch; late “recovery” is therefore partly calendar. The intensity comparison (first treated month vs August) is correctly labeled non-causal, but the “1.59 hours per 10 pp” figure is still a three-point coincident movement of use, learning, mix, and season. Sensitivity ranges are invented (±5 / ±10 hours) rather than computed from alternative specifications.

**The “conditionally causal ~9%” step is too strong.** The 9% figure is 4.5h / pre median. It does not net out the +10% routing campaign, FTE losses, or the May complexity jump, all of which the author showed are timed with adoption. If those are part of the bundle, the estimand is not an automation effect. If they are confounders, parallel trends are implausible and the rung should stay “suggestive.” Putting a 9% availability effect on the conditionally causal step, then telling leadership they may safely quote “about four hours better,” is more confident than Sections 3 and 6 justify. Escalation confidence of 60 is also high given that March’s desk already lifted escalation everywhere and queue waits are unobserved.

**Code is usable but does not make the memo reproducible.** Validation, monotone adoption, printed trends, pre/post means, relative-time indicators, clustered covariance, and explicit non-causal comments are all good. The script does not produce the headline DiDs, intensity first-vs-last table, or sensitivity ranges, so the claims that matter cannot be regenerated from the “reproducible” block. Printing a TWFE event study after rejecting TWFE, with five clusters, adds little except a warning the reader already has.

**Smaller unclarities.** Section 1 is slightly compressed on how 4–6% pre-adoption fast-path use should be treated (leakage vs another version vs measurement). E9 correctly says heterogeneity is not identified, then Section 8 treats Delta and Epsilon as interchangeable even though Epsilon’s mix is the relevant transport target. Guardrails (5% p90, 1 pp rework, 0.10 CSAT) are arbitrary. “Visualizes or prints… without subplots” is met only by printing matrices.

**Net.** This is a good causal-policy memo with the right structure, the right threats, and a defensible decision. It is not a 9–10 answer: the evidence tables are broken, the modern design is described rather than executed, and the headline 9% / four-hour claim is pushed one rung too high. Those are enough, under a hypercritical bar, to land well below excellence.
