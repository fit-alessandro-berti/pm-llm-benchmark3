**6.4**

The final six-section writeup is a strong, on-rubric answer: it favors a conformant second-review strategy, ranks A/B last, refuses to treat re-screening / SAR / self-certification as substitutes, accounts for P-901, and keeps exception paths labeled as not yet enabled. It is not nearly flawless. Several analytical shortcuts and internal inconsistencies are material under a strict reading.

**What is solid**

- Exact required headings, all of A–G ranked, and the four conformance labels used correctly.
- A and B are treated as deontically blocked, not as EV-positive shortcuts. That is the main thing the rubric punishes if missed.
- Screening is explicitly *not* a substitute while risk ≥ 70 or the fuzzy match remains unresolved.
- Four-eyes, SAR non-disclosure, and “SAR does not authorize execution” are handled correctly.
- Parallel information-gathering and a time-boxed S2/S4 choice show the intended integration of prediction, conformance, spillover, and uncertainty.
- Section 4’s mapping of rules to actions, and the argument that a favorable forecast cannot rehabilitate A/B, is the right kind of reasoning.

**Material flaws**

1. **Invented S4 review duration.** The prompt gives S2 review as 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 to record/execute. The answer repeatedly assumes S4 review is “20–45 minutes.” That unsupported compression is what makes “S4 at 35 → finish in 60–90” look feasible. If S4’s review is the same order as S2’s, S4-at-35 implies roughly 75–125 minutes to execution, i.e. the deadline is probably already gone. Rank 1 and the 35-vs-65 reranking in §5 rest on this invention.

2. **Availability is conflated with completion.** “S4 available in 35–45 minutes” is not “S4 can independently review within 35–45 minutes.” The table, trees, and §6 blur those. That is not a wording nit; it is the quantity that decides D vs C.

3. **Wait-then-interrupt is often dominated, and the answer still ranks it first.** A 40–45 minute cutoff followed by C yields roughly 80–130 minutes *and* still hits P-901. That is worse than immediate C on both P-884 timing and spillover whenever S4 is late. The answer notices this in §5 but does not let it discipline Rank 1. If S4’s arrival is roughly uniform on 35–65, the fallback branch is the more common one. Ranking the composite first without an explicit expected-harm comparison is a logical gap, not a taste difference.

4. **Internal inconsistency on the time-box.** The table describes waiting then switching (“fallback to C after a hard cutoff → 85–130”). §6 is better: get a firm ETA *now* and do not wait passively. Under the grading rule that later corrections count, §6 is the intended plan — but the ranked table, which is the required artifact, still sells the dominated wait-then-C path.

5. **Threshold mix-up (40 vs 70).** Auto-clear requires no alert *and* risk < 40. Second review is required if risk ≥ 70 *or* an unresolved fuzzy match. The D-tree talks about “below the mandatory-review threshold” and then “auto-clear after refreshed screening” as if those were the same gate. A cleared match with refreshed risk 50 would drop the second-review mandate without permitting Auto Clear. The answer’s conservative default (“still get a second review”) papers over the error rather than resolving it.

**Smaller issues that still count under a hypercritical standard**

- E as rank 3 is poorly motivated as a *next* action on a 90-minute clock. Six-to-24-hour evidence is a hold/escalation path, not a close competitor to C/D. F is at least a same-horizon control action; ranking it below a near-certain deadline miss needed a sharper dominance argument.
- Several table cells are qualitative mush (“low regulatory harm if independent review”) while still labeled as ranges.
- The 40–45 minute cutoff is arbitrary relative to S2’s 35–75 review band; once that band is respected, the cutoff is not deadline-preserving at all.
- Observational/non-transportability of the 0.48–0.64 SLA and 0.55–0.75 approval estimates is mentioned, then mostly unused in the ranking arithmetic.

**Net.** This is competent expert process-monitoring reasoning with the right normative skeleton. It is not a 8–10 answer because the operational ranking of D over C is propped up by an invented review time, a blurred availability/completion distinction, and a composite that is often stochastically worse than immediate C. Those are the exact places the task asked for careful counterfactual and spillover reasoning.
