**8.0**

The answer is substantively strong: all required headings and table schemas are present, event-time reconstruction is stated correctly, and every case classification is defensible. It is not near-flawless. Formalization, code fidelity, and a few judgment calls keep it well below a 9+.

**What is actually good.** Blanks are treated as non-updates; there is no backward fill; final attributes are not copied onto earlier events; `event_index` is a real total-order key; event / effective / record / evidence time are distinguished. Those choices are then used, not merely announced. TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-306, TX-307, TX-308, TX-309, and TX-311 are decided on the right snapshot and the right trigger. TX-305 correctly refuses to promote the 7 April settlement amount into the 6 April release. TX-302 is internally consistent: an independently valid `Enhanced_Review` yields `COMPLIANT`, with EX-77 treated as redundant rather than load-bearing. Section 6’s failure modes are concrete and case-anchored; section 7 hits the requested control changes.

**Formalization is not as tight as it looks.** `State` is defined as `State(c, a, e)` and then used as `State(amount, q)`. `Fresh_Enhanced_R1` depends on an undefined “review scope” and an undefined “R1-material” predicate. R2’s `ApprovalPacket(a) covers EnhancedReview(h)` is a label, not a definition. R1_OK is written with two-valued `NOT Trigger_R1` even though the prose insists on Kleene unknown. R3 is thinner than R1: no comparable unknown-handling, and “no baseline screen” is waved at in the table as a “separate unsupported condition.” R4 is formalized as a helper, which is right, but the interaction with R1 is stated twice (no later material change **and** `AmountInvalid`) without defining whether those are the same test. For a section titled “Normalized Rule Table” plus “formal pseudo-logic,” this is sloppy.

**TX-310 is the softest case call.** `INDETERMINATE` is allowed by the prompt’s “no single canonical answer” clause, and section 4 lists the NONCOMPLIANT reading. A stricter R5 reading is at least as natural: validity is a conjunction, and “the evidence supports that approval existed at the relevant time” is not met when the qualified certificate is created after release, the PDF clock is declared non-conclusive, and the only witness is the requester. Calling that `INDETERMINATE` with “HIGH” confidence that validity is unresolved slightly over-credits a conflict-of-interest oral claim. The unused `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` label is consistent with their taxonomy, but it also means the four-way scheme is never fully exercised on the data.

**The code is useful pandas with a PM4Py fig leaf.** It does the eight mechanical things asked: `StringIO`, `format_dataframe`, within-case ffill, release snapshot, latest-control tracking, coarse R1–R4 flags, exemption join, unknown-value diagnostics, plus a toy trace-order table. It does **not** hard-code case IDs to narrative labels. That is the right design.

It still has real gaps:

- PM4Py is used only to rename/format a frame. There is no event-log object, no DECLARE model, no conformance API. The “optional DECLARE” requirement is met by a boolean table that any `groupby` could produce.
- Section 1 advertises review-baseline / cumulative >20% detection. `_is_material_change` uses consecutive steps. `amount_invalidation_after` saves the cases in this log, but the implementation does not match the stated semantics. Two small post-review increases that only jointly exceed 20% would be missed by the material-change flag.
- R2 relevance is “any `risk_level` / `customer_type` update,” including Create and non-triggering type changes, plus a brittle `note.str.contains("standard review packet")` filter. That is a log-specific hack, not a packet-linkage check.
- `evidence_status` (`PROVEN_BEFORE_RELEASE` vs `DISPUTED`) is a human verdict baked into the exemption frame. It is not a case-id lookup, but it is the switch that forces TX-310 to `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. The narrative’s hardest evidential judgment is therefore not derived by the program.
- R3 matching via forward-filled `jurisdiction` on the list-update row happens to work for XZ; it is not a model of “this update added the release-time jurisdiction.”
- `enhanced_scope_unknown` collapses “amount unknown at review” into invalid-or-unknown R1 coverage, which is conservative and fine for TX-305, but the coarse outcome vocabulary (`PASS_COARSE`, `NONCOMPLIANT_COARSE`) is a parallel taxonomy the reader must mentally align with section 3.

**Smaller defects.** “POTENTIAL HIGH” is not a defined severity. R5 is listed as a “triggered rule” for TX-302/TX-310 only. The dangling PM4Py marketing citation does no work. Same-timestamp handling is correct for TX-311 and well discussed; the alternative “sticky obligation” reading is properly rejected on the text of R1.

**Net.** This is a high-quality compliance writeup with no crashed cases and a runnable reconstruction sketch. Under a strict reading it is still short of excellent: the logic is not fully formal, the code does not implement the semantics it claims, PM4Py is ceremonial, and the one designed evidential crux (EX-88) is parked in `INDETERMINATE` rather than forced through R5’s conjunction. That is an 8.0, not a 9.
