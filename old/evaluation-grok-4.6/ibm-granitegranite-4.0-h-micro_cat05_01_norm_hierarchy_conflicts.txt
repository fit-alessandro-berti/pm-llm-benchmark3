3.2

The submission has the required headings and table shells, but it is not a defensible compliance assessment. It repeatedly misstates the sources, invents rules the record does not contain, applies the wrong instrument to the wrong hour, and then assigns labels that contradict its own stated reasons. A reviewer could not rely on it.

**Method is asserted and then abandoned.** Section 1 claims that the most restrictive control wins and that GPIP-4 is the highest-authority procurement text. The same section also treats ECD-2 as “most authoritative during its window,” and Section 2 then lets a non-controlled CFO email override GPIP-4, the SOP, and ECD-2. Those three positions cannot be held together. The write-up never separates facts, interpretations, assumptions, and recommendations as required. It never takes a clear position on the dispositive authority question: ECD-2 is jointly signed by the COO and Incident Commander, GPIP-4 reserves written exceptions to the Chief Risk Officer, and the CRO did not ratify ECD-2. A4 (emergency waives tendering only) and A6 (later approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment control) are essentially unused.

**The hierarchy table is internally incoherent and treats a non-norm as a norm.** GPIP-4 is said to “override” Finance and Compliance; those are controls it *creates*. The SOP is said to be able to override GPIP-4, which A5 expressly forbids if the effect is to weaken GPIP-4. ECD-2 is given a general power to override GPIP-4, although C4 only claims precedence over ordinary timing and supplier onboarding. The CFO email is given Low weight and, in the next columns, power to override the three higher instruments; D3–D4 cut the other way. System Outage ON-44 is placed in the norm hierarchy even though the brief says it changes evidential weight and is not an exception. The prompt asked for issue-specific orderings (timing vs onboarding vs Finance vs Compliance vs SoD vs scope). There is only one confused stack.

**The rule catalogue is not a normalization of the sources.** R1 (request before commit) is not a stated rule. R3 assigns Compliance_Clearance to a Finance Controller and times it to Create_PO rather than to commitment (A3). R4 invents a Legal-clearance requirement for all new suppliers; Legal appears only in D2 above EUR 150,000. R6 says Create_PO must precede commit with exception “None,” which contradicts B4, C1, and the author’s own R5. R8 uses the wrong threshold and the wrong geographic limiter. R9 and R10 are the same row twice. Segregation of duties (A1), A4, A6, C3’s two-person / four-hour record rule, B4’s six-hour definition and delegation limit, and ECD-2’s “critical sterilization-line lots” scope never become rules. The pseudo-logic invents a “CFO email within 1 hour of ECD-2 start” condition that does not exist (the email is the next morning) and treats C2 as a waiver of Emergency_Authorize and of Compliance.

**Case work is the fatal section.** Classifications often do not follow from the “controlling interpretation,” and several rows contaminate one case with another case’s timestamps.

- **P-501:** Commitment is 08:50; ECD-2 starts at 09:00. ON-44 makes ERP stamps late, not early, so the real act is even further before the directive. Treating C1 as controlling is a basic timing error. SHIFT-1 committed with no delegation while the Plant Manager was reachable (B4), and Plant/Finance approvals are after commitment (A6). The row still lists C1 as satisfied and then calls the same conduct NONCOMPLIANT because C1 “explicitly allows this.” That is not an assessment.
- **P-502:** Finance_Approve at 13:05 is on the record; the row says there is no Finance approval. Compliance at 18:30 is after commitment on a new-supplier purchase (A3 + A6). The CFO email does not yet exist. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is not supported on a disciplined reading.
- **P-503:** NONCOMPLIANT is the right label, reached for the wrong reasons. The 12:55 recording is P-502’s fact. The actual defects are SoD (PROC-3 is requester, committer, and one authorizer), no Compliance on EUR 140,000, no Finance, and a serious C6 scope problem (backup line, three days of stock).
- **P-504:** The label is right; the rule columns are not. R2 is listed as both satisfied and breached. The purchase is outside ECD-2’s stated scope. The live violations are unapproved supplier without Compliance, commit-before-PO without a B4 emergency, and a fabricated “site continuity” emergency.
- **P-505:** COMPLIANT with high confidence cannot be reconciled with “ECD-2 expired” and “Finance after expiry.” The decisive contemporaneous evidence is the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment, plus a possibly fast ERP clock. That evidence is ignored. R9 is misused. Create_PO and Plant_Approve are absent and undiscussed.
- **P-506:** NONCOMPLIANT is right, but the controlling line treats an unrecorded overnight extension as if it were a fact. Plant_Approve after commit and Create_PO after commit are not analyzed. The table row is also malformed.
- **P-507:** COMPLIANT overstates a file with no Finance event. The cited “Legal clearance (16:00)” belongs to P-508. The signed PDF at 12:40 is the actual four-hour-record issue and is not used. Eight hours versus B4’s six-hour emergency definition is ignored.
- **P-508:** SoD is inverted. BUY-8 is requester/committer and is *not* an Incident Command authorizer; that is the A1-compliant pattern. Compliance and Legal are timely. The live question is whether D1/D2 can stand in for Finance/Plant above EUR 150,000. Calling this NONCOMPLIANT because the buyer is not the Incident Commander is the opposite of the rule.

**Alternatives mostly fail the brief.** The task is a premise change that would *switch* the primary label. P-502’s alternative keeps JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. P-501’s alternative leans on a CFO email that is neither in force nor relevant to a EUR 38,000 approved-supplier buy. The interesting reversals — P-505’s 17:59 acknowledgment, P-503’s scope/SoD, P-507’s missing Finance, P-508’s D2 reading — are not the ones developed.

**Evidence and remediation do not make the required distinctions.** Policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and inability to prove compliance are not separated. Several remediations target the wrong case fact (P-502 already recorded authorization inside four hours; P-506 is an approved supplier; P-508 is omitted). Monitoring is generic.

**Section 7 recreates the conflict it is asked to close.** ECD is placed unconditionally above GPIP-4; an informal CFO email is written into the waiver path; CRO exceptions may be issued retrospectively. That last point is the opposite of a safeguard against emergency normalization. The four-hour documentation / 30-day CRO review idea is the only usable fragment.

The piece looks like a completed opinion and is not one. Directionally correct NONCOMPLIANT stamps on P-501/P-503/P-504/P-506 appear to be coincidences sitting on top of contradictory reasons. Under a strict standard, that is well below a competent independent analysis.
