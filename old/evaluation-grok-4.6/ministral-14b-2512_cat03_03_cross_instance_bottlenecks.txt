## What is actually solid

Section structure matches the spec. Machine capacity is correctly not ranked first. J1/J2 batch waits (3h 55m, 3h 40m) and the J1 packing-to-dispatch interval (13:30 → 19:25 = 5h 55m) are arithmetically correct. The piece does say lot events are inconsistently bound to jobs, and it does not treat machine-idle gaps as self-sufficient proof. Those are the main reasons this is not a 3–4.

That is not enough. The analysis is unreliable on the evidence it claims to decompose.

## Material factual errors

**FIX-A is assigned to the wrong jobs.** The edge list has `J1 → FIX-A (*holds*)`. The log never supports that. J3 holds FIX-A 09:00–11:40; J4’s `Machine Process` at 11:45 relates to J4, C4, CNC-2, and FIX-A. J1 is a B1/OVEN-1 job. This is not a slip in prose; it is in the compact graph the rest of the paper is supposed to rest on.

**Affected-object sets are systematically inflated.** Batch synchronization is written as hitting J1–J6, but B1 = {J1, J2, J3} and B2 = {J4, J5}. J6 is not batched. FIX-A contention is written as hitting J3–J6; only J3/J4 (and CNC-2/FIX-A) are in that episode. CAL-3 is written as if only J5/J6 require it; every `Final Test` requires CAL-3.

**Narrative 2 attaches the wrong machine to the lot.** CNC-2’s 10:05–10:47 idle is the J4/FIX-A window. J5 is the L7/C5/B2/OVEN-1/RACK-7 job. Linking L7 quarantine to CNC-2 idle is a crossed-wires causal story, not a cautious inference.

**L7 timing is inverted.** 10:20 is `Quarantine Lot`, not “quarantine end.” The 3h 30m “J5 lot wait” is just the quarantine interval. J5 is ready for B2 at 14:40, *after* `Release Lot` at 13:50. If readiness is trusted, lot release is not on J5’s critical path for batch start. The prompt specifically warned not to ignore inconsistent job–lot linking; the answer names the override for J2 and then invents a J5 delay the timestamps do not support.

**B2 / RACK-7 is over-attributed.** Rank 1 claims “B2 idle 1h 55m due to RACK-7 cleaning.” OVEN-1 is idle 12:55–14:50; RACK-7 is quarantined until 14:35; J4 is ready 13:10; J5 is ready 14:40; B2 starts 14:50. After 14:35 the binding constraint is J5, not the rack. The shared-rack motif (B1 then B2 on RACK-7) is the interesting coupling and is never cleanly stated.

## Quantitative section fails its own rules

Five calculations are present, but two of the five are the wrong counterfactual.

**CAL-3 / J6.** Ready 16:05, start 17:25 is 1h 20m of *total* wait. J5 occupies TEST-1/CAL-3 16:10–16:35. Even with no recalibration J6 cannot start at 16:05. The calibration-associated piece is 16:35–17:20 (plus the 17:20–17:25 lag), about 45–50 minutes. The answer treats the whole 1h 20m as a CAL-3 effect and repeats “J6 could test at 16:05” in Section 5. That mixes serial tester occupancy with tool recalibration.

**FIX-A / J4.** 11:45 − 09:50 = 1h 55m is an upper bound on time-from-arrival, not a identified FIX-A delay. J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; CNC-2 is idle only from 10:05; J3 releases FIX-A at 11:40; processing starts at 11:45. A careful bound would separate (a) unknown 09:50–10:05 activity, (b) CNC-2 idle while FIX-A is held, (c) the 5 minutes after release. Instead the text says tool unavailability is “the true blocker.” That violates both “state the counterfactual” and “do not present as causal proof,” and it equates succession plus an idle gap with diagnosis.

**S1 / J1.** 5h 55m is the packed-to-dispatch span. S2/S3 show 25–35 minutes of ordinary consolidation slack after the last packed job. The *excess* wait relative to that policy baseline is never computed. Ranking then puts this largest single wait fourth, with no justification for preferring smaller FIX-A and lot effects.

The B1 numbers are copied from the prompt’s “if readiness is trusted” column without asking whether J2’s 08:25 readiness survives a 10:20 quarantine and a supervisor override into B1. That is exactly the trust caveat the table handed them.

## Falsification

Six bullets exist, but several do not falsify the claimed mechanism. Uniform S1 waits would *contradict* consolidation (early jobs wait more by construction); the answer has this backwards. “J5 starts at 14:40 despite quarantine” is already what the data show after a 13:50 release. ## Strictness bottom line

This reads like a competent template filled with the prompt’s nouns, then allowed to drift off the timestamps. The coupling story is not “shared resources cause delays” in slogan form — good — but several of the specific couplings it asserts are the wrong objects, the wrong intervals, or both. That is disqualifying for a high score on a task whose only job is careful object-centric reading.
