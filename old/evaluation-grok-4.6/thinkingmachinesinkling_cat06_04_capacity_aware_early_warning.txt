**6.4**

The write-up is complete, hits the main traps (I-03 ineligible; I-05 suppressed vs I-07 re-evaluated; spillovers and calibration treated as first-class), and is more decision-theoretic than a `p_now` ranking. It is not close to flawless. Several internal contradictions would make the stated rule hard to deploy as written.

**What is solid.** Stage eligibility is a hard gate, so I-03 is correctly refused despite the highest risk and value. I-05 is suppressed as a refresh artifact; I-07 is allowed back in because of the new log, then rejected on value and remaining time—this is the distinction the prompt asks for. NEIV includes heterogeneous effects, swarm cost, and false-alarm burden rather than a single probability cutoff. The three scenarios are numerically auditable, and Adverse is allowed to go net-negative, including a near-zero effect on I-09. Fairness and monitoring sections propose concrete controls (equity floor, sign-off, holdback, queue-composition pause, trigger-based recalibration), not slogans.

**Rule vs. allocation inconsistency (largest flaw).** Section 1 defines Tier 1 as `NEIV_mid > €2,500` and only blocks on `NEIV_upper < 0`. I-08’s midpoint is about €4.5k—higher than I-02 and I-09—yet it is labeled Tier 2 and waited. The robustness story (negative lower bound, €5k disruption, Strategic overprediction, weak effect) is reasonable, but it is not in the deployable rule. A policy that can be overridden ad hoc is not the rule that was claimed.

**I-09 is treated inconsistently.** Non-Native cases are supposed to take a 0.75 confidence factor and/or the lower half of the effect range. The live table still quotes ~€3.6k (undiscounted midpoint) and Base uses `r = 0.24`. Under the stated discount, I-09 is closer to Tier 2 and is a weaker third slot than the write-up implies. Choosing the sparsest-calibration case as one of three active swarms, then showing that the same portfolio is net-negative in Adverse, needs an explicit robust-choice justification (why not I-04+I-02 only, or I-01/I-06 instead). “Reserve the fourth slot” does not hedge failure of one of the three chosen cases.

**`p_adj` does almost no work.** Segment offsets are specified, then ranking is almost entirely `r × V − C − F`. Calibration is used as narrative color (“true risk ~0.40”) rather than as an input to eligibility, effect capping (`r` cannot exceed `p_adj`), or a robust objective. That leaves unused the prompt’s point that `p` is observational and not the intervention probability. Related: false-alarm burden is subtracted in full in every scenario, as if every swarm incurs `F` with certainty. If `F` is the cost of a false alarm, expected cost should depend on how often the alarm is wrong; that is never stated.

**Operational and spillover looseness.** The rule cites a 3-case diagnosis pool, then spillover math uses the specified six untreated cases and an invented ~€22k average breach value with little sensitivity analysis. Two seniors, 45-minute swarms, and 8–20 (or 15–30) minutes of queue delay are barely binding on the choice of three simultaneous slots. The 90-minute / `|Δp| < 0.10` suppression rule, applied strictly, would also put I-02 and I-09 (Δp of 0.08 and 0.07) at risk of lockout; they are waved through as “valid” without reconciling that rule. The SME/Public equity floor is announced and then not used, and it is never reconciled with the regulatory reserve (two different claims on the fourth slot).

**Smaller issues.** Effect ranges are plugged into EV as if they were scenario-conditioned causal estimates; the text warns against that, the numbers do not. I-04 is selected without the “double-driver” check later required for high-`F` cases. The holdback design ignores power: a 25% draw from a thin NEIV band, at four slots a day, will not support the quarterly ATT updates described.

Headline judgments are right; the policy does not quite cohere as a single deployable rule under uncertainty. That is why this stays in the mid-6s rather than the 8–10 band.
