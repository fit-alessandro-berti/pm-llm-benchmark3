**6.5**

The write-up is complete, follows the required headings, and is stronger than a typical dump of “emergency therefore fine” or “any deviation is fatal.” The hierarchy is dimension-specific, A6 is applied at the act rather than at case close, contemporaneous artifacts are preferred to ERP stamps, and ECD-2 is not allowed to swallow GPIP-4 integrity controls by silence. Those are real strengths. They are not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

**The central methodological break.** Section 1 puts the burden on the actor and says missing proof yields NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE, not a pass. Sections 4 and 3 then let an uncontrolled CFO email (D1), read generously, carry **P-505** and **P-507** to JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION. That is not a small labeling quibble. A2 is conjunctive: independent budget owner *and* Finance Controller, both before commitment. The materials say Incident Command is not chartered as budget owner; D3 limits the CFO to Finance interpretation; D4 walks D1 back. Even a full-strength D1 therefore leaves the budget-owner prong unproved. Classifying those files as justified exceptions treats a contested, extra-repository gloss as if it were an A7-quality waiver. INDETERMINATE (or NONCOMPLIANT with D1 as the alternative) would have matched the method the memo itself announced.

**Timing error on D.** D is sent 2026-07-15 07:20. **P-502** is almost certainly 14 July: same-day filters on the affected line, commit 10:15, and a telephone recording offered exactly in the ON-44 pattern (outage 08:30–13:00). The table still treats D1 as a possible A2 satisfaction. Classification stays NONCOMPLIANT because A3 is independently late, so the verdict survives, but the supporting rule application is anachronistic. Rules were not applied only as they stood at the act.

**Over-reading several triggers.**
- **P-503 / A1:** PROC-3 is one of two joint authorizers, not clearly “the” final business approver. Joint IC authorization can be read as leaving IC-1 as the independent check. The memo states an A1 breach too flatly (the alternative in §5 is better than the primary row).
- **P-504 / A3:** “Unapproved” is not the same predicate as “new supplier,” and SOP B’s scope is maintenance/production-support, which a cafeteria freezer may not be. NONCOMPLIANT is still the right headline (ECD-2 does not cover food service; B3 if B applies), but the A3 path is asserted rather than argued.
- **B2** is written as a presence requirement, not as A2’s explicit pre-commitment clock. Treating late Plant/Finance on **P-501** as a B2 breach is a purpose inference, presented as if it were textual.

**Classification set is thinner than the ambiguity the materials planted.** C6, A7, D4, the 9-minute clock, and the budget-owner gap are all designed to support INDETERMINATE on at least one file. The memo never uses it. Confidence adjectives are not a substitute when the official palette includes that class and the facts are incomplete by design.

**Smaller defects that still count.** Section 1 says D1 substitutes for “Finance/budget”; the hierarchy table then says budget-owner substitution is uncertain—same memo, two rules. “A3 n/a” appears among satisfied rules. JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is used for a CFO interpretation, not a CRO exception, which blurs the catalogue’s own exception path. **P-507** has *no* Finance event at all; calling that a justified exception, rather than a logging-or-proof failure, collapses the §6 distinctions the memo otherwise draws well.

What remains genuinely good: **P-501** (pre-09:00 act, failed B4 delegation), **P-504** scope, **P-506** expiry, **P-508** (D1 cap + Legal does not eat Finance), the P-505 supplier-ack vs ERP treatment, and the §7 clause (CRO ratification, 24-hour lot definition, no oral extensions, anti-normalization trigger). Those pieces are defensible. The grade is held down because the two JE calls, the D1 timing slip, the A2 budget-owner under-enforcement, and the unused INDETERMINATE class are not nits—they are the exact failure modes the prompt told the analyst not to commit.
