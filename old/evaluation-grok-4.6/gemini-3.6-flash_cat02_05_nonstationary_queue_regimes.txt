The write-up is complete, well structured, and often operationally sharp. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has several factual errors, a few conceptual slips the author had already warned against, and internal contradictions between the prose, the figures, and the code.

The eight required headings are present and used for the right work. Estimands are defined with units and an opening-hours convention. The threat list is better than the minimum of five: overnight carry-over, service vs flow time, the mix shift, outage catch-up, burstiness, and aggregation bias are all real issues in this table.

Regime-level arrival, completion, median/p90 service, WIP, and FTE figures for R1–R6 check against the daily table. Outage recovery is not treated as a new steady-state productivity regime. ## Conceptual and logical flaws

**Median used as mean after forbidding that substitution.** Capacity math in Sections 4 and 7 uses \(114 \times 35 = 66.5\) touch-hours and an 80% × 72 h = 57.6 h supply. The prompt gives median and p90, not mean service. With p90 ≈ 83 vs median 35 the distribution is right-skewed, so mean touch time is **above** 35 and 66.5 h is a lower bound, not a workload. The 80% utilization is also an unlabeled assumption.

**Invalid wait-time residual.** For R1 the memo writes \(W_q \approx 146 - 30 = 116\). Little’s Law, when it applies, returns a **mean** sojourn. Subtracting a median (and not even R1’s median of 25) is the exact error Section 1 told the reader not to make.

**Mean WIP treated as closing inventory.** Daily `mean_wip` is an open-hours snapshot average, not end-of-day backlog. The 07:00 stock is not observed. Net flow of +13.4 cases/day on R4 weekdays is a fair **flow** statement; reading 37 → 50 as a continuous inventory path, and writing \(L_{19:00}>0\) as a fact, is not. The Section 7 cartoon that labels day 9 as both WIP = 50 and “+120 cases backlog” contradicts the table and the surrounding text.

**Little’s Law verdicts are a notch too confident.** R1/R2 are the only plausible windows, but unknown opening backlog, discrete snapshots, overnight closure, and \(n=7\) / \(n=4\) make **`applicable` too strong**; **`approximate only`** is the honest label. The identity \(L=\lambda W\) still has a windowed accounting reading; what fails in R4 is the **stationary sojourn** interpretation, not “Little’s Law” as such.

**`complex_pct` is treated as proven work-content change.** The prompt allows a real mix shift, a coding change, or both. Service times move with the flag, which is evidence, but the memo repeatedly calls it a 40% increase in “base work content” without keeping the coding hypothesis alive.

## Smaller but real issues

- Snapshot grid “\(N=48\), 07:15–19:00” is invented and not marked as an assumption.
- The CUSUM helper computes a CUSUM and then ignores it; it is a jump/threshold rule mislabeled as CUSUM.
- Representative hourly profiles disagree with `peak_08_10_pct` on the same dates (04-07: 08:00–09:59 is 17/101 ≈ 17%, table says 31). That is a data-audit miss.
- Hourly completions on 04-21 are much smoother than arrivals; that queue-buffering point is barely used.
- Lag-1 autocorrelations mix weekly seasonality, the 20 April step, and true carry-over; only arrivals get that caveat.
- Projecting “>150 cases” / “TAT > 12 hours” from a median-based capacity gap overstates what the 28-day series shows (WIP peaks near 50).

## Format and uncertainty

The required skeleton is followed. Observations, estimates, and recommendations are often separated. They are not separated where it matters most: snapshot construction, 80% utilization, \(L_{19:00}\), and the mix-shift interpretation. Precision is inconsistent: regime cells are careful, pooled cells and the backlog cartoon are not.

## Scoring

This is a competent process-mining memo with a serious quantitative hole in the one section that must show pooling is misleading, plus reuse of median service as if it were mean work content. On a lenient rubric it might sit in the high 7s. Against a nearly-flawless bar, those defects are enough to keep it well below 8.
