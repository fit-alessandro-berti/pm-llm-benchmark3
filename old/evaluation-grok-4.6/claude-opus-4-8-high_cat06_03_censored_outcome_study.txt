6.5

The design gets the core identification point right: 90-day default is defined only after activation, the 21,600-row export is a selected sample, and rejected/withdrawn/open cases are not recoded as non-default. That is necessary, not sufficient. Under a strict reading the write-up has enough factual errors, as-of holes, and design–code contradictions that it is not nearly flawless.

**Target.** The activation-conditional estimand is defensible and the competing-risk and counterfactual alternatives are correctly distinguished. Two unclarities remain. First, “applications we would activate” is causal language for an observational quantity, \(P(Y\mid X, A=1)\). Second, the business question is a score on every application *before* the decision, but the primary model is only identified on cases that later activate. The limitation is stated; the operational story (“gate or price accepted applications” at a pre-decision cut) is still slightly incoherent. Survival is described both as a refinement of the primary target and as part of the primary analysis, so it is never fully clear which estimand is locked.

**Cohort.** The 48,000-row reconciliation is correct and the 12.5% rate is correctly scoped. Then the text misstates \(21{,}600/(21{,}600+3{,}600)=85.7\%\) as “88% of *activated-matured* accounts.” The labeled export *is* the activated-matured set (100%); 21,600 is 85.7% of *activated* accounts. Permanently missing partner feeds are never given a row in the flow table, so they are silently absorbed into the 21,600 or the 3,600. That matters because MNAR missingness among activated accounts biases even the primary estimand, not only a coverage footnote.

**Censoring.** The five mechanisms are named. Lumping the 5,100 still-open cases with the 3,600 immature activations as “ordinary right censoring” of default is wrong: open cases never entered the post-activation risk set, so default-after-activation is undefined, not censored. The later primary-analysis paragraph treats only the 3,600 as survival-censored, which is internally inconsistent. Heckman is a poor default for a binary outcome (bivariate probit / IPW would be the honest analogue). IPW is listed as sensitivity without positivity/overlap, which the July 2025 policy change likely violates. Non-informative censoring is not tested by “censoring times independent of risk features.”

**Prefixes and features.** Fixed-count vs elapsed-time, short cases, loops, and the October rename are all discussed. Implementation does not match: `k` is not placed relative to first Credit Review; elapsed-time prefixes, `is_short` padding, and document-loop cuts are comments only. `risk_score` as-of is specified as the *earliest* pre-decision value; a valid as-of value is the *last* observation at or before the cut. `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is a period-grouped log aggregate, not case-level start→KYC timing. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is not justified on a log with no start/complete lifecycle. Both are the kind of unjustified PM4Py use the prompt warned against.

**Leakage table.** The required fields are all present, and drop/repair calls on `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, and labels are correct. `documents_total` is to be rebuilt from upload timestamps that are not in the given schema. Resource experience is “repair, else drop” with no repair in the skeleton.

**Validation.** The argument against a random split is sound. Section 7 then promises calendar folds, a regime-pure test, and rolling as-of cutoffs; section 9 implements 60/20/20 quantiles of *labeled* start times with a single global cutoff of 15 Feb 2026. Labeled late starters are speed-selected (they must activate and mature before extract), so the “test” tail is not a realistic 2025–26 production cohort. Maturity is also over-constrained: requiring *both* `label_available_at <= cutoff` *and* `activation+90+35 <= cutoff` discards cases whose feed already arrived inside the 7–35 day window.

**Code (concrete failures, not missing polish).**
- Events and terminal states are never filtered to `as_of_cutoff`. A backtest cutoff would classify later activations as `activated_not_mature` rather than open — violating the as-of rule the design itself states.
- `POST_DECISION_ACTS` omits Rejected / Withdrawn / Transferred, so competing terminal events can sit in prefixes.
- `default_90d` is `dropna().unique()[0]`, which is arbitrary if the flag updates from 0 to 1.
- `get_prefixes_from_log` is applied to a DataFrame with no `convert_to_event_log`; depending on version it may rewrite case IDs or drop short traces, breaking the merge. Short cases, promised in §4, are not kept.
- DFG/variants/temporal objects are computed and then ignored; `documents_total` repair is a comment.
- Leakage asserts are useful but only cover what the truncated frame still contains.

**What stays creditworthy.** No fabricated non-defaults; competing outcomes kept as competing; leakage audit mostly right; random split rejected; survival hooked to lifelines/scikit-survival rather than a fake PM4Py API; deployment feedback (selection, gaming, policy coupling) is real. Those are the difference between a failing design and a middling-good one. They do not cancel the arithmetic error, the still-open/censoring mix-up, the first-vs-last score bug, the unimplemented as-of filter, or the validation story that the skeleton does not actually run.
