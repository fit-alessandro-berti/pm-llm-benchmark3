5.7

The case table is the strongest part of the answer: it does not treat same-user as automatic failure or distinct-user as automatic success, and the AP-204 / AP-209 / AP-205 / AP-203 / AP-207 distinctions show real control-objective reasoning. That is not enough. Under a strict reading of the required analysis, the submission has material specification misses, internal contradictions, and a code block that would not do the job it was asked to do.

**Section 1 is incomplete against the prompt.** Five threat scenarios are named, but ED-1 is not mapped onto each of them as prevent / detect / correct / fail-to-address. AV-2 is wrongly recruited against “initiation–authorization convergence”: a clean AV-2 pass does not test actor identity, shared credentials, or same-person prepare/release. Important FEP-1-adjacent threats are missing or collapsed: compromised or borrowed accounts, rubber-stamped MG-4, goods-not-received, and emergency-token abuse. “Recovery failure” is a timing property of other controls, not a distinct FEP-1 threat.

**Section 2 never operationalizes the four-way equivalence scale it was asked to define** (fully equivalent / partially equivalent / merely mitigating / ineffective). Timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, and evidence quality appear as a list, not as a test that would classify a package. Collusion resistance is required in the test and is only implied. The expected-loss versus integrity point is sound.

**Section 3 has the right labels on the hard cases and the wrong mechanics on several rows.**

- AP-201 vs AP-207 is internally inconsistent. Both have independent MG-4 after release and before settlement; AP-201 parks MG-4 under detective controls, AP-207 under preventive. Corrective capacity for AP-201 is overstated as “High (Recall possible)” while audit is ~25 hours after release. “Token logs for emergency claim” is the wrong missing evidence: the file already says there was no emergency.
- AP-202 is correctly OBJECTIVE_FAILED, but listing “AV-2 override” as a preventive control does exactly what the prompt forbids: treating occurrence as effectiveness. The override was the same operator using a manager credential.
- AP-204 OBJECTIVE_FAILED is defensible because of the fabricated receipt, not because pairing “confirmed collusion.” Residual risk as Medium with Severity High understates realized fraud. Organizational-design uncertainty is acknowledged, which is good.
- AP-205 is correctly LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION.
- AP-206 OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET is a reasonable reading of a late token plus otherwise strong pre-release MG-4. It is not a full ED-1 pass.
- AP-208 is correctly OBJECTIVE_FAILED, but the rationale is too narrow. ED-1 also fails on amount (EUR 900,000), bank-account change inside seven days, AV-2 down, post-settlement ratification, and late audit. Listing “AV-2 unavailable” as a preventive control is another occurrence-as-control error. The payment’s later legitimacy does not restore the control objective; the answer mostly gets this, then undermines it in section 5.
- AP-209 INDETERMINATE is the right non-mechanical call.

**Section 4’s hybrid semantics for post-release, pre-settlement MG-4 are the best conceptual paragraph in the paper**, and then are not applied consistently. The AV-2 “failure condition: Blocked” is backwards: a block is control success. The table is a taxonomy, not a comparison of the nine packages.

**Section 5 is thin and sometimes misstates the loss mechanism.** AP-203’s mechanism is not “minimal.” AP-206’s mechanism is not “token after prepare.” AP-208 was a legitimate payment; the plausible counterfactual is undetected diversion to a recently changed account while AV-2 was down, not “unauthorized payment to a new vendor.” AV-2 had already warned on AP-202’s bank change, so “independent releaser would catch the bank change” underspecifies the actual break (stolen override path plus conflicted, late MG-4). No qualitative residual-risk ranges are given.

**Section 6 lists the requested monitors and distinguishes alert from proof**, then proposes flagging *any* MG-4 after Release_Payment, which would alert the same ED-1 pattern the case analysis treats as acceptable (AP-207). The 1% override threshold is invented.

**Section 7 fails most of its own checklist.** The import `pm4py.objects.conformance.dev.filter` is not the required `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` API. Violating cases are never emitted. There is no separate identification of blocked attempts (AP-205). The facts frame is not joined to the log. Bank-change flags are absent. Repeated-pair detection cannot fire because `u12`/`u13` are not in `facts_df` rows. AP-204 `audit_hours` is 4 rather than ~94 hours (days recorded as hours), so the >24h audit flag would miss a late audit. The fallback `len(events) < 6` is unrelated to four-eyes. Version uncertainty is not explained (in particular whether the filter returns violators or the complement, DataFrame vs EventLog). The “evidence not verdict” label is present; the executable evidence pipeline is not.

The intellectual core of the review is above average. The required structure, consistency of preventive/detective labeling, ED-1 condition coverage, and especially the PM4Py artifact are not. That caps the score well below a high band.
