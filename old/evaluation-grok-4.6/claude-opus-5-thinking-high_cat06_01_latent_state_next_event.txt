**6.3**

The answer is process-aware and far above a DFG-copy baseline, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has repeated calibration failures, a few genuine semantic errors, and several places where rhetoric outruns the evidence.

**What is actually strong.** Control-flow obligations, object/lot context, queues, and feed delay are used as first-class inputs rather than decoration. C6-A vs C6-B is the right contrast: same prefix, opposite obligation sets, opposite exit eligibility, opposite queue priority. C6-C correctly lets rule 4 override the more frequent `Technical Investigation Completed → Disposition Recorded` arc. C6-D treats the worklist snapshot and the orphan attachment as observation-model evidence, not as proof the tasks never happened. C6-E holds S3 and a latent rule-5 supplier gate at the same time and refuses a cheap low-severity close. The DFG critique (marginal not conditional, observational not normative, missing legitimate arcs, concurrency artefacts) is accurate. Residual mass is left un-normalised on purpose. That is the right uncertainty discipline in principle.

**The decisive flaw is fake case-specific calibration.** Rank-1 for C6-A, C6-B, C6-C, and C6-E is the same band, `0.55–0.75`. Those situations are not interchangeable.

- C6-A has been in the review queue ~1h50m, high severity, likely expedited.
- C6-B has a similar wait but is the natural item to deprioritise; the text even says so, then refuses to lower the interval.
- C6-E entered the queue at 13:20. At 14:00 it has ~40 minutes against a 1–5 h post-entry window and is low severity. Reusing A’s interval is unjustified.
- C6-C is the worst reuse. Regulatory Assessment has **12 waiting**, service **2–14 h after assignment**, and **no evidence it is assigned**. Medium / no injury is a weak human trigger. The write-up itself says the event may miss today — then assigns the same high band used for a short evidence-review step. That is an internal contradiction, not honest uncertainty.

The cross-case claim that “sharing a rank-1 label is not sharing a forecast” is therefore only half-true: the labels differ later, but the headline probabilities do not.

**C6-C is overconfident in the legal reading as well as the numbers.** “Repeated malfunction *can* be reportable” is a possible signal, not a recorded determination. Calling RA “unavoidable” and the outstanding obligation “unambiguous” overstates rule 4. The alternative S5 reading is real; it should have widened and lowered the RA interval, not sat in a footnote while rank 1 stayed at 0.55–0.75.

**State labels are wrong for A and B.** After `Evidence Received`, the case is **S3 EVIDENCE_REVIEW**, not an “S2→S3 transition.” S2 is defined as request without receipt. The operational story is fine; the abstraction is not. That is a direct miss on the supplied state machine.

**C6-A over-fires rule 5.** Rule 5 needs two or more related *complaints* on the same lot/supplier/component. C6-A is one complaint event linked to two devices, plus three “similar” complaints of unspecified lot. Treating Supplier Analysis as mandatory in `open_or_suspected_obligations` is an overclaim; the unknowns section later admits the link is unproven. The obligation list and the unknown cannot both be right.

**C6-D rank 2 violates the output contract.** “Delayed backfill of a missing evidence-cycle event (Customer Evidence Requested, Evidence Received or Evidence Review Completed)” is three symbols collapsed into one rank. The task asked for a next logged activity, not a delay class. Marking that class `process_enabled: true` also blurs the distinction the answer otherwise lectures about: a late historical completion is an observation-channel event, not an enabled action now. Listing a *probable* evidence cycle under `completed_obligations` is the same error in the other direction — inferred, not logged, and the attachment may have arrived with the original complaint.

**`process_enabled` is applied inconsistently.** Fast Close after an evidence cycle (B) and Disposition before RA (C, D) are reasonably marked false. TI/RA completions on A are marked true without a start event. A third evidence request on E is correctly false. The boolean is doing three different jobs (normative enablement, possible early fork, delayed observation) without a stable rule.

**Smaller but real issues.** Fast Close is constrained by rule 2 on *attributes*, not only by an S1 exit; calling it non-enabled is a bit more rigid than the text supports. C6-C’s rank-3 re-request after a completed review and a finished technical investigation is a thin DFG path. Residual “~0.10–0.15” boilerplate is copy-pasted. Rank-1 wording is templated. None of these would matter if the probability model were actually case-conditioned.

**Net.** This is a strong process essay wearing a prediction JSON. The reasoning about obligations, objects, and the log-as-sensor is well above average. The scored objects — states, enablement flags, and especially the intervals — are less careful than the prose. Identical 0.55–0.75 bands, the C6-C contradiction, the S2/S3 mislabel, the rule-5 overclaim, and the collapsed C6-D prediction are enough to keep it well out of the top band.
