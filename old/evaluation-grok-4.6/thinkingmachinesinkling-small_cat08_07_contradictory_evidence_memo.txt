**Format and presentation defects.** The executive memo almost certainly exceeds the 700-word cap; it restates the later analytical sections instead of staying decision-short. Directional arrows are dropped throughout (`14.2  10.8`, `0.87  0.94`), and minus signs disappear in both the memo and the evidence table (simple-house “median 6.0d, p90 8.0d, complaints 1pp”). **Factual and attribution slips.** The bottom line cites “82% first-time-right in baseline” as if it characterized the simple-house cohort; 82% is the overall baseline, not that segment. Apartments improved on *median* duration (−2.0 days) and only deteriorated on tail, FTR, and complaints; the memo treats the non-simple 66% as a uniform failure mass and credits the median win almost entirely to simple houses. That oversimplifies the distributional story the prompt asked to be explained. SLA improvement for simple houses and NPS (+21 → +17) are barely used. “Zero conformance violations” is an unrealistic decision threshold given log noise and incomplete attributes.

**Causal overconfidence.** The write-up treats coordinator ownership + merged scheduling as the proven good parts and skip/post-billing as the proven bad parts. The pilot never isolated those components. Apartments’ worse tail is consistent with skip and missing permits, but it does not show that coordinator ownership is unsafe for apartments. Alternative C (coordinator everywhere, skip and post-billing off) plus permit enforcement could preserve apartment/business/rural *median* gains while cutting the new failure modes. The answer notes C only as a weaker intermediate and then locks onto E with more certainty than the identification strategy supports.

**Table hygiene.** Several rows blur classes (“Fact / Data limitation,” “Interpretation / Assumption”). Item 13’s cost-artifact claim is an assumption stated with interpretive force. That is a direct hit on “do not merge facts, interpretations, and assumptions.”

**Net.** Strong consulting synthesis with the right headline decision and a real VoI section, undermined by a long memo, broken signs, a misapplied 82% figure, underplayed apartment median gains, and tighter causal claims than the quasi-experiment can bear. Not a weak answer; also not nearly flawless.
