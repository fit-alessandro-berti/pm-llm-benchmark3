### Hard spec violations
Section 3’s justification is ~95 words against a required 150–250. Section 8 is ~170 against 250–400. These are not trim summaries; they omit material the prompt asked for (why never-treated remain valid given heterogeneous May/fraud-desk shocks; why Delta rather than Epsilon is the higher-value next unit; how n=1 phase-in can identify anything).

Causal assumptions are not written as comments.

### Quantitative errors (the most serious problem)
Section 5 is the core of the assessment and is not reliable.

- Stated windows are not the windows implied by the arithmetic, and they are not applied consistently across branches or outcomes.
- Alpha median is reported as **+0.9 h** with a parenthetical that itself shows 49.4 → ~48.8, i.e. a *decline*. March vs May–Aug is about **−0.9 h**, not +0.9. That is a sign error on a primary outcome.
- Gamma is reported as **−4.9 h** for “Jun vs Aug.” The raw Jun→Aug change is **45.9 → 36.0 (−9.9 h)**. Rework is −2.0 pp, not −0.7; p90 is −18.3 h, not −13.3 if the stated window is honored.
- Never-treated “Aug vs Apr–Jun” figures (−1.3 / −3.3) do not match that window (closer to −2.3 / −5.2). Using the *same* Jun-vs-Aug window as claimed for Gamma, Epsilon falls **49.4 → 40.8 (−8.6 h)**—almost Gamma’s move. The implied DiD is about **−1 h vs Epsilon**, not a clean late-adopter success. This is the comparison that should have disciplined the whole write-up; it is understated.
- “All five branches show rising medians and p90s from Jan–Mar” is false. Alpha, Delta, and Epsilon dip in February. The common March escalation jump is +2.0 pp in *every* branch (fraud desk), which the pre-trend paragraph does not isolate.

Sensitivity analysis that “reverses Gamma” is then built on a compressed Gamma contrast and an understated control recovery. Approximate arithmetic was allowed; internally inconsistent windows and a sign error were not.

### Estimands and identification
The table hits the required families, and “partially / no” identification calls are directionally sensible. Presentation is sloppy (`automation=01`, `025 pp`), LATE is named without an instrument, and population/unit clash (case-level LATE, branch-month data). CSAT-ITT as wholly unidentifiable is too binary; the data identify a contaminated aggregate association.

The design menu (event study with never-treated, synthetic control, ITS) is appropriate, and preferring event-study over a two-donor synthetic control is right. The write-up still underplays the binding constraints: Alpha/Beta were *selected on the outcome*; never-treated are not exchangeable on volume/complexity; SUTVA is already violated by the shared fraud desk; Gamma has two post months; five clusters make pre-trend tests theater. “Conditional on observables, parallel trends” is asserted more cleanly than the design can support.

### Mechanisms, threats, effects
Section 4 is the strongest part: substitution vs. congestion, learning, mix, staffing, tails, survey selection. Even here, rework is summarized as “rose slightly in Alpha” while April shows a dip then rebound, and Beta/Gamma are not described. Staffing is one-sided (Alpha/Gamma losses; Beta and Delta *gains* are ignored in §1 and §6).

The threat table covers the required dozen items. Several bias directions are too tidy: binary availability is a different estimand, not merely an attenuated intensity effect; fraud-desk interference is not uniformly “downward”; anticipation as upward bias is unexplained. Missing: post-treatment controls as colliders, routing as a direct co-intervention, treatment-version (different local practices).

Section 7’s qualitative caution is mostly earned—*not identified* for median and CSAT is the correct leadership message—but escalation “+2 pp” partly reflects the common March desk shock, and p90 “possible worsening” does not confront the same May spike and later recovery in never-treated branches. Confidence integers are decorative.

### Rollout
`expand with an embedded evaluation` is the only defensible box. The design is thin for 250–400 words of policy: Delta-only is the *safer* operational choice and the *worse* learning choice (low complexity, historically healthy; Epsilon is closer to the problem population). A one-branch randomized phase-in cannot deliver a 90% CI that excludes +2 h. The OR-rule (“or uptake >35% with no p90 deterioration”) allows scale-up on fidelity alone. Case-level or within-branch randomization, queue/fraud-desk instrumentation, and a real stop-rule for tail harm are underdeveloped.

### Claim ladder
The four-rung format is correct and the bottom rung is the right headline. The “Observed” escalation claim over-attributes a partly common shock. The “Suggestive” tail claim asserts complexity control that §5 does not actually show. “Conditionally causal: −3 to +4 h” is a reasonable interval only as a *scenario bound*, not as something the identification strategy identified.

**Net:** conceptually literate, executably careless. A process-mining lead would not take the §5 numbers or the Python artifact to a steering committee. That caps the score well below the “nearly flawless” band.
