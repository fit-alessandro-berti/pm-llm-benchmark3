**7.3**

The answer is a strong process-mining portfolio memo: it refuses frequency-first ranking, keeps humans and controls in the loop, and grounds most claims in the table, DFG, resource network, and control findings. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has material structural gaps, one overclaim on controls, and several process-level omissions that a bank would notice.

**What holds up.** The matrix is the right kind of ranking. A1 is correctly demoted despite ~12k volume: 8 minutes of work, 1.5h wait, and back-office utilization already below 65% after 15:00. A4 is correctly treated as high-leverage despite 1,750 cases (31h wait, 38% rework, critical path in 82% of occurrences). A5 before A6 is the right prerequisite pair for the `Financial document review → Request missing documents → Financial document review` loop (3,300 / 2,800). A11 and unsupervised A8 are correctly blocked on upstream readiness. The two deep integrations are spent on A4 case-state and A9 decision-to-contract, with A5 on the existing portal and A6 in a workbench — that is a real reading of the six-month IT constraint. Exception queues, specialist concentration on two seniors, and analyst overload after cleaner inflow are treated as first-class design constraints, not footnotes.

**Structural failure relative to the required shape.** Section 3 defines three portfolios; section 4 analyzes only those three; section 7 recommends a fourth set (A5, A6, A4, A9) that is never given its own effects analysis. That is not a clever hybrid so much as a missing object. The recommended mix is bottleneck minus A7 plus A9. The process-level consequences of that swap — losing the 58h external join, gaining contract-remediation and a control module — are asserted in the finale rather than compared on cycle time, waits, rework, handovers, demand, and violations. The reader cannot score the chosen portfolio on the same grid as the discarded ones.

**The A6-over-A7 choice is under-argued given the problem’s own thesis.** The prompt says lower-frequency synchronization delays can dominate end-to-end performance. `Wait for external valuation` is the longest wait in the log (58h, 19% late, required in 71% of Equipment finance). A7 is ranked 5th with “high” process-level benefit, then dropped from year 1 with a partner-readiness shrug. A6 (20h wait, 75 min analyst work, internal) is a defensible *controllability* pick, but the answer never states the product-mix condition under which that pick fails: if Equipment finance is material, year-1 median/tail time can remain dominated by an untouched external join even if A4/A5/A6 all work. That is exactly the mistake senior management was warned about, inverted onto “internal feasibility.”

**Control overclaim.** The compliance-first write-up says it is “the strongest portfolio for reducing … four-eyes violations.” A3, A4, A9, and A12 do not implement the stated four-eyes rule (Credit analysis vs Committee packaging, High-exposure SME, 5.8% month-end). A12 could *record* a violation; none of the four *prevent* the same person completing both steps. The recommended portfolio has the same hole and treats four-eyes as a residual workflow property. Month-end legal overload and the 5.8% rush pattern are described, then not turned into a gate (freeze High-exposure dual-role assignment, cap month-end WIP, independent packager rota).

**Variant and cadence thinness.** The log is built on Micro / SME / High-exposure and Working-capital / Equipment / Credit-line renewal. Section 4 rarely conditions effects on those variants. Committee is 3,900 cases against 8,900 credit analyses; High-exposure has a weekly cadence that no packaging or contract bot removes. That cadence is almost unused, so “committee packaging wait” is treated as if it were mostly an assembly problem. Classification error (8%, “misroute cases”) determines collateral, KYC intensity, and committee path; A2 is ranked 10th and appears in no portfolio, including no conservative recommender-with-confirmation hedge. That is a process-level blind spot, not prudent de-risking.

**Smaller defects that still matter.**
- Phase 0 “weeks 0–8” overlaps Phase 1 “months 2–4.”
- Quick-win puts A10 beside A1 without A3/A4, then warns that signing automation is unsafe without KYC gates — the portfolio is defined as attractive and unsafe at once.
- “Register application occurs in 98% of cases” collapses a *position* statistic with a *frequency* statistic (11,880 / 12,000).
- Several effect sentences stay at the level of “gain will be less than the sum of local gains.” True, but not process-specific.
- Unused or underused artifacts: Receive already 72% digital; valuation 19% late as an explicit KPI; RM-triggered incomplete instructions as a change-management design, not only a checklist feature.

**Net.** Portfolio reasoning and dependency writing are well above a generic automation list. The answer still fails a strict process-level test in three places: it does not analyze the portfolio it recommends, it underweights the dominant external synchronization point it itself ranked highly, and it claims four-eyes improvement it does not instrument. Those are not polish nits. They keep the score well short of the near-flawless bar.
