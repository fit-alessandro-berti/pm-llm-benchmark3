The answer has the required architecture and is often process-aware, but it is not internally consistent and it misclassifies at least two cases relative to its own hierarchy. Under a strict reading that is a core failure, not a polish issue.

**What holds up.** Section 1 correctly refuses a single total order, treats GPIP-4 A4/A6/A7 as sticky, and does not let a later ERP stamp defeat contemporaneous supplier evidence. The hierarchy table is dimension-specific in the way the prompt asked. P-504 and P-506 are clean. P-503’s segregation finding (PROC-3 as requester, committer, and authorizer) is sharp. **The decisive flaw is a broken Finance/A2 theory.** The write-up says ECD-2 and the CFO email probably cannot override GPIP-4 A2, then applies three different rules in the cases:

- P-502: late Finance is a deviation → NONCOMPLIANT, high confidence  
- P-505: D1 waives operational Finance → **COMPLIANT**  
- P-507: same D1/A2 clash → INDETERMINATE  

That is not residual uncertainty. It is the same clause being decided three ways. A judge scoring consistency has to mark this down hard.

**P-505 COMPLIANT is the worst primary call.** On the answer’s own premises, A2 still requires an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller *before* commitment; IC members are not designated budget owners; D3/D4 and A4 leave integrity controls in place; Finance at 19:00 is post-commitment; A6 says later approval does not cure. The supplier ack can save *timing relative to ECD-2 expiry*. It cannot manufacture a missing pre-commitment Finance/budget-owner act. The honest labels are NONCOMPLIANT or INDETERMINATE. Listing GPIP-A3 as “satisfied” is also sloppy: 88k, approved supplier, A3 is not triggered.

**P-501 JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is too kind, and Section 5 quietly admits it.** B4 allows commit-before-PO in a four-hour stoppage. The stronger reading is the one parked as an “alternative.”
