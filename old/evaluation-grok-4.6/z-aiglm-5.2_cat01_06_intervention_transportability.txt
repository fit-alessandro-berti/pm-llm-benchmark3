**6.2**

The final answer is structurally complete and, on causal transportability, clearly above average. It still falls well short of “nearly flawless.” Several quantitative and model-fidelity errors are serious enough that a strict reading cannot go much higher.

**What is actually strong.** The piece correctly refuses to treat Site A’s 9-hour median drop as an identified treatment effect and keeps the co-interventions (mix shift, added FTE, API upgrade, huddles, timestamp cleanup, no negative control) in view throughout. It does not copy “create both tasks after Register for every case.” Statutory originals-before-document and legal-review constraints stay in place. Bottleneck shift, case lock, role separation, and supplement-loop interference are treated as effect modifiers rather than afterthoughts. The recommendation (`conditional go`, start with digital non-legal cases) matches the process structure. Sections 1, 3, and 4 are the best parts: required columns are used, the mandatory features appear, and the transfer table is appropriately pessimistic.

**Format and internal consistency.** Section 2’s prose overshoots the 180–280 word cap (roughly 340+). Section 9 overshoots 250–400 (roughly 500). That is not cosmetic when the spec is explicit. Scenario 1 reports −4 to −7 h without stating whether that is the eligible subset or the caseload median; the write-up then gives both (−4 to −7 on eligibles, −2 to −4 overall). Scenario 5 reports a verification-phase median as if it were end-to-end cycle time. Digital × legal stratum shares (23/15/37/25) quietly assume independence of two attributes that are likely correlated. `case_complexity → assessment_capacity` and `originals_required → document_capacity` confuse demand/gates with capacity. Queue huddles are coded as a selection node even though they are a Site A co-intervention.

**Quantitative errors that should not have survived a check.** Assessment WIP is defined as max 8 × 3 = 24, then “80% of capacity” is written as “>24.” Legal WIP is max 6 × 2 = 12, then the throttle is “>16,” which is already over 100%. The pilot’s volume math is simply wrong: ~130 cases/week × 10 weeks ≈ 1,300 total, not “~700 per stratum per arm.” The 15% stratum cannot supply 150 cases per arm in 10 weeks (~98). Mentioning a 5-week crossover on a case-randomized design is confused. Invented WIP caps and a “known data-dependency” screen are presented as if they already exist.

**Model fidelity (Section 8) is the largest substantive miss.** The Site B POWL forces `Identity Check < Document Check` for every case. That encodes something close to the *current* serial verification fragment, not the *candidate* (identity ∥ document for digital cases; identity ∥ originals, then document, when originals are required). Assessment does wait transitively for both checks, but the intervention’s only new concurrency is not in the partial order. That sits badly next to the instruction not to substitute a more sequential Site B model. `StrictPartialOrder(..., order=[...])` is not the usual PM4Py construction (`StrictPartialOrder(nodes=...)` plus `order.add_edge`); the hedge about `add_order` is still the wrong method name. `LOOP(Legal Review, Request Supplement)` does not send work back to Document Check; the limitation note is honest, but then the model still is not the candidate in Section 6. Digital skip of originals is discussed and not built.

**Net.** Strong analogical and transport reasoning, a sensible modified intervention, and a pilot that at least tries to separate mechanism failure from implementation failure. Not a 8–10 answer under a hypercritical bar: the POWL candidate is structurally wrong, the pilot is internally inconsistent on sample size, and several numbers in the operational design do not add up.
