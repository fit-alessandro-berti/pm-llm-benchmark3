**6.0**

The write-up matches the required nine-heading skeleton, keeps assumptions labeled, and is strongest where it stays close to the tables: mixture means, daily case-type volumes, and base offered load are auditable and correct. The policy specs are operational rather than slogans, the specialization–pooling tension is the actual decision, and §§6–7 and 9 treat secondary effects, logging, and a staged pilot with P1 protection in a way that fits the brief.

It is not close to flawless.

**Material analytical errors.** U2 is mis-sized. Doubling E and ES only in 12:00–16:00 and renormalizing adds on the order of ~3 Electrical cases and well under one net Software case, not a jump from 797→1150 and 495→770 standard minutes. That error drives the U2 bottleneck story and part of the scenario table. Chen is repeatedly called the only Electrical+Software resource; Emil and Farah both have both skills. Planned capacity is taken as 480 (overtime already in the denominator) and skills are added as if they do not compete for the same person. The required time-block × skill capacity view is mostly narrative; the 08:00–10:00 Electrical window (Ada slow, Chen, Farah slow; Deepa/Emil not yet on) is under-analyzed.

**Internal inconsistency.** Objective formulas are stripped of operators (O2 as written is not a fairness gap). Seed counts are 50, then 30, then `n_seeds=3`. The comparison table is presented as informed by simulation that this document never ran. A non-existent “appendix” is cited.

**Simulation does not support the claims.** `random.Random.poisson` does not exist, so the block will not run. Cases are scheduled in arrival order through every stage before the next case is considered, so a later P1 cannot displace an earlier P3—priority and preemption, which the recommendation leans on, are not in the dynamics. Preemption is parameterized and then left unused (`preempted_flag` always false). `hash(...)` seeds are not reproducible. “Stage-specific wait” is start minus arrival (cumulative), not queue time at the stage. U4 tail doubling is implemented inconsistently. These are not small omissions; they break the required comparable, uncertainty-aware policy metrics.

**What still earns a mid score.** Base load arithmetic, explicit handover/setup/coordination constants, a robustness definition that is not base-case mean delay, a defensible hybrid recommendation with mode triggers, and an honest list of what expected arrivals cannot identify. Under a strict bar, the U2/skill-matrix mistakes plus a non-working, non-priority simulator keep this well below the top band.
