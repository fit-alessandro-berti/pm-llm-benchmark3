**6.7**

The answer is complete, on-format, arithmetically mostly accurate, and correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has a repeated identification–execution gap, several over-interpreted contrasts, internally inconsistent magnitudes, and a rollout design that does not actually deliver the learning it promises.

**Format and coverage.** All ten required headings, table schemas, estimand types, mechanisms, threats, outcomes, the four-rung claim ladder, and a self-contained Python block are present. Section 1 stays descriptive. That is the floor of the task, not a reason for a high score.

**The central flaw is that the stated design is not the design that is used.** Section 3 nominates a staggered event-study with relative-time indicators as primary and correctly warns against naïve two-way fixed effects. Section 5 then reports windowed pre/post means and an informal DiD. Section 9 fits exactly the naïve TWFE the prompt told the author not to use without an HTE/timing discussion (`median_cycle_h ~ automation + … + C(branch) + C(month)`), plus the same model with `automated_fast_path_pct`. Relative time is built and printed, then discarded. Never-treated units get `NaN` `rel_month` and are never placed in a reference bin. There is no event-study regression, no cohort-time estimator, and no mention of TWFE negative weighting / heterogeneous-timing bias (Sun–Abraham, Callaway–Sant’Anna, imputation). For a staggered-adoption question that explicitly flags this issue, that is a serious miss, not a documentation quibble.

**Gamma is the load-bearing “success” and the window is stacked in its favor.** Alpha/Beta pre is Jan–Mar; Gamma pre is Apr–Jun, which already contains the May complexity shock (Gamma median 45.4–45.9, p90 100–101). Jul–Aug then looks like a 6.5 h median and 9 h p90 gain. Versus Gamma’s own Jan–Mar (median ≈37.5 h, p90 ≈77.4 h), Jul–Aug is essentially a return to winter medians and still worse tails (≈84.9 h). Controls also mean-revert after June. A relative DiD still mildly favors Gamma, but the write-up treats the Apr–Jun→Jul–Aug drop as evidence of beneficial substitution and “strongest supporting evidence” for medians, tails, rework, and CSAT. That is selective. Skipping Alpha/Beta April–May as “transition” also hides the one clean go-live signature in the table: rework drops in April (Alpha 13.2→11.2, Beta 12.8→10.4) then rises. The post window includes Alpha’s worst month (June: 53.5 / 119.9) and best month (August: 43.3 / 101.2) and calls the average “stabilized.”

**Numeric claims do not agree with each other.** Section 5.5 converts a rough DiD into “on the order of 5–10%” median reduction. Section 7 widens this to −15% to +5% without a new calculation that supports the optimistic bound. The claim ladder then says “single-digit percentages.” Three different numbers for one estimand. The −15% end is not implied by the arithmetic (Alpha/Beta vs Delta/Epsilon is about 4–6 h on a ~47 h base, i.e. high single digits, and that is already the confounded ITT). Escalation is given confidence 60 for “+2 to +4 pp vs own pre-period,” which is not a causal interval; March already adds exactly +2.0 pp in every branch. CSAT confidence 50 is hard to defend given missing response counts.

**Estimands and process logic are good, with real sloppiness.** Availability vs intensity, median vs tail, interference via the fraud desk, and treatment-version heterogeneity are all recognized. E2 nevertheless interprets a branch-month *median* regression as a “per-case average,” which is the wrong functional. E4 bundles rework and escalation, which move for different reasons. Routing is treated as time-varying confounding throughout, even though it starts in the adoption month for Alpha/Beta and is part of the implemented policy; the ITT of “availability as rolled out” is not cleanly separated from the ITT of “automation holding volume fixed.” Epsilon is called a “better-performing comparator”; it has the highest rework and CSAT similar to Alpha. Load (`arrivals/fte`) is the obvious process covariate and is used only inside the OLS formula, never in the descriptive tables, despite Alpha going from ~32 to ~43 arrivals per FTE.

**Code meets the checklist, not the scientific claim.** Panel validation, separate (non-subplot) trend plots, pre/post windows, clustered SEs, and causality disclaimers are there. Clustered OLS with five branches is still presented as an “ITT-like panel model.” `high_complexity_pct` and `arrivals_per_fte` are post-treatment for several of the shocks the narrative treats as confounders; the comments never flag bad controls. `rel_month` uses `days // 30` and is unused. The script therefore does not operationalize the author’s own preferred strategy.

**The rollout recommendation is the right *label* and a weak *design*.** “Expand with an embedded evaluation” is defensible. Randomizing adoption order across the last two branches, then also randomizing products/channels inside them, while asking for statistical significance on a 5–10% median gain, contradicts the paper’s own small-cluster warning. With two remaining units, a stepped wedge has almost no power; expanding both remaining never-treated branches eliminates the only clean controls unless a long holdout is explicit. Guardrails (20% p90 breach for two months vs randomized controls) are too coarse for monthly p90s. The text never chooses between Epsilon (more like treated branches) and Delta (stronger holdout), nor does it keep a branch-level never-treated series as a first-class learning device.

**What remains genuinely strong.** The answer does not invent a precise ATE; it separates availability from use; it keeps medians and tails apart; threats cover the required list; arithmetic on the chosen windows is correct; substitution vs capacity creation is named; and the final ladder correctly marks a system-wide X% slogan as unsupported. Those are necessary virtues. They do not offset the event-study that was never estimated, the Gamma window that was over-read, the wandering effect sizes, or a learning design that spends the last controls too quickly.

Not a 9+: too many identification and quantitative-discipline failures. Not a 5: the structure, mechanism table, threat table, and refusal to overclaim are real. **6.7** is a strictly graded score for a competent but leaky causal memo.
