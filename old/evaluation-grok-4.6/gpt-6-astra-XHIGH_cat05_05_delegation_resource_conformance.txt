**8.4**

This is a strong, well-structured investigation with generally sound event-time reasoning, but it is not nearly flawless. Under strict grading the defects below are enough to keep it well short of the top of the scale.

**What is actually strong.** Alias normalization (`u_bk`/`b.koch`), refusal to treat `svc_qms` as a person, event-time roles rather than the current master, and the decision not to invent extra SoD rules (Eva approving and verifying; Grace verifying; drafter also implementing) are all correct. CHG-501 as `VALID_SUBSTITUTION`, CHG-502/508/510 as `UNAUTHORIZED` on RR1, CHG-507 as unauthorized HIGH-risk approval, CHG-503/506 as identity problems, and CHG-510’s prohibited subdelegation are the right calls. RR4 business-day counting (Tue 11 Aug → deadline end of Thu 13 Aug) is stated clearly and applied consistently: CHG-505 is late, CHG-510 is timely. Section 4 does not treat 18/23 pairing as collusion. The required headings and labels are used.

**1. The “executable” PM4Py block is not shown to be executable.** This is the largest concrete failure against the stated task. `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(..., keep_violations=True)` is version-sensitive; several public APIs use `positive=False` or have no such keyword and would raise `TypeError`. `pd.offsets.BDay` on UTC timestamps is a known pandas footgun (tz dropped or aware/naive comparison errors in `impl_time <= e[T] <= deadline`). `review_deadline` as `normalize() + BDay(2) + 1 day - 1ns` is opaque; the prose is right, the arithmetic is easy to misread as Friday. Requirement 7 is skipped rather than exercised as a negative control. A grader cannot accept “one executable Python code block” on faith.

**2. Three-valued absence logic never fails a required absence condition.** `principal_absence` returns `PASS` on recorded leave and `UNKNOWN` otherwise — including Saturday 15 Aug, after Asha’s leave ended, and during the VPN window when she may have been available. For D-QM-02 (“principal must be absent”) that converts a condition that is **not demonstrated** into `UNKNOWN` rather than `FAIL`. CHG-509 as `INDETERMINATE` is therefore softer than the grant language. The VPN window is likewise never treated as availability that would suspend D-QM-01, even though the narrative understands that issue.

**3. CHG-505 is flattened.** The late, out-of-scope, and identity-weak retrospective review is enough to kill the RR4 exception, but the primary label `UNAUTHORIZED` bundles (a) a possibly valid Farid/D-IC-03 initial approval, (b) unproven CI-44 linkage and unproven emergency credibility, and (c) a late/unqualified review. The secondary label only partly unpicks this. A stricter case row would split initial authority from the failed exception.

**4. Narrow grant reading is asserted, not fully carried in the matrix.** The text correctly refuses to stretch D-QM-01 to cooling-system retrospective review, but the Ben row still presents D-QM-01 as a general “QM approval” interval and only later caveats duty and scope. A reader of the matrix alone could think Ben was a valid QM substitute for CHG-505/510 reviews.

**5. Residual unclarity and over-precision.** CHG-504 as `INDETERMINATE` with secondary `AUTHORIZED` is defensible, but the code then blanks Asha the same way it blanks Ben on CHG-506, undercutting the claimed evidential distinction. Confidence/severity mix exposure with identity doubt (CHG-504 “high potential approval exposure” while also calling Asha’s role high-confidence). Handover counts in the extract are careful, but “six Diego-to-Eva handovers” still invites over-reading a 10-case slice. `emergency_credible` is hard-coded `UNKNOWN` for every emergency, so the IC path can never `PASS` in code even if D-IC-03 is otherwise perfect — a silent conservative bias not equivalent to a documented credibility test.

**6. Control recommendations are generic relative to the findings.** Section 6 hits the requested topics, but several items are policy boilerplate (rotation, retention, SNA monitoring) rather than tightly tied to the actual failure modes: delayed QMS ingestion of D-QM-01, email subdelegation, shared `svc_qms` without step-up auth, and sick-leave use of `u_bk`.

The legal/process core is well above average and mostly right. The score is held down by unverified code, systematically lenient absence logic, and a few classification/presentation compressions that a strict judge should not ignore.
