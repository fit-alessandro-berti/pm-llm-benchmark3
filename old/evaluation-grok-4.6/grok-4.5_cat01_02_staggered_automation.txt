7.1

The answer gets the scientific bottom line right and satisfies the required scaffold, but it is not close to flawless. Quantitative presentation is sloppy, several numbers are off, the most favorable adopter is over-weighted, and the implemented analysis does not match the design advertised as primary.

## What works

The piece correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It separates availability from intensity, selection from treatment, median from tail, and ITT from dose-response. Section 1 stays descriptive. The estimand list is mostly well posed, interference/SUTVA and treatment-version problems are named, and the threat table is broad enough. The rollout choice (`expand with an embedded evaluation`) is the only option that jointly serves operations and identifiability. The claim ladder is ordered and labeled properly. The Python block is self-contained, validates the panel, prints the five outcomes without subplots, builds relative time, fits a binary model and an intensity model, clusters by branch, and puts the important caveats in warnings/comments rather than in causal rhetoric.

## Section-by-section problems

**1. What is observed.** Content is complete and within the word band. Minor unclarity: pre-rollout `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6 is noted but never interpreted (measurement floor, shadow use, or coding artifact). That omission later weakens the intensity estimand.

**2. Estimands.** E1–E3, E6–E8 are usable. E4 and E5 collapse “availability or intensity” into one contrast, so the intervention is not defined. “Partially / weakly / poorly / not credibly” is impressionistic; the table never states what would identify each target. Eight rows meet the “at least six” rule, but two of them are underspecified.

**3. Identification.** Cohort-aware event study is a reasonable primary design in the abstract, and rejecting naïve TWFE is correct. With 3 treated units, 2 cohorts, and 2 donors, Sun–Abraham / Callaway–Sant’Anna is barely a design and mostly a slogan. The write-up admits fragility, then still sells this as preferred over a simpler Gamma 2×2 or branch ITS, which are more honest given *G*=5. Alt B’s donor pool is too small for SCM; that is mentioned, then left as a “credible alternative.” The preference paragraph is the right length and mostly sound.

**4. Mechanisms.** Coverage of the required mechanisms is good. The signature columns are often unreadable (“median_cycle_h  as more cases skip specialist”, “rework  from misuse”). Directional claims are the point of that table; without them it does not do its job. Fast-path substitution is not linked to a checkable path-mix identity (automated fast + automated escalated + manual paths must sum to 100%).

**5. Quantitative evidence — the main failure.**

Arithmetic and signs are not reliable enough for a lead-scientist memo.

- Alpha Jan–Mar median is 48.9; Apr–May is 48.9; the table’s “~+0.3” is wrong.
- Alpha later p90 change is about +6.9 hours, not “~+5.1”.
- Several rework/CSAT cells omit the sign while neighboring cells use `+`, so the table can be read as saying Gamma’s median *rose* 8.5 hours. Surrounding prose knows the direction; the table still fails “clearly separate arithmetic facts from causal interpretation.”
- Intensity text is broken: “auto_fast 40”, “53.543.3”, “~4945”, “~10799”. That is not a usable dose analysis.
- Later windows for Alpha/Beta are Jul–Aug, silently dropping June (Alpha 53.5 / 119.9, the worst month). That is window shopping unless justified.
- Gamma is treated as the clean case. Raw May–Jun → Jul–Aug is about −8.5h median and −15.6h p90, but Delta/Epsilon recover about 3–5h and 6–10h over the same window. A rough DiD is nearer −4h / −8h, not the raw Gamma move. The sensitivity bounds “[8 h, +3 h]” and “[15 h, 0]” are not well-formed intervals and are anchored on the most optimistic raw contrast, not the control-adjusted one.
- Intensity is almost perfectly collinear with months-since-adoption. That identification problem is mentioned in passing, not treated as fatal for E2.

Pre-trend/RTM discussion is better than the tables. Control comparisons exist, as required, but they are not allowed to discipline the numeric ranges.

**6. Threats.** Thirteen threats, including every required item. Direction-of-bias cells are sometimes “ambiguous” when a signed bias is available (routing into treated branches biases against finding a benefit; shared-desk congestion can make controls look worse and inflate ATT). Still one of the stronger sections.

**7. Effect assessment.** All five outcomes appear, and “not identified” is used appropriately for CSAT and for the sign of p90 at scale-up. Confidence scores are arbitrary but low, which is honest. Safe-claim language is good. The ranges inherit Section 5’s sign and Gamma-weighting problems, so the table looks more precise than the evidence.

**8. Rollout.** Right discrete choice. Within-branch stepped wedge, freeze routing/staffing, case-level path logs, desk waits, CSAT denominators, and an explicit scale-up rule are all on target. Weaker points: Epsilon-first vs “randomize order” is slightly internally inconsistent; 8–12 / 12 week windows are asserted, not derived from the observed learning ramp; no power or minimum detectable effect given *G*=2 remaining branches. Word count is in band.

**9. Code.** Meets the checklist on paper. It does *not* implement the Sun–Abraham/CS design sold in Section 3; Model A is the naïve TWFE the identification section disavows, and Model C drops month FE in a setting whose main confounder is a calendar shock. Relative-time dummies for never-treated are all zero, so the event study is weakly separated from branch FE. Intensity tertiles include adopters’ pre-period months (`automated_fast_path_pct` 4–6) in the “low” bin. Warnings about *G*=5 and aggregated medians are present and correct. The script would likely run; it would not estimate the primary estimand.

**10. Claim ladder.** Four bullets, correct prefixes, correct order. The “Conditionally causal” bullet is almost a retraction of itself (“assumptions are not credible enough”), which is intellectually honest but makes the label decorative.

## Scoring rationale

Evaluated strictly on estimands, identification, process interpretation, quantitative discipline, and uncertainty:

| criterion | why it is not high |
|---|---|
| estimands | E4/E5 smear two interventions; ID column is vague |
| identification | right family of designs; primary estimator is infeasible and unimplemented |
| process interpretation | mechanisms named; signatures half-erased; substitution vs capacity not quantified |
| quantitative discipline | wrong deltas, missing signs, garbled intensity, cherry-picked windows, optimistic bounds |
| uncertainty | conceptually strong; numeric ranges and code SEs do not match that caution |

A nearly flawless answer would have internally consistent signed contrasts, control-adjusted (not raw Gamma) bounds, no dropped June, a primary spec that is actually estimated, and readable mechanism signatures. This is a strong policy memo with a weak results section. Under the requested standard, that is a 7.1, not an 8+.
