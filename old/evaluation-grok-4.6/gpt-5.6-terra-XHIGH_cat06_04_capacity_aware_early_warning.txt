**7.2**

The answer is decision-theoretically serious and operationally more complete than a threshold-and-rank memo. It refuses I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05 from I-07, treats `p_now` as untreated risk, prices queue externalities, and writes a real allocation-and-monitoring policy. It is not close to flawless. Several stated rules are not applied, and the required impact table contains a sign error large enough to invert the adverse case.

**Decision rule.** The core object is right: incremental portfolio value under an effect distribution, not a probability cutoff. Capping \(\Delta p\) at calibrated \(p^0\), refusing imputed features as evidence, and putting calibration in intervals rather than a single offset are all coherent. Two problems remain. First, \(B_i\) is charged as a sure cost while also being described as false-alarm burden; that is conservative and disclosed, but it is not the same quantity as expected disruption, and it quietly dominates low-value cases (I-07, I-08’s lower tail). Second, the deployable score \(S_i=N_i-0.02W_Q\) is not deployable on the information given. The rule outsources the binding constraint to an unobserved live-queue total, then ranks cases on \(N_i\) anyway. A rule that cannot be scored on the live sheet is only half a rule. The 0.10 calibrated-risk re-entry bar is also arbitrary and untested against the SME under-prediction band (0.04–0.09), so a real SME deterioration can be suppressed as “not material.”

**Allocation.** Arithmetic on midpoints is correct, I-03 is correctly ineligible, I-05 is correctly suppressed, and I-06 is correctly held for validation. The selected set I-02 / I-09 / I-04 plus a 15:00 reserve is a defensible urgency-plus-EV portfolio, not a `p_now` ranking.

The application does not match the rule. Section 1 says effects are shrunk toward zero for Strategic over-prediction, sparse segments, and weak mechanisms. Section 2 uses raw midpoints: I-02 0.15, I-04 0.19, I-09 0.24. After the stated Strategic correction, I-02’s untreated risk is about 0.54–0.61 and its lower-bound net is thin (\(\approx\) €950 before spillover). I-09 is the case the rule itself says is least identified, yet it is locked as the second slot. “Intervene if review passes” is not a completed decision; no alternate is named if review fails (I-01 is the obvious substitute and is better calibrated). I-04 is still in triage with 10 hours and a €2,000 disruption cost; calling that “intervene” rather than “hold until diagnosis starts or the alert persists” spends scarce paired-specialist time early. I-08 as the leading post-15:00 candidate is weakly robust: at the lower effect bound, \(0.05\times€100{,}000-€450-€5{,}000<0\) before any spillover. I-01 or I-10 survive the same stress better. The realized portfolio is two Strategic cases and one sparse-language case; no SME and no Public Sector case is treated, which sits awkwardly next to the later fairness text and the claim that SME under-prediction should prevent misses.

Operational sequencing is under-specified. Two specialists deliver one 45-minute swarm, so three swarms are serial. From 12:00 that is roughly 2.25 hours plus handoff, which collides with a reserve held through 15:00. The text never checks whether I-02 then I-09 already consume the only paired capacity that could absorb a 13:00 regulatory arrival.

**Expected-impact table.** Optimistic and base rows are auditable and internally consistent (0.58–0.80 and ~0.58 breach-equivalents; €19.95k–€28.30k and ~€19.95k direct benefit; 0.18 and 0.36 excess breaches; nets about +€9.8k to +€20.9k and +€4.4k to +€9.8k at the stated \(W_Q\) band). Adverse is not. Lower-bound effects with one wasted slot give direct benefit €5.6k–€9.6k; cost €4.75k; spillover \(0.09W_Q\approx\) €8.1k–€16.2k. Net is about **−€15.4k to −€3.3k**, not “€3.3k to €15.4k.” The following sentence only makes sense if the author knew the downside was a loss, so this is a labeling/sign error rather than a conceptual miss — but the section asked for auditable arithmetic, and the published adverse net has the wrong sign. Confidence labels are impressionistic. \(W_Q=€90\)–\(180k\) is invented; that is acknowledged, but then “net-impact interpretation” is still presented as if it were a portfolio result rather than a sensitivity cartoon.

**Repeated alerts.** This is the strongest section. Alerts are stateful; refreshes are not new work; post-swarm repeats are barred; I-05 is suppressed; I-07 is valid re-entry and still not swarmed; I-02 is an unresolved first opportunity, not a banned repeat. The 2-hour windows are fiat, and I-07’s “rapid non-swarm review” is underspecified relative to a 1.5-hour SLA, but the distinction the rubric cares about is drawn cleanly.

**Fairness.** All five required risks are named and paired with concrete controls (value cap / concave transform, language-access review, segment calibration intervals, treatment-tagged labels, pre-dispatch view of the six spilled-on cases). Two slips: “clinically” is the wrong domain, and the “small safeguarded learning allocation” is in tension with forbidding repeats and reserving a slot — it is asserted, not fitted to four remaining slots and a possible regulatory arrival. The current pick list is not run through the safeguards. A fairness discussion that never asks whether today’s I-01 / I-07 / I-10 starve is ornamental.

**Monitoring.** The measurement families and the target-trial-plus-interference design are appropriate, including cluster/time-block randomization and the warning not to train on post-swarm outcomes. Triggers for changing the rule are specific enough to use. What is missing is a numeric stop-rule tied to this shift (SME +0.04–0.09 and Strategic −0.05–0.12 are already known and should have already fired recalibration, not merely “trigger work”). Randomizing live critical-incident swarms is treated too lightly relative to the same memo’s reserve-for-regulatory preference.

**Net.** This is a strong policy memo with the right objects (eligibility, persistence, effect heterogeneity, calibration, spillover, feedback). It is not a 9-level answer: the adverse net is wrong as written, shrinkage and calibration are announced then ignored in the scores, I-09 is over-weighted relative to the author’s own uncertainty doctrine, I-08 is a fragile backup, and three serial swarms plus a 15:00 reserve are not shown to be jointly feasible. Those are not polish issues. They are the difference between a coherent design and a deployable one.
