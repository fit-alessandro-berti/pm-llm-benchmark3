The answer is unusually complete and mostly coherent: it refuses to smash incompatible Legal/Operations rules into one silent encoding, partitions R1–R12 across formalisms with an explicit authoritative layer, and the T1–T8 matrix is largely calculated correctly. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several modeling and specification defects are material.

**Significant design hole: the procedural layer false-fails legitimate R11 traces.**  
`Verify Bank Account` is in `BACKBONE_ALPHABET` and the tree allows it only once, in the opening AND-block. R11’s required re-verification sits *between* `Activate Vendor` and `Release First Payment` (R10 forbids a second payment, so that is the only place the re-verify clause has bite). A projected R11-compliant trace is therefore  
`…, Activate Vendor, Verify Bank Account, Release First Payment, Close Onboarding`,  
which cannot replay on  
`→(XOR(MA, →(EDD, CA)), AV, RFP)`.  
Section 3 claims post-activation behavior was “deliberately excluded,” but only `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor` / escalation / opinion were removed. The extra VBA was left in the projection. Meanwhile the skeleton advertises `Verify Bank Account ∈ {1,2,3}`. That is an undisclosed cross-layer contradiction, not a disclosed approximation.

**Implemented skeleton still contradicts R11.**  
`equivalence` / `always_after` on `(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` is left in the live model. Section 9 admits this breaks when suspension halts payment, but the constraint is not removed or scoped. A “completed-case universe” caveat does not justify shipping a cardinality rule that the same document’s R11 path falsifies.

**Overall-verdict contract is violated.**  
Allowed cells are only `pass` / `fail` / `warning` / `not evaluated` / `policy-dependent`. T7’s overall cell is `pass with warning`. By the authors’ own severity ladder that cell must be `warning`.

**Layer arbitration is underspecified wherever the “authoritative” layer and an approximation disagree.**  
R3 is declared DECLARE-authoritative (`altsuccession`), yet the tree pins clarification to the pre-classification slot and can fail a pairing that DECLARE accepts (or accept a pairing that still violates “before any final outcome”). No rule says whether overall follows the authoritative layer or any-layer-fail. T5 hides this because every control-flow layer fails together.

**T6 resource/data under Resolution A is inconsistent with the chosen policy.**  
Resolution A “rejects R6 for automated checking” and lets DECLARE `nonsuccession` decide. R9 and R11 are clean on T6 (Cora ≠ Ivo, Ana ≠ Faris, no bank change). The A-row should be `pass` on that layer, not `policy-dependent`, unless the emergency check is always on — in which case Section 7’s “only meaningful under B” is wrong.

**Smaller technical nits.**  
The altsuccession rationale (“`Classify Risk` may sit between request and response”) contradicts the tree, which forbids that interleaving. `nonsuccession(AV, OLO)` is a reasonable R5 stand-in but is *unconditional*, so a non-escalated post-activation opinion is a false fail; that is disclosed, but it is still a looser encoding than the text claims (“forces opinion-before-activation whenever escalation occurred” only together with R4, and only if both activities occur). `activ_freq` caps `{0..3}` / `{0..2}` are formalism artifacts presented as model content. Unary `existence` of `Classify Risk` / `Close Onboarding` plus `init` are completed-case assumptions baked into DECLARE; running cases would be massacred, which is stated once and then forgotten in the deployment order.

**What remains strong, and why this is not lower.**  
R5/R6 is handled honestly (A vs B vs dual reporting, unobservable authorization, T6 shown both ways). Template literacy is real: noncoexistence vs nonsuccession on T7/T8, alternate vs chain on R3, precedence vacuity on R7, temporal-as-warning vs missing-pair blindness on T5/T12. Resource code is the only T4 detector and states a missing-`org:resource` policy. Vacuity, support/confidence-as-metadata, and “do not average Legal vs Operations” are the right deployment points.

The answer reads like a high-quality design note with one structural hole, one shipped inconsistency, and a broken output token. That is well above a mediocre PM write-up and well short of a clean, executable hybrid model.
