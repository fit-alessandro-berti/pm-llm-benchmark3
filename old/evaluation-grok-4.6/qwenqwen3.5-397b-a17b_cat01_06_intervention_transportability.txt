**5.2**

The write-up follows the required skeleton and lands the right high-level call (`conditional go`, do not copy the all-case tree, Site A is confounded). That is not enough. Under a strict reading of the rubric—process structure, transport, co-interventions, quantitative mapping, model fidelity, pilot design—the answer has several material errors and several required insights it never makes.

**What it gets right.** Site A’s before/after is treated as mixed up with the API upgrade, added reviewers, marketing mix shift, huddles, and timestamp cleanup. Blanket copying is rejected. Originals and legal review are named as blockers. There is a digital / no-legal segment, a bottleneck-shift scenario in which the median can improve while the tail worsens, a case-level stratified pilot, and an attempt to separate mechanism failure from implementation failure. Headings, table column names, and most word-count bands are in bounds. The recommendation is in the correct band.

**Process structure and analogical miss (serious).** Site B already allows `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` after `Register` (DFG: 2,050 and 1,860). The unused concurrency is identity work overlapping the ~18 h originals delay, then `Document Check` only when documents exist. Copying “create Identity and Document Check together after Register” is the wrong analog and is illegal for the 62% originals stratum. The answer never uses the DFG this way. It also never computes the mechanical bound at A: sequential identity+document service ≈ 10.7 h versus max(3.5, 7.2) ≈ 7.2 h, so pure overlap can explain only a few hours of wait, not the 9 h median drop. That calculation is the obvious way to stop treating A’s 9 h as mostly treatment. It is absent.

**Transport graph.** Required node names appear, but several edges are causally wrong: `originals_required → document_capacity`, `case_complexity → legal_capacity`, `API_latency → identity_capacity`, `queue_huddles → identity_capacity`. Originals delay start, they do not create staff; complexity raises legal demand, not capacity; API latency is a service-time shifter. Selection nodes are incomplete (`S_case_complexity`, `S_legal`, capacity selection, etc.). The prose is better than the graph and does note shared overlap-wait mechanism versus site-specific law, lock, and roles.

**Quantitative scenarios.** Two scenarios assign **100% eligible share** to starting Document Check with Identity. That violates the statutory rule the rest of the memo insists on. “Optimistic structural transfer” is then restricted to 38% anyway, so the label and the design disagree. Digital share (≈38%) is repeatedly treated as the pilot population even where eligibility is digital **and** no legal review; if those flags are even roughly independent the eligible slice is closer to ~23%. Point estimates are not derived from A’s wait change, FTE add, or mix shift; they are asserted. IT-lock failure worsening p90 by +5 h is unexplained.

**Intervention / POWL.** Eligibility instincts are conservative and mostly sound. The POWL is not. It omits `Originals Receipt`, starts `Document Check` unconditionally after `Register`, and encodes supplement as a one-shot optional path rather than a loop that can return through document check, assessment, and legal. Section 6 and Section 9 also drift: one says digital ∩ no-legal, the other says “digital-only first.”

**Section 8 is a fail on the stated spec.** The prompt required POWL classes, **explicit order edges** so Assessment waits for both checks, and constructors consistent with the PM4Py POWL API. The code invents `POWLModel` and `pm4py.objects.powl.operator` (`Sequence` / `Parallel` / `Choice` / `Loop` / `Empty`). Standard construction is `Transition`, `SilentTransition`, `OperatorPOWL`, `StrictPartialOrder`, and `order.add_edge`. There are no partial-order edges. A try/except fallback to a process tree is exactly the simplification the prompt forbade. Site A trees are fine; soundness/playout comments on data, capacity, locks, and duration are fine. That does not rescue the required Site B model.

**Smaller cuts.** Section 1 is telegraphic and underuses WIP=6, independent external data, and cross-training. Section 3 still assigns confidence 50 to transferring the median cycle-time drop after saying the effect is not identified. Pilot power (200/arm) versus 8 weeks on a ~23–38% slice is tight and unreconciled; interference among shared assessors/legal is thin. Assumptions are eight and formatted, but they miss the central identifying assumption (A’s before/after is not a unique transport effect) and Assumption 5’s “if false” clause restates the assumption.

**Net.** Right genre of answer, right top-level decision, real recognition of confounding and of not starting illegal work—undercut by a wrong Site B analogy, illegal 100% scenarios, a broken causal graph, unused DFG/arithmetic, and non-compliant PM4Py. That is mid-pack, not near-flawless.
