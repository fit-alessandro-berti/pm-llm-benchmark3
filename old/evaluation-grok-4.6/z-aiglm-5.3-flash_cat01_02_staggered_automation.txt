**7.0**

The write-up is conceptually strong: it refuses a single “X%” claim, separates availability from dose, keeps median and p90 apart, treats rollout as endogenous and bundled, and recommends a phase-in that serves both operations and identification. Format compliance is high. The grade is held down by quantitative and logical errors that a strict reading cannot treat as nits.

**What holds up.** Estimands E1–E7 correctly split program ITT, dose, tails, process intermediates, CSAT, dynamics, and interference, and they flag treatment-version and SUTVA problems. The identification stack (cohort event study, synthetic DiD, intensity/IV-with-failed-exclusion) is appropriate; TWFE is used only as a labeled descriptive association. Mechanisms match process structure (substitution vs capacity, specialist/fraud-desk congestion, learning, routing, staffing, rework displacement, tails, survey selection). Threats cover the required set, including shared-desk spillovers and five-cluster inference. The claim ladder is well ordered. Core pre/post means, escalation steps (+0.8 to +1.0pp vs 0 in controls), load/FTE ratios, and raw dose slopes are right.

**Quantitative discipline (main deduction).** The matched-DiD table is internally inconsistent. Alpha/Beta “Delta/Epsilon” control shifts (+4.8h median, +16.9h p90) are Delta’s changes, not the two-control average (+3.6h and +14.7h). That produces headline DiDs of −8.1h / −3.9h instead of about −6.9h / −2.7h. Section 5.5 then switches to the averaged-control figures (−6.9h, −2.8h) without reconciliation. Gamma’s p90 “adopter Δ” of −16.1h uses June as pre, while the defined window (Jan–Jun) is −0.9h; the asterisk does not fix the table. Section 7’s “mature matched DiD −3.9 to −4.9h” matches neither the −8.1/−3.9 table nor the branch-level averaged-control estimates. Cases/FTE is variously 12–37%, 19–37%, and +10% to +40%. The Python block would compute pooled control deltas and therefore **would not reproduce the narrative DiD table**—a serious fault next to a “reproducible analysis” requirement.

**Logical flaw in the decision.** Section 8 requires six instrumented pre months (Sep 2026–Feb 2027) and also a first go-live on 2026-10-01. That is one month of new pre-data, not six. “Stratified” randomization of two sites is just a coin flip.

**Smaller issues.** E1 dumps every outcome into one estimand. “Callaway–Sant’Anna-style” with two cohorts and two controls is oversold (randomization inference is the part that actually fits). “5–8pp complexity everywhere” overstates Beta (+3pp at the May jump). Markdown eats some minus signs in bounded intervals. CSAT “+0.09 DiD” is noise-scale, which they mostly admit.

**Not enough for a higher score under a strict bar.** Recognition of staggered intensity, tails vs medians, queue spillovers, and substitution vs capacity is genuine and well argued. Those strengths are why this is a 7 rather than the mid-5s the arithmetic errors alone might suggest. It is not nearly flawless.
