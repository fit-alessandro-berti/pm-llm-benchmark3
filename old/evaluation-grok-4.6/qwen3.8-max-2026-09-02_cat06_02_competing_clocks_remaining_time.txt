**6.7**

The six required sections are present, the parallel join is treated as a maximum, weekend/staffing calendars are used, dependence is not assumed away, and the answer avoids a single false-precise completion time. Those are real strengths. They are not enough for a high score: the required tables and headline forecast contain internal contradictions and several arithmetic mistakes.

**What is done well**

The structural model is the right one: \(T_{\text{join}}=\max(T_{\text{chem}},T_{\text{micro}})\), then QA wait, service, rework, and release, each passed through the stated calendars. Microbiology incubation is correctly placed at Sun 20 Sep 12:00–20:00. The answer sees that Sunday QA is closed, so a Sunday readout does not by itself produce a Sunday terminal event. Chemistry is not finished by adding means: Friday residual capacity, FIFO vs risk-priority, pause-at-18:00, and rerun-cannot-span-close are all used. Temperature excursion is treated as a common-cause switch rather than an independent coin flip. QA’s future queue is handled with a rough capacity argument (current 5, Fri/Sat reviewers, 0.35/h arrivals, overdispersion) instead of freezing the Friday queue. Scenario overlaps are declared instead of forced to 100%. Uncertainty drivers and the next-event update are in the required form.

**Major problems**

1. **Scenario C’s probability band and its times do not match.** The band 45–75% is driven by *no weekend authorization* (about 0.55–0.75). The decomposition later says that delay often does **not** move the join if chemistry is still Monday 10:00–16:00. Yet the table’s *central* terminal time for C is **Tue 22 Sep 10:00–20:00**, which is the contamination path (the 8–25% subset). A high-probability, often-immaterial delay is labeled with the times of a lower-probability material delay. That is a defect in a required deliverable, not a wording nit.

2. **The advertised P50 is the no-rework lump, not a mixture median.** Scenario A is only 25–45%, Scenario D is 25–55%, rerun 10–30%. A right-skewed mixture can still have its median in the main mode, but only if “smooth or equivalent timing” is at least ~50%. The forecast text explicitly treats the P50-like band as the “no-major-rework region” (Mon evening–Tue afternoon). That underweights the query/investigation mass the same answer assigned. The upper edge (Tue 14:30 / 95 h) is too tight once 4–12 working-hour queries and Monday reruns are included. P80 as Wed–Thu is more coherent with those weights than P50 is.

**Calculation and clock errors**

- From a query raised Mon 14:00, **12 QA working hours** are Mon 14:00–20:00 (6 h) plus Tue 08:00–14:00, i.e. **Tue ~14:00**, not “Tue 10:00–11:00”.
- **145 h** after Fri 18 Sep 15:30 is **Thu 24 Sep 16:30**, not ~14:30.
- **160 h** is **Fri 25 Sep 07:30**, not “Thu 24 Sep late”.
- **280 h** is **Wed 30 Sep 07:30**, not ~15:30.
- Scenario A earliest is ~10:00; the summary earliest is 09:00. Monday 09:00 also ignores that QA still has a processed-but-nonzero queue after Friday/Saturday, so sub-10:00 release is not really “plausible earliest.”
- Friday chemistry *completion* is rarer than the stated “<5–10%”: B-417 does not outrank the urgent sample, the short server is free ~16:00 with only ~2 h left, and \(P(\text{triangular}(1.5,2.5,4.5)\le 2)\approx 8\%\). Start-and-pause Friday is plausible; finish Friday is not at 5–10%.

**Smaller issues**

FIFO is 55%, not an exotic tail; treating Tuesday chemistry spill as “strict FIFO / long service tail” understates that mass. Overtime sensitivity of “0.5–2 days left” is overstated: micro readiness and Sunday-closed QA still block an early terminal event. Dependence is argued qualitatively but never given explicit conditionals in the final write-up. Driver 2 labels contamination confirmation as mainly *calendar* uncertainty; it is mainly path uncertainty with a calendar readout constraint. Scenario D’s “earliest” Mon 20:00 is not earliest for a short query after a morning join.

**Bottom line**

This is a competent process-forecast: calendars, parallelism, queues, and correlated tails are in view, and the answer never dumps unconditional means into one clock time. Under strict grading it is not near-flawless. The Scenario C table is internally wrong, the P50 band is too optimistic relative to the same answer’s rework probabilities, and several hour-to-clock conversions are simply incorrect. Those are enough to keep the score well below the high 8s–9s.
