**5.4**

The answer follows the required seven-section skeleton and is clearly trying to be multi-criteria rather than “ban air.” That is not enough. It has a core factual mix-up, invents numbers the prompt forbids, and then builds the redesign on those errors.

**Grounding is the main failure.** Standard-stock Regular (median 31h, 7% late) is repeatedly treated as “Regular.” Made-to-order Regular is 94h median, 180h p90, 18% late. Service-contract is likewise collapsed: Standard-stock SC is 18h; MTO SC is 78h. Batch caps of 24h/48h, ground slack thresholds of 24h/12h, and the claim that a 24h wait “aligns with Regular median 31h” are therefore calibrated to the wrong process. SP1 violations are 22% of **Standard-stock Service-contract**, not MTO Regular; Option A mis-targets the highest air-abuse segment. Q1 is 8 kg; the packaging rule silently becomes 5 kg. Inspection “2% defect,” “1% waiver,” “25% buffer,” “30% of vans,” and “4–6h shared-lane guarantee” are not in the log.

**Slack is defined circularly.** Estimated processing time is set to historical median cycle time plus a buffer. Cycle time already includes batch wait, rework, and transport. Using it to decide whether ground is feasible will both keep air when pick–pack–ground is actually short and approve ground using a median that already embeds the slow path. That is not process-path reasoning.

**The effects table is not evidence-based.** Cutting air barely moves `energy_kwh` (stock air is 1.0 vs 0.9 kWh); that is transport. Almost every late-delivery and resilience cell is “↓ or →,” which denies the trade-offs the prompt states (heat-treat batch wait 1.7× late risk, consolidation 12–24h, CNC_B 18% rework, 6% obsolescence). Several cells contradict themselves in the same sentence. Section 4 also sneaks in ranges the prompt said not to invent.

**The package drops the highest-leverage, still-safe levers and calls that “balance.”** CNC_B → CNC_A is not redundant with batch-wait caps: the CNC_B path is 32 kWh, 1.8 kg scrap, 32% late. Consolidation of non-urgent stock is the 42% ground path and a 35–55% kgCO2e cut; excluding it because A “already handles routing” leaves the volume lever unused. S9 (true vs precautionary Critical) is the prompt’s own way not to treat all vans as equal; leaving the 52 kgCO2e emergency-van path unchanged plus an exception log does not reduce empty returns. Partial shipments (7% of cases, 24% late, second trip) and 530 delay-cancellations are almost ignored. Adding an inspection *gate* without addressing inspection-cell queues (the stated cause of missed defects) can add wait and more rushing, yet cycle time is marked down.

**Rebound section is longer than it is sharp.** Six-plus risks are listed, but several are immediately “mitigated” by the same invented thresholds, so displacement is narrated away. Missing: CNC_A overload if B-to-A shift *were* done; inspection-capacity rebound; promise-date gaming; obsolescence from still-large heat-treat batches; green routing that increases cancellations.

**Lifecycle boundary is acceptable but thin.** Distributor gate-to-gate is fine given the log, but packaging mass, rework loops, empty backhauls, and unsold batch stock are the actual hidden burden and are not carried consistently into indicators or the recommendation. Resilience indicator invents `alternative_route_available` instead of using delay-cancellations, single-route Critical vans, and R1.

**Validation over-promises.** “0% missing SP2,” “20–30% transport cut,” “late delivery 18% → 15% in four weeks,” 15-minute weekly reviews, and customer copy are not supported by the model’s stated low precision. There is no before/after path comparison, no holdout/segment control, and no plan for the emergency/partial-shipment spaghetti the DFG already flags.

It is a structured essay with the right headings, not a nearly flawless, log-faithful redesign.
