6.4

The write-up is well organized, hits the required headings, and gets the main survival-analysis architecture right: time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72h, open cases retained as right-censored, `Withdraw`/`Reject` treated as competing terminals rather than default non-informative censoring, `Escalate` flagged as post-baseline/immortal-time, and a usable Aalen–Johansen plan with transfer sensitivities. That is not enough for a high score under a strict bar. Several evidence and estimand errors are material.

**Evidence mistakes.** Late internal resolutions are S03 (76), S20 (74), S13 (110), and S28 (88). The answer repeatedly lists only S03/S13/S28 and never uses S20. The process map omits two observed transitions: `AutoCheck→Resolve` (S01) and `Rework→Resolve` (S27). Grouping S32 with “already-observed breaches” is sloppy: at 96h the observed terminal is transfer, not late resolve.

**Estimand design.** E1–E4 are real quantities, but E2 \(=1-F_R(72)\) is mis-sold as SLA *tail* / breach risk. That functional mixes early rejects/withdrawals (S05 at 9h, S12 at 15h, S23 at 11h, etc.) with genuine tail mass (late resolve or still active at 72). A 9-hour reject is not elapsed-time SLA tail risk. E3 and E4 are the better tail/workload estimands; the decision-to-estimand mapping required by the prompt is therefore only partly right. “Group transfer with non-resolve” is also not a distinct sensitivity for \(F_R\): if transfer remains a competing terminal, AJ for Resolve is unchanged versus the primary encoding.

**Competing-risk statements.** Saying the direction of bias is “not guaranteed” when `Withdraw`/`Reject` are coded as ordinary censoring is too weak and, for the usual KM-of-resolution contrast, wrong: treating competitors as censored inflates the implied resolution incidence. The encoding table collapses all other causes to code 2, and the code never delivers the cause-specific CIFs the SAP itself called primary.

**Code.** The table is complete, open cases are kept, three transfer scenarios and cohort/priority strata are implemented, timestamps are not invented, and the discrete AJ update uses \(S(t-)\) correctly. Gaps: no per-cause CIFs; `get_variants` keys joined with `>` is version-fragile; the printed `events_after_first_esc` feature is leakage-prone if treated as baseline; last-gap informative-censoring is discussed but not encoded.

**What remains solid.** Concrete IDs for survivor bias, stale gaps (S26/S11/S25/S09), open-below vs open-above 72, and non-randomized cohort B with priority imbalance (5/17 vs 3/15 P1) are correct. Transfer is given at least three interpretations. Immortal time and prefix leakage are handled. Limitations (missing intermediate timestamps, external post-transfer outcomes, rollout compliance) are the right ones.

Net: a competent, format-compliant plan with real process-mining and competing-risk literacy, undercut by a missed late-resolved case, an incomplete control-flow map, and a blurred tail-risk estimand. Not near-flawless.
