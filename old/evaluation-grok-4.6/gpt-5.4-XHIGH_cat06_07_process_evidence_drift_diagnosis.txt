8.3

The answer is a strong, rubric-aligned audit: it refuses a single-cause story, keeps April / June / August–September / October mechanistically distinct, and generally separates evidence from hypothesis. It is not near-flawless. Several second-order inferences are underspecified, one bias direction is left unnecessarily agnostic, August is repeatedly bundled with September, and the required code sketch is thinner than the surrounding architecture claims.

## What is actually correct

The six-way diagnosis is the right one, and the month assignments match the timeline rather than the headline metrics.

- **April is treated as a schema/pipeline break**, not a business collapse. Unknown-activity 17%, missing queue 14%, unmapped variants 18%, prefixes built at the wrong event, predicted risk 0.28 vs breach 0.19, and the May rebound after remapping are used as a coherent package. Residual May contamination (cached pre-diagnosis counts, dedup only after 20 April) is not wished away.
- **June is mix/process change, not concept drift.** V2 34%→52%, remote-fix latency stable, within-variant breach rates essentially unchanged (V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35). That is the correct reading.
- **July is correctly marked as onset**, not as the peak. Supplier date, parts edge 42h→65h, V3 0.35→0.46, overall metrics only moderately worse at 98% maturity.
- **August is the cleanest concept-drift month** in the narrative (section 2): parts edge 130h, V3 breach 0.64, scores still ~0.19, no Partner Z yet. That is the right causal cut.
- **October is not read as recovery.** 0.16 observed breach against 155h parts latency, 41% V3, 55% maturity, and longer-path backlog is treated as non-comparable. This is the main quality-criterion trap, and it is avoided.
- Tests are actual discriminators, not a checklist. Each row states two expected patterns and a falsifier. The staged decision (contain → targeted recalibration → feature repair → retrain) is proportionate: June can be recalibrated; August–September cannot be fixed with a global intercept; October is barred from training.
- Monitoring gates are in the right order: maturity and lineage suppress retraining; process/DFG alarms can still go to operations.

Numbers were checked against the tables. No material arithmetic or timeline errors.

## Faults that keep this well below a top score

**1. Censoring bias is left directionless where the prompt gives a direction.**  
The problem states that the outcome-API backlog “disproportionately affects cases with longer resolution paths,” and V3 is both longer and much riskier. Among available labels, observed breach is therefore more likely a **downward** bias, not a symmetric “upward or downward” fog. That matters:

- August 0.28 and September 0.31 are probably *lower bounds*.
- Partner Z’s 0.45 vs 0.27 is not equally free to flip sign; slower maturation plus more V3 should hide breaches, not invent them.
- The answer uses this directional point well for October, then throws it away for August–September and Partner Z. That is an inconsistent use of the same mechanism.

**2. August and September are separated in prose and then re-bundled in the places that drive action.**  
August’s V3 share jump (22%→35%) and AUROC collapse (0.76→0.68) occur **before Partner Z exists**. The covariate-drift row and the AUROC-collapse argument still treat “August–September” as one object, and section 4 marks both months equally unsafe to train on. That is too coarse:

- August is 93% mature, has no new source, and already has a matured V3 rate of 0.64.
- September is 82% mature, 24% Partner Z, and has a slower-maturing rural/V3 mix.

A strict reading should treat August as the primary identifiable concept-drift cohort and September as that drift **plus** population shift **plus** material censoring. The narrative knows this; the table and the training decision do not operationalize it.

**3. The August V3 mix shift is described, not explained.**  
V2 falls 50%→40% and V3 rises 22%→35% in August with Partner Z still at 0%. That is a real process-mix change independent of onboarding. Possible readings (more cases needing parts, remote path becoming infeasible, selection into V3) are not even posed. Calling it “covariate drift” without asking why the route mix moved is a missed triangulation, especially because the same row later attributes V3 enrichment to Partner Z.

**4. Concept drift is asserted more than mechanized.**  
Prediction is immediately after diagnosis. Parts delay is a *downstream* edge. The supplier shock therefore changes \(P(Y \mid X_{\text{diagnosis}})\) *because* the post-prediction process changed. The answer places process drift and concept drift in adjacent rows and says V3 “became much riskier,” but it never states this timing argument cleanly. Without it, a reviewer can still ask whether this is “just process drift” or “just unobserved delay.” The distinction the rubric wants is: process evidence explains *why* the concept changed; it does not make the concept change disappear.

**5. Population vs covariate vs process mix are taxonomically sloppy.**  
Partner Z’s absent rural-distance and device-age features are listed under covariate drift *and* population shift. June’s V2 surge is covariate *and* process. That overlap is allowed by the prompt, but the table never says what would count as population shift *net of* mix and *net of* missing features. The transport test in section 3 is the right instrument; the diagnosis table still double-counts the same facts as two drift types at medium-high and medium confidence.

**6. Recalibration vs retrain is slightly too tidy on June and too blunt on August.**  
June ECE 0.060 with AUROC 0.79 does support intercept/segment recalibration, but only if variant is known at score time. If V2 is a path that is only fully observed *after* the remote-fix decision, pooled recalibration on a diagnosis-time score can punish V1/V3 to fix a V2 prevalence shift. That caveat is missing. Conversely, calling August “unsafe for training” until maturity is “close to complete” ignores that matured V3 labels already exist and are the exact concept-drift signal a later model needs. Conservative is not the same as precise.

**7. The code block does not fully honor the spec it sits under.**  
Required elements are present in name (normalize, dedup, both cohorts, DFG / performance-DFG / variants / temporal features, no invented detector). Execution is weaker than the architecture section:

- Cohort *interpretations* are one-line comments, not the actual point: event-calendar mixes leftover cases from prior months; case-start attributes future edges to the start month and is right-censored for recent starts. The sketch computes DFG/performance-DFG only on `case_slice` and never shows how the two views would disagree in October.
- Open-case censoring of `Order Part → Part Received` in Sep–Oct is unmentioned, so the monitor that is supposed to keep process evidence honest can itself be biased in the same months as the labels.
- `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` are unguarded on empty slices.
- Dedup is exact `(case, activity, timestamp, resource)` after renaming only the diagnosis label. April’s failure mode was also `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` clones and post-20-April source-asymmetric dedup; those are not handled.
- V1/V2/V3 shares — the actual process-variant monitor — are not computed; only `top_variants`.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is called and then reduced to a row count. That is cargo-cult compliance with the function list.

**8. A few local overclaims and hedges.**  
“Very high” on April pipeline is earned. “Medium-high” on concept drift is fair, then undercut by training language that already treats V3 concept change as established enough for score floors. “No documented outcome-definition change” is used well as a negative, but it is not evidence against concept drift; it is evidence against *label-definition* drift. The table places it under concept drift’s contradicting column, which is the wrong column.

## Net

This is a competent, well-structured strong answer: it hits every required section, does not flatten ten months into one drift label, and does not treat October’s 0.16 as a real improvement. Under a strict bar it still loses material points for (i) refusing a directional censoring argument the prompt supplies, (ii) collapsing August into September in the decision-relevant places, (iii) not pinning concept drift to the diagnosis-time information set, and (iv) shipping a monitoring sketch that is more API roll-call than an auditable extraction. Those are not cosmetic nits on a 10; they are exactly the triangulation gaps the question asked to be closed.
