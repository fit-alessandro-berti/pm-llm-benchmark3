**7.0**

A strong, format-compliant causal-policy memo with real process literacy. It is not close to flawless. Quantitative discipline is the main failure: signs, windows, and several advertised contrasts do not survive a hard check.

## What is strong

The answer understands the problem. Availability is separated from uptake. Targeting of weak, high-volume branches is treated as selection, not a nuisance. The fraud desk, routing campaign, FTE moves, May complexity shock, and manager discretion are treated as co-interventions. Aggregated medians/p90s, pathway-as-outcome labels, and CSAT without response counts are correctly treated as measurement limits, not minor data-quality footnotes.

The estimand table is the best part of the writeup. ITT-like availability, endogenous intensity, median vs tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, dynamics, heterogeneity, and a network/interference contrast are all present. E3 and E5 correctly refuse identification. Treatment-version and SUTVA issues are named rather than waved away.

The identification discussion is conceptually right: staggered timing, not-yet-treated controls, no already-treated Gamma comparison, cohort-specific effects, and an honest statement that five branches make this sensitivity analysis rather than an estimator. The mechanism table is process-aware in the way the prompt asked for (substitution vs congestion vs displacement vs tails vs survey selection). The rollout choice — expand with an embedded evaluation, Delta first, Epsilon held as a contemporaneous control, randomize availability/encouragement not completed path, arrival-cohort logs, fraud-desk monitoring, numeric kill criteria — is the correct policy shape.

The Python block does what was asked: embedded CSV, validation, printed trends, pre/post windows, relative time, a labeled descriptive panel, an intensity model, clustered SEs, and explicit non-causal comments.

## Why it is not an 8–10

**1. The main quantitative tables are not safely readable.**

In the adopter pre/post table, improvements are written as unsigned quantities: Alpha median `48.9 → 48.5 (0.4 h)` instead of `−0.4`; Beta rework `(0.6 pp)` instead of `−0.6`; Gamma median/p90/rework similarly unsigned. The early-cohort DiD table then reports median `4.50 h`, p90 `6.37 h`, rework `2.09 pp`, and untreated CSAT `0.084` without signed direction. Later ranges repeat the problem (`0 to 6 h`, `8 to +2 h`, `8 to +8 h`, `0.10 to +0.15`). The prose knows the intended signs. The artifacts leadership would actually use do not. For a memo judged on quantitative discipline, that is not a cosmetic defect.

**2. Several numbers used as evidence are wrong, not just unsigned.**

- Alpha/Beta mean rework change is about `−0.05 pp`, not `0.06 pp`.
- Untreated escalation is about `8.07% → 9.89%` (`+1.82 pp`) if months are weighted equally. The writeup’s `8.23% → 9.77%` (`+1.53 pp`) looks like unique-step averaging of a stepwise series (counting Delta’s `6.8` once instead of twice). That inflates the escalation DiD from ~`+0.9 pp` to `+1.17 pp`.
- Untreated post rework is ~`14.52%`, not `14.63%`.
- Gamma vs Delta/Epsilon, Apr–Jun vs Jul–Aug: median DiD is about `−3.9 h`, not `−4.4 h`. p90 DiD is about `−7.1 h`, not `−4.7 h`. Calling this “roughly” does not cover a 2+ hour miss on the tail contrast.
- The advertised robustness check is simply false. August vs March is **not** “Alpha/Beta improvements of 6.25 h versus 1.45 h in controls, again about 4.8 h.” Alpha improves `6.1 h`, Beta only `1.4 h`, so the equal-branch mean is ~`3.75 h`. Controls **worsen** by ~`1.7 h` (Delta `36.2 → 39.0`, Epsilon `40.2 → 40.8`). The components, the control sign, and the “again 4.8 h” story are all wrong. This is the one sensitivity they use to argue the `0 to −6 h` median range survives dropping May–June.

**3. Pre-trend characterization is sloppy.**

Epsilon’s Jan–Mar median falls `1.2 h` (41.4 → 40.2). Grouping Beta (`+1.0 h`) and Epsilon as “nearly flat” hides a control that was already improving. That matters because the whole design is a fragile parallel-trends argument on five series.

**4. Intensity arithmetic is endpoint theater.**

The “per 10 pp uptake” slopes use first-to-last post months only. Alpha’s path is 48.3 → 49.5 → 53.5 → 47.9 → 43.3. An endpoint slope of `−2.2 h / 10 pp` is not a dose summary; it is August versus April. The caveats are good. The numbers are still presented as if they were intensity evidence. They are calendar/tenure associations.

**5. Confidence is too high relative to the author’s own identification section.**

Median gets 55 and escalation 60 after the author has said the effects are not point-identified, trends are not parallel, the donor pool is tiny, SUTVA is doubtful, and May–June recovery can manufacture Gamma’s contrast. Those scores are not consistent with the threats table. CSAT at 25 is the only calibrated entry.

**6. Smaller but real issues.**

Section 1 is ~258 words against a 150–250 limit. Gamma’s “pre” window is the same May–June shock the author later says drives recovery, so the nicest DiD is the most contaminated one; that is discussed, but still used as a pillar. The “descriptive panel model” is exactly the naïve TWFE the prompt warned against; comments help, they do not make five-cluster month-FE plus event-time a serious specification (collinearity is unmentioned). Arrival-weighted robustness is named as a threat and never computed. E1’s horizon text (“1–2 and 3–5 months”) is slightly muddy. The claim leadership “may safely make” on median still leans on the broken Aug–Mar check.

## Bottom line

This is a good scientist’s memo with a bad analyst’s table. Causal structure, process mechanisms, estimands, threats, and the rollout design would support a high 8 if the arithmetic were clean and signed. Under the requested standard — minor defects count, only near-flawless work gets a very high score — the dropped signs, the wrong control-side Aug–Mar comparison, and the incorrect Gamma p90 DiD cap it at **7.0**.
