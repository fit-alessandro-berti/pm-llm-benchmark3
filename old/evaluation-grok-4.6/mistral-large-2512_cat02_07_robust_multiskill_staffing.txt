The write-up is complete in outline and occasionally competent in prose, but it is not analytically trustworthy. Under a strict reading it fails the quality bar on evidence, internal consistency, and the required executable analysis.

**What is actually acceptable.** The nine required headings are present. Assumptions are labeled. There are two objective statements, one SLA-oriented and one fairness-oriented. Three policies are named with at least some operational language. Sections 6 and 9 cover the requested secondary-effect and validation topics. A few service-time means are computed correctly (for example Mechanical \(23\cdot0.85+45\cdot0.15=26.3\)).

**Those strengths do not offset the core failures below.**

**1. Offered-load arithmetic is repeatedly wrong.** Electrical arrivals in 08:00–10:00 are written as \(7\times(0.25+0.10)=3.85\). That product is \(2.45\). U2 renormalization is also wrong: doubling E and ES on a 100-point mix must divide by \(100+\Delta E+\Delta ES\). For 12:00–14:00 the correct shares are \(56/140=40\%\) E and \(24/140\approx17.1\%\) ES, not \(43.8\%\) E. For 14:00–16:00 the denominator used is \(112\) rather than \(142\), which inflates E+ES from about \(5.9\) branches to \(7.5\). U1 then treats two blocks with different mix percentages as if a single mix applied to the summed arrivals. These are not rounding issues; they are the numbers on which bottleneck claims rest.

**2. Speed multipliers and shifts are mishandled, which breaks the capacity story.** A multiplier of \(1.40\) means Farah is slower, not that she contributes \(1.40\) capacity units. Summing multipliers and multiplying by 120 minutes is the wrong capacity model. Farah’s shift ends at 12:00, yet she is counted as Software/Electrical capacity in 14:00–16:00. Ben has no Electrical skill, yet is folded into morning Electrical capacity in U1. Chen works until 16:00 and is omitted from the base 14:00–16:00 Electrical pool, then U3 drops Deepa as well and uses only Emil (\(1.30\)) for the same window. Ada’s Electrical skill (\(1.35\)) appears and disappears across scenarios. The analysis therefore cannot identify bottlenecks.

**3. Section 2 and Section 4 contradict each other.** Base Electrical utilization is computed near \(44\)–\(50\%\), while the comparison table reports \(75\)–\(90\%\) and mean delays of \(35\)–\(55\) minutes with P1 SLA already at risk. If the load numbers were believed, the delay table could not be; if the delay table were believed, the load numbers could not be. The table values are not outputs of the model. They are asserted ranges.

**4. The recommendation is dominated by the author’s own table.** In every scenario Policy 2 is better on mean delay, tail delay, P1/P2 SLA, peak utilization, fairness, and resilience. Policy 3 wins only on handover. The text then selects Policy 3 as “robust,” claims P1 SLA \(>85\%\) in all scenarios (the same table gives \(70\)–\(80\%\) in U5), and claims peak utilization \(<95\%\) in all scenarios (U5 is \(95\)–\(100\%\)). Policy 3’s listed failure mode is “P1 starvation,” which is the opposite of a reservation policy. Fallback logic is also inverted relative to elementary pooling theory: full pooling is preferred when the system is *underloaded*. Pooling’s value is highest when utilization and variability are high.

**5. Objectives are not usable as written.** The fairness objective adds peak utilization, a utilization standard deviation, and handover minutes with arbitrary weights and incompatible units. The SLA objective is clearer, but neither objective is actually optimized or even scored in Section 4. Decision variables for reservation quantity, parallelization threshold, and overflow are listed and then left numerically vague (“reserved capacity on Ada/Chen/Deepa,” overflow if queue \(>3\)).

**6. Policies are not internally coherent.** Policy 1 is defined as “primary skill only,” then Deepa is given both Mechanical and Electrical, Farah is given every skill, and Chen is barred from Intake despite being qualified. Policy 2’s SERPT is specified as a local activity score plus ad hoc penalties, not remaining case work. Parallel-versus-sequential choice is stated as a rule and never used in the quantitative work. Preemption, the 8-minute ME/ES coordination delay, the extra 5-minute team-family handover, and the 30-minute overtime cap are not carried into the load or delay estimates.

**7. U5 is modeled incorrectly.** The outage blocks Intake from 09:30–10:00; it does not delete arrivals. The text uses the *base* 7 arrivals for 08:00–09:30 instead of the surged \(7\times1.7\), zeros the outage interval, and treats deferred Intake as vanished Electrical load. That is the wrong shock.

**8. The Python block does not satisfy the specification and would not run.**  
- `{"complex_prob_multiplier": 2 for _ in service_time_ranges}` is a `SyntaxError`.  
- U4 never updates complex-tail probabilities. U5’s `"09:30-10:00"` block is not in `demand_base`, so the outage is a no-op.  
- Arrival generation does not reset to each block start and can skip later blocks.  
- `advance_activity()` is called in `complete_activity` and again in the completion handler, so stages are skipped.  
- `is_available` ignores whether the resource is already busy, so servers are not exclusive. Breaks and overtime are not enforced during service.  
- `handover_count` / `setup_count` are never incremented; `parallel_execution` is never set; the 8-minute coordination delay and skill-family +5 rule are absent.  
- The “preemption” block fires on a *freed* resource and is not preemption.  
- Utilization is recomputed by *re-sampling* service times after the fact.  
- One seed, no replications, no uncertainty summary.  
- The event log leaves `start_timestamp` null for most rows.  
- The code calls APIs that are not identified as omitted scaffolding and several that are not standard PM4Py (`get_waiting_time`, `discover_bottleneck_net`, `get_resource_utilization`). A scaffold is allowed only if omissions are explicit. They are not.

**9. Process-mining design is only partly specified.** Required attributes such as distinct queue-entry events, assignment-decision records, planned versus actual service, and pause/resume intervals are missing or collapsed into flags. SLA-clock and eligible-set logging are named but not tied to the simulated log. Invented PM4Py calls undermine the claim of mining competence.

**10. The pilot cannot answer the question it is meant to answer.** Excluding P1 from the hybrid pilot, while understandable as a safety instinct, means the policy’s only distinctive mechanism is not tested. That conflicts with “distinguish the policies while protecting P1.”

**Net.** This is a formatted narrative with fabricated performance numbers, contradictory capacity math, a recommendation that ignores its own evidence, and a non-executable simulation presented as if it generated the results. Coverage of topics is not the same as process-mining competence. On a strict scale the answer is well below a passing technical standard.
