**6.5**

The answer is conceptually well-aimed and format-compliant, but it is not close to flawless. It understands the right distinctions (policy vs. timing, readiness vs. completion, local gain vs. spillover) and then repeatedly undercuts them with sloppy magnitudes, an incomplete K-104 intervention, and queue arithmetic that ignores the snapshots.

**Format and constraint framing.** Sections 1–8 exist, tables have the required columns, and the four derivation subsections use the required labels. The feasibility model correctly separates high-risk/high-value compliance from ordinary close, and it correctly treats K-337 as a decision-policy change rather than a duration trim. That is the strongest part of the answer. Even here the formalization is messy: the “partial-order” block mixes true precedence (`Register < Triage`) with issuance rules (`RequestMoreInfo <- {…}`), four-eyes inequality, and concurrency annotations. Rule 4 is incomplete: Identity Check may precede originals, and Document Check is required *with* originals, not only when originals are absent. The parenthetical on `Triage < DocumentCheck` can be read as if Document Check is conditional on no originals.

**Case table: the outcome column is not usable as written.** “Cycle time 4 to 8h”, “net cycle time 10 to 20h”, “Cycle time 2 to 4 business days”, and “Cycle time 2 to 6h typically” do not state whether the numbers are remaining duration or deltas. K-104’s factual span is Monday 08:10–Wednesday 18:40; a reader can honestly think the author claimed a 4–8 hour case. K-219 then mixes an apparent absolute (“10 to 20h”) with an explicit delta (“+4 to +10h vs factual”) in the same cell. For a counterfactual exercise whose grading axis is temporal consistency, this is a serious presentation failure, not a nit.

**K-104 is not a minimal-change implementation of the stated intervention.** The intervention is: packet to **S1**, reserve a provisional **C1** slot, prelim work may overlap checks, final CR still follows final Assessment. The path drops S1 entirely and invents `PreliminaryComplianceScreen(C1)`. That is a different design. C1 already has a 20h Monday queue and is the only certified reviewer; putting C1 on an early screen is exactly the scarce-resource collision the complication warns about, and it is asserted in the path rather than derived. The 4–8h “cycle time” improvement is not built from the snapshots. Factual CR is 4.5h; the guidance allows 1–3h prelim savings. The extra hours are hand-waved as reservation benefit without saying when the reserved slot actually falls relative to Assessment completion Tuesday 19:10 and C1’s Wednesday 8h backlog. Best case “midday Wednesday instead of evening” is plausible; it is not shown.

**K-219 contains a clear numerical error and a snapshot contradiction.** Originals arrive Monday 17:30. D1 starts Tuesday 09:30 (12.5h later). D2 at Monday 16:00 has 5h forecast wait, so an optimistic continuous-time start is ~21:00 Monday — about 12.5h earlier *start*, not “readiness ~16–20h earlier.” D2’s standard service band is 4–8h versus D1’s factual 4h, so earlier *completion* is roughly 8–13h in the friendly case, not 16–20h. Best-case decision advance of 12–24h is also inflated: factual Decision is already Wednesday 08:30, and M1’s next window may still be Wednesday morning.

Worse, Monday 16:00 D2 = 5h and Tuesday 08:00 D2 = 4h. The document queue barely moved overnight. D1 *did* work overnight (K-104 Mon 21:40–Tue 05:40; K-219 D1 starts 09:30). Treating D2 as free at 21:00 Monday ignores the only occupancy evidence in the prompt. If D2 is mostly day-shift, start slips to Tuesday morning and the intervention can lose to waiting for faster D1. The author lists “D2 has genuinely open capacity at 21:00 Mon” as an assumption instead of testing it. The table worst case (+4 to +10h) also contradicts both the service-time guidance (rework 8–36h external) and the author’s own derivation (a full Rule-7 loop can finish *later* than the factual Wednesday decision).

**K-337 is the best case write-up and still soft on Friday timing.** Suppressing RFI2, forcing approve-with-conditions or reject, preserving CR after the new latest Assessment, and keeping A1 ≠ C1 are all correct. “Removes ~2–4 business days” is in the right band. Friday CR start is still impressionistic (“backlog was clearing”) rather than tied to Thursday’s 5h C1 forecast and likely day-shift behavior. Acceptable uncertainty; not precise.

**K-411 is directionally right and overstates the typical gain.** Creating both tasks at Triage is legal under Rule 3; real-time timestamps are the right integrity constraint; Assessment-queue absorption is correctly identified. Tuesday 08:00 D2 = 4h supports a ~12:00 start versus 15:00. That is ~3h earlier document completion if duration is unchanged. A2 is 6h at Tuesday 08:00 and 5h at Wednesday 08:00; a case ready Tuesday ~16:30 often still waits overnight for Assessment. “Typically 2–6h” end-to-end is the optimistic side of that mechanism, not the typical one. Base 0–3h would have been more honest.

**Capacity section is stronger than the case arithmetic.** C1 reservations as zero-sum, D1↔D2 substitution with a rework threshold, K-337 converting latency into a shifted decision distribution, and K-411 bottleneck migration plus measurement contamination are the right system points. The 100-case write-up hits the required themes. Residual weaknesses: “100 similar cases” is applied to four *different* policies as if they were commensurate rollouts; K-219 at scale is about handwritten/standard work, not “complex/handwritten” as the rollout paragraph later says (complex is D1’s comparative advantage; that slip matters). Weekend effects are mentioned, not quantified.

**Ranking and pilots are reasonable, not compelled.** K-411 > K-219 > K-104 > K-337 is defensible if quality and reversibility dominate. It is not robust to a different loss function: if handwritten clarification rates are low, K-219’s benefit is larger than K-411’s modest concurrency fix; the rank then looks like a preference, not a result. Pilots cover the required rows and have sensible stopping rules. The 25–30% D2 rework trigger is arbitrary and disconnected from the 8–36h rework cost that would justify a much lower threshold.

**Assumption audit.** Eight items, labeled, mostly real. Item 8 (“workarounds can be logged without false concurrency”) is not a resource assumption; it is behavioral/measurement. Missing, and more important than several listed items: calendar vs. business-hour interpretation of queue forecasts. That omitted assumption is load-bearing for K-219 and K-104.

**Net.** The answer knows what a good counterfactual looks like and still fails the thing the prompt most cares about: locally computed, snapshot-consistent times with stable units. Strong structure, several material quantitative and minimal-change errors. That is a mid-to-high 6, not an 8+.
