**7.4**

This is a strong, well-structured investigation that hits the required headings, event-time role logic, alias normalization, SoD, alternative explanations, and a serious PM4Py/pandas design. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading there are factual contradictions, at least one material rule-application error, and several overconfident classifications.

**Factual error that is repeated and then used as a systemic claim.** Diego did **not** implement every case. Clara implemented CHG-508 and CHG-509. The case table itself records this (`RR2 OK (Clara/Eva)` and `Clara implemented, Eva verified`), yet the matrix says Diego is “Implementer in all 10 cases,” the cross-case note says he “implemented every case,” and the executive conclusion repeats “one implementer for every change.” That is not a wording slip; it is used to inflate resource-concentration risk. Section 4’s “nearly everything” is the accurate phrase and contradicts the other summaries. Anyone who actually ran the included pivot/network code would have seen Clara as implementer on the training cases.

**CHG-509 is too generous.** D-QM-02 requires that the principal **must be absent**. Asha’s leave ends 2026-08-14. 15 August is a Saturday with **no recorded absence**. Weekend ≠ formal unavailability. Treating that as `VALID_SUBSTITUTION` (even “conditional”) with medium-high confidence converts an unmet condition into an inference. The right primary label is `INDETERMINATE` or a qualified non-substitution until roster/on-call evidence exists. Encoding the same generosity in code (`weekend_no_formal_evidence` as a soft reason) bakes the error into the checker.

**CHG-506 overreaches.** The prompt forbids treating ambiguous identity evidence as settled. Ben’s denial plus an office IP is a serious indicator, not proof that the act was unauthorized. The same memo correctly holds that leave does not revoke Asha’s QM role on CHG-504; sick leave therefore cannot, by itself, revoke Ben’s delegation if he actually acted. If someone else used `u_bk`, that is identity failure first. Primary `UNAUTHORIZED (probable)` is too strong; primary `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` or `INDETERMINATE` with an unauthorized hypothesis is the consistent stance. Medium confidence does not fully cure the label.

**CHG-501 sits on a tension the interpretation section itself creates.** A later QMS row cannot prove earlier authority; the 08-12 09:15 migration record therefore cannot validate the 08-11 approval. Classifying the case as `VALID_SUBSTITUTION` rests on an unverified PDF “timestamp.” The secondary `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` is the better description of what is actually known. `INDETERMINATE` until the signature/timestamp is shown to be trusted would have matched Section 2 more cleanly.

**CHG-505’s row violates the one-primary-classification rule.** “Approval: VALID_SUBSTITUTION. Case overall: UNAUTHORIZED” is two primaries. Lateness of the retrospective review is a real RR4 fail under a Monday–Friday, two-business-day reading (Friday 08-14 is late if Tuesday 08-11 is the trigger). That point is good. The table still needed one lead label. Calling Ben’s reviews “scope-disputed” is a fair strict reading of D-QM-01 (“QM **approval** for **production-equipment**”), but the code then hard-fails `Retrospective_QM_Review` as `NO AUTHORITY` while the narrative keeps CHG-510’s review in a softer “disputed / timely” bucket. Narrative and checker disagree.

**Business-day wording is slightly sloppy.** `numpy.busday_count(Tue, Fri)` counts weekdays in `[Tue, Fri)` (Tue/Wed/Thu), which is not the same thing as “3 business days vs 2.” The `BusinessDay(2)` deadline test is the actual decision rule and is reasonable; the elapsed-day gloss is not.

**Code is thoughtfully designed but not as clean as claimed.** Strengths: in-memory CSV, `format_dataframe`, alias map before four-eyes, dual STRONG/WEAK service-account handling with a unique `UNATTRIBUTED[...]` token, pandas interval checks, handover/working-together, optional `filter_activity_done_different_resources`, and an explicit list of manual judgments. Weaknesses: `filter_four_eyes_principle(..., resource_key=..., keep_violations=True)` is version-fragile; the TypeError fallback may not be the only failure mode; if a newer API ignores `resource_key`, the weak-attribution RR2 hypothesis is silently wrong; the pandas cross-check covers only `org:resource`, not `resource_hypothesis`; CI-44 is approximated as `cooling_system` with no incident-id predicate; sick-leave and QMS-record lag are soft in code but sometimes hard in the write-up. The “output obtained when executed” paragraph also repeats the Diego-centric network story the dataframe would not fully support. That undercuts the claim of an executed, inspected run.

**What the answer still does well (and why it is not a mid-score paper).** Event-time roles are not read from a current master: Eva is never QM; her Independent Verifier interval ends 08-11; CHG-507 is a clean RR3 miss. Aliases are normalized before SoD, which is the only way CHG-508 is visible. Service accounts are not treated as humans; CHG-503 is unevidenced verification rather than a proven Diego self-verify, with a hypothesis track if the weak session is later confirmed. RR1 is applied at all risk levels and to emergencies (no invented SoD holiday). D-IC-04 is void both for prohibited subdelegation and for email-only evidence, and CHG-510 remains unauthorized even if Farid/Diego were available. RR6 is respected: pair frequency, reciprocity, shared workstation, and 08-13 credential clustering each get a benign and an adverse reading plus discriminating evidence, with no collusion finding. Control improvements map onto the actual failure modes (pre-effective-time recording, person-level SoD, re-auth on GxP actions, incident-linked emergency review).

**Net.** Case-level SoD and most authority calls are careful. The grade is held down because the same document contradicts itself on who implemented the work, treats an unmet absence condition as a valid substitution, and promotes identity ambiguity to unauthorized conduct. Those are not polish issues; they are the exact failure modes the prompt asked the analyst not to make.
