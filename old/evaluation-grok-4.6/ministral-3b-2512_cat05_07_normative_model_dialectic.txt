**3.5**

This looks like a complete seven-part brief and is not one. It repeatedly inverts the record, misunderstands the POWL it is supposed to police, treats the central authority dispute as a stipulated fact, and then delivers a revised model that is neither executable nor faithful to the routes it claims to encode. The essay shape is competent; the process-mining substance is not.

**Facts are altered until they fit the brief.** N4 says about 70% of notifications are *mirrored*; missingness rose from 2% to 29%. The answer converts that into “70% notification logging gaps.” SUP-702’s quality review was the *next* business day, which is inside PD-3’s “up to two business days” window; the prosecution both rewrites it as “two business days after” and then treats “up to two” as if two were a breach. SUP-703 is a high-risk new foreign supplier on 2026-06-20, *inside* the PD-3 window and *ineligible*; the answer calls it low-risk, PD-3-eligible, and/or post-expiry depending on the section. SUP-704 has `Sanctions_Check` before `Activate_Supplier` and is the FD-7 happy path (in window, spend €18k, monitoring, retrospective quality and approval); the matrix claims it violates FD-7’s sanctions-before-activation rule. SUP-712’s aliases resolve to the *same* human; the defense and the judgment say they resolve to different humans. These are not glosses. They are inverted exhibits.

**POWL semantics are wrong in a way the prompt already forecloses.** M0’s `checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[sanctions, quality])` has no order edges. The prompt states that the two checks are concurrent. The defense is built on the claim that M0 “enforces sequential checks,” and later architecture text still confuses concurrency of the two checks with the separate question of quality *after* activation. A process-aware reviewer who cannot read a two-node partial order is not reviewing the model.

**Authority is incoherent across sections.** Section 1 stipulates that PD-3 and FD-7 are “authoritatively binding” in window. That is the dispute, not a fact: PD-3 is minutes-plus-email, never folded into CS-9; the Compliance observer later denied approving a weakening of CS-9; FD-7 expired automatically at 2026-07-12 23:59. The prosecution then treats the same instruments as unauthorized. The judgment accepts 704/705 as justified because the overlays are “better evidence,” then accuses the *prosecution* of overstating their formal authority — the opposite of what the prosecution argued. Expiry is handled loosely: 705 (day after FD-7 died, no writing) and 714 (2 August, pilot dead, “approval imminent”) are graded `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. That is exactly the normalization of exceptions the task told the prosecution to press and the defense to concede.

**Dialectical fairness fails the explicit instructions.** The prosecution does not seriously concede M0’s missing loop, missing resource rule, CRM unlinkability, rejected-lead / reactivation scope, or approved label rename. The defense does not concede SUP-703, SUP-708, the 14 ineligible Auto_Approve cases, Internal Audit’s severe flags, or the limits of informal practice. Aggregate 47 `Request_More_Info` cases *never* activated before evidence arrived; the prosecution invents a “risk of incomplete approvals” the data refute. “Most” of 312 Auto_Approve cases are called unauthorized when 289 are PD-3-shaped — true only if PD-3 is void, which Section 1 has already denied. The system finding is a split-the-difference “partially compliant with defects.” The prompt forbade that move.

**The matrix and scorecard recycle the same errors and then miscount them.** 702 is not a PD-3 timing breach. 703’s dominant reason is written as post-expiry. 704 is not an FD-7 sanctions failure. 705 is too generously justified. 708’s defense (“data error”) is unevidenced; confidence is marked Low *because* of that invention. 712 should be resource noncompliance or MIXED, not a justified exception. 714 is unauthorized continuation, not an exception. 715 is at least arguable (disclosure duty, no explicit spouse ban), but the defense cell (“justified because disclosure was not recorded”) is circular. MIXED is never used where it is the honest label (712, arguably 710/713/715). The judgment then says five cases are noncompliant and six are justified while listing three and five.

**Section 6–7 are the required constructive deliverable, and they fail on their own terms.** The “revised POWL” is not a POWL. It depends on undefined names (`pilot_eligibility`, `emergency_flag`, `more_info_iterations`), uses Python `if/elif` as if they were process operators, and puts the same `quality` / `activate` `Transition` objects in `standard_checks`, `auto_branch`, and `emergency_branch`. `standard_checks` is a mandatory predecessor of `decision`, so every route — including reject, auto-approve, and emergency — still requires quality *before* the XOR. That makes the auto and emergency branches impossible to execute as specified (quality-after-activate) and forces quality twice if the child branches are read literally. `Auto_Approve` is constructed and then omitted from `auto_branch`. Emergency never includes retrospective `Manual_Procurement_Approval`. `Request_More_Info` is a `SilentTransition` even though it is a logged activity; `Operator.LOOP` is never used; the “loop” is a runtime counter comment. The pandas sketch uses placeholder `...` rows, 2024 timestamps for 2026 cases, and a wrong emergency flag on SUP-703. The code would not run, and if the conditionals were deleted it would still not represent the minimum routes the prompt listed.

**What is actually good is narrow:** 701 and 713 are handled; 706/711 as data-quality problems are reasonable; 707/709/710 as model or scope defects are in the right family; 703 and 708 are at least classed noncompliant; the idea of layering POWL / data constraints / organizational overlays is the right architecture even though the encoding is not.

That is a structured, fluent answer with broken exhibits, broken model theory, broken authority logic, and a non-executable revision. Under the stated rubric it cannot sit in the middle of the scale.
