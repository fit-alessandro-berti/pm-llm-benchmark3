The answer has the right skeleton and clears the three explicit “strong answer” checkpoints (I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is not treated like I-07, spillovers are mentioned). Almost everything that has to be numerically or logically consistent then fails. Under a strict reading this is a structured but non-deployable policy with a broken objective, non-reproducible scores, and a selected portfolio that contradicts the author’s own table.

### Decision rule is not deployable

The stated score is missing operators:

`Score = (p_now × Risk-Reduction_Midpoint × Value)  (False-Alarm_Burden + Swarm_Cost)  (Segment_Adjustment × Calibration_Uncertainty)`

That is not a rule an operations team can implement. Two further design errors make it theoretically wrong even after the operators are guessed.

1. **Multiplying by `p_now` is a decision-theoretic mistake.** Absolute risk reduction is already a change in breach probability. The prompt’s own estimator is `ARR × value − cost − false-alarm burden`. Using `p_now × ARR × value` double-counts probability and is a confused version of treating the observational score as if it were the causal effect.
2. **The spillover term is dimensionally nonsense.** Subtracting `0.02 × 6 = 0.12` from a euro-denominated score does not represent “each used slot raises breach probability by 0.01–0.03 on six untreated diagnosis-stage cases.” A coherent penalty is on the order of `slots × 6 × Δp × (typical untreated case value)`, i.e. thousands of euros, not €0.12.

Repeated-alert logic is internally inconsistent: suppression unless `p_now > p_prev + 0.15`, then a second “minimum evidence change” of `+0.10`. Those cannot both be the rule.

Calibration adjustments are also wrong or unused. SME *underprediction* of 0.04–0.09 should *raise* assessed risk; multiplying the score by 0.8 does the opposite. Strategic Enterprise overprediction is only crudely handled by ×0.9 on the whole score rather than a shift on `p_now`. Non-native-language support is penalized ×0.7 in §1 and then given a *higher* weight in §5. None of these multipliers appear in the §2 arithmetic.

The rule says reserve one slot. Section 2 uses four slots and reserves zero.

### Expected-impact table is not auditable in the sense the prompt asked

“Direct breaches avoided” as integers 4 / 3 / 1 treats probabilistic ARR as if every selected case is saved. Optimistic “`(€9k+€25k+€60k+€15k) × 0.75 = €105k`” implies an ARR of 0.75 on every case, which is far outside every supplied range (max 0.40). Base uses `× 0.5` the same way and quietly drops I-06 from benefits while still charging four × €450. Adverse uses `€60k × 0.25` even though I-04’s lower bound is 0.10, not 0.25.

Order-of-magnitude check the author should have shown:

- Base direct benefit for the four named cases ≈ `0.265×9k + 0.15×25k + 0.19×60k + 0.225×15k ≈ €21k` before false-alarm burdens, not €57k.
- Four slots × six untreated diagnosis cases × 0.01–0.03 is a **+0.04 to +0.12** breach-probability hit on each of those six cases. Even at a €10–20k average untreated value, spillover is thousands to tens of thousands of euros, not Optimistic €0–300 or Base €600–1,800.
- Net ranges such as Base “€54k–€55k” are spuriously tight given heterogeneous effects, calibration bands, I-06 imputation, and congestion.

False-alarm burden is omitted from the impact table entirely. “High” confidence on the Base row is incompatible with the prompt’s instruction not to treat the ranges as known causal effects.

### Temporal policy: the I-05 / I-07 distinction is asserted, then violated

Cool-down and “new event vs model refresh” are the right ideas, and I-05 is correctly suppressed. I-07 is not handled cleanly. Section 1 gives new evidence a 50% priority boost; section 4 says defer only if `p_now` drops below 0.70; I-07 is at 0.72 — and section 2 still waits. The new log also *lowered* predicted risk (0.75 → 0.72), which a careful policy would treat as possible good news, not an automatic escalate. There is no explicit post-swarm re-entry rule even though repeated swarms are “normally forbidden unless a materially new event has occurred.”

### Fairness and monitoring: headings without operational teeth

The five required risks are named. The safeguards are weak or contradictory:

- “One slot/week for low-value high-urgency cases” does not bind *today*, and I-07 (1.5 h to SLA, €50 false-alarm burden) is exactly that case and was deferred.
- SME +0.10 in §5 reverses the §1 ×0.8 penalty and was applied to nobody.
- You cannot “cap spillover to 0.01” by policy; you can only use fewer slots. I-03 is already past diagnosis, so “I-03’s peers” is the wrong spillover population.
- Non-monetary / public-sector obligations are essentially absent beyond the unused reserve preference.
- Selective-label bias is not actually fixed by “compare intervention vs no-intervention breach rates.” Treated cases are selected on risk and then causally altered; that comparison is confounded. Missing: treatment flags, freeze rules for training labels, holdouts on the margin.

Monitoring cadence is generic. The causal-estimation plan is the serious flaw: difference-in-differences on pre/post **`p_now`** estimates a change in a model score, not a change in breach. `p_now` is observational and not the potential-outcome probability under intervention — the prompt already said this. Proposed A/B tests ignore that leftover capacity will not be a random sample of critical incidents. Recalibration trigger “spillover harm > €500/case” is disconnected from any coherent spillover calculation in the memo. Daily “alert if <1 slot reserved” would fire on the author’s own allocation.

### What keeps this from collapsing below ~4

I-03 is not funded because `p_now` is high. I-05’s stale third alert is not treated like I-07’s new log. There is an attempt at portfolio scenarios, segment uncertainty, and feedback loops rather than a single probability threshold. That is the difference between a weak outline and a failing one. It is not enough for a high score: a live policy whose scores cannot be reproduced, whose ranking does not determine allocation, whose SME adjustment has the wrong sign, and whose impact table invents ARRs of 0.50–0.75 is not decision-theoretically coherent and not operationally realistic.
