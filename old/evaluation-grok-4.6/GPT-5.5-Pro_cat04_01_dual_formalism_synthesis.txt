## What is actually correct

The POWL edge set is the right one: `Register` forks to `Contain`/`Analyze`; `Contain → Plan`; `Analyze → Design`, `Analyze → Risk`, `Analyze → Supplier`; `Plan` and `Design` both precede the trial loop; `Trial`, `Risk`, and `Supplier` join at `Release`; `Release → Archive`. Unordered pairs are the ones that should be unordered. The risk XOR, optional supplier XOR, and do-redo trial loop are the right control-flow stand-ins for data that must not appear as labels.

The tree’s phase join — `(Contain → Plan) || (Analyze → Design)`, then `(Risk || Supplier? || Trial)` — is a genuine language subset of the intended process. Trial start, release join, loop shape, and exclusive risk routes are preserved; early approvals and early supplier work are excluded. That is a defensible conservative policy, and A4 is a real counterexample to exactness rather than a hand-wavy claim.

A1–A5 and F1–F3 satisfy the coverage constraints (low risk, high risk, supplier, two trial iterations, tree approximation, cross-branch forbidden). F1’s earliest violation is correctly identified as `Execute Trial` before `Plan Verification`. Loop semantics in both models forbid F3.

## Material deductions

**Approximation wording is sloppy.** Assumption 5 says the tree excludes approvals/supplier work that “complete immediately after root-cause analysis but before the plan/design … join.” The excluded set is larger: any review or `Consult Supplier` whose completion occurs before *both* `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action`. “Immediately after” is false. Section 3 states the real constraint more carefully than Section 1, so the policy statement is internally inconsistent.

**D3 is the wrong kind of row.** A source/target table should not encode “Contain Material → Analyze Root Cause / concurrent.” Concurrency is not a directed precedence. D17 then restates overlap again. The intended-dependency section is otherwise good; this row is conceptually messy.

**Exact process-tree impossibility is assumed, not shown.** The task asked for an explicit defense when an exact tree is impossible. The answer never states the non-series-parallel reason: `Analyze` forks to design and approvals, `Plan`/`Design` join at trial, approvals and trial join only at release. That N-shape cannot be a process tree without extra order, missing order, or duplicated labels. The approximation is reasonable; the justification is incomplete.

**Self-critique is not residual.** Weakness 1 restates the central, already-advertised tree policy. Weakness 3 is true of any completion-log model and is not a defect of this encoding. The one real residual semantic gap they *should* have listed — XOR is observationally more liberal than a data-determined risk/supplier decision; validation never distinguishes “could happen” from “would happen on this case” — is only gestured at elsewhere.

**“True concurrency” is oversold.** With completion-only labels, both models specify linear extensions of a partial order. Calling this “true concurrency at the model level” is stronger than what the artifacts support. Self-critique 3 later admits this, which means Section 1 overclaims and Section 8 walks it back.

## Smaller issues

- LOOP do-redo (`body`, then optional `Adjust` + `body`) is used correctly but never pinned down in the assumptions, even though the entire pass/fail abstraction depends on it.
- The tree code is self-contained, but introducing `leaf` / `tau_leaf` / `op` is extra machinery the prompt did not need; not a violation, just noise.
- Vestigial GitHub footnotes do not help semantics and slightly violate the clean eight-heading deliverable.
- Section 6’s first row treats the tree’s `(Contain → Plan) || (Analyze → Design)` as a faithful encoding of the *initial* two streams. The Contain/Analyze overlap is faithful; bundling successors into that same parallel block is already the approximation. The table is too generous there.
- Validation is a sensible checklist, not evidence. That matches the requested plan, but phrases like “PM4Py exposes … in current documentation” are filler. There is no discussion of how alignment would treat the supplier `tau` versus a missing `Consult Supplier` event — the one silent-behavior pitfall that actually matters.
- Compact POWL is a sketch, not a parseable object, while the tree expression pretends to be near-`parse_process_tree`. Uneven rigor.

## What keeps the score from falling further

There is no fatal modeling error in either construction, no mislabeled required witness, and no disguised representational loss. The release join, optional supplier, high-risk unordered pair, and repeat-until inspection abstraction are all handled in the standard, inspectable way. Under a normal rubric this would be closer to 9. Under the required strictness, the imprecise policy statement, the D3 confusion, the missing impossibility argument, and the weak self-critique are enough to move it firmly out of the top band.
