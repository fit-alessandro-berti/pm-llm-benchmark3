**7.4**

The answer is a strong, well-structured dialectic with unusually good case hygiene, but it is not nearly flawless. Several process-semantics and authority mistakes are real, not cosmetic.

**What it does well.** The stipulated/disputed split is mostly clean, the burden rule is stated in operable form, and both briefs argue at full strength without strawmen. The prosecution correctly treats expiry, eligibility override, and M0-invisible SoD/COI failures as the core case; the defense correctly treats concurrency, the missing loop, overlay lag, CRM loss, reactivation, rejected leads, and label drift as yardstick failures. The matrix is complete, including 712/715. Classifications generally follow the stated burden rather than averaging the two briefs. The layered revision (POWL vs DECLARE vs pandas vs overlay registry) is the right architecture for this record.

**Model-semantics errors in M0/M1.** M0 analysis is mostly accurate (nested `checks` as a true partial order; both checks before the XOR, including reject; sequential 701 is conformant). M1 is not. The auto and emergency routes *force* `Activate_Supplier → Quality_Review`. PD-3 and FD-7 only *permit* deferred quality (“may occur up to two business days after”); a more conservative trace with quality before activation is still allowed and should replay. As a normative language of permitted behavior, that is an over-restriction, not a defensible approximation, and it is not disclosed as a limitation.

Worse, the auto route requires `Master_Data_Validation`, an activity that appears in **no** observed case. Eligible in-window traces such as 702/703/714 cannot replay on M1 at all. The emergency route replaces `Manual_Procurement_Approval` with `Retrospective_Manual_Approval` and adds `Record_Emergency_Authority` without a mapping rule, so 704—the textbook justified exception—also fails M1. Those choices are fine as a *prospective* workflow only if labeled as such; presented as the revised normative model, they break historical conformance. “Verified executable against PM4Py 2.7.x” is an unsupported claim (no run, no version pin beyond a comment).

Further M1 gaps: `Request_More_Info` exists only on standard and auto routes, so an emergency or reject case with the endorsed loop is unexplained; daily monitoring is entirely off-model; reactivations are deferred to an undefined `M1-R`. Duplicate same-label transitions are acknowledged, but the MDV/QR-order/label-break issues are not.

**Authority and burden are not applied evenly.** The brief states that a justified exception needs written authority from a competent body, in force, with eligibility and compensating controls. 702 is still called `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` despite PD-3 being minutes plus email, never folded into CS-9, and later disavowed by the Compliance observer. Capping confidence is not the same as meeting the test the answer itself announced. 705 is then failed for missing paperwork. The distinction (window vs instrument) is real, but the write-up treats D1 as if it were a hygiene footnote rather than a failed element of the exception burden.

Absence-of-record is treated charitably for notification (706 → indeterminate, citing 29% systemic loss) and harshly for COI disclosure (715 → noncompliant, high confidence) without saying why the logging failure mode differs. That is a proof-standard slip. 711 as `COMPLIANT` is also slightly too clean: the raw trace is not M0-conformant; the better primary label is data-quality with a compliant interpretation *after* dedup.

**Smaller inaccuracies.** Defense use of “92.6% eligible vindicating the pilot” underplays that 14 ineligible plus 9 unknown is already a failed gate, not a rounding error. 708’s matrix line (“QR before activation is fine”) is true of CS-9/PD-3/FD-7 but underspecifies the full M0 break (activate before the `checks` node completes, and manual approval after activate). 713 as `COMPLIANT` rather than a scoped label/mapping defect is contestable: the mapping was approved *after* the case. None of these sink the judgment, but they are sloppy.

**Net.** Analytic fairness, traceability, and system-level refusal to let a bad ruler excuse 703/708 or let 703 validate M0 are well above average. The revised POWL—the one artifact that must be process-correct—contains avoidable normative errors and silent incompatibilities with the very justified traces the opinion endorses. Under a strict reading, that keeps the score well short of the top band.
