**6.4**

The answer is structurally complete and passes the three headline quality tests (I-03 excluded on stage, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers treated as first-class). Under a normal rubric it would sit in the high 7s. Under the required hypercritical standard it is not close to flawless: the decision rule is not actually deployable, the live allocation contradicts the rule’s own robustness/fairness clauses, and the impact table is arithmetically inconsistent with both the prompt formula and Section 1.

**Decision rule.** Stage gating, evidence-change criteria, and refusal of a raw `p` threshold are sound. The calibration bands are directionally right. Everything else is underspecified or broken on the page. Operators in the priority formula are missing (`Value  SwarmCost`, `p_now  0.05`), so the “deployable rule” cannot be executed. `ConfidenceWeight`, `UrgencyWeight`, and `SpilloverExternality` are unnamed functions with no numeric schedule. Spillover is a portfolio externality; subtracting it inside a per-case score mixes units. Value is capped “for ranking only,” but no worked scores are shown, so the cap is rhetorical. Segment corrections are applied to `p` and then largely abandoned: ranking is `ARR × Value`, so the carefully stated SME/SE adjustments do not actually change the ordering except as later prose. ARR is still treated as a usable midpoint despite the instruction not to treat supplied ranges as known causal effects; the caveat is verbal, not operational (no robust/lower-bound ranking, no interval dominance check).

**Allocation.** I-03 is correctly killed. I-06 hold-for-verification and I-08 wait (weak ARR, €5k false-alarm burden, SE overprediction, lower-bound EV negative) are the best calls in the memo. The selected book I-04 / I-02 / I-09 plus a soft reserve is only partly justified.

- I-04 as **Highest** and **Intervene now** fights the author’s own urgency weight. It is still in Triage with 10h of runway. The swarm is only useful before `Diagnosis Complete`; option value of waiting one cycle is the right decision-theoretic move and is applied to I-01/I-08 but not to the highest-value SE case. SE overprediction also cuts I-04’s `p` into the mid-0.3s. Spending a scarce slot immediately is not compelled by the evidence.
- I-09 as a firm intervene contradicts Section 5’s own safeguard (“human sign-off before committing scarce slots” to NNL). After the stated 0.5–0.7 confidence shrink, I-09’s mid EV is no longer clearly above I-01 or a verified I-06. Putting the least-identified effect in a three-slot book that the author later shows can go net-negative is not robust. A coherent maximin/base-case rule would swap I-09 for I-01 (calibrated Public Sector, complete data) or keep the slot in reserve until I-06 is verified.
- Reallocation of the reserve to **I-07 ahead of I-01** is a second ranking error. I-07’s mid EV is ~€800, it is still in Triage with 1.5h left, and a 45-minute swarm may not finish before the deadline. I-01 is higher EV, better identified, and operationally feasible. Time-criticality is not a reason to burn a slot on a low-stakes case that may already be infeasible.
- Tiers are asserted, not computed. Without a score column the table is not auditable against the formula in Section 1.
- I-05 suppression is right on evidence, but the memo underplays that I-05 has never been swarmed and has the widest ARR in the book plus SME underprediction. Escalation to a *non-swarm* owner review is correct; calling the case low-priority rather than “eligible but cooldown-blocked” blurs that.

**Expected-impact table.** The three-scenario frame and the qualitative conclusion (base positive, adverse can flip) are the right shape. The arithmetic is not.

The prompt formula is `ARR × Value − swarm cost − false-alarm burden`. FA on the selected set is €2,000+€600+€800 = €3,400 and is incurred if you swarm, not only if you are wrong. Optimistic and base subtract only €1,350 labor. Optimistic “~€22,800 net” should be ~€19,375 before spillover; base “~€18,600” should be ~€15,200. Adverse subtracts only I-09’s €800. That is an inconsistent cost model, not a scenario difference.

The benefit column is labeled “range” and filled with point estimates. Optimistic spillover text (`3 × 6 × ~0.015 × €20k`) is €5,400, but the cell says €3,000–5,000. The €20–25k queue value-at-risk is invented; if spillover lands on untreated SME/public cases the harm is overstated, and the fairness section later claims exactly that concentration. Breach-equivalents treat ARR midpoints as additive and independent; no covariance, no “one reason-for-alert is wrong” except the I-09 zero in adverse. Confidence labels are reasonable; the audit trail is not.

**Temporal policy.** The I-05 vs I-07 contrast is the strongest page in the answer and matches the grading key. Cooldown / suppression / non-swarm escalation / re-entry is operationally realistic. Gaps: no explicit post-*swarm* lock (the process rule forbids repeat swarms without a material new event); cooldown duration is “until next cycle” rather than a time or stage rule; I-07’s slight `p` drop is correctly ignored, but there is no feasibility gate (can a triage case with 1.5h left absorb a 45-minute swarm?).

**Fairness.** All five required risks appear, and the safeguards are concrete (value cap, 1-in-4 low-value quota, NNL audit, treatment flags, 1-in-10 holdout, spillover-by-segment tracking, compensating bump). Two problems. First, the live policy does not implement them: I-09 is taken without sign-off; I-04/I-02/I-09 is a high-value/SE-NNL book; I-05/I-07/I-10 are parked. Second, a 10% deliberate holdout among “otherwise-eligible high-priority” cases is expensive at four slots a day and is not costed against the same spillover/EV machinery. Public-sector non-monetary duty is almost absent (I-01 is treated as a backup € case).

**Monitoring.** Coverage is complete at the checklist level (ops, calibration, causal, capacity, fairness, revision triggers). It is not an estimation design. Matching on `p`, stage, and segment after a value-and-urgency policy will not identify ARR; the holdout is too small to re-estimate heterogeneous effects quarterly; there is no pre-registered stopping rule, no process-compliance check (swarm after `Diagnosis Complete`), and no specialist-load metric beyond “track interruptions.” Policy-induced label shift is named, which is more than most answers do.

**Net.** This is a competent policy memo with the right architecture and the right refusals, damaged by a non-implementable score, an allocation that violates its own uncertainty and fairness rules, and an impact table that drops a first-order cost term. Those are not polish issues. They are the difference between a coherent decision system and a well-written rationalization of a high-EV heuristic.
