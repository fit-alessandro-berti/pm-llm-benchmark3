**7.4**

The core modeling work is strong: the POWL captures the non-block-structured dependencies with the right edges, the process tree is an honest conservative under-approximation rather than a disguised one, A5 actually exhibits the tree/POWL split, and the required format is followed. Under a hypercritical standard that still leaves several avoidable reasoning errors and a few semantic imprecisions.

**F3 misidentifies the earliest violation.**  
`… Execute Trial → Inspect Trial → Adjust Parameters → Release Material …` is a valid *prefix* through `Adjust Parameters`. Taking the redo branch is exactly how a failed inspection is represented. The first event that is not a valid continuation is `Release Material` (the redo body was never followed by another `Execute Trial → Inspect Trial`). Pinning the violation on `Adjust Parameters` and then adding “also AP must not occur after a passing inspection” mixes two incompatible readings of the same unlabeled inspection. The task explicitly required the earliest violated condition; this is a real logic error, not a wording nit.

**Section 6 overstates what the tree gets wrong.**  
Forcing `Plan Verification` to complete before the trial is *intended* (D11), not an overconstraint. The extra barrier is that the same join also delays *approvals and supplier consultation*. Bundling “approvals/supplier/trial” as one overconstraint blurs an exact fragment with an approximate one. The risk-route row also calls data-free XOR “overapprox” even though the prompt required that abstraction; that is not a model defect relative to the stated task.

**The tree approximation is a chosen trade-off, not the only block-structured option.**  
Putting the approval/supplier XOR in a second parallel *after* `PV ∥ DCA` preserves trial–approval overlap and release join, at the cost of early starts after `Analyze Root Cause`. The dual encoding (approvals inside the analysis branch) would allow early reviews and instead forbid overlapping the trial. Duplication could recover more of the language. Assumptions 4–5 mention duplication, but they never state this trade-off, nor that the tree also delays the *risk-route choice itself*, not only the review activities. That is extra representational loss beyond “reviews start late.”

**A5 is an interpretive stretch that is never owned as such.**  
`Supervisor Review` before `Contain Material` is allowed by the letter of the precedence rules and by the POWL, but the write-up never flags that “reviews may finish before containment even starts” is a modeling decision, not something the narrative asserts. A conservative reading could have treated that as undesirable; calling it simply “intended” is too clean.

**Self-critique is recycled.**  
The three points restate already-disclosed limitations (tree barrier, missing data, finite witnesses). They do not catch F3’s earliest-violation error, the PV/trial conflation, or the unexamined alternative tree encodings—the actual residual weaknesses of *this answer*.

**Smaller issues that still cost points.**  
D10/D16 leave supplier overlap with planning, design, approvals, and trial implicit rather than listing it as a relation. Assumption 2 says consultation “may complete at any point after” analysis, which is looser than the start constraint. The validation plan is well-specified but entirely prospective; the `tau` / `StrictPartialOrder([...])` constructor dialect is asserted, not shown to execute. None of these would matter much if F3 and Section 6 were exact.

**What remains clearly good, and why this is not lower.**  
The POWL edge set is the right one: `CM → PV`, `ARC → {DCA, approval, supplier}`, `{PV, DCA} → trial`, `{trial, approval, supplier} → RM`, with QR/RR unordered and trial unordered w.r.t. approvals/supplier. Loop semantics `*(ET→IT, AP)` / `LOOP(PO(ET→IT), AP)` correctly encode pass-as-exit and never-AP-after-pass. No unjustified label duplication. Witnesses cover low risk, high risk, supplier, two-iteration rework, a cross-branch dependency (F1: trial before `Plan Verification`), and the tree under-approximation (A5). Those are the hard parts of the task, and they are largely correct.
