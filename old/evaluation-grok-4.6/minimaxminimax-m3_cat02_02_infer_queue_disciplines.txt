**6.2**

The write-up has the right shape for this task: it reconstructs eligibility sets, compares FIFO / priority / SPT / appointment (and, where relevant, resume and EDF), resists the usual trap of reading every FIFO inversion as priority, and keeps uncertainty visible. The required seven-section skeleton is present and usable. That is a competent process-mining answer, not a surface labeling exercise. Under a strict standard it is still well short of excellent, because several errors sit in the exact place the rubric cares about—decision sets, competing predictions, and what would actually discriminate them.

**Section 1 misuses its own columns.** For QA the stated primary rule is FIFO, but the “contradicting” episode is A02 beating A04 and A08 finishing last. Those episodes *support* FIFO and contradict priority. The episode that actually contradicts FIFO is A03 starting at 08:20 ahead of earlier A02. For QD, skill-based routing is the primary label, yet D10 last and D06→D-X are filed as contradictions; both are skill-feasible and do not undercut routing. The table therefore overstates how cleanly each primary label is identified.

**There are factual mistakes in the reconstructed decisions.** At 08:58 the QA table says A06 has not arrived; A06 arrived at 08:29. The error does not change the 08:58 pick (A04 still wins under FIFO, priority, and SPT), but it is an invented waiting-set. In QC the EDF column is wrong at two non-trivial times: at 10:18 and 10:32 the earliest deadline among eligible jobs is C05 (10:50), not C03 or C01. Those are exactly the moments that should have been used to separate SPT from deadline-driven choice. Getting the competitor’s prediction wrong weakens the later claim that C05 is a clean “deadline-missed promotion.”

**A binding QD decision is missing.** After D03 ends at 11:36, D-F is free until the 12:00 meeting. D10 is already waiting (priority 1, electrical, 12 minutes) and would finish before the meeting. D-F instead idles, and D10 later starts on D-X at 12:16. That is one of the few places skill, priority, SPT, reservation-for-D05, and “do not start a job that would hit a break” can be pulled apart. Treating QD as almost settled FIFO-within-skill, at confidence 85, ignores that hole. The 11:20–11:30 D-X idle (hold for D06’s appointment rather than start D07, the only mech+electrical job) is likewise under-played as a decision.

**Internal consistency on data quality is poor.** Section 5 claims delayed scans cannot reorder anything because the smallest gap is four minutes (A02–A03). QD arrivals are one to three minutes apart, and two QB events sit exactly on the two-minute bound (B02 at 09:14 / preemption 09:16; B05 at 09:46 / B01 stop 09:48). Test A then uses a delayed scan to hide A04 at 08:20, which is impossible: A04 arrived at 08:13, seven minutes earlier. The same constraint is used carefully in one place and carelessly in two others.

**Diagnoses are directionally defensible, not tight.** QA as FIFO with an appointment override is the best-supported of the four; A03 is the only appointment that actually changes order, and A06’s late start is just FIFO behind A05. QB as preemptive priority plus resume-unless-P1 is a reasonable hybrid, and the B02 preemption plus the two B01 resumes are real evidence—but B03 starting at 09:48 while B05 (P1) is already waiting is a hard problem for that story, and “dispatcher reassignment” is left half-interpreted. QC as SPT plus a deadline patch is fair through C06; C01 over C07 at 11:40 is correctly flagged as a genuine anomaly. Calling that “long-job deferral” sits badly next to C05 (longer than C01) having been started earlier. Observational equivalence between P1, missed deadline, and a one-off hold is acknowledged in prose more than it is enforced in the table.

**Later sections are stronger than the core tables.** The counterfactual tests have the right *form* (two theories, one extra observable), especially B and C. Section 6 is well tailored: eligibility snapshots, preemption codes, revised remaining time, and a C01 eligibility flag are the right next measurements. Section 7’s ranking (QD most identifiable, QC least, global priority the most dangerous inference) is plausible; “priority is decorative everywhere except some QB preemptions” is the one cross-queue conclusion the log actually supports.

What keeps the score from falling further is that the author is clearly working from waiting sets, not from start-order folklore, and does not force a single mechanism onto all four queues. What keeps it from rising is that a grading pass that checks eligibility sets and competitor predictions finds multiple concrete errors, one important missed decision, and a diagnosis table that does not mean what its column headers say. That is solid mid-tier work, not a near-flawless reconstruction.
