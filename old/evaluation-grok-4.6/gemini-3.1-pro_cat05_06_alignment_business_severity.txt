The write-up is structured correctly and gets the central diagnostic point right: raw alignment cost is edit distance, not risk, and the dangerous cases are the zero-cost policy breaches plus irreversible payment-before-approval. Under a strict reading of the required analysis, the answer has repeated classification errors, underspecified uncertainty, and several missed mandatory deliverables.

**Materiality framework.** The dimensions requested are present and the severity/confidence distinction is stated clearly. It remains generic. It does not say how the dimensions trade off when they collide (small amount + hard fraud control; high amount + weak evidence; disputed scope). “Severity 1–5” is never anchored to those trade-offs, which later shows up as inconsistent scoring.

**Case-level errors and overclaiming.**
- CL-602 is treated as a proven bypass. The log is missing `Fraud Screen`; the approver claims a verbal check; policy says a missing event is not proof of non-occurrence. “Actual operational deviation / confidence High / bypassed” overstates the evidence. Residual investigation uncertainty belongs in the row.
