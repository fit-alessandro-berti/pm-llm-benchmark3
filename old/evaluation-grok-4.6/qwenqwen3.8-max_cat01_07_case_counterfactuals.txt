**7.6**

The final write-up (from `## 1. Feasibility model` onward) is a strong, format-compliant answer. It is not close to flawless under a strict reading.

**What is actually good**

Format compliance is high. The eight required headings, table schemas, case subsections, labeled paragraphs, eight typed assumptions, and both word-count bands are present. The feasibility model correctly refuses a single linear sequence and distinguishes timing (K-104), routing (K-219), decision policy (K-337), and concurrency/logging (K-411). The central evaluation traps are handled: four-eyes is preserved; final compliance still follows final assessment; queue snapshots are treated as forecasts; earlier readiness is not equated with earlier completion; deleting the second rework loop is treated as a policy change, not a free time cut; local gains are allowed to migrate congestion and risk.

**Material weaknesses**

The quantitative scaffolding is looser than the prose suggests. K-104’s table allows a ~12 h best-case cut, while the derivation’s “midday Wednesday” finish is closer to 6–8 h from a Wed 18:40 notify. A 12 h cut would push Decision/Notify into an overnight window that M1 never occupies in the data. K-411’s 1–12 h band has the same problem: a 12 h cut from Wed 14:30 implies a nocturnal M1 decision unless Assessment and Decision both move in ways the snapshots do not support.

Snapshot numbers are cited more than used. Mon 16:00 D2 = 5 h plus originals at 17:30 should have pinned a D2 start (late Monday vs Tuesday morning) instead of “soon after originals.” Tue 08:00 A2 = 6 h and Wed 08:00 A2 = 5 h should have bounded how much earlier Assessment can actually start. C1’s 20→18→16→8→5 h path is acknowledged as scarce, but K-104 still quietly spends C1 twice—preliminary screen *and* a reserved later slot—without a calendar that shows those hours coming from somewhere.

K-219 overreaches at the single-case level (“D1 underused”) and treats Document-Check-initiated clarification as if it were a first-class RMI even though Rule 6 only authorizes Assessment/Compliance to issue it. The problem text invites that reading, but a strict answer should have said the clarification is a quality defect that *then* forces Assessment to open RMI, not that D2 itself opens the loop.

**Smaller faults**

The 100-case section hits every required topic but is schematic: four parallel paragraphs, little interaction among reservation idle time, D1/D2 mix, Friday cutoff effects, and assessment absorption. Ranking K-337 last on robustness is defensible; calling K-219 more robust than K-337 is also defensible, but the robustness column does not really stress-test K-411 against a saturated A2 queue, which is the main way that intervention fails. Several “expected outcome ranges” remain plausible stories rather than derived intervals.

This is a competent, well-structured counterfactual memo with the right conceptual commitments. It is not a tight, snapshot-constrained, internally consistent derivation.
