**6.3**

The final write-up hits the required seven headings and is directionally the right kind of answer: it does not treat the redesign as automatically good or bad, it does not collapse everything to mean cycle time, and it treats East as a different case from North/South. The remaining defects are not cosmetic. Several of them break the analytical claim.

**Format and coverage.** The skeleton matches the prompt. Mechanism map, three scenarios, indicator table, hidden-risk section, pilot, conditional recommendation, and a code sketch are all present. That is the main reason the score is not lower. Coverage is not the same as correctness.

**Section 1 is conceptually sloppy.** The prompt’s own expected routing table does **not** show a general handover reduction. Local-to-tech rises 36→41, tech-back-to-caseworker 31→36, approvals 42→49, legal/risk 9→12. Only cross-team cover falls (6→3). Calling 36→41 a “nominal handover reduction” is wrong. A careful answer would say: management *claims* fewer local specialist loops, while its own frequencies imply more, longer, more centralized handovers and a change in *type* (local loop → hub-and-spoke). That contradiction is the whole forecasting problem. The map also misuses SNA objects: `discover_working_together_network` is co-case collaboration, not utilization; `get_service_time` is activity/case service duration, not resource occupancy. Utilization needs busy/idle or occupancy analysis, not those two calls.

**Sections 2–4 are the strongest part, with residual thinness.** Optimistic / central / adverse are complete against the required axes, and East vs North/South is carried through. The adverse over-routing story (55/100) is plausible. Still: the staff’s best mechanism — that the same central pool also absorbs legal/risk triage *and* hard Quality Check — is underweighted. Exception-handling is muddled: fewer absence-driven cross-team variants can be a *benefit* of a shared pool; the real hidden risk is a single central failure mode, not “variants disappear therefore resilience collapses.” Several numbers (9h, 55/100, +2–3d tails) are asserted rather than derived from the given waits, utilizations (Legal/Risk already 91–93%, E_Tech 82%), or queueing logic.

**Section 3 is usable but mis-timed.** Waiting time after the new hub, queue tail, reassignment count, and segmented waiting-time gaps are the right leading measures. Role/activity entropy and learning loss can start moving as soon as routing changes; parking them as “months-only lagging” is too slow. Fairness gaps in waiting time are detectable in weeks and should have been leading, not only lagging. `local_expertise_flag` and `handover_reason` are in the log and underused.

**Section 5 has a real design error.** Piloting only East, the team already most likely to gain (E_Tech at 82%, worst cycle time), is selection bias. North as “control” cannot identify the North/South expertise-loss harm that the same section later uses as a rollback trigger. “If North/South show degradation” is not identifiable under the stated design. A competent pilot would treat North or South (or a complexity slice *inside* a strong local team) as the treated unit, or use a crossed/phased design that can falsify the East-only benefit. Guardrails and feedback are otherwise specific enough.

**Section 6 is appropriately cautious** (pilot first; do not read early averages as success). It does not repair the biased pilot it inherits.

**Section 7 is the largest technical failure.** The prompt listed high-level PM4Py functions; the sketch largely invents modules that do not exist (`pm4py.algo.discovery.handover_workflow`, `...working_together`, `...subcontracting`, `...discovery.performance.dfg`). SNA in PM4Py lives under organizational mining, and the intended calls are `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_subcontracting_network`, `discover_performance_dfg`. Further API errors: `format_dataframe` has no `team_key` / `resource_key`; `filter_event_attribute_values` takes a value set, not a lambda, and is the wrong tool for a time split (`filter_time_range` or a timestamp mask); discovery is applied to a DataFrame as if that were interchangeable with a log. Required analyses are incomplete: `required_specialty` and `customer_segment` are named and then not filtered; SLA misses are promised and not computed; before/after objects are built and never compared. `performance_spectrum` is imported and unused. For a section whose only job is a PM4Py sketch against a given API list, this is not a minor slip.

**Net.** Competent organizational reasoning with the right headings, spoiled by a false “handover reduction,” a pilot that cannot see the main downside, and a code appendix that would not run and does not use the functions the question named. That is clearly above a shallow or one-sided answer, and clearly below a strict 8+.
