**4.0**

The answer has the right skeleton and avoids the dumbest failure modes (it does not swarm I-03 just because \(p=0.82\), and it distinguishes I-05 from I-07 in words). That is not enough. On decision-theoretic coherence, operational realism, and arithmetic auditability it is unreliable. Several rules, if executed as written, would forbid the very portfolio the author then selects.

**Capacity math is broken.** Four slots exist. The table selects **I-02, I-04, I-07, I-08**, then says “**(3 slots)**” and “**Reserved Slot: 1**.” That is four interventions plus a reserve, or three interventions with an unnamed dropout. Either reading is infeasible. The impact table then prices **four** slots at €1,800. A deployable policy that cannot count slots fails the assignment.

**The decision rule contradicts the allocation.**
- Alert persistence requires a “20% increase in \(p_\text{now}\) from \(p_\text{prev}\)” (absolute) or a 10% relative increase if \(p_\text{prev}>0.5\). I-02 (+0.08), I-04 (+0.18), I-08 (+0.08) fail a 20-point rule; I-07 **falls** 0.75→0.72. Section 4 then requires I-07’s \(p\) to rise 15% from its prior peak before intervening. The author still swarms I-07.
- Value filter is “only intervene if value **> €5,000**.” I-07 is exactly €5,000 and is selected; I-01 is €9,000 and is marked ineligible as “low value.”
- I-01 is labeled ineligible for “Public Sector + low value” and “\(p_\text{now}\) too low after adjustment.” Public Sector is the segment whose calibration is “broadly acceptable”; a +0.05 uplift is the SME bias, not PS. Applying their own +0.05 would move I-01 to 0.66, which is not low. Eligibility should be stage-based (Triage: yes). “No” then “Wait” is also a category error.
- NNL rule: floor \(p=0.6\). Section 5 adds value > €15k. I-09 is 0.69 and €20k and is still waited. With 2.5 h left in Diagnosis Started, “wait” is close to “forfeit the window,” and mid-range direct EV is among the highest in the book.
- I-04 is called a “repeated alert.” The table says **first alert**.
- I-08 rationale: effect may be low if the model “**underpredicted**.” Strategic Enterprise **overpredicts** by 0.05–0.12.

**Calibration “robustification” is ad hoc and factually wrong.** PS is lumped with SME (+0.05). SE is shaved by only 0.05, the *least* conservative end of 0.05–0.12. NNL is not recalibrated; it is given an arbitrary probability floor. Observational \(p\) is never treated as distinct from \(P(\text{breach}\mid\text{swarm})\), which the prompt required. Effect selection (“upper bound if \(p_\text{now}\) is near the high end of the range”) confuses the forecast with the causal range.

**Section 2 is operationally sloppy beyond the slot error.** Required action set is intervene / wait / do not intervene. I-03 is “Wait.” After Diagnosis Complete the swarm is almost worthless; the action is **do not intervene**, not defer. I-06 is parked as “high false-alarm burden” (€300 is not high); the real issue is a 0.20→0.52 jump on an imputed queue feature, which needs a data-quality gate, not a FAB story. Time-to-deadline is almost unused: I-04 (10 h) and I-08 (8 h) are spent immediately while I-09 (2.5 h) and a possibly-real I-06 (4 h, Diagnosis Started) are idle. Option value of waiting on long-horizon, high-FAB, low-ARR SE cases is ignored.

**The expected-impact table is not auditable and is arithmetically wrong.**
- Optimistic effects 0.15 / 0.12 / 0.25 / 0.10 are **not** the upper half of 0.08–0.22, 0.10–0.28, 0.20–0.32, 0.05–0.15. Base uses 0.10 and 0.20 for I-04 and I-07 (lower bounds, not midpoints). Adverse does not sit on the lower bounds either.
- Benefit is written as \((0.62\times€25\text{k} + 0.12\times€60\text{k} + \cdots)\). That applies I-02’s stake to **total** breaches and then adds the other cases again. The printed totals (€19,700 / €13,500 / €7,200) do not match that formula *or* the correct case-wise products. €13,500 is roughly \(0.53\times€25\text{k}\), i.e. the first term only.
- False-alarm burden is omitted despite being in the prescribed EV identity.
- Spillover is \(0.02\times 6=0.12\) **once**, independent of slots. Three (four) slots produce about 0.18–0.54 (0.24–0.72) extra breach-mass on the stated model, plus 8–20 min (adverse 15–30 min) of queue delay from pulling two seniors. Harm is left in breach units next to euro benefits, so “High ROI,” “Break-even; spillover neutralized,” and “Negative net” are unearned slogans. Optimistic “Confidence: High” is indefensible.

**Temporal policy is internally inconsistent and unrealistic.** Distinguishing I-05 (refresh, no new event → suppress) from I-07 (new log → allow re-entry) is the one clear hit. Then a **24-hour** post-swarm cool-down is imposed on cases whose remaining SLA is 1.5–12 hours; the cool-down can outlast the process. Magic constants (0.7, 15%, 25%, 30%, 40%) proliferate and do not match Section 1. “Prioritize even if not in queue” is undefined. Repeat swarms are supposed to require a **materially new event**; the author substitutes percentage jumps in \(p\).

**Fairness mitigations sometimes implement the failure mode.** A €5k value floor is what starves I-01/I-10; it is not a safeguard. A safeguard is a reserved low-value/SME quota, a constrained ranker, or an explicit audit of intervention rates by segment after value-adjustment. “Retrain quarterly with post-intervention labels” **is** the selection/feedback loop. Holding out cases where swarms “failed” conditions on the outcome. Preferential SE allocation if PS bears spillover concentrates help on the already high-value, overpredicted segment. NNL is acknowledged then parked despite passing the author’s own gates.

**Monitoring is generic and causally weak.** Daily slot counts and weekly crude intervened-vs-untreated breach rates will be confounded by exactly the selection rule just designed. “Ab-abs analysis” is not an estimator; before/after after a risk spike is mean reversion. There is no design for effect identification (randomized encouragement when slots remain, eligible-unslotted controls, logging of assignment propensity, ITT vs as-treated). Annual fairness review is too slow. I-03 is not a “missed breach” of the swarm policy. The change trigger “give leftover slots to Strategic Enterprise” again inverts equity.

**What remains in its favor, and why that is not a 6+.** Six required headings; I-03 correctly excluded; I-05 not swarmed; reserve mentioned; some verbal awareness of heterogeneity, FAB, and spillover. Under a loose rubric this is a mid paper. Under the stated bar—coherence, operational realism, explicit uncertainty, auditable arithmetic, no unwarranted precision—the contradictions, case-table misreads, slot/cost/spillover errors, and backward causal/fairness fixes are disqualifying. Not nearly flawless; not close.
