**7.2**

The final write-up hits every required section and is clearly above a generic “add capacity / urgent-first” answer. Weekly renormalization of the unequal observation windows, use of DFG edge counts, queue/utilization tables, variant notes, and the I1–I10 conflict hints are all real process-mining reasoning, not atmosphere. Tail metrics and mix shift are kept in view; average cycle time is not treated as the objective.

It is not close to flawless.

**Material process flaws**

- S1’s bottleneck sequence starts at courier/registration. The distinctive S1 shock in the log is mix (Urgent+Complex per week ~713 → ~1,617; medical-validation events/week ~530 → ~1,367; specialist holds/week ~36 → ~203), which loads assay, technical validation, medical validation, and specialist review. Intake is the S4 story. Conflating them weakens the migration analysis.
- P2 reserves M1 for Urgent/Complex; P3 dumps Routine onto M1 when M2 degrades. There is no precedence, WIP arithmetic, or statement of which case type loses. That is exactly the “cannot prioritize everyone” constraint, left unresolved.
- “Cap urgent arrivals” fights the 18h contractual penalty. The lab does not control physician ordering; refusing or delaying Urgent intake is not an operational lever in the same class as courier slotting.
- The known, calendar-regular specialist gap (“unavailable every second Friday”) is handled only as a reactive queue trigger. A robust design would pre-allocate review/overflow on those Fridays rather than wait for hold age >24h.
- Cross-training registration or pre-analytical staff into accession correction is recommended “where feasible” without analyzing overload of those pools (peak 85% and 96%). That violates the instruction not to propose an intervention without the station it overloads.
- Exception-based / auto technical validation for Routine is invented and only loosely tied to downstream risk. In peak, the medical path—not Routine tech→release—is the mix-driven constraint; the intervention is underspecified clinically and as a flow control.

**Smaller inaccuracies and muddle**

- Recollection/repeat “rates” are event counts over received/assay events, not unique cases; open WIP at period end is ignored (e.g. hold 610 vs hold→medical 560).
- The trade-off cell for I5+I10 (“reduce repeat validation backlog”) confuses assay-repeat rework with validation queue.
- After reserved urgent slots replace ad-hoc bypass, “urgent bypass 60–70% = green” is a confused KPI.
- Accession specialists (n=2, peak queue 14h) are central in the DFG and then almost absent from the daily dashboard.
- P1’s “temporary pre-analytical triage support” is a bit of the forbidden add-capacity reflex.
- “Extend Complex due dates” is a contractual workaround, and Complex has no stated SLA; the 150h “green” band is invented without saying so clearly.
- Several thresholds (courier 1.3/1.8, hold 3%/5%) are round numbers, not derived from the log.

**What still holds the score up**

Format is exact. I1/I2/I3/I4/I9/I10 are modified or gated instead of swallowed whole. Downstream overload is discussed for the main candidate set. Residual harm from the redesign itself is named. Leading vs lagging indicators are separated, with on/off policy triggers.

Those are the right shape of answer. The migration logic, policy conflicts, and a few unconstrained interventions keep it well short of a high score on a strict rubric.
