**2.4**

The answer is structurally compliant JSON and uses some of the scenario’s vocabulary, but it fails the core task: reading prefixes, applying the given state definitions, and producing process-legal next observations. That is not a minor calibration issue. It is a systematic misparse of the event log.

**State inference is wrong on four of five cases, and the fifth is internally broken.**  
S2 is defined as “Customer Evidence Requested has occurred, but no corresponding Evidence Received event has yet been logged.” C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E all already have `Evidence Received` in the prefix (12:10, 12:16, 13:20). Their primary state is therefore S3 `EVIDENCE_REVIEW`, not `EVIDENCE_PENDING`. C6-E has already completed a full first evidence cycle and a second request/receipt; calling it evidence-pending is indefensible. C6-C’s alternative `EVIDENCE_PENDING` is absurd once `Evidence Review Completed` and `Technical Investigation Completed` are visible. Completed-obligation lists are consistently truncated and omit events that are literally in the prefix.

**Two cases predict an activity that has already been logged.**  
C6-C’s prefix ends with `Technical Investigation Completed (today 11:45)`; rank 1 is that same activity at 0.70–1.00. C6-D’s prefix ends with `Technical Investigation Completed (13:05)`; rank 1 is that same activity at 0.90–1.00. Predicting a completion event that is already in the trace is a disqualifying error. For C6-C the open obligation is Regulatory Assessment (country rule: repeated malfunction can be reportable). For C6-D, R-R2 opened the case at 12:40 and TI finished at 13:05; the process-enabled work is RA (and possibly a delayed evidence cycle), not another TI completion.

**Next-activity predictions violate the stated evidence cycle.**  
Rule 3 is `Customer Evidence Requested → Evidence Received → Evidence Review`, with a new request only after review. C6-A and C6-B both put another `Customer Evidence Requested` first, with unjustifiably high mass (0.65–1.00 and 0.80–1.00), after evidence has already been received. The historically dominant successor after `Evidence Received` is `Evidence Review Completed` (356). Completeness 0.86 / 0.98 is a reason a *reviewer* might later request more evidence, not a reason the next logged event is another request.

**Mandatory control-flow obligations are ignored.**  
C6-A: high severity, injury, two devices on one lot, three similar complaints → TI + RA required, Fast Close forbidden, Supplier Analysis mandatory. None of that appears in the obligation lists or top-3. C6-E: one device in a lot now linked to six complaints → Supplier Analysis is mandatory and disposition must wait; the answer never names it. C6-E is also mislabeled “Medium severity” in supporting evidence; the case is Low. C6-B is Fast-Close eligible on paper (low, no injury, single device, no suspicious lot, 0.93 duplicate), but the answer ranks another evidence request first, puts `Close — No Safety Issue` third with `process_enabled: false`, and lists the duplicate similarity as *contradicting* evidence even though it supports non-investigation closure.

**Observation-model and operating-context evidence is misread or unused.**  
C6-D is flagged `migration-affected`, has a source-system attachment with no evidence events, and has an RA worklist open at 12:40. The answer asserts the feed flag is “normal,” then later mentions migration delay—internally contradictory—and still treats a missing evidence event as support that TI is “in progress” (it is already complete). Queue load, engineer unavailability, RA priority-not-FIFO, 16:00 notification batch, and the 8–15% / 6 h feed-delay model are essentially unused. The required distinction between a process-enabled action and the next *logged* completion is not operationalized; `process_enabled` flags are arbitrary (TI “false” on C6-A; closure “false” on C6-B while the prose claims fast-close is favored).

**Uncertainty is not honest.**  
Intervals are wide in form but attached to illegal or already-observed events, so the width does not buy credit. Midpoints are not a coherent top-three (C6-A ≈ 0.83 / 0.45 / 0.18). Rank-1 floors of 0.65–0.90 express false confidence. Contradicting-evidence lists recycle supporting facts or irrelevancies (“one related complaint in the past 30 days (3 total)”). Unknowns are thin and sometimes invented (migration delay on a `normal` feed).

**Cross-case discussion is shallow.**  
It notes severity and completeness for A vs B but not the actual semantic split: Fast Close legality, injury/reportability, multi-device same-lot supplier obligation, and that both cases are in review after receipt rather than pending receipt. DFG criticism (“does not account for partial evidence”) is true but unused; the answer does not use the DFG where it *does* help (Evidence Received → Review; TI ↔ RA; Disposition → Notification).

A nearly flawless answer would have placed A/B/E in `EVIDENCE_REVIEW` with Review Completed as the leading next log event, kept A and B sharply separated by safety/lot/fast-close/supplier constraints, treated C’s next observation as Regulatory Assessment Completed (with delayed-log and disposition as alternatives), and treated D as a migration-corrupted fork with RA in progress and possible late evidence events. This submission does almost none of that. Valid schema and some domain words are the only reasons it is not nearer 1.0.
