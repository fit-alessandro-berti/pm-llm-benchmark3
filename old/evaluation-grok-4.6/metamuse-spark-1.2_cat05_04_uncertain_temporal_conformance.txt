The write-up has the right *shape* of an interval analysis: offset direction, resolution-before-correction, DST unions with causal filtering, inferred starts that keep both uncertainties, dual treatment of T-406/T-407, and midpoints quarantined as lossy. That is not enough. The task is a precision exam. This answer fails several designed borderlines, omits required rows, and then misstates its own numbers.

**Material classification error.** T-402 Alert→Investigation is given as `[958.5, 1231.5]` vs ζ=2 band `[240, 960]`, then labeled **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** because it is “wholly above” the band. `958.5 < 960`. The same false claim is repeated in §4 (`CERTAIN_VIOLATION (>960)`). This is the exact trap the prompt is built around; missing it is not a rounding quibble.

**False midpoint narrative.** The secondary comparison says T-402’s midpoint duration is `971s` and would *pass* (including under +2 min). The interval midpoint is `09:18:15` UTC, i.e. **~1095s**, which still *violates* S1. The “point estimates hide a certain violation” lesson is therefore inverted for the one case they chose as the exhibit.

**Wrong “robust findings” summary.** §4 claims the only robust violations across +5 min grace *and* ζ=3 are T-402 S1 and T-408 S2/S3. Under a strict rubric that reserves high scores for near-flawless work, that is a mid-fail, not a high pass.
