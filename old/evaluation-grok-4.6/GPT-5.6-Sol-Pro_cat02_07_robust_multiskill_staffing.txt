7.1

This is a strong, format-compliant process-mining design analysis with unusually careful capacity arithmetic and implementable dispatch rules. It is not nearly flawless. Several numbers, policy claims, and simulation mechanisms do not line up, and the robustness conclusion is better argued than it is evidenced.

## What holds up

The write-up follows the required nine-heading structure, separates observations from assumptions, and defines two distinct objectives: a tail/SLA risk score and a minimax robustness/fairness score. It does not pick a winner from base-case mean delay.

The service-mixture means, block workloads, daily 2,720.6 minutes of nominal work, 2,490 regular minutes, 2,670 with overtime, 5.44/22.41/14.15 priority counts, U1 arrival scaling to 53.2, and U4 diagnostic increment of ~242 minutes are internally consistent and auditable. The evening Mechanical infeasibility (Emil cannot do M; Deepa overtime is only 30 minutes) and Software fragility before 12:00 are the right bottlenecks. Parallel vs sequential ME/ES comparisons at the means are directionally correct and used to motivate a Validation-aware parallelization rule.

Policies S/P/H are specified as dispatch rules rather than slogans. The PM4Py section correctly warns that `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is case-level, that parallel elapsed service is not labor effort, and that handover-of-work graphs are unsafe for concurrent branches. The validation plan (shadow mode, no case-level randomization, P1 guardrails, stop rules) is operationally serious.

## Material inaccuracies and inconsistencies

**The daily “minimum max utilization” column is not one quantity.** Base/U1/U2/U4 match aggregate work ÷ regular capacity (109.3%, 138.2%, 109.8%, 119.3%). U3 136.4% and U5 172.6% do not: Chen’s absence gives 2,720.6/2,040 ≈ 133.4%, and U5 gives 3,442/2,040 ≈ 168.7%. No assignment vector is shown that would justify the extra points. Worse, Section 2 itself proves regular-hours Mechanical after 18:00 is infeasible, so a calendar-and-skill-aware fluid *z* for the base day cannot be 109.3%. The table understates the constraint the prose just established.

**Offered load is computed in speed-1 minutes.** Ada/Emil/Farah Electrical multipliers of 1.30–1.40, Farah’s 1.40 on all diagnostics, and forced overflow under U3/U5 mean realized resource-minutes are higher than 2,721/3,442. The later remark about “slow-resource assignments” does not bound this. Evening and Software claims are still directionally right; the utilization percentages are optimistic.

**Section 4 is not an audit trail.** Ranges such as 390–750 or 70–90% P1 are labeled “directional” and “optimistic,” which is allowed, but they are not the printed output of the code, and the code’s default is two seeds. Comparative statements (H beats P on P1 in every shock; P wins mean delay especially in U2) are therefore hypotheses restated as results. P1 percentages with ~5–9 expected cases are noisier than the table’s tightness implies, even with Wilson intervals in the scaffold.

**“Delay” is sojourn/flow time** (completion − arrival, unfinished cases censored at 2,880). That matches the SLA clock, but it is not waiting time, and mixing unfinished mass at the horizon with completed flow times blurs p90. This should have been defined once and split into completed vs censored.

## Policy text vs model

Policy H in Section 3/5 is richer than Policy H in Section 8.

- Overtime is described as contingency; the simulator treats the 30-minute window as ordinary availability every day. That silently adds 180 minutes and makes every policy look more feasible than the 90% planning rule.
- Strict sequential fallback “if one branch lane is unavailable until the next shift” is always `parallel` in code.
- Hybrid parallelization is *not* tightened when Validation cover exceeds 30 minutes.
- Stress-mode triggers, P3 promotion after six hours, and the 90%/80% assignment veto are prose only. Aging is a weak `+2 × wait` term.
- `queue_pressure` sums `1/multiplier` over primary names even when Chen is absent, so U3/U5 overflow is too timid.
- The same clock can mark Emil as the Electrical *and* Software reserve.

Dispatch also uses **realized** `sampled_service` (clairvoyant remaining-time and mode choice). That is never labeled. It favors P and H over S and overstates the value of “transparent earliest-risk” scoring. `expected_case_remaining` after Intake **sums** both ME/ES branches and then adds 8 minutes, i.e. neither a parallel max nor a true sequential path. `projected_free` adds remaining work as if breaks and off-shift gaps do not exist.

Setup is keyed off activity, not the mechanical vs electrical/software family used for the 5-minute team transfer. That is a defensible reading of “skill family,” but it is a different definition than the coordination rule and should have been stated.

These are not nits: the recommended policy is only partly the policy that was simulated.

## Smaller issues

- \(J_{\text{risk}}\) is mangled in the Markdown (`(0.90-S_{P1})*+`) and never evaluated on the run table, so the “hybrid wins on \(J_{\text{robust}}\)” claim is narrative.
- U5’s 172.6% is said to exclude outage timing damage, then used as if it already captures combined stress.
- Jain-on-utilization is correctly critiqued in Section 6, then still used as the fairness column in Section 4.
- The minute-step DES is transparent but \(O(T \times R \times N_{\text{tasks}})\) over 2,880 minutes × 18 scenario-policy cells; with `N_REPLICATIONS = 2` it is a demo, not a Monte Carlo.
- PM4Py calls are wrapped in `ImportError` (good) but `get_service_time` / `discover_handover_of_work_network` may need an event log, not a dataframe, depending on version. Handover events are stripped before the SNA call, so the demonstrated network is not the explicit handover log the monitoring design treats as authoritative.

## Verdict

The answer meets the quality bar on specialization vs pooling, calendars, multipliers (qualitatively), and not worshipping base-case mean delay. Process-mining competence in Section 7 is high. The failures that block a high score are: inconsistent utilization definitions, speed-1 load presented as binding capacity, clairvoyant and incomplete implementation of the recommended policy, and a scenario table that is not reproducible from the attached experiment. Under a strict reading, that is a very good design memo with a scaffold, not a closed, auditable comparison.
