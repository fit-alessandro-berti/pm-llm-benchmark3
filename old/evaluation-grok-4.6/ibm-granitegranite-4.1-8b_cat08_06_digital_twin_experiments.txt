**3.4**

The answer looks complete because it has the eight required headings, but it fails the assignment’s hard constraints in multiple places. Coverage is not the same as competence. The design is not nearly ready to advise a municipality.

**PM4Py grounding is largely fabricated.** The question required `pm4py.format_dataframe` on the standard columns `case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, and `time:timestamp`, plus `pm4py.parse_process_tree` (or a clear account of why the given tree string is limited). The skeleton does the opposite: it renames the XES columns away from the standard names, then calls `format_dataframe` with invented kwargs (`event_attribute`, `activity_attribute`, `case_attribute`). It never parses the supplied tree. Instead it invents `pm4py.discover.dfg`, `pm4py.discover.process_tree`, `pm4py.statistical_analysis.service_time`, `pm4py.distributions.estimate_parameters`, `pm4py.summarize_arrivals`, `pm4py.analyze_rework`, `pm4py.replay.simulate_log`, `pm4py.analyze_bottlenecks`, and `pm4py.util.defaults.default_policies`. None of these match current PM4Py conventions. The unused `pm4py.objects.log.importer.csv` import is the same family of error the prompt warned against. Required computations are missing: no variants, no performance DFG, no service/waiting columns derived from timestamps, and no DataFrame built from the provided excerpt.

**Several interventions are mis-specified, which invalidates the experiment matrix.** D4 adds a “peak-month” inspector in January–February and August–September. Those are not the peak months; March–May and September–November are. The intervention as coded would add capacity in the trough. D5 is operationalized as “add 0.5 FTE legal,” but the policy is to *parallelize* a legal pre-check, i.e. change synchronization, not headcount. D2 is described as overriding `requires_fire`; the rule skips *site inspection* for low-value Simple renovations with no prior violations. D3 is reduced to a 0.7 travel factor on D3/D5 and omits the stated trade-off that the first case in a bundle waits longer. D7 is implemented as restricting submissions to Mon–Wed 09:00–12:00 rather than smoothing Monday bursts. Expected medians (8d, 12d, 45d, 25% reduction) are invented and then treated as planning facts.

**The twin is still mostly an independent-case Poisson model.** Architect bursts, related applications in the same week, geographic inspection grouping, and neighborhood objections that hit several cases are named in Section 1 and then largely abandoned. Arrivals are `Poisson(baseline × season × weekday)` despite the log summary that the top 20 architects generate 31% of cases in weekly clumps. That is overdispersed, clustered demand, not a thinned Poisson of independent cases. The skeleton has no routing module, no bundle state, no district tour, and a toy FIFO `Queue`. Cross-case dependence is a comment, not a mechanism.

**State, calendars, and time are too thin for the stated purpose.** Missing first-class state includes: pending vs completed branches of the AND-split, decision-drafting synchronization, appeal, no-show/applicant readiness, bundle membership, inspector itinerary, prior violations (required for D2), aging/SLA clocks, and legal blackout weeks. Calendars are copied into a table, then implemented as `weekday in [0, 4] and hour == 17` with a 2025 Christmas holiday for a 2026 log. Fire Tue–Thu, inspector Tue–Fri, Monday portal clerk, 40% environmental reservation, and council-meeting weeks never become executable rules. Weekend arrivals exist in the excerpt (P002) and are ignored.

**Waiting vs service is mishandled.** The prompt forbids collapsing them without explaining the limitation. The plan says: infer waiting as “arrival-to-completion minus logged service time.” If start timestamps are missing, logged service time is exactly what you do *not* have. That is circular. There is no lifecycle (`start`/`complete`) handling, no business-time vs calendar-time split, and no identification strategy for the wait/service decomposition.

**Objections are treated as if they were observed, then later denied.** Step 6 draws `objection_extension` from “the empirical distribution from the log.” Objections are *not* in the log; public-notice duration is a mixture of the 14-day minimum and an unobserved objection process. A ±30% band on p90 is not a model of that missing mechanism. Using duration as a noisy proxy is acceptable only if the mixture and the missingness are explicit. Here they are not.

**Non-stationary design is internally inconsistent.** Seasonality is applied to some rows of the matrix and not others; D4 and D10 are “seasonal,” the baseline is not clearly so. Warm-up is “1000 days = 3× median cycle,” but 3×45 days is 135, not 1000. For a seasonal, calendar-driven system the right design is a terminating run over whole years after a short transient, not a 1000-day warm-up plus 10,000-day quasi-steady-state. 200 replications and bootstrap CIs are fine on paper; they do not fix a wrong horizon. There are no common random numbers, no input-vs-stochastic uncertainty split, and almost no sensitivity on the actual data gaps (start-time inference, incomplete fire/environment attributes, uncoded rework reasons).

**Validation and decision criteria fight the data.** KS p>0.05 and RMSE<5% on 31k cases after fitting the same log are not validation; they are a ritual that will either always fail or always be gamed. Utilization cannot be “observed” when many activities lack start times. The SLA “90% of cases within 45 days” is impossible: Complex variance already has median 140d and p90 260d. A blended median of 42–45 days erases the case-type differences the municipality must manage. Fairness is a garbled “median between worst and best district = 5d.” Utilization targets of 70/80/90% treat congestion as a goal. Cost/benefit > 1.5 and a “robustness index of 0.85” are undefined numbers with no cost model.

**Routing assumptions contradict the known gaps.** Fire and environment are treated as deterministic flags, but the prompt says area thresholds and district sensitivity are incomplete. Rework probabilities are constant and taken from activity-name counts, while rework reasons are unstructured and also occur after planning, inspection, legal, and appeal—not only the two loop labels in the tree.

**What is salvageable is only the outline.** The eight sections exist. Calendars and non-stationarity are mentioned. D1–D10 plus three stresses appear. Assumptions are tabulated. The code is at least split into “log handling” vs `SimulationWorld`. None of that offsets wrong peak months, a misread inspection-waiver rule, fake PM4Py, Poisson independence, an impossible SLA, and a wait/service estimator that assumes the missing data already exist.

Not a usable digital-twin design. It is a template filled with plausible headings and incorrect machinery.
