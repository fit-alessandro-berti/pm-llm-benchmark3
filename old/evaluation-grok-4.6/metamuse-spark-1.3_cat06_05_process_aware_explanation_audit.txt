**7.1**

The audit is substantively strong: it covers the required items, catches both obvious leakage and subtler lineage defects, preserves queue and prefix-age signals, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as later-legitimate rather than impossible, and generally separates predictive association from causal or operational claims. Under a hypercritical standard it is not nearly flawless. Several internal contradictions, one overstated leakage claim, and repeated technical unclarity keep it well below the top band.

**Internal contradictions (largest penalty).**  
Case X/Y are marked `recompute` in §4 because some t0-valid drivers remain, but §5 P0 says to “Invalidate Cases X/Y/Z scores.” Those are different decisions. `initial_reserve` is “unusable until verified” in §1, then appears as a Case Y `credible_driver` in §4. `claimant_channel` is “Retain as proxy” in §1/§3, then dumped into `removed_or_repaired_drivers` and omitted from the Case X narrative. The task explicitly said not to delete every correlated available feature; region is kept for Y while channel is effectively deleted for X, with no principled distinction. `removed_or_repaired` is also the wrong bucket for a feature the author claims to retain with a caveat.

**Factual overclaim.**  
`total_case_duration_hours` is correctly critical leakage, but it is not a “deterministic transform of the label.” The target is a 10-day *settlement* clock; duration is to *close* and can include post-settlement appeal time. The feature is still illegitimate at t0; calling the team story tautological overstates the relationship.

**Classification and schema slips.**  
The “unusable until lineage is repaired” class mixes repairable PIT bugs (`prior_claim_count`, `same_day_document_count`, `risk_score_current`) with features that cannot be repaired for a t0 model (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`). The following paragraph notices the difference; the taxonomy does not. `claimant_channel` severity is `low/medium`, which is not in the required `{critical, high, medium, low}` scale.

**Unclarity.**  
Comparison and inequality symbols are missing in many of the places that matter (`event_time t0`, `upload_time t0`, `Current at-t0`, `Predictive causal driver`, point-in-time joins `` t0 ``). An audit whose job is temporal cutoffs cannot afford that. Case X’s narrative (“0.82 … cannot be retained”) also fights its own `recompute` status.

**What still holds.**  
§1 coverage of the top-12, five patterns, and local-only fields is complete; the 65% `final_handler_team` match is correctly rejected as proof of availability; Close→Assessment is treated as same-timestamp/process-infeasible, unlike Appeal→Decision; queue and elapsed time are kept; §2 is a usable prefix/PIT/conformance suite and does not confuse DFG frequency with permission; §3 states the four standards (predictive validity, causal interpretation, actionability, fairness) without collapsing them; the §4 JSON shape matches the spec and does not replay leaked outcomes as facts; §5 hits feature-store validity, prefix replay, calibration after removal, stability, fairness, and drift.

Those strengths are why this is not a mid-range answer. The contradictions and the duration/label overclaim are why it is nowhere near a high one.
