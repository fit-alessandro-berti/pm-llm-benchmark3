**5.3**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and conceptually in the right family: it separates backbone control flow from DECLARE obligations, skeleton cardinalities, a warning-only temporal profile, and an attribute-aware SoD/emergency layer, and it does not pretend R5 and R6 are compatible. Temporal arithmetic, T3/T4/T8 overall calls, and the R9 resource check are mostly sound. That is not enough. The task is explicitly about constraint semantics, and the executable models contradict both the requirements and the verdict table.

**Fatal DECLARE / skeleton misuse.** `precedence(A, B)` and Log Skeleton `always_before(A, B)` mean: if B occurs, A must already have occurred. Encoding both `precedence(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` and `precedence(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` requires *both* approvals before every activation. That falsifies R2 and R8 and makes T1 and T2 DECLARE/skeleton failures under the author’s own dictionaries. The same holds for `precedence` / `always_before(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)`: it demands a legal opinion on *every* accepted case, not only after `Sanctions Escalation`. T1/T2/T4 have no opinion and would fail. Pairing `responded_existence(Activate, Manager)` with `responded_existence(Activate, Compliance)` at `confidence = 0.5` is not an XOR; PM4Py will still require the missing disjunct. Confidence is discovery metadata, not a soft-gate. The verdict matrix marking T1 and T2 as DECLARE and Log Skeleton **pass** is therefore false.

**Procedural model does not match the traces it claims to accept.** Clarification is placed *after* `Classify Risk`. T2 and T5 request clarification *before* classification, so token replay on this tree cannot pass T2. Silent nodes are created with `label="tau"` rather than `label=None`, so they are visible activities named `tau`, not skips. T6 is failed as a foreign-activity trace because escalation/opinion were excluded; T7 contains the same two activities (and inserts `Obtain Legal Opinion` between EDD and Compliance) but is marked procedural **pass**. That is internally inconsistent. T5 is marked procedural pass in the table while the surrounding sentence admits the `do` child requires `Receive Clarification`.

**Further verdict errors.** T7 violates the author’s own `directly_follows(Enhanced Due Diligence, Compliance Approval)` (opinion sits between them) and should not be Log Skeleton **pass**. T6 is only a skeleton “warning” even though `always_before(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is a hard fail on that dictionary. Log-skeleton conformance is not a warning channel. Resolution B is declared the implemented policy, but the tree still has no emergency branch; T6 under B is hand-waved as policy-dependent rather than encoded.

**What remains credit-worthy.** R5/R6 is named as a real conflict with two resolutions and a stated compliance risk. R9/R6/R11 are correctly pushed out of activity-only formalisms, missing-attribute behavior is specified, `zeta = 2.0` and the three hour→second pairs are right, T7’s 3.67σ legal lag is computed correctly, and §9–§10 are honest about vacuity, timestamp limits, and not treating support/confidence as law. Those strengths keep the answer above a collapse grade. The semantic mistakes on precedence, responded existence, tree placement, tau, and the T1/T2/T5/T6/T7 matrix are too central for anything near the top of the scale.
