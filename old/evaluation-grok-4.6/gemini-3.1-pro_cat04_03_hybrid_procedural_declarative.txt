4.4

The write-up is complete in outline and sometimes insightful (R5/R6 named as a conflict, R10 vs R11, vacuity, projection of the tree). Under a strict reading it is not a reliable hybrid model. The executable artifacts and the verdict matrix do not implement the same policy.

**Critical: DECLARE `precedence` is the wrong encoding of R5, and Section 8 does not evaluate the model that was written.**  
`precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` means every activation requires a prior opinion, not “after escalation, opinion before activate.” That is stated in the semantics table (“Activation cannot occur unless Opinion has already occurred”). T1, T2, T4, and T5 contain `Activate Vendor` and no opinion, so they must **fail** DECLARE. They are marked **pass**. That is not a wording slip; it is a broken correspondence between model and evaluation. Strict R5 is a *conditional* order: if escalation occurred, opinion must precede activation. Standard DECLARE cannot say that with a single precedence pair. The honest move is an external check (or a conservative approximation labeled as such). Unconditional precedence is a different, much stronger policy that rejects the entire low-risk happy path.

**Critical: Log Skeleton pairs do not match PM4Py, and the `always_after` explanation is false.**  
In PM4Py, `always_before(a, b)` means: if `a` occurs, `b` already occurred; `always_after(a, b)` means: if `a` occurs, `b` occurs later. The written set has `(Receive Onboarding Request, Activate Vendor)` and `(Close Onboarding, Receive Onboarding Request)`. Under the real API those say activate-before-receive and receive-after-close, and would fail the backbone traces. The author “defines” the opposite orientation, which is not an allowed substitute for the requested dictionaries. The claim that `always_after(Obtain Legal Opinion, Sanctions Escalation)` would force opinion to *always* occur is simply wrong: both skeleton relations are conditional on the first activity. Empty `directly_follows` is also unsafe: in PM4Py that relation is typically the *allow-list* of adjacent pairs, so `set()` can make every multi-event case deviate. “Populate only what you can defend” does not license an unconstrained reading of that key.

**R3 and R11 are under-encoded.**  
`altresponse(Request, Receive)` handles “no second request before a receive.” It does not require a receive *before Activate/Reject*. A trace `Request → Activate → Receive` can satisfy alternate response and still violate R3. That limitation is not stated. R11 is reduced to `response(Change Bank Details, Suspend Vendor)` plus a standing `precedence(Verify, Release)`. Initial verify already makes the latter true on every normal case; it does not require a *later* verify after a bank-detail change. The R10/R11 clash in Section 9 is well observed; the encoding still does not implement the verify-before-later-release clause.

**R5/R6 is named, then implemented inconsistently.**  
Resolution 1 is chosen (hard control-flow). The emergency script is Resolution 2 (activate-before-opinion allowed if opinion arrives in 24h). Deployment then calls emergency activation a *soft* warning, while T6 is a hard DECLARE fail. Joint authorization is never treated as a missing required attribute: if the log cannot show authorization, R6 is not satisfied. Two resolutions are given, which the prompt asked for, but the chosen one is not the one the code and the ops narrative use.

**Smaller but required-quality failures.**  
The prompt asked to distinguish alternate vs chain response and noncoexistence vs nonsuccession; the table never does. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is a *filter* (default keep = cases that already satisfy four-eyes), not a conformance verdict; missing `org:resource` is waved at as “warning or policy-dependent” with no rule. R2’s “no approval on a rejected case” and “exactly one approval on an accepted case” are left to the XOR only; T8 is caught for R1, not for that part of R2. Process-tree projection is disclosed and the tree itself is a fair backbone, but “filter ad-hoc work then replay” is an extra-logical assumption that makes the procedural column almost non-informative for T5–T7.

**What remains creditworthy.**  
Format and coverage of R1–R12; two R5/R6 readings and a risk statement; process-tree concurrency/XOR and an explicit exclusion of loops and post-activation work; temporal pairs converted correctly (48h/24h, 12h/6h, 72h/36h) with `zeta = 2.0` and a sound “warning not reject” rationale; T4 four-eyes, T5 unanswered clarification, T7 34h > 12+2×6, and T8 mutual exclusion are the right *ideas*; the Section 9 discussion of vacuity, missing emergency attributes, and support/confidence as non-normative is better than the models it accompanies.

Those strengths do not offset a DECLARE rule that falsifies the main accepted traces, a skeleton that is inverted relative to the API the task specified, and a verdict matrix that does not execute the written constraints. For an executable-style modeling exam, that is a failing correspondence, not a minor omission.
