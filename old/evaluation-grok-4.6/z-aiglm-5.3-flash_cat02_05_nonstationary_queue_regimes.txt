**7.6**

The final write-up is a strong, format-compliant process-mining analysis with the right operational story, but it is not nearly flawless. Several numerical and conceptual errors keep it well below a high score under a strict reading.

**What is done well.** The eight required headings are present and used as intended. Estimands, units, and the 07:00–19:00 convention are defined before any modeling. Carry-over, quantile-versus-mean service, jobs-versus-cases, serial dependence, and the unlogged 4/29 start are treated as first-class threats. Recovery (4/16–17) and the outage day are isolated and not sold as a high-throughput steady state. Little’s Law is handled correctly: median service is not used as flow time; implied sojourn is restricted to chosen windows, in opening-hours units, with an explicit lower-bound interpretation; no window is oversold as fully applicable. Pooling is shown to reverse conclusions (weekday/weekend composition, mix step, outage/intervention), including a genuine Simpson-style utilization reversal (pooled ρ ≈ 0.90 vs ≈ 0.65 / 1.16). The hourly-versus-`peak_08_10_pct` contradiction is a real data-audit finding. The Python block is self-contained, uses pandas, labels the CUSUM split as a heuristic, computes Fano/CV and lag dependence, restricts LL to named windows, and keeps PM4Py in comments with a clear “not an event log” disclaimer.

**Errors and inconsistencies that cost points.**

1. **Wrong “busiest two-hour” shares in T3.** Fixed 08:00–09:59 shares (16.8 / 18.1 / 9.6 / 11.8%) are correct, but the claimed maxima 31.7% (4/07) and 33.3% (4/15) are the 09:00–11:00 bins, not the true consecutive maxima (20+16 = 35.6% on 4/07; 22+17 = 37.1% on 4/15). The code would print the true maxima; the prose does not. An audit section that mis-indexes its own reconciliation is a serious slip.

2. **Regime A statistics are internally inconsistent.** The table includes Friday 4/10 (92 arrivals, 5.5 FTE), so arrivals run 7.7–8.9 per opening hour, not 8.0–8.9, and daily-arrival Fano is ≈ 0.26, not 0.16. The 0.16 figure is what you get if 4/10 is dropped. Means include the Friday; range and Fano quietly do not.

3. **Era I “median svc ~28.4” is wrong.** The mean of daily medians for 4/06–4/19 is 27.4 (384/14). The column is labeled median service.

4. **“Staff unchanged 6.0” in regime E is false.** 4/24 is 5.5 FTE. The saturation cross-check still roughly holds, but the claim is inaccurate.

5. **Conceptual error on WIP versus flow accounting (T6).** Overnight backlog is present in every 15-minute snapshot the next day, so “late-day accumulation weighted lightly” cannot hide on the order of +110 cases while mean WIP stays in the 20–50 range. Abandonment, definitional leakage, or mis-specified counters are the live hypotheses; the census-weighting story is not.

6. **Mean service and ρ are sharper than the data support.** Two quantiles do not identify E[S]; the lognormal imputation is labeled, but regime tables still report ρ ≈ 0.65 / 1.16 as if they were measurements. Sensitivity bounds belong in those tables, not only in surrounding caveats. The pooled E[S] ≈ 37 min used for ρ ≈ 0.90 is not derived in the text.

7. **Minor code/presentation slop.** `LL_WINDOWS` contains a dead `B_SPLIT_MARKER`; recursive CUSUM always splits once `n ≥ 8` with no gate, so extra “candidates” are guaranteed; a few pooled quantities in prose are round-number restatements of what the script would print rather than the printed values.

**Net.** The regime narrative, Little’s Law matrix, pooling demos, and the refusal to treat drain as capacity are competent and mostly quantitatively grounded. The grade is held down by avoidable arithmetic mistakes in the audit and dispersion sections, an inconsistent regime-A definition, and a confused explanation of the census-versus-accounting gap. Those are not cosmetic in a task that grades internal consistency and uncertainty handling.
