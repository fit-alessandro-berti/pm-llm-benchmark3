The write-up is well structured, mostly faithful to the required headings, and stronger than a start-order narrative: it reconstructs decision points, names assumptions, and usually compares FIFO / priority / SPT / appointments / skills. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading. The task asked for decision-set competence and careful uncertainty. Several tables and causal claims do not meet that bar.

**Material errors in the decision sets (most costly).**  
On QD at 11:20, D-X is mechanical *and* electrical. Jobs then waiting and skill-compatible include at least D03 (11:03, electrical), D04 (11:05, mechanical), D06, D07, D08 (11:16, mechanical, priority 1, `est_min` 8), and D09 (11:20). The table keeps only D07, D06, and a hedged D03. From that truncated set it predicts FIFO would start **D07**. Even inside its own set that is wrong: D06 arrived earlier than D07, and D03 earlier still. A complete set makes the FIFO prediction D03 (or D04 if D03 is treated as already claimed by D-F). This is exactly the failure mode the rubric warns against: inferring discipline from who eventually started, not from who was eligible.

The same hole hides a sharper appointment test. D08 is 8 minutes; D-X is idle 11:20–11:30. D08 could finish by 11:28 and still protect D06 at 11:30. Idling is therefore not just “appointment hold.” It is refusal to pack a shorter, higher-priority, skill-legal job into a known gap. That distinguishes *hard reservation / no look-ahead* from ordinary appointment-when-due. The answer never runs this comparison.

**QC’s SLA story is overfit and internally strained.**  
Primary SEPT is already broken at 10:32 (C04 over C06 and over C05). Calling C05 a deadline/SLA override is weaker than it sounds. C05 arrives 10:11 with `est_min` 40 and deadline 10:50: the SLA is infeasible at arrival, and still infeasible at the first real choice after C02 (10:18). Serving C05 at 10:54 is “the pri-1 job eventually ran,” not evidence of a well-posed endangered-SLA rule.

The opposite case is missing. At 11:40, C07’s deadline 12:00 is still meetable (18 minutes → 11:58) and C01’s 13:00 is not at risk. Taking C01 causes C07 to miss. That is a live EDF/priority/SPT failure and a gift to FIFO/aging. It also undercuts “QC becomes SLA-aware when deadlines are in danger.” The note that C01-before-C07 “fits none of the pure rules” is simply false: FIFO predicts it. That sentence should have been caught.

**Estimate noise is discussed without checking the estimates.**  
Every `est_min` in all four queues equals realized service time (including B01’s three segments summing to 45). The prompt flags noisy/strategic estimates; the data reject that escape for QC. Listing “noisy `est_min`” as a serious alternative without this audit is sloppy. It also means QA’s rejection of SPT is *stronger* than written: estimates were exact, and A1 still chose A02 over A04 at 08:30 and A05 over A07 at 09:05. The text only says SPT loses at 09:05.

**Smaller but real slips.**  
QA’s “strongest contradicting episode” does not contradict appointment+FIFO; it contradicts an alternative. That column is used correctly for QB/QC/QD and incorrectly for QA.  
If QB were preemptive priority, B05 (pri 1 from 09:46) should preempt B03 (pri 2, 09:48–10:00), not only possibly preempt B01 in a 2-minute scan-delay window. The B03 interval is the cleaner test and is underplayed.  
QD confidence 88 and “most identifiable queue” treat hard skill feasibility as if it were a discovered dispatching rule. Skills are given in the resource table; the *policy* content is per-resource FIFO, appointment reservation, and pool-vs-sticky routing. The last of those is not identified. QA’s appointment-vs-FIFO-vs-priority forks are cleaner.  
QC substituting EDF for the required appointment column is defensible; still a format deviation.

**What remains good, so this is not a low score.**  
QA’s 08:20 / 08:30 / 09:05 forks are the right discriminating episodes. QB’s labeled preemption and labeled reassignment are not laundered into a pure priority law; resume-then-FIFO is a fair hybrid. Frozen-backlog SEPT vs live SEPT vs EDF at 10:32 is a real discrimination test. Section 6 is tailored rather than generic. Section 7’s warning — that a single urgent preemption is a dangerous basis for claiming preemptive priority — is the right kind of conclusion.

The piece reads like a strong draft that was not pressure-tested on eligibility sets, clocks, or the two QC deadlines. Under the requested strictness, that caps the grade well below “nearly flawless.”
