**6.5**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly right on the hard temporal cases. It is not close to flawless. Two defects are large enough that a strict reading cannot go much higher.

**TX-302 is reasoned incorrectly.** At release the state is GB / 180000, so R1 fires — but `Enhanced_Review` at 09:20 already sits after the only prior data-setting event and before release, with no later material change. R1 is satisfied on the primary path. The exemption is surplus. Calling the case `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` because “R1 [is] waived by EX-77,” and then claiming that rejecting EX-77 would make the case `NONCOMPLIANT`, is a direct failure of event-time evaluation on a case the prompt required you to treat explicitly. EX-77’s evidence analysis itself is fine; the obligation logic is not.

**The required checker is a skeleton, not a checker.** The eight-point spec is only partly met. `track_events` is defined and never used. There is no computation of last material change, no comparison of amount deltas to 20%, no real R3 sequence test, and no R2 order test. R3/R4 are hardcoded `REVIEW_REQUIRED`. The exemption join is an ID lookup, not a scope/time/evidence check. The diagnostics frame has no evidence timestamps. The frame is never sorted by `(timestamp, event_index)` before `ffill`, so TX-311’s same-timestamp pair is not actually protected. `REVIEW_REQUIRED` was allowed for *human* exemption judgment, not as a substitute for the mechanical tracking the prompt listed.

Smaller issues still count under a strict bar:

- R2 is formalized with an extra “after last material change” constraint the policy text does not state.
- “Material data change” is broadened to any jurisdiction/risk/customer update; that is a defensible reading, but it is not distinguished from R4’s amount rule as cleanly as it should be.
- Forward-filling `exemption_id` is sloppy even if it does not bite these traces.

What remains is genuinely strong: blanks vs. nulls, event vs. effective vs. record vs. evidence time, no final-state leakage, TX-305/TX-310/TX-311/TX-307/TX-309, the six control-flow failure modes, and the logging recommendations. Those keep the score in the mid-6s rather than lower. They do not offset a missed satisfying event on a spotlighted case or an unimplemented checking pipeline.
