**7.2**

The write-up is well structured, uses decision sets rather than start-order folklore, and gets the four primary labels in the right neighborhood: QA is FIFO with appointment insertion; QB is not a clean 3-tier rule; QC is SPT-like with overrides; QD is skill-feasible routing plus FIFO and appointment reservation. That is real process-mining competence. It is not nearly flawless. Several required pieces contain clear logical or evidential errors, and a strict reading has to mark them down hard.

**Material errors and overfits**

1. **Section 4, Episode 1 does not discriminate.** The stated competitors are two-tier FIFO among P2/P3 versus 3-tier priority with aging. The main counterfactual (`B04_alt` at 10:22 vs `B06` at 10:05) predicts `B06` under *both* stories. A test that both theories pass cannot separate them. The “alternative” with `B08` then quietly swaps Explanation B for *pure* priority without aging. That is a broken discrimination test in a section whose only job is discrimination.

2. **QB is labeled “preemptive priority” while a P1 job fails to preempt.** `B02` (P1) interrupts `B01` at 09:16 with an explicit “urgent arrival” note. `B05` (also P1) arrives at 09:46 *during* `B01`’s second segment and does not interrupt. At 09:48 the server is given to `B03` (P2), not `B05`. Scan delay of at most two minutes can hide `B05` until 09:48; it cannot turn a systematic P1-preemption rule into “interrupt only once.” The diagnosis table’s contradicting episode is `B04` ahead of `B06` and the 09:31 resume — not the more direct falsifier of preemption. Resumption at 10:14 (`B01` remaining 14 over `B04`/`B06`/`B07`) is highly informative and is omitted from Section 2.

3. **QC “frozen batch SPT” is fitted to one anomaly and then abandoned.** Rolling SPT is rejected at 10:32 because `C04` (22) beats `C06` (6). A locked 10:10 order `[C02, C03, C04, C01]` explains that row, but the realized sequence is `C02, C03, C04, C05, C06, C01, C07`. After the C05 SLA story, the next job is `C06` (SPT), not remaining-batch `C01`. Batch lock therefore explains *one* decision and is immediately discarded. Documented scan delay is at most two minutes; `C06` arrived at 10:25 and `C04` started at 10:32. Treating 7 minutes as scan delay, or inventing 5–15 minute staging in Section 2 without always flagging it as extra-theoretical, overreaches the prompt. Confidence 81 is too high for a primary rule that needs three distinct patches in six starts.

4. **QD appointment idling is argued on the easy alternative.** At 11:20, `D-X` can take `D07` (28 min) and miss `D06` at 11:30 — that is obvious. It can also take `D08` (mechanical, P1, 8 min) and finish about 11:28, still on time for the appointment. Idling through *that* option is the sharper fact: reservation looks exclusive, not merely “don’t start a job that overruns.” Section 2 never puts this on the table. The 24-minute `D-F` idle after 11:36 is likewise stronger than a meeting buffer: `D10` (12 min) fits before 12:00, and the note “unavailable until 12:20” describes the 12:16 state, not 11:36. Confidence 96 and “by far most identifiable” sit poorly next to that idle and next to FIFO vs most-constrained-job equivalence on `D07` vs `D10`.

**Smaller but real issues**

- QA at 09:05: appointment lookahead does not uniquely predict starting `A06` ten minutes early. Given `A03` waited until the exact slot, the appointment forecast is idle-until-09:15 or a job that finishes by 09:15 (`A07`, est 9). Listing `A06` as the appointment prediction is sloppy, and it is the row that is supposed to show FIFO beating lookahead.
- At 08:32, `A02`’s 20 minutes land exactly on the 08:52 calibration start. FIFO and “fill the window until the break” are observationally equivalent there; the text notices the coincidence and then treats FIFO as identified.
- Estimates equal realized durations on every job in all four queues. That is direct evidence that SEPT = SPT here and that estimate noise is *not* a live confounder. The prompt required that confounder to be accounted for; it is barely mentioned.
- Section 1’s QC secondary field omits the starvation/C01 rule that Section 2 relies on. Section 5’s top-ranked exception (5–15 min staging) is a new operational story, not the prompt’s two-minute scan bound.

**What still holds**

QA walk-ins are monotonic in arrival; `A03` is a clean appointment insert; `A05` at 09:05 is a genuine rejection of priority, SPT, and hard reservation. QB’s P1-vs-the-rest split is a reasonable *hypothesis*, and `B04` over `B06`/`B07` really does kill vanilla 3-tier priority and SPT. QC’s 10:10 and 11:34 rows are clean SPT-over-FIFO/priority/EDD. QD skill partitions (`D05`→`D-F`, `D07`→`D-X`) and `D04` before `D08` on `D-M` are solid. Sections 6–7 are usable; inferring a plant-wide priority rule from these logs would indeed be the dangerous mistake.

The format is complete. The reasoning is mostly decision-set based. The failures are not cosmetic: a non-discriminating “discrimination” test, a preemption label that the same queue contradicts, a batch story that does not survive the next two starts, and a missed tight appointment counterexample on `D-X`. Under a strict bar that is a good analysis with several load-bearing cracks, not a near-flawless one.
