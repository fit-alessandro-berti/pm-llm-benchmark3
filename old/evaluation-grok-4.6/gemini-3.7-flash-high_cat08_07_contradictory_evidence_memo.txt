**6.2**

The memo is strategically competent and complete on the required skeleton (E rather than A/B, segment-aware, facts table, conflict explanations, A–E, M1–M8, pause/scale/measure/thresholds). Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless. Repeated factual slips, invented operating numbers, and internal contradictions keep it well below a high score.

**Material inaccuracies**

The €23 visible-cost movement is the *overall* pilot mean (€184 → €161). It is not a simple-house result. The segment table has no cost columns. The memo still assigns that €23 to low-risk houses in the executive summary, again in Alternative B (“34% of volume loses … €23 savings”), and again in 6.3 (“capturing €23 savings … on 34%+ of national volume”). That is a core economics error, repeated.

The Track 2 box labels apartments + old buildings + business + rural as **55%**. Those four shares are 29+18+8+11 = **66%**. 55% was the three-segment total without rural. The diagram and the memo are inconsistent.

“Bypassed municipal permit checks” is written as an established cause in the memo. The log only shows a move-on-model (expected permit check not observed). The evidence table correctly treats actual unpermitted work as an assumption; the memo does not.

Complaint mix shifting from wait-time to “installed but not working” / “billing before activation” is a stated finding. The table parks it under interpretations.

**Logical contradictions**

Alternative E in the prompt is deploy R for simple houses, add controls on hard segments, *and measure before wider scale*. The answer immediately nationalizes Track 1, including survey-skip, while still listing incomplete pre-week-3 attributes and unvalidated risk classification as open assumptions. That is scaling on the same uncertain triage it flags.

M5 is ranked as an unresolved design question (1:1 vs shared ownership). Section 6.1 nonetheless implements 3-person pods immediately and asserts utilization will fall from 93% to “~78%.” That number is invented. The memo cannot both need M5 to decide org design and treat pods as a ready intervention.

M7 is conformance *monitoring*. The VOI table treats it as installing ERP locks that “instantly” remove the main NPS driver. Measurement and control are collapsed. That is a VOI-category error.

**Overclaim and false precision**

“Destroyed” resilience, “catastrophic” collapse, “toxic” / “dead fiber,” and “Optimal. Strongly recommended” overstate a 6-week, composition-biased, cost-truncated pilot.

Rollout gates (Track 2 FTR ≥ 85%, complex p90 ≤ 25 days vs a 31-day *overall* baseline p90, **0.0%** billing-before-service complaints, FNR ≤ 4%, pods 75–82%, Track 1/2 costs ≤ €180/€195) are presented as if empirical. Absolute-zero complaints is an unusable rule. Kingman’s formula is cited as \(W_q \approx \rho/(1-\rho)\), which drops the variability and service-time terms; queueing is a plausible story, not a fitted mechanism.

**Weaker analysis than the format suggests**

Low precision is equated with “invalid” / “spaghetti” / “chaos.” Precision is model permissiveness, not a compliance verdict. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) is tabled and then barely used, despite being the PM signal most relevant to nationalizing Track 1.

SLA (13% → 16%, better on simple houses), activation-specialist load, the working-together network, and the specific temporal-profile edges (install → activation on apartments; failed visit → second visit under high coordinator utilization) are thin relative to the instruction to use those artifacts.

M1 is ranked sixth even though the live policy uncertainty is whether skip can ever leave the simple-house cell. M8 last is defensible; the M7/M5/M1 order is not.

**What still works**

A/B are correctly rejected; C and D are evaluated on real conditions; E matches the evidence shape (asymmetric segment effects, tail/rework/activation harm). The evidence table has ≥8 items and mostly keeps the three columns apart. The five metric conflicts are explained in the right causal register (happy-path vs exception queues, star vs distributed network, prevention vs truck-roll cost, topology). Immediate halt of skip-on-complex and post-billing activation is the right risk control.

Those strengths make this a usable executive draft. The cost misattribution, 55/66 error, monitor-vs-gate confusion, and implement-then-measure inconsistency are enough that it is not a high-quality decision memo under a strict bar.
