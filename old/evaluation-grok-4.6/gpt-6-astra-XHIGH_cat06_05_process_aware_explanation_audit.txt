**8.6**

This is a strong, process-aware audit that hits the required distinctions and the five-section contract. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several judgment, calibration, and completeness defects keep it well below a top score.

**What the answer gets right.** It treats occurrence in the lifecycle and *availability at t0* as separate tests, and it applies that split consistently. Complete-trace fields (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_handler_team`, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`) are excluded rather than reinterpreted as early warnings. It notices subtler lineage bugs, not only the obvious ones: the ±5-minute queue window, the 23:00 same-day snapshot, `claim_created_at <= extraction_date`, frozen vs current scores, and employee experience joined at extraction. It correctly refuses to treat 65% agreement as a license to use `final_handler_team`. Mean |SHAP| is not treated as a signed causal ranking. The appeal subsequence is handled as future but *normatively possible* after Partial/Deny, while `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is treated as future, order-ambiguous, and lifecycle-inconsistent; the tie is not promoted into a proven reversal. Region is kept as a reviewable proxy rather than deleted. Revised Case X/Y narratives do not smuggle later outcomes. The JSON statuses (`recompute` / invalidate-then-recompute) and the refusal to emit a patched probability are the right epistemic stance.

**Severity banding is inflated and internally inconsistent.** The preamble defines *low* as “no identified defect,” yet `initial_damage_complexity` is *low* while also being tagged for causal overstatement. *High* is used for true complete-trace leakage, a possibly one-minute-future queue snapshot that is “normally valid,” unverified-but-plausible registration fields (`claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`), the SHAP base/other aggregate, and employee experience (not even in the displayed drivers). That flattens risk. `appeal_seen_anywhere` is *critical* while `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is only *high*, even though both are equally unavailable at t0; process-feasibility belongs in the finding type, not as a severity discount for the same temporal leak. Relative to the stated quality bar, withholding `queue_length_at_t0` from credible drivers is over-conservative: the feature is the legitimate congestion signal the task asked to preserve, and the join defect is a repair, not the same class of contamination as a full variant id.

**Credible-driver directions still lean on the contaminated explainer.** Section 3 correctly says surviving signs are not clean-model SHAP. Section 4 then writes `"direction": "increase"/"decrease"` from the leaked attributions (and, for region, from that model alone). Complexity and elapsed time can be motivated from the process, but that is not how the JSON is framed, and Case Y’s sole remaining driver is a correlated proxy whose sign should have been marked unknown pending refit. The Case X causal boundary also names “channel” after channel was removed as unverified.

**Process-aware content is thinner than the validation essay suggests.** For Case X, `initial_damage_complexity = High` is left as a generic workload hint. Under the stated lifecycle, high complexity is exactly the information that makes the low-complexity fast track a poor description of the enabled path and makes evidence cycles the plausible continuation. That is a prefix-legal, non-causal mechanism the revised narrative does not use. `risk_score_current` is treated as untimestamped mutability only; a current risk score is also a plausible *label proxy* if it is trained on delay, reserve, or related outcomes. Case X’s `final_decision = Partial` is removed, but the audit never notes that this future label is the enabling condition for the leaked appeal flag, i.e. two views of the same unavailable suffix. Sequence patterns that start at the observed coverage event (fraud opening) are lumped with purely future pairs without discussing same-timestamp enablement at the cutoff.

**Section 2 is conceptually right and still not a crisp test suite.** Alignment, declarative constraints, prefix replay, suffix-invariance, and “DFG frequency ≠ norm” are used correctly, including parallel fraud and no duplicate mandatory assessment. The “minimum regression set” is a list of scenario names, not specified traces with expected pass/fail on availability vs conformance as separate oracles. That is a clarity gap in a section whose job was to describe a validation suite.

**Remaining nits.** The base-value row mixes feature leakage with background-distribution contamination. Calendar week is handled well as a policy/holiday confound, but the audit never asks whether the encoded week is t0, registration, or something else beyond trusting the lineage sentence. “Normally zero document requests at this checkpoint” is right for the given prefixes and should have been labeled as checkpoint-specific, not a general property of the feature after repair.

The piece is well above a weak SHAP-as-explanation audit. The misses are calibration, over-withholding of repairable congestion, residual dependence on leaked signs, and a few unused process facts—not structural failure. That is a high-8 answer, not a 9–10.
