**7.6**

This is a strong, coherent study design that avoids the usual failure modes (treating omitted cases as non-default, training only on the 21,600-row export, leaking post-decision fields, random splits). It is not nearly flawless. Several design choices are underspecified or internally inconsistent, and a few would bias estimates or evaluation if implemented as written.

## What is actually sound

The primary estimand is honest: 90-day default is defined only after activation, rejected/withdrawn/open cases are competing or undefined outcomes, and the counterfactual “default if activated” is correctly labeled as unidentified. The 48,000-row flow table balances, the 3,600 pending cases are not invented, and the five missingness mechanisms are kept distinct. The leakage table and the temporal split (including the Oct 13 / Feb 17 maturity arithmetic) are mostly right. The skeleton uses real PM4Py APIs, prefixes by event order, and does not invent a survival function.

That is the floor for a good answer, not a reason to ignore the gaps below.

## Material design flaws

**1. The primary landmark makes the advertised process features nearly vacuous.**  
K=3 with the third event required to be the first `Credit Review` is Start → KYC → Review on the stated happy path. Document loops, review revisits, variant rarity, DFG features, and most rework/handoff counts are then almost constant. The write-up still sells those as core process-aware features. A stage-based landmark (all events up to first Credit Review, variable K) or a later pre-decision landmark would be the process-aware choice; K=3 is a brittle event-count proxy for that stage. Extra post-migration technical events will silently destroy coverage rather than be absorbed.

**2. Evaluation is length-biased and this is not treated as selection.**  
`reporting_complete` requires activation + 125 days before 15 Feb 2026. Combined with a split on *application start*, late 2025 test cases enter the labeled set only if they activate unusually fast. Time-to-activation is likely associated with risk, channel, and the July 2025 policy. Section 7 notes that the migration block cannot support confirmatory 90-day evaluation, but it does not say that the Jul–Oct “test” block has the same problem in graded form. Inception-cohort language in §3 is not carried through to the validation design.

**3. The IPW target is sloppy.**  
The estimand is default among landmark-eligible journeys that activate under regime *g*. The text then says weights target “all historically activated accounts.” That mixes in cases that never reached the pre-decision landmark and changes the population the score is calibrated to. Feed-observation IPW among *reporting-complete, landmark-eligible, activated* cases is the coherent version; what is written is not.

**4. Horizon semantics from the prediction time are left implicit.**  
Y is 90 days after *activation*, so calendar time from the landmark to the outcome window is activation delay + 90 days and differs by case. That is defensible, but it is not a “90-day early warning” from now, and two applications with the same prefix are not at the same residual exposure time. A study that claims operational early warning needs that stated.

**5. Section 1 and Section 8 disagree about deployment.**  
§1 allows the score to be “shown on all applications” with a verbal caveat. §8 correctly says it is not calibrated for rejected or not-yet-activated cases. Showing a selection-conditional risk on the full applicant stream is exactly how this model gets misused. The decision-use boundary should have been tight in the target section, not repaired later.

**6. Subgroup evaluation is proposed without an events budget.**  
2,700 defaults, a 2024-only training window, a short mature post-policy test window, then slices by channel, region, amount, partner, transfer-linkage, and month. Many cells will have tens of events or fewer. Bootstrap CIs do not fix that. No minimum-event or pre-specified subgroup rule is given.

## Smaller but real issues

- Training only on 2024 is a clean shift test and a poor operational model: the primary fit never sees the post-July policy that the lender will actually run. Pre/post models are a sensitivity, not the validation plan.
- Case attributes and `risk_score` are taken as first/last non-null *inside the prefix*. That does not implement the warehouse-backfill risk the leakage table itself describes.
- Accepted-but-not-activated is not a first-class state; it is folded into still-open. Fine for the binary target, misleading for the multi-state alternative.
- Transfer linkage, activity renaming, and feed IPW are specified in prose and left as empty stubs (`ACTIVITY_MAP = {}`). After 15 Oct 2025 the eligibility rule depends on that map.
- Same-timestamp terminals are resolved by column order (activation first). That should be an audit failure, not a silent winner.
- High-cardinality `sequence` is dumped into the feature table with no encoding rule tied to training-only variants.

## Code skeleton

Functionally closer to an implementation than most skeletons: as-of labels, terminal classification, maturity flags, temporal split, prefix truncation, DFG on train only, leakage asserts. Failures relative to the spec and to the design:

- Length is ~220–260 lines against a stated 80–160. That is not “approximately” in range; it buries the audit path.
- `get_prefixes_from_log` usage is valid (truncate to first K, keep case IDs), and dropping short traces rather than padding is correct.
- Events are hard-cut at 31 Dec 2025 while the extract is 15 Feb 2026. Any activation between those dates is recoded still-open. If the snapshot’s 25,200 activations include January 2026 activations, the flow check will fire and the labels will not match terminals.
- `model_ready` requires full 90+35 even for known defaults, which matches an inception cohort but not the §3 sentence that positives may be used earlier.
- Elapsed-time prefixes are a required design element and do not appear even as a sketch.
- Discovering a DFG and only printing `len(dfg)` does not connect process structure to features; it is decorative.

## Verdict

Statistically literate and far above the weak-answer pattern. It would not be safe to treat as a near-final protocol. The main remaining ways this study would still mislead are: a landmark that erases process variation, a test set selected on fast activation, an IPW population that does not match the estimand, and a score whose validated meaning is narrower than the proposed display policy. Those are design errors, not writing nits, and they keep the grade well below the near-flawless band.
