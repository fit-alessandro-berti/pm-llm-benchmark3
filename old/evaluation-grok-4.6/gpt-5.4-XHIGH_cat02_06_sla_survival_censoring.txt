The answer is a strong, well-structured survival-analysis design that meets the required heading contract, puts estimands before estimators, keeps open cases in the risk set, and treats transfer/withdrawal as competing or informative rather than casual KM censoring. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several factual and identification errors keep it well below the top band.

**What is actually good.** Time origin, horizon, and the open-below-72 vs already-breached-open-above-72 distinction are correct. The three estimands map cleanly onto different decisions (customer promise, backlog control, rescue). Transfer is given multiple explicit assumptions. Section 3 uses real case IDs for survivor bias, competing terminals, stalled gaps, and non-random cohort mix (P1 5 vs 3; B non-AutoCheck paths S20/S23/S26/S30). Immortal time / leakage on `Escalate` is handled properly. Primary AJ plus transfer-side-by-side reporting is the right primary plan for n=32. The code embeds the table, encodes two estimands and three transfer modes, keeps opens, stratifies, implements a correct discrete-time AJ update, and builds a timestamp-free PM4Py log with `get_variants`.

**Factual errors on the supplied log.** The state diagram forces `Rework → Review` only; S27 is `Review>Rework>Resolve`. That is an observed transition, not a hypothetical. S28 is cited as a long path that re-enters `Review` after `Escalate` or `Rework`; its prefix is `Review>Escalate>Resolve` and does neither. Section 3 says S21 and S32 are P1 as if that were the distinctive transfer pattern; S08 is also P1, and section 6 later says all transfers are P1. Using AutoCheck in cohort A as compliance evidence is sloppy: the prompt’s incomplete-rollout signal is B cases that never hit AutoCheck.

**Identification and estimator mixing.** Landmarking at 24/48h on “stage so far,” “escalation already occurred,” and `last_event_gap_h` is recommended as the secondary method, but intermediate times are not in the extract. For a case still active at 48h you cannot recover stage or gap at 48h from a terminal prefix plus a cutoff gap. The write-up correctly says a timed multi-state model is unidentified, then proposes landmark covariates that fail for the same reason. Estimand 3, `P(72 < T_resolve < ∞)`, is not identified with opens still past 72 (S09, S11, S25, S26); `CIF(max)−CIF(72)` is a lower bound and is printed as “late-resolution mass” without that caveat. The binary `breach72` “observed-case” mean is a complete-case proportion on uncensored-by-72 rows, not `1−CIF_resolve(72)`. Bounds and AJ are present, but the prose treats those quantities as interchangeable.

**Overclaim.** “A process-aware view will usually shrink or even reverse” the resolved-only B advantage is a hypothesis stated as a likely fact, with no in-text numbers. Transfer sensitivity is called “material” for a move on the order of several points among known cases. IPCW on 32 rows is mentioned without enough warning that it is unstable here.

**Smaller nits.** `aalen_johansen(..., horizon)` ignores `horizon`. Assumptions are discussed but not consistently labeled. `last_event_gap_h` is a now-or-terminal feature, not a reconstructable time-varying covariate. The code is a plan demonstration, not a full landmarked analysis.

The piece would sit in the high 8s if the diagram, S28 citation, landmark identification, and late-CIF bound had been exact. They are not.
