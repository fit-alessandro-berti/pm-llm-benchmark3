**5.7**

The write-up hits the required seven-section skeleton and is recognizably grounded in the logs, so it is not a generic ops essay. It is also well short of a near-flawless redesign. Several claims misuse the process-mining evidence, the “robust” package contains a quantitative priority error the prompt warned against, and a few mechanisms are invented rather than inferred.

**What works.** The four scenarios map onto the four mandated disruption families. Section 2 at least attempts bottleneck *sequences* and names downstream overload (I1 → WIP/starvation; I3 → M2 repeats; I6 → benches; I7 → pre-analytical; I4 → technical validation). Scenario policies have on/off triggers and a real I4 guardrail. Residual vulnerabilities include redesign-caused harm (false rejects, cross-train theft from tech val, reservation-driven Routine p90). The dashboard splits leading/lagging and ties several thresholds to the given peak queues (pre-analytical ~9.5 h, accession ~14 h, specialist ~36 h).

**Process-mining evidence is sloppy.** Recollection is cited as `760 → 780` as if the loop barely moved. Those are *period totals* over 8 vs 3 weeks. Weekly volume is ~2,300 vs ~3,300; pre-analytical→recollection is ~4.2% vs ~8.0%; accession→recollection is ~14% vs ~26%; recollection→cancel is ~54% vs ~71%. The conclusion “rework gets worse” is right; the evidence presented is wrong. The most important DFG *structure* shifts are underused: technical validation→medical validation rises from ~24.5% to ~47%; tech val→release falls from ~74% to ~56%; hold-for-specialist roughly quadruples as a share of cases. Raw frequencies are often dumped as decoration.

**The robust package is not robust.** Reserving **30%** of bench/machine slots for Urgent *and* Complex-panel jointly contradicts both the data and the constraint “do not assume all case types can be prioritized simultaneously.” Peak mix is already Urgent 29% + Complex 20% = **49%**. A 30% reservation is undersized on the historical peak, forces the two premium classes to fight each other, and will spill Complex-panel into Routine batches—the opposite of robustness. I8 is labeled robust even though registration is not binding (68%→85%) and the prompt’s own conflict note is that I8 *exposes* pre-analytical and assay. I7 does nothing for S2/S3. The station with the worst peak queues (specialist 36 h, medical 22 h) and the only resource over 100% (technical validation 101%) are almost absent from the always-on package; I4, when used, makes the 101% station worse.

**Scenario design is thin.** S1 is mostly a restatement of the given 3-week peak, not a constructed stress case. There is no compound scenario (mix shift + Friday specialist gap + 10:00/17:00 courier spike), which is the realistic winter operating point. S2 never specifies M1 vs M2, duration, or remaining capacity. S3 mis-locates WIP: specialist hold sits *after* technical validation, so absence piles work at hold/medical release, not automatically in the tech-val queue unless release from tech val is gated. I5 “handover errors” is not in the log.

**I3 as a high-load policy is the conflict case.** Activating M1 dedication when M2 repeats or utilization is already high is exactly when routing all Routine onto the higher-failure machine is most dangerous. “Monitor repeats” is not a mitigation. Repeat-after-failure (peak median 145 h, p90 240 h, night-shift/M2 clustering) is never given a first-class control loop.

**Table and dashboard are weaker than they look.** Directional effects in the trade-off table are unreadable (arrows dropped; several cells are fragments like “median wait” / “Possible errors”). I8’s coverage (S1, S4) contradicts calling it a cross-scenario robust lever. Nine “leading” indicators are not a *minimum* dashboard; several are coincident queues, not leading signals. Missing true leads: inbound mix (Urgent+Complex share), specialist calendar, courier arrival CV, M2 night-shift repeat rate, digital-metadata completeness. Bypass <60% and several queue-age triggers fire after the peak state is already in place.

**Net.** Competent format and some real bottleneck-migration reasoning, undermined by rate-blind DFG use, an internally inconsistent priority design, over-claiming I8, and a robust package that leaves the actual peak chokepoints to optional overlays. Not a 3 (structure and constraints are mostly respected). Nowhere near a 8–10 under a hypercritical bar.
