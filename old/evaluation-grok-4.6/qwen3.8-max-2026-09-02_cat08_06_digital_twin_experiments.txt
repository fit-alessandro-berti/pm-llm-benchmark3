**7.2**

The answer is a strong, complete design that hits the required eight sections and avoids the explicit failure modes (independent-case-only simulation, ignored calendars, silent waiting/service collapse, treating objections as observed, fake optimality, `pm4py.read_csv`). It is not nearly flawless. Several concrete inconsistencies and design gaps keep it well below a high score.

**What is solid.** Scope, entities, and state are specified at the right grain: cases, tasks, pools, calendars, architect groups, district bundles, and latent objection clusters. Arrivals are non-stationary (season, weekday, architect bursts). Parameter estimation is process-mining grounded (variants, DFG, performance DFG, sojourn vs inferred start reconstruction). Assumptions are split into the required categories. The experiment table includes baseline, D1–D10, extra combinations, and four stress scenarios with trade-offs. Warm-up is correctly treated as transient rather than steady-state; CIs, CRN, heavy tails, and sensitivity are all present. Validation covers face, replay, distributions, utilization, bottlenecks, and holdout. Decision rules are multi-criteria and explicitly non-optimal. The Python skeleton follows the stated PM4Py conventions, wraps version-fragile calls, and keeps log handling separate from the engine.

**Material defects.**

1. **Contradiction with the log on arrivals.** P002 is submitted Saturday 18:21 via the portal. The arrival generator sets weekend factors to 0 and never models 24/7 portal intake waiting for the next clerk shift. Monday 2.2× Wednesday is applied without renormalizing the weekly volume against a base rate computed over all calendar days, so generated demand will not match the historical rate.

2. **Council-week legal restriction is dead code.** Legal officers are given pattern `"mon_fri_09_17"`, but `is_available` only skips council weeks if `"legal" in self.pattern`. A stated calendar rule from the problem therefore never fires.

3. **Seeds are not reproducible.** `abs(hash(scenario.name))` depends on `PYTHONHASHSEED`. That undercuts the replication/uncertainty section’s reproducibility claims.

4. **Working time vs clock time is unspecified.** Planning p90 is 14 hours and legal p90 is 16 hours—more than one shift. The design never states that sampled service must be consumed only inside calendars (with overnight/holiday interrupt). That omission biases every cycle-time and utilization result.

5. **Cross-case machinery is thinner in the skeleton than in the prose.** Inspection batching is a stub; architect grouping and objection clusters have no types or event hooks, even as placeholders. No-shows are named once and then dropped. Planner skill split (two simple-only planners) and Monday portal-support dedication appear in text but not in pools.

6. **SLA targets are muddled.** “Median target” / “p90 target” sit next to SLA compliance, and simple-renovation 20-day targets sit below the empirical missing-docs median (22d). Baseline SLA rates would be hard to interpret.

7. **Minor PM4Py nits.** `get_performance_dfg` is not a stable public name (fallback saves this). `main()` computes the required statistics and then discards them with no print/return of the actual objects beyond a nested dict.

The design reasoning, experiment plan, and missing-data treatment are good enough that this is clearly above a middling answer. The log contradiction, broken legal calendar, unreproducible seeds, and calendar-time gap are enough that a strict reading cannot go much higher.
