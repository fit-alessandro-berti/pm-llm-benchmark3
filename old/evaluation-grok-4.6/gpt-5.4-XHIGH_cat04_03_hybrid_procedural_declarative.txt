**7.2**

This is a strong, well-structured hybrid design that mostly uses each formalism for what it can actually express. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading there are internal inconsistencies, a few API/semantics slips, and several places where the implemented model is stronger or weaker than the stated policy without the verdict matrix fully tracking that.

## What is actually good

The R5/R6 write-up is the right shape: two incompatible policies, an explicit implemented choice, and a real compliance risk (false positives on legitimate emergency activations). The answer does not quietly merge Legal and Operations into one activity-only rule.

The R1–R12 mapping is complete and mostly honest. Control-flow exclusivity goes to the net, pairwise negatives and eventualities to DECLARE, frequency bounds to the skeleton, statistical latency to the temporal profile, and SoD / emergency evidence / instance-relative bank-change duties to an external layer. That partition is defensible.

The Petri net is the best part of the submission. The silent AND-split, the clarification token that blocks `Classify Risk` while a request is open, the exclusive post-classification routes, and the escalated join of `Compliance Approval` and `Obtain Legal Opinion` before `Activate Vendor` are coherent. Token-replay of T1–T8 against that net matches the claimed procedural column, except where the matrix later refuses to own the implemented strict policy on T6.

DECLARE template choice is mostly literate. The write-up correctly distinguishes `response` from `altresponse`, `precedence` from `response`, and `noncoexistence` from `nonsuccession`. The R5 overlay (`response(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)` plus `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)`) is a real approximation with a disclosed false-positive mode, not a handwave.

Temporal arithmetic is correct: hours were converted to seconds, `zeta = 2.0` bands are right, and the T7 34-hour escalation-to-opinion deviation is the only temporal warning among the eight traces. T7’s 2-hour payment is inside `72 ± 72`.

## Material defects

**1. The “compact formal description” contradicts the net.**  
Section 3.1 says the escalated path is  
`(Enhanced Due Diligence || Obtain Legal Opinion) → Compliance Approval → Activate Vendor`.  
That forces Compliance to wait for the opinion. The net does not: Compliance is enabled by EDD alone; only Activate waits for both. T6’s `Compliance Approval@12` before `Obtain Legal Opinion@23` is legal in the code and illegal in the prose. For a modeling exam that asked for a compact formal description *and* code, this is a real error, not a wording quibble.

**2. T6 is scored as if no policy had been chosen.**  
The implemented model is strict Legal. Under that model T6 is a hard fail in the net, a hard fail in DECLARE (`nonsuccession`), and a hard fail in the R6 checker when `strict_legal=True`. The matrix still writes `policy-dependent` for the resource/data cell and for the overall verdict. The prose explains both resolutions, which the prompt asked for, but it never actually applies the chosen resolution. A strict answer would have been `fail` under the implemented model, with a second line or annotation for the exception-aware reading.

**3. Post-activation activities are excluded from the net and then forgotten.**  
Omitting `Change Bank Details` / `Suspend Vendor` / later `Verify Bank Account` is a legitimate backbone choice. It is not legitimate to then recommend “run procedural conformance against the canonical Petri net” without saying that those labels must be projected away first. On any real R11 case the net will report move-on-log deviations even when the external R11 checker passes. Section 9 discusses instance-relative duties but not this layer collision. That is a deployment bug.

**4. Several constraints are silently tightened past the brief.**  
- Escalation exists only as a fourth XOR arm after classification, on an accepted high-risk path. R7 only requires it after screening. A case that escalates and then rejects, or escalates on a low-risk path, is unrepresentable. This is disclosed once as an assumption and then treated as if it were the requirement.  
- Clarification is frozen after `Classify Risk`. R3 only requires pairing before the next request and before a final outcome.  
- `nonsuccession(Activate Vendor, Obtain Legal Opinion)` is global, not conditioned on escalation. They admit the over-approximation; they still put it in the operational DECLARE model at confidence 0.9, which is a second, weaker policy hiding inside the “strict” one.

**5. Resource-layer imprecision.**  
R9 is “the” verification versus “the applicable” approval. The code intersects every `Verify Bank Account` resource with every approval resource after `dropna()`. Missing resource *values* (as opposed to a missing column) become silent passes. A later R11 re-verification by the earlier approver would be reported as an R9 failure. The `filter_four_eyes_principle` comment also treats the return value as the suspect set; in PM4Py that filter keeps cases that *satisfy* four-eyes. The hand-written intersection is the actual check and is closer to right, but the named API is misused.

**6. Smaller coherence nits that add up.**  
The R2 row shows a 3-way XOR; the net is 4-way. `activ_freq` forces `Classify Risk` and `Close Onboarding` to occur exactly once, which is a closed-world assumption not stated as loudly as the others. T5’s temporal cell is `pass` because payment timing is fine; the unanswered-clarification pair is simply absent, which should have been called out the way T3 was. Support/confidence are handled thoughtfully in Section 9, but putting 0.95 / 0.9 on “should” rules is still a risk if a checker treats those fields as enablement thresholds rather than commentary.

## Test matrix

T1–T5, T7, and T8 are scored correctly on the models as written: T4 is a pure R9 fail, T5 is a genuine R3 fail in net / DECLARE / skeleton, T7 is control-flow-legal with a temporal warning, T8 is a terminal-outcome contradiction. That is better than most answers on this prompt. It does not redeem the T6 scoring problem, and it never shows an R11-bearing trace, so the most fragile part of the hybrid design is untested.

## Bottom line

This is a competent, above-average design with real command of DECLARE idioms, skeleton orientation, and Petri-net encoding. It is not exam-flawless. The formal description and the net disagree, the chosen R5/R6 policy is not the policy used in the T6 verdict, and the excluded post-activation fragment would break the recommended conformance order. Those are not presentation issues. They are modeling errors. That caps the grade in the low 7s.
