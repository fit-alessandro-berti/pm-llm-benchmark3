**6.8**

This is a strong design essay that gets the core identification problem right, then loses a good deal of credit to statistical sloppiness, internal contradictions, and a skeleton that does not implement the rules the prose just announced. It is far above a naive “train on the 21,600 and call everyone else non-default” answer, but it is not close to flawless.

**What is actually good.** The primary target refuses a well-defined 90-day default for applications that were never exposed to credit. Rejected, withdrawn, transferred, and open cases are not recoded as `default=0`. The 48,000-row cohort flow is complete and arithmetically consistent (21,600 + 26,400). The five missingness mechanisms are distinguished instead of being collapsed into one “unlabeled” bin. The leakage table hits every required element (`final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, as-of `risk_score`, extract-time resource experience, label fields) with the right keep/repair/drop calls. Random splits are rejected for the right reasons (policy change, migration, maturity, temporal dependence). Limitations and feedback loops are stated at the correct causal altitude.

Those strengths are why this is not a 5. The deductions below are why it is not an 8+.

**1. The estimand is not a well-specified competing-risk target.**  
Section 1 writes \(CIF_j(t_0)=P(\text{State}=j\mid\mathcal{H}(t_0))\) and includes `Censored_Immature/Open` as a cause. That is not a cumulative incidence function. Censoring is a missing-data process, not a process outcome; its probability is an artifact of the extract date. Section 3 then quietly drops censoring and fits five causes with IPCW. The two sections disagree.

Worse, `Default_90` and `NoDefault_90` live on the activation clock, while `Rejected`/`Withdrawn`/`Transferred` live on the application clock. A single Fine-Gray/CIF from an early landmark \(t_0\) is not identified on a common time scale. The identity \(P(\text{Default}\mid\text{Activation})=CIF_D/(CIF_D+CIF_{ND})\) is a multinomial renormalization of *eventual states*, not a competing-risk identity. Calling the object a CIF while defining a mixture of eventual-state probabilities, delayed-label windows, and administrative censoring is a real specification error, not wording.

Related muddles: withdrawn is labeled both a competing event and informative censoring; Accepted is listed as a landmark-terminal state in §1 but is not a terminal event in the process (Accepted → Contract Signed → Activated). Landmarking only on cases still “alive” at \(t_0\) also quietly changes the population from “every application” to “applications that survived to the landmark.” That should have been the estimand’s population statement.

**2. Horizon and label delay are conflated.**  
The 90-day default definition is not a 90+35-day outcome. The extra 7–35 days are *when the warehouse learns the 90-day label*. Treating \([t_{act}, t_{act}+90d+35d]\) as the prediction horizon mixes the scientific target with the observation lag. The text sometimes knows this (`label_available_at`) and sometimes does not.

**3. The validation windows do not do what they claim.**  
Train is “starts in [2024-01-01, 2025-03-31] *and* mature by 2025-06-30.” Maturity needs activation + 90d + up to 35d, i.e. activation by roughly late February 2025. March 2025 starts cannot mature by 30 June 2025. The “~60% of mature cases” figure is invented. Validation starts in April–June 2025 with a 1 October 2025 cutoff have the same problem. TEST-1 reuses the validation start cohort, so it is not an independent test. The code then ignores all of this and splits only on `start_time`, never applying `case_tbl_train_cutoff`. Design and implementation are not the same study.

**4. Prefix / leakage engineering is better in the table than in the pipeline.**  
The audit table is the best part of the answer. The code does not execute it.

- Leak columns are never dropped. They ride along from the CSV into the 72h prefix.
- The assert is `columns absent OR all-NA`. If `label_available_at` / `default_90d` are denormalized onto every event of a mature case—the usual warehouse pattern—the 72h slice still contains them and the assert fails (or, if an implementer deletes the assert, the features leak). “Repeated on events when known” does not protect you when the export writes the case-level label onto early rows.
- `documents_total` is “repaired” by counting `Request Documents` activities, which is not the same attribute as the nightly snapshot.
- `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time` is not justified on a log with a single `time:timestamp` and no start/complete lifecycle. Service vs waiting is not identified.
- Chaining `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` into `insert_case_service_waiting_time` then a case-level `groupby` is unspecified: those helpers do not all return an event log of the same grain.
- `first_term` takes `iloc[0]` without an explicit timestamp sort inside the group.
- `get_prefixes_from_log` is called on the *full* train log (correct for k-prefixes) while the leakage assert is run on a different 72h object; there is no check that prefix timestamps are ≤ \(t_0\) per case, which the prose promised.
- Unused `pm4py.util.constants`; unused maturity table; cohort audit prints `terminal_type` rather than the A–F table.

The skeleton is in the requested length band and uses the requested PM4Py entry points, but it is not “concrete enough to implement” without re-doing the hard parts.

**5. Smaller but real issues.**  
No single primary landmark is chosen (k ∈ {3,5} or h ∈ {24,72,168}). Short terminals are excluded from k-prefixes, so the “every application” claim is again false for fixed-k models. Transfer linkage (60%) is a sensitivity only; the unit of analysis is never reconciled with re-identified partner cases. Partner MNAR feeds are described well and then absent from the primary fitting strategy. Policy change is an indicator, not a pre-specified interaction or separate regime model. IPCW + Fine-Gray is named without stating the censoring model, the time origin, or that Fine-Gray subdistribution hazards for “NoDefault at 90d after a random activation time” are poorly defined. `pm4py.split_train_test` is correctly sidelined.

**Net.** The author understands selection, as-of logic, and why the business question is ill-posed. That is the main thing the rubric asked for. The statistical object they then put on the page is a collage of multinomial states, CIFs, landmarking, and IPCW that would not survive a methods review, the temporal split is arithmetically inconsistent, and the PM4Py pipeline does not enforce the leakage rules the table states. Under a strict reading that is a clearly incomplete 7, not a high 8 or 9.
