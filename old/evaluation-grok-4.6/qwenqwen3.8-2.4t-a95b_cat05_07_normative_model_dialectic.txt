**7.8**

The final write-up is strong on structure, case coverage, and both-sides advocacy, but it is not nearly flawless. Several authority, overlay, and model-semantics slips are real, and a few of them sit inside the judgment the author then treats as settled.

**What is solid.** The piece uses the required headings, argues A and B at strength, and does not flatten everything into “mixed.” Burden of proof is explicit. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including control-flow-clean resource failures (SUP-712, SUP-715). Core calls are right: 701 compliant; 703/708 severe; 704 inside FD-7; 705/714 unauthorized after expiry; 706 indeterminate; 707/709/710/713 model or scope; 712 SoD. The layered revision (POWL vs DECLARE vs data checks vs org controls) is the right architecture, and the PM4Py sketch actually encodes the required routes, concurrent standard checks, finite RMI, reject/notify/close, plus overlay tables.

**Authority is softer than the author’s own test.** Section 1 says an exception needs documentary authority, time, eligibility, and compensating controls. PD-3 was never folded into CS-9/M0, and the compliance observer later denied approving a weakening. Classifying SUP-702 as `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` (even with “medium” confidence) is a judgment the brief does not fully earn. Steering-committee minutes can be *evidence of a monitored trial*; they are not shown to be a valid derogation. That is the main logical crack.

**Evidence slips.** N4’s CRM cutover is 2026-07-01; SUP-702 is 2026-06-15. Treating N4 as that case’s overlay is chronologically wrong, even if the case text mentions a CRM `notification_id`. Severity also drifts: the matrix gives 702 and 706 “low to medium,” the judgment table drops both to Low. The 289 / 14 / 9 / 6 Auto_Approve aggregates are used rhetorically but never classified under the same rubric as the sampled cases.

**Model semantics are mostly right, not tight.** RMI is only on the standard branch and is unordered with both checks, so Quality can finish before information requests complete—looser than the “before decision / before evidence arrives” practice the defense relies on. Auto-approval *requires* `Automated_Master_Data_Validation`, which appears in no historical trace; that is fine as a *future* norm and badly underspecified as a conformance baseline. The 0–5 XOR unroll of same-label `Request_More_Info` transitions is an admitted approximation, but the write-up never flags the practical PM4Py risk that label-based equality can collapse those nodes. Defense also underplays a clean process-aware point: M0 forces Quality before *reject*, which CS-9 does not.

**Net.** Dialectical fairness and case-level traceability are well above average; the revision is usable. The score is held down by the PD-3 exception overreach, the N4 dating error, internal severity inconsistency, and several control-flow approximations that are stated more confidently than they are engineered.
