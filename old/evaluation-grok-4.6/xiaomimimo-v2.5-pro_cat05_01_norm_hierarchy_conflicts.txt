The write-up is structurally complete and, at the level of method, stronger than most answers to this prompt. **What holds up**

The source treatment is mostly disciplined. Board policy is treated as a structural floor; A7 is correctly identified as the only stated waiver path for GPIP-4 integrity controls; B7/C4 are confined to ordinary local timing and supplier-onboarding; D1–D4 are read as Finance interpretation plus a self-limiting integrity clause, not as a controlled policy. Scope is handled well on the easy cases: P-504 is outside “critical sterilization-line lots,” and P-503’s backup pumps with three-day stock are treated as a contested, audit-vulnerable claim rather than as automatically covered. Timing is handled well on P-501 (commitment at 08:50 cannot borrow ECD-2’s 09:00 window) and, conceptually, on P-505 (supplier call/email over a late ERP stamp). The alternatives in §5 are real alternatives: each names the premise that would have to change. The policy rewrite in §7 is concrete on authority, thresholds, SoD, new suppliers, retrospective records, expiry, and emergency normalization.

**Material errors**

Thresholds are wrong in the case table. P-501 is EUR 38,000, so GPIP-4 A2 / the author’s R2 (EUR 50,000) does not fire; the live local rule is B2 at EUR 25,000. P-504 is EUR 24,000, so B2 / R7 Plant and Finance approvals do not fire either. Listing those as deviations is a basic trigger error. P-501’s A1/R1 finding is also unsupported: ENG-1 requested and SHIFT-1 committed; there is no fact that either person was the final business approver. The live SoD problem on that case is B4 delegation (Plant Manager reachable, no active delegation), which the answer mixes into R1.

The Finance/CFO-email theory is not stable across cases. P-505 (EUR 88,000, Finance at 19:00, after ECD-2 expired) is **COMPLIANT** because D1 supposedly substitutes operational approvals. P-507 (EUR 110,000, still ≤ EUR 150,000, Compliance and IC authorization before commitment, signed PDF inside the four-hour C3 window) is **NONCOMPLIANT** because D1 is “persuasive only.” P-508 (EUR 155,000, above D1’s ceiling, no Finance event) is then **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**. Those three outcomes cannot be generated by one reading of D1/D3/D4 and A2. If D1 cannot defeat A2, P-505 cannot be COMPLIANT. If D1 can substitute Finance up to EUR 150,000 during the window, P-507 should move with P-505, not against it. P-508 is the case where D1 is weakest, yet it receives the more favorable label.

P-502 as **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** fights the author’s own rules. A3 requires Compliance_Clearance before commitment for a new supplier; clearance is at 18:30 after a 10:15 commitment; A4 leaves non-tendering controls in place; A6 says later approval does not automatically cure; R3 in §3 says no instrument other than A7 waives that rule; and the organizational facts say incident directives have not been treated as overlays for new-supplier clearance. D1 is also not yet in existence (sent 07-15 07:20). Calling the late clearance a satisfied R3, then classifying the case as a justified exception because controls were “ultimately” completed, is post-hoc cure under another name.

**Smaller but real defects**

R11 (ON-44, 14 July 08:30–13:00) is applied to P-505 on 18 July. The case’s own clock-offset fact is enough; the 14 July outage is not a free-floating license to discount ERP time on other days. Assumption 10 puts the burden on the party claiming compliance, then P-505 is still called COMPLIANT on a commitment that is inside the window only if one resolves a disputed clock in the buyer’s favor—**INDETERMINATE** or **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** would have matched the stated burden. P-506 lists R8 as satisfied while describing a missing emergency form. Several “Satisfied rules” cells include rules that were late or only arguably met. Severity is asserted, not calibrated: P-504 (EUR 24,000 cafeteria) is Critical on the same scale as P-503 (EUR 140,000 SoD plus missing Finance and Compliance), while P-507’s missing Finance on EUR 110,000 is only Moderate.

**Bottom line**

This is a serious process-compliance analysis with a usable hierarchy and good alternatives, not a superficial score-sheet. It is not close to flawless. Wrong numeric triggers, a self-contradictory D1 application, and a P-502 exception that the author’s own A3/A4/A6/R3 block are enough to keep the score in the low sixes under a strict reading.
