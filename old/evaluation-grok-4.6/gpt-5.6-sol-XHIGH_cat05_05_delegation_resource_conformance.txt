**8.2**

The answer is strong on the hard parts of this case: event-time roles (especially Eva), alias normalization, service-account attribution, delegation evidence timing, prohibited subdelegation, and the suspicion-versus-proof line. It is not flawless, and the remaining defects are enough to keep it well short of a top-band score.

**What is actually good**

Event-time reasoning is disciplined. Eva is not treated as QM because a later master says Quality Engineering. Asha’s leave is not treated as a revocation of standing QM authority. A later QMS row is not treated as proof of an earlier delegation; the signed PDF is used as the real pre-use evidence, and CHG-507 is correctly excluded because it predates both the interval and the PDF. D-IC-04 is rejected on the no-subdelegation clause rather than on informal “email looks weak.” `b.koch` / `u_bk` are collapsed before SoD, which is the only way CHG-508 is a real RR1 failure. `svc_qms` is not globally rewritten to Diego. RR6 is applied as stated: pair frequency is a screening indicator, not proof.

The rule formalization is mostly precise. Unknown human attribution is indeterminate, not a four-eyes pass. RR2 is not inflated into a hidden “must still hold Independent Verifier.” Business-day counting for RR4 is explicit, internally consistent, and does not change either emergency conclusion under a same-time alternative. Alternative explanations are actually paired, not decorative.

**Material analytical defects**

CHG-505 is over-aggregated. Late retrospective review is a definite RR4 failure. Farid’s approval is only as good as an unproven CI-44 link. D-QM-01 does not obviously authorize a `cooling_system` retrospective review. Ben’s account is on certified sick leave. Those are different findings. Folding them into primary `UNAUTHORIZED` with a slashed secondary `INDETERMINATE/DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` blurs authority failure, deadline failure, scope mismatch, and identity dispute. The write-up understands the pieces; the classification does not.

CHG-504 as `AUTHORIZED` is slightly too clean. The VPN window matches, and leave does not strip QM authority, but the file itself says credential sharing cannot be excluded. The identity caveat is present; the primary label still treats the act as authorized rather than authorized-if-and-only-if Asha was the operator.

The matrix overstates Ben’s absence condition (“her recorded leave covers the interval”) after the narrative has already admitted Asha was on VPN during that interval. Those two statements needed to be reconciled in the matrix, not only later in prose.

**Code defects**

The pandas interval logic and the three-valued delegation checks are the best part of Section 5. The network extraction is not. `discover_handover_of_work_network` / `discover_working_together_network` commonly return an SNA object with a `.connections` map, or a NetworkX graph. The converter only understands `.items()`. If that object is not dict-like, the required handover and working-together output silently becomes empty. That is a real gap against an explicit requirement, even though the rest of the script is careful (`keep_violations` detection, alias-first SoD, unresolved service identity, no automatic collusion label).

Smaller inconsistencies: D-IC-04 is `preexisting_evidence=True` despite “email only”; open-ended roles end on the unexplained sentinel `2262-04-11`; delegation ends are `23:59:00` rather than end-of-day. None of these flip a case, but they undercut the claim that the code faithfully encodes the evidence grades used in the narrative.

**Lesser unclarities**

“None/low” is not a severity. Dual cooling emergencies on the same day are never tested as one CI-44 incident with two change records. Diego is standing IC and also the implementer on the emergency cases; that is not forbidden by the written rules, but it deserved a one-line SoD/control-objective note. Those are omissions, not misreads.

**Bottom line**

This is a high-quality investigation memo with the right control instincts and almost no invented misconduct. It loses material points for one overloaded case classification, one over-confident identity call, and network code that may not actually emit the PM4Py evidence it promises. Under a strict reading, that is an 8.2, not a 9-band answer.
