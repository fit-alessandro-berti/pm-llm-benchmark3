The write-up is unusually complete and, on the compliance substance, mostly well controlled: event-time roles, alias collapse before SoD, RR5/RR6 discipline, and refusal to treat pair frequency as proof. Under a strict reading it is still not near-flawless. Several defects are material, and a few are internal contradictions.

**PM4Py checks are not trustworthy as written.** `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(..., keep_violations=True)` and the same flag on `filter_activity_done_different_resources` are not part of the public API. The high-level four-eyes helper keeps *conforming* cases. If the kwargs are rejected the block crashes; if they are ignored the printed “violations” are the compliant set. Claiming “verified output” of `{CHG-502, CHG-508, CHG-510}` is therefore unsupported. The pandas pivot is the only SoD check that would actually do what Section 3 asserts. That is a serious miss on a rubric that explicitly scores PM4Py quality.

**The RR4 path in code is brittle.** `df["emergency"] == True` will not match if `read_csv` leaves `"true"`/`"false"` as strings (the usual default). Section 5’s own RR3 loop uses `str(...).lower() == "true"`; RR4 does not. Emergency review logic can silently skip CHG-505 and CHG-510. D-IC-03 is also flattened to `{"cooling_system"}`, which the prose itself says the log does not justify.

**Section 3 and Section 5 disagree on CHG-509.** The matrix of findings calls it `VALID_SUBSTITUTION` (secondary `INDETERMINATE`). The code’s own absence predicate fails D-QM-02 on 15 Aug, and the commentary admits that. RR3 was formalized as five *conjunctive* tests. If a required conjunct is unproven, the honest primary label is `INDETERMINATE`, not a valid substitution. That is a classification error, not a nuance.

**Availability is reasoned cleanly in one place and overwritten in another.** Leave does not strip Asha’s QM role (correct). The same leave is then treated as automatically satisfying D-QM-01’s “principal absent” clause even during a live VPN session she claims she operated. That may be the better policy reading, but it is not argued against the competing reading (she was factually available at 09:22). Ben’s sick leave is later loaded as a third “failure mode” of CHG-505’s review, which is closer to an identity caveat than an authority void—the opposite of the Asha rule. The two absences are not held to one standard.

**A few case calls are tighter than the evidence.** CHG-504 as primary `AUTHORIZED` is too firm once security cannot exclude credential sharing; identity is the open question, so `INDETERMINATE` / `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` should lead. CHG-505 as a single `UNAUTHORIZED` collapses a possibly valid IC approval into the failed retrospective control; the row text knows this and the label does not. CI-44 is assumed for D-IC-03 after stating the incident ID is missing. None of these are wild, but they over-resolve ambiguity the prompt told the analyst not to resolve.

**What remains strong, and why this is not lower.** The actor matrix, alias/service-account handling, late-DB-versus-signed-PDF point, CHG-507/508/510 legal analysis, and Section 4’s paired benign/adverse explanations are high-grade. Controls and the executive close match the record. Those parts are why this is a 7 rather than a mid-5: the investigation is real; the executable checks and a few headline classifications are not as tight as the prose pretends.
