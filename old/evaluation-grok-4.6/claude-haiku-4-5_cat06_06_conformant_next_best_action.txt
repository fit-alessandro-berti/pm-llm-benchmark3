The answer has the right *shape* of a strong response—A/B last, G not enabled, a conformant second-review primary path, parallel information-gathering, and an S2/S4 spillover analysis—but it would not survive a careful audit. The quantitative apparatus is internally inconsistent, several process facts are rewritten to favor the preferred plan, and the ranking contradicts the author’s own dominance arguments.

### Fatal or near-fatal analytical errors

**The ranking disagrees with itself.** Section 1 ranks E above F. Section 3’s own expected-value table says E is dominated (−€13k to −€71.5k) while F is positive *if* the rejection threshold holds (+€18k to +€103k). Section 6 then silently swaps them (F third, E fourth). A ranking that cannot stay consistent across the six required sections is not a ranking.

**Expected values are double-counted, then used as if they were decisive.** Fuzzy-match confidence 0.46 is first treated as P(true match)=0.46, producing “expected regulatory harm” of 0.46 × €100k–€500k = €46k–€230k. For F this figure is multiplied by 0.46 *again* (€21k–€106k). False-rejection harm is likewise probability-weighted twice (0.25–0.45 baked into €5k–€50k, then ×0.54). Those numbers are the backbone of Section 3 and the “net position” table. They are not usable.

**The 0.46 figure is also inverted.** Section 3 uses 46% true-positive; Section 2 claims “0.46 confidence suggests 54% true-positive probability.” That is not a slip in tone. It is a sign the probabilistic story was never checked.

**Fuzzy-match confidence is not a calibrated P(sanctioned party).** A 0.46 name-similarity score is not a 46% prior that execution is unlawful. Using it to shrink the prompt’s already-asymmetric €100k–€500k regulatory range makes A/B look *less* dominated than the case statement itself. The qualitative conclusion (A/B unacceptable) is right; the quantitative justification is the wrong kind of right.

### Process and action-definition errors

**Action D is misread.** The prompt’s D is already “alternate screening, *then* a second-officer review” by S2 or S4. Rank 1 (C1) is essentially D done in parallel with waiting for S4. Rank 5 then re-ranks “D” as screening *without* a concurrent second review and slaps on a homemade label, `CONFORMANT IF PURSUED IN PARALLEL WITH C1/C2`. That label is not one of the four allowed strings. The table therefore both double-counts D and violates the output contract.

**C1’s timeline is arithmetically incompatible with the given durations.** S4 arrives in 35–65 minutes. The only review duration in the prompt is 35–75 minutes, plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute. Feasible totals are roughly 75–155 minutes, not “55–105” or “decision by min 70.” The 30–45 minute S4 review used in the final recommendation is invented to make the 90-minute SLA look attainable. Branch A1 then calls execution at minute 80–100 an **SLA MET** outcome, which is already false for any time >90.

**A valid Rule 10 override is treated as still unlawful.** G is correctly tagged `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`. The commentary that even a completed director authorization plus incident identifier “does not reduce compliance harm” and still carries €100k–€500k regulatory exposure contradicts Rule 10: that path is a permitted exception once both conditions actually hold. The low rank is fine; the theory of exceptions is not.

**E is ranked as if it were a serious next action.** Six-to-24-hour evidence collection almost certainly burns the deadline and the €15k–€80k customer-harm range. Ranking it third—above a policy-conditional reject and above the prompt’s actual D—is not implied by conformance, by the author’s EV table, or by the instruction to favor a conformant *second-review* strategy.

### Invented quantities presented as case facts

P-901’s monetary loss (€18k–€50k), cascade risk to a “third case,” API-call cost €200–€500, re-screen failure 15%, director reachability 40%, override-granted-if-reached 20%, risk drop below 40 at 0.30–0.50, and several SLA probabilities are not in the prompt. Observational approval rates are also transported under interruption/expedite interventions more confidently than the prompt allows, then used to two-decimal “net positions.”

Net-position cells for A/B/E/G omit the minus sign, so the summary table literally shows large *positive* nets for dominated options. Confidence for E (0.85) is an outcome-prediction confidence, not comparable to the decision confidences elsewhere.

### What is actually good (and why that is not enough)

A and B are correctly `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT` and last; the write-up is clear that a favorable SLA forecast cannot waive Rules 6, 7, and 9. G is correctly not yet enabled. F’s conditionality is named. The S4-at-35 vs S4-at-65 sensitivity, the refusal to spend S2 on P-884 by default, parallel re-screening while waiting, and explicit rerank triggers (S4 slip, screen result, minute-50 alarm) are the right operational ideas. Those are why this is not a 3–4. They do not offset a self-contradictory rank table, broken arithmetic, a rewritten review clock, a non-allowed conformance label, and a 46%/54% flip on the central risk object.

Under the required strictness, this is a polished but unreliable analysis: correct policy instinct, unsafe numbers, and a ranking that does not survive comparison with its own Section 3.
