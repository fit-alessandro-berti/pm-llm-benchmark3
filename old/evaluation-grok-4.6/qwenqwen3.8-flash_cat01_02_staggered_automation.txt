**7.3**

The final write-up is format-complete, arithmetically careful on the main pre/post and DiD tables, and correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It also hits the core scientific points: endogenous targeting of weak branches, availability versus `automated_fast_path_pct`, mixed median versus p90, fraud-desk interference, substitution versus capacity, and a rollout that tries to buy both operations and learning. That is a strong answer, not a near-flawless one.

**Format and estimands.** Sections 1–10 match the required headings, table schemas, word bands, and claim-ladder prefixes. The estimand table is the best part: ITT availability, intensity/treatment version, median, tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, dynamics, heterogeneity, and interference are all named, and identifiability is mostly denied rather than faked. Section 1 stays descriptive.

**Identification is thinner than it looks.** The prompt invited a staggered event-study, and the answer takes that invitation too literally. With five branches and three adopters, a relative-time design is barely a design. The primary strategy table never grapples with negative weights, cohort-specific ATTs, or the fact that Gamma contributes only two post months. The 150-word justification restates staggered timing; it does not show why this estimator is preferable *in these data* to transparent DiD-plus-bounds. Alternatives (synthetic control with two donors; intensity ITS) are named, then left equally underpowered. That is a real identification-logic gap, not a cosmetic one.

**Quantitative discipline has one material hole.** Alpha/Beta windows and the never-treated contrasts check out. Intensity is handled as a separate estimand, and median is not collapsed into p90. The failure is Gamma. Using Apr–Jun as the pre window bakes in the May complexity spike; the displayed −6.5h / −9.3h drop is largely mean reversion. A Jan–Mar versus Jul–Aug contrast is about −0.3h on the median. The text mentions reversion but never shows that alternative number, so the “most successful adopter” remains visually overstated. March’s fraud desk also contaminates the Alpha/Beta three-month pre period (two months without the desk, one with); that is noted as a shock, not as pre-period contamination. The section 10 “conditionally causal” 3–6h / 6–7h figures are sharper than the answer’s own −6 to +2 and −7 to +3 bounds, and no naive percent change is computed in order to refute leadership’s X% request directly.

**Uncertainty and process logic are uneven.** Escalation is called “likely increased” at confidence 60 on a ~1pp DiD. p90 is given higher confidence than median (45 vs 35) even though unrecorded fraud-desk waits make the tail the worse-measured outcome. CSAT is correctly treated as unidentified. The mechanism table is strong on substitution, congestion, rework displacement, and survey selection. Staffing and case-mix rows are present but not used to produce a second, fully adjusted numeric contrast in the evidence section.

**Policy.** `expand with an embedded evaluation`, Delta first, case-level intensity randomization, frozen Epsilon, queue-state collection, and explicit guardrails are the right class of recommendation. The “scale if median falls ≥2 hours” rule is arbitrary relative to the earlier bounds and could reject the same effect the analysis just called suggestive. That is a decision-rule inconsistency, not a wrong category choice.

**Code.** The script satisfies the spec: embedded CSV, validation, printed trends with no subplots, pre/post windows, relative-time dummies, labeled descriptive TWFE and intensity models, cluster SEs, small-cluster and aggregated-quantile warnings, assumptions as comments, no fake event logs. It was not run, and the intensity TWFE is overparameterized; both are acknowledged in spirit by the warnings.

Net: a high-competence exam answer that would survive a careful reader, with enough robustness, identification, and uncertainty mismatches that a strict grader should not go into the 8–10 band.
