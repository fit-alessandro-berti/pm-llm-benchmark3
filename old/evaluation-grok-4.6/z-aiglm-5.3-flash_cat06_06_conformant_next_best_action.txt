**6.8**

The write-up has the right skeleton: six required sections, lettered actions A–G all ranked, nonconformant shortcuts at the bottom, a parallel conformant second-review plan on top, P-901 treated as a real constraint, and F/G correctly labeled as conditional rather than free. That is necessary for a high score; it is not sufficient. Under a strict reading, the answer is a strong outline with several invented numbers, one timing error, and a ranking that is more confident than its own spillover analysis.

**What is actually right.** Actions A and B are `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT` and ranked last; G is `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`; C and F use `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS` in line with the prompt. Re-screening is not treated as a substitute for second-officer review while score ≥ 70. Four-eyes, SAR non-disclosure, and “filing a report does not authorize execution” are all respected. The observational-vs-interventional caveat is used, not just recited. Section 6 gives a primary plan, a fallback, a confidence level, and concrete rerank triggers (S4 ETA, refreshed score plus match resolution, director authorization with incident ID, t+45 with no reviewer). Those are the pieces a good answer must have.

**Fabricated probabilities.** This is the main failure. The prompt gives SLA-breach 0.48–0.64 under normal processing, approval 0.55–0.75 after conformant review, fuzzy-match confidence 0.46, and S2 review 35–75 minutes. The answer then publishes, as if estimated: re-screen clears 0.3–0.5 vs escalates 0.5–0.7; conditional approval 0.65–0.85 / 0.2–0.35; P(meet SLA) 0.5–0.75 / 0.45–0.7 / 0.25–0.5 / 0.45–0.85; G success ~0.3. Those ranges are not in the case and do not even partition cleanly (0.3–0.5 and 0.5–0.7 overlap). In a task that is explicitly about uncertainty and non-transportable predictions, filling the table and the trees with made-up posteriors is a methodological error, not a presentation choice.

**Timing arithmetic.** Action C is specified as 35–75 minutes review, then 5–15 minutes to record and, if approved, execute — total 40–90 to an execution decision. The table writes “decision t+40–90; execution t+45–105,” which double-counts the record/execute window. Row 1’s “t+40–55 (best draw)” is also optimistic relative to the given 35–75 band. S4 review is assumed 25–45 minutes with no basis; the only duration in the prompt is S2’s 35–75. That assumption is what makes the zero-spillover plan look feasible at the 35-minute S4 arrival, so it is load-bearing and unjustified.

**P-901 is asserted, then under-weighted.** The case never gives P-901’s avoided-loss amount. The table still writes “€3–10k expected if similarly sized” and uses that figure as if it were an expected-harm range for Rank 1. Section 5 then admits the magnitude is unknown and states a rank flip: S4 at 35 minutes should put the no-interrupt plan first. Rank 1 nonetheless interrupts immediately. A short S4-ETA gate while screening starts would have been consistent with that sensitivity analysis; the chosen primary plan is not. That is an internal contradiction, not a mere style difference.

**Smaller defects.** Section 4’s rule map is conceptually correct but sloppy on the page (dropped ≥, leftover “-as-substitute / -as-complement,” missing success/fail marks). The Confidence column is used inconsistently: estimate quality for Rows 1–6, “very high that it is impermissible” for A/B. “SLA prize” and similar phrasing oversell a 20–35k operational band that is already swamped by the regulatory tail. None of these alone would sink the answer; together they show the write-up was not tightened to the same standard as its framing.

**Bottom line.** The ordinal story a grader wants is present: stay inside the feasible set, get an independent second officer, gather screening information in parallel, do not burn the window on an override lottery, and do not execute or self-certify. The numbers, clocks, and Rank-1/§5 consistency are not at that standard. A near-flawless answer would have kept every probability either quoted from the case or labeled as an uncalibrated scenario, gotten C’s 40–90 window right, and made the S2/S4 choice explicitly contingent on ETA instead of locking interrupt-first and then describing the opposite ranking in the spillover section.
