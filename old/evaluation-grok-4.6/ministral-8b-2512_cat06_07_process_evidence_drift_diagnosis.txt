The answer gets the headline periodization right—April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a mix/routing change, August–September is overlapping real change, October is label-censoring—and that is the main thing that separates it from a weak one-label collapse. Under a strict reading of the required structure, the technical definitions, and the evidence-to-action chain, it is still well short of a strong audit.

## Structural misses

The prompt required **one row for each of six named types**, including **covariate drift**. That row is absent. In its place the table invents **“Performance/process drift”**, which is not one of the required types and then steals the August–September evidence that should have been split across **process drift**, **concept drift**, and **covariate drift**.

That is not cosmetic. It produces a wrong map:

- **Process drift** is confined to June (variant mix). The July–October explosion on `Order Part → Part Received` (65h → 130h → 148h → 155h) and the V3 share jump (22% → 35% → 42%) are the clearest process-performance drift in the file, and they are not in that row.
- **Covariate drift** is never named: missing queue features, unknown-activity prefixes, the June shift in variant-conditional feature mix, Partner Z’s rural distance and device age, and (if wait features exist) the part-lead-time distribution. Several of those are mentioned in passing, but they are not diagnosed as \(P(X)\) change.
- **Concept drift** is marked **Low / partial** and hung mainly on the AUROC drop. The stronger concept-drift evidence is the **matured V3 breach rate**: 0.35 (Jun) → 0.46 (Jul) → 0.64 (Aug) → 0.61 (Sep) while V1/V2 stay flat. That is a change in \(P(Y \mid \text{variant/path})\) for the same named path, and it should be medium-to-high confidence, not a residual afterthought.

Label delay is restricted to **October**. September is 82% mature, median delay is already 42d, Partner Z labels mature more slowly, and October’s backlog “disproportionately affects longer resolution paths.” August (93%) and September are partially censored; treating “no other month shows this level of immaturity” as a clean contradiction understates a graded problem.

## Evidence handling

**July is misread.** The timeline dates supplier misses to 10 July; the DFG edge is already 65h (vs 42h in June) and matured V3 breach is already 0.46. Calling July “mild performance drift (e.g., technician workload)” is an **ungrounded hypothesis** presented as diagnosis. The prompt asked for that distinction.

The most useful triangulation in the table—**mean predicted risk vs observed breach**—is only half used:

The answer flags April over-prediction and October unreliability, then underuses June’s over-prediction and August–September’s under-prediction. September’s Partner Z rate of 0.45 vs 0.27 is cited, but the slower Partner Z maturity is not followed through: **0.45 is a lower bound**, not a finished contrast.

Falsifiers are uneven. April’s “reprocess and metrics should recover” is good. October’s “excluding October should not affect model stability” does not falsify censoring; the right test is **fixed-vintage completion**: when the October start-cohort matures, observed breach should rise, especially on V3. That test is named in the prompt and is missing from §3.

## Concrete technical errors

The label-maturity hypothesis is **backwards**. Immature October labels should produce a curve that has **not yet plateaued**, not one that “plateaus early.” Mature months plateau near 33–35 days; October’s 52d median delay and 55% maturity mean the curve is still climbing, and the climb will be steeper for long V3 paths.

May’s leftover defect is a **cached** “events before diagnosis” feature that was not recomputed. The answer calls this “uncached features” and says backfilling **April** should fix **May**. That reverses both the cache story and the month.

“No evidence of concept drift (AUROC stable)” in June is too coarse. AUROC can stay high under a mix shift while ECE moves (here 0.060, predicted 0.20 vs 0.15). Discrimination and calibration are being treated as interchangeable.

Several §3 tests give only one expected result. The prompt required **two competing hypotheses per test**. Test E then collapses the distinction it claims to test: a supplier-driven change in \(P(Y \mid \text{same path/original } X)\) **is** concept drift if wait time is not in \(X\), and covariate/process drift if it is. “No coefficient change if the drift is external” is not a coherent alternative.

Population reweighting is framed as a test of whether Partner Z is “unfairly penalized.” The right contrast is transport: if reweighting to the pre-Z covariate distribution restores calibration/AUROC, the hit is **population/covariate shift**; residual error is **concept drift or missing features**. Fairness is a later question, and the prompt already warned against jumping to it.

## Remediation is not proportionate

June does **not** justify a V2-specific model. V2 breach rates and diagnosis→Remote Fix times are stable; only the share changed. A mix-aware recalibration or a variant feature is enough.

**Safe vs unsafe training months are inverted for the new regime.** January–March are safe as a pre-break reference. June is usable for mix, not as a clean “good” window if you need the post-supplier concept. July already contains the supplier break. August–September are **unsafe until labels and features are fixed**, but they are the months you **must eventually train on** if the new V3/Partner Z world is the deployment world. Listing them as permanently unsafe, then planning “full retraining (October)” while excluding October, is internally inconsistent.

Excluding May wholesale is defensible only until the cached feature is recomputed; after that May is a mature, near-normal month. The write-up never separates “do not use as-is” from “do not use even after repair.”

## Monitoring code does not meet the spec

The code block is a sketch that would not run and omits required pieces:

- `pm4py.filter_time_range` and `pm4py.get_variants` are never called.
- `format_dataframe(..., parameters={"parameters": {"format": "event_id"}})` is not the PM4Py API (`case_id`, `activity_key`, `timestamp_key`).
- `event_time` is never parsed, then `.dt.to_period` is used.
- The month loop is left as `"2026-02", ...`.
- Event-calendar vs case-start cohorts are not interpreted, and the case-start construction is wrong: filter events to the month, then `groupby.first()`, yields **one event from cases that touched the month**, not full traces of cases **started** that month.
- Discover calls pass unformatted frames and invented parameter nests.
- Nothing returns an auditable monthly summary (variant shares, edge waiting times, unknown-activity rate, missingness).

It correctly avoids inventing a PM4Py drift detector. That is the only spec item it cleanly satisfies.

## What still works

April is not called concept drift. June is not collapsed into model failure. August–September are allowed to be several things at once. October’s 0.16 is not treated as a real improvement. §6 hits the four failure modes the prompt cares about (immature retraining, calibrating a pipeline bug, ignoring supplier-driven change, fairness talk without strata). Those are the reasons this is not a 3–4.

It is not close to 8+. A high score needed the six required rows, a correct July/label-delay scope, a right-side-up maturity test, two-hypothesis diagnostics, training advice that contains first and then **learns the new concept**, and working cohort/DFG code. This answer has the right story in outline and too many broken joints underneath.
