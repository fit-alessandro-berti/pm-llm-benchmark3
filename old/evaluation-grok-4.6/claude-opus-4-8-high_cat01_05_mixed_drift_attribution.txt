The answer has the right causal skeleton and hits the evaluation emphasis: March is treated as semantic/provenance drift, later movement is not collapsed into logging, rework and queueing are tied to supplier and staffing, priority redistribution is flagged as masked, seasonality is separated from novel drift, and monitoring is built to keep those distinctions. **Quantitative error (most serious).** The stated L1 distances are wrong. Against the January vector `[100, 98, 95, 12, 11, 4, 83]`, the sums of absolute deviations are **3, 40, 59, 28, 64, 36**, not **3, 32, 49, 26, 62, 33**. May is off by 8, July by 10. Ranking luckily survives, so the qualitative reading (March ~0 after mapping; summer = rework edges; October = senior-assessment) still holds, but Section 3 is supposed to be the transparent numeric core. The Part A code would print the correct distances, so the write-up contradicts its own reproducibility artifact. That is exactly the kind of false precision the prompt forbade.

**A real logic error in attribution.** The revision clause “if a clean prior renewal season showed the same October complexity spike is anomalous rather than seasonal” is self-contradictory. The same spike year-over-year would **confirm** seasonality. The intended falsifier is the absence of that spike. This is not a wording nit; it reverses the test.

**Process-mining gaps that matter here.** Monthly DFG decision rates (83→72) and the October senior-assessment burst are interpreted as structural/mix change with almost no mention of right-censoring: longer cycles leave more cases short of `Decision` inside a calendar-month fragment. That is a standard DFG-on-aggregates bias and should have been an explicit assumption beside “omitted edges.” The known historical inconsistency of `Senior Assessment` logging is confined to a limitations cell, then October is still attributed as a clean case-mix signature at ~70% confidence. M7’s alert (“standard waiting rises while high falls”) detects only priority redistribution and can miss the actual staffing failure mode (both classes waiting longer). In Section 5 the pre/post-staffing “behavioral” pattern is “routing unchanged, throughput falls,” which is a **performance** signature, not behavioral drift. L2×supplier as primary population is asserted to keep ≥50 cases/cell; January–February Supplier Z is only ~85–90 cases, so high×Z is likely under 50.

**Smaller defects.** Section 9 overruns the 250–400 word cap (~410). Hypothesis 8 claims a superlinear p90 effect that was not computed. `monthly_dfg` does not group by month. `filter_time_range(..., mode='events')` can split cases before DFG/variant/temporal-profile calls. `fast_variant_pct` is unused. Overlapping timeline rows (May–Jun, Jun–Aug, Jul onward) blur onset types. The secondary logging change point at 6.2–6.5% missingness is noise-level.

**What is actually good, and why this is not a 6.** Change-point multiplicity is argued correctly and with restraint. Cycle-time shares are ranges, not fake hours, and the service-vs-queue split uses the one hard clue in the packet (Assess service +0.6h vs reconstructed wait). The hypothesis table, segmentation collider note, dual fitness monitors, YoY seasonal references, and the seven IF/THEN/BECAUSE rules are usable.

A nearly flawless answer would have had correct L1 arithmetic consistent with its code, a coherent falsification test, censoring and senior-assessment logging as first-class alternatives, and alert logic that matches the mechanism it claims to watch. This is a strong diagnostic memo with a broken quantitative center and several rigor slips. Under a strict reading that is a mid-7, not an 8+.
