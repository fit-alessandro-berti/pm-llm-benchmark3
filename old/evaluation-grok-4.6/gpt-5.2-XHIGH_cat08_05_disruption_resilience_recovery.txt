**7.3**

This is a complete, well-structured response that hits every required section, uses the event-log metrics, and gets the core thesis right: hospital protection was real, several workarounds were control-preserving, and T4 “recovery” still carried a compliance tail. It would be a strong answer under ordinary grading. Under a strict temporal/abductive/resilience rubric it is not nearly flawless.

**What it does well.** The T0–T4 arc is correctly sequenced. Hospital-critical fill (91%) and cycle time (8h→10h) are not treated as a general success story; pharmacy fill (61%), cancellation (7.8%), fitness (0.96→0.71; 91%→44% fitting traces), and `Invoice close → Audit remediation` (2,300) are used as the counterweight. A–L are classified with keep/modify/retire logic that does not punish every baseline deviation. C and G are retired rather than laundered as agility. The recovery model includes the six required ingredients, and the management paragraph states what to accept, what to forbid, and what evidence would change the call.

**Temporal reasoning is the largest miss.** T2 is only Day 0 afternoon–Day 2 (~2–2.5 days). Reported T2 p90s of 118h (pharmacy) and 180h (bulk), and even the bulk median of 96h, cannot be realized inside that window. T3 (four days) has a bulk p90 of 160h, which has the same problem. Those figures almost certainly mix in-phase starts with later completions, or they are right-censored. The answer reprints them as if they were in-window process times. It also never notes that T2 DFG closure is thin: `Spreadsheet invoice hold → END` 2,100 plus `Cancel → END` 430 is only ~2,530 of 5,600 cases (~45%). T2 cycle-time and exception rates are therefore conditioned on an incomplete, selected subset. That is exactly the temporal caveat a process-mining reading should raise, and it is absent.

Related T4 under-reading: compliance holds rise to 1,300 versus 700 in a larger baseline; `Reconcile manual reservation → Cancel order` is 300 of 1,400; audit remediation is 2,300 in and 2,100 out. Daily volume in T4 (~2,300/day) exceeds baseline (~2,000/day), which helps explain lingering p90s (pharmacy 74h vs 49h, bulk 120h vs 72h). The answer cites slower medians but does not use these DFG quantities.

**Abductive gaps in the DFG.** The T2 path is written as one concatenated happy path with `(Temporary release control?)`. The question mark is sloppy, and optionality is the point: `Manual pack → Dispatch without scan` 1,800 versus `Manual pack → Temporary release control` 1,650, then `Temporary release control → Dispatch without scan` 1,450. Release was minority and usually still dispatched without scan. Section 3 still lists F as “true resilience.” That overstates F. F preserved intent relative to C/G, but in the log it was a partial gate sitting on top of the largest traceability break, which is why dual-control missing still jumped 0.2%→2.9% and cold-chain missing 0.4%→4.8%. E is handled more carefully (“resilient only with evidence capture”); F is not held to the same standard.

T3 is thin. There is no T3 DFG in the source, so some caution is fair, but 11,400 cases, fitness 0.82 / 59% fitting, and exception rates stuck halfway between T2 and T4 deserved a stronger hybrid-process hypothesis (intake back, scan/sync still broken, reconstruction already starting). Baseline fill rate is never given, so “91% protected” is an inference; it should have been labeled as such.

**Resilience vs concealed noncompliance is conceptually right, empirically slightly too clean.** The definitions are good and they do not equate audit remediation with real-time control. The three-bucket split is the right shape. The weakness is boundary placement: F and unqualified E-adjacent language lean optimistic given the T2 frequencies; H is correctly treated as weak evidence for controlled product, but the 2,600 phone-PoD volume is not tied back to the 3,250-ish dispatch-without-scan mass. A/D/I as ambiguous is the correct call.

**Recovery redesign is usable but not tightly identified from the log.** Triggers, risk matrix, minimum control sets, evidence packets, fairness floors, and a visible operational-close vs compliance-close split are the right design. Several numbers (15-minute WMS trigger, 95% scan, 30-minute sync, 24–48h reconciliation SLAs, OTP/photo) are unspecified inventions. That is allowed in a redesign, but they are not derived from observed break points (stale snapshot over multi-day T2, G as the dominant gap, T4 audit tail). The model also under-specifies how T3-like partial recovery should behave when intake is up and scanning is not.

**Smaller defects that add up.** “Keep (with modifications)” versus “Modify” is the same recommendation with inconsistent labels. Section 1’s T2 variant sketch drops frequency mass (fast lane 1,000, skip 1,600, defer/backlog leak). Fairness is framed almost entirely as hospital vs pharmacy; wholesale-bulk p90 180h and deferral-driven cancellation are second-class. Recommendations 1–8 are on target; 9–10 are generic extras.

**Net.** Format compliance, data grounding, and the resilience/noncompliance distinction are well above a median answer. The score is held down by the unexamined T2/T3 time-window problem, incomplete-trace bias, over-crediting F, unused T4 DFG signals, and a recovery model that is sensible rather than identified. Those are not cosmetic nits on a temporal-reasoning task.
