**7.8**

This is a strong, constraint-compliant design with a few factual and consistency slips that keep it short of near-flawless.

The pipeline does the hard parts correctly. The decision tree separates inspection triggers from decisions, refuses to treat `(activity, timestamp)` or shared objects as proof, and maps every required terminal to a concrete action. Conservative vs aggressive paths are not a stack of library calls: e03/e04 and e11/e16 are evidence-backed merges; e06/e07 and e13–e15 stay ambiguous with two named scenarios; e08/e09 is a harmonization collision; e05 and e14 are never dropped in the production paths. Ordering is a stored DAG plus a linear extension, with R1–R3 tagged as semantic and R5–R6 as convention only. The microseconds-first example at 12:00 is exact: ingestion order encodes a second Issue Invoice after captures. `have_common_object=True` is explained properly: e13/e14 share O1 and V1, so the guard cannot protect unit-of-work objects. Provenance, reversibility, sensitivity patterns, and the eight required failure modes are all present and usable.

Hard constraints are met: original timestamps kept, ingestion not used as business order, total order not sold as physical truth, `ocel_raw` not mutated.

Issues that block a higher score:

1. **Wrong label count.** Conservative unique activities go 10 → 13, not 12. Relabeling e07, e09, and e15 adds three names while Pack Package, Dispatch Package, and Capture Payment remain.

2. **Section 2 table is sloppy on retain-both rows.** e13/e15 and e14/e15 list canonical eid `e13`/`e14` even in scenario A, where both events are kept. That contradicts the decision column.

3. **R1 in prose is not R1 in code.** Pick ≺ pack and invoice ≺ payments are described as domain constraints but implemented only as R3 lifecycle edges. The Order template then orders almost every tied pair because every event carries O1; that over-constraint is never called out.

4. **`ocel_drop_duplicates` C′ counts are overconfident.** Typical PM4Py behavior drops duplicate *relations* on `(activity, timestamp, object)` and does not delete events. Orphans e04/e07/e09/e15/e16 would likely remain, so “12 events” is wrong unless a version-specific cleanup runs. The hedge does not cancel the stated numbers.

5. **Spec vs implementation drift.** The audit schema (roles, original relations, reviewer, scenario id) is richer than what `merge_verified_pairs` / `refine_labels` actually write. `pm4py.filter_ocel_events` and in-place `ocel.events` mutation are version-fragile.

6. **Small analytic looseness.** Scenario B’s Order DFG is sketched as `Capture→Capture→Confirm Delivery`, which hides Issue/Authorize still present. Q1’s “keep first-ingested” sits awkwardly next to fact 9, even for identical rows.

Event/E2O arithmetic for A (15/39), B (13/35), and C (10/28) is correct; C’s collapse of e05 and e14 is correctly treated as damage. Remaining deductions are for avoidable factual errors and internal mismatches, not for the overall design.
