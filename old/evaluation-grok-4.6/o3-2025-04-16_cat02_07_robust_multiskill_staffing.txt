## What works

The nine required headings are present. Assumptions A-1–A-5 are mostly explicit (Poisson arrivals, two-point uniform mixture, P1-only single preemption, 10% remaining-time restart). Naming a dynamic ME/ES rule is better than leaving parallelism implicit. Two objective *intents* exist: one SLA/tail-weighted, one fairness/robustness. The three policies are described as dispatch rules rather than slogans. Sections 6, 7, and 9 are the strongest: starvation, skill erosion, estimate gaming, Validation as a late bottleneck, shadow-then-partial rollout, and a useful event-attribute list.

That is not enough. The quality bar asked for audited load, shift-aware capacity, and metrics that can be traced to a method. This answer does not deliver those.

## Critical analytical errors

**ME/ES work content is treated as a single skill.** In the 08:00–10:00 block, Mechanical load is written as `7 · 0.3 · 27 ≈ 57`. ME is 20% and requires Mechanical *and* Electrical; ES is 10% and requires Electrical *and* Software. Correct Mechanical incidence is `0.30 + 0.20 = 0.50`, Electrical `0.25 + 0.20 + 0.10 = 0.55`, Software `0.15 + 0.10 = 0.25`. This is not a rounding issue. It understates dual-branch offered load by tens of percent and invalidates every later bottleneck claim that depends on that worksheet.

**Service-time expectations are inconsistent and arithmetically wrong.** Mechanical is defined as `0.15·45 + 0.85·23`, which is **26.3**, not 25.2 (that figure is `0.10·45 + 0.90·23`). Intake is then computed from the ordinary midpoint only (`7·6.5`), dropping the 0.08 tail the author just said they would use. Validation is `7·E[12] = 84`; the mixture midpoint is about 15.7 minutes, so about 110 minutes, not 84. The “2.3 FTE” morning total (~273 minutes) is therefore both mis-added and conceptually incomplete. A corrected work-content sketch is closer to ~3.7 FTE before handover, setup, coordination, and restart penalties.

**“Weighted harmonic mean ≈ 27 min” is the wrong object.** Offered load is demand in base minutes (or after a *chosen* resource’s multiplier). Averaging Ada/Ben/Deepa/Farah service times into one number, then treating it as block demand, mixes supply into demand and hides that Farah is only present until 12:00 and Deepa only from 10:00. Time-varying shifts are discussed in prose and then not used in the arithmetic.

**U-scenario mechanics are asserted, not computed.** U2 requires doubling E and ES *then renormalizing* type shares; that is never shown. U3’s “−35% Electrical/Software capacity” is a guess that ignores who is actually on shift after 12:00 and Farah’s half day. U4’s “15–20% higher mean service” is roughly plausible for some activities and high for others; it is not derived. U5’s “P1 SLA unattainable without aggressive preemption” is a hypothesis presented as a finding.

## Internal contradictions and empty evidence

Section 4 is supposed to be the decision table. `mean_delay` and `p90_delay` are **blank for every row**, despite a scale being announced. SLA figures are given to the percentage point (`91%`, `98%`, `82%`) with no seed, no replication, no interval, and no link to code. That is false precision.

The recommendation then contradicts its own table: H is said to stay “never above 105% even in U5,” while the table marks H/U5 `max_util` as **VH**, defined by the author as `>105%` and requiring overtime. Handover “~12% vs 20% of total time” appears only in the recommendation, not in the table or the simulator.

Policy S is not a coherent specialization rule. Intake windows (`Ben`/`Chen` only 08–12) are unexplained. Software is “Emil only” with overflow to Chen, which is already pooling, while the same policy says “no cross-skill work except Farah morning.” Preemption is a process rule in the prompt; S waves it away without analyzing SLA impact.

Objectives O-tail and O-fair are not usable as written: every weight is printed as `w`, then `www>0 (baseline 40/6/1/3)`, and the fairness coefficients are missing symbols (`+ ·(util_range)`). They cannot be recomputed.

## Python: not a three-policy comparison

The prompt allows a scaffold *if omitted mechanisms are identified* and if comparable policy × scenario metrics are still produced. This code fails that standard.

- `policy` is accepted and then **ignored**. S, P, and H execute the same earliest-finish greedy assignment.
- ME/ES run **one** diagnostic (`ME → Mechanical`, `ES → Electrical`). The second branch, the +8 coordination delay, and the sequential critical-path alternative do not exist.
- Handover (+7), team-change (+5), setup (+5), preemption/restart, reservations, overflow triggers, overtime, and priority queues are unimplemented.
- `within_shift` and `on_break` are defined and **never called**. Deepa’s 10:00 start, Emil’s 12:00 start, Farah’s 12:00 end, and all lunches are ignored.
- U2’s `E_mult` is never applied. U5’s `combine: ['U1','U3']` is never expanded, so U5 is only a 09:30–10:00 Intake freeze—not U1+U3+outage.
- `random.seed(4)` does not seed `numpy`’s Poisson draws.
- Case IDs are `C1`, `C2`, …; P1 SLA is `wait['case:concept:name'].str.contains('P1')`, which is always false. That is not a labeled PM4Py waiting-time analysis.
- `get_service_time` mean is treated as if it were delay. No stage-specific queue metrics, no utilization, no SLA clock, no repeated-seed summary.
- `pm4py.format_dataframe` / `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `get_service_time` / `discover_handover_of_work_network` are called, which satisfies the letter of item 6 and almost nothing of items 2–4, 7–8.

Section 4’s numeric SLAs therefore cannot have come from this program. Presenting them as scenario results is the single most damaging credibility failure.

## Process-mining competence

Conceptually, the author knows the right vocabulary (handover network, service-time drift, bottleneck migration). Operationally, the event log has only three interval activities, no queue-entry, no pause/resume, no eligible set, no planned vs actual, no policy version, no SLA deadline. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is not interpreted at case vs activity grain. Token replay is mentioned in §7 and not demonstrated. This is API name-dropping on a log that cannot support the analyses promised.

## Uncertainty and robustness

“Robust” is defined across U1–U5 in words, which is the right move, and the author does not pick a policy only on base-case mean delay. But robustness is then “shown” with unauditable point SLAs and a simulator that does not apply the shocks. No bounds, no sensitivity to the uniform-vs-midpoint choice, no replication. The fallback rule (switch H→P if P1 queue > 3) is operationally clear and also untested; under the author’s own table, rolling back to S on a P1 breach—as the pilot in §9 proposes—would be the worst stress response.

## Scoring rationale

A high score required correct definitions, coherent calculations, defensible uncertainty, and a method that can actually distinguish policies. This submission has the outline of a good answer and a broken interior: dual-skill load omitted, arithmetic errors, blank delay columns, self-contradictory utilization, fabricated SLAs, and a single-path stub simulator. Those are not cosmetic defects. Credit for structure, policy language, and the later qualitative sections lands at **4.0**, not higher.
