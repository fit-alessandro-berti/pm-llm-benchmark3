The answer is unusually disciplined: every major claim is tied to supplied identifiers, the six flattenings are compared on the same semantics, copied events are treated as both impact evidence and execution duplication, and decision **C** is argued rather than asserted. Counts that are easy to get wrong (42 Order incidences from 31 events; 24/16 Item; 12/10 Package; 6/6 Delivery; 7/7 Invoice; 4/4 Payment; D2 = 4h55m; V2 instalment gap = 20h20m; six item-level transitions vs four packs and three dispatches) check out. Required coverage is complete. That is not enough for a top score under a hypercritical standard.

**Material interpretive error.** Distortion 3 says e14/e17/e27 on O3 “follow its earlier P3/D2 branch.” They do not. O3 is `e13` (Pack P3, 10:12) → `e14` (Pack P4, 10:20) → `e16` (Dispatch D2, 11:05) → `e17` (Dispatch D3, 11:15). P4 is packed *before* D2 is dispatched. The P3/D2 and P4/D3 “branches” are interleaved, not sequential. The same interleaving is underplayed for O1: `e12` (Pack P2) is immediately followed by `e15` (Dispatch D1), so the Order DFG does not merely add Pack→Pack / Dispatch→Dispatch self-loops; it also creates a **cross-branch** Pack→Dispatch edge. Calling this “serializing separate package branches” is too tidy.

**Unclarities.** DFG edges are written as `Add ItemAdd Item` and `Pack PackagePack Package` throughout the matrix and §3, which is sloppy in a flattening paper. “Future M3 lifecycle” for propagating `e22` is ambiguous (`e22` precedes `e25`; the real leakage is a *prior* foreign payment in M3, and symmetrically a *future* `e25` in M2). Payment “divergence” is really fragmentation of one invoice lifecycle across singleton cases. `E16`/`e16` notation is inconsistent.

**Small logical tensions.** §4 endorses type-specific equivalent-execution clustering; §6 omits `cluster_equivalent_ocel` on the stronger claim that rejecting a lead object forbids it. Decision C is coherent, but the residual “use Order if the owner says the contractual unit is an order” is position B in all but name; the write-up never quite admits how close the two positions are. “None” duplication for Invoice/Delivery is correct for *type-internal copies*, yet those rows also describe high aggregation—fine if read carefully, easy to misread as “no convergence problem.”

**Code/outline gap.** The script satisfies the required API list and correctly refuses to treat discovery as semantic resolution. It does not compute the 42/24/12 incidence table it relies on, extract the O1/O3 variants, or implement any §7 check. For an “outline” that is acceptable; it still leaves the strongest empirical claims unverified in the artifact that was supposed to operationalize them.

**What remains strong.** Direct-E2O vs labelled propagation; 1/k weighting; V2 `e22`–`e24`–`e25` vs invoice-level payment self-loop; D2 counted 3/3/2/1 times by Order/Item/Package/Delivery; P3/P4 activity-sequence invisibility; Payment zero-duration DFG; the explicit ban on treating longest traces as the right case notion. Those pieces are specific, internally consistent, and grounded.

The analysis is well above a competent pass. It is not nearly flawless: one timeline error about the central O3/P4/D2 structure, several presentation defects, and a mild over-clean model of “branches” keep it out of the 9–10 band.
