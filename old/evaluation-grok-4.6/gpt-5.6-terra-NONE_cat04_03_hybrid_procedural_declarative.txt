**5.2**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and strong on policy narrative. The executable artifacts and the verdict matrix do not cohere. For a task whose quality bar is internally consistent PM4Py models plus layered test evaluation, that is a core failure, not a presentation issue.

## What is actually good

Section 1 is the best part. Two R5/R6 resolutions are stated, they are not silently merged, Resolution A is chosen, and the compliance risk is named. Section 7 is mostly the right layer: four-eyes, outstanding-clarification state, R11, and an evidence-gated R6 check, with missing attributes treated as non-evaluable. Sections 9–10 correctly flag DECLARE vacuity, R10 vs R11, support/confidence as non-normative metadata, and the need to surface contradictory findings separately.

That is not enough to carry the answer. The models would reject the happy paths they then mark as passing.

## Critical: the artifacts contradict the matrix

**Process-tree loop polarity is wrong.** `LOOP(Q→RC, τ)` is `(Q→RC)+`, not `(Q→RC)*`. PM4Py’s binary loop executes the first child at least once. The formal expression and the comments say zero or more; the code requires at least one clarification. T1, T3, T4, T6, T7, and T8 have no clarification and therefore fail the tree as written. The matrix marks them procedural `pass`. The math and the code already disagree with each other.

**The high-risk fragment cannot generate T6 or T7.** Optional escalation is encoded as `XOR(τ, SE→LO)` *then* EDD. T6 is `SE, EDD, CA, AV, LO`. T7 is `SE, EDD, LO, CA, AV`. Both are deviations from that tree. Alignments would record model/log moves. The matrix still says procedural `pass`. The task required disclosure of block-structured approximations *and* honest evaluation of their effect. Here the approximation is hidden by incorrect verdicts.

**DECLARE R5 is the wrong constraint, then evaluated as if it were the right one.** `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` means every activation requires a prior legal opinion. That is not R5. R5 is conditional on `Sanctions Escalation`. Under the written dictionary, T1, T2, and T4 fail DECLARE. The matrix marks them `pass`. Even granting the “intentionally conservative” reading in §4, the test table is then false. You cannot claim both.

The limitation note is also backwards. Precedence `(LO, AV)` does not over-constrain “legal opinion for unrelated reasons.” It over-constrains every accepted case with no opinion, which is most of the test suite.

**The log skeleton is not a PM4Py log skeleton.** In PM4Py, `always_before(A,B)` is: if `B` occurs, `A` occurred earlier. Putting both `(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` and `(Compliance Approval, Activate Vendor)` in that set makes every accepted trace fail one of the two constraints. T1 has no compliance approval; T2 has no manager approval. The matrix marks both skeleton `pass`.

`always_after` is worse. PM4Py’s `(A,B)` means: if `A` occurs, `B` occurs later. The pairs `(Close Onboarding, Reject Vendor)` and `(Close Onboarding, Release First Payment)` therefore require every closed case to contain *both* rejection and first payment. T1 and T3 fail. The inline comment says `(earlier, later)` while the prose says `A` occurs after `B`. Those cannot both be true of `("Close Onboarding", "Reject Vendor")`.

The “only when both activities occur” semantics is a private weakening, not what `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton` implements. For an executable-style task that is a modeling error.

## High: other formal mistakes

R2 is under-encoded. `nonsuccession(Reject Vendor, Manager/Compliance Approval)` allows approval then reject (exactly T8’s approval-before-reject prefix). Mutual exclusion of approvals on a rejected case needed `noncoexistence`, plus an accepted-case existence check the DECLARE model never states. The prose admits pairwise rules are insufficient, then still treats DECLARE as a R2 carrier.

`pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` keeps traces that *satisfy* resource separation. The snippet names the result `violating_log`. Hedging with “depending on version” does not fix an inverted API.

R7 is narrowed without comment: escalation exists only on the high-risk branch. The requirement is only “after screening.”

Temporal T3 is `pass` while T5/T8 are `not evaluated` for the same reason (no pair instance). Pick one convention.

## Medium / small

The conservative R5 DECLARE rule, if taken seriously, should have made overall T1 a DECLARE failure or forced R5 into the data layer as a conditional check. Neither happened. Clarification is pinned to a single pre-classify block; that is an acceptable approximation only if T6/T7-style interleaving is not then waved through. R11 is specified reasonably but never exercised. Hours-vs-seconds log construction is ignored. No `exactly_one` / existence rules back R2 or closure.

## Bottom line

This reads like a strong design essay with un-simulated models. The required headings are present. The conflict is not hidden. The resource/data layer is the right idea. Then the process tree, DECLARE dictionary, and log skeleton each encode something other than the stated policy, and Section 8 grades the intended policy instead of those encodings. Under a rubric that punishes inaccuracy and demands nearly flawless executable modeling, that caps the score in the mid-5s.
