**7.1**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly internally consistent. The POWL model, dependency table, and eight witnesses are the strongest parts. It is not nearly flawless: the process-tree choice is weaker than the task’s “as faithful as possible” bar, one important modeling distinction is blurred, and a few claims are overstated or unchecked.

**What is solid.** Assumptions resolve the required points (risk XOR, optional supplier via silent skip, loop-as-pass/fail, no data predicates, overlap as missing order). The POWL uses the right objects: `XOR` for risk and supplier, a two-node `StrictPartialOrder` with no edge for `QR`/`RR`, `LOOP(ET→IT, AP)` for the trial, and explicit `.order.add_edge` for the real cross-branch constraints (`C→PV`, `A→DCA`, `PV/DCA→trial`, `trial/approvals/supplier→RM`). Unconnected pairs are explained rather than hand-waved. Witnesses hit every mandated case (low risk, high risk, supplier, two trial cycles, PT-approximation leak, cross-branch forbidden). T6’s earliest violation (`ET` before `PV`) is correctly identified. Self-critique is more honest than most answers.

**Process tree is the main miss.** The tree is a safe under-approximation, but not the most faithful simple tree. Forcing the approval route and supplier to join *before* the trial drops the behavior the narrative stresses hardest: approvals and supplier may overlap the trial and need only finish at release. A block tree such as

`sequence(R, parallel(C, A), parallel(sequence(parallel(PV, DCA), trial-loop), risk-XOR, supplier-XOR), RM, AC)`

keeps that overlap and release sync, at the cost of extra waits (e.g. `PV` after both `C` and `A`). Both trees are conservative and both are safe. The write-up treats “cannot express the non-block sync, therefore finish approvals before trial” as if that were the only safe option. That justification is too strong. “Release safety” does not decide between the two approximations.

**Duplicate labels are mis-framed.** The text treats duplicate visible labels as if they would put extra events in traces. That is true of AND-duplication, not of the same label on exclusive XOR branches. Early-vs-late XOR copies of the approval/supplier fragments would still yield one occurrence per trace and would recover more intended completion orders. Rejecting all duplication therefore throws away a standard, trace-clean way to get closer to the intended language.

**Smaller defects.** Section 6 calls POWL “not overconstrained” on approval/trial overlap, which is true only if concurrent compound nodes interleave at event granularity—the same assumption later listed as a residual risk. Calling free risk/supplier XOR “underconstrained wrt case data” sits awkwardly next to the task’s instruction not to encode those data conditions. The validation plan is concrete but unrun; `play_out(..., max_traces=...)` is likely the wrong PM4Py parameter (`no_traces` is the usual name). There is no allowed witness where `CS` overlaps the trial, which would have shown both intended flexibility and the PT loss more clearly than another pre-trial supplier trace.

**Net.** Part B and the witness/comparison apparatus are good enough that this is clearly above a mediocre solution. Part A’s approximation policy and the duplicate-label argument keep it well short of a high score under a strict reading.
