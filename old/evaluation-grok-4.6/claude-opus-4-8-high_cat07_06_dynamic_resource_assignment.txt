**5.5**

The answer is well structured and hits every required heading, but it is not close to flawless. The policy write-up is stronger than the actual assignment, and several core constraints are misapplied or contradicted by the recommended queue.

**Section 1 is the strongest part.** Hard vs. soft constraints are separated cleanly, the six objectives are framed as a trade-off rather than a single optimum, and H-constraints vs. S-constraints is the right audit distinction. That said, rule 4 (“avoid high overtime unless SLA is urgent **and** no safer alternative exists”) is treated as a preference that can be relaxed because SLA slack is *ample*. That inverts the exception. The exception is urgency, not spare time.

**Section 2 looks complete and is not.** Required ingredients are named, then left half-defined:

- The total uses `wU*U*SK_gate`, but `wU` is absent from the weight table and `SK_gate` is never defined. Urgent weights already sum to 1.0 without `wU`, so the formula does not compute.
- `specialists_needed_elsewhere` / `scarcity_pressure` are labels, not procedures.
- `est_service_time_for_activity` is not in the snapshot; it is only implied later via PM4Py.
- Eligibility pseudocode omits H6 (Quality batching). H5 is checked per resource, which is correct, but batching is not part of the filter despite being listed as a hard constraint.
- Tie-break and continuity override (0.05 band) are reasonable. Supervision and “escalate rather than violate H” are the right exception paths.

An explainable policy cannot have an undefined objective.

**Section 3 is where the answer should have been won, and it is not.** Several assignments are defensible; several violate the policy’s own rules.

Correct or reasonable:

- C002 Farid, C005 Eva, C007 Celine, C008 Gita, C016 Eva: continuity + eligible skill, consistent with rule 5.
- C009 Amal supervised by Celine: genuine training use of a normal, high-slack diagnosis case.
- C015 kept with Hugo: Eva and Jonas are Security **0**. H3 allows supervised 1–2, not 0. Flagging a curriculum gap is one of the few precise readings in the table.

Material errors:

1. **Overtime (rule 4) is broken, then justified backwards.** David (OT high, 14 open, 3.0h) gets C003 and C014, both *normal* with 18h/25h slack. Safer alternatives exist (Celine is Diagnosis 3 / Rework 3, low OT, 6 open, 6.0h; Jonas is Rework 2, low OT). Bruno (OT high) gets non-urgent C010. Iris (OT high) gets non-urgent C006. Logging “monitor overtime” does not satisfy “no safer alternative.”
2. **Training quota (rule 3) is not met.** Normal/low cases are C003, C004, C006, C009, C010, C012, C013, C014, C015 (9). Only C009 is a real training assignment. C010 is Bruno Approval 2; his training goal is **quality**, not approval. C013 is Jonas on Eligibility; his goal is **security**. 1/9 ≈ 11%. C012 is explicitly “suitable for trainee” and is given to Quality specialist Jonas. Bruno (Quality 1, training = quality) is the obvious supervised training fill and is unused.
3. **C006/C012 batching is internally inconsistent.** C006 is “Iris + batch with C012”; C012 is “Jonas + batch with C006 logic.” Two resources cannot be one batch. H6 is invoked in prose and not implemented.
4. **H3 is overstated on C001.** Celine is Security 1, so a Gita/Hugo-supervised pairing is *legal*. Specialist routing may still be right (urgent, 3h, complexity 0.88, enterprise), but “H3 forces a specialist” is false. The write-up also says Gita is “committed to C008” while processing C001 *before* C008. That is an order-of-operations error; the same Hugo outcome can be justified by load/hours/OT without it.
5. **Farid’s dual urgent load is under-analyzed.** C002 (2h) + C011 (4h) on 6.5h remaining is plausible only if service times are short. No check against complexity or historical service time. VIP + high-value approval on one person is an SLA concentration risk the policy claims to avoid.
6. **C003 continuity is weakly motivated.** Reassignment count 2 and a “repeated diagnosis loop” are reasons to consider a fresh specialist (Celine), not to add work to the most loaded, high-OT owner. Rule 5 requires *high* handover cost; C003 is medium.

Caps (≤4) and Security/Approval floors on the *chosen* assignees are mostly respected. That is not enough.

**Section 4 reasons about the right dimensions but overclaims.** The qualitative contrast with first-available (mismatch, reassignment, 1.8× rework) and specialist-only (Security/Approval queues) is directionally sound. The table then calls the proposed policy “lowest” SLA risk and “best” resilience without instantiating either baseline on C001–C016. First-available on C001 would likely pick Celine (fewest open among skill ≥1), which is a useful concrete failure; it is never shown. “No single optimum” at the end sits poorly next to superlatives in the table.

**Section 5 is good socio-technical writing** — continuity, skill floors, overtime, learning as opportunity, reassignment as customer cost. It is slightly sanitized: it does not admit that C003/C014 piled onto a high-OT specialist, or that a labelled trainee case (C012) was not used for training. “C001 moved because a rule required a specialist” repeats the H3 overstatement to workers.

**Section 6 is largely what was asked.** The ten indicator families match the spec; H-violations vs. S-overrides is the right defect split; 1.8× reassignment evidence is wired to an alert. Weaknesses: skill mismatch `< 2` treats every trainee execution as a quality defect, including allowed supervised work; workload “Gini / CV” is named without a construction from the log; `discover_working_together_network` is a weak proxy for load.

**Section 7 is a competent sketch with real bugs.** It names the requested PM4Py surfaces (handover, working-together, activity-based similarity, organizational roles, SNA viz, service/waiting time, performance DFG, batches, variants, attribute and performance filters). Failures:

- `filter_case_performance(..., min_perf=0, max_perf=3600*24)` keeps cases **shorter** than 24h, not long cases.
- Training share uses all cases as denominator; the constraint is normal/low assignments.
- Replay is described, not specified (decision-point extraction, which event is the assignment, how supervision is represented in the log).
- `get_service_time` / handover-cost medians are mentioned as calibration inputs but never closed with the undefined scoring terms from §2.

**Net:** this is a coherent essay about a multi-objective policy, not a reliable policy. Explainability, audit indicators, and the C015 skill-0 catch are clearly above average. Undefined score terms, inverted overtime logic, a missed training quota, a batching contradiction, and baseline comparison without assignments are enough that a strict reading cannot go higher.
