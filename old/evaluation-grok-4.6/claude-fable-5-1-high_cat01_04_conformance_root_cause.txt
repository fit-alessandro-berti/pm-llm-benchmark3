**7.4**

This is a strong, format-complete investigation that does the hard things well: it separates measurement collapse from operational risk, uses staged repairs as abductive evidence, refuses to indict staff from moves-on-log, and treats an optional sign-off tree as an over-permissive control-flow approximation rather than a policy model. It is not nearly flawless. Several concrete errors, one structural attribution mistake, and a few silent overclaims keep it well below a high score under a strict reading.

## Format and completeness

All eight required headings appear, with the specified table schemas, four diagnostic phases, six governance rules, and a single PM4Py block covering the nine mandated steps. That is a pass on scaffolding.

Two format defects remain. Section 7 is substantially over the 250–400 word cap (the body is ~500 words). Section 3 is inside 180–280, so the overrun is not global verbosity; it is a missed constraint on the conclusion. Hypothesis codes (H1–H6) are defined and usable, but the ranked table still has to be read as “cause of the drop” vs “operational concern” packed into one cell, which is denser than the required columns and occasionally blurs the ranking criterion.

## Factual inaccuracies

**Queue Routed inventory is wrong.** E4 states the event is present in 530/650 June–July traces (82%). The DFG edge `Risk Screen → Queue Routed = 530` excludes the 30 `Classify, Queue Routed` traces. Presence is 270+145+70+30+25+20 = **560/650 (86%)**. The same slip feeds the claim that gateway coverage “grows to 100%.” The direction is right; the count is not.

**Events-per-case is not “almost exact.”** Variant expansion gives June–July 3975/650 ≈ **6.12**, not 6.3. That gap is the same class of extract mismatch the answer correctly flags in E16, then waves away in E12.

**Resource-missingness is not proportional to technical-event volume.** If `Queue Routed` lacked a resource, ~560 extra events on ~4k events would be ~13% missing-resource, not 5.9%. E12 treats the rise 1.2% → 5.9% as confirmation of H6. The arithmetic does not confirm it. The hypothesis can still be true; this particular exhibit does not carry it.

**Section 6’s “executed” precision figures are theoretically off and unverifiable from the given aggregates.** Fitting-trace percentages after each repair (0% → 13.8% → 82.3% → 93.1% June–July; 55.6% / 89.5% / 94.4% August–September) follow from the variant table by inspection. Alignment precision **rising** from 0.921 (normative) to 0.936 (optional sign-off) is surprising: adding a free skip/take branch enlarges the model language. Reporting three-decimal “executed” precision without reconciling that movement reads as fitted decoration, not a result.

## Logical and abductive flaws

**Repair-order attribution is used as causal share after the author showed it cannot be.** E16 correctly notes that **none** of the listed June–July variants can have cost 0 against the normative tree, so the reported 32% / 58% fitting rates cannot come from the same object as the variant table. The code even computes the coherent alternative: label map alone yields **13.8%** (only the 90 traces without `Queue Routed`). Sections 2 and 7 nevertheless rank H3 first and assign “~26 / 23 / 8 pp” to H3 / H6 / H4. That 26 pp is an artefact of applying the label map **first** in an unreproducible extract. Phase 2 explicitly warns that “order of repairs changes attribution,” then the conclusion commits the error the warning describes. On the complete variant table, H3 and H6 are jointly necessary on most traces; sequential fitting-% recovery is not a share.

**August policy is stacked onto the June collapse.** Section 3 calls the 32% fitness the joint product of four interventions, including the 2026-08-01 sign-off rule. That rule does not exist in June. The ranked table later restricts H2 to August–September, so the author knows the period split and still writes a four-cause stack for the initial drop. Relatedly, ranking “obsolete model” above the timestamp defect as a **cause of the measured collapse** is period-confused: the collapse is June–July, when the tree still matched the sanctioned control flow.

**Recovery is compared to 100% rather than the April–May baseline.** After defensible repairs the alignment summary is 89% vs a 93% pre-change baseline. That is essentially full recovery of the reported drop. Treating an 11% residual against perfection inflates leftover H1/H5.

**Routing-mix shift is under-weighted.** Enhanced-review share 30% → 26% → **43.5%** is a large behavioral (or mix) change. Sign-off is path-independent—60 sign-offs sit on the standard path—so the policy does not require steering cases into enhanced review. H1 at 55 with “~3–5 pp via overrides” buries this. E13 is noted, then not allowed to move the ranking.

**“Alignments are additive in cost” is used as if fitting-trace percentage were additive.** Two defects on one trace still yield one non-fitting trace. That is exactly why mapping without removing `Queue Routed` barely moves fitness on the variant table. The prose slides between cost additivity and percentage-point recovery as if they were the same quantity.

## What still works

The evidence ledger is wide enough (variants, DFGs, staged alignments, chronology, outcomes, audit, omitted attribute, timestamp semantics, lineage). Reliability ratings are mostly calibrated (E11 at 40, E14 at 30, E9 at 55). Discriminating tests are specific: screen-title vs routing-outcome audit, gateway capture-rate for `Compliance Check`, source-sequence flip count, high-value stratification of sign-off, source-system audit of the 55 check-less enhanced cases. The adjacency list includes the right hidden variables (`gateway_capture`, `extraction_lineage`, `model_maintenance_lag`). Counterfactuals include a genuine falsifier for an over-permissive optional-check model. The PM4Py tree → net path, staged `Risk Screen`→`Classify` map, event-level drop of `Queue Routed`, counterfactual reorder clearly labeled as a ceiling, and the data-aware caveat on EUR 50,000 are the right technical objects. Governance rules map 1:1 onto the six required controls and are operational, not slogans.

Unchanged outcomes are handled correctly: they neither prove data error nor clear a few-dozen skipped checks. `Manual Override` is not laundered into a technical event. Those are the evaluation-critical judgments, and they are right.

## Net

The piece reads like an expert note that was not re-checked against its own counts. The ranking would survive the corrections (H3 and H6 remain the dominant recording-chain causes; H5 remains a serious open residual, not the explanation of the dashboard collapse). Under a rubric that penalizes minor inaccuracy and logical slippage harshly, the arithmetic error, the inconsistent attribution, the June/August stack, the word-count breach, and the ornamental precision numbers are enough to knock it out of the high band.
