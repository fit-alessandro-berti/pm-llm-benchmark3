The answer is strong on the intended rubric—confounded before/after evidence, statutory gates, bottleneck shift, co-interventions, and a fidelity-aware pilot—but it is not nearly flawless. Material inconsistencies between the structural analysis and the quantitative/intervention sections, plus process-model errors, cap the score.

**What is done well.** Site A’s −9h median is correctly treated as unidentified. Co-interventions (API, +2 FTE, mix shift, huddles, timestamp artifact) are carried through the transfer table with appropriately low confidence. Blanket copy is rejected; Document Check is not started before valid originals; IT lock is treated as blocking `true_overlap`, not as a reporting detail. The selection-node DAG, modifier table, S5 (mean up / tail down), V0–V3 arm split, and mechanism-vs-implementation logic are the right shape for transport, not cargo-cult process-tree copying. Recommendation `conditional go` with E1-first is the correct decision class.

**Material analytical error: E2 is largely baseline.** Site B already allows Identity Check and Originals Receipt after Register (rules 2–3). DFG counts confirm it: Register→Identity 2,050, Register→Originals 1,860. Identity service (3.4h) is typically swallowed by originals delay (18h), which also explains 6% verification overlap. Creating Identity ∥ Originals is therefore not a transferred treatment for 62% of volume; the incremental lever is starting Document as soon as originals are valid *without* waiting for Identity (Identity→Document 1,160), which is small at the median. Section 1 states overlap is “only realizable” for digital cases, then S1 credits “~60% partial overlap” and −5 to −8h overall median. That contradicts the structure the answer itself established and inflates transportable effect.

**Eligibility arithmetic is inconsistent.** E1 is defined as no-originals *and* not legal, then repeatedly sized at ≈38%. Legal is 40%. Under any positive overlap the clean first-wave cell is well below 38% (roughly ~23% if independent). First-wave volume, S2 dilution, and §9’s “E1, ≈38%” are therefore misstated.

**DFG evidence is underused.** The 3,400-case relation counts are the best Site B identification of *current* partial order. They are almost unused in scenarios and in distinguishing “already concurrent” from “newly concurrent.” That is a process-mining miss, not a cosmetic one.

**Model fidelity.** Rule 7 requires that a supplement may repeat document checking *and assessment*. Both the §6 POWL and the §8 loop are `LOOP(Legal Review, Request Supplement → Document Check)`, then Decision. Assessment is not in the redo. The Petri net therefore encodes the wrong rework path and cannot speak to tail/rework claims that depend on that loop. The candidate POWL also forces Originals Receipt → Document Check for every case, so it does not represent the E1 digital path the policy recommends (an XOR of gated vs ungated Document would). Duplicate `Document Check` transitions are acceptable; omitting the join after supplement is not.

**Quantitative mapping is loose.** Treating a confounded −6h wait change as the planning target, then assigning −5 to −8h at B in S1 despite ~38% full eligibility and already-parallel Identity, is not a disciplined bound. S2/S4/S5 are more coherent; S1 should have been the place the structure constrained the numbers. Identity capacity (4 vs ~18 verification FTE) is a first-order site difference and is thin in the selection graph.

**Smaller issues.** “Digital claims: no harmful signature if capacity holds” underplays the same assessment/legal flood the rest of the memo describes. Inhibitory vs enabling edges are collapsed in the adjacency list. Pilot power still aims at A’s confounded 6h. `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net` / `play_out` on POWL may be version-fragile; that is secondary to the loop error.

**Net.** This is a good advisory memo that would be usable after fixing E2 incrementality, E1 mix, S1 magnitudes, and the supplement/Assessment loop. Under a near-flawless bar those are enough to keep it at 7.0, not in the high 8s–9s.
