The answer is unusually complete and well structured: it hits every required heading, keeps the four cases from collapsing into one sequence, treats K-337 as a decision-policy change rather than a timing trick, and repeatedly separates earlier readiness from earlier completion. Those are the right ideas. Under a hypercritical standard they are not enough, because the write-up still contains a serious timing error, several internal inconsistencies, and more numerical precision than the evidence supports.

**The K-337 base case does not add up.** Assessment 2 ends Friday 10:30. A Friday-evening decision is about 104 h and a saving of about 110 h; that part is fine. A Monday 09:00–13:30 compliance review and Monday 14:00 decision is not 124 h and is not a 90 h saving. The table range 104–130 h quietly drops the Monday path the prose still treats as the base case. That is a temporal-consistency failure on the case whose cycle-time claim is the largest.

**Two other timings fight the answer’s own constraints.** In K-219 the table starts D2 around 21:00–22:30 after a 5 h queue at 16:00 and originals at 17:30; the best case then starts D2 at 19:00 with no extra justification.
