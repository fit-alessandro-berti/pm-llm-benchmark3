**6.2**

The JSON is well-structured, case-specific, and clearly better than a DFG-copying baseline. Primary states are mostly right, A vs B is not treated as the same prefix problem, migration is not read as “task never happened,” and the intervals are appropriately wide. That is the ceiling. Under a strict reading of the rubric, several requirement-level and process-semantics failures keep this far from a high score.

**Required distinction is essentially abandoned.** Every one of the 15 predictions has `process_enabled: true`. The prompt asked to separate a process-enabled action from the event most likely to appear next in a completion-only log. That distinction is the point of the observation model. Setting the flag universally turns it into decoration. The failure is sharpest on C6-D (predicting delayed appearance of events that, if the attachment is real, already occurred), C6-C rank 2 (Disposition Recorded cannot be enabled if Regulatory Assessment is still mandatory), C6-A rank 2 (another Customer Evidence Requested is not enabled until Evidence Review decides), and C6-E / C6-C rank 3 (Supplier Analysis *start* may be enabled; *Completed* today is not a serious next-event).

**Several “next logged” items are eventual outcomes.** Fast Close and Close — No Safety Issue on C6-B, Technical Investigation Completed on C6-A, and Supplier Analysis Completed on C6-C/E are downstream results, not the imminent completion event. After Evidence Received, the normative next log event is Evidence Review Completed. Fast Close is a triage-time path (286 DFG counts from Initial Triage). Using it as rank 2 after an already-executed evidence cycle is a control-flow error. C6-B’s alternative state “S1 TRIAGED” is worse: the case has left S1.

**Internal contradiction on C6-C.** The write-up argues that the country rule and repeated malfunction make Regulatory Assessment likely mandatory, then ranks Disposition Recorded second with `process_enabled: true` and a 0.20–0.50 band that almost competes with rank 1. If the obligation argument is serious, disposition is not enabled. Rank 3 Supplier Analysis is also weakly grounded: the complaints share a failure mode *across different lots*; rule 5 requires same lot or supplier component. Treating SA Completed as a top-three *next* event overstates both enablement and imminence (6–36 h typical).

**Operating constraints are underused.** Authority Notification batches at 16:00 never appear in any case, despite high-severity injury (A, D) and an explicit reportability signal (C). Technical Investigation has 7 waiting and one of three engineers out; Regulatory Assessment has 12 waiting and risk-based priority. These are mentioned in passing or not at all when they should move both ranking and interval width, especially C6-D (RA opened 12:40; at 14:00 the case is only ~1.3 h into a 2–14 h assignment window, so 0.25–0.55 for RA Completed as the *next* event is optimistic).

**Smaller but real evidence misses.** C6-B is assigned R-T4, who “usually handles potentially reportable cases”—that undercuts the clean duplicate / Fast Close story and is listed as supporting evidence without the tension. C6-D rank 3 Customer Evidence Requested is inferior to Evidence Review Completed once an attachment already exists in the source system. C6-A hedges Supplier Analysis as “likely” even though two devices from the same lot plus three similar complaints already meet rule 5.

**What is actually good.** S3 for A/B/E and S4 for C/D are the right primary labels. C6-D’s migration reading (attachment present, worklist shows RA open, feed flag migration-affected) is the strongest piece of observation-model reasoning. Cross-case text correctly refuses to clone A and B. DFG limitations are stated accurately (observational, unconditioned, cannot tell delay from absence). Intervals are not fake-precise.

The result is a competent process-aware forecast that still misses a named required distinction, conflates next event with later outcome in multiple cases, and leaves material queue/batch evidence on the table. That is not near-flawless.
