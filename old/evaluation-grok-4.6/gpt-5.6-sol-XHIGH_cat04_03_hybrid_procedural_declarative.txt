**8.1**

This is a strong, internally coherent hybrid design with a correct R5/R6 split, generally accurate test verdicts, and real PM4Py-shaped artifacts. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several silent over-constraints, API-semantic risks, and small completeness gaps keep it well below a 9.

## What is actually good

The policy section does the required work: completed-trace semantics, accepted/rejected defined by `Activate Vendor` / `Reject Vendor`, missing `risk_level`, and two explicit R5/R6 resolutions with the implemented choice named and its false-positive risk stated. Mapping R1–R12 to layers is mostly right: XOR lifecycle in the net, pairwise exclusions in DECLARE/skeleton, R9/R6 outside the activity alphabet, R12 as warnings only.

The Petri net is the right formalism. Orthogonal clarification and sanctions places, XOR after classification, AND-join of the three checks, and a labeled pair of `Close Onboarding` transitions are the correct structural moves. Manual replay of T1–T8 against the net matches the matrix. DECLARE template usage is explained, and the R5 stand-in (`response` escalation→opinion plus `nonsuccession` activate→opinion) is conservative and disclosed. T6 is shown under both policies; T7’s z-scores (`(34−12)/6 = 3.67`, `(2−72)/36 ≈ 1.94`) are correct.

## Material technical problems

**1. Log-skeleton `directly_follows` is under-specified and possibly wrong for PM4Py.**  
The dict contains only `("Release First Payment", "Close Onboarding")`. In discovery, PM4Py fills `directly_follows` with *all observed* pairs and conformance often treats that set as an allowed-pair relation. If that is the runtime semantics, T1–T7 fail immediately on `Receive Onboarding Request → …`. Empty-default in the spec argues for selective constraints, and the prose intends “payment must be immediately followed by close.” That intent is never reconciled with the library. Marking T1/T2/T3/T4/T7 skeleton **pass** is therefore only safe under an unproven reading.

**2. The net is stricter than the rules in ways that are only partly costed.**  
- After `Change Bank Details`, payment is disabled until *both* `Suspend Vendor` and a new `Verify Bank Account` complete. R11 requires suspension *eventually* and re-verification *before a later payment*. `Change → Verify → Payment → Suspend` satisfies R11 and is illegal here.  
- Escalation and bank-change cycles are serialized; R4/R11 are not 1–1 matching rules.  
- `Activate Vendor` consumes `p_clarification_idle` and `p_sanctions_clear` and never returns them, so post-outcome clarification or a later opinion is impossible. Fine for the chosen scope, but it is a language restriction, not a theorem of R3–R5.  
The compact regular expression also omits the reject-path “close only if sanctions are clear” gate that the net implements. The prose says the formula ignores cross-cutting state, then the net uses that state as a hard completion condition.

**3. DECLARE does not police the exclusive backbone as claimed in spirit.**  
Missing `noncoexistence(Enhanced Due Diligence, Reject Vendor)`. R2 names the two *approvals*; the backbone still forbids EDD+reject. A trace `… Classify, EDD, Reject, Close` fails the net and can pass DECLARE if Compliance never appears (`succession(EDD, Compliance)` would then fail — so this particular hole is smaller than it looks). The larger hole is real: `Receive … Classify, Close` satisfies every listed DECLARE rule (all `response`/`succession` obligations are vacuous) and is accepted by DECLARE while the net deadlocks. That is acknowledged, but then DECLARE is still treated as a reliable fail layer for T5/T8 rather than as a partial projection.

**4. Alternate response is not R3.**  
`altresponse(Request Clarification, Receive Clarification)` allows `Request, Receive, Request, Activate, Receive`. R3 forbids a final outcome with a pending request. The custom monitor is the actual R3 checker; the matrix’s “DECLARE fail” on T5 is true only because the response is *missing*, not because DECLARE encodes the outcome boundary.

**5. Log skeleton is incomplete relative to DECLARE without a principled reason.**  
`always_before(Reject Vendor, Classify Risk)` is absent while the same fact is a DECLARE `precedence`. `never_together` also omits EDD/reject. `always_after` is weaker than `altresponse` on multi-cycle traces; that is explained. The reject/classify omission is just a hole.

**6. Resource layer is slightly wrong on R9 and vague on the PM4Py helper.**  
R9 is about the verification that supports the *applicable* approval, not every later `Verify Bank Account` after a post-activation bank change. Comparing all verifies to the approver is a silent strengthening. `len(approvals) == 0` yields a vacuous pass (T3); `not evaluated` would be more honest. `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` is dropped in as `*_candidates` with no statement of whether the API returns violators or four-eyes-satisfying cases (PM4Py docs have used both phrasings). The custom checker is usable; the helper citation is not.

**7. R6 exception is a verdict story, not a model.**  
The implemented net and DECLARE *are* strict R5. The T6-exception row assumes an uncoded net that “relaxes the activation gate” and a DECLARE dict with `nonsuccession` removed. That is enough for the required contrast, but it is not an executable second policy.

## Smaller nits

- `exactly_one` on capture/screening/classify/close is a completed-case convention, not a requirement; it will false-fail running prefixes, which §10 only mentions at the end.  
- `succession(Activate Vendor, Release First Payment)` makes any activate-without-payment a DECLARE fail even when the interesting defect is R1 (T8). Harmless but noisy.  
- `nonsuccession(Activate, Opinion)` rejects a post-activation opinion with *no* escalation; the net is more precise. Disclosed, still a layer disagreement.  
- Temporal “0–96 / 0–24 / 0–144 hours” replaces the two-sided interval’s negative lower bound with 0; PM4Py still scores `|elapsed − mean|` vs `zeta · sd`. Same pass/fail on these traces, sloppy description.  
- T5 temporal **pass** vs T3 **not evaluated** is inconsistent: T5’s clarification pair is absent (like T3), and only the payment pair is scored.  
- Emergency-auth `bool(cell)` treats any non-empty string as true.  
- Support/confidence `1.0` is handled correctly as non-empirical in §9.

## Verdict matrix

T1–T5, T7, T8 are right under the stated layers. T6 strict vs exception is the right split. No test is clearly mis-scored *given their semantics*. That is the main reason this is still an 8 rather than a 6.

## Bottom line

The answer understands hybrid process modeling: it does not dump every rule into DECLARE, it does not hide R5/R6, and the net plus dictionaries are coherent enough to replay. Strictly, it still over-encodes R11, leaves DECLARE/skeleton holes, and asserts skeleton/four-eyes behavior the APIs may not implement. Those are exactly the class of defects this task is designed to catch.
