**6.5**

The answer is complete, correctly formatted, and substantively stronger than a typical response: it does not treat the restructuring as inherently good or bad, it differentiates East from North/South, it treats fairness and learning as first-class effects, and it refuses a full rollout without a pilot. Those are the hard constraints, and they are met. The score is still far from high because several analytical claims do not survive close inspection.

**The pilot cannot test the thing it claims to test.** Converting only East while leaving North and South embedded is internally incoherent. Either N_Tech and S_Tech stay local, in which case there is no pooled Technical Shared Service to evaluate, or they move into the TSS, in which case North/South are no longer on the current model. East is also the smallest team (520 cases/month versus 820 and 760). A three-specialist pool serving only East volume will look under-utilized; the 95% utilization and 2×-wait guardrails will not fire. The design therefore systematically hides the central bottleneck that is the main operational risk at full scale. The answer never acknowledges this.

**The control-group claim is wrong.** The text says North/South “isolate… expertise-loss effects.” Those teams are unchanged, so the pilot cannot observe the loss of North/South local knowledge. At best this is a difference-in-differences on East’s relief, not a test of the harm pathway that the mechanism map itself treats as central.

**Optimistic cycle-time gains contradict management’s own table.** Every high-frequency proposed wait is *longer* than the current analogue (TSS 4.0 vs 3.2 h, return 2.2 vs 1.6 h, approval 3.0 vs 2.4 h, legal/risk 10.5 vs 9.6 h), and approval frequency *rises* (42 → 49). An unargued 10–15% median cycle-time improvement in the optimistic scenario is not derived from the given estimates. Cycle times are in days and handover waits in hours, so a serious forecast needed a different mechanism (rework, East relief, fewer failed local loops)—and should have said so.

**Queueing on Legal/Risk is under-analyzed.** A 9 → 12 per 100 rise is a one-third volume increase on resources already at 91–93% utilization. Management’s 10.5 h wait is not a mild deterioration; it is implausible without added capacity. The answer notes “near capacity” but never uses that fact.

**Measurement errors.** Activity counts are not utilization. `discover_working_together_network` does not measure utilization. `filter_event_attribute_values` is the wrong primitive for case-level fields (`case_complexity`, `customer_segment`, `outcome`). `format_dataframe(..., resource_key=...)` is not a reliable PM4Py signature. `filter_variants` is listed as relevant and then not used. Role discovery is as likely to show *cleaner* specialist activity profiles after centralization as “blurred” ones; the mapping is too casual.

**Learning horizon.** Skill atrophy is a core mechanism, yet the live pilot is 8–12 weeks—too short for the lagging resource-similarity and `local_expertise_flag` effects the answer itself places on a months-long clock.

**What remains good, and why that is not enough.** The mechanism map correctly flags that *nominal* local loops can fall while *net* handovers stay flat or rise; the three scenarios are distinct and carry East-specific paths; the invisible-risks section is the strongest part; the recommendation is appropriately conditional. Those strengths make this a competent consulting memo. They do not make it nearly flawless. The pilot is the decision instrument, and it is misspecified. That is a structural defect, not a polish issue.
