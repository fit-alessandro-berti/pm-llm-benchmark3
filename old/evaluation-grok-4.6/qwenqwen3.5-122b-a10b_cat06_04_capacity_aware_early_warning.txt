**5.0**

The write-up has the right skeleton and hits the three advertised “strong answer” tells (I-03 is ineligible by stage, I-05 is suppressed while I-07 is re-entered on new evidence, spillovers are named). It is not close to a high score. The quantitative core is internally inconsistent, the stated decision rule is not the rule actually used, and the chosen portfolio would change under the author’s own formula once the omitted terms are restored.

**The decision rule is not decision-theoretically coherent.** Section 1 announces a calibration adjustment to `p_now` and then defines NEV as `ARR × value − cost − burden − spillover`, where ARR is the midpoint of the supplied effect band. Calibrated `p` never enters the score. Time-to-SLA never enters the score. The calibration step is inert. Charging every case the same €2,400 spillover term is also inert for ranking; spillover is a portfolio externality (how many slots you burn, and which untreated diagnosis-stage cases absorb delay), not a case-level attribute. Midpoints of the effect ranges are treated as known causal effects, which the prompt forbids.

**The live allocation is the wrong portfolio under the author’s own arithmetic.** I-03 is correctly refused. I-05 is correctly held. After that the ranking is hard to defend:

- I-04’s midpoint direct NEV is on the order of \(0.19 \times 60{,}000 - 450 - 2{,}000 \approx 9{,}000\), and even the low end stays clearly positive. It is parked as “Tier 3 / wait” because 10 hours remain.
- I-08 is promoted to “Tier 1 / intervene” at 8 hours. The thinking and the implicit score use \(0.10 \times 100{,}000 - 450 = 9{,}550\) and drop the **€5,000 false-alarm burden**. Restored, midpoint NEV is about €4,550; at the 0.05 bound it is **negative** (\(-450\) before spillover). Strategic overprediction further cuts the case for acting now. An 8h vs 10h distinction cannot justify preferring the lower-effect, higher-burden, overpredicted case.
- I-07 is a defensible urgency/evidence exception (1.5h, new log), but its midpoint NEV is ~€800 and cannot carry spillover. Selecting it *and* I-08, then claiming I-08 is “needed for the math to work,” is circular: a better third case (I-04, or a calibrated I-01) does not need I-08’s headline value.
- I-01 is well-calibrated Public Sector with the strongest effect band in the set and is waited; I-09 is cautiously deferred, which is reasonable, but the non-monetary public-sector obligation is never weighed.

**Section 3 would fail an audit.** Optimistic “1.8 breaches avoided” is \(0.72+0.66+0.43\), i.e. the sum of observational `p_now`. That is the exact failure mode the rubric flags: treating the prediction as the intervention effect. Midpoint ARRs for the chosen set sum to ~0.51, not 1.1; upper bounds sum to 0.69, not 1.8. The economic note (€15,050) is in a different universe from the “breaches” column. False-alarm burden (€50+€600+€5,000 = €5,650) is missing from the impact table despite being in the Section 1 formula; with it, base net is thin and adverse is deeply negative, especially if I-08 is the case that “receives little or no benefit.” Optimistic spillover is written as €2,400, which is one slot at the base rate, not three slots at the low end (\(3 \times 0.01 \times 6 \times 20{,}000 = 3{,}600\)). The €20k untreated-case value is asserted, not derived from the actual diagnosis-stage residual set (I-06, I-09, and whatever is unlisted).

**Repeated-alert policy is the strongest section** and is scored as such: cool-down, material-event re-entry, and an explicit I-05 vs I-07 contrast. Even here the rule is thinner than it looks (no numeric change-in-evidence threshold, no separate rule for I-02’s second alert, no link from new evidence to whether ARR should be re-estimated).

**Fairness and monitoring are generic-concrete rather than tight.** The required topics appear, and a 20% fairness floor, shadow labels, and a holdout are real safeguards. They are not applied to *this* board (one SME slot is I-07-for-urgency, not a floor). Segment-specific `p` thresholds on top of the unused calibration offsets would double-count. Randomly withholding 10% of critical incidents is mentioned without a capacity-rationing or overflow identification strategy, which is the natural experiment this setting actually provides. Policy-induced drift, specialist preemption, and whether spillover lands on untreated SME/NNLS cases are described as principles plus a quarterly audit, not as instruments with owners and stop-rules that would change tomorrow’s four slots.

Net: a plausible-looking six-section memo whose qualitative process rules are serviceable and whose numbers, ranking, and use of information are not reliable enough to deploy. The gap between the promised NEV rule and the cases that actually get slots is large enough that this cannot sit in the upper half of the scale.
