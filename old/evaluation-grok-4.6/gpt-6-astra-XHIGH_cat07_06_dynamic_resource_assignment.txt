## What it gets right

It uses the required headings, separates hard gates from preferences, and does not optimize for throughput alone. Skill 0 is treated as unauthorized even under supervision, which correctly blocks the C015 note that Eva/Jonas could train on Security. High/urgent work is not given to skill 1 without a named supervisor. Bruno, David, and Iris get no new execution work. Quality batching is limited to C006/C012. Those are necessary conditions for a high score, not sufficient ones.

## Section 3 is the main failure

The allocation is presented as the output of Section 2. It is not. No eligibility set, urgency score, component score, or joint-capacity check is shown for even one case. An auditable policy needs a replayable decision. This one cannot be replayed, so Section 6’s “deviations from assignment policy” indicator has no operational baseline.

**C001 → Hugo is the weakest call.** Remaining SLA is 3h, complexity is 0.88, handover cost is high, and the prompt’s own evidence is a median **2.7h extra wait** on high-complexity owner changes. Constraint 5 prefers continuity unless the owner is skill 0/1 **and supervision is not feasible**. Celine is skill 1; Gita/Hugo supervision is feasible; Constraint 6 explicitly allows Security at skill 1–2 under those supervisors. The answer overrides this with the case note “needs specialist,” without comparing:

- transfer: ~2.7h handover delay against a 3h deadline, versus
- keep + supervise: the 3.3h weak-skill service penalty, possibly reduced by specialist sign-off.

Both options can miss. That is worth stating. Choosing the transfer without that comparison, then adding “use Gita if safer,” is an incomplete exception, not a reasoned optimum. The structured handover does not cancel the cited delay.

**C003 is a poor training vehicle.** David already owns a repeated diagnosis loop, is a Diagnosis specialist, and has reassignment count 2. Moving ownership to 3 is exactly the regime tied to 1.8× rework and 1.4× appeals. Constraint 4 forbids **adding** work to high-overtime people; it does not require stripping existing work. C014 leaving David is justified. C003 leaving him is not, except as a quota move so Farid can count toward 20% training. Splitting a looping diagnosis into Celine-as-lead plus Farid’s “bounded segment” also blurs accountability on the case most likely to bounce.

**Celine is loaded in a way that contradicts the resilience objective.** Accountable or supporting roles: C003 lead, C007 (high, 6h, complexity 0.81, third reassignment), C009 supervision, C016 consultation (high, 7h, complexity 0.79). Hours remaining: 6.0. C009 is **normal** priority, so Constraint 2 does not require supervision at all; spending Celine on optional coaching while C007 is live is a capacity error. The comparison table later admits Celine as a support dependency. The assignment creates that dependency.

**Farid’s batch is not shown to be feasible.** C002 (urgent Approval, 2h) and C011 (urgent VIP Negotiation, 4h) already conflict on 6.5 remaining hours. Adding C003 training “after urgent work” is a wish, not a schedule. The answer repeatedly says calendars are missing, then still writes a dispatch table as if the named sessions fit.

**Hours remaining are never used as numbers.** Open-case counts are correctly demoted to a signal, then not replaced with even a rough start-time/sequence check for the 2h/3h/4h/6h/7h items. That is the data the SLA component supposedly needs.

Smaller allocation issues: C013 saturates Jonas at the invented “four involvements” cap while Amal (Eligibility 3) still has slack; C010 moves a 20h normal Approval onto Eva, who already holds C005 and C016 in a 5-hour remainder; skill-0 people whose training priority is Security/Approval get no onboarding path, so those priorities are permanently unsatisfiable under this policy.

## Policy design issues

The conservative rewrite of Constraint 1 (count retains, supervision, and consultation toward a cap of four) is not the stated rule. The answer then **binds Celine and Jonas at that rewritten cap**, which is how a safety check becomes a bottleneck.

The linear score is unspecified where it matters (`w_sla`, `w_skill`, …) while the urgency formula is given to two decimals. Those weights cannot have produced Section 3. Tie-breakers (incumbent, utilization, scarce-skill recency, resource ID) are listed and then not cited in the table.

“No high-overtime exception is required” is true only because existing cases were removed from Bruno/David/Iris. That is a different policy than “do not assign more work.” Socio-technically, zeroing three people in one batch while Jonas takes four is exactly the kind of pattern workers will read as a ranking, which Section 5 then has to talk down.

## Sections 4–7

Section 4 is the best part: directional, no fake percentages, gated vs raw baselines, stress tests. It still does not apply those tests to **this** snapshot (e.g., Hugo or Celine absent, Farid’s two urgents).

Section 5 is the right tone and still too vague about the actual controversy (all queued work leaving the high-overtime owners; C001 leaving Celine).

Section 6 is thorough and unusually careful about decision-time snapshots. It is also oversized relative to the fact that the policy cannot be reconstructed from scores. Several indicators will measure symptoms of Section 3 (C003’s third transfer, Celine utilization, training cancellations) that the design already invites.

Section 7 names every required PM4Py symbol and handles single-timestamp logs correctly. The SNA/role/similarity objects are discovered and then barely connected to scoring: handover cost is not calibrated from `discover_handover_of_work_network`, supervision pairing is not informed by `discover_working_together_network`, and roles are not used as an authorization check. That is a learn/audit sketch, not a closed loop.

## Bottom line

Hard constraints on skill, Security/Approval rules, overtime **additions**, batching, and the 20% training floor are mostly respected. Continuity, SLA physics, joint feasibility, and replayable scoring are not. For a policy that must be explainable **and** auditable, the gap between the algorithm and the C001–C016 table, plus C001/C003/Celine, are enough to keep this well below a high score.
