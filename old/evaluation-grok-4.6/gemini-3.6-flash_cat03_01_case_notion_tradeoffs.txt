## Core conceptual failure: convergence is not applied as defined

Section 1 defines convergence as an event related to multiple objects of the *same lead type*. The matrix then abandons that definition for four of six rows.

- **Item / Low.** This is wrong. `e13` relates to `I21` and `I31`; `e14` to `I32` and `I41`; `e16`/`e24` to `I12`, `I21`, `I31`; `e17`/`e27` to `I32` and `I41`. That *is* convergence, and it is exactly why the next column says duplication is High. “Items do not converge into each other; they only co-participate in shared operational events” is a contradiction, not a qualification.
- **Delivery / Moderate.** No event relates to more than one Delivery. The E2O sets for `e15`–`e17` and `e23`, `e24`, `e27` are pairwise disjoint on `{D1,D2,D3}`. The author even writes “Duplication: None … D1, D2, D3 are disjoint in E2O.” Convergence cannot be Moderate if duplication is None.
- **Invoice / High, duplication Moderate.** No event relates to more than one Invoice. Flattening on Invoice yields three cases with no copied events. `V2` billing `O2` and `O3` is many-to-one O2O structure; it causes *Order*-flattening convergence, not Invoice-flattening convergence. “Multi-order propagation duplications” is the wrong mechanism for this row.
- **Package / High** is only half-right. `e16` and `e24` do relate to both `P2` and `P3`, so some same-type convergence exists. “`P3` and `P4` contain items from multiple orders” is not Package-level convergence; it is an O2O fact that belongs on the Order/Item rows.

Only Order (High) and Payment (None) are rated consistently with Section 1. A comparison matrix whose main technical columns are wrong on 4/6 types cannot carry the rest of the argument.

Divergence is also treated loosely. Two instalments on `V2` (`e22`, `e25`) are the invoice’s own sequential lifecycle, not moderate divergence. Classic divergence on this log is O1 (`P1`/`P2`, `D1`/`D2`) and especially O3 (`e13`/`e14`, `e16`/`e17`, `e24`/`e27`). O3 is the ugliest Order trace in the snapshot and never receives a dedicated distortion.

## Factual and technical errors

**Distortion 2 reverses the wait.** `I32` is added at 09:23 (`e09`), `I41` at 09:25 (`e10`), `P4` is packed at 10:20 (`e14`). `I32` waits for `I41`, not the reverse. The durations 57 vs 55 minutes are computed correctly and then attached to the wrong causal story. That is careless, and it is one of the two required cross-order-package examples.

**Distortion 1 overclaims “artificial” DFG edges.** `e07 → e13` in O2 and `e08 → e13` in O3 are real within-case precedences. The harm is frequency inflation of one physical pack, not a fabricated Add→Pack order.

**Distortion 4 presents a story as a fact.** Joint issuance of `V2` at 12:05 does not show that billing of O2 was “held back until O3 was ready.” Invoices `V1`–`V3` all go out between 12:00 and 12:10 after the last dispatch. A noon billing batch is at least as consistent with the timestamps.

**Section 7, check 3 confuses DFG edges with activity pairing.** In O1 the consecutive dispatch/confirm pairs are `e15 → e16` and, much later, `e23 → e24`. `e15 → e24` and `e16 → e23` are not DFG edges. The 1h 25m vs 5h 00m range is a possible *performance-annotation* mismatch, not a DFG-path fact. Labeling it “DFG edge pairing” is a technical error in the validation plan.

Smaller slips accumulate. “`e13`, `e14`, … merge O1, O2, O3, O4” is false (`e13` is only O2/O3). “The DFG edge Add Item → Pack Package gets a frequency of 2” is false as written; all six items contribute that edge. “200%–300% inflation” is used as if 3 copies were both 200% and 300%. Position B is never argued against, even though “Order plus secondary Item/Invoice/Package lifecycles” is the obvious alternative to C.
