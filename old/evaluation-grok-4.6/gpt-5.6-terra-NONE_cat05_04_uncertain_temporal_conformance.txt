The write-up has the right skeleton: offset direction is stated correctly, resolution is applied before clock correction, DST alternatives are not blindly convex-hulled, `Alert_Sent` is not treated as `Alert_Received`, T-404 keeps the 18–25 minute band, midpoints are quarantined to the PM4Py comparison, and the SLA/profile and certain/possible/indeterminate distinctions are conceptually sound. Sections 6–7 are the strongest part of the deliverable. That is not enough. The published numbers are frequently wrong, sometimes internally contradictory, and in several places they change the classification that the whole exercise exists to produce.

**The tables do not implement the formula in §1.**  
For a non-symmetric offset \([a,b]\), true time is \([\text{recorded_lo}-b,\ \text{recorded_hi}-a]\). OPS-BER displayed `10:14` is therefore `[08:12:00Z, 08:16:29.999Z]`, not `[08:12:30Z, 08:16:59.999Z]`. The published interval is `true ≈ recorded + offset`, the inverse of the stated definition. The same inversion (or an extra minute of floor error) appears on VENDOR-NY (`08:04:59.999Z` should be `08:03:59.999Z`; the span is 480 s after the text promised 420 s), LEGACY-BER (lower bound is one minute too early; width 240 s instead of 180 s), and T-409 (`[15:50Z, 16:28:59.999Z]` should be `[15:52:00Z, 16:16:59.999Z]`). Symmetric HUB/SENSOR bounds hide the bug, which is why those rows look fine.

**T-405, the DST case, is mishandled in the one place that matters.** Fold selection is right: Alert in the first `02:50` (CEST), start and containment in the second pass (CET). From the author’s own selected intervals that implies S1 ≈ `[660, 1140]` s, i.e. a **possible** 15-minute breach. The findings table reports `[6600.001, 8359.999]` s (~110 min) and `CERTAIN_VIOLATION`. That is a factor-of-ten error, and it is inconsistent with the same row-set’s S2 (~33–41 min) and Alert→Containment (~48–56 min). Those three durations cannot be true together. Correct arithmetic on the correct bounds is S1 ≈ `[720, 1080]` s: still `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`, not certain. Calling T-405 S1 a settled breach in §3 and §6 is therefore false on the author’s intervals and false on the right ones.

- T-402 Alert→Containment is two HUB seconds: `[3717, 3723]` s. The table’s upper bound `4323` s is ten minutes too large and fabricates a zeta=2 `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION`. The pair is a `CERTAIN_PASS` at zeta=2 and a `CERTAIN_VIOLATION` at zeta=1.

**§3 and §4 disagree with each other, and both disagree with §5.** The sensitivity table marks T-401 S1 / T-402 S1 / T-404 S1 as passing once tolerance is added, then the notes retract those calls. Dead helpers (`duration_seconds` raises, negative-duration clipping via `fromtimestamp(0)`) and special-cased T-407/T-409 classifications make the script a sketch, not a reproduction of §2–§3. **What remains credit-worthy is the interpretive layer, not the verdicts.** T-407 as `INDETERMINATE` is the right instinct. T-408 S2/S3 are genuine certain SLA breaches. Under a strict reading this is a well-structured, conceptually informed answer whose concrete interval analysis cannot be trusted. That is a mid-score, not a high one.
