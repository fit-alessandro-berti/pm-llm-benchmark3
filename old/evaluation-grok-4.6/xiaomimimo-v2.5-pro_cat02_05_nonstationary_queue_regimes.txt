The write-up is structurally complete and conceptually stronger than its numbers. It hits every required heading, refuses to treat outage recovery as a normal high-throughput regime, keeps median service distinct from flow time in principle, and gives a real alternative segmentation plus a pooled-vs-segmented policy contrast. Those are the right instincts. Under a strict reading they are not enough, because several **featured quantitative claims are wrong, internally inconsistent, or definitionally sloppy**.

### What holds up
- Threats in §1 are mostly well chosen: overnight carry-over, outage distortion, mix shift, hidden burstiness, pooling that hides an intervention. The opening-hours convention and the “not an event log” limit are stated clearly.
- Primary regimes (stable mix → outage/recovery → high mix at old staff → high mix after 29 Apr) match the main non-stationarities. Recovery on 16–17 Apr is correctly read as catch-up, not extra capacity in steady state.
- The alternative two-regime mix split is a genuine different operational story, and the strength/weakness comparison is fair.
- §5’s qualitative hourly contrast (smooth weekday vs 34-in-one-hour burst vs weekend flatness) and the warning that four vectors cannot identify a stochastic model are sound.
- The Little’s Law **verdicts** (R1 usable, R2 not, R3/R4 only approximate, pooled not defensible) are the right calls.
- §7’s “6.5 FTE looks fine when pooled, fails in the high-mix week” is a proper Simpson-style policy warning.
- The Python block does the required seven jobs and does not pretend the daily table is a PM4Py log.

### Material errors (these dominate the grade)

**1. Little’s Law time units are wrong, and the text contradicts the code.**  
\(W=L/\lambda=20/100=0.20\) **days** is then converted to **1.4 hours**. That is a 7-hour day. Opening hours are 12 hours; \(0.20\times12=2.4\) open-hours, or \(0.20\times24=4.8\) clock-hours if a calendar day is the unit. The same 7-hour factor is reused (weekend 2.3 h, R3 3.3 h, R4 2.1 h). The code multiplies by 12. An “applicable” LL claim cannot survive a unit error of that size.

**2. Daytime \(L\) and daily \(\lambda\) are combined without a boundary argument.**  
`mean_wip` is a snapshot mean (almost certainly opening hours). Overnight WIP is frozen. Using \(L_{\text{open}}/\lambda_{\text{per calendar day}}\) is not a consistent Little window unless overnight occupancy is folded in. The matrix talks about “boundary stability” but never fixes this.

**3. Median service is subtracted from implied mean sojourn** (“1.4 h − 0.4 h ≈ 1.0 h wait”) after the task—and the answer’s own audit—forbade substituting median service for mean flow time. That wait is then called “plausible” at \(\rho\approx0.58\) with 6 FTE. In a standard multi-server queue that utilization does not produce ~14 extra bodies in WIP. The discrepancy (WIP definition, skew so that mean \(\gg\) median, parked work) is not confronted.

**4. Several comparison numbers used to argue pooling are simply incorrect.**
- Pooled median arrivals is **103.5**, not 99; pooled median WIP is **23**, not 26; weekday median arrivals is **~107.5**, not 109.
- 15 Apr net is \(105-58=+47\), not “+54”.
- 15 Apr peak share is \(22/105\), not \(22/101\).
- Weekday arrival Fano is about **0.63–0.66**, not 0.78 (variance is ~68–72, not 84). Mean arrivals are ~90, not 88.
- R1 weekday completion median is 100, not 98; R2 weekday arrival median is 105, not 107.

The **direction** of the pooling critique survives. A section titled “quantitative comparisons” does not.

**5. Segmentation and stationarity claims are looser than they look.**  
R3 is dated 18–28 Apr, which dumps 18–19 Apr (complex 23–24%, ordinary weekend service) into “high-complexity.” The mix break is 20 Apr. R3 is called non-stationary in the LL matrix and “NOT DEFENSIBLE” in code, but “approximate only” in the table. Offered load \(\rho\) is computed from **median** service and then used as if it were utilization for FTE targets (including a confused “7.5 FTE ⇒ 0.78 on a p90-driven estimate”).

### Smaller but real issues
- Required regime table is split across three tables; confidence is only on boundaries, not on the rate estimates.
- Alternative segmentation is still a calendar mix cut, not a structurally different cut (e.g. weekday/weekend-first, or peak-share/burstiness-first).
- Lag-1 “0.3–0.4” is asserted in prose without a number from the series; one outage–recovery pair can dominate that correlation.
- Rolling-5 complex%>35% first fires on a window **ending ~22 Apr**, not “19–20 Apr.”
- Overnight arrivals “not recorded” is an extra assumption, not in the prompt.
- Hard-coded medians in the last printout bypass the tables the script just built.

### Bottom line
This is a competent regime essay with the right refusal points on recovery, pooling, and LL abuse—then it mishandles the one law it does apply, mis-states several of the pooled statistics that are supposed to carry §4, and contradicts itself between narrative and code. That is well above a shallow or format-fail answer, and well short of nearly flawless. **6.0.**
