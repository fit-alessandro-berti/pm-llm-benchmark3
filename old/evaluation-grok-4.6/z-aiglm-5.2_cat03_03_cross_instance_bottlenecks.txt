**6.5**

The final write-up is a strong, well-structured object-centric analysis: it uses concrete IDs and timestamps, keeps counterfactuals explicit, refuses to treat machine capacity as the primary bottleneck, and treats the L7–Job linking inconsistency as a first-class issue. It is not close to flawless. Several stated requirements are missed, and a few numerical/causal claims are sharper than the evidence.

**What is done well.** All eight required sections appear. Observed vs inferred edges are separated. Motifs A–C are genuinely higher-order (lot–job–batch–machine–tool; lot coupling across B1/B2; fixture → CNC-2 → S1 → J1). The six required delay calculations are present, arithmetically correct (3h55m, 3h40m, 11:45−10:05 = 1h40m, 19:25−13:48 = 5h37m, 50 min, 3h30m), and each states a counterfactual. Ranking machine capacity last is justified by 44–58% utilization. Flattening critiques are specific (Job DFG has no `Wait for Tool` / lot events; Shipment DFG hides J1’s 13:30–19:25 wait; low utilization is starvation). Interventions include sync reduction, tools, shipment policy, and logging, each with harm, perverse metric, and an OC evaluation hook. Causality hygiene is generally good.

**Requirement misses (costly).** Two of the three “four object-type” narratives fail that bar. Narrative 2 is L7, C2, C5, J2, J5 — three types (ComponentLot, Component, Job), not five. Narrative 3 is J5, CAL-3, TEST-1, J6 — three types (Job, Tool, Machine). Only narrative 1 clearly has four types. Section 8 asks for six observations that weaken the *top-ranked* explanation (batch sync). Only items 1–2 clearly do; 3–6 attack FIX-A, S1, CAL-3, and the override story. That is a different, weaker deliverable. Word count is also well above the 1,800–2,800 target (roughly ~3.3k+ excluding code).

**Analytical flaws.** The 1h40m FIX-A figure assumes CNC-2 stayed available from 10:05 to 11:45. The log contradicts that: CNC-2 is idle only 10:05–10:47, so something happens at 10:47 while FIX-A is still held until 11:40. The conservative overlap is ~42 min, not 1h40m. The alternative is mentioned, then underweighted. Rank 1 vs 2 is under-argued: S1 imposes ~5.5h on J1, larger than J1’s B1 wait, yet shipment is ranked below batch without saying why breadth/recurrence beats magnitude. B1 and B2 are lumped as one mechanism even though B2 is at least as much RACK-7 quarantine (until 14:35) and L7-delayed J5 as a classic wait-for-peers barrier. The claim that J1/J2’s ~4h waits are “absorbed into” a 1.1h Stage→Machine *median* is statistically muddy; those waits would sit above the median unless oven, CNC, and test are being flattened into one heterogeneous edge (a point that should have been made explicitly).

**Smaller defects.** “Six object types” then seven are listed; Motif C is called six types but is five. The observability column does not walk every flattening on every row. The PM4Py block includes every requested symbol and correctly avoids inferring wait only from machine gaps, but it is not executable as written: `shared_batches` stores triples and is later unpacked as pairs; OC-DFG `event_count` / `unique_objects` fields are invented; `discover_performance_dfg` is unpacked as a 2-tuple.

Under a hypercritical rubric these are not nits. The piece is good applied analysis with incomplete spec compliance and one over-precise delay attribution. That is a mid-6, not an 8–10.
