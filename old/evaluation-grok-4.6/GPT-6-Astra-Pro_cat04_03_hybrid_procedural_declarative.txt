**8.4**

This is a strong, internally coherent hybrid design: R5/R6 is treated as a real policy conflict rather than a mergeable pair of rules, the Petri net is 1-safe and matches the backbone, the DECLARE/skeleton/temporal artifacts are mostly well-typed, and the T1–T8 math (including T7’s \(70/36 \approx 1.944\) non-warning) is correct. It is not near-flawless. Several taxonomy, executability, and verdict-presentation problems remain.

**R5 is not a resource/data property, but the matrix treats it as one.** Under policy S, T6’s decisive failure is activity-order history: activation with an outstanding escalation. That can be checked from the event sequence alone. Putting the fail in “Resource/data layer” blurs the distinction the task asked for. The reason — keeping the net policy-neutral between S and E — is understandable, but then the matrix needed an explicit policy-monitor column or a procedural/policy split. As written, T6 looks like a data-quality miss rather than a legal-precedence miss.

**R11’s control-flow half is left entirely outside the activity models.** “If change occurs after activation, suspend must follow; a later payment needs a subsequent verification” is ordering, not an identity or attribute check. The net still allows `Activate Vendor → Change Bank Details → Release First Payment`, and DECLARE has no conditional response for that pattern. The external monitor is more precise than a naïve global `response(Change Bank Details, …)`, but the procedural envelope is then strictly weaker than the stated backbone-plus-R11 process. None of T1–T8 exercises this, so the gap is untested as well as unenforced in the named models.

**The named `log_skeleton` is not the model the verdicts assume.** The static dictionary omits repeatable activities from `activ_freq`. The pass/fail matrix depends on `skeleton_for_log`. If the cited PM4Py behavior is right, `pm4py.conformance_log_skeleton(log, log_skeleton)` is not what Section 8 evaluates. If it is wrong (several PM4Py paths only constrain keys that are present), the “missing key ⇒ frequency deviation” claim is a false API statement. Either way, the required artifact and the scored artifact diverge.

**“Overall pass” overstates what was actually certified.** Section 7 correctly refuses to infer `risk_class` from the approval route, then Section 8 still marks T1–T3 `pass` overall. The footnote about an “observation boundary” does not appear in the cell. For a matrix whose vocabulary includes `not evaluated`, T1/T2 should have been `pass` with an explicit coverage caveat in the resource/data cell, or overall should have reflected the unevaluated route-matching obligation.

**The “compact formal description” is not the procedural model.** The process tree is only the happy-path backbone. Clarification, escalation, opinion, bank change, suspend, and re-verify exist only in the net. That is disclosed, but Section 3 then asks the reader to treat the tree as the language and the net as a silent expansion. A compact description of the net (places for `idle`/`pending`, `pre`, `screened`, `post`) was the object that needed formalization.

**Smaller but real nits.** T5 temporal is scored `pass` because the payment pair is in range; the unmatched clarification pair is a missing measurement, which their own Section 6 says is not an elapsed-time deviation — `pass` is then easy to misread as “clarification timing was fine.” Skeleton backstops R1/R8/R10 but not R4/R7, without a clear rule for which DECLARE constraints get a skeleton twin. `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` is never used or even wrapped, despite being the suggested API for R9. Alternate-precedence is an extra strengthening of R3, which they admit, but that strengthening is then treated as authoritative in T5’s DECLARE cell. Support/confidence are handled correctly as metadata; the GitHub footnotes do not substitute for executing the dictionaries.

**What still holds.** S vs E is not silently conjoined; T6 is scored both ways, and missing joint authorization under E is `not evaluated` rather than a fake pass. Unconditional `precedence(Obtain Legal Opinion, Activate Vendor)` is correctly rejected as too strong for T1. `exactly_one` is not abused for optional terminals. Alternate response is not confused with chain response. Noncoexistence is used where order-symmetric exclusion is required. The net’s duplicate labels (`Close Onboarding`, `Verify Bank Account`) are enabling-disjoint. Temporal warnings are not promoted to control-flow fails, and R12’s 24-hour profile band is not identified with R6’s 24-hour exception deadline.

The result is a high-quality modeling write-up with a sound policy stance and accurate test arithmetic, undercut by layer-category mistakes, a non-executable skeleton/verdict gap, and an overall matrix that is slightly cleaner than the coverage story it tells.
