**7.3**

The memo is complete, format-compliant, and strategically sound: it refuses national scale, refuses a blanket rollback, separates facts from interpretation, and recommends a hybrid that matches the mixed evidence. It is not nearly flawless.

**What works.** Alternative E is the right call. The memo treats simple-house gains as real and tail/rework/activation failures as binding constraints. The evidence table is the strongest section: 16 items, labeled cleanly, and the causal claims (skip-rule error, post-billing complaints, coordinator single-point-of-failure) sit in Interpretation rather than Fact. Metric-conflict explanations correctly use fitness vs precision, DFG edges, temporal profiles, and the handover vs working-together networks. Alternatives A–E are evaluated with conditions, and rollout gates are operational.

**Material weaknesses.**

1. **Value-of-information ranking is conceptually off.** M7 is a control dashboard for deviations already visible in the alignments. Classic decision value belongs first with M4 (does net cost flip the scale decision?) and M1/M6 (does the skip rule survive by stratum?). Ranking monitoring #1 conflates “do this immediately” with “this information can change the choice.”

2. **Internal inconsistency on rural and timeboxes.** Section 1 pauses survey-skip and post-billing activation for apartments, old buildings, and business only. Section 6 also reverts rural cases, which were weaker but not in the same failure class as permit-sensitive apartments. “2–4 weeks” for regional expansion and “4–6 weeks” for the controlled period are stated without reconciliation.

3. **SLA is a core metric and is almost unused.** 13% → 16% overall, improved for simple houses, is in the source table and barely appears. That undercuts the claim that all process-mining and operational artifacts were integrated.

4. **A few fact/wording slips.** The executive memo’s fitness/precision sentence (“the process is less controlled than the model suggests”) is backwards: low precision means the *model* is too loose. Evidence row 4 parks an incomplete estimate in Fact. The “~6.5%” complaint bar is invented. “Overall p90/p95 return toward 31/45” reintroduces a mix-confounded headline the memo itself warns against.

5. **Uneven use of given artifacts.** Business-customer billing disputes, field-survey idle capacity (78% → 52%) vs second-visit overload (66% → 92%), sparse start timestamps, and incomplete attributes before week 3 are tabled or implied but not used to shape the immediate operating model as sharply as they could.

The recommendation would survive a management review. Under a strict reading—minor inconsistencies, one VoI error, a missing core metric, and a few soft or invented thresholds—it is a strong memo, not an excellent one.
