**6.0**

The write-up follows the required seven-section structure, stays mostly inside the given process (segments, DFG paths, SP1/SP2/Q1, batching and transport evidence), and does not recommend a blanket lowest-emission design. That is the ceiling. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Lifecycle / quantitative reasoning is sloppy.** The largest error is treating MTO Regular’s **18.5 kWh/order** as comparable to CNC_A’s **1.3–1.5 kWh/item**. Those are different units and different scopes (order-level mix including heat treat, inspection, and rework versus one machine’s per-item energy). Repeating “1.3–1.5 vs 18.5” in both the intervention text and the effects table is a material lifecycle mistake, not a rounding issue. Several other figures are invented despite the explicit ban on unsupported precision: obsolescence “6% → ~2–3%”, Critical transport “38.0 → ~25–28”, scrap “0.90 → ~0.50”, and an arbitrary “30%” of Critical orders on a shared lane.

**Two of the eight options are the same lever.** S1 and S4 both move Standard-stock Service-contract off air when slack allows. Counting them twice inflates coverage and double-counts the same 7.8 → ~4.2 kgCO2e movement. That leaves the package thinner than it looks.

**S6’s mechanism is internally inconsistent.** Autodowngrading reinforced packs on ground, or escalating to air/van because packaging is reinforced, fights Q1 and can raise damage returns or emissions. The log’s actual failure is packaging–route–geometry mismatch that triggers rework packaging, not “reinforced ⇒ air.”

**Important paths are underused.** Partial shipment + backorder is 7% of volume, 24% late, and a second shipment; cancellations (530) and heat-treat batch wait (1.7× late risk, best energy above batch 10) barely shape the design. Critical is not split into true downtime vs precautionary (S9), so dedicated-van use is only partly triaged. R1 (keep an alternative route for Service-contract in disruption) is listed as an indicator, then undercut by language that treating air as a “dependency” is automatically good for resilience. Air and vans are sometimes the backup, not only waste.

**The “balanced package” is mostly “do S1–S8.”** There is little sequencing, no explicit non-goals, and weak confrontation of conflicts (e.g., S5’s 12–24h consolidation wait vs Service-contract’s 18h median; S8’s service risk if shared lanes are slower). Rebound coverage is adequate in count and themes (batching, mode shift, packaging, rework time, obsolescence, customer stockpiling, air rebound from consolidation), but several items overlap or describe baseline trade-offs rather than effects of the proposed change.

**What still works:** segment- and slack-aware shipping; CNC_B → CNC_A when due-date slack exists, tied to 18% vs 9% rework; wait caps against the batch-energy curve; SP1 22% / SP2 31% used correctly; a usable indicator set and a governance plan (conformance, segment KPIs, exception log, before/after). Those keep the score in the middle rather than lower. The energy accounting error, invented ranges, duplicate interventions, broken packaging rule, and missing partial-shipment / Critical-triage design are enough to block anything near the top of the scale.
