**6.1**

The write-up is complete, readable, and hits the required skeleton. It is not close to flawless. Several claims are factually sloppy, one central SNA distinction is later contradicted, and a few recommendations outrun the evidence the rest of the memo treats as ambiguous.

**Format and calibration.** Headings match the spec and the four required hypotheses appear. The executive summary is about 230–250 words against a 120–180 limit, so it fails a hard constraint. “Nearly doubled” for the absence shock is wrong: 8.4 → 13.9 days is a ~65% rise, not a doubling.

**Network reading.** Section 1 correctly treats handover as sequential transfer, working-together as co-occurrence, and subcontracting as A–B–A re-entry. That is the right way to start reconciling high HO/subcontracting centrality with low WT. The memo then wastes the insight. It treats 3.5 minutes Bot_Rules → Mira as queueing; that edge is the same order as Bot_Rules → Alice (2.8) and Bruno (3.0) and is evidence of fast exception routing, not a pile-up. It treats 42 minutes Mira → Omar as “time between her actions and next steps,” which is specialist wait, not her service. It accepts the scenario’s duration story for low WT uncritically: standard working-together is case co-occurrence, so short events do not by themselves suppress the metric. A sharper account is fan-out plus normalization (many counterparties, high case count) and possible metric construction—not “she only appears briefly.”

Vendors and bots are under-used. The happy path is Bot_Ingest → Bot_Rules → team leads → adjusters → Vendor_Pay. Mira is the exception branch. Vendor_Verify (2.1 h) and Vendor_Pay (1.3 h) dominate waiting time; they barely affect interpretation or risk. The claimed Vendor_Verify → Mira → adjusters bridge is not in the top handover table.

**Hypotheses.** Mechanisms are distinct enough, but evidence is recycled (Bot_Rules in-edges, A–B–A loops, the absence week) instead of being made discriminative. Hypothesis 1 “predictions in future event logs” include training records and written SOPs, which are not log observables. Hypothesis 3’s claim that real boundary spanners should have high WT is theoretically backward: brokers often look exactly like high betweenness and low pairwise co-occurrence. Naming her `Mira_Coordinator` is used as if it settled formal vs informal status.

**Risks.** Rating exception-path key-person risk high is fair. The rest is uneven. Coordination risk is scored high *because* WT is low, which contradicts Section 1’s own warning that WT ≠ collaboration quality. “No audit trail” is inferred from summary tables, not shown. 48% `exception_flag` is read as high-risk/compliance; the flag is not that. Fairness compares 920 Bot_Rules → Mira handovers with 610/590 team-lead inflows—an intentional split, not inequity. Data-quality risk is rated low even though logging of short assign/reopen/clarify events is the whole HO-vs-WT puzzle.

**Further analyses.** All required technique families are named. Several PM4Py calls are over-specified (`discover_handover_of_work_network` does not take weekly windows; working-together does not take `amount_band` weights). The proposed cycle-time regression never mentions selection: Mira is assigned hard cases, so her involvement will correlate with long cycle time even if she shortens it. That is a basic causal error. The absence week is the best identification strategy in the packet and is not developed as a natural experiment.

**Recommendations.** Not removing her, and saying the coordination function must be replaced, satisfies the constraint. Immediate “codify her rules” and “do not merge her into a team” are too sure for a memo that insists the role is unknown; premature codification can destroy judgment work. Backups on Carla/Diego ignore that those adjusters already have the heaviest human loads. Segregation of detection vs specialist selection—the compliance reviewer’s actual point—is not designed, only papered over with case notes.

Net: competent survey, several interpretation errors a process-mining reviewer would reject, internal inconsistency on WT, and a failed word limit. Not a 4 (structure and coverage are real). Nowhere near an 8.
