The final write-up has the right causal skeleton: endogenous staggered rollout, availability vs intensity, median/tail split, queue spillovers, small-cluster uncertainty, and a refusal of a single “X% cycle-time” claim. Estimands, threats, mechanisms, and the embedded-evaluation recommendation are mostly well aimed. Under a strict reading, execution errors are large enough that this is not a high-scoring answer.

**Quantitative tables are internally inconsistent and, as written, often have the wrong sign.** Alpha’s median change is printed as `0.4` rather than about `−0.4`; Gamma’s pre/post median, p90, and rework are printed as `6.5`, `9.3`, `1.2` rather than reductions; DID cells are printed as `4.5`, `6.3`, `1.9` rather than negative relative changes. Section 7 ranges repeat the problem (`6h to 0h`, `10h to +3h`, `0.10 to +0.15`). A reader who trusts the tables would invert the central median/p90 relative-to-control results, especially for Gamma. The surrounding prose still says “less deterioration,” so the section disagrees with itself. That is a serious quantitative-discipline failure, not a rounding nit.

**Section 1 misstates staffing.** Alpha and Gamma lost FTE; the text says “Alpha 2 in May” and “Gamma 1 in June,” which reads as gains. Later mechanism text gets Alpha’s loss right, which only highlights the observation-section error.

**The required Python block is not reproducible as submitted.** `sparkline()` sets `chars = ""`, so `len(chars) - 1 == -1` and indexing the empty string will throw before trends are printed. `(Period - Period).n` is also version-fragile (pandas often returns an `int`, which has no `.n`). Visualization is a stated requirement; a crash there stops pre/post, DID, and the clustered models.

**Identification and code are misaligned.** The primary design is comparative interrupted time series with level *and* slope change. The code estimates two-way FE plus covariates—the design the prompt said not to treat as sufficient—and never estimates the CITS slopes. Comments correctly deny causality, but that does not close the gap.

**Smaller issues.** E6 collapses two outcomes into one estimand. The rollout note under-specifies a minimum *pre*-period relative to the prompt. Escalation is called “likely” increased (confidence 45) on a ~0.9pp descriptive DID while March’s fraud desk hit every branch. None of these would sink a clean answer; they compound the sign and code problems.

Those do not offset inverted tables and non-running analysis under the requested strictness.
