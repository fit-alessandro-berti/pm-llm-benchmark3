The submission has the right skeleton and several defensible case calls, but it is not close to a high score under a strict reading. It contains outright factual errors, a broken POWL construction, failed mandatory acknowledgments on both sides, and several places where process-model semantics are simply wrong.

**Factual and authority errors.** The prosecution asserts that SUP-704 (EUR 18,000) and SUP-705 (EUR 12,000) *exceeded* FD-7’s EUR 20,000 cap. They did not. The same brief then treats that cap as a PD-3 rule and uses it to explain SUP-703. PD-3 has no spend cap; SUP-703 is ineligible because it is a high-risk new foreign supplier, not because of amount. Grouping SUP-702 (in-window, eligible, 2026-06-15) with post-expiry Auto_Approve is sloppy. SUP-702 is also cited for mutable CRM links; the file says that case has a *stable* `notification_id`. Mutable linkage is SUP-706. FD-7 is misread as requiring retrospective *approval* within two business days; the two-day clock is on Quality_Review. PD-3/FD-7 are called “higher-authority” than CS-9; they are not. CS-9 is the controlled standard; PD-3 is an unincorporated monitored exception whose observer later denied weakening CS-9. That tension is the core authority problem and is never argued cleanly.

**Both briefs fail a stated requirement.** The prosecution was required to concede genuine model/data defects. It instead concludes “M0 is fit for purpose,” and treats Request_More_Info as if it were a CS-9 breach. It is not: the loop precedes required checks, and all 47 such cases waited for evidence. The defense was required to concede genuine risk and the limits of informal practice. It does not confront SUP-703, SUP-708, 14 ineligible Auto_Approve cases, or six post-pilot uses. Claiming M0 “cannot express concurrency” is false: M0’s `checks` PO is already concurrent. “Compliance inaction implies acceptance” is exactly the normalization argument the defense was told to cabin.

**Classifications are uneven.** SUP-701, 702, 703, 704, 708, and 714 are directionally right. SUP-706 as data-indeterminate and SUP-707 as a model defect are right. SUP-711 is not indeterminate: same source transaction ID, migration duplicate, otherwise a clean M0 trace — that is compliant process plus a log defect. SUP-715 is overcharged. No rule bans the spousal relationship; the recorded gap is missing CoI *disclosure*, which is not CS-9’s requester≠approver rule. SUP-712 is a real CS-9 SoD break, but the matrix’s reversibility test (“revise M0 to include resource rules”) confuses model completeness with case liability. System judgment then treats the 289 *eligible* pilot cases as operational noncompliance. That inflates the deviation rate and is the split-the-difference move the prompt forbade.

**Section 7 does not implement the required model.** `standard_decision` / `standard_approved` are never attached to a root. The root XOR’s first child is only `standard_checks`. The same `Transition` objects are reused across disjoint routes. `emergency_decision` is not in `emergency_route.nodes` but is used in edges. Emergency post-activation work is an XOR of quality *or* manual approval; FD-7 requires both, plus monitoring. `Provisional_Activate_Supplier` will not match log label `Activate_Supplier`. The “loop” is not `Operator.LOOP`; labeled silent start/end nodes with a single forward edge cannot replay SUP-707’s two `Request_More_Info` events. A competent approximation would be `LOOP(body, request_more_info)` under a single register → checks → XOR(routes) → notify → close spine. The overlay table and field list are a start, but they omit bitemporal effective/record time, decision-time eligibility snapshots, delegation, and authority references as first-class event attributes.

The piece looks complete and is not empty of insight. Under the stated rubric — fairness, POWL semantics, traceability, authority, and a working revision — the errors are not cosmetic. They are load-bearing.
