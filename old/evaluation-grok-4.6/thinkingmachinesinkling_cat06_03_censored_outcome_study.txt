**6.5**

The nine-section writeup is conceptually in the right family: it refuses to recode rejects/withdrawals/opens as non-default, treats activation as a selection mechanism, keeps an as-of prefix, and rejects a random split. That is why it is not a failing answer. It is also not close to flawless. Under a strict reading there are design contradictions, an estimand that does not match the proposed prediction time, a muddled maturity rule, and a skeleton that would not run and does not do what the prompt required.

**Target and estimand.** The primary target — \(P(T_{\mathrm{def}}\le 90\mid X,\text{Activated})\) on mature activated accounts — is a legitimate observational estimand and is better than the warehouse’s informal “reject = 0” story. The multi-state alternative is correctly described as a different question. Two problems remain. First, “90-day hazard” is used as if it were the same object as a 90-day cumulative incidence; those are not interchangeable. Second, prediction is placed at event 3 / 72h, usually *before* activation, while the population is defined by a *later* event. That is conditioning on the future relative to \(t_{\mathrm{pred}}\). Section 8 then says to deploy only after activation, which makes the early prefix poorly motivated: if the model is only used post-activation, a later as-of time would be more coherent; if it is used on every application at event 3, the conditional-on-activation estimand is not identified for the cases in front of the model. That mismatch is the central design flaw.

**Cohort and censoring.** The flow table sums to 48,000 and separates labeled, immature, competing, and open cases. Good. The maturity rule is not. The text simultaneously offers 15 Nov 2025 (90-day window barely closed), ~12 Oct 2025 (90 + 35-day feed), and “`label_available_at` present.” Those are three different cohorts. Sections 2 and 3 also disagree on whether the 3,600 immature activated cases are in the training set (yes, as censored) or excluded from training (complete-case 21,600 only). For an “uncertainty-aware” study, making complete-case the primary analysis and parking survival in a sensitivity is a weak primary strategy: it discards delayed labels and ordinary censoring and selects on label maturity, which is itself time- and process-dependent. Treating a passed 90-day window with a missing feed as “censored at 90 days” can mark actual defaults as non-events; that is not ordinary right censoring.

**Prefixes, features, leakage.** The leakage table covers every required field and the keep/repair/drop calls are mostly right (`final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, label fields). Process-aware feature families are named and split into static / prefix / reference-data. Deduct for internal inconsistency: §1 takes \(\min(3\text{ events}, 72\mathrm{h})\); §4 uses 72h only when the case is short. Building the migration map from `decision_reason` is a leakage-adjacent idea. The noon cutoff for `documents_total` is a heuristic, not an as-of rule. The resource-experience “repair” formula is just \(t_{\mathrm{event}}-t_{\mathrm{hire}}\) written the long way around.

**Validation.** The argument against a random split is correct (policy, migration, maturity). The proposed split, however, trains only pre-1 Jul 2025 and tests Oct–Dec 2025, so post-policy and post-migration performance is almost guaranteed to look like shift, and the mature subset of that test window is heavily selected for fast activation. The writeup notes the bias and does not replace it with a maturity-aware rolling origin (start dates that can actually finish 90 days plus feed delay before 15 Feb 2026). Metrics are fine for a complete-case binary target and thin for the censoring story the rest of the memo emphasizes.

**Code skeleton.** This is the largest mechanical failure. The prompt required a demonstration of `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`; the skeleton never calls it. The block is also well above the 80–160 line band. More importantly it is not executable as written:

- `case_agg["default_90d"]` is used but never created.
- The feature merge asks `case_agg` for `requested_amount`, `case:region`, `case:channel`, and `risk_score`, none of which were aggregated.
- `eval_train` is the DataFrame *index*, then used as `case:concept:name` values.
- `open_flag` tests `"Account Activated" not in s.name` on a timestamp Series; `s.name` is the column name.
- Truncation via `loc` mixed with `get_loc` is incorrect on a non-range index.

A skeleton does not need a fitted model. It does need to show a runnable as-of pipeline. This one would die before producing cohort counts or a prefix table. Elapsed-time prefixes are also absent from the code.

**Net.** The memo knows the right failure modes (selection, delayed labels, transfers, policy break, leakage). It does not hold a single consistent study design long enough to implement it, and the required PM4Py pipeline is both incomplete and incorrect. That is a solid draft, not a near-final statistical design.
