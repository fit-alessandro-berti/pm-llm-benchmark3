**5.5**

The answer is competent process-monitoring work with a real A/B distinction, mostly right primary states, and a strong cross-case section. It is not close to flawless. Several control-flow mistakes, a next-event vs. suffix confusion, and a broken required format keep it well below a high score.

**Format.** The required artifact is one valid JSON object. Case C6-A emits `unknowns": [` (missing the opening quote), so the object does not parse. That is a hard miss on an explicit constraint.

**Next-event prediction is repeatedly treated as suffix prediction.** From S3, the event that should dominate the next-log distribution is `Evidence Review Completed`. `Technical Investigation Completed`, another `Customer Evidence Requested`, `Close — No Safety Issue`, and `Supplier Analysis Completed` are typically *subsequent* events after review (or after an investigation that has not been shown to have started). Ranking those as “next logged” is only justified if review already finished off-log or a parallel obligation is already running. The write-up sometimes says that, then still assigns those skip-ahead events 0.15–0.40 as if they were ordinary next-event mass. That is a core PPM error.

**`process_enabled` is used incoherently.** The field should mark whether the process currently enables the action, not whether a completion will appear soon. C6-A sets TI `process_enabled: false` while claiming the task is “likely already in progress.” C6-C sets `Disposition Recorded` to `true` while the primary state still has Regulatory Assessment open, which violates rule 4. C6-C/D put `Authority Notification` in the top three with `process_enabled: false` — i.e., they predict as next an event they admit is not enabled. Enablement and observation are exactly the distinction the prompt asked for, and it is blurred in every case.

**C6-B is the worst case-level error.** After `Customer Evidence Requested` → `Evidence Received`, the case is not in S1. Fast Close is the S1 exit (`Initial Triage → Fast Close`, 286), not a legal alternative state once an evidence cycle has started. Merging `Close — No Safety Issue / Fast Close` smuggles two different terminals into one slot and treats a non-enabled outcome as enabled. Attribute eligibility (low, no injury, single device) is not the same as control-flow enablement. Contradicting evidence is listed as “None,” which ignores that the case already left the fast-close path and that R-T4 “usually handles potentially reportable cases.”

**C6-C/D Authority Notification is mis-sequenced.** Rule 6 and the DFG both put notification after a reportable assessment, and historically after `Disposition Recorded` (44), in a 16:00 batch. It cannot be the next log event unless RA *and* disposition are already done and hidden. That is not the primary state they defend. A better third event is `Close — No Safety Issue`, `Corrective Action Opened`, or (for D) a late evidence completion.

**C6-D underuses the observation model.** This is the migration-affected case with a source-system attachment and a worklist open at 12:40. The prompt’s own example is that a missing event is not proof of a missing task. A delayed `Evidence Received` / `Evidence Review Completed` (or a delayed RA completion) is a first-class *next logged* candidate. It is relegated to caveats, while `Authority Notification` is ranked. Evidence is also missing from `open_or_suspected_obligations`. `contradicting_evidence` is used as a data-quality note, not as evidence against the predictions.

**C6-E is mis-calibrated on obligations and timing.** Completeness 0.72 after a second package is a strong reason to rank another `Customer Evidence Requested` in the top three; it is omitted. `Supplier Analysis Completed` at 0.20–0.40 is too high for a 6–36 h activity when the second package arrived at 13:20. SA is correctly treated as mandatory given six same-lot complaints; treating its *completion* as the likely next log line is not. Possible reportability from lot clustering is ignored (no RA obligation).

**C6-A is the strongest case and still thin.** S3 vs S4 is reasonable. SA belongs in open obligations (same lot, two devices, three similar complaints) and they put it there, then never let it affect ranking or the “disposition cannot proceed” constraint. Review is described as “actively under review” even though four items are waiting and only ~1h50m have passed. Contradicting evidence is essentially empty.

**Evidence discipline.** DFG counts are almost unused as observational (not normative) signal: 173 review→TI vs 101 review→another evidence request vs 68 review→close would have sharpened A vs B. Queue facts, the 16:00 batch, and R-T4 are cited unevenly. Intervals are honestly wide, which is good; midpoints for E overshoot a coherent top-three, and several rank-1 bands (0.50–0.70) are too low if the author really believes the case is still in S3.

**Cross-case discussion is the best part.** A vs B is explained by severity, injury, objects, and compliance rather than prefix cloning. DFG limits (concurrency, evidence loops, context) are correctly stated. That section is why this is not a 4.x answer. It does not offset the S1/Fast Close error, AN-as-next, broken enablement flags, skip-ahead predictions, or invalid JSON.

Not generic, not DFG-copying, and not clueless — but too many process-semantic mistakes for anything near the top of the scale.
