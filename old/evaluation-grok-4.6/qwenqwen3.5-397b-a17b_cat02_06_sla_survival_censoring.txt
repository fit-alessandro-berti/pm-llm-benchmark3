4.3

The write-up has the right skeleton and vocabulary (origin at `Submit`, 72h horizon, competing risks, immortal time, landmarking), but it is not close to the quality bar. Several required distinctions are wrong or missing, and the code does not implement the estimands it claims to encode.

**Estimands.** CIF of resolution by 72h is a valid primary target. Estimand 2 is not. \(S(72)=P(T_{\text{terminal}}>72 \cup \text{Terminal}\neq\text{Resolve})\) is not a well-defined probability statement, and it is not a survival function. What is written is closer to \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{resolve}}(72)\), which mixes still-open cases with withdrawal, rejection, and transfer. The prompt asked for at least two *distinct* operational targets (e.g. not resolved by 72 vs still no terminal disposition vs cause-specific risk). Those are collapsed here. Case contributions are also incomplete: S03, S09, S06, and S08 are not walked through *under each* estimand. Calling S03 a “breach for CIF” confuses a binary SLA indicator with a cumulative incidence. Under \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\text{resolve}}(72)\), a resolve at 76h is simply not an event by the horizon; it increments the CIF only after 72.

**Already-observed breach vs censoring.** This is the most important omitted distinction. Open cases already past 72h (S09, S11, S25, S26) and late resolutions (S03, S13, S20, S28) are *observed* failures of “resolved by 72.” Open cases still below 72h (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) are the only true right-censoring for that horizon. Section 3 never makes this contrast, despite the prompt requiring it. S09/S11 are described as “likely” breaches; they are already past the horizon unresolved.

**Encoding.** The status table is usable, and not treating withdraw/reject as ordinary censoring is correct. Transfer Scenario C is internally contradictory (event=1 if \(t<72\), else breach or competing; then “failure regardless of time”). Coding `open` as competing-risk code 4 mixes censoring into the cause variable. Treating a 26h transfer (S21) as an SLA breach is not a coherent 72h-SLA assumption.

**Methods.** Fine–Gray / subdistribution hazards and restricted mean time are required comparisons and are absent. AJ as primary for a CIF estimand is reasonable; KM’s competing-risk bias is stated correctly. With \(n=32\), there is almost no discussion of variance, sparse causes, or how unstable AJ/KM would be by cohort×priority.

**Process features.** Leakage and immortal time for `Escalate`/`Rework` are the strongest part of the answer. The state diagram is not faithful to the extract: it omits the Cohort B signature path `Submit→AutoCheck`, and also `Screen→Escalate`, `Screen→Reject`/`Withdraw`, and `AutoCheck→Resolve`. `last_event_gap_h` is measured at cutoff, so using it as a from-\(t=0\) covariate is not a clean time-varying feature; that is not flagged.

**Sensitivity / operations.** Three themes are named, but withdrawal/rejection encoding is never varied, and “which qualitative conclusions could reverse” is not answered with a definite claim (e.g. whether B looks better than A). Fairness checks are generic and unused against the actual P1 pattern (several transfers and open-past-72 cases).

**Code (fails several hard requirements).** All 32 rows are embedded and opens are not dropped from the frame. That is the main compliance. Failures:
- Two estimands are not encoded; a single homemade “breach rate” is used.
- No risk-set, KM, or Aalen–Johansen table is built. No survival library is named and no real fallback estimator is implemented.
- `encode_status(..., 'breach')` is identical to `'competing'`.
- Open cases with age \(<72\) sit in the denominator as non-breaches — i.e. incomplete follow-up is treated as success. That is the opposite of a survival analysis.
- No priority-stratified analysis.
- The PM4Py log keeps only `Submit` and the last activity, so `get_variants` cannot recover the prefixes. The pandas fallback is the only part that actually uses the control-flow data.

**Limitations** are the second-best section and correctly prioritize missing timestamps, post-transfer outcomes, cutoff/start dates, assignment, and compliance.

Net: a structured, partly competent plan with multiple definitional errors, a missing required contrast (open \(<72\) vs \(\ge72\)), incomplete method coverage, and code that does not compute the stated estimands. Not a low-effort answer, but far from nearly flawless.
