That is the core pathology of Model S, and it is stated sharply.

The B1–B8 matrix is accurate if one reasons from the tree strings. C really does reject B2 and B5; S really does accept B3 and B4. The third model’s backbone — mandatory concurrent checks, `*(tau, correction)` as zero-or-more correction cycles, recheck XOR of `{CC, TR, both}`, optional SR∥ER, XOR approval — is the right shape. `*(tau, …)` for zero-or-more is the process-tree detail many answers get backwards. ## Factual errors (these are the main deductions)

**Model C’s Supplier Review count is wrong in more than one place.** C has SR in branches 3, 4 and 6 only — three labelled leaves, not four. The breakdown “48 labelled leaves … Supplier Review ×4 …” sums to 49, not 48. §8 then says SR is “present in 4 branches, absent in 4”; it is present in 3, absent in 5. This is careless in a review that attacks the undocumented duplicate-label figure of 33 for being unreproducible. If you are going to count C’s labels in public, the arithmetic has to close.

**§2 and §4 contradict each other on arc-degree simplicity.** §2 claims a standard conversion yields S ≈ 0.64 and C ≈ 0.89, so the reported 0.918 / 0.281 is backwards and the whole pipeline is untrustworthy. §4 then explains C’s *reported* 0.281 as the natural result of an 8-way XOR at the initial place. Those cannot both be the account of the same metric. One high-degree place among many degree-2 sequence nodes would *not* typically pull arc-degree simplicity down to 0.281, so §2’s directional claim is more plausible than §4’s causal story — but stating 0.64 / 0.89 as conversion results, then later treating 0.281 as explained by the same conversion, is a logical flaw. Those specific counter-numbers are also presented with a precision the rest of the review correctly refuses to grant other people’s figures.

**Baseline mixing.** §4 says C contains 7 of the 36 P2/P4-legal correction-free orderings. That 36 allows SR anywhere in the check block (S’s language, filtered). C and the third model only allow SR after both checks, a 20-trace universe. Later the answer uses 20 and “13 false negatives” correctly. The 7/36 sentence is the wrong denominator.

## Semantic and design gaps in the third model

The tree forces **both first-pass checks to finish before any Correct Dossier**. That is not in P6 and is not listed with D-a/D-b/D-c. Traces such as `RD, CC, CD, CC, TR, MA, RO` are policy-ambiguous, not obviously forbidden, and the third model rejects them without saying so.

D-a/D-b (SR after checks; no correction after SR/ER) are documented, which is better than C’s accidental correlations — but the **checker does not encode them**. Combined verdict on T6 is therefore `structural_fit=False` with policy still `conformant-unverified`. §8’s “Reject (design decision D-a)” is only the backbone. That split is actually interesting and should have been stated.

`POLICY_DECLARE` does not match the checker: `existence: {CC: 1, TR: 1}` reads as exactly-once while the loop allows repeats; `response: {CD: {CC, TR}}` looks like both rechecks while the code requires a non-empty subset. The dict is documentation theatre unless those semantics are defined.

Minor construction nits: the internal family member becomes `+(tau, X('Expedite Review', tau))` via string replace; `existence`/`response` aside, the executable tree string itself is clean. Simulation frequencies and a business-facing diagram (distinct from the enforcement tree) are almost absent after §1 promised those use cases.

## Smaller issues

TBR described as `1 - mean(1/sqrt(visits))` is close enough to be useful and the “rename it transition coverage” recommendation is right; the exact PM4Py aggregation (all transitions vs. visited only, including silent) is not nailed down. `check_soundness` unpacking and alignment cost parameter names are hedged in §9 and not in the first snippet. Saying the third model overestimates fitness “in the same way S does” is true for lost Supplier Review, not for lost mandatory checks — that overstatement undercuts the main design claim.

## What keeps it well above a middling score

Probe verdicts for S and C are right. Mandatory checks are not silently optionalized. Missing attributes become unverifiable rather than τ. The negative-suite / rejection-rule program is the correct replacement for positive-log precision. Governance (S as labelled sketch only, C retired as a model but kept as a POSITIVE suite, third in shadow mode) follows from the analysis rather than from a blended score.

The grade is not higher because a review that prosecutes unreproducible counts, log-free metric inversion, and invented correlations cannot itself miscount C’s branches, contradict itself on simplicity, and leave a first-pass-before-correction rule implicit. Under a strict reading those are not polish problems; they are the same class of defect the board is being warned about.
