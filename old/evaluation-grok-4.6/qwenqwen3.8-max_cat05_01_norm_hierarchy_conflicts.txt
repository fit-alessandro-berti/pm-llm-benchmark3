**7.3**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly process-disciplined. It uses the required headings, separates facts from assumptions, refuses a single total ranking, applies rules at the time of commitment, and produces mixed classifications rather than treating every emergency buy as compliant or every defect as fatal. Alternatives, evidence types, and a concrete anti-normalization clause are all present. Those are real strengths. They are not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

The central legal move is unstable. GPIP-4 A4 keeps all controls other than competitive tendering in force unless the CRO issues a written A7 exception. ECD-2 is silent on Finance, Compliance, SoD, and the CRO; the CRO never ratified it. The CFO email is uncontrolled and, by its own terms, is not meant to remove essential integrity controls. The analysis still lets that email substitute for GPIP-4 A2 up to EUR 150,000, and that substitution is what turns P-505 and P-507 into `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. Calling the move “contestable” does not repair it. A2 also has two conjuncts—independent budget owner **and** Finance Controller—which are collapsed into a single “operational approvals” bucket. D3 authorizes interpretation of Finance controls, not a silent waiver of the budget-owner limb.

There is an internal inconsistency on P-502. Under the stated D1 accommodation, A2 should already be treated as substituted (EUR 62,000, in-window ECD-2). The case row still lists missing Finance/budget approval as an apparent deviation “if CFO accommodation rejected,” which is hedging against the author’s own rule. The `NONCOMPLIANT` result is still right because A3 is independently fatal; the reasoning is not clean.

Evidential handling is good on the main points—supplier acknowledgments and the P-507 signed PDF are preferred to late ERP stamps, and A6 is applied so later Compliance or Plant approval does not cure a missing pre-commitment control—but ON-44 is misused. The ERP outage is 14 July 08:30–13:00. P-505 is 18 July. The case supplies its own clock-fast fact; citing ON-44 as the reason the 18:06 stamp is unreliable is a concrete evidential error, even though the conclusion (commitment before 18:00) is still supported by the 17:59:31 acknowledgment.

Two classifications are softer than the record. On P-501 the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate SHIFT-1. B4’s substitution rule therefore fails on stated facts, not on a missing authority matrix. `INDETERMINATE` is available, but the failed-delegation fact is under-weighted. On P-504, cafeteria repair is only questionably inside “maintenance and production-support,” and “unapproved” is not “new” for A3. `NONCOMPLIANT` is still defensible as emergency-form misuse, but Medium-High confidence overstates how secure the SOP hook is.

None of this makes the answer weak. It is a serious, usable assessment with visible uncertainty and usable remediation. It is not nearly flawless: one structural exception theory does too much work, one outage citation is wrong, and a few case rows are looser than the source text.
