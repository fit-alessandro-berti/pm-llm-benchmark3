**Identification and logic problems.** Calling the primary design “synthetic control” overstates what two never-treated donors and common May shocks can support; this is a tiny comparative ITS, which the failure-mode cell admits but the lead framing does not. Interference bias is incomplete: if the fraud desk worsens *controls*, DiD is biased *toward* finding a benefit, and that direction is not stated. The within-branch dose gradients are largely the same pre/post availability contrast plus a time ramp, so they are not an independent intensity design; that is underplayed. Section 3 correctly rejects naïve TWFE, then Section 9’s main model is exactly TWFE with a disclaimer—workable if labeled, but the tension is unresolved. Arrival growth is treated as the 10% routing campaign; Alpha’s jump (1023 → 1258) is much larger, so organic demand and routing are mixed.

**Quantitative and presentation nits.** Section 5 never reports the FE or intensity coefficients the code would produce, nor event-study lead/lag estimates, despite building `rel_cat`. E5 bundles rework and escalation, which the rest of the memo treats as opposite-signed. Section 1 is slightly over the 150–250 word cap and is vague on the *direction* of FTE changes. Table figures are rounded (48.15 → 48.2, −3.95h → −4.0h) as if exact. The “conditionally causal 5–10% median reduction” sits next to an unconditional bound of −8% to +2%; the distinction is defensible but easy to misread. Transportability of an Alpha/Beta ATT to Delta/Epsilon is thinner in the effect table than the rollout section needs.

**Net.** High-quality reasoning and a sound decision, with enough real gaps in identification, dose independence, and delivered quantification that a strict grade cannot go into the 9s.
