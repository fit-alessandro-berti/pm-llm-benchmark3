**7.5**

The final write-up is a strong, constraint-aware reading of an incomplete object-centric norm. It uses open-world reasoning correctly, keeps the four constrained edges out of the violation bucket, separates behavior from data quality and model incompleteness, and ranks true control breaches above rare unregulated paths. It is not nearly flawless. Several numerical and abstraction points are missed or mislabeled, and those are exactly the distinctions this task is testing.

**What is done well.** Section 1 states the right deontic inventory and the right closed-world boundary: forbidden relations, and mandatory relations only inside stated scope. Typical absence and unlisted edges are not treated as noncompliance. The table includes the unusual and benign observations, cites norm IDs and counts, and gives dual classes where the evidence is ambiguous (N1, N4, E6, E8, T6, E10). N3 and T6 are correctly prioritized; Manual Compliance Review, customer links, codeath, and reopen/close are not escalated because they are rare. Sections 6–8 are operationally usable: six real norm gaps, containment vs. repair vs. revision, and a clean split among robust conclusions, incompleteness, suspected artifacts, and what a graph summary cannot decide.

**Abstraction error on N3.** The required discussion of “eventually expected” vs. directly-follows is applied mainly to N1, where DF over-calls missing validation. N3 is not a DF rule. It forbids dispatch *after* cancellation. Observing `Cancel Order -> Dispatch Package` on two orders is therefore a strong positive signal, but DF can *under-detect* the same prohibition whenever intermediate events sit between cancel and dispatch. That false-negative risk is not treated with the same care as N1’s false-positive risk, even though it is the more serious control failure mode.

**Unexamined count inconsistency.** 98 of 100 orders show `Create -> Validate` and 3 show `Create -> Manual Compliance Review`. If those DF relations are exclusive immediate successors, 98 + 3 exceeds 100. The extra context that an unrecorded review may sit between create and validate is mentioned, but never reconciled with the three review cases. That is a missed chance to show that the DF abstraction is not just “too strict,” but internally in tension with the rest of the summary.

**Table calibration nits.** Rank 18 labels 92 Invoice–Payment interactions as “typical-pattern absence”; that is a presence, not an absence (the absences are already ranks 9–10). Ranks 16–17 largely restates the E2/E6 gaps already ranked 5–6. E10 is High severity with “high confidence” it is a join artifact: process-violation severity should fall if the DQ reading is that confident; data-integrity severity can remain high, but the table does not separate those. N1 remaining Medium, despite explicit evidence that DF is the wrong encoding, is slightly hot.

**Smaller issues.** The code sketch calls the required APIs, preserves raw diagnostics, and keeps deontic metadata outside the graphs, but `classify_finding` does not implement the DQ / abstraction / insufficient-evidence layer argued in prose. Fully conforming mandatory edges are omitted, which is defensible, yet the “every unusual observation” bar is then met only if one already agrees those rows are not unusual. Prose-plus-table length is at or over the 2,600-word cap.

The normative skeleton is right. The grade is held down because the answer still flattens the DF-vs-eventual distinction on the forbidden rule, does not interrogate its own counts, and mis-tags a few typical/mandatory rows.
