The write-up is well-formatted and hits all seven required sections, but the process reasoning is repeatedly sloppy, sometimes internally contradictory, and often unfaithful to the log. Structure is carrying a weak analysis.

**Scenarios are the right *categories* with the wrong mechanics.** Mix shifts are bolted onto disruptions without a causal story. An assay outage does not raise the Routine share. A courier strike does not raise Repeat-after-failure; the log ties repeats to night shifts and M2, not intake. “Specialist Absence” is defined as a medical-validator outage, then analyzed as Hold-for-specialist-review—these are different pools (med val 58/93% vs specialist 43/87%, second-Friday absence). Mix deltas (“Urgent +20%”) are ambiguous (pp vs relative) and ignore that peak already *is* the mix-shift evidence (Urgent 18→29%, Complex 13→20%).

**Bottleneck-migration contains process-flow errors, not just gaps.**  
- Machine outage → “technical validation overloaded because M1 output spikes” is backwards: losing M2 *cuts* assay throughput, so tech val is starved, not flooded.  
- Specialist delay → “assay repeats rise” is topologically false. Hold sits *after* assay and tech val; it builds finished WIP, it does not send tubes back to M1/M2.  
- Queue times, performance-edge medians, and utilizations are used interchangeably (14h “batch wait queue” is the prep→batch edge; 24h “med val queue” is the tech→med edge).  
- Peak vs normal *counts* are compared as if the windows were equal (8 weeks vs 3). Peak weekly load is ~3300 vs ~2300; recollection, batch-wait, repeat, and hold *rates* all worsen sharply (batch-wait ~4.4→12.7%, repeats ~6.7→14.8%, hold ~1.8→7.5%, tech→med ~24.5→42.3%). Almost none of that is used.  
- The most important variant is mishandled: Urgent batch-bypass *falls* 74→58% in peak, so Urgent work is *trapped in batches*, not only fragmenting machines.

**The “robust” package violates the problem’s own conflict hints.**  
- **I4 always-on** pulls from technical validators already at **101%** / 16h peak queue. The brief literally warns this starves tech val. Claiming accession util falls 62→40% uses the *normal* rate and invents the 40%.  
- **I2 always-on** doubles batches while peak mix shifts to Urgent/Complex and batch→assay is already 22h. Larger batches lengthen fill time; asserting 6h→3h is the wrong direction. Robustness is then excused by pairing with I1, which is *not* always-on.  
- **I6** is the best pick (recollection→receive 42/60h, peak cancel-after-recollect rises), but “760→300 cases” is an invented, period-mismatched count, and a new 2h gate on an 18h Urgent SLA is not discussed as a delay.

- “Next bottleneck: None” on the core bundle is a direct constraint violation.

**Scenario policies contain a show-stopping contradiction.** Under **M2 failure**, the prescribed policy is I3: send Routine to M2. That routes work onto the dead machine. The trigger (util >95% for 2h) would also fire on ordinary peak (M1/M2 already 98%), so I3 becomes an always-on peak rule. I1 during a Complex-panel surge starves the other spiking class; the brief forbids prioritizing every type at once. I3 during specialist absence accelerates inflow into the broken *downstream* station. Courier-collapse “prioritize night-shift samples” when intake stops after 16:00 prioritizes work that is not arriving.

I1+I5 is scored as **+20% Complex-panel speed** even though I1 is defined to starve Complex-panel. Core package “Neutral” on Urgent ignores I6 latency. Urgent p90 warning >18h vs normal p90 already **19h**. Med-val queue (22h peak), case-mix share, recollection rate, tech-val util, and the known Friday specialist gap are missing. That is not a leading-indicator design grounded in this process.

Under a strict rubric this is a mid/low answer with a polished wrapper, not a robust redesign.
