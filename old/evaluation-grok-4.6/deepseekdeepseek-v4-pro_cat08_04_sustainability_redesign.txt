**6.4**

The final write-up is complete, on-format, and mostly process-grounded, but it is not close to flawless. It is a competent multi-criteria memo with several invented magnitudes, one clear inconsistency with the path table, a duplicated intervention, and a rebound section that misses a required category.

**What works.** Sections 1–7 are present and usable. The boundary is explicit (order-to-confirm, with rework, packaging, transport, and batch obsolescence inside; upstream extraction and most returns outside). Indicators map to the given attributes. Interventions are tied to real variants (stock–air, CNC_A batch–heat–ground, CNC_B–rework–ground, dedicated-van critical). The package correctly refuses a blanket ban on air or vans, keeps CNC_B as a relief valve, and treats slack and downtime justification as the switch. That satisfies the main service/resilience constraint.

**Unsupported or wrong quantities.** The prompt forbids precise lifecycle numbers that are not in the evidence. The answer invents several: packaging rework cut “by at least 60%”; shared courier “30–50%” vs dedicated van; I1 segment transport “40–60%”; I3 energy “5–10%.” The last one contradicts the path table: the CNC_B–rework path is 32 kWh and 1.8 kg scrap versus 16 kWh and 0.70 kg on the CNC_A–heat–ground path. Calling the energy effect a small single-digit percent is a factual miss, not a rounding choice. I8 then overclaims “no service deterioration” for true downtime while moving volume off the 14 h / 3% late van path; the only lower-emission critical comparator in the log (stock/ground exception) is 23 h / 9% late.

**Weaker grounding and structure.** I1 and I4 are the same SP1/air-to-ground lever. Partial shipment (7% share, 24% late, second shipment) and cancel-due-to-delay (~530 cases) are barely used, though both waste transport, packaging, and already-sunk production energy. Heat-treatment batching (lowest energy above 10, 1.7× late risk) is folded into a generic wait cap instead of treated as its own trade-off. SP1’s 22% is restated as “used air despite sufficient lead time,” which is a reasonable gloss but not what the finding literally says. The effects table is hard to read: many cells are blank or arrow-stripped, so required qualitative directions are often missing. Resilience is the weakest indicator (two `route_mode`s “appearing” in the log is not R1).

**Rebound section is incomplete relative to the brief.** Six risks are given, and batching, mode substitution, packaging mass, obsolescence, and customer gaming are real. Rework is not given its own rebound, despite the instruction to include it: e.g., tighter ground/batch SLAs → rushed inspection → more missed defects → rework loops and scrap. That is the hidden waste the case is built around.

**Recommendation and governance.** The five-pillar package is coherent and better than a list of green ideas. Validation covers SP1/SP2/Q1, segment service, and exception review. It still under-specifies obsolescence, cancellation, empty-van/backhaul, and R1 alternative-route availability as post-go-live checks.

Net: strong structure and the right balance thesis, pulled down by invented percentages, a wrong I3 energy reading, duplicate shipping interventions, a damaged effects table, and an incomplete rebound set. That is clearly above a thin or generic answer, and clearly below a near-flawless one.
