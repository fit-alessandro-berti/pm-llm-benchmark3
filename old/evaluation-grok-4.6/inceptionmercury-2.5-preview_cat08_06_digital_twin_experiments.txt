**3.5**

The submission has the required eight headings and a few sound instincts (treat objections as unobserved, keep applicant delay off the municipal clock, mention inspection bundling). Under a strict reading it is still a weak, incomplete, and technically inaccurate design. It would not be a credible digital-twin specification.

**1. Model scope is too thin to support the stated interventions.**  
The boundary is a one-line municipal-vs-construction split. Level of detail is never chosen (activity-level DES, hybrid DES/ABS, or case-level). State is only “status / busy-idle / queue length / clock.” That cannot represent aging (D6), appointment slots (D7), environmental triage attributes (D8), a latent objection process (D9), or a legal pre-check that runs in parallel (D5). Decision drafting is not specified as an AND-join that must wait for planning, fire, environment, public notice, and inspection when those apply. Neighborhood objections that couple related cases—called out in the process context—are omitted; only architect/district inspection bundles appear. Resource pools, FTE, part-time fire calendar, 40% environmental reservation, Monday portal clerk, and “two planners only handle Simple renovation” are not encoded as state. Rework is wrongly described as “decision rejections return by variant,” which does not match rework after planning, inspection, legal check, or appeal.

**2. Parameter estimation is vague and partly wrong.**  
“Variants of F” is meaningless. Routing from variant shares (e.g. “18% fire + notice”) confuses joint variant frequency with independent branch probabilities and ignores `requires_fire` / `requires_environment` / case type. Arrivals are a time-dependent Poisson with the given month/weekday multipliers, but the log’s dominant extra non-stationarity—top-20 architects submitting 31% of cases in weekly bursts—is not estimated. Missing start timestamps are not handled with a real method (interval-censored service, sojourn vs idle split against calendars, multiple imputation). The proposed wait formula `(completion − median service − previous completion)` folds calendar idle, queues, and service into one residual and then treats the median as known truth. Calendars are “mapped from HR” with no check against log idle patterns, even though the brief says calendars were reconstructed from HR, not from the log. PM4Py is named but not used as a concrete estimation pipeline (no DFG/performance DFG, no case-arrival statistics, no attribute-aware filters).

**3. Assumptions mix reasonable points with contradictions.**  
Objections as a mixture on public-notice duration, not as observed events, is correct. Applicant delay as a separate heavy-tailed process is correct. Independent Poisson arrivals contradict architect bursts. “Bundling only for inspection; other reviews independent” contradicts stated neighborhood coupling. Binary skills contradict specialist planner/fire/legal pools. Inferring waits from a constant median service is an assumption that silently destroys queueing dynamics. Non-stationarity is reduced to two multipliers; holiday/council interruption of legal and public notice is not assumed as a calendar process.

**4. Experiment matrix fails the brief.**  
D5–D9 are absent. D1–D4/D10 are marked with empty cells and dashes, so factor levels are unreadable. There are no levels for checker effectiveness, skip-inspection eligibility, batch window, temp-inspector months, legal pre-check start point, aging threshold, appointment capacity, triage policy, or early-warning lead time. D10 is not defined as an interaction design. Three “stress” rows exist, but they are slogans (inspectors −50%, arrivals ×2, “D2 error rate high”), not a factorial design. Expected trade-offs are one-liners and ignore applicant effort, inspection-risk, travel-vs-wait, and fairness.

**5. Warm-up and uncertainty mishandle non-stationarity and tails.**  
A 3-month warm-up toward “steady state” is invalid for seasonal 1.7× demand; the process is cyclostationary. An 18-month run after that warm-up does not cover integer seasonal cycles and censors end-of-horizon WIP. Thirty replications and 95% CIs are named without quantile/bootstrap intervals for p90/p99, common random numbers across policies, or separation of input vs stochastic uncertainty. Sensitivity is only “p5/p95 service times.” Matching simulated p90 service times to the same empirical p90 used for fitting is circular, not uncertainty treatment.

**6. Validation is generic and partly circular.**  
“Replay the 18-month log” confuses trace replay, historical-arrival replay, and generative simulation. Planner utilization “70–85%” and bottlenecks “legal and inspection scheduling” are invented, not taken from the log. Hold-out by district is mentioned; temporal hold-out (needed for non-stationary demand) is not. No plan to validate rework rates, AND-join waits, Monday spikes, holiday/council effects, or cross-case correlation without reusing the fitted statistics.

**7. Decision criteria are mis-specified and incomplete.**  
The brief required rework, backlog, SLA by case type, and fairness across districts *and* architects. Rework is missing; architects are missing. Median duration is called primary, then the first target is a 50% tail cut for Commercial cases—an arbitrary, ungrounded stretch goal. Complex-variance p90 “< 260d” is the *current* p90, so it is not an improvement screen. “90% SLA” is invented; no statutory SLA was given. No cost, risk-of-skipping-inspection, or applicant-effort metric appears, so D1/D2/D4 cannot be decided.

**8. Python skeleton violates the stated PM4Py conventions and would not run.**  
`pm4py.format_dataframe` is called with a fictional `parameters={pm4py.variants.FormatParameters...}` object; the conventional API is keyword `case_id` / `activity_key` / `timestamp_key`. `ProcessTreeRoot`, `pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg.discover`, `pm4py.algo.filtering.log.simple.filter_by_columns`, and `pm4py.get_performance` are not the agreed interfaces. The tree string **is** PM4Py `parse_process_tree` notation (`->`, `+`, `X`, `*`, `tau`); claiming it is “not native” and must become JSON is false, and the string is then replaced by `...`, which cannot parse. The excerpt DataFrame is internally inconsistent (22 case-id rows vs 20 activity/timestamp rows; P001/P002 event counts do not match the excerpt). There are no start/complete columns, so service vs waiting cannot be computed; that computation is simply omitted. `len(log)/(18*30.44)` treats a two-case excerpt as an 18-month log. Variants/DFG/performance DFG/case-arrival average are not computed with the conventional calls (`get_variants`, `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg`, `get_case_arrival_average`). The “simulator” is one empty `run` with `pass`; there are no placeholder modules for arrival generation, routing, resources/calendars, or experiment execution as required.

**Constraint breaches.** Independent-case thinking is only lightly patched by inspection bundles; calendars are named but not operationalized; service and wait are collapsed via a median hack; PM4Py usage is inconsistent with the given conventions. Missing objections are at least not treated as observed—that is the main constraint the text respects.

The piece reads like a section-shaped outline with invented APIs, missing interventions, and several textbook simulation errors. It is far from a near-flawless design.
