**4.2**

The answer is structurally complete and rhetorically competent, but it fails the actual decision problem. A policy that violates its own hard rules, misreads the given evidence, and produces at least one near-certain SLA breach cannot be scored as a solid solution.

**Core assignment failures.** C001 is the decisive error. The log says the case needs a specialist, SLA remaining is 3h, complexity is 0.88, and the brief states that keeping a weak-skilled owner adds a median 3.3h of service time. Assigning skill-1 Celine as executor therefore predicts an SLA miss on the most sensitive Security case. Constraint 5 is a *preference* for continuity, not a mandate to keep a trainee when supervision exists; the answer hardens it into a must-keep rule and then follows that mistake. Giving Celine C001 *and* C007, plus reviews on C009 and C016, is not a feasible day.

Farid receiving both C002 (urgent Approval, 2h) and C011 (urgent VIP Negotiation, 4h) is an unexamined collision. One of those SLAs is likely to break. Adding C013 and a C010 review on top converts the only Approval specialist into a bottleneck the policy claims to avoid.

**The table contradicts the algorithm.** Step 2 excludes high-overtime resources unless priority is urgent/high. C003, C006, C010, and C014 are normal/low and still go to David, Iris, and Bruno. David already has 14 open cases, 3h left, and overtime risk high; giving him two non-urgent cases (C003, C014) violates problem constraint 4, section 1 rule 5, and section 2 eligibility at once. Continuity does not license that. Safer Diagnosis/Rework capacity exists (Celine, Hugo, Jonas).

**Training claims are factually wrong.** Farid’s priority is Diagnosis, not Approval or Eligibility, so C010 and C013 are not documented training. Eva’s priority is Security, so C016 is not training. Eva has Security skill 0; she cannot execute C015 even under supervision (rule 6 allows only skill 1–2 with Gita/Hugo). Shadowing is not an assignment. The table marks five training cases; the summary says four of eleven. There are nine normal/low cases, not eleven. The 20% quota is therefore both miscounted and partly fictional.

**The scoring design does not do what it claims.** `sla_score` is constant across candidates for a case, so the 0.35 weight never chooses a worker. Case order is unspecified, yet capacity and the 4-assignment cap make order material. `f1`, `g1`, `required_min`, and `sla_threshold` are undefined. Training and resilience terms are scaled into noise (0.015 and 0.005). Quality batching is a hard rule in section 1 and absent from the procedure. Section 1 allows overtime-high load only for *urgent*; section 2 allows *high* as well. Workers are told urgent work is allocated first; the pseudocode never sorts the queue.

**Comparison is asserted, not computed.** First-available and specialist-only are never applied to C001–C016. “+8% rework,” “<2% throughput,” and “lowest overall SLA risk” are invented. Continuity is called “only 1 new handover” while C013 is a new case and most rows simply keep the owner—including owners who should have been relieved. Resilience is rated “medium-high” after parking two urgents on one person and a trainee-led urgent Security review.

**Audit and PM4Py are thin.** The indicator list matches the prompt, which is the strongest part after the worker note. The code does not join the skill matrix, so `df.skill_level` does not exist. `get_service_time` is treated as a frame column; `discover_batches(..., activity_key='Quality Check')` is the wrong shape; `filter_case_performance` is never used; nothing *learns* weights from handover, working-together, role, or similarity networks. That is a sketch of imports, not a learning/audit method.

**What remains creditable.** Headings and required components are present. Several keep-owner assignments are reasonable (C002, C004, C005, C007, C008). The worker explanation is clear and non-punitive. Hard/soft constraints are at least separated, even if later ignored. Those are format and communication merits, not a working policy.

The answer looks like a finished design document and behaves like an unexecuted scoring template plus an internally inconsistent roster. Under strict grading, that is well below a passing implementation.
