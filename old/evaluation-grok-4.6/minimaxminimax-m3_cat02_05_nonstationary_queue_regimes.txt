6.5

The write-up is conceptually strong and hits the required shape: eight correct top-level headings, a real attempt at regime thinking, explicit outage/recovery isolation, Little’s Law caveats, and a mostly complete Python block. It would sit in the high 8s if the arithmetic and some claims were clean. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**What is actually good.** Estimands and threats in §1 are the right ones (median service ≠ sojourn, overnight carry-over, mix vs coding, non-independence). R1/R2 are not treated as a high-throughput steady state. Demand, mix, capacity, and backlog are separated rather than collapsed into one “busy period.” The LL matrix structure is appropriate, and the “cut FTE because monthly completed/FTE looks normal” policy in §7 is the right kind of pooled-vs-segmented warning. The code embeds the data, computes Fano/Cv, lag-1 autocorrelation, a disclosed-as-heuristic split scan, and LL only on named windows, with PM4Py correctly confined to comments.

**Numerical and internal-consistency failures (these are not rounding).** Several headline numbers are simply wrong, and they feed later claims.

- R3 weekday completion intensity is **8.18/h** in §§2 and 6; the table implies **8.38/h** (704 completions / 7 / 12). That error is reused for \(W = 326\) min (closer to **317** on their own \(L\)).
- R4 weekday \(\lambda\) is **9.45**; it is **9.58** (345 / 3 / 12).
- Pooled \(\lambda = 7.66\) is **7.50** (2519 / 28 / 12).
- Alternative-mix table has low-mix \(\mu > \lambda\) (**7.21 vs 7.13**). Over Apr 6–19 completions are **1162** and arrivals **1184**, so \(\mu \approx 6.92\), \(\lambda \approx 7.05\). That reverses the backlog sign for the low-mix bucket.
- High-mix intensities (**8.46 / 7.78**) should be about **7.95 / 7.42**.
- Stated utilisation (~0.55 R0, ~0.88 R3) does not match the formula given next to it (~0.58 and ~0.92 using median \(S\)).
- Robust summaries are wrong: median \(\lambda\) is **~8.63** open-h\(^{-1}\), not 7.96; median \(L\) is **23**, not 24.5.
- Hourly Fano/variance for Apr 15 and especially Apr 25 are off (Apr 25 Fano **~1.29**, not 1.20).
- “~14.7 cases per FTE-day” and the segmented 14.0 / 13.6 / 13.0 / 14.6 series are not reproducible from FTE-days in the table; they look like completions (or a blend) divided by a weekday-6 FTE even on weekend-inclusive windows.

§6 says throughput is **completions** when the window is stable, then R0 \(W\) is computed from **arrivals** (8.49). The Python, if run, would not reproduce the prose tables. That is a reproducibility failure on a task that required one.

**Segmentation is weaker than it claims.** The “materially different” mix split (`complex_pct` ≷ 35) is the **same cut as 20 Apr**. Every pre-20 Apr day is ≤27%; every later day is ≥39%. Alternative A is only a coarser *aggregation* of the primary break, not another partition. Alternative B is named and then dropped. Weekend vs weekday, or FTE-state, would have been a real alternative; they were not developed.

**Little’s Law over-applies on weekends.** R0 weekend is “applicable / low carry-over” and R4 weekend is “applicable / low carry-over.” Saturday–Sunday WIP after Friday stocks of ~20 (R0) and 32 (R4) is not a closed weekend system. R4 Sat WIP 22 after a 32-WIP Friday is leftover drain. Those rows should be **approximate only** or **not defensible**. Implied weekend flow times (234 / 302 min) vs weekday (144 / 220) are presented without that contamination story.

**Smaller but real issues.** IOD-proxy is Fano under another name, so the third “dispersion” number adds nothing. Lag-1 ACF is reported without stripping the 7-day weekend cycle, so +0.34 on arrivals is not a clean serial-dependence measure. R0 is treated as homogeneous even though 13–14 Apr already drift (WIP 21–23, p90 48–50, complex 22–24%). FTE productivity in §4 is a confused metric and blunts an otherwise good Simpson discussion.

**Net.** Process-mining framing, uncertainty language, and format compliance are well above a mid-range answer. Strict grading is driven by wrong intensities, a non-alternative “alternative,” LL verdicts that ignore weekend carry-over, and prose that is not consistent with its own code or formulas. That is clearly short of “nearly flawless.”
