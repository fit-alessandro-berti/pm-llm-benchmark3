**5.3**

The submission follows the required heading scheme and is recognizably engaged with authority, time, and evidence. It is not a serious compliance analysis. Material misreadings, self-contradictions, and broken formalization are frequent. Several high-confidence classifications are simply wrong.

**Method and hierarchy are asserted, then abandoned.** Section 1 ranks GPIP-4 over ECD-2 and treats integrity controls as non-waivable except by a CRO A7 instrument. Section 2 repeats that ECD-2 “probably cannot override” SoD, Compliance, or Finance. The case table then lets ECD-2 or the CFO email extinguish Finance in P-507 and P-508, and treats late Compliance in P-502 as a justified exception. That is not a dimension-specific hierarchy; it is one ranking used when convenient and discarded when a case becomes hard. A7 is listed as a high-weight instrument while the decisive organizational facts—CRO unavailable, no ratification, prior overlay practice *except* for new-supplier Compliance—are almost unused. The open question the file actually poses (whether COO+IC can count as A7) is never decided.

The hierarchy table also misdescribes GPIP-4 as something that “probably cannot override” SoD and Compliance. Those rules *are* GPIP-4. That is a category error, not a nuance.

**The rule catalogue is not usable as formalization.** Comparison operators are missing throughout (`Purchase EUR 50,000`, `stoppage 6 hours`, `amount EUR 150,000`). R1–R3 and R6–R8 are therefore ambiguous at the exact thresholds the problem turns on. R7 attributes `Record_Emergency_Form` to ECD-2 and to “critical lots”; the duty is SOP B6 and is not confined to ECD-2 scope. R9 is written as a global GPIP-style rule; it is a local SOP rule. A2’s *independent budget owner* is collapsed into “Finance_Approve,” so a distinct GPIP control disappears. A4 (emergency waives tendering only) and A6 (later approval does not cure) are not rules in the catalogue at all, then A6 is quoted in P-501 and ignored.

The pseudo-logic is not defeasible reasoning. Conjunctions and inequalities are stripped; `R5 overrides R8` is treated as a blanket Finance waiver; there is no `overrides_for` split among timing, supplier onboarding, SoD, Compliance, and Finance. The prompt asked for that split.

**Case work fails the prompt’s own constraints.**

- **P-501 as COMPLIANT / High is indefensible.** Commitment is 08:50, before ECD-2. B4’s Shift Lead substitution requires an *absent* Plant Manager *and active delegation*; the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate. B5 does not waive Finance; B2 is live at EUR 38,000. Plant and Finance occur hours after commitment. The same row cites A6 (“does not cure”) and still marks the case compliant, and counts “R8 (retrospective)” as satisfied. That violates “apply rules at the time of the act” and “do not treat post-hoc approval as automatically curative.” Severity “Low” is unserious.

- **P-502 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is internally inconsistent.** The author says A3/R3 was *not* waived, then uses late Compliance as mitigation. That is A6-as-cure under another name. The CFO email is dated 15 July 07:20; this commitment is 14 July 10:15, so D1 cannot authorize it. R7 is marked satisfied though no emergency form appears in the file. A defensible JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION would have argued C4’s “supplier-onboarding” language against A3, then confronted the note that the firm has *not* treated overlays as covering new-supplier Compliance. That argument is not made.

- **P-503 NONCOMPLIANT is the right label,** but C6 is barely examined. Three-day backup stock is exactly the Operations vs Internal Audit dispute. SoD is independently fatal, so the classification survives; the scope analysis the prompt demanded does not.

- **P-504 NONCOMPLIANT is right; the rule list is not.** EUR 24,000 is below B2’s EUR 25,000 floor, so R8 is not triggered. R1 SoD is alleged with no dual-role facts. The actual breaches are B3 (unapproved supplier), B1 (commit before PO), and absence of any B4/ECD-2 predicate.

- **P-505 INDETERMINATE fights the evidence rule.** The file says not to treat a late ERP stamp as conclusive against stronger contemporaneous evidence. Supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is that evidence; a nine-minute-fast ERP clock would put 18:06 at 17:57. Remaining live issues are Finance after expiry and whether D1 still covers 18 July. Those are legal questions, not a missing clock. R3 is marked satisfied even though EUR 88,000 / approved supplier does not trigger A3. R7 is marked broken though the form is not in the record.

- **P-506 NONCOMPLIANT is right; the facts are not.** Finance_Approve is 06:05, commitment 06:20. The table says Finance was after commitment. Plant after the fact and the expired directive are enough for noncompliance; inventing a Finance-timing breach is sloppy.

- **P-507 JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION overclaims.** ECD-2 does not mention Finance. The only colorable hook is D1 as a CFO *interpretation* of Finance controls. Section 5 then calls the contrary reading “High” credibility and rejects it anyway. That should have produced INDETERMINATE or NONCOMPLIANT, or at least low confidence. The 4-hour recording issue (verbal 08:55, PDF 12:40, ERP 14:30) is dumped into “logging defect” without applying C3.

- **P-508 as COMPLIANT / High / Low severity is the worst error.** D1 makes joint IC authorization sufficient *up to* EUR 150,000. D2 requires Legal *as well* above EUR 150,000—not Legal *instead of* Finance. EUR 155,000 therefore still needs A2 Finance/budget-owner approval before commitment. None exists. Treating Legal as a Finance substitute inverts the email. High confidence on that reading is disqualifying for a process analyst.

**Alternatives are incomplete and one-sided.** Four cases are discussed, as required, but the classification that most needs an alternative—P-508—is absent. P-501’s undelegation problem is absent. P-505’s alternative is rejected on burden-of-proof grounds that contradict the outage instruction. P-507’s stronger reading is acknowledged and then waved off.

**Evidence and remediation mix categories.** P-501’s afternoon Plant/Finance approvals are “late evidence of a timely act.” They are not. They are later acts. P-507 Finance is a policy breach in §6 and a justified exception in §4. “Recover costs from SHIFT-2” is a penalty, not a control remedy. The useful distinctions (breach vs logging vs unprovable compliance) are named and then misapplied.

**The policy rewrite is the strongest section** and still sloppy: “time window ( 72 hours)” is incomplete; verbal extension is unaddressed; “operational approvals” remains the same weasel phrase that caused P-508. The anti-normalization review is a real safeguard.

**Bottom line.** Format compliance and a few correct noncompliance calls (P-503, P-504, P-506) keep this above a failing stub. Hypercritically, two high-confidence wrong results, a hierarchy the cases do not obey, threshold/date/timing fact errors, and a non-functional rule calculus are enough. This is a structured draft, not a defensible assessment.
