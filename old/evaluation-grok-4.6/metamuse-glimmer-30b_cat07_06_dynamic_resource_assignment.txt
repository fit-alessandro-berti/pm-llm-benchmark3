**5.4**

The write-up has the right skeleton and is complete, but it is not close to a rigorous policy design. Several assignments and API claims are wrong, and the “explainable” scorer is mostly unspecified weights.

**Format and coverage.** Sections 1–7 match the required headings, the queue table covers C001–C016 with the requested columns, and the comparison axes are the ones asked for. That is the main reason the score is not lower. Completeness is not correctness.

**Hard-constraint failure on Security (most serious).** Constraint 6 allows skill 3, or skill **1–2 under Gita/Hugo**. Skill 0 is “not allowed.” C015 is given to **R5_Eva (Security = 0)** with Hugo supervising. The table even says “skill 0 performer.” That is a documented compliance breach, not an edge-case judgment. The notes invite Eva/Jonas as training candidates; a careful policy would treat that as a conflict with the skill matrix and either keep Hugo or write an explicit exception. This answer silently rewrites the rule.

The algorithm does the same: `skill < 3` is allowed if supervised, which admits skill 0. Section 1 states the real rule; Section 2 and the C015 row contradict it. That inconsistency is disqualifying for an auditable policy.

**C001 SLA vs handover is mishandled.** Historical evidence says owner change on high-complexity work adds a **median 2.7h wait**. C001 has **3h** left, complexity 0.88, handover **high**, and “needs specialist.” Moving it to Hugo may be right on skill, but the write-up never faces the obvious implication: the handover itself can miss the SLA. Celine is Security 1, so **supervised continuity is legal** and is the option that preserves the 3h clock. It is not analyzed.

**Eva is overloaded.** She is given C005 (keep), C010 (new), C015 (new, illegal), and C016 (keep): four cases, **5.0h** left, including a high-complexity diagnosis with 7h SLA. The batch cap is treated as a sufficient fairness check. Hours remaining vs work content is not. Farid’s two urgent cases are at least owned and skilled; Eva’s bundle is not.

**Constraint 4 and C003.** David is overtime **high**, 14 open cases, **3.0h** left. C003 is **normal**, 18h, and Celine is Diagnosis 3 with spare capacity. Keeping David is argued as continuity, but reassignment count is 2 (not yet the >2 regime) and handover is only medium. For a fairness/resilience constraint written as “avoid unless urgent,” this is a weak, under-argued exception. Hours remaining also make completion today implausible; that is ignored.

**Training target is fragile and the hints are missed.** After discarding illegal C015, only C009 is a real training assignment (~1/9, below 20%). C010 is labeled in the snapshot as “potential trainee approval under supervision” and is given to Eva (already competent, already loaded). C012 is “suitable for trainee” and stays with specialist Jonas. Constraint 7 (skill 1 may prepare, not finalize) is never used as a supervised-prep pattern. The 22% claim depends on the invalid Security assignment.

**Baselines are not actually run.** First-available and specialist-only are fully specified on this snapshot. The comparison is generic prose (“best SLA protection,” “highest immediate starts”) with no baseline assignment table. That is a core deliverable, not a nicety. Claims about SLA and fairness cannot be checked against the same queue.

**“Explainable” policy is not explainable.** Section 2 lists w1–w8 with no values, no normalization, and no worked example. SLA urgency, service-time estimates, and the resilience “bonus” are names, not procedures. Workers cannot reconstruct why Eva beat alternatives. Batching (constraint 8) is omitted from the algorithm and miscategorized as a preference; it is a restriction on *when* batching is allowed.

**PM4Py sketch has concrete errors.** `format_dataframe` is applied to an XES log and given a non-existent `org_resource_key`. `filter_case_performance` is used as an SLA filter; it filters case duration. `filter_event_attribute_values` is not a lambda API. `discover_batches` is in the prompt’s relevant list and is unused despite a batching rule. Role/similarity/SNA outputs are never wired into eligibility or scoring. “Learn the weights” is a sentence, not a method.

**What is actually good.** Several rows are sound: C002/C011 Farid, C005 Eva, C007 Celine, C008 Gita, C004 Amal, C016 continuity with skill 2, C006 off Iris (overtime high) onto Jonas with legal batching conditions. Section 5 is appropriately non-punitive. Section 6 names the right audit families. The piece does not pretend there is a single optimum.

**Net.** Right genre, complete template, multiple legally or operationally wrong assignments, an algorithm that contradicts its own hard rules, no instantiated baselines, and a broken PM4Py audit sketch. Under a strict reading that is mid-pack, not strong.
