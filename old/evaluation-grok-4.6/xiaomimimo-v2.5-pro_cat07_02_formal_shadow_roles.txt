**6.4**

The write-up hits the required six headings, uses complexity and substitution evidence rather than raw activity counts, and treats shadow work as both useful and risky. That is the floor for a passing answer, not the ceiling. Under a strict reading it is still short of a clean solution.

**Role structure is too tidy where the data is not.** Chen and Dina are the scenario’s main anti-clustering example (0.94 activity similarity, 0.71 vs 0.48 median complexity, 42 vs 9 critical cases, 24 vs 6 security reviews). Putting them in one “Technical Engineering” row, then waving at the complexity gap in a caveat column, is exactly the naïve merge the prompt warns against. Amira and Ben are flattened the same way (Amira’s heavier medium/high load and 18 pricing events vs Ben’s 5). Giorgio never gets a table row, so the one resource the prompt marks as hybrid is structurally an afterthought. Escalation level, product line, and customer segment are almost unused.

**Internal contradiction.** Section 2 states Dina “is a junior technical analyst.” Section 4 correctly treats that as unresolved. You cannot claim the assignment and then list it as ambiguous.

**Section 4 underuses evidence already in the tables.** Giorgio’s 72 A7 and 88 A8 events cannot be substitution-only: the Fatima-absent window accounts for 31 A7 and 18 A8. The deciding test is already partly answered. Calling negotiation “occasional” for Amira (42 events) is also loose relative to her other non-core work.

**Code is the weakest required section.** `discover_organizational_roles` returns a list of `Role` objects (`activities`, `originator_importance`), not a dict, so `roles.items()` is wrong. `format_dataframe` is given a `resource_key` argument it does not take. `temporary_substitution` is assumed boolean without justification. The join to complexity is a print loop, not an actual profile join, and there is no inspection of role objects against authority or risk.

**What is actually good:** Giorgio / Mateo / Erik are discussed with benefits and failure modes (38→74 min pricing, 13/21 approvals rechecked, two critical cases waiting >2 days). Temps are separated from permanent front office because of rework, not volume. The two staffing cautions (Dina ≠ Chen; do not delete Giorgio’s cover without capacity) match the constraints. Format compliance is complete.

Those strengths make it a competent process-owner memo. The Chen–Dina merge, the Giorgio omission from the table, the section 2/4 clash, and the broken PM4Py API keep it well below “nearly flawless.”
