**8.0**

The write-up is strong on the things that matter most: it uses the required headings, treats the given alignments as authoritative, separates technical cost from business risk, correctly reads silent/`tau` moves as fitting, and correctly flags the zero-cost Auto Approve cases (CL-609, CL-610) as data-aware policy failures. Case-level alignment semantics are mostly accurate (especially CL-604’s early `L(Pay)` / later `M(Pay)`, CL-601 telemetry, CL-603 retries, CL-611 label split, and the denial/withdrawal scope cases). Uncertainty is handled reasonably, and missing events are not treated as proof of non-occurrence.

It is not near-flawless.

**Severity is internally inconsistent with the stated framework.** Amount/exposure is listed as a core criterion, then CL-610 (EUR 4,500) is scored 5 — the same as CL-602 (EUR 480k, no fraud evidence) and CL-604 (EUR 90k, payment before approval). Bank-change Auto Approve is a serious control failure, but equating residual loss of 4.5k with those two cases, and bundling all three in Priority 1 without distinguishing exposure, undercuts the materiality model.

**A few policy readings are slightly sloppy.** CL-606’s “P2 satisfied if the external assessment is accepted” mixes the process-tree `Assess Loss` gap with P2, which actually concerns Fraud Screen + Manual Approve before Pay (those *did* occur). CL-609 correctly cites P1, but never resolves the compliant path: P1 forbids Auto Approve, while P2 does *not* require Fraud Screen/Manual Approve for EUR 18k / MEDIUM / no bank change. That ambiguity should have been named.

**Section 7 misses part of its own spec.** The dataframe keeps `raw_cost` and `severity` separate, which is right, but the aggregation does not actually roll up risk levels or policy-overlay flags as required. `convert_dataframe_to_event_log` is version-fragile; timestamps are synthetic and therefore cannot reproduce the one case where timing *is* the finding (CL-604). That does not sink the narrative, but it is not cleanly executable reproduction either.

**Smaller nits:** CL-606 confidence is low given a signed licensed-adjuster report; a few remediation rows collapse unlike residual-loss cases; prose in the wide case table is occasionally denser than it needs to be.

Net: high-quality, judge-aligned analysis with real but limited defects. Too many calibration, wording, and spec-compliance issues for 9+.
