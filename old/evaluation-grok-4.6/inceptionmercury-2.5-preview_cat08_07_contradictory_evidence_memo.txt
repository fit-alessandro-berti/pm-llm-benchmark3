4.8

The memo is structurally complete and picks a defensible family of action (do not scale R nationally; do not full rollback). That is the floor, not a high score. Under strict grading the submission has material errors, omitted required content, internally inconsistent advice, and thin use of the process-mining evidence.

**Executive memo.** The call for Option E is directionally right, but the memo does not actually specify E. It slides into a C/E blend: “scale the coordinator model for low-risk homes” while “suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation for medium/high-risk properties.” Option E is deploy **R** only for simple low-risk houses, add **controls** for apartments/old buildings, and **measure before wider scale**. Immediate national rollout of even a subset after a 6-week, mix-biased, incompletely instrumented pilot underweights the data-coverage caveats (pilot regions heavier in apartments/older buildings; segment/risk attributes missing before week 3; start timestamps sparse; cost truncated after week 4). Fitness/precision is cited with broken arrows (`0.87  0.94`). Estimated full cost is treated as a firm worsening despite the prompt labeling it incomplete. Complaint-type shift, alignment moves, resource reallocation, and the working-together network are absent from the decision narrative.

**Evidence table.** Meets the eight-item minimum and little more. Four facts are correct but cherry-picked; first-time-right, SLA, manual activation, second visits, DFG edges, alignments, generalization, mix shift, and data-quality limits are missing. The second “assumption” is a logic error: “Coordinator bottlenecks would resolve if volume increased further” is not an open uncertainty—it is implausible at 93% utilization and is the opposite of what should be flagged (whether coordinator capacity can scale at all). The cost row is mis-labeled: the supported **fact** is that downstream cost is incomplete after week 4; “estimates are accurate enough to decide” is the uncertain assumption. “Exception loops” slightly misstates the precision finding (too many **combinations** of skip/manual/second-visit/billing, not necessarily loops). Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated in form, not in quality.

**Metric conflicts.** This is the strongest section and is still incomplete. Fitness vs precision and median vs tail are explained at a textbook level. The median/tail discussion omits the central confound: the pilot is not a like-for-like mix with baseline. Handovers vs resilience is fine. Visible vs downstream cost is fine but overconfident. Segment heterogeneity is correct but does not use the actual alignment evidence (billing before activation; install without survey; missing permit checks; complaints after billing) or the temporal profile (failed visit → second visit delay under high coordinator load). Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) is ignored.

**Decision alternatives.** Option C is **misread**. C suspends survey skipping and post-billing activation; the listed risk “does not fix survey/billing errors” and the condition “if risk rules are fixed immediately” contradict the alternative as defined. D is reduced to a cost-data gap; D is a controlled experiment with better segment/risk measurement. A and B are sketched, not stress-tested against precision, SLA, NPS, activation-before-billing, or the 34% simple-house gain. Evaluations are slogan-length (`High risk`, `Safe but inefficient`) and do not state when a rational actor would actually switch.

**Value of information.** **M1 is missing** from a required ranking of M1–M8. That is a completeness failure, and it is the highest-leverage causal test of the skip rule. M8 is dismissed as “PR”; split CX is decision-relevant because complaints already moved from wait-time to “installed but not working” / “billing before activation,” which is exactly the speed-vs-quality trade-off. M2 is undervalued given sparse start timestamps (cycle-time facts are completion-based). M5 is parked despite 93% utilization and stall-on-absence. Most items lack a decision-flip: what result would move E→C, E→D, or justify A. The 90% rule-precision cutoff is invented.

**Final recommendation.** Labels E, then scales coordinator ownership **nationally** for simple houses, pauses skip/post-billing only for complex segments, and measures only M6 and M4. That is not E (no explicit apartment/old-building **controls**, no M7 conformance gates, no M1, national scale before measurement). Thresholds are broken in the text (`cost is  Baseline`, `complaint remains  6.1%`) and poorly specified: 6.1% is the **overall** baseline, not a sensible hurdle “across all segments”; p90/p95, first-time-right, SLA, precision, and billing-before-activation never appear as rollout gates.

**Net.** Competent outline, correct rejection of blind scale/rollback, and a fair mechanism sketch in section 3. Not a reliable decision memo: wrong reading of C, omitted M1, ignored mix and measurement bias, inconsistent “E,” and unjustified national subset-scale.
