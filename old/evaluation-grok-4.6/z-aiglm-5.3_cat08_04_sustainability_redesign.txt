**7.4**

The final write-up is a strong, process-grounded redesign: it follows the required seven-section structure, stays inside the event attributes and DFG, distinguishes slack vs. true urgency, and does not treat “lowest emissions” as the right answer for genuine downtime. Under a hypercritical reading, it is not nearly flawless. Several numerical, labeling, and table-logic errors are material enough to block a high score.

**What is actually good.** The boundary is lifecycle-aware (scrap and obsolescence in; customer downtime out, with the displacement warning). The indicator set maps onto the log. All twelve levers are analyzed with path, mechanism, service/quality/resilience effect, and monitoring evidence. The portfolio arithmetic in Section 4 (MTO ≈ 87% of energy / ≈ 86% of scrap; Critical ≈ 40% of transport CO2e; CNC_B rework path ≈ one-fifth of energy) is directionally consistent with the given averages. Rebound coverage is the best part of the answer: batching→expedite, obsolescence, packaging vs. returns, inspection queues, mode-shift capacity atrophy, urgency inflation, and critical-use displacement. The recommended package is coherent (govern first, then production, then critical-tier split, then demand-side slack) and states explicit non-goals.

**Factual and attribution errors.**  
- S5 calls “5,100 ground confirms.” The DFG has `Ship ground → Confirm delivery: 6550`. `Package standard → Ship ground: 5100` is a different arc. That is a direct misread of the frequency graph.  
- S4’s “~5–6 tCO2e from ~420 orders” treats every Standard-stock Service-contract SP1 violation as a stock-air case at 18.5→3.6 kgCO2e. SP1 violations are not shown to be identical to the 5% stock-air variant (600 orders, mixed Regular/Service-contract; air also exists on reinforced-pack paths). The tonne figure is an upper bound presented as an expected Phase-1 result.  
- S11’s “roughly double the clean ground path” is sloppy: transport 7.2 vs 3.6 is double; energy 1.4 vs 0.9 is not; late 24% vs 8% is triple.  
- Section 1 counts cancellation as in-boundary energy waste. Most cancels (`Availability check → Cancel due to delay: 410` of 530) never reach production.

**Logic and table defects.**  
- S4 resilience is marked “↓ unless air kept as backup.” With the stated legend (↑ = risk worsens), that clause is backwards; S5/S8 use the opposite, correct construction (“↑ unless fallback/dual-lane”). The table is internally inconsistent on the policy the rest of the text gets right.  
- S9’s late-risk cell (3%→9%) applies the `Critical → stock/ground` exception to the whole precautionary tier, even though the same option still allows expedited air (stock-air late rate is 3%). Service deterioration is overstated for the air-retained slice and under-specified for the mix.  
- S2’s material column is only “obsolescence ↑.” Larger CNC/heat batches also change *process* scrap per item; that channel is omitted.

**Clarity and format nits (each small, together not free).** Extra H1 title violates “exactly the following top-level sections.” Several indicators (van occupancy, forecast error, route reliability at decision time) are future instrumentation, not observables in the current log, and are not always labeled as such. “Negligible service risk” for Phase 1 sits poorly next to S4’s own +12–24 h cycle-time note, even with the 85% slack gate.

**What keeps it well above a middling score.** It never recommends a blanket air ban, max batching, stripping Q1 reinforcement, or van elimination for true downtime. Rework, returns, and obsolescence are first-class. Validation (SP1/SP2/Q1 as conformance rules, segment stop-losses, rebound watches, R1 audits) matches the process-mining setting.

Net: high-quality multi-criteria reasoning with enough concrete misreads and over-precise savings claims that a strict grader cannot treat it as near-flawless.
