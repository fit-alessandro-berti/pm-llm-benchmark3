**7.0**

The final five-section audit is substantively strong: it covers the required inventory, keeps legitimate prefix and congestion signals, and treats `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` as future-but-legal rather than as the same defect as `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed`. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several process and conceptual gaps, plus sloppy wording, keep it well below the top band.

**What is done well.** Section 1 hits every global top-12 feature, every listed sequence pattern, and the local-only fields (`claimant_channel`, `final_decision`, `initial_reserve`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`). Obvious leakage (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`) is correctly marked critical. Subtler lineage bugs are also caught: the `prior_claim_count` SQL cutoff, the 23:00 `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `final_handler_team` populated at closure without a history timestamp, mutable `risk_score_current`, and `last_resource_role` as a complete-case field. `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are preserved rather than discarded. Section 2 is a real validation suite (prefix timestamps, order, ties, complete-trace vs prefix, enabled vs future-possible, appeal semantics) and correctly warns that a frequent DFG edge is not a normative rule. Section 4 does not replay leaked outcomes in the narratives; Case Z is invalidated on the right ambiguities. Section 5 covers the requested control plane.

**Material defects.**

1. **Required distinction under-delivered.** The brief asks to separate temporal leakage, process impossibility, mutable lineage, correlated proxies, legitimate signals, *and unsupported causal stories*. The table almost never tags the last class. “Appeals cause breaches” and “the final team determines delay” are false even if the columns were available; that is not the same finding as “this column is post-`t0`.”

2. **Process miss on repaired document features.** Replacing `num_document_requests_total` with a prefix count is incomplete. At this `t0` (immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`), document-request cycles are not yet enabled, so a correctly computed prefix count is a near-constant 0 and is not an “early warning.” That is exactly the kind of process-aware point the task is testing.

3. **Order checks omit allowed concurrency.** Fraud investigation may run in parallel with evidence collection and damage assessment. A linear partial order will false-flag legitimate interleavings. The suite also under-specifies whether fraud can already be enabled at `t0` after a trigger.

4. **Severity is inconsistent for equally inadmissible future patterns.** `Appeal → Settlement` is critical, while `Documents Requested → Documents Received` and `Damage Assessment → Settlement` are only high, even though none of those edges can exist in the stated prefix.

5. **`initial_reserve` is over-trusted.** No lineage is given. Calling it a low-severity “defensible predictive signal” assumes registration-time freezing that is not in the record.

6. **Correlated proxies are treated inconsistently.** Region is kept (correct, given “do not simply delete every correlated feature”); `claimant_channel` is stripped from Case X’s credible drivers for fairness. Same standard should apply to both, with a predictive-vs-actionable caveat.

7. **Clarity failures in load-bearing sentences.** Comparison operators disappear (`feature_timestamp t0`, `valid_as_of t0`), and the key boundary sentence collapses into “Predictive validity causal effect operational actionability.” In an audit, that is not a cosmetic typo.

**Bottom line.** This is a credible, usable audit of leakage and lineage, with revised Case X/Y/Z write-ups that mostly respect `t0`. It is not tight enough on causal-story classification, t0-enabled activities, or internal consistency to score in the 8–10 range under the stated rubric.
