**6.5**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and often careful about authority windows, logging, and non-control-flow rules. It is not close to flawless. A required deliverable mis-encodes the central control-flow dispute, and several authority and evidence claims are internally inconsistent.

## What is actually strong

Sections 1–5 mostly do the job the prompt asked for. CS-9 is treated as the standing baseline; PD-3 and FD-7 are treated as time-bounded overlays rather than silent amendments; desirability is not allowed to rewrite policy. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including traces that fit M0 but still break resource rules. The best classifications are right: SUP-701 compliant; SUP-703 and SUP-708 high-severity noncompliant; SUP-704 justified and SUP-705 not, on written expiry; SUP-702 versus SUP-714 split on the PD-3 window; SUP-706 indeterminate; SUP-707/713 model defects; SUP-711 process-compliant with dirty data; SUP-712 SoD failure. The system-level close correctly rejects both “deviation rate proves chaos” and “model gaps exonerate operations.”

That is a competent review. The defects below are why it is not a good one.

## Fatal model-semantics error

The revised POWL is supposed to stop treating legitimate pilot traces as deviations. It does not.

`low_risk_auto_route` puts `checks_choice` (which always contains `Quality_Review`) before `Auto_Approve` and `Activate_Supplier`. Under PD-3, quality may occur up to two business days *after* provisional activation. SUP-702, SUP-703, and SUP-714 all have that order. Section 6.1 repeats the same mistake in prose: concurrent checks, *then* a low-risk auto-approval branch.

Section 6.2 then says overlays may allow post-activation quality “in DECLARE.” That is not how the layers work. DECLARE/log-skeleton constraints restrict a language; they cannot admit traces the POWL already forbids. Emergency is encoded with quality after `Provisional_Activate_Supplier`; auto-approval is not. The architecture therefore rebuilds M0’s core defect for the exact overlay the case study is about, and it does not disclose that limitation. The prompt required an explicit approximation rather than a hidden one.

Related POWL problems:

- `checks_choice` is the same object in two parent routes. That is an unsafe POWL sharing pattern, not a clean XOR of copies.
- The emergency path invents a second `Activate_Supplier` after quality and manual approval. Observed FD-7 traces have a single activation, then retrospective quality and approval. A future-state distinction is defensible only if the logging proposal says so; here the model will still fail the cases it claims to legitimate.
- Rejection still requires `Quality_Review`, which recreates the SUP-709 scope fight after section 6.2 said early rejects belong in a separate intake model.
- `Request_More_Info` is a one-shot XOR arm pinned between sanctions and quality, not a `LOOP`. The prompt allows an approximation, but this one cannot represent RMI after both checks, repeated cycles as such, or RMI as a waiting pattern around the decision. `SilentTransition` is imported and unused; `Operator.LOOP` is the construct they needed and did not use.
- PD-3’s required automated master-data validation never appears, even as a labeled skippable step.

So section 7 satisfies the checklist of activity names more than the process semantics.

## Authority and burden-of-proof cracks

Section 1.3 contradicts itself. Exceptions first require a documented overlay *in force at the time*. The next paragraph says matching intent plus weak or legally ambiguous documentation is still a justified exception. SUP-702 is classified under the loose rule (PD-3 never entered CS-9; Compliance later denied weakening CS-9). SUP-705 is classified under the strict rule (no written extension, even though the factual emergency and control pattern are conceded). That is not a reasoned standard. It is two standards.

Prosecution 2.6 then says PD-3 “barely applies” to SUP-702. It does not. The case is inside the window, eligible, and documented. The strongest prosecution argument is lack of authority to override CS-9, not thin eligibility. Grouping SUP-702 with expired SUP-714 blunts the prosecution’s best point and makes the later 702/714 distinction look retrofitted.

The defense underplays the same aggregate the prosecution should have pressed harder: 14 clearly ineligible auto-approvals plus 9 missing attributes is not a rounding error on a sanctions-adjacent gate. Calling this “mostly correct internalization” is too generous for a steelman that was required to admit real risk.

## Evidence mistakes

SUP-702’s missing `Notify_Requester` is dated 2026-06-15. N4 moves notification to CRM on 2026-07-01. The matrix notes that the migration “does not yet apply,” then treats a CRM `notification_id` as ordinary migration residue. That does not follow. Pre-migration absence from the onboarding log is a different fact from post-migration 70% mirroring (SUP-706). The final JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION may still be right because of a stable ID, but the dating problem is not analyzed.

SUP-710 as `MIXED` is the classification the prompt warned against. The dominant reading is given—ungoverned reactivation, valid-looking residual controls—but the label still dumps a scope gap and a missing quality event into one bucket. `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT` with residual control weakness stated in the reason would have been cleaner.

SUP-715 is called noncompliant for missing CoI disclosure, which the source actually supports, but the matrix “what would reverse” line (“absence of CoI policy would weaken the charge”) is sloppy: the prompt already states the disclosure rule. The uncertainty is whether missing disclosure, without a relationship ban, is process noncompliance or an HR/policy breach outside M0. The write-up never quite separates those.

## Smaller but real defects

- M0 is described as faithfully encoding CS-9’s core flow. CS-9 requires sanctions and quality *before activation*, not quality before every XOR, including reject. Equating M0 with CS-9 on rejected leads is exactly the scope error SUP-709 exists to expose.
- “Preponderance of the evidence” is the wrong tool for instrument hierarchy. Whether PD-3 can displace CS-9 is an authority question, not a fact-weighing question.
- Prosecution acknowledgment of model/data defects is thin relative to the prompt: CRM mutability, aliasing, label mapping, and duplicate register events are mostly parked in the matrix rather than conceded in the brief.
- Section 5.2’s “PARTIALLY NONCOMPLIANT” is a conclusion, not a classification vocabulary term, and it sits next to an insistence that the case was not a split-the-difference exercise. The case-level table is better than that slogan.

## Net

Dialectical coverage and case-level judgment are well above average. The piece fails the two things an LLM judge was told to privilege after fairness: model semantics, and a coherent account of scope and authority. A revised POWL that still makes PD-3-legal traces unfit is not a small coding slip; it is the assignment’s payload.
