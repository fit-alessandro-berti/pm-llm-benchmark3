**7.8**

This is a strong, process-aware submission: states, obligations, object links, queues, and the completion-only/migration observation model are used as reasoning machinery rather than decoration. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading of the spec and the process semantics, several repeated slips keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually good.** Primary states are right for all five cases (C6-A/B/E in S3, C6-C/D in S4). The C6-A vs C6-B discussion is the one place the prompt most wants discrimination, and it is done properly: same prefix, different obligation sets, different competitors for the next log line, different remaining lifetime. `process_enabled` is used as a real distinction on C6-B (closures), C6-C (disposition before RA), and C6-D (backfill / premature disposition), not as a default `true`. Missing events are treated as unobserved, not as proof of non-occurrence, and C6-D’s worklist snapshot is the right kind of side-channel. DFG counts are used as mixed-population frequencies; the inflow/outflow gaps (972 vs 824 at triage, 513 vs 356 on the evidence cycle, 266 vs 229 at disposition) are correctly noticed. Intervals are wide, midpoints leave leftover mass, and rank-1 “Evidence Review Completed” is not cloned across A/B/E.

**Spec violation.** C6-D rank 2 is `"Customer Evidence Requested / Evidence Received (backfilled evidence-cycle event)"`. The task asked for exactly three next logged activities or terminations, each one activity. A slash-fusion of two repair events is a dodge: it is unclear which token would actually appear, and it is not comparable to the other twelve predictions.

**Obligation, enablement, and progress are repeatedly collapsed.** Rule 4/5 say what must exist before disposition, not what is enabled now. C6-A still marks Technical Investigation and Regulatory Assessment `process_enabled: true` from an S3 primary, on the strength of “already owed” plus a possible invisible start. C6-E does the same for Supplier Analysis and Technical Investigation. C6-C marks Supplier Analysis enabled while the text says the lot/component trigger is unconfirmed. C6-D’s primary state treats “R-R2 opened this case at 12:40” as Regulatory Assessment in progress; the contradicting list later admits that may have been queue triage. Those are the exact distinctions the prompt asked to keep separate.

**Some rankings fight the author’s own evidence.** After Evidence Received the log has one listed successor (356). C6-A still puts Technical Investigation next at 0.10–0.22. That would require completion in the same 1–3 h window as a review that is already near its 2.8 h median, from a 09:08 triage against a 9 h median, with one of three engineers out and no DFG arc from Evidence Received into investigation. The contradicting-evidence bullet essentially refutes rank 2; the probability does not come down accordingly. C6-E rank 3 (Technical Investigation on a Low, no-injury, single-device case) is weakly motivated and its interval (0.06–0.16) sits inside rank 2 (0.08–0.20), so the ranking is barely a ranking. C6-B’s supporting list argues for Close after review, while rank 2 is Close *as the next log event*; eventual path and next observation are mixed.

**State and obligation hygiene is sloppy.** “S1-like” and “S3/S4 hybrid” are not in the given abstraction. C6-D lists an unlogged, uncertain evidence cycle under `completed_obligations`. C6-A puts “Fast Close is NOT permitted” in `open_or_suspected_obligations`. Those lists are supposed to be the obligation inventory, not a narrative dump.

**Smaller process nits.** The 14:00 evidence-review queue (4 waiting, 2 reviewers) is treated as an exogenous load; C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E are the obvious candidates to *be* those waiting items, and that is never considered. C6-C still leans on TI→Disposition (119) vs TI→RA (74) after `dfg_limitations` has already said that arc is the wrong population for a reportability-signal case. Probability cuts such as 0.55–0.75 and 0.45–0.65 are finer than the evidence warrants, even with the calibration disclaimer. No prediction horizon is stated, so C6-D’s “RA is next” (earliest plausible completion ~14:40) blurs next-event-unbounded with next-event-soon.

The cross-case section is the best part of the paper and is largely consistent with the case write-ups. The defects above are not cosmetic: they are exactly the failure modes (enablement vs observation, DFG as a prior not a policy, one activity per rank, no false precision) the rubric told the author to avoid. That is why this stays in the high-7s rather than the 9s.
