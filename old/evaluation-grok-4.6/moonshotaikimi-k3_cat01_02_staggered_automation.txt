This is a strong, format-compliant answer with unusually careful arithmetic and the right policy instinct. It is not nearly flawless. Under the required strictness, several real defects keep it well below the 9–10 band.

**What is done well.** The ten required headings, table schemas, coverage constraints, and word-count bands are met. Section 1 stays descriptive and cleanly separates availability, intensity, targeted selection, co-events, and measurement limits. The estimand table is the right shape: ITT vs. dose, median vs. tail, process quality, CSAT, dynamics/heterogeneity, and a non-identified spillover estimand, with SUTVA and treatment-version notes. Identification is not naïve TWFE: staggered not-yet-treated event study is the primary design, TWFE negative weighting is named, and two credible alternatives are specified. Mechanism analysis is process-literate (substitution vs. congestion, mix/routing, staffing, tail bimodality, survey selection). DiD arithmetic I recomputed (Alpha/Beta/Gamma own-changes, never-treated windows, April short-window escalation and rework) is accurate. The recommendation correctly refuses a single “−X%” claim and pairs operations with identifiability. The claim ladder prefixes are used in the right order.

**Material defects.**

1. **Factual overstatement in the strongest claim.** The Observed bullet says that by August never-adopters “remained ~3–5h above” their Jan–Mar medians. Delta August is about +4.5h; Epsilon August is **+0.5h** (40.8 vs. 40.3) and has essentially recovered. The 3–5h figure only works if one silently switches to a longer post window. That error sits in the claim the author presents as most secure.

2. **Staffing signs are unreadable in several places.** Section 1 (and later echoes) reads “Alpha 2 in May” and “Gamma 1 June” next to “Beta +1.” The minus signs are missing. A reader can invert the Alpha/Gamma FTE story, which matters for bias direction. This is not a cosmetic typo; it garbles a listed confounder.

3. **Stated primary estimator is not what the code estimates.** The design section rejects naïve TWFE and nominates Callaway–Sant’Anna / Sun–Abraham. Section 9 estimates two-way FE `post_avail` plus an intensity regression, and only *prints* event-time means. Comments mitigate this, but the reproducible analysis does not implement the identification strategy the memo defends.

4. **Section 8 contradicts itself on timing.** It wants expansion now (Delta/Epsilon, September/November) *and* “at least 6 months of event-level baseline before each switch.” Those cannot both be true in September 2026. The design also under-treats residual fraud-desk interference in the proposed case-level RCT.

5. **Safe claims sit slightly ahead of the author’s own bounds.** Median confidence is 45 and the sensitivity bound includes 0, yet leadership is told adopters improved “roughly 3–6 hours.” Gamma’s large own-change is window-dependent (they note this, then still lean on the Jan–Jun contrast). The August cross-section “dose–response” check does not residualize Gamma’s permanently lower baseline.

**Lesser issues.** Missing operators elsewhere (`k = 3…+5`, `n  100`) look like the same character-stripping problem. Complexity slope 1.5 h/pp is a reasonable scenario, not a tightly estimated object. CSAT and small-cluster caveats are handled well.

**Bottom line.** Conceptually this is an 8.5–9.0 answer: right estimands, right threats, right refusal of a single percentage, right “expand with embedded evaluation.” Strictly graded, the August control error, broken FTE signs, code/design mismatch, and rollout-timeline contradiction are enough to drop it to **7.0**.
