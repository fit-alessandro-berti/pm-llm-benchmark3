**8.4**

The final five-section audit is strong: it covers every required item, catches both obvious leakage and the subtle lineage defects, preserves the legitimate `t0` signals, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as process-valid-but-future rather than as the impossible-looking `Claim Closed -> Damage` tie, and keeps predictive / causal / actionable / fairness standards apart. It is not nearly flawless.

**What is done well.** Section 1 hits all 12 global features, all five sequence patterns, and the local-only fields (`claimant_channel`, `final_decision`, `initial_reserve`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`). It also correctly flags the extra lineage landmines: `prior_claim_count_before_t0` using `<= extraction_date`, the 23:00 same-day snapshot, `final_handler_team` with no as-of timestamp despite the 65% coincidence, mutable `risk_score_current`, complete-case `last_resource_role`, and extraction-date employee experience. `queue_length_at_t0`, `initial_damage_complexity`, and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are retained. The Closed→Damage pattern is treated as unresolved same-timestamp ordering, not as proven reverse process. Section 2 is a real validation suite (prefix cutoff, future-perturbation canaries, tie permutations, enabled vs future-possible vs impossible, repeated settlement after appeal) and correctly refuses to treat a frequent DFG edge as a rule. Section 3 does not delete every proxy. The JSON structure matches the spec; Case X/Y narratives do not replay leaked outcomes; elapsed keeps its local *decrease* instead of the team’s “less slack” story.

**Defects that keep it well short of 9–10.**

1. **Case Y’s “process-aware narrative” is a prohibition, not an explanation.** It mostly says later activities must not be cited. That is weaker than Case X and undersells what a repaired prefix explanation should actually say.

2. **Over-deletion of `claimant_channel`.** The rubric warns not to drop every correlated feature. Channel is almost certainly a registration attribute; parking it only under “removed until lineage is proven” is overly conservative relative to how `claimant_region` (also a proxy) is handled.

3. **Class membership is slightly muddy.** `initial_reserve` and repaired prior-count sit in both “defensible” and “proxy,” and elapsed/prefix-age sit in both “defensible” and “process-state,” without a crisp “unusable now → class Y after repair” migration. That blurs the five-way taxonomy the prompt asked for.

4. **`total_case_duration_hours` is slightly underspecified.** Calling it near-label is right, but duration-to-closure is not identical to a 10-day *settlement* target (appeals can extend the case after a decision). The table flattens that.

5. **Dominance of leakage on the local scores is underplayed.** Case X’s appeal (+0.26) and `final_decision` (+0.06) are a large share of 0.82; “recompute” is correct, but the write-up never makes clear that the published probability is not a small perturbation away from a valid prefix score.

6. **A few finding-type/severity bundles are imprecise.** `Appeal -> Settlement` is marked *critical* for the same reason as a tautological closure feature, even though the finding type correctly says the *order* is legitimate. `same_day_document_count_snapshot = 6` is treated only as batch lookahead; a stricter process audit would also ask whether six documents at first coverage are even enabled before any request.

7. **Section 5 is competent but generic** once the case-specific work is done—standard feature-store / PIT / calibration / fairness checklist rather than tests tied to these exact defects (e.g., a canary that flips `appeal_seen_anywhere` or permutes the Case Z tie).

These are not catastrophic. The audit would still be usable. Under a hypercritical bar they are enough that this is a high-quality answer, not a near-perfect one.
