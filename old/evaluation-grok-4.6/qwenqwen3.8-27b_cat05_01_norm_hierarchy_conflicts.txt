**6.5**

The submission is complete, well structured, and generally careful about time, evidence, and post-hoc approval. It is not nearly flawless. A strict reading exposes a central methodological failure plus several material analytical errors.

**Hierarchy and classification.** Every case is `NONCOMPLIANT`. That flattens the problem the prompt set. The instructions say not to let the global instrument automatically defeat every other source, and not to treat every deviation as noncompliant. The write-up *says* the hierarchy is issue-specific, then applies GPIP-4 as an almost undefeated floor unless the CRO signs. ECD-2, B7, prior practice of treating signed incident directives as temporary overlays, and the CFO’s stated authority over Finance are all subordinated so hard that no justified exception can ever win. Internal consistency is not the same as a defensible balance of sources.

**D3 is mishandled.** Source D expressly gives the CFO authority to interpret Finance controls and only withholds Compliance and GPIP segregation of duties. If that sentence is taken seriously, missing `Finance_Approve` after valid IC authorization at ≤ EUR 150,000 is not automatically a GPIP breach. Under that reading, P-505 and especially P-507 are live `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` (or at least `INDETERMINATE`) cases. The primary table treats A2 as untouchable integrity in every such file. Section 5 later admits the alternative, which shows the author saw the point and still refused to let it affect any primary call.

**B2 timing is imported from A2.** A2 is explicit that budget/Finance approval must precede commitment. B2 only says purchases ≥ EUR 25,000 “require” Plant and Finance approval. The catalogue converts that into a pre-commitment duty. For P-501 (EUR 38,000, so A2 is off), that overread is load-bearing: same-day Plant/Finance approvals could satisfy B2, leaving mainly the undelegated SHIFT-1 commitment. The missing-evidence cell flags the issue; the classification does not.

**P-503 segregation is incomplete.** PROC-3 is requester, committer, and one of two IC authorizers. A1 bars the requester/committer from being the *final business approver*. Joint authorization by PROC-3 and IC-1 can be read as the pair being the approver, with IC-1 supplying independence. That is not a winning argument, but it is the strongest SoD counter-reading and is not pressure-tested. P-503 is still noncompliant on A2/A3 even in-scope; the SoD analysis is still thinner than the confidence score implies.

**P-505 invents a satisfied rule.** The case file has no `Create_PO`. The table still lists C1 as satisfied. That is an evidential error, not a wording quibble. The timing analysis of the 17:59:31 acknowledgment versus the 18:06 ERP stamp is otherwise one of the stronger passages.

**C3’s four-hour clock is slightly wrong.** C3 requires the (initially verbal) authorization to be recorded within four hours. The pseudo-logic starts that clock at commitment. It does not change P-502 or P-507 on these facts, but it is an inaccurate formalization.

**Scope and organizational practice.** The 24-hour reading of “critical sterilization-line lots” is declared as an assumption and applied consistently, which is good. The competing Operations reading, and the fact that the organization has previously treated signed incident directives as overlays, are then given almost no work in the primary assessments. C6 is treated as a footnote rather than a genuine scope dispute.

**What is actually strong.** Facts, assumptions, and recommendations are separated. Rules are tested at the time of the act. Late ERP stamps are not treated as conclusive where a PDF or supplier acknowledgment is better (P-505, P-507). A6 is applied correctly: later Finance/Compliance does not cure. P-501 is correctly placed before ECD-2; P-504 is correctly placed outside ECD scope; P-506 is correctly treated as post-expiry with an unproven verbal extension. Section 5 is the best part of the paper: five real reversing premises, each with a credibility and a weakness. Section 6 distinguishes breach, logging defect, late proof of a timely act, and failure of proof. Section 7 is a usable clause and includes a concrete anti-normalization safeguard.

**Net.** This is a thorough, usable compliance memo with real command of the file. It is also systematically tilted toward GPIP supremacy, produces a monotone case table the prompt warned against, over-reads B2, under-reads D3, and has a few concrete evidence/formalization slips. Those are not cosmetic. A near-flawless answer would have let at least one or two cases come out `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` or `INDETERMINATE` under an explicit, issue-specific allocation of Finance versus Compliance versus SoD, without pretending GPIP always wins.
