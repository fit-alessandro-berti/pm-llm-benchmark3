This answer looks organized and follows the required headings, but it is factually unreliable on the cases that matter most. A change-control assessment that invents violations, misses the clearest unauthorized HIGH-risk approval, and inverts principal-versus-delegate logic is not a competent analysis. Format compliance cannot offset that.

**Case reasoning is broadly wrong.** CHG-501 is the cleanest valid-delegation example in the log: Clara drafted, Ben approved under D-QM-01 while Asha was on leave, Diego implemented, Eva verified. The answer states that Clara drafted *and* approved and classifies the case **UNAUTHORIZED**. That is a primary-source error, not a close call. Once that mistake is made, the rest of the write-up treats Ben/Clara as if CHG-501 were proof of illicit pairing.

CHG-504 is inverted. Asha is the standing QM. Leave is a condition that can *enable* a delegate; it does not strip the principal of authority. The approval sits inside her VPN window, and she claims she handled one urgent case. Security cannot exclude credential sharing, so the honest label is **INDETERMINATE** on identity, **AUTHORIZED** if the actor was Asha. Calling the principal **UNAUTHORIZED** because she was on leave is the opposite of event-time role reasoning.

CHG-506 is also inverted. D-QM-01 runs through 2026-08-14. An 13:00 approval on 13 August is inside the interval, not after expiry. The real issues are sick-leave onset, Ben’s denial, and an office IP. That is identity/compromise evidence, not a lapsed delegation. **UNAUTHORIZED** with “delegation expired” is simply false.

CHG-507 is the most damaging miss after CHG-501. Eva approved a HIGH production-equipment change on 9 August with no QM role and no live delegation. That is a clean RR3 failure.
