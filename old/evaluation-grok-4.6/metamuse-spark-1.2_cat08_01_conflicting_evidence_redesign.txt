**7.1**

The diagnosis is the strongest part of the submission: it names the central speed/cost vs. quality/compliance contradiction, keeps segment effects separate from averages, and actually uses the artifacts (frequency DFG, performance DFG, replay/alignment, temporal-profile deviations, rework stats, segment outcomes, resource notes). The three redesigns are directionally distinct—routing/conformance, evidence-and-gate friction, fulfillment-policy change—and the later sections mostly respect the “no exact ROI / no global dominance” constraint.

It is not close to flawless.

**Material logic errors**

- Redesign A’s flow still allows Fast approval after a compliance gate, but its impact claims borrow the 96–120h reviewed/*repair* medians. Those times include Repair quote → approval → repair → QC, not “review then dispatch.” Forced review is not forced repair; that conflation makes A’s cycle-time and CX sacrifice overstated and blurs A with C.
- Path performance is treated as roughly causal. The 22%/18% vs 5–6% complaint split is observational. Cases on the reviewed path are a selected set; you cannot promise that diverting today’s fast-path high-value volume will inherit the residual path’s rates. The experiment section never really corrects this.
- B’s mechanism says a 6–18h pre-dispatch gate on ~2,300 dispatches can be staffed from two “underused” inspectors with no new FTEs. Even with 100% high-value and 20% low-risk sampling that is a large load increase. Section 6 later admits QC becomes the bottleneck—so the improvement story and the capacity story contradict each other.
- B also says Premium low-risk median moves from 8h to 14–26h as if everyone hits the gate, while the same design samples only 20% of low-risk. Inconsistent.

**Process-mining and evidence hygiene**

- Sequential *means* (0.8+1.0+3.0+5.0) are sold as a “theoretical median.” Medians do not add, and edge times are not a trace duration.
- High-value device and R1’s EUR 400 threshold are repeatedly fused. They are not the same predicate; A1 even writes ``EUR 400`` without an inequality.
- “Customer complaint/reopen after close” is not itself an R3 violation. R3 is about the *reopened* case passing Manual review before close/denial. Escalation → deny (10) is the sharper R3 risk.
- Offline “fitness check” is used as if it were an online control. Log fitness 0.91 and 68% fitting traces are also blurred when A predicts fitting-trace gains “without manual auditing.”
- Mid-risk devices are in the process context and then disappear. Defect-type is barely used except as a portal checklist.
- B’s table invents a “portal adds 1–5h” delay the prompt does not give.

**Redesign distinctness and completeness**

- A3 already inserts a structured evidence portal, which is B’s core lever. That leak makes A and B less “materially different” than claimed.
- C’s “Fraud/risk as triage, not full investigation” may not satisfy R1, and routing all high-value replacement exceptions through escalation does not sit well with a one-slot board, even after the async rewrite.
- A correctly warns about a 15–35% manual inflow shock, then still presents A as the low-complexity first move without a capacity design. That is a known failure mode treated as an acceptable residual rather than a hole in A as a standalone redesign.

**Table and experiment**

- The comparison table is the right shape and rightly refuses strict Pareto dominance, but qualitative signs are missing or stripped, so several cells are hard to read. Current process marked “dominated? No, but conditional” is muddled.
- Stage 1 uses Premium high-value as a DiD control for a Standard high-value rule change. Those segments have different SLAs, complaint rates, and credit exposure; parallel trends are implausible. Randomization *within* Standard high-value was the obvious design.
- The Stage 1 go-rule that the “31% → 9%” fast-path cost gap must “narrow further” misreads the statistic. That figure is the illusory cheapness of Fast approval after rework, not a KPI that improves when you *remove* cases from Fast approval.
- Observation windows and “shifted vs solved” checks are the best experimental thinking in the piece; they do not rescue the control-group and cost-metric errors.

**Net.** Stronger than a generic Lean rewrite, and clearly grounded in the DFG/conformance/rework/segment material. Too many causal overclaims, internal contradictions, and sloppy quantity handling to sit in the high band under a strict rubric.
