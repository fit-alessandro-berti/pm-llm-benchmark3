**5.6**

This is a complete, well-organized eight-section design with the right *topics* (non-stationary demand, calendars, rework, latent objections, bundling). It is not close to flawless. Several issues are structural, not cosmetic, and they undermine the twin’s stated purpose: comparing resource and routing interventions.

**Simulation design (largest defect).** The text never decides how waiting is produced. Section 1.5 says service *and* waiting times are “drawn from” distributions; §2.3 says to “explicitly maintain separate random variables for service and waiting in the simulation.” At the same time the model has resource pools, calendars, and queues, which should *generate* municipal waiting. Those two mechanisms cannot both be primary. If waiting is sampled from the log, D3/D4/D6/D7 cannot be identified: congestion is frozen at historical levels. If waiting is endogenous, sampling waiting times double-counts delay. The design also fails to split the three delay types that actually matter here: resource-queue delay (endogenous), calendar delay (endogenous), and applicant/third-party delay (exogenous). Applicant response is discussed; the split is not operationalized.

Related contradictions: §3.2 assumes service times independent across cases while inspectors share tours and travel; Decision drafting is said to wait on all applicable results, but the engine has no join/synchronization object; the given tree sequences public notice and inspection after the AND-block even though the variant notes say reviews are “partly parallel,” and D5 (legal *pre*-check) fights the tree’s legal-after-drafting order with no execution semantics.

**Process-mining grounding.** Estimation ideas (NHPP/GLM arrivals, variants → routing, mixture on public-notice duration, clusters from architect×district×window) are directionally right, and objections are correctly treated as latent. Gaps: missing starts are called “mostly at random” though the brief says *some resources* only record completions (systematic, not MAR). “Prior violations” for D2 and the fire-area threshold are not in the log and are not given an estimation/proxy plan. The tree omits planning/legal/appeal rework that the process text includes; that mismatch is waved off as “rare variants.” The excerpt already contradicts reconstructed calendars (fire review Sat 17 Jan 2026; inspection Sat 10 Jan 2026 vs Tue–Fri field visits). The answer’s calendar check is generic and misses the evidence in the prompt.

PM4Py usage is internally inconsistent with the high-level conventions the question named (`format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree`). The skeleton uses removed/legacy factory paths (`pm4py.objects.conversion.log.factory`, `pm4py.algo.discovery.dfg.factory`, `pm4py.statistics.variants.log.get`) and `dfg_factory.apply(..., variant="performance")` as a bare string. `log_converter.apply(df)` without a conversion variant is not a reliable dataframe→log conversion. `parse_process_tree` is try/excepted into `None` with no importer fallback, so the tree never actually constrains routing.

**Code.** `CaseState` uses mutable defaults (`completed_activities: List[str] = None`, same for `rework_counts`) instead of `field(default_factory=...)`. Inter-event time is stored as `waiting_time_h` when starts are absent — that is sojourn time, i.e. the collapse the brief forbids, with only a comment as mitigation. `DigitalTwinSimulator` has no cluster, tour, objection-correlation, or AND-join state, so the code is an independent-case DES with calendars, violating “do not design a simple independent-case simulation only.” `ExperimentRunner` has no seed/CRN streams despite §5.

**Experiments and decisions.** The matrix lists B0, D1–D10, extra combo C1, and four stresses — enough coverage. It is still mostly one-factor-at-a-time; D1’s catch rate and D3’s batch window are placeholders; D2’s missing “prior violations” field is ignored; hypothesized effect sizes are not tied to the empirical medians/p90s. SLA “targets” largely reprint current variant performance (e.g. Complex p90 &lt; 260d vs observed 260d), so the as-is system already “passes.” No cost metric for D4 staffing, no residual-risk metric for skipped inspections, no Pareto/constraint rule — only a vague “prefer interventions that improve X and do not worsen Y.”

**Uncertainty and validation.** Warm-up options, 12–24 month horizons, 30–50 reps, CRN, and tail-aware reporting are competent. Parameter uncertainty is OAT ±10–30%, not log-bootstrap or joint posterior; no multiple-comparison handling; no CRN paired tests in the runner. Validation lists the required headings but inherits the waiting-time confusion (replay with “stochastic waiting” plus resources). Seasonal 12/6 split can miss a full demand cycle. Cross-case bundling, weekend/holiday timestamps vs HR calendars, and Decision-drafting joins are not validation items.

**What is still good.** Boundary, entity attributes, role calendars (fire Tue–Thu, legal council weeks, env 40% reserved), architect bursts, inspection travel/batching trade-off, latent objection mixture, explicit “no optimality” posture, and modular separation of PM4Py analysis from a DES engine. Those do not offset the endogenous-waiting contradiction, the independent-case skeleton, the broken/legacy PM4Py wiring, or the tautological decision thresholds.

Not a 7+: too many issues that would produce a calibrated replay of history rather than a twin that can identify D1–D10. Not a 4: the scope, assumptions list, and experiment table are usable as a draft. **5.6.**
