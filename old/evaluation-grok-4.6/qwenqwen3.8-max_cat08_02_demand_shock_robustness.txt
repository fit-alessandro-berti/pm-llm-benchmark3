**7.6**

The final write-up is a strong, well-structured process redesign. It follows the required seven-section format, stays mostly inside the given logs and candidate interventions, and does not optimize the normal-period average. The main deductions are for a missing sufficiency argument, a few category/measurement slippages, and some structural inconsistency.

**What is solid.** All four required stress types are present and tied to real figures: mix shift (Urgent+Complex 31% → 49%), assay saturation (98% utilization, 18h queue, M2/night repeat clustering), validation-tail absence (medical 22h, specialist 36h, hold→medical 48h), and courier compression (intake 7.5h, register→receive 8h, receive→pre-check 9h). Bottleneck-migration analysis is the best part of the answer: it walks each scenario as a sequence, cites DFG frequencies and performance edges, and states how relieving one station overloads the next. The package correctly rejects blunt I1, blanket I2, and default I9; uses WIP caps and case-class lanes so not every type is prioritized at once; and names downstream risks for I4, I5, I8, I10, and machine allocation. Residual risks include two that the redesign itself creates (hidden WIP from batch/release holds; I4 robbing technical validation). The dashboard separates leading from lagging signals and uses bands grounded in the peak metrics.

**Material gaps.** The question is to keep the process *acceptable* under those scenarios. The answer never defines acceptable (e.g., Urgent p90 ≤ 18h, Routine p90 ≤ 72h) and never argues that the package is sufficient rather than merely better than unmanaged peak. That is the central claim, and it is missing. S1 is mostly a restatement of the given peak period, not an independent constructed mix-shift. Combined effects of the package are thinner than the one-row-at-a-time trade-off table.

**Inaccuracies and unclarities.** Mix shares partition the case types, but the text treats “urgent complex” as a hybrid when discussing I10. Recollection and accession correction are collapsed into a single “12.3% defect rate” even though they are different failure modes. I9 is placed in the “robust” package and then described as not robust. Several control limits (6h/10h batch wait, 4h medical-queue green band) are asserted without a time budget back to the 18h/72h clocks. The registration-clerk overtime/error note is unused. The dashboard is not “few” indicators. Repeat-after-failure has the worst tails in the data and is only a supporting lane, not a first-class scenario dimension.

These are not catastrophic, but under a strict reading they keep the answer well short of nearly flawless.
