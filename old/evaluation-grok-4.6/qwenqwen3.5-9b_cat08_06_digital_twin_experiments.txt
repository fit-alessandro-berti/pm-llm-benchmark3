**4.6**

The write-up follows the required eight-section structure and touches the right themes (non-stationary demand, calendars, rework, inter-case dependence, missing objections). Under a strict reading it is still far from a usable digital-twin design. The process-mining core is wrong in several places, the simulation specification is thin, and the experiment/uncertainty/validation sections recycle textbook checklists with methods that do not fit this process.

**Process-mining grounding is the largest failure.** `X(tau, 'Fire-safety review')` is standard process-tree XOR-with-skip, not a wait state. Stripping `X(tau,` and then **all** closing parentheses would destroy the tree. `parse_process_tree` returns a process tree, not a Petri net, and a DFG is derived from the log, not from that tree. The skeleton invents `pm4py.get_dfg` / `get_performance_dfg` and calls `format_dataframe` with nonexistent `case_attributes` / `time_attributes` kwargs. The “arrival average” is mean events per case, not interarrival time (`pm4py.stats.get_case_arrival_average` or equivalent). Required outputs are missing: variants, and start/complete-aware service vs waiting columns. The log loader never actually `format_dataframe`s the excerpt and assumes a `case` column the excerpt does not have.

**Simulation design is underspecified for the constraints.** Section 1 lists generic DES entities but not the state that would make this a twin rather than independent cases: case attributes (`case_type`, `district`, `architect_id`, `application_value`, flags, prior violations), per-pool queues, calendar objects (Monday clerk, fire Tue–Thu, env 40% reserved, inspector Tue–Fri field, legal blackout in council weeks, holidays), AND-join “drafting waits for all applicable reviews,” applicant/third-party clocks, bundle membership, and neighborhood/objection clusters. Calendars appear in prose and almost vanish in code (capacity only). Cross-case dependence is named, not formalized (who waits for a district batch, how related objections couple cases, how architect bursts enter the arrival process).

**Assumptions and data-gap handling are uneven.** Treating objections as latent is correct. The proposed rate \((p90-\mathrm{median})/\max\) is unjustified. Service times are declared independent of other cases, which contradicts inspection batching and shared legal/calendar effects. Rework is a single historical probability despite unstructured reasons and distinct loops (completeness, inspection, legal, appeal). Missing starts are acknowledged, then sidestepped by “use the summary table” without a rule for when sojourn may not be split. The flattened log’s under-representation of dependence is mentioned and then not designed around.

**Experiments, uncertainty, and validation look complete and are methodologically sloppy.** D1–D10 plus three stresses is the right matrix, but almost every lever is a binary on/off (no aging threshold, slot capacity, batch rule, peak-month window). D2’s “later violation risk” is outside the stated boundary. D5’s trade-off is misstated (parallel legal pre-check wastes legal effort on cases that later fail, it does not merely “increase load”). Warm-up of ~1 month / 5% is inappropriate: demand is seasonal (do not hunt steady state) and complex-variance p90 is 260 days (one month does not flush the system). Twenty replications and 95% CIs on means are weak for heavy tails; “log-normal so as not to skew the mean” is confused. Sensitivity ±10% ignores the actual uncertain objects (objection process, start/service split, environmental sensitivity, unobserved area threshold). “Replay validation” is just comparing simulated KPIs to summaries, not alignment/replay. KS tests on service times are not identified in this log. A trailing 3-month holdout is a poor out-of-sample design under March–May / Sep–Nov seasonality. Decision rules use a single 90-day SLA (the brief asked for SLA **by case type**), omit architect fairness, and impose an arbitrary 5% p90 / CoV 0.2 rule that reads like a false selection procedure.

**The skeleton is not a coherent engine, even as a stub.** `random_arrival_interval` / `case_handler` are undefined; `sim.results` is never set; matrix results overwrite every seed; there are no real placeholders for routing or calendars; D1–D10 are string labels with no model hooks. That would be acceptable if the PM4Py half were correct. It is not.

Coverage of the prompt’s headings is the main reason this is not lower. The errors are not cosmetic: they sit in the exact dimensions the task said would be judged.
