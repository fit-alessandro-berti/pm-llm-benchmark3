**7.6**

The diagnosis is largely the one the prompt asks for: April as a schema/pipeline incident, June as a routing-mix change that is not concept drift, July as the supplier onset, August–September as stacked process/concept/population effects, and October as label-maturity confounding. That framing, the triangulation with DFG edge times and matured variant rates, and the staged containment plan are the strongest parts of the answer. They are not enough for a high score under a strict reading. The required monitoring artifact contains a methodological error that recreates the exact selection bias the rest of the answer warns against, and several supporting claims are looser than the table they rest on.

**What is actually right.** The six-row table correctly splits mechanisms and timing. April is pinned to prefix construction, not outcomes: unknown-activity 17%, missing queue 14%, unmapped 18%, predicted risk 0.28 versus observed 0.19, while matured V1/V2/V3 rates stay near the Jan–Mar bands. June is treated as composition (V2 34%→52%, overall breach 0.15) rather than a new P(Y|path). V3’s matured breach 0.35→0.46→0.64 against a stable remote-fix edge (3.8–4.6h) and a collapsing `Order Part → Part Received` edge is the right isolation of a parts-path failure. October’s observed 0.16 is correctly read as censoring of long, breach-prone paths, not as process improvement. Section 4’s training exclusions (April until a verified recompute; October and immature September) and the refusal of a single global recalibration are proportionate. Section 6 hits all four requested failure modes with usable mitigations. Alert gating (`maturity_ok` then `data_quality_ok` before retrain) is the correct dependency structure.

**Required falsification is only half-delivered.** The prompt asked to distinguish evidence from hypotheses *and state what would falsify each explanation*. Competing hypotheses are deferred to §3, which is competent, but the diagnosis table’s “contradicting” cells often restate the preferred story (“the true outcome process was roughly normal”; “this is not model deterioration at all”) instead of giving a sharp killer observation. Covariate drift, population shift, and concept-as-omitted-variable are admitted to be entangled, then left that way in the table rather than tied to a single decisive test.

**June is misdescribed, not just rounded.** Calling AUROC 0.79 and ECE 0.060 “near baseline” is false on the calibration side. Baseline ECE is 0.022–0.025; June mean predicted risk is 0.20 against observed 0.15. That gap is exactly what a mix shift looks like when scores do not fully follow the new variant weights. The conclusion “do not retrain” still holds; the claim that June metrics are essentially healthy does not. It also underplays the one place covariate drift of *model inputs* (variant mix) is cleanly visible, which makes the later covariate-drift row weaker.

**Covariate drift is conceptually sloppy.** Travel distance and device age “not present in the original model” cannot be covariate drift of X. They are omitted variables attached to a new source. True input-distribution shift in this problem is the V2/V3 mix and any in-model queue/prefix features. Folding Partner Z’s unused attributes into the covariate row blurs the prompt’s split between covariate drift, population shift, and concept drift, even though the text later notes the entanglement. Confidence “Medium” does not repair the category error.

**The Python sketch fails the cohort contract it writes down.** Comments define the case-start cohort as cases whose *first* event falls in the month — the right denominator for start-conditioned prediction and maturity. The call is `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., mode="traces_contained")`, which keeps only traces whose *entire* lifecycle sits inside the calendar month. With median label delays of 33–52 days, that drops almost every parts-dependent case that starts in-month and finishes later. Performance-DFG statistics computed on that subset are biased toward short, fast traces — the same long-path omission the October analysis treats as the central measurement failure. Using that object for `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, `get_variants`, unknown-activity counts, and temporal features means the monitor would go quiet on `Order Part → Part Received` in the months where that edge is the story. `mode="events"` for the calendar cohort is fine; the start-cohort implementation is not a sketch simplification, it is the wrong estimator.

**The monthly summary is too thin for the architecture it supposedly feeds.** After requiring variant mix, performance-DFG edges, and dual cohorts, the returned dict stores `n_variants`, `len(dfg)`, `len(temporal)`, and a raw `perf_dfg.get(part_edge)` (often a stats mapping, not a median). It never emits V1/V2/V3 shares, a numeric median on the parts edge, or a maturity denominator. `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` is correctly left unmapped, and exact-key dedup plus a dupe counter is a reasonable first pass, but those do not offset the cohort bug or the missing process KPIs.

**Smaller inaccuracies.** May is described as convalescence, then listed among training-safe months after recompute without a verification gate as strict as April’s. August is called “borderline” for training while being the first month with a fully visible V3 concept/process break — usable only with matured, variant-stratified weights, which is mentioned but not operationalized. `filter_time_range` is given timezone-naive strings after `utc=True` parsing. None of these would dominate if the start-cohort filter were correct; together they keep the piece well below “nearly flawless.”

**Net.** This is a strong audit narrative with a serious implementation error in the one artifact that had to encode process evidence correctly, plus avoidable imprecision on June calibration and on the covariate/population distinction. Under the stated scoring rule, that is a clear drop from the 9–10 band.
