**3.2**

This answer has the required heading skeleton and a few sound qualitative caveats (median service is not flow time; the outage is not a normal throughput regime; four hourly vectors cannot identify a stochastic model). Under a strict reading it is still a weak submission: the regimes are incoherent, several headline numbers are wrong or unsourced, Little’s Law is over-claimed, and the required Python artifact is not reproducible.

**Section 1.** The estimands are only half-defined. Arrivals, completions, and service percentiles are described, but the answer never states a precise target (e.g., opening-hours time-average WIP, throughput as completions versus arrivals, intensity per open hour versus per calendar day). WIP snapshots and `staff_fte` are left without a time base. Ten “threats” are listed, which meets the count, but several are garbled (`5.56.0`, `4436`) and some are not threats to inference so much as restated data facts. The audit also asserts that “no post-opening carryover is reported,” which conflicts with the prompt’s explicit carry-over cautions and with later sections that invoke carry-over.

**Section 2 (decisive failure).** The primary segmentation does not partition the series in an operationally usable way.

- `2026-04-06–04-14` is labeled **Baseline Weekday** but includes Sat–Sun 11–12.
- `2026-04-16–04-20` mixes true outage recovery (16–17, extra FTE, completions ≫ arrivals), a weekend, and Mon 20, which is the start of the case-mix/peak-load break (`complex_pct` 41, `peak_08_10_pct` 42).
- `2026-04-25–05-03` is labeled **Weekend/Recovery** with arrival intensity **3.6–4.4/h**. That range is only true of the weekends. The same window contains Mon–Fri volumes of 109–121/day (~9–10/h) and the actual staffing intervention (29 Apr onward). This is not a small labeling slip; it attributes weekday post-mix, post-intervention dynamics to a weekend regime.
- Outage recovery is *said* to be treated as distinct, then folded into “Post-Outage Burst.”
- The Apr 29 intervention is not a primary cut, despite being a stated design factor.
- “Capacity utilization ~30–40%” / “~25%” / “~50–60%” is never derived. Using `mean_wip / staff_fte` confuses occupancy of *cases* with server utilization; using median service as if it were mean service is exactly the substitution the prompt forbids. During the outage, calling the system “underutilized” because servers are forced idle, while `mean_wip=47` and completions collapse, is operationally misleading.

**Section 3.** The alternative is not a serious competing story. It dates an “Intervention” regime to **15–20 Apr** and then claims this “aligns with known intervention timing (2026-04-29).” That is an internal contradiction. Extra FTE on 16–17 Apr is recovery staffing after a 180-minute outage, not the later temporary intervention. A competent alternative would have been weekday/weekend × mix-shift, or backlog-state (stable / accumulating / clearing), or pre/post 20 Apr mix change with the outage isolated.

**Section 4.** The required pooled-vs-segmented contrast is present in form and wrong in substance.

- Unweighted mean WIP is \(836/28 \approx 29.9\), not 27.5.
- Unweighted mean FTE is \(\approx 5.14\), not 5.8.
- Weekend share of arrivals is \(\approx 14\%\), not 20%; weekday share is \(\approx 86\%\), which also contradicts the answer’s own “88% weekdays.” Completions split almost the same way, not “75%/25%.”
- Unweighted `complex_pct` is about 33%, not 28%. Early weekends are ~21–24%, not 39–42%.
- The table therefore cannot demonstrate Simpson-like reversals; it just publishes inconsistent summaries. Net inventory, the cleanest pooling critique in this file, is never computed: roughly +1, +21, +78, +10 across the four weeks, i.e. the pooled “almost keeping up” story is almost entirely a Week-3 accumulation plus a Week-2 outage hole.

**Section 5.** Fano, CV, and lag-1 values read as invented.

- Weekday-stable daily arrivals (96, 101, 98, 104, 92, 103, 107) are **under-dispersed** (Fano \(\approx 0.26\), CV \(\approx 0.05\)), not Fano 1.2–1.3 / CV 0.35.
- A pooled daily CV near 0.3–0.4 is mostly the weekday/weekend mixture — the exact composition effect that should have been the point.
- Hourly Fano on 7 Apr is already ~3; 21 Apr is far more bursty. None of this is shown.
- Lag-1 “0.3–0.4 post-outage” is not estimable from a handful of daily points and is not computed.
- “Staffing changes alter arrival patterns” confuses demand with capacity.
- Change-point evidence is asserted, not constructed.

**Section 6.** Correct refusal to plug median service into \(L=\lambda W\). The matrix then overreaches.

- Pre-intervention \(18/8.0=2.25\) h uses an unspecified \(L\) (range minimum, not the period mean) and arrivals rather than a justified throughput proxy.
- Hourly 7 Apr is marked **defensible** while only a *daily* `mean_wip` exists. That is not a Little’s Law window; it is a daily stock divided by an hourly flow.
- Weekend 25 Apr uses \(L=23\) with \(\lambda=4.0\) even though that day has 51 arrivals and 43 completions (unstable inventory).
- Recovery/clearing days (16–17 Apr, 29 Apr–1 May), where completions are the more defensible throughput proxy, are not treated carefully.
- Verdict inflation on the one “hourly” row is a competence error, not a wording quibble.

**Section 7.** Some sensible data-collection asks, then several unsafe translations.

- High post-mix WIP (43–50) is described as something regimes “justify” or show is “acceptable.” That is congestion, not a new SLA.
- “Schedule non-complex cases during 11:00–14:00 gaps” treats an outage as a planning slot.
- The actual intervention signal — completions overtaking arrivals on 29 Apr–1 May at 7.0/6.5 FTE — is almost unused.
- The required “looks good pooled, risky segmented” policy is only half-formed. The stronger pooled illusion is “arrivals ≈ completions, mean WIP ~30, add little staff,” which hides systematic Week-3 backlog growth and 08–10 burst load.

**Section 8 (hard requirement miss).** The prompt demanded one self-contained, executable block that embeds the tables, prints pooled vs regime summaries, computes **two** dispersion measures **and** a serial-dependence measure, implements a transparent change-point/segmentation heuristic, computes Little’s Law only for chosen windows, and comments PM4Py on raw events inside that same block.

What was delivered is a sketch with `...` placeholders, so it cannot run; hourly completions are omitted; `outage_min` is omitted; only Fano is coded; there is no autocorrelation and no change-point heuristic; comparison tables are missing; `scipy.stats` is unused; a **second** Python block appears, violating “exactly one”; and the coded segments do not match Section 2. Comments that name PM4Py functions cannot rescue a non-embedded dataset.

**Internal consistency.** Intervention date, weekday/weekend shares, regime boundaries, and the code’s filters all disagree with each other. Confidence labels (“High”/“Medium”) sit next to numbers that are not recoverable from the table.

The piece is not empty and it knows a few textbook warnings. It is far from a defensible process-mining analysis of this series. Invented moments, a mis-specified primary partition that erases the intervention, and non-runnable required code are each independently enough to keep the score in the low 3s.
