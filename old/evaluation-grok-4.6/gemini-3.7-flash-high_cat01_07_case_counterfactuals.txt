**5.8**

The answer is complete, correctly formatted, and conceptually stronger than a typical write-up: it preserves four-eyes and mandatory final compliance, treats K-337 as a decision-policy change rather than a time cut, keeps rework on the table for K-219, and discusses reservation, D1/D2 substitution, avoided rework, assessment bunching, weekends, and timestamp fiction. Those are the hard points. They are not enough. Under a strict reading the piece is quantitatively sloppy, temporally over-optimistic, and internally inconsistent. It is not close to flawless.

**Format and model.** Headings and tables match the spec. The poset is still formally broken: item 8 is a cycle (`Assessment < Request More Info < Document Check < Assessment`) and therefore not a partial order. Item 16 smuggles the K-104 intervention into the normative model. Intervention typing is also wrong on its own terms. K-411 is not “timing only”; dual task creation is a control-flow change. K-104 is not “routing only / resource substitution”; it adds activities and hoards the single certified reviewer. The prose later admits this; the classification heading does not.

**Internal numbers do not agree.** This is the most damaging drafting failure because it is systematic.

- K-104 table: 39.5–51.5 h (base ~45 h). Derivations: 37.0 / 50.3 / 57.0 h.
- K-219 table: 28–35 h / 52–76 h. Derivations: 26.5 / 33.5 / 72.0 h.
- K-337 table: 98–106 h. Derivations: 99.5 / **122.5** / open-ended legal aftermath.
- K-411 table: 11–26 h, base ~15 h. Derivations: 9.0 / **26.0** / 29.0 h.

The summary table repeatedly drops the author’s own base or worst case. That is poor uncertainty calibration, not harmless rounding.

**Arithmetic and calendar.** K-337’s factual span is Register Mon 11:00 → Notify the *next* Wednesday 09:10 ≈ **214 h 10 m**, not 190.2 h (and 10 minutes is 0.17 h, not 0.2 h). The 190.2 h figure is what you get if you treat the end as the following Monday 09:10 and drop Tuesday–Wednesday. Every quoted saving against 190.2 h is therefore understated by about a day. The worst-case “40+ hours of senior management review” is also not process cycle time to `Notify`; it conflates case duration with post-decision dispute work.

**Queue forecasts are treated as optional.** This directly fails the evaluation emphasis on forecasts, readiness vs completion, and feasibility.

- **K-104:** Mon/Tue C1 backlog is 20 h → 16 h. A reserved final slot Tue 19:00–21:00, with M1 the same evening (best 37 h), is not a reservation; it is preemption of a single-server queue. Preliminary C1 work on Monday *plus* a held Tuesday slot double-books the only certified reviewer. Pre-analysis also does not move A1’s start from 15:10 to 14:30; it does not change A1’s queue. The real levers are skipping post-assessment S1, a shorter final C1 read (guidance: save 1–3 h, so residual ≈ 1.5–3.5 h, not a typical 1.5–2.0 h), and possibly a modestly earlier Wednesday C1 start. The advertised 7–19 h cut, and especially the Tuesday-night close, are not supported by the snapshot.
- **K-411:** Tue 08:00 D2 queue is **4 h**. Creating the task at 09:35 does not let D2 start at 10:00 or finish at 14:30. A 4.5 h service after the residual queue ends nearer mid-afternoon. Both checks ready ~3 h earlier is plausible; 5 h earlier and a 9–15 h cycle time are not. Same-day A2 then M1 ignores the Tue 08:00 A2 queue (6 h) and M1’s factual next-day pattern. The table’s base ~15 h is the derivation’s *best* case in disguise.
- **K-219:** Overnight D2 is somewhat licensed by K-104’s overnight D1, but M1 at Tue 17:30–18:00 and A2 at Tue 08:30 (best 26.5 h) still assume away stated queues. The 20–30% rework rate is invented, then treated as if known, while the assumptions column simultaneously asserts handwriting is “sufficient for D2.”

**Feasibility of paths (control-flow) is mostly fine.** Mandatory order, originals before document check, compliance after the latest assessment, preliminary ≠ final, and A1 ≠ C1 are respected. That is the main reason the score is not lower. Minimal-change discipline is weaker: too many service times, start times, and manager hours move at once.

**Scale-up.** Interference direction is right; magnitudes are not. One C1 cannot absorb “100 similar” high-risk reservations in a week. “150 hours of reserved capacity” and “queue inflation exceeding 35 hours” badly understate a single-server collapse. The same is true of 100 handwritten files on D2 (~600 h demand on one generalist). The rework-multiplier, assessment-batching, and weekend-external points are clearly seen; the capacity infeasibility of the thought experiment is not.

**Ranking and pilots.** K-411 first and K-337 last are defensible. Rank 2 for K-104 is not, given the author’s own claim that K-104 is the least transportable policy and that C1 reservation starves urgent work. Softening the intervention to “asynchronous prep, not slot preemption” ranks a different policy than the one specified. Pilots have the required rows and sensible stopping rules; odd/even IDs are not randomization.

**Overclaim.** “Zero quality penalty,” “Strongly Positive,” “Extremely High” robustness, and an unchanged K-104/K-411 decision as if certain are inconsistent with the logging, bunching, and incomplete-packet risks the same text elsewhere admits.

In short: the control-flow logic and interference story are real; the clocks, queues, and cross-section numbers are not reliable. A strict grader should not reward a polished structure that cannot keep its own counterfactual durations still.
