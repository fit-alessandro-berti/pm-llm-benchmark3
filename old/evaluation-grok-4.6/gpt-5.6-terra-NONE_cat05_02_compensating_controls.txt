**7.8**

The answer is a strong control-objective analysis with the right architecture: it refuses mechanical four-eyes counting, treats ED-1 as a conditional permission rather than a waiver, and keeps timing, independence, coverage, and recoverability in view. It is not nearly flawless. A strict reading finds internal contradictions, at least one material classification dodge, several overclaims, and reference-data errors in the required checking code.

## What it does well

Sections 1–2 are the high point. Eight threat scenarios are more than the minimum, and the mappings are not decorative: AV-2 is correctly confined to the checks it actually performs; fabricated receipt and collusion are treated as largely outside AV-2; RA-7 is detective unless recall is still live; ED-1 is a bounded exception, not a disappearance of FEP-1. The sufficiency test is structured rather than impressionistic, and the rejection of “same expected loss” as the sole standard is on-point (deterrence, tail risk, control integrity, accountability).

The case table mostly avoids the forbidden shortcuts. AP-203 is the one same-user case that can defensibly be `OBJECTIVE_MET`. AP-202 and AP-208 are clear `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` packages, and the AP-208 line that legitimacy does not retroactively prove control sufficiency is exactly the right principle. AP-205 is correctly carved out as `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`. AP-206 is correctly left `INDETERMINATE` because the prompt itself offers two explanations for the late token. Hybrid semantics for post-release / pre-settlement manager action are well argued and applied consistently to AP-201/207 versus AP-202/208.

Section 6 is operationally useful: alerts versus proof, pair concentration, shared workstations, credential mismatch, outage/fail-closed rules. Section 7 meets the formal spec (StringIO, `format_dataframe`, the required four-eyes call, blocked-attempt handling, joined facts, evidence flags, “not a verdict” labeling) and, unusually, does not naively trust PM4Py’s return polarity.

## Material defects

**1. Section 7’s case-facts dataframe contradicts the essay and the source facts.**

AP-201 is coded `emergency_token_active=True` and `manager_independence="none"`. The source facts are the opposite: no emergency token, and “Manager m04 is independent of u17.” Those two fields feed `token_before_prepare_flag` and `manager_independent_flag`, so the printed evidence table would misstate the one low-value convenience case the narrative analyzes carefully. Settlement times given only in the narrative (AP-201 at 10:00, AP-203 at 12:00) are never entered, so `manager_after_release_before_settlement_flag` cannot fire for AP-201 even though that hybrid control is central to the AP-201 finding. `emergency_token_minute_after_midnight=1000` on AP-201 is leftover junk. For a deliverable whose point is evidence-for-judgment, wrong reference data is not a cosmetic bug.

**2. AP-204 as `INDETERMINATE` underweights a completed integrity failure.**

Formal user distinctness plus a fabricated consulting receipt is not a missing-fact problem of the same kind as AP-206’s token clock. Money left on fake service evidence. Either the second actor did not challenge the receipt, or independence was already compromised (shared workstation, 37 joint payments). Uncertainty about *collusion versus negligence* is real; uncertainty about whether the control objective was *met* is much weaker. `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` (or at most `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` with an explicit “form held, substance failed” holding) would have been tighter. `INDETERMINATE` here blurs “we cannot prove who cheated” with “we cannot tell whether the package was sufficient.” Those are different.

**3. “Substantive” is asserted where the record does not support it.**

AP-206’s facts say only that m11 approved before release and settlement. They do not say invoice, bank details, or receipt were reviewed. The findings table still writes “independent m11 **substantive** approval.” That is exactly the error the prompt forbids: treating the occurrence of a manager event as a quality review. AP-203 and AP-207 earned that adjective; AP-206 did not. Section 5 quietly drops “substantive,” which makes the table overclaim look accidental rather than reasoned.

**4. Severity and residual risk are conceptually mushy.**

AP-201 is a correct EUR 1,800 office-supply payment with pre-settlement independent review, yet residual risk and severity are both “Moderate.” AP-203 is `OBJECTIVE_MET` with no defect found, also “Moderate” severity. The column never states whether it measures inherent deal risk, residual loss given controls, or gravity of the control breach. Without that, “Moderate” is a decorative constant. A strict scorecard would have split inherent versus residual, or scored control-gap severity separately from dollar exposure.

**5. Recall-window arithmetic is sloppy on AP-201.**

The text treats an audit that *began* 25 hours after release as “likely outside the normal recall horizon.” Settlement is 10:00 the same day; audit starts 09:00 the next day — about 23 hours after settlement. If the 24-hour recall clock runs from the transfer that the bank can still pull, this is inside or on the boundary, not clearly outside. The code then hard-wires “one business day ≈ 24 hours,” which can mark a next-morning audit as late. The approximation is disclosed, but it is then used as if it were a finding.

## Smaller issues that still cost points

- AP-207 meets every stated ED-1 predicate, including independent approval before settlement and a documented evidence review. Calling it only `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is internally consistent with the author’s “post-release is weaker” rule, but it is slightly at odds with Section 2’s claim that ED-1 is the designed full-equivalence route when its conditions are actually met. The distinction from AP-203 is real; the label is harsher than the author’s own test requires.
- AP-209 as `INDETERMINATE` solely because the same pair appears in AP-204 is conservative in the direction the prompt invites, but it still collapses *pattern risk* into *case classification*. A cleaner split would have been `OBJECTIVE_MET` or `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` on this payment, with a structural independence finding that is not itself a case verdict.
- Manager grade L3+ is an explicit MG-4 condition and is never evidenced for any manager. It belongs in “Missing evidence” more loudly than a generic residual clause.
- Override/credential misuse is the load-bearing fact in AP-202 and is discussed in prose, but the evidence code has no credential-owner mismatch flag even though Section 6 recommends one.
- A few Section 1 cells describe capability in general terms rather than cleanly labeling prevent / detect / correct / does-not-address, which was the requested decomposition discipline.

## Code, besides the bad facts

The PM4Py caution is warranted and the pandas cross-check is the right mitigation. Blocked-attempt logic for AP-205 is correct. ED-1 aggregation is conservatively designed (unknown does not become eligible). Those are genuine strengths. They do not offset shipping a facts table that gets AP-201’s emergency status and manager independence wrong, or omitting the settlement timestamps the narrative depends on.

## Net

This is well above a mechanical or shallow review. The control theory is mostly sound, the worst cases are called correctly, and the monitoring recommendations are usable. Under a nearly-flawless standard it still fails: the required code emits false flags, AP-204 treats a paid fabricated receipt as an open epistemic question, “substantive review” is invented for AP-206, and severity is not an operational quantity. Those are not nits on an otherwise perfect paper; they are the difference between a polished analysis and a reliable conformance judgment.
