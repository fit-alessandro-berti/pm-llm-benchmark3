**6.2**

The memo is structurally complete and lands on a defensible class of action (do not scale R nationally; do not full rollback). That is the floor, not the ceiling. Under a strict reading of the brief, the answer is too loose with evidence, internally inconsistent, and thin on the process-mining artifacts it was explicitly required to use.

**What works.** Alternative E is the right neighborhood. The memo does not pretend one lever wins on all metrics, does not treat tail/rework/activation deterioration as ignorable, and does not throw away the simple-house gain. The five conflict explanations are present and mostly directionally correct. A-E are all evaluated. M1–M8 are ranked. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are at least *attempted* as separate classes.

**Factual overreach in the decision memo.** The claim that apartments/old buildings face “second visits (+7pp) and manual activations (+8pp)” is not in the segment table. Those are *overall* deltas (11%→18%, 7%→15%). Complaints of +5–7pp are segment-supported; the other two figures are smuggled in as if they were. Visible-cost improvement is also treated as a simple-house win even though cost is not segmented. That is an executive-level misstatement, not a rounding issue.

**Fact / interpretation / assumption bleed.** The table is the minimum viable version of the required split, not a serious one. Eight facts, almost all headline KPIs. Missing as *facts*: fitness 0.87→0.94, generalization 0.70→0.56, cost 184/198 vs 161/207, manual activation, second visit, SLA, NPS, alignment diagnostics, temporal-profile edges, DFG share shifts, survey utilization 78%→52%, second-visit utilization 66%→92%, incomplete attributes before week 3, cost truncated after week 4, sparse start timestamps, adverse pilot mix. Several “interpretations” are stronger than the log supports (“survey-skipping *caused* second visits”). The assumption that coordinator ownership is the *primary* driver of the 22pp handover drop is correctly labeled uncertain—and then contradicted in the recommendation.

**Internal contradiction.** Section 2 calls coordinator-as-cause uncertain. Section 6 scales coordinator ownership to *all* cases as “proven handover reduction.” That is the same object treated as unknown and as settled. It also means the write-up is not actually E. Prompt E is “deploy R only for simple low-risk houses, add controls, measure.” The operational plan is E for the happy path plus C (coordinator everywhere, skip/post-billing suspended). C is undervalued in the alternatives table (“may not address tail issues”) even though suspending the two most toxic design pieces is the most direct response to alignments and DFG shifts. The label “E” papers over a C+E hybrid that is never argued as such.

**Process-mining artifacts are mostly ignored.** The brief forbids this. Fitness/precision appear; generalization does not. Alignments are never used as alignments: no move-on-log (billing before activation test; repeated install without survey; complaint after billing) and no move-on-model (permit check absent on older apartments). Temporal profile is unused: validate→schedule improved for simple houses; install→activation worsened for apartments; billing→complaint is a *new long-tail relation*; failed visit→second visit delay rose where coordinators were saturated. DFG frequencies are unused (survey 31%→18%, install→manual 8%→19%, billing→complaint 3%→8%, etc.). Social structure is unused: handover-of-work simpler, working-together centralized on coordinators and activation specialists. Resource shift (survey idle, second-visit at 92%) is barely present. A memo that cites “process-mining evidence” and then works almost entirely from the KPI table has not done the assignment.

**Precision explanation is slightly wrong.** High fitness / low precision is not “the model allows unsafe paths that did not occur in the baseline.” Precision is over-permissiveness relative to *the log used to discover the model*. The pilot log already contains those exception combinations (that is what the alignments show); the discovered model then allows even more interleavings of skip / manual activation / second visit / billing start. Treating precision as “unsafe unobserved baseline paths” muddles log behavior, model language, and compliance risk.

**Identification and data quality are underplayed.** Pilot regions were heavier in apartments and old buildings. That mix should have *worsened* the median; the median still fell, which actually strengthens the location-shift claim and implies tails may be part treatment, part composition. The write-up logs the mix as a fact and never uses it. R was a bundle; effects cannot be attributed to coordinator vs skip vs post-billing from this design. Six weeks vs a 30-day complaint window, attributes missing before week 3, and cost missing after week 4 are first-order threats to the 9.8% complaint rate and the €207 estimate. They belong in the evidence table as facts and in the memo as reasons the economic case is not yet decision-ready.

**VoI ranking fights the recommendation.** If coordinator ownership is to be scaled to every case despite the resilience finding, M5 is not rank 7. It is a gate on that choice. M6 is the direct test of the skip rule and is outranked by M7, which is a control, not a decision experiment. M8 is last even though the complaint mix already shifted from “waiting” to “installed but not working / billed before activation,” which is exactly the split M8 would quantify. Impact statements are generic (“could justify…”) rather than decision-switching: what result on M4 moves E→B or E→C; what M1 result licenses skip in medium-risk apartments.

**Thresholds are sloppy.** “p90 improves by 15%” does not say versus pilot or versus baseline. From 38.5 days, −15% is still worse than the baseline 31. From baseline, the bar is severe and unargued. FTR “recover to 80%” is closer to coherent (apartments ~74%, old buildings ~71% if the overall 82% is the right base—which itself is an unstated assumption). Utilization <85% is invented without linking to the observed stall mechanism. “Approve in 1 week to avoid drift” is theater.

**Net.** Competent strategy deck, insufficiently careful analyst memo. Right decision family, incomplete evidence discipline, contradicted causal claims, and a conspicuous skip of the mining artifacts that were supposed to drive the synthesis. Not a failure. Not close to flawless.
