**7.2**

This is a strong, internally consistent modeling write-up with a basically correct POWL construction and an honestly labeled tree approximation. It is not nearly flawless. Several specification, semantic, and validation defects remain, and under a strict reading those are enough to keep the score well below the top band.

**What is actually good.** Assumptions 2–5 and 8 are the right control-flow abstractions for this prompt. The tree is a genuine conservative under-approximation rather than a disguised over-approximation: after `+(CM, ARC)` the middle `+` block preserves the trial join, approval/trial overlap, optional supplier, and `*(ET→IT, AP)` repeat-until shape, while coarsening only the split entry edges. The POWL edge set is the right non-block-structured skeleton (`CM→PV`, `ARC→{DCA, RISK, SUPP}`, `{PV, DCA}→TRIAL`, `{TRIAL, RISK, SUPP}→RM`). Witnesses A1–A5/F1–F3 hit every coverage constraint, and A5/F1/F2 are the right kind of counterexamples. Section 6’s over/under-constraint diagnoses are mostly accurate.

**Specification defects.** D13 is broken as a table row: source/target/relation collapse into `IT (failed) | AP; AP | next ET`, so the required dependency specification does not actually state the loop. D06 is forced into a precedence schema it does not fit. Those are not cosmetic. Section 2 is supposed to be the implementation-independent contract; a garbled loop axiom and a mis-typed XOR row weaken every later “exact / approximate” claim.

**Unresolved ambiguity, then an overclaim of exactness.** Narrative 6 lists what Consult Supplier may overlap and does not mention containment. Whether `CS ∥ CM` (and `SR/QR/RR ∥ CM`) is intended is a real choice. It is not resolved in §1, where the prompt required ambiguities to be settled. It appears only later, in §6, as if CS-before-CM were obviously intended. The POWL is then declared to represent the intended language “exactly.” That is too strong: exactness is relative to a completion-only rewriting plus three free XORs plus this late, undefended overlap reading.

**Start constraints are weakened and then described as enforced.** “X may start only after Y is complete,” observed only at completion time, is *not* the same as “complete(Y) precedes complete(X).” The latter is necessary and strictly weaker. Assumption 1 states the rewrite clearly enough, then §3–4 talk as if the start-after-complete rules were represented. At completion granularity the models cannot reject an X that started too early and finished after Y.

**Tree-gap characterization is asserted, not established.** §3 claims every tree-rejected intended trace “differs from an accepted one only in moving PV before ARC or moving DCA/reviews/CS before CM.” That is plausible and A5 supports one family, but it is not a language-containment argument. Self-critique 2 admits this; the body of §3 still states it as fact.

**Validation plan is less concrete than it pretends.** `len({t.label for t in net.transitions if t.label}) == 14` does not prove uniqueness: a duplicated label plus 14 distinct labels still has set size 14. `pm4py.play_out(model)` is asserted for both artifacts; high-level playout on a POWL object is not a reliable API without conversion. Those are exactly the checks offered as evidence of silent/duplicate hygiene.

**Smaller nits.** The tree helper is fine if `children` returns the live list; that is version-sensitive in the way critique 3 says, but the main code path never shows an equivalent `parse_process_tree` call that would make the compact string executable. “May overlap” is handled well for POWL (omitted edges) and less cleanly for the tree, where CS/approvals are also delayed until containment finishes—an extra over-constraint that §1 under-describes relative to §3/§6.

The POWL is the right canonical model and would likely survive a careful Petri-net inspection. The answer fails a strict bar because it overstates exactness, leaves a material overlap ambiguity out of the assumption list, ships a damaged dependency row, and offers validation checks that do not test what they claim.
