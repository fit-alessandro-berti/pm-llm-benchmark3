**5.2**

The answer has the right skeleton and a real grasp of the speed/quality contradiction, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict evidence-use standard it fails on counts, segment logic, sequencing, and at least one explicit constraint.

**Evidence use is sloppy.** The dominant-path claim is wrong. `Auto eligibility screen -> Fast approval` is 2,550; `Fast approval -> Dispatch replacement` is 2,300. Treating 2,550 as the dispatch path, then later as “2,550 cases/year,” invents both a volume and a time base the log does not have. Fast-path cycle time is not “median ~1 hour post-intake”: the performance DFG already has dispatch at median 1.8h after fast approval, and the segment table gives 8–28h medians on the fast path. Fitness 0.91 is described as weak while the more important signal (68% fitting traces, low precision) is barely used. The 26% repeated-evidence figure is for cases that *enter* evidence request, not for the unclear-defect cohort as a whole. The “9% gap” is misread: fast-approval cases are still 9% *cheaper after* rework, not a 9% rework-cost penalty. Premium SLA “124h” is fabricated; the prompt’s service-credit trigger is 24h for premium low-risk accessories.

**Process-mining reasoning is incomplete.** The diagnosis correctly uses the 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen split, R1/R2 deviations, and premium high-value 22% vs 5%. It then over-attributes all fast-path complaints to wrong-part selection. The DFG also shows `Fast approval -> Customer complaint/reopen` (140) *before* dispatch, `Fast approval -> Quality/compliance close` (350), and `Fraud/risk review -> Fast approval` (240). That last edge is the hybrid the answer never uses: R1 can be satisfied without dumping high-value work onto the 96–120h repair path. Evidence ping-pong (`Request missing evidence -> Manual eligibility review` 890) and underused quality inspectors are sitting in the log and are not used.

**Redesign A is internally inconsistent and too coarse.** The description forces *Premium customers and* high-value devices into manual/fraud review; the target then says only the 35% high-value slice. Premium low-risk accessories (18%) are exactly the R2 exception and the 24h credit-risk segment; putting them on the 46h manual path is the opposite of segment-aware routing. The prompt also asked not to treat the slow reviewed path as uniformly better. A does that.

**Redesign B and C are distinct, but borrowed and mistargeted.** B is the prompt’s pre-dispatch gate; C is the prompt’s evidence portal. That is acceptable if the resource and segment logic is tight. It is not. B would steal the three quote technicians even though quality check is “underused except after repair campaigns.” Applying a 6–18h gate to *all* fast approvals would blow premium low-risk SLAs for a EUR 95 accessory error. C’s mechanism is the strongest of the three, but it still does not use defect-type attributes or the manual-side evidence loop.

**The Pareto table violates a stated constraint.** Marking the current process `dominated? Yes` claims dominance the data cannot support. Current remains faster and still cheaper after rework for several segments. Arrow glyphs are missing, so several cells are unreadable. Implementation complexity for A is also off: the prompt says auto-screen rules can ship in two weeks.

**Experiment sequence contradicts the redesigns.** First pilot “high-value only (20% of volume)” disagrees with the earlier 35%. Stop rule at >90% reviewer utilization ignores the given 82% queue-break. Second pilot puts the evidence portal on standard low-risk (25%), not the 140h unclear-defect slice it was designed for. Third pilot runs B on premium high-value *after A is stable*—the segment A already removed from the fast path, so the gate has nothing to gate. The 2x cost-savings rule pretends a ROI threshold the prompt forbade.

**What still works:** required section structure; three materially different interventions rather than Lean slogans; explicit sacrifices; control-group language; some bottleneck migration (fraud team, abandonment, technician load). Those keep it out of the failure band. The factual errors, A/B sequencing contradiction, wrong pilot segment, dominance claim, and unused DFG paths keep it far from a high score.
