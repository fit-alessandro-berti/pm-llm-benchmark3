**6.4**

The write-up is formally complete and often sophisticated, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has a material internal contradiction, a process-purpose inversion across cases, and a burden-of-proof rule the author states and then does not apply.

**What it does well.** It uses the required headings and table schemas. It refuses a single total ordering and separates facts, interpretations, and assumptions. Timing is handled with real care: P-501 is assessed before ECD-2, P-506 after expiry, P-505 against contemporaneous supplier acknowledgment rather than the 18:06 ERP stamp, and P-507 against the 12:40 PDF rather than the 14:30 ERP entry. A6 is applied consistently. Emergency purchases are not rubber-stamped. Sections 5–7 are usable: the alternative-classification premises are specified, logging defects are distinguished from policy breaches, and the replacement clause covers authority, scope, thresholds, segregation, new suppliers, records, expiry, and anti-normalization.

**The central logical flaw.** The hierarchy treats GPIP-4 A2 (independent budget owner **and** Finance Controller before commitment) as high-weight integrity control, notes that Incident Command members are not budget owners, and says a CFO email probably cannot waive board-level GPIP controls. The case work then lets D1 swallow A2 for P-505 and P-507 (`JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`) while enforcing A2 for P-508 (`NONCOMPLIANT`) solely because 155,000 exceeds the 150,000 figure in D1. That is not a clean competence theory. D2’s “obtain Legal clearance **as well**” is the more natural reading that Legal is additive to joint Incident Command authorization, not a withdrawal of D1 and a snap-back to full A2. The author flags this only as an alternative, then builds the primary catalogue (CFO-FIN-150 / CFO-LEGAL-150 and the pseudo-logic) as if the stricter reading were settled.

**Process-purpose inversion.** P-508 is the best-controlled high-value buy in the set: in-scope, in-window, Compliance and Legal before commitment, two IC authorizers who are not the requester/committer. P-505 has Finance only after ECD-2 expired, no Create_PO, and unknown requester/committer identities. P-507 has no Finance event and unknown identities. Ranking P-508 below those two is the opposite of what the author’s own “core integrity vs operational overlay” story should produce. A 5,000 overrun of an informal email cap is doing more work than Compliance, Legal, and clean segregation.

**Burden of proof is announced, then ignored.** Section 1 says the exception-claimant must prove validity, scope, timing, and that a core control was not removed; missing decisive facts should yield `INDETERMINATE` or `NONCOMPLIANT`. P-505 and P-507 lack IC roles, independence, and requester/committer identities—the exact facts needed to use D1 without breaking A1/A2. They are still `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`, and P-507 is given “Moderate-High” confidence. `INDETERMINATE` is never used as a primary label despite C6, A7’s last sentence, D3–D4, ON-44, and the charter gap on budget owners. That hides ambiguity the materials were written to force.

**Smaller but real defects.**
- B2 is treated as a pre-commitment rule as if the SOP said so; unlike A2, it does not. That interpretive step is under-marked.
- SOP B is applied to the cafeteria freezer (P-504) without asking whether “maintenance and production-support” covers staff amenities. A3 still supports noncompliance if unapproved equals new, but the SOP hook is sloppy.
- C1 allows commitment to *precede* Create_PO; it does not license never creating a PO. P-505’s missing PO is filed as a logging gap, then the case is still justified.
- P-502’s date is inferred, not stated; the inference is reasonable but should have been labeled as such in Section 1, not only as missing D1 evidence.
- Unapproved supplier is equated with “new supplier” for A3 by assumption. That is disclosed, but it is doing a lot of work on P-504 and P-507/P-508 and is not tested against the weaker reading (register gap only).

**Bottom line.** This is a strong process memo with genuine evidential discipline on clocks and records. It is not a 8–10 performance. The finance-exception theory is unstable, the P-508 ranking is backwards, and the method’s own uncertainty valve is left unused. Those are structural argumentation failures, not polish issues.
