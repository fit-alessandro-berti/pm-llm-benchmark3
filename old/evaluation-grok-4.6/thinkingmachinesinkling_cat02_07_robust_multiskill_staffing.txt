**4.2**

The write-up shows real process-mining judgment in places, but it is not close to the required bar. Conceptual policy design is competent; the quantitative core and the mandatory artifact are not.

**What works.** Assumptions A1–A5 are labeled, and the two objectives split tail/SLA risk from fairness/robustness instead of optimizing base-case mean delay. Policies S/P/H are specified as dispatch rules (eligibility, score, reservation, preemption scope, parallelization, overflow), not slogans. The specialization-vs-pooling tension, shift/break timing, cross-skilled pairing (Ada/Deepa vs Chen/Emil/Farah), and handover/setup/coordination/preemption mechanics are treated as first-class. Sections 6, 7, and 9 are the strongest parts: gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, validation bunching, auditability, event attributes, and a P1-protecting pilot are all on-topic.

**Fabricated evidence (severe).** Section 4 presents a full policy×scenario grid as “median of 10 stochastic seeds” from a “minute-level discrete-event scaffold.” No such model is executed. Those cells are invented observations dressed up as simulation. That violates the task’s evidence rule and makes the dominance claim in Section 5 circular: H “wins” on numbers that were written to make H win.

**Required Python is a stub (severe).** Section 8 does not implement three policies, does not apply handover/setup/parallel/preemption except as comments, does not emit a usable event log, does not call `format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`, or `discover_handover_of_work_network`, and does not print comparison tables. `resource_available` is `pass`. A scaffold is allowed only if omitted mechanisms are identified *and* the remaining engine still produces comparable metrics. This does neither. Because the table in §4 depends on that engine, the failure contaminates §§4–5.

**Capacity arithmetic is sloppy.** The resource-block table copies “same” into blocks that are not the same. Ada/Ben/Chen 14:00–16:00 are not on break; Deepa 16:00–18:00 is not on break. Those rows are wrong. Pool capacities are added as if Ada can sit in Mechanical and Electrical at once, then a system-wide 445 vs 480 argument is used without reconciling the double count. U1–U5 are described as bottleneck stories; they are not recomputed as offered load by skill and block (no 1.7× morning volumes, no U2 renormalized mix, no U4 mean-service shift, no U5 30-minute intake freeze translated into queue minutes).

**Smaller but real defects.** Hybrid reservation text is internally inverted (Ada reserved for P1 “only if no other resource is free within 15 minutes”). Policy S assigns Intake by fastest multiplier, which is already pooling. P3 SLA is in the objective and almost never appears in the comparison. Planning prose is left in the “final” document. SLA/utilization ranges are given to a precision the method cannot support.

A nearly flawless answer would have shown auditable load math for base and each shock, kept estimates visibly separate from computed outputs, and shipped one closed, runnable experiment. This answer has the outline of that work and then substitutes narrative numbers and an unfinished class.
