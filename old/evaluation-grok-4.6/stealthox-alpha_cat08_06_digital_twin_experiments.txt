**6.5**

This is a strong, well-structured twin design that hits every required section and respects the hard constraints (cross-case coupling, calendars, service/wait split, latent objections, no `pm4py.read_csv`, no optimality claim). It is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative and logical errors would actually mislead the municipality if taken as written.

**What is done well.** The boundary, entities, queues, and six coupling channels (shared pools, architect bursts, inspection bundles, latent objection clusters, calendar shocks, policy spillovers) are the right DES scope. Waiting times are correctly treated as emergent calibration targets, not inputs. Objections are a latent process matched to notice duration, not fake observed events. PM4Py is confined to log/model analytics; the engine is separate. Assumptions are tagged fitted/calibrated/expert. The experiment matrix covers D1–D10, extra combinations, and five stresses. Uncertainty treatment (Welch warm-up, CRN, nested parameter noise, GPD tails, Sobol on finalists, paired CIs) is appropriately ambitious. Validation and decision sections are complete on paper, and the skeleton is modular and mostly faithful to the stated PM4Py conventions.

**Major flaws**

1. **Log-normal fit is wrong for quantile matching.** `fit_lognormal` sets \(\sigma = \ln(p_{90}/m)/z_{0.9}\) (correct) then \(\mu = \ln(m) - \tfrac12\sigma^2\) (correct only if \(m\) is the *mean*). For a log-normal the median is \(e^\mu\), so \(\mu=\ln(\text{median})\). Planning review (3.5h / 14h) would simulate at median \(\approx 2.0\)h, not 3.5h; applicant response and objection extensions are similarly biased down (~40%). This formula is used everywhere service or delay is drawn. It is a core specification error, not a skeleton shortcut.

2. **Validation tolerances are looser than decision thresholds.** Adoption requires a 10% median/p90 cut with CIs excluding zero, while distributional sign-off allows ±15% median and ±20% p90 vs the empirical table. A model that is only trusted to 20% on the tail cannot support a 10% tail decision. This is a logical contradiction between §6 and §7.

3. **Out-of-sample design cannot test the long-running types.** A 3-month hold-out (and a Sep–Nov peak slice) cannot validate Complex variance (median 140d, p90 260d) or even Commercial (p90 130d): those cases started in the hold-out will not have finished. No lagged/cohort completion analysis is specified. Cross-case structure (bundle sizes, architect-week clustering) is also never validated.

4. **Calendars are capacity tables, not work-time.** Legal, planning, and environmental service times routinely exceed a shift (planning p90 = 14h). The state model and `ResourcePool` never freeze, carry remaining work, or preempt when \(c(t)\) drops at 16:00, weekends, holidays, or council weeks. Given the problem’s emphasis on calendars, this is a first-order fidelity gap, not an implementation detail.

**Serious design/experiment gaps**

- Intra-day arrivals are promised in §2 and omitted in the generator. Intensity is constant over 24h, so most portal submissions land off-shift and manufacture a morning queue that was never estimated. Monday 2.2× is then the wrong mechanism.
- Parallel reviews need an AND-join before decision drafting (stated business rule). That join is not a state variable and is not in the event handlers.
- Inspection scheduling has a named resource in the log; no scheduler pool exists.
- D1, D2, D5, D7, D8, D9 are flags with almost no engine hooks; only D3/D4/D6 are wired. D2’s eligibility rule is not in `RoutingModel`.
- E18/E19 are described as testing D9’s objection model but run baseline vs D10. D9, the intervention whose parameters are most uncertain, is never stressed on S3.
- CRN is specified, but structural change (D2 removing inspections, D3 batching) desynchronizes streams; that bias is ignored.
- Utilization 0.70–0.90 as a *two-sided adoption gate* would reject a successful D2 that idles inspectors. SLA cutoffs (30/60/90/180) are invented and not labeled as policy choices; they sit awkwardly next to empirical p90s (Commercial 130d, Complex 260d), so the 85% SLA bar is likely infeasible at baseline and the relative “no −5pp” clause does all the work without being called the primary rule.
- D10 preference rule (“gain exceeds the additive sum minus an interaction discount”) confuses synergy with diminishing returns.

**PM4Py / code inaccuracies**

- `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` returns `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)`, not a 5-tuple; `perf[-1]` is end activities, stored as `edge_performance`.
- `pm4py.stats.get_variants` is not the stable public alias (`pm4py.get_variants` / statistics variants module).
- All batch extras are hard-coded to architect `A17`.
- Off-peak arrival arithmetic is inconsistent (57.8/day vs ~43/day vs code ~45/day). DOW factors other than Mon/Wed are invented but tagged **[F]**.
- `Engine.run` drains the arrival heap into one queue and returns empty traces; placeholders never schedule `START_SERVICE`. Acceptable only as a stub, but then `ExperimentRunner` cannot produce the KPIs §5–7 assume.
- Broken markdown in the trade-off column hides the actual signs of several effects.

**What keeps the score from collapsing.** The answer does not make the disqualifying mistakes (independent cases only, ignoring calendars, treating objections as observed, collapsing wait into service without comment, claiming optimality). Scope, assumption hygiene, and UQ design are well above a generic DES write-up. Under a normal review this would sit in the high 7s; under the required strictness the specification bug, the validation/decision inconsistency, the unworkable hold-out, and the missing shift-spanning semantics are enough to bar anything that looks like a high score.
