## 1. Definitions — conceptually sloppy

The component formulas for `upstream_elapsed`, `Q_i`, `S_i`, `W_i`, `join_ready = max_i end_i`, `downstream_queue`, `final_service`, and `total_flow_time` are the right objects.

The double-counting argument is not. Synchronization waits are concurrent, not sequential. The sentence “they overlap sequentially under the fork-join rule” is false. The displayed inequality \(W_A+W_B+W_C \ge\) `fork_join_elapsed` is not the identity that prevents double counting and is not even the interesting relation. The relation that must be shown is:

\[
Q_{i,c}+S_{i,c}+W_{i,c}=\textit{fork\_join\_elapsed}_c \quad \forall i
\]

hence \(\sum_i(Q_i+S_i+W_i)=3\cdot\textit{fork\_join\_elapsed}\). Adding all three sync waits (or all three sojourns) counts the same calendar interval three times. An additive case decomposition that does *not* double-count is

\[
\textit{total}_c=\textit{upstream}_c+\max_i(Q_{i,c}+S_{i,c})+\textit{downstream}_c+\textit{final}_c.
\]

That identity is never written. `fork_join_elapsed` is defined and then abandoned.

## 3. Aggregate analysis — inverted sync-wait meaning, wrong means, no reasoning

Mean C queue is reported as 15. Observed C queues are \(10,5,5,35,5,15,5,25,5,5,5,5\); mean \(125/12\approx 10.4\). Mean B service is 37.5, not 40.

“Mean synchronization wait” is required as a number. The table gives “Moderate / High / Low” and **reverses the ranking**. The last-completing branch has \(W_i=0\). B is last in 8/12 cases, so B has the *lowest* mean sync wait (\(\approx 4.3\) min). C finishes first almost always, so C has the *highest* (\(\approx 30.4\) min). Calling B’s sync wait “High” and C’s “Low” is a process-mining error, not a rounding issue.

## 4. Counterfactuals — qualitative hand-waving

No per-case times, no aggregate minutes saved, no slack check, no masking check. That fails the section.

What a serious pass had to compute:

- **A −20%** is −10 min on a 50 min service. Join_ready moves only on F01 and F11. F11 today starts F at 11:50 because F1 is occupied by F09 until 11:50; moving `join_ready` from 11:30 to 11:20 does **not** move `final_end`. F01 can move. Claiming “direct benefit” for both A-critical cases is false once downstream blocking is admitted.
- **B queue −50%** is not “8/12 cases save half the queue.” On F10, cutting B’s 20 min queue by 10 min makes A (11:15) critical; join_ready falls by 5, not 10. On F02 the cut produces an A/B tie. F04 and F08 are C-critical and get **zero** join_ready change. Secondary effect: earlier join_ready can lengthen downstream wait if F1/F2 are busy (F03, F05, F07, F09, F11 are tightly packed on F1).
- **Final capacity.** Several “downstream queues” are **not** resource waits: F01 waits 8 min for F1 with F1 idle; F02 waits 15 min for F2 with F2 idle. Adding a third F resource cannot remove those gaps. The submission never inspects F1/F2 occupancy and therefore cannot tell capacity from a release policy.

“Largest potential flow gain” / “unpredictability risk” is not analysis.
