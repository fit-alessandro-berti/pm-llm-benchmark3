**6.4**

The formal core is mostly right: A and B are not trace-equivalent, P1 fails because B has one global check and can put it after rework or after approval, P2 matches only after the stated dashboard projection, and the four traces A1/A2/B1/B2 are valid and inside the P3 bound. The loop reading, the approve-after-AND fact in A, and the claim that S is a proper restriction of A are also correct.

That is not enough for a high score. The required supporting sections contain real errors, and the writeup overstates what it has shown.

**Footprints are mischaracterized.** Process-mining footprints are pairwise order relations (causal / parallel / exclusive), not “sets of activities that can co-occur.” Saying footprints “ignore order” is backwards: they are a coarse order abstraction. The example footprint `{'Analyze Revision', 'Check Revision', 'Rework Case'}` is not a footprint. Equal footprints still would not prove language equality — that conclusion is right for the wrong reason. The normalization code treats footprints as a bag of activity sets and would not match what `discover_footprints` actually returns.

**The Section 5 explorer would not run on PM4Py as written.** Petri transitions and places expose `in_arcs` / `out_arcs`, not `.inputs` / `.outputs`. `sorted(m.items())` is unsafe because place objects are not ordered. `pm4py.get_initial_marking` / `get_final_marking` are invented fallbacks. The BFS *idea* (silent moves, visible bound, analyze bound, accept only on final marking) is sound; the API is not. A required “self-contained” comparison that dies on `AttributeError` is a major defect.

**EMD usage is hand-wavy.** `compute_emd` expects stochastic languages (trace→probability), not two lists of traces. The try/except does not fix the type error. Uniform-over-enabled-transitions is at least labeled as arbitrary; that part is fine.

**Smaller but real issues.** “Any node/arc-based simplicity metric should treat A as at least as simple as B” is unsupported: B’s AND-with-a-loop vs A’s AND-inside-the-loop expand differently. “Parallel/exclusive” in the operator list is sloppy. P1’s `C_P1` is a reasonable operationalization, but it still does not encode “own check” beyond “same Rework-block,” and the text never says that this is a design choice rather than the unique formalization. Petri-net simplicity is promised and then not computed; that is acceptable only because no fake number is given.

**What stays strong:** verdict table, counterexamples (including two-analysis traces both ways), tau/optional-approve discussion, S as an explicit P1/P2 compromise with regression and negative traces, and the bounded-vs-unbounded warning. Those pieces are why this is not a low score. They do not cancel the footprints mistake or the non-executable comparison code.
