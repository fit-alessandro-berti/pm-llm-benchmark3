**6.0**

The write-up has the right skeleton: six required sections, `max` rather than a sum at the join, calendar pauses, queue vs service, overlapping path bands, and a latent temperature-excursion factor. It is not close to flawless. Several required tables and the headline forecast are internally inconsistent or calendar-blind, and one stated formula is simply wrong.

**Material errors**

1. **Scenario 3 confuses join time with terminal time.** Primary confirmation can be technically ready Tuesday 04:30 and read from 08:30, but QA wait, QA service, and release still remain. Tuesday 08:30–12:00 is not a plausible *terminal* window. The decomposition later says “earliest Tue 14” and a central of Tuesday 15:00–Wednesday 18:00. The required scenario table and the narrative disagree.

2. **Scenario 4’s earliest terminal is too late.** A documentation query adds 4–12 *working* hours before QA resumes. After a Monday review, a 4-hour query clears Tuesday morning; residual QA plus release can finish Tuesday afternoon. Wednesday 08:00–12:00 is a reasonable earliest only for a long query or an investigation, not for the short end of the scenario as defined.

3. **The headline P50/P80 clocks ignore the working calendar the rest of the answer depends on.** 80 h is Monday 23:30; 105 h is Wednesday 00:30; 135 h is Thursday 06:30. QA cannot accept after 20:00, and chemistry cannot work overnight. Completions cluster in opening hours (Monday evening *or* Tuesday morning, then Wednesday daytime). Reporting overnight timestamps as central/P80 results treats elapsed hours as if the plant were 24/7 after the join.

4. **Wrong “standard” triangular mean.** The ledger asserts mean = (min + 2×mode + max)/4. For a triangular law the mean is (min + mode + max)/3 (≈ 2.83 h, not 2.75 h). The formula given is neither triangular nor PERT. Calling it standard is a factual error, even if the numerical gap is small.

**Conceptual weakness vs. the scoring rubric**

The remaining-time mass on the smooth path is mostly **calendar dead time**, not chemistry service. Friday 15:30 → Monday 08:00 is 64.5 h in which neither branch can finish (lab closed; incubation ready Sunday but unread without on-call). Chemistry then usually sets the Monday *time of day* under FIFO. Labeling the smooth-path dominant clock as “chemistry queue/service, then QA” buries the weekend floor that the rubric expects to lead. A tight answer would say: weekend staffing sets an almost hard Monday morning floor; chemistry spillover often loses the Monday race and moves the join to Monday afternoon; QA congestion and correlated rework dominate the right tail.

**Other defects that block a high score**

- Monday 12:00 as earliest completion is optimistic once the current five-batch QA queue, Saturday-only 4 h of QA, and 0.35/h overdispersed arrivals are taken seriously. Empty QA at 10:00 Monday is a thin tail, not a “plausible earliest” center.
- Excursion uplifts (rerun 0.20–0.35, contamination 0.15–0.30) are invented and not labeled as judgmental. Dependence is asserted, not bounded.
- Sunday on-call + contamination is underplayed: a Sunday hit plus 20 h can be readable Monday, so “micro delay ⇒ Tuesday” is not always true.
- Smooth-path 30–45% is narrower than propagating the stated input ranges (~0.27–0.56 if those factors are treated as independent given no excursion).
- Shortcuts (flow-balance QA queue, lognormal QA mean, mixture over discipline) are buried in the ledger rather than stated as approximations that drive the bands.

**What is actually good**

Friday chemistry cannot finish B-417 (≈5 analyst-hours left vs. four waiting jobs). FIFO vs risk-priority is used to move Monday start by a few hours, not as decoration. QA backlog is estimated from initial queue, arrivals, and Friday/Saturday/Monday capacity instead of freezing “five batches forever.” Overlap among rerun, contamination, and QA tails is declared rather than forced to 100%. Temperature excursion as a common shock, and that flag as the highest-value next observation, are the right uncertainty calls.

Those strengths are why this is not a 4. The table-level time errors, the overnight P50, the false triangular formula, and the mis-identified dominant clock are why it is nowhere near an 8–10 under a strict reading.
