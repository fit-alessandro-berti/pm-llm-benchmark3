**7.3**

The answer is substantively strong: it uses the required six-section structure, refuses I-03 on stage grounds, splits I-05 from I-07, treats observational `p` as non-causal, applies segment calibration, and prices spillover as a portfolio externality rather than ranking on `p_now`. Under a normal rubric this would sit higher. Under the requested standard it is not nearly flawless. Several inaccuracies, missing comparisons, and operational holes keep it well below the top of the scale.

**Arithmetic and auditability.** The prompt asked for auditable arithmetic. Operators are routinely dropped (`0.265×9000700`, `0.15×250001050`, `0.05×1000005450`), so a reviewer cannot check the tables without reconstructing the intended minus signs. Most reconstructed figures are correct; I-08 is not. Lower-bound net is `0.05×€100,000 − €450 − €5,000 = −€450`, but the text reports `€450` and “lower-bound net ~€450 to positive.” That is a sign error on the highest-value case. Optimistic avoided-breaches are written as `0.66–1.0` when the upper-half envelope is `0.655–0.87`. False-alarm cost is described as `(1−p)×burden` in the rule, then subtracted in full in every net, so the theory and the numbers disagree.

**Allocation is coherent but not decision-theoretically closed.** Selecting I-01, I-02, I-09 and reserving one slot is defensible: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is chatter, I-06 is imputed, I-07 is inside a 1.5 h window, I-08’s lower bound is weak. The gap is that the chosen portfolio is never compared with the obvious alternatives. I-04’s lower-bound net (~€3,550) is the largest robust direct benefit in the set, yet it is deferred while I-09—the sparsest, most poorly calibrated segment—is committed now. Urgency and option value can justify that, but only if shown. There is also no 2-slot vs 3-slot vs 4-slot comparison, even though the base case is only marginally positive after spillover and the adverse case is net negative. If adverse is plausible, using three slots plus a reserve is not implied; it is an untested preference. I-06, if the imputation is real, has no pre-assigned claim on the reserve versus the regulatory arrival.

**Operational realism is thinner than the rule claims.** “Four slots” plus two specialists implies sequencing, not a simultaneous portfolio. I-09 (2.5 h, diagnosis started) and I-02 (3.0 h) cannot both be swarmed first. A deployable rule would order them, state whether a swarm occupies both specialists, and map 3×45 min onto the 12:00–15:00 regulatory window. None of that is specified. The 2.0 h feasibility cutoff and the €800 conservative-net bar are arbitrary and look fitted to today’s table (I-10 at ~€720 is just below). Spillover harm in € uses an invented €15–20k bystander value with no sensitivity; the “marginally positive” base case hangs on that number.

**Uncertainty is named more often than it is allowed to change the math.** Strategic over-prediction is used to haircut `p` but not the effect range, even though you cannot harvest an absolute reduction that is not there. Public Sector is treated as well calibrated despite a moderate sample. I-09 is flagged as sparse, then still given Slot C with only a “brief” pre-check, which sits poorly next to the fairness rule “never auto-swarm or auto-deny on this flag alone.” Effect ranges are handled as scenario bounds, which is right, but they are still treated as if the midpoint were a usable expected causal effect.

**Sections 4–6 are the best part and still not tight.** Cool-down, suppression, and re-entry are operational, and the I-05 vs I-07 contrast is the correct one. Fairness safeguards are concrete (segment cap, treatment logging, IPW, tie-break randomization, spillover by segment). Monitoring covers the five required layers. Remaining weaknesses: no power/sample-size honesty (a few swarms per day will not identify heterogeneous effects quarterly, especially Non-Native-Language Support), RDD on cool-down is waved at rather than specified, and “change the rule if bias shifts 0.03” is a knob without an error-rate or ownership model.

**Landmark tests.** I-03 is refused; I-05 is suppressed; I-07 is re-opened on new evidence and still not swarmed; untreated-queue harm is in the impact table. Those are the items that separate a serious answer from a `p_now` ranking. The score stays in the low-to-mid 7s because of the I-08 sign error, unauditable formulas, missing portfolio-size and I-04/I-09 comparison, assumed bystander values, and the gap between a “deployable” rule and an actual 12:00 schedule.
