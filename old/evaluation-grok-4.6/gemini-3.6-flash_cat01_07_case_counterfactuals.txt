**5.4**

The write-up is format-complete and conceptually aware — it preserves four-eyes and final-after-last-assessment compliance, treats K-337 as a decision-policy change, and notices that earlier readiness need not mean earlier completion. Those are the right instincts. They are not enough. The core deliverable is *minimal-change, temporally consistent* counterfactuals. On that standard, two of the four cases are internally contradictory, and several headline numbers are unsupported.

**Fatal timeline errors (K-104, K-219).**  
K-104’s intervention does not touch D1 or the assessment pool. Documents still finish Tue 05:40; Tuesday 08:00 A1 wait is ~9h; the factual A1 start Tue 15:10 is already the minimal-change continuation. Moving Assessment to Tue 08:00–11:30 is an unexplained queue jump. That invented start is what makes a Tue 13:00 C1 slot look feasible. It is not. Monday/Tuesday C1 wait is 16–20h; a Monday-morning “Tue afternoon” reservation is either wasted (Assessment cannot be done yet) or it displaces the urgent compliance work the snapshot already flags. The stated complication is the *likely* case under a hold-other-things-fixed reading; the answer buries it as a −4h worst case and advertises −18h to −26h as the expected range. That is not counterfactual reasoning. It is a different process.

K-219 is worse because it disagrees with itself. Originals arrive Mon 17:30. D2 at Mon 16:00 has ~5h wait; Tue 08:00 still has ~4h. A working-hours reading has D2 starting ~Tue 12:00, not Mon 20:00 and not Tue 08:00 with zero queue. D2’s standard band is 4–8h versus factual D1’s 4h ending Tue 13:30, so a clean D2 path can finish *later* than the factual path. The derivation then has D2 also completing Tue 13:30 — i.e. no document-side gain — and still starts A2 at Tue 14:00 instead of the factual Tue 20:00. Same join time cannot produce a 6h earlier assessment. Table (−16h to +20h) and derivation (−16 / −12 / +24) do not match. “D2 can transcribe without error” in Assumptions contradicts the intervention’s stated risk.

**Weaker but material slips.**  
K-337’s path and policy reading are the strongest piece (Assessment 2 → C1 → Decision; no silent ignore). Even there: Doc Check 3 + Assessment 3 is 3.5h + 2.5h = **6h** of internal service, not 8.5h; Friday 11:00 C1 start assumes idle certified capacity the snapshots never show for Friday; incomplete evidence should lengthen, not shorten, compliance; a defensible C1/M1 refusal to sign would recreate delay and is missing from the worst case.

K-411 is the only case that treats downstream absorption honestly (base ~−3.5h, worst 0h). Then Sections 5 and 7 sell “3.5h to 20h” and “immediate cycle-time savings.” A Tue 19:15 managerial signature is calendar fiction. D2 starting Tue 11:30 also understates the 4h Tuesday document queue.

**Feasibility model and classification.**  
The partial order is a useful sketch, not a model: `Triage < Originals Receipt` is not a normative rule; iterative rework, reaffirmed compliance, and early-but-not-final compliance are underspecified. Constraint 2 lost its intersection/empty-set symbols. Calling K-411 “timing only … without changing the sequence” is false — enabling `{I, D}` is a control-flow change. K-104 is capacity reservation plus extra advisory work, not merely routing.

**Scale, ranking, calibration.**  
The 100-case brief is asked to apply *the same policy to 100 similar cases*. For K-104 that is 100 C1 reservations against one certified reviewer, not a 30% subplot and a made-up “+40%.” Timestamp-vs-true-concurrency is required and is reduced to arrival batching. Ranking K-337 second on the back of 90–115h cycle-time cuts overweight speed relative to the rubric (quality, spillover, learnability). K-104 last is fair; calling it *the* most dangerous intervention while treating unresolved high-risk discrepancies as a clean reversible policy is one-sided. Invented sharp thresholds (85% utilization, 3% defects, 80% reservation efficiency) are presented as if measured.

**What still works.**  
Required headings and table schemas are followed. Rework is not treated as a free time-save. Adverse local effects (C1 starvation, D2 clarification, A2 surge, audit trace) are named. K-411’s overnight-queue argument and the K-337 decision-shift are the parts another judge should keep. The assumption list is labeled but omits the load-bearing ones the text actually uses (after-hours service, honor of provisional slots, evening M1 availability).

Right envelope, two broken clocks. That is a mid-fail against a near-flawless bar.
