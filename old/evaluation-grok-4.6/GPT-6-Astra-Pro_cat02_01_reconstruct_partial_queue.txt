**7.6**

This is a strong process-mining reconstruction: missing starts are treated as interval-valued, OPEN work is elapsed rather than completed, queues are not dropped at 14:00, S1 completion is S2 arrival, and Little’s Law is not forced into a transient window. Many headline numbers are internally consistent (completed totals 2050, unfinished age 1375, S1/S2 areas 2085/1340, busy 595/560, period rates summing to the full-window means). The grade is held well below the top of the scale because of avoidable unclarities, a reproducibility miss, and a few places where the main tables overstate tightness.

**What is actually good.** A1–A8 are usable working assumptions, not decoration. Half-open reported-time state, coupled wait/service for each missing start, and the P11 constraint (C2 after P08, not after 11:05) are the right ideas. Censoring is handled correctly: P15/P17/P21 contribute elapsed service to busy time; P16/P18–P20/P22 contribute accrued wait; none of those are treated as finished durations. The bottleneck section correctly separates an early S1 arrival surge from later S2 congestion, treats the C2 outage as a local amplifier, and flags station-level non-FIFO (P09 vs P08, P17 vs P16) and the I2/P22 idle gap without inventing a policy. The area identity \(N=Q+B\) and the demonstration that \( (14/6)\times(70/60)=2.72 \) misses the unfinished S2 mass are the right finite-window check.

**Reproducibility and format (largest hit).** The required PM4Py path is not shown to have run. A `try/except ImportError` plus a sandbox path and a GitHub footnote do not satisfy “demonstrate `insert_case_service_waiting_time` and `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate`.” The pandas reconstruction appears to have been executed (the printed figures match a hand rebuild), but the mandated case-level helpers are speculative. The extra kwargs (`start_timestamp_key`, `arrival_rate_column`, `finish_rate_column`) may not match current PM4Py signatures; if they do not, the `else` branch fails. Zero-duration “External Arrival” markers are a reasonable workaround, but they are unlabeled in any executed output.

**The main metrics table is tighter than the analysis behind it.** Section 4 reports S1 utilization **74.3–84.0%** and S2 **73.0–80.9%**. Those are missing-start-only ranges with timestamps glued to nominal values. Section 6 later opens the same quantities to roughly **61.5–96.9%** and **63.8–89.4%** once rounding and resource order are optimized. Both can be true, but “plausible utilization range” in the station table is then misleading. The late-limit bound also drives busy time with **zero-length mandatory activities**; that is disclosed in A6, yet it is still used as the lower utilization endpoint as if it were an operational scenario.

**“Range” is overloaded.** In the case table, `[20–50]` is a feasible start interval. In the station table, `25.3–28.8` is the range of **means across imputations**, not the spread of service times (fully observed S1 services are about 20–30 minutes). A reader can easily take the latter as a case-level summary. Waiting-time “means” further exclude still-waiting cases; that is stated, but the headline S2 wait **37.2** is then an average over starts, not a lower bound on eventual wait for the 20 S2 arrivals (the unfinished waits 85/50/35/15 already pull a presence-weighted figure higher).

**Small logical/wording cracks.** (1) A3 says simultaneous reported events are applied atomically so they do not create artificial peaks; §6 says true 11:30 order could produce S1 WIP 9. Both are defensible if split into reported-time vs actual-time, but that split is not made cleanly. (2) The capacity sketch cites fully observed means 28.1 and 40, then divides 120 by the **central** completed means (~28.3, 37.5) to get 4.25/h and 3.2/h. (3) The 5-minute “central” start is a handover heuristic, then treated as the primary point estimate in §3–§4. (4) Period queue means are given as 3.25/0.63 etc. with no imputation or rounding band. (5) C2 outage classification in the LP uses **nominal** end/start to decide before vs after maintenance (safe on this log, not a general method). (6) Mean-WIP envelopes clip arrivals at 08:00, which sits awkwardly next to A1’s “P01 may already be initial WIP.”

**What is not wrong.** Treating missing starts as 0–max rather than a single fill; not using completed cases alone for utilization; not claiming a single all-day bottleneck; not subtracting 15 minutes from every wait as a maintenance counterfactual; not applying steady-state \(L=\lambda W\) to a system that starts empty, absorbs a surge, loses C2, then starves of arrivals.

The reconstruction is competent and mostly numerically trustworthy. Under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless: the official station metrics under-communicate uncertainty, the required PM4Py demonstration is unrun and possibly API-incorrect, and several headline “ranges” are easy to misread.
