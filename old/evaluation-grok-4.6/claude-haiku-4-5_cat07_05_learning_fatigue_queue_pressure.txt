**6.2**

The answer is structurally complete, follows the required headings, and correctly refuses a single-cause story. The core narrative—early case-mix and senior selection, mid-shift stabilization, late speed-up as a WIP-driven speed–quality tradeoff—is the right one, and the intervention/ethics sections correctly reject punishment and “improve throughput by dropping QC.” That is not enough to save the grade. Under a strict reading, this is a polished write-up built on too many misreads of the very tables it claims to interpret.

**Factual attribution errors are the main failure.** Sequence 31–50 is not the “lowest service time” (12.9 min); 76+ is 10.8. Lowest error is 16–30 (2.5%), not 31–50 (2.7%). Sequence 1–5 queue 46 is not the “highest” queue; 76+ is 88. Night 15.2 min / 5.1% and Day 10.9 min / 7.5% are last-two-hour figures, not shift averages. The 7.5% vs 6.8% comparison is treated as if Day and Late were being measured in the same 14:00–17:00 window; they are not (Day last two hours are 15:00–17:00; Late last two hours are 20:00–22:00). Junior first-20 error of 5.1% is not “even on low-complexity cases.” The 2.1% / 5.8% pair is the WIP×complexity table, not early vs late simple cases. The 4.4-minute “complexity normalization” effect is not derived from the numbers cited (22.4 vs 12.7 is 9.7 minutes, and those cells are not sequence-specific). The 9100/18300 “50%” share does not correspond to any coherent denominator.

**Section 5 contains an inversion that contradicts Section 2.** It says early-shift cases are “simpler (mean complexity 0.72 vs. 0.43 late).” 0.72 is *more* complex. The same section (and the dashboard) assigns 8.2% rework to early shift; 8.2% is the 51–75 bin. Early rework is 6.1%. A fairness argument that garbles the selection pattern it is warning about is not a small slip.

**Several causal claims do not follow from the clues.** Sequences 1–30 and 51–75 cannot be “the same clock times in a single shift” (≈22–151 min vs ≈378 min). That sentence is used to dismiss fatigue; the conclusion may still be right, but the test is invalid. “Corrections of Bot_Risk rise when queue > 70 because oversight is deferred” is backwards: more corrections under high queue is not a deferral signature. Senior 15.9→11.8 is labeled “learning” even while the paper’s own thesis is that later speed is largely pressure.

**The PM4Py/analysis code would not do what it claims.** Service time as `max(minutes_since_shift_start)−min(...)` by case is case span, not event service time; `get_service_time` / `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are unused. `filter_event_attribute_values(..., lambda x: x < 0.5)` is not the API. `mixedlm(..., family=Binomial())` is not a valid statsmodels model. `discover_batches` is not a list of DataFrames with `.iloc`. Dashboard code overwrites `queue_length_at_start` first/last, computes completion rate off a RangeIndex after `reset_index()`, and passes `family='binomial'` as a string to `glm`. Required waiting-time monitoring is missing; queue length is not a substitute.

**What remains good does not offset that.** The mechanism table, intervention risk register (especially 3, 6, 7, 8), and the warning that management-created WIP is then blamed on workers are the strongest parts. They are also exactly the parts that depend on getting the tables right. A nearly flawless answer would have stratified without inventing cells, used event-level service/waiting times, and not reversed complexity or rework. This is a capable essay with too many invented or swapped numbers to score higher.
