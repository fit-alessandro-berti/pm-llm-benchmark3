**6.4**

This is a competent, well-structured policy memo that clears the main traps (no slot for I-03; I-05 suppressed vs I-07 re-entered; calibration, spillovers, and feedback loops are actually used). It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has a real logic error, an internal ranking contradiction, a scoring rule that cannot be executed as written, and several operational holes that a live duty manager would hit before 13:00.

## What is actually sound

The hard gates are the right ones: stage eligibility, no spend after `Diagnosis Complete`, no re-queue of unchanged 30-minute refreshes, ARR treated as an uncertain suggested effect rather than a CATE, and `p_now` never used as a standalone threshold. The `p*` offsets (SME +0.06, SE −0.08, Public Sector unchanged, NNLS interval widening) match the stated backtests and are correctly described as ranking corrections, not causal effects. Refusing I-03 despite `p_now = 0.82` and €40k is the correct structural call. Holding a fourth slot, making I-06 conditional on restoring the live queue field, and waiting I-04/I-08 on clock, false-alarm burden, and weak/negative robust EV are all coherent. Section 3’s three-scenario ledger is mostly auditable and, importantly, lets the adverse case go net-negative on both breaches and money — that is the actual reason three slots beat four. Section 4’s I-05 vs I-07 contrast is the distinction the prompt asked for. Sections 5–6 propose concrete controls (action-conditioned labels, near-tie logging, spillover cap, segment ECE slices) rather than slogans.

## Decision rule: stated as deployable, not actually deployable

The index \(S = \mathrm{ARR}_{\mathrm{rob}} V - 450 - B_{\mathrm{FA}} + U - C - \lambda Q\) is never parameterized. \(U\), \(C\), and \(\lambda\) have no units, no default values, and no worked example, yet Section 2 publishes scores of 81, 76, 73, 64, 62, 58, 45, 40 as if they came from that formula. A second operator cannot reproduce the table. That is a failure of the thing Section 1 claims to be: a deployable rule.

The eligibility text also promises a “narrow urgency override” to the \(\underline{\mathrm{EV}} > 0\) gate and then never defines it. I-07 happens to clear the gate (~€500), so the omission is latent today and still a hole in the standing policy.

The spillover hurdle “mid ARR ≲ 0.12 ⇒ unjustified” compares a probability to a probability and ignores that 0.12 extra untreated breaches are not commensurate with one treated case’s ARR unless untreated breach values are specified. The score tries to put this in euros via \(\lambda Q\), but again \(\lambda\) is unspecified, so the hurdle and the index can disagree.

## Capacity table: one contradiction and a bad first rank

**I-01 vs I-04 is inconsistent.** Watch scores are I-04 = 62 > I-01 = 58, but the reserve claimant order is I-06, then I-01, with an explicit instruction not to put the fourth slot on I-04. If the reasons for preferring I-01 (better calibration, higher ARR, lower disruption, 7 h vs 10 h) are decisive, I-01 should outscore I-04. If the score is decisive, I-04 is the next claimant. The memo does both.

**I-07 as top act-now case is weakly justified.** Lower-bound EV is the smallest among selected cases (~€500). After even one slot’s base spillover (≈0.12 extra breaches), I-07 is likely net-negative in isolation. It is still in Triage with 1.5 h left and four stages after diagnosis. The 45-minute swarm can start immediately and still leave almost no path to `Closed`. The supplied ARR band is the only reason to believe breach is still avoidable; the memo never checks remaining-horizon feasibility against that band. Ranking I-07 (81) above I-09 (73) and I-02 (76) — both already in `Diagnosis Started`, both with much larger robust EV — is fairness rhetoric standing in for a scored rule. A fairness floor that blocks a long-clock high-\(V\) wait case from pre-empting I-07 is defensible. A fairness floor that makes I-07 the first swarm is not implied by the rule as written.

**Specialist calendar is missing.** Two people deliver one 45-minute swarm. Three planned swarms are 135 minutes if sequential. Starting I-07 at 12:00 pushes I-09’s swarm to ~12:45 (≈1.0 h of SLA left) and I-02 to ~13:30 (≈1.5 h left), and leaves the regulatory reserve only after ~14:15, which is a thin hedge on “before 15:00.” If I-06 confirms at 12:45 and consumes the reserve, the political option is gone while seniors are still mid-rotation. None of this is scheduled. For a prompt that scores operational realism, that is a material omission, not a formatting nit.

I-06-as-verify, I-03-ineligible, I-05-suppressed, and I-08-wait are the right calls.

## Expected-impact table: arithmetic mostly holds; framing is slightly generous

Optimistic 0.65–0.84 avoided and €9.9k–€13.1k gross are correct for the upper halves. Base 0.26+0.15+0.24 = 0.65 and €9.85k are correct. Adverse 0.20+0.08+0 with FA still charged is a fair stress (zeroing I-09 is the costly identification failure). Cost €1,350 and FA €1,450 are correct.

Issues: (i) untreated breach values are unidentified; the €8k–€25k mix is invented and then used as if it supports a net range; (ii) optimistic net “+€3k to +€9k” rounds the low end up from about +€2.5k; (iii) base “breakeven to +€3k” does not match €2k–€9k spillover against €7.05k after cost, which is closer to +€5k to −€2k; (iv) FA is subtracted as certain, which is conservative, but never distinguished from the case in which the swarm is correctly targeted and the listed “false-alarm burden” should not fully apply. Approximate ranges were allowed; claiming an auditable table and then smoothing the net columns in the favorable direction is still a defect.

## Repeated-alert policy

The I-05 / I-07 contrast is correct and clearly written. Cool-down of 90 minutes and a 0.08 `p*` move are arbitrary, with no sensitivity to 60 vs 120 minutes or 0.05 vs 0.10. More importantly, I-07’s `p` *fell* (0.75 → 0.72) after the new log. That is also consistent with the new evidence being reassuring. The memo treats the artifact as automatically swarm-useful and does not say what would count as a new log that should *not* break cool-down. Re-entry after a swarm is otherwise tight enough.

## Fairness: one clear logic error

Value-starvation, NNLS exclusion, label contamination, and spillover incidence on untreated (often quieter) work are the right risks, and most safeguards are specific enough to implement.

The calibration paragraph is wrong where it matters. Uncorrected SME underprediction and SE overprediction cause the policy to **over-swarm SE and under-swarm SME**. Naive \(V \times \mathrm{ARR}\) ranking does the **same** thing (I-04/I-08 over I-07). Those distortions compound; they are not “the opposite of the raw-value bias.” That sentence is a concrete reasoning error, not a wording quibble, and it sits in the section that is supposed to show the author understands selection bias.

Smaller points: requiring an “NNLS-capable senior” is not checked against the two available specialists; the daily low-\(V\) slot floor can collide with the three-slot spillover cap and is not ranked against that cap; “freeze SE-only expansion until ECE < ~0.05” ignores that SE is already outside that band, so the freeze should already be on.

## Monitoring

This is the strongest section: untreated-prefix recalibration, action flags on every prefix, near-tie quasi-experiments, holdout only on Watch-tier, spillover measured on the six designated untreated diagnosis cases, and explicit rule-change triggers. Residual problems: trigger (1) “segment ECE > 0.05 for two weeks” is already true for SE, and the memo never says the current rule is therefore provisional; stepped-wedge/20% holdout on Watch cases is asserted without a sample-size or contamination analysis; there is no pre-registered stopping rule for the I-06-style imputation slice. Those are gaps, not empty generalities.

## Net

The memo would be a high 7 or low 8 if the scores were derived, I-01/I-04 were consistent, I-07 were sequenced rather than crowned, the urgency override existed, and calibration were not described as opposing value bias. Those are not polish items. They are the difference between a persuasive write-up and a rule someone can run at 12:00 without improvising. Hence 6.4, not 8+.
