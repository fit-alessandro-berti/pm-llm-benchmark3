The answer has the right skeleton and hits the three surface “strong answer” checks (I-03 ineligible, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers mentioned). Under a strict decision-theoretic and operational reading it is not deployable. The scoring rule is the wrong quantity, the capacity plan is physically impossible, and the impact table is arithmetically and conceptually wrong.

## Decision-theoretic failures

The stated score is not the object the prompt asked for. Benefit should be driven by **absolute risk reduction × breach value**, not `adjusted p_now × value`. The prompt already warns that `p` is observational and is not the intervention effect. Ranking by calibrated `p × V` is a dressed-up probability rank: it systematically prefers high-`p`, low-value, high-effect-range cases (I-07, I-01) over lower-`p`, high-value cases (I-04). That is the central allocation error.

The score formula is also not a formula. Operators are missing:

`Score = (Adjusted p_now × Value)  (False-alarm burden + Swarm cost)  (Spillover harm estimate)`

A “deployable rule” that cannot be computed is not deployable. The €5k / €2k tier cuts are therefore undefined.

Spillover is attached to the **treated case’s own value**. Spillover lands on untreated diagnosis-stage cases. Using the target’s value as the externality is the wrong incidence.

NNLS “lower half of effect ranges (0.05–0.15)” is invented. I-09’s supplied range is 0.18–0.30; the lower half is not 0.05–0.15.

## Internal contradictions in the rule

The persistence rule requires `p_now` to rise by 0.10 **and** a new event, else 2-hour suppression. I-07’s `p` **falls** (0.75 → 0.72). Under the written rule it is suppressed. It is then selected for a slot. The I-05 / I-07 distinction is narratively correct and formally broken.

Calibration is applied inconsistently. Public Sector is defined as unadjusted, then I-01 is scored as `0.61 + 0.065 = 0.675` (the SME offset). I-05’s non-intervention rationale cites a **€2,000** false-alarm burden; I-05’s burden is €100 (I-04’s is €2,000).

Section 5 then reverses the SME bias: underprediction is said to cause **over**-intervention. If the model underpredicts SME risk, raw-`p` policy under-intervenes. The text had already added +0.065 to SME `p`, which is the right direction for risk, and then treats that correction as a reason to raise the SME bar. The fairness section does not know what its own calibration means.

The NNLS safeguard is also backwards: capping spillover harm at 50% makes uncertain NNLS cases **more** attractive.

## Capacity allocation is infeasible and poorly chosen

Four slots exist. The portfolio spends all four **and** reserves one. That is not a policy; it is an arithmetic refusal of the constraint. If the regulatory option value is real, the maximum intervene-set is three.

The four names are also a weak robust set:

| Case | Why the choice is weak |
|---|---|
| I-07 | Mid EV ≈ 0.26 × €5k − €500 ≈ **€800** before spillover. One slot’s spillover (≈0.06–0.18 extra breach-mass on six untreated cases) can erase that entirely. |
| I-01 | Public Sector, 7h remaining, mid EV ≈ **€1.7k**. Option value of waiting is ignored. Wrong calibration boost. |
| I-06 | Eligible, but the only sharp rise is flagged as possible imputation artifact. Spending a scarce slot as “Tier 1 intervene” is not robust. |
| I-02 | Defensible on stage/urgency, but SE overprediction plus a modest effect band (0.08–0.22) is not treated as a reason to shrink the set. |

Meanwhile I-04’s lower-bound EV is already ≈ `0.10 × 60k − 2,450 ≈ €3.6k` (mid ≈ €9k) and it is still in Triage with 10h left. Waiting can be justified by option value; **replacing it with I-07 is not**. I-09 is in `Diagnosis Started` with 2.5h left — waiting can mean missing the only effective window — yet it is parked in Tier 2 on a made-up effect band.

Lower-bound EVs that a robust rule should have looked at, approximately:

- I-04 ~€3.6k, I-09 ~€2.4k, I-06 ~€1.5k (data-quality discount), I-02 ~€1.0k, I-01 ~€0.9k, I-10 ~€0.7k, I-07 ~€0.5k, I-08 ~−€0.5k

A coherent 3-slot + 1-reserve book would not contain I-07.

Timing of the 45-minute swarm versus 1.5h (I-07) and specialist preemption (two people, four swarms) is not analyzed. Queue externalities are named, then not allowed to change the headcount.

## Expected-impact table is not auditable

Optimistic “4 breaches avoided” and “€54k” are the sum of **case counts and full breach values**, not `Σ Δp × V`. Upper-bound reductions on the selected set are 0.35+0.22+0.30+0.32 = **1.19 expected breaches**, value ≈ €3.15k+€5.5k+€4.5k+€1.6k = **~€14.8k**, not €54k. That is not rounding; it is the wrong random variable.

The footnote makes it worse: “`adjusted p_now × effect` exceeds a 50% threshold” is not P(avoid breach). Effect already **is** the absolute reduction.

Base “2–3 breaches” / “€34k” and Adverse “1 breach / €9k (I-01 only)” repeat the same category error and then arbitrarily zero-out cases instead of applying the lower bounds (sum ≈ 0.61). Adverse net “~€10k to €27k” does not follow from “€9k − €1.8k − €18–36k”, which is negative. Spillover lines omit ×(number of slots) in one row and inflate `0.03×6×avg V` to €18–36k in another; `avg value` is never fixed. Optimistic “High” confidence on a book that includes I-06 imputation and I-07’s thin EV is unjustified.

False-alarm burden is in the prompt’s EV template and mostly disappears from the table.

## Temporal policy

The I-05 vs I-07 **story** is right and is the one place the answer clearly meets a stated quality bar. The **rule** still fails I-07, and “suppress indefinitely or until 0.70” is two different policies.

“Permanently ineligible if the case breached after a prior swarm” does not address the training-label feedback loop it claims to fix. For the same incident, post-breach swarming is already pointless. For later incidents it is an arbitrary ban. Escalation on “Diagnosis Started > 4 hours” confuses time-in-stage with time-to-SLA (I-06’s 4.0h is the latter).

## Fairness

The five named risks are the right list. The safeguards are either contradictory or non-operational:

- SME “stricter because underpredicted” fights Section 1.
- NNLS spillover cap fights conservatism.
- `p_now × time-to-deadline` as a spillover fix **favors long-horizon cases** and contradicts the live allocation.
- A 1-slot/week high-`p` carve-out is a different problem than today’s four slots.
- 10% withhold is directionally right; with four slots it needed a cost.

Non-monetary obligations (public-sector duty, regulatory arrival) are named via the reserve, then the reserve is not taken.

## Monitoring

This is the strongest section (cadence, segment backtests, some experimental language, queue-adaptive capacity). It is still not tight enough to rescue the memo:

- DiD “matched on `p_now`” after a value-and-stage policy remains confounded; a case has no clean pre-period.
- “If >30% of swarms show no breach reduction” is not identifiable unit-wise; the counterfactual is missing.
- Treated/untreated breach ratio > 1.5 is selection, not fairness; high-risk cases were the ones treated.

## Bottom line

The memo looks like a policy and fails as one: ineligible I-03 is the easy call; the hard calls (effect vs `p`, 3+reserve vs 4+reserve, I-04/I-09 vs I-07/I-06, honest `ΣΔp` accounting) are wrong or contradictory. Under the requested strictness that is a low-4, not a mid-range pass.
