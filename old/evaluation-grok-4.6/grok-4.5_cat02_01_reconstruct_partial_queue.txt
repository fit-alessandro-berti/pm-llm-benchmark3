**5.8**

The write-up looks like a strong process-mining analysis until the numbers are checked. Assumptions, formulas, missing-start logic, and the bottleneck story are mostly competent. The required metrics, however, contain several factual errors and one internal contradiction that should have been caught.

## What is actually solid

Assumptions A1–A10 are explicit and usable: end-before-start on a resource, rounding as a band rather than a second imputation layer, no silent single fill for `MISSING`, OPEN elapsed as a lower bound, and station sojourn point-identified when only the start is missing. That last identity is the right one.

Case-level waits and services are largely correct. Feasible windows for P05 / P07 / P11 / P15 match the resource chains (C1 after P03, I1 after P05, C2 after P08, I1 after P13). Completed sojourns for P01–P06 and P08–P14 are right. Censoring is not swept aside. Utilization includes OPEN elapsed time. Section 5 correctly describes an S1-first morning, a C2-outage shock, then a joint constraint, and it labels hypotheses. Section 6 is honest about what is stable. The Python block hits the eight required items and correctly refuses to treat PM4Py case-level helpers as station queue metrics.

## Errors that sink the score

**Wrong completed sojourn for P07.** 11:00 − 08:45 = 135 min, not 155. The P05–P08 list `110, 135, 155, 180` skips P07 and inserts P09’s sojourn. The reported mean ≈148 is therefore wrong (correct is ≈146).

**Period counts do not match the data.**
- S2 completions in 08:00–10:00 are **3** (P01–P03). P04 ends 10:05. The station table’s “4–5” and the rate table’s `4/2 = 2.0/h` are both false.
- S1 mid completions are **6 or 7**, not 8; late finished S1 visits are **6**, not 5.
- External arrivals are not 14 / 6 / 2. P21 arrives at **11:50**, which is mid-window, not late. A consistent partition is 13/8/1 or 14/7/1 depending on whether 10:00 is closed. The Python (`[lo, hi)`) would print 13 / 8 / 1 and contradict the prose.

**WIP is reconstructed incorrectly and contradicts the rest of the memo.** Column text says queue+service. At 14:00 the authors themselves count S2 as 2 in service + 4 waiting = **6**, then report S2 peak WIP “~4–5”. S1 peak is about **7–8** from ~09:35 through late morning (e.g. 11:00: P11–P12 in service, P13–P18 in queue), not 4–5. Mean S1 WIP “3.0–3.5” is inconsistent with their own waits: λ ≈ 3.67/h and mean S1 wait ~65 min already imply Lq ≈ 4, plus ~1.7 busy ≈ **5.5–6**. They never show a WIP path in §§1–6 despite requiring one.

**Utilization ranges are not the ranges they defined.** Central busy minutes (I1 315, I2 290, C1 300, C2 270) are fine. Pessimistic late starts cut P07/P15/P05/P11 service by ~30+30+25+20 = **105** min, not “~30–40”. I1 can fall to ~255 min (U ≈ 0.71); C1 to ~275 (U ≈ 0.76). Reported bands `I1+I2 … 0.80–0.92` and `C1 … 0.83–0.88` are central-only numbers dressed up as ranges.

**“Three” imputation scenarios are two.** Optimistic and central are defined to be the same start. A midpoint of each feasible interval was the obvious distinct central case and was not used.

**§7 is not the source of §§3–6.** The WIP profiler, if run, would not have produced peak S1 WIP 4–5. Bounds are hardcoded rather than derived from the resource calendar. That is acceptable only if the narrative matches the code; it does not.

## Smaller defects (still counted)

P18–P20 S2 waits are residual waits but only P16 is marked “so far”. Several operators were dropped in prose (`S2 end external arrival`, `svc 5`). A1’s “conservative” tie-break is described as arrivals last, then the snapshot includes those arrivals. Full-window Little’s Law is treated with appropriate caution; the early-window check is too loose to be more than a gesture, and they never close the loop against station-level L = λW using their own waits.

## Net

This is not a weak student sketch. It is a complete-looking report whose core queue/WIP/rate/utilization aggregates are wrong in ways that matter for the assigned task. Under a hypercritical reading, that is well below “nearly flawless.”
