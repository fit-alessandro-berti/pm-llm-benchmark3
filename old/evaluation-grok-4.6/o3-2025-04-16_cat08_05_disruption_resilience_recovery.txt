**5.4**

The answer is well organized and hits every required section, but it is not a high-quality process-mining analysis. It repeatedly invents numbers, misreads the DFGs, overstates recovery, and then treats those inventions as evidence. Under a strict rubric that rewards grounding, temporal accuracy, and a clean resilience/noncompliance distinction, that is disqualifying.

**Grounding failures (most serious).** Large parts of the adaptation table are not in the log, DFGs, or notes, yet they are written as findings: “16% of fast-lane orders skipped electronic auth,” “+8% throughput for Ambient,” “no auth violations found in sample,” “0 CS diversion events,” “sample QC 98%,” “missed temp on 1.3%,” “cut dock dwell 40%,” “5,200 items,” “book-stock to 99.7%,” “1,400 lines/day,” and “no regulator finding yet.” Those are not abductive inferences; they are fabricated precision. Several also contradict the DFG. Skip-authorization (1,600) is a sibling of the hospital fast lane (1,000), not a 16% subset of it. Reconcile-manual-reservation is 1,400 cases across T4 (Day 7–21), not 1,400 lines per day. Dispatch-without-scan is 1,800 + 1,450 = 3,250, and phone POD is 2,600; neither is “5,200 missing scans.” The 2,600 figure is reused for missing scans, which conflates two different events.

**Temporal reasoning is only partly sound.** T0–T2 framing is mostly right (conformance 91% → 44%, hospital fill 91%, control gaps jumping to 9.5% / 4.8% / 2.9%). T1 “throughput instantly falls to near zero” is not in the data. T3 “3–5× baseline” is wrong: 5.4 / 0.8, 2.3 / 0.4, and 1.4 / 0.2 are about 6–7×. T4 is the biggest miss: pharmacy median 28 h vs 23 h and p90 74 h vs 49 h, wholesale 58 h / 120 h vs 36 h / 72 h, traceability 2.2% vs 0.8%, rework 5.8% vs 3.5%. Calling this “almost baseline” erases the segment that actually absorbed the shock. Wholesale-bulk and the T2 pharmacy fill rate of 61% are nearly absent from the timeline, so the before/during/after story is hospital-centric and incomplete.

**Resilience vs concealed noncompliance is conceptually started, then blurred.** The sentence that resilience requires mission continuity *plus* immutable controls is the right test, and retiring C and G is defensible. The classification then fails the problem’s own constraint. K and L are labeled “true resilience.” They are after-the-fact reconstruction and a hidden workload; the prompt explicitly says audit remediation is not real-time compliance. E is also called true resilience “when paired with later scan,” which is exactly the reconstruction pattern the log shows as a traceability debt. F is treated as resilient for CS while the same control missed cold-chain documentation; that belongs in the ambiguous bucket. The unused DFG split is more informative than the invented QC rates: 1,800 orders went pack → dispatch-without-scan and never even hit temporary release.

**Recovery redesign is usable but internally inconsistent.** Triggers, a minimum-controls matrix, time-bounded snapshot use, risk-weighted backlog, and a 5-day audit SLA are the right objects. The model then holds any shipment that cannot offline-scan, which collides with the hospital-critical mission the rest of the memo prioritizes. T3 (intake up, inventory/scanning unreliable) is not designed as its own mode, even though that is where hybrid risk actually sat. The 6-hour snapshot rule is a reasonable design choice, but earlier it was implied as an empirical finding.

**Indicators and recommendations are the strongest parts, still thin.** Leading/lagging coverage of fill, control completeness, backlog age, rework, and fairness is appropriate. The fairness index (pharmacy median ÷ hospital median) ignores wholesale, fill, and cancellation, which is where inequity actually appears. Recommendations are prioritized and actionable, but they inherit the same overconfidence: several “low residual risk” claims assume the invented compensating controls work.

**Trade-off paragraph is the cleanest writing in the piece** and roughly correct: accept documented, time-bounded adaptations that protect hospitals; reject unauthorized or unscanned release. It cannot rescue the analysis, because the evidence offered for what is “documented and reconciled” was not in the case.

Net: competent exam structure, weak forensic discipline. A nearly flawless answer would stay inside the DFG counts, keep T4 degradation visible, and refuse to count K/L as resilience. This one does not.
