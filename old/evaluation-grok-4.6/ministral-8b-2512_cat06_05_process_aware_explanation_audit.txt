**4.7**

The answer has the right skeleton and catches the obvious full-trace leaks, but it fails the central audit: it systematically misunderstands what can exist at `t0`, smuggles future process outcomes back into the “revised” explanations, and misses required coverage. Under a strict reading of the brief, those are not nits.

## Core conceptual failure: `t0` is immediately after first Coverage Check Completed

The prefix is `Claim Registered → Coverage Check Completed`. Documents, damage assessment, first settlement, appeal, and closure are **future-possible**, not prefix-observed. The answer repeatedly treats them as if they were already on the prefix.

That error contaminates every later section.

- `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` is labeled a **“legitimate prefix signal” (low)**. It is process-legal on a **complete** trace (appeal can trigger a second decision) and **inadmissible at `t0`**. The quality bar asked for exactly this distinction versus the impossible `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed` pattern. The answer treated them differently, but in the wrong way: it promoted the appeal pattern into a prefix feature.
- Remediation `appeal_triggered_at_t0` / “if Partial/Deny recorded by `t0`” is process-impossible. Settlement cannot precede coverage-check completion.
- `Documents Requested → Documents Received` and `Coverage Check Completed → Fraud Investigation Opened` are also called valid prefix signals. Immediately after coverage check they are typically not yet realized; as sequence-explainer patterns they are almost certainly full-trace associations.
- `prefix_variant_id = REG-COV-DOC-DOC-DMG` is not a prefix at `t0`. It is the leaked full variant with the tail chopped off. That still encodes post-`t0` document cycles and assessment.

Other Section 1 problems:

- `total_case_duration_hours` should be **dropped**, not “replaced” by `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, which already exists as rank 10.
- Suggesting `fraud_investigation_opened` as a substitute for `appeal_seen_anywhere` is a category error and a new leak risk.
- `Claim Closed → Damage Assessment Completed` is reduced to timestamp ambiguity. It is also **normatively impossible** (closed before assessment). That is the contrast with `Appeal → Settlement`.
- `queue_length_at_t0` is over-penalized (medium, “minus 5 minutes”). The brief says it is a timestamped snapshot, normally valid; the quality bar says **preserve** congestion signals.
- Comparison operators are stripped in remediations (`initiated  t0`, `feature_timestamp  t0`), so the table is not even operationally precise.

## Section 2 is partly cargo-cult process mining

A validation suite is sketched, but several checks are misnamed or logically wrong.

- The “alignment check” is a timestamp SQL filter, not alignment to a process model.
- “Footprint” is used as “don’t use cross-claim data.” In this setting footprint means activity relations (causality/parallelism), not data scope.
- The appeal/repeated-settlement SQL flags **legitimate** reconsideration (`Settlement` after `Appeal`) rather than testing the actual rule: a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` is semantically valid only after Partial/Deny **and** an appeal; the first decision is not an appeal sequel.
- Enabled vs **impossible** vs **merely future-possible at `t0`** is not operationalized for explanations. That was an explicit required check.
- “A frequent DFG edge is not a normative rule” is never actually argued. Citing a nonstandard “PyWorkflows” library does not substitute for that point.

## Section 3 mixes classes and under-specifies the four standards

Some classifications are fine (`calendar_week` as drift; region as fairness proxy; elapsed as predictive). The rest is inconsistent with `t0`:

- Congestion/process-state is filled with `doc_received_before_t0`, `fraud_investigation_opened_at_t0`, `damage_assessment_completed_before_t0` — features that are ~always false at this prediction time.
- `queue_length_at_t0` is parked under “defensible predictive” rather than process-state/congestion.
- “Unusable until lineage repair” lists only the four cartoon leaks. It should also include `final_handler_team`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0` (until SQL is fixed), `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`.
- The brief asked for different **standards**: predictive validity ≠ causal interpretation ≠ operational actionability ≠ fairness review. The table gestures at “don’t say cause,” but never states those standards cleanly. Actionability is especially thin (queue length can be a control lever; region cannot).

## Section 4 fails the constructive task

This is the highest-weight deliverable. Both selected-case writeups are not credible.

**Case X — `retain` is unjustified.**  
`appeal_seen_anywhere = 1` (+0.26) cannot be true on this prefix. `final_decision = Partial` is an outcome leak and is **never removed**. The revised “credible” list invents `appeal_triggered_at_t0` and the narrative says “a prior Partial decision enabling an appeal.” That **reproduces leaked future outcomes**, which the brief forbids. `final_handler_team` is “removed” even though it was not a Case X local driver. `claimant_channel` is deleted pending fairness review, which violates “do not simply delete every correlated feature.” Queue, complexity, and elapsed are the only defensible leftovers; after stripping leaks the 0.82 score cannot be retained.

**Case Y — `retain` is worse.**  
The dominant driver is the full path `…-DEC-APP-DEC-CLOSE` (+0.31). Status should be **recompute/invalidate**. Using a truncated future variant as `prefix_variant_id` and writing “high risk stems from a multi-document cycle path” is still a future-outcome story. `prior_claim_count` is treated as already “fixed” without recomputing the leaked SQL. Features not in Y’s local explanation (`num_document_requests_total`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`) are listed as removed. Region and reserve can stay as **predictive proxies with caveats**; that part is closer to the brief.

**Case Z is the only locally competent revision.**  
Invalidate + mutable score + last role + missing sequence index is right. Still incomplete: week 52 as holiday/policy proxy, nightly document snapshot contamination, and the **order impossibility** of Closed before Assessment are not in the ambiguity list.

JSON shape matches the schema. Content does not.

## Section 5 hits the topic list, then sets the wrong success metric

Feature timestamps, prefix-only recompute, conformance, calibration, stability, fairness, and drift are all named. Then the plan says target **&lt;5% AUC drop** after removing ranks 1–4 and 7. That is the wrong objective. Those features were the cheat codes; a large AUC drop is expected and desirable. Optimizing for stable AUC after leakage removal is how teams keep leakage. Arbitrary 10%/15% SHAP and event-distribution thresholds, plus tool name-dropping (Feast, AIF360, River), read as checklist fulfillment rather than a process-aware monitoring design. The plan also understates that **the model**, not just the explainer, must be retrained on prefix-only features because current SHAP ranks are themselves distorted by leaked columns.

## What is actually good

Obvious critical leaks are named: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`. Subtler lineage bugs are at least seen: extraction-date SQL on prior claims, 23:00 same-day snapshot, warehouse team without as-of time, ±5 minute queue join, Case Z mutable score. Causal language is mostly restrained. Congestion and prefix age are not discarded. Region is not naively called a “delay driver.” Case Z is appropriately thrown out. Structure matches the five required headings.

That is a mid-competence audit memo, not a reliable one. The revised explanations still tell the business that appeals and later document/assessment paths are early warnings at coverage-check time. That is the failure mode the brief exists to catch.
