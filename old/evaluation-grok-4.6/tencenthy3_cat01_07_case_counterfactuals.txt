**6.7**

The answer is complete, correctly formatted, and gets the hard conceptual points: K-337 is a decision-policy change rather than a timing tweak; earlier readiness is not earlier completion (K-411); queue figures are forecasts; four-eyes and mandatory compliance are preserved; local gains can migrate congestion. Those are the criteria that separate a real counterfactual from a duration subtraction. The write-up is still not close to flawless. Several timestamps, resource-capacity claims, and the K-104 reservation story are internally inconsistent, and a strict process-mining reading has to mark those down.

**What is actually strong**

Section 1 does not smash the four cases into one sequence. Conditionals for originals, RMI, and high-risk compliance are the right structure. The timing / routing / policy split is accurate, especially the K-337 call.

The case table and the four derivations keep mandatory control-flow: Register→Triage; originals before Document Check; Identity ∥ Document when allowed; final Assessment before Compliance on high-risk work; Decision before Notify; A1 ≠ C1. K-337 correctly treats Assessment 2 as the last assessment and still inserts Compliance. K-411 correctly leaves identity start times almost unchanged (I2’s Tuesday queue, not the case lock, was binding) and treats the assessment queue as the residual bottleneck.

Uncertainty is mostly well handled: ranges rather than point estimates, a rework branch on K-219, approve-with-conditions vs reject on K-337, and an explicit “evidence that would reverse” paragraph on each case.

Section 4 names the right interference channels (C1 hoarding, D1→D2 substitution, avoided vs added rework, assessment-queue bunching, weekend/external tails, logged vs true concurrency). Section 7’s pairing — K-411 most robust, K-337 most dangerous for the speed it offers, K-104 least transportable — matches the analysis.

**Defects that keep the score well below the top band**

1. **K-219 start time contradicts the snapshot.** Monday 16:00, D2 still has a 5 h queue. Originals arrive 17:30, so D2 is not free at 17:30–18:00 unless this case jumps the queue. The path still writes “start Mon 17:30 / ~18:00.” That is not a small rounding error; it is the intervention’s first temporal claim. Calling it an “assumption” does not repair a path that is presented as the counterfactual. Downstream “6–14 h saved” inherits that optimism. A consistent path is closer to D2 start ~21:00, finish in a wide Tuesday window, and a smaller, more fragile cycle-time gain.

2. **K-104 reservation is three different mechanisms at once.** The table sequences Preliminary Compliance *after* both checks. The derivation says C1 works on partial files *while* D1 is still running, then also has C1 start Tuesday 06:00 after documents complete Tuesday 05:40, then has final review Wednesday 09:00 because “the slot is reserved.” Monday 08:00 C1 is 20 h; Tuesday 08:00 C1 is 16 h. A reserved slot in that queue is either (a) a place in line ~20 h later, (b) preemption of the two urgent Monday 16:00 cases, or (c) extra C1 hours that violate “no two service intervals at once” unless someone else is displaced. The write-up never picks one and then traces it. Single-case analysis was supposed to hold other system behavior approximately fixed; a free Tuesday-morning C1 interval is exactly the thing that cannot be assumed without naming the displaced work.

3. **K-411 “concurrency” is oversold.** With D2’s Tuesday 08:00 queue at 4 h, D2 becomes free around 12:00–13:00. I2 runs 11:30–13:20. Overlap is on the order of an hour, not a full parallel verification. The qualitative conclusion (assessment still dominates) is right; the path language is not.

4. **The 100-case section is thin relative to the spec.** It is at or under the 250-word floor, and the mandated topics (weekend/external response, timestamps vs true concurrency) are each a clause. “C1 queue +200 h” is an unsourced lump, not a resource calculation. That section is the right shape and the wrong density.

5. **The K-104 pilot contradicts the interference analysis.** Ranking K-104 second for robustness, then proposing a 100-case C1-reservation pilot, ignores the paper’s own finding that 100 reservations *are* the dangerous rollout. A serious pilot on a singleton bottleneck is a small, capped reservation share with an explicit displacement dashboard — not n=100.

6. **Smaller but real nits (they add up under a strict rubric).** Rule 6 allows Compliance, not only Assessment, to issue RMI; the poset omits that. “Processfeasibility check” drops the required hyphen. K-337 and K-411 paths are much less clocked than K-104/K-219. “Rule 7 loop suppressed” is slightly wrong: they avoid *issuing* the second RMI, which means rule 7 never fires — they do not waive a required rework step. Ranking K-104 #2 for robustness while calling it the least transportable policy is reconcilable, but never actually reconciled. M1’s calendar is ignored whenever a Friday/Tuesday evening decision is waved through.

**Net**

This is a competent operations write-up that would pass a normal review. It is not a nearly flawless counterfactual. The two cases that most depend on scarce-resource timing (K-104, K-219) are the two whose clocks do not survive contact with the snapshot table. Under the stated hypercritical standard — minor temporal and capacity inconsistencies count — that is a mid-6s to low-7s paper, not an 8+.
