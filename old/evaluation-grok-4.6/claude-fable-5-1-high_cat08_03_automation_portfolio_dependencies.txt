**7.6**

The answer is strong on TOC-style reasoning, resource arithmetic, loop structure, and the political vs. process trade-off, but it is not nearly flawless. Several claims are internally inconsistent, concurrency is acknowledged then underused, and a few ratings are convenient rather than tight.

**What it gets right.** It does not rank by frequency. Register is correctly treated as a high-count, low-constraint conveyor (~1,600h in a slack 12-FTE pool, 1.5h wait, position 2 in 98% of cases). Analyst demand (~30,000h on 9 FTE), specialist scarcity (3 FTE, two-person handover hub, 31h wait, 82% critical path), and the three DFG loops (FDR↔missing docs 2,800; KYC↔mismatch 1,160; contract↔remediate 510) are used as the real constraints. A11 is last; A3 is gated on A4; A5 is paired with A6 so cleaner inflow does not dump work on analysts; A9 takes a deep slot and satisfies the “one control module” rule; the two-deep-integration cap is applied consistently. Portfolios Q/B/C/R have distinct objective functions and explicit sacrifices. Controls (provisional signing 6.5%, four-eyes 5.8%, contract mismatch 8.5%/14%, audit trail of suggestion/override/final) are operationalized, not recited.

**Logical contradictions.** (1) Portfolio B is said to deliver “slightly better cycle time (A7 instead of A9)” while Portfolio R is also credited with year-1 collateral *re-sequencing* as a zero-module change. If re-sequencing already takes the 58h wait off the critical path, A7’s incremental effect is mostly routing/lateness (19%), not the wait itself—so R should match B on valuation and beat it on the contract path. That comparison cannot be true on the answer’s own premises. (2) A6 is ranked above A5 even though the dependency map says A6 depends on A5 for parseable documents, and A5 without A6 overloads analysts. Rank and prerequisite order disagree. (3) A1’s process-level benefit is “Low” in the matrix, then “genuine control value” via duplicate detection in the final rec. (4) “No pool sees increased inflow without a matching capacity gain” is false for R: faster FDR/normalize/credit raises arrivals at committee packaging, legal ops (already month-end peaked), signing, and activation. The answer knows legal ops is starved then flooded; the blanket statement erases that.

**Concurrency is the main process-mining miss.** The brief states KYC and FDR are often concurrent and must not be ignored. Phase 0 logging is a good patch; section 4 never uses it. Classify→KYC 7,250 vs Classify→FDR 3,940 is repeatedly treated as an exclusive split, including in Portfolio Q’s “faster Register → Classify → KYC/FDR.” If the branches run in AND, KYC’s 4h wait is often dominated by FDR’s 18h+rework, which lowers A3’s cycle-time value and raises A5/A6’s—exactly the kind of join logic used well for committee packaging and then dropped for the KYC/FDR pair. Mismatch cases (31h, 82% critical path) are the exception where KYC-side work *does* dominate; that distinction is implied, not analyzed.

**Sequencing and self-serving framing.** A4 is light integration on the scarcest pool, yet it waits until months 6–9 while two deep builds occupy Phase 1. Nothing in the dependency map requires that delay; it undercuts the “specialists are the constraint” argument. Deep vs light is also fitted to the desired slots: A6 and A9 are deep, A8 is “medium,” A4/A5/A7 are light. Plausible, but not evidenced, and it makes R uniquely feasible. Re-sequencing valuation and an e-sign “adoption drive” harvest A7/A10 benefits without spending modules—clever, but it double-counts those effects when scoring R against B and when claiming year-1 process-level wins the candidate list did not fund.

**Smaller inaccuracies and gaps.** A6 is called “the only lever” that lets analysts absorb inflow; A5 already cuts 24% FDR rework and 52% repeat requests. Queue-burden language for A10 (44h × 7,300) is the largest WIP, yet A7 outranks it without reconciling Little’s-law WIP vs local critical path. A2 is underweighted: 8% misroutes are a control-path failure (four-eyes and committee bypass), not just a 12-minute task. Hours are labeled “per year” though frequency has no period. “8h + customer response + 18h” treats the 8h wait before *Request missing documents* as if it were customer delay; the notes say customer response dominates and is unmeasured. Portfolio Q’s critique that “A10 without A4” leaves KYC as the gate conflates mismatch (1,750) with KYC clearance (11,500). Format is slightly off: a long preamble plus a Rank/deep-slot column that already encodes the recommended portfolio.

**Still well above a mediocre answer.** Constraints are respected, exception capacity is modeled, compliance is not delegated to the model, and the anti-A1 argument is grounded in the table, DFG, and resource network rather than slogans. The deductions are for inconsistency, incomplete join logic, and a rollout that does not fully follow its own constraint diagnosis—not for missing the plot.
