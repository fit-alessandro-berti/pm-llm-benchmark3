**5.4**

The answer has the right skeleton and hits the three “strong answer” tripwires (I-03 ineligible, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers/feedback). It is not a weak `p_now` ranking. Under ordinary grading that would land in the high 6s. Under the required standard it does not, because the stated decision theory is not the theory used, the required impact table is not auditable, and several operational rules contradict the data they are meant to govern.

## Decision-theoretic coherence

The central object is **CANEV**, defined as midpoint effect × breach value − €450 − false-alarm burden, then reduced by a flat €2,000 queue penalty. No CANEV is ever computed. Tiers are asserted. That is already a failure of the metric the answer itself nominated.

Recomputing the formula they wrote (midpoints, no extra discounts):

| Case | Mid effect × V − 450 − FA |
|---|---|
| I-04 | ≈ €8,950 |
| I-08 | ≈ €4,550 |
| I-09 | ≈ €3,550 |
| I-02 | ≈ €2,700 |
| I-06 | ≈ €2,625 (≈ €1,950 after their 20% effect discount) |
| I-01 | ≈ €1,685 |
| I-05 | ≈ €1,725 (correctly suppressed) |
| I-10 | ≈ €1,490 |
| I-07 | ≈ €800 |
| I-03 | ineligible |

I-07 is one of the *worst* eligible economic cases, including at the top of its effect range (≈ €1,100). Calling it “Tier 1 (High CANEV)” is false. Calling I-08 “Highest CANEV” is also false; I-04 is higher at both midpoint and upper bound. I-09 beats both selected I-02 and I-07 on the published rule and is parked as “Wait” with only a qualitative uncertainty shrug. The allocation is therefore not an application of Section 1. It is a rubric-shaped portfolio (exclude I-03, punish I-05, reward I-07’s new log) with CANEV used as decoration.

Calibration adjustments have the same problem. Strategic Enterprise −0.08, SME +0.06, NNLS “±50%,” I-06 20% effect haircut are specified in detail, then **never enter the ranking**. CANEV does not use `p_now`. The prompt’s point about observational `p` ≠ interventional probability is acknowledged in passing and then ignored: supplied effect bands are treated as known `E[Effect]` via the midpoint. That is exactly what the brief forbids.

The €2,000 externality penalty is a made-up scalar, not derived from 6 × (0.01–0.03) × untreated breach values, and it is not visible in any tier. Time-to-SLA and stage (Triage vs Diagnosis Started) are absent from the rule except as a hard `Diagnosis Complete` gate. That is a serious omission: I-04 still has 10 h in Triage; I-08 has 8 h; I-07 has 1.5 h *and is still in Triage* facing a 45-minute swarm. Feasibility of completing diagnosis after a swarm inside 90 minutes is not discussed. Specialist interruption (two seniors leave ordinary diagnosis) is the actual spillover mechanism and is never operationalized.

## Allocation and reserve

I-03 is handled correctly. I-05 suppression is correct. I-06 caution on imputation is directionally right.

The selected set `I-02, I-04, I-07` plus a half-committed I-08 is not robust and not internally consistent:

- **I-07** has low stakes, a *falling* probability (0.75 → 0.72), and a tight clock. A new log can justify *re-scoring* and breaking suppression. It does not justify burning a scarce slot when I-06, I-01, and especially I-09 have better effect × value even after uncertainty penalties.
- **I-04** is the only clean high-CANEV take, but 10 h remaining and SE overprediction (0.48 → ~0.36–0.43 after the correction they proposed) argue for *wait and re-score*, not immediate swarm. The table says Intervene.
- **I-02** is the most defensible intervene (Diagnosis Started, 3 h, second alert, no prior swarm). SE overprediction and a weak lower effect bound (0.08) are noted and then ignored.
- **I-08** is simultaneously “Intervene (Reserve / Slot 4 if needed)” and held unless time-to-SLA ≤ 6 h by 13:30. That hedge is the most operationally intelligent sentence in Section 2, and it is immediately undone by costing four slots in Section 3. Management’s reserve preference is treated as a 13:30 option, then as if already exercised.
- **I-09** is the live fairness/uncertainty case and is not even a runner-up. A coherent robust rule would either impose an explicit maximin/CVaR haircut large enough to drop it below I-02, or take it and say why. Neither happens.

“At most 3 slots” and “I-08 assigned to Slot 4” cannot both be the policy. The reader cannot tell whether today’s book is three interventions plus a true unused reserve, or four.

## Expected-impact table

This section fails its own auditability claim.

Optimistic “1.8 breaches avoided” is impossible. Upper ends of the four selected effect ranges sum to 0.22+0.28+0.32+0.15 = **0.97**. Midpoints of the upper halves sum to ~0.84. Three-slot max is 0.82. There is no arithmetic path to 1.8. Base “1.1” is likewise too high (midpoint sum ≈ 0.70). Adverse “0.4” happens to match the four lower bounds (0.43) — so they used lower bounds for Adverse and something else, unstated and infeasible, for the other two rows.

Direct-benefit ranges are inflated the same way. Upper-bound effect × value on the four cases is ≈ €38.9k *before* false-alarm burdens (€7,650). Their optimistic band goes to €54k. The Base footnote multiplies **one** 0.15 by the *sum* of heterogeneous stakes. That is the wrong aggregator when I-08’s effect mid is 0.10 and I-07’s is 0.26; it also does not match the €18–31k cell sitting next to it.

Spillover language contradicts itself inside one table: the Base row says “24 untreated cases” (4×6 counted as distinct people) while the footnote does 4 × 6 × 0.02 × €15,000, i.e. six people each hit four times. €15,000 is invented. Optimistic spillover (€1.2–2.4k) does not use the same formula (that formula at Δp=0.01 is €3.6k). Delay minutes and euro harm are mixed without a conversion. Optimistic “zero false-alarm backfire” is wishful: false-alarm burden is a cost of intervening, not something that disappears when effects realize high.

I-08’s own adverse unit economics are net-negative (0.05×€100k − €450 − €5,000 = −€450) and are not surfaced. Confidence labels are atmosphere.

## Temporal policy

The I-05 vs I-07 *distinction* is correct and required. The *implementation* is slack. A 60-minute cool-down against a 30-minute rescoring cycle is underspecified (does the second refresh in the window get ignored?). Suppression is defined on “no material event,” then I-07 is escalated even though risk *fell* and their written escalation rule requires SLA compression >50% *and* a material update — I-07 does not meet that trigger. Post-swarm re-entry (“normally forbidden unless a materially new event”) is the constraint in the brief and is barely specified. No rule for withdrawing a reserved slot, no rule for I-04/I-08 still sitting in Triage with many hours left.

## Fairness and feedback

The five requested risks are all named, and some safeguards are concrete. That is the best section. It is still sloppy.

- A 25% monthly floor for V<€15k is fine as a governance idea; it does not explain *this* hour’s choice to spend a slot on I-07 while leaving I-01 and I-09.
- The 20-minute SME/PS queue-lag freeze on Strategic Enterprise swarms will fire almost whenever a slot is used, because expected collateral delay is already 8–20 minutes per slot. The safeguard is incompatible with using the intervention at all.
- A mandatory 5-minute senior review for NNLS spends the scarce resource the policy is rationing.
- “Autologistic feedback loop” is the wrong term (performativity / selective labels / confounding by indication). IPW/causal forests are named, not specified at the level needed to be implementable (what is the propensity model, what is censored, how is the holdout assigned).
- Public-sector / regulatory non-monetary duty is almost entirely reduced to “keep a slot.” I-09 as a language-support case left untreated is discussed in general and not as a decision about *this* case.

## Monitoring

The five-pillar outline is competent and would look fine in a runbook draft. Gaps that matter under a strict read:

- No numeric CANEV hurdle was defined, yet recalibration “tighten the CANEV hurdle by 15%” presupposes one.
- 10% encouragement holdout with four slots a day is almost certainly underpowered; ethics and selection into “borderline” are unaddressed.
- Detecting policy-induced drift is thinner than Section 5’s vocabulary; no segment-wise reliability diagram / ECE cadence, no pre-registered estimate of swarm lift given confounding by indication.
- Queue-length “8” and “two consecutive weeks of negative net-impact” are free parameters, like the €2,000 penalty and the 20-minute freeze.

## What keeps it above a 4

It is not a probability sort. It will not swarm I-03. It will not restun I-05. It knows effects ≠ `p_now`, writes a reserve-with-clock, and can name IPW, spillover incidence, and segment calibration as first-class objects. That is a real policy memo, not a leaderboard.

## Why it cannot go much higher

A deployable rule is a rule you can re-run on the table and recover the same three names. This one cannot. The one table the brief requires to be arithmetically checkable contains an impossible 1.8, a 24-vs-6 spillover, a 3-vs-4 slot cost, and a footnote that does not match its row. I-07 is promoted *against* the author’s own objective to satisfy the I-05/I-07 narrative. Calibration, time, stage, specialist preemption, and effect uncertainty are described, then not allowed to change the book.

That is a well-written, structurally complete answer with a broken decision core. 5.4.
