**6.5**

The six-section writeup gets the *main* audit story right: April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a routing/mix change rather than concept drift, August–September is overlapping process, concept, and population shift, and October is not comparable because labels are immature. That alignment with the quality criteria is the main reason the score is not lower. The answer is not close to flawless.

**Section 1.** The table is complete and mostly well evidenced, but several cells misuse the “contradicting” column. May’s partial recovery does not contradict an April pipeline failure; V1/V2 stability supports *segmented* concept drift rather than contradicting it. Process drift is collapsed into “June–August,” which blurs two different phenomena (June V2 policy mix vs July–October parts-wait explosion). The concept-drift row compares *global* mean predicted risk (~0.19) to *V3-specific* breach rates (0.64). That is not valid evidence: we have no stratified scores, so the model could already have been high on V3 and low on V2. October’s prompt-stated fact that the backlog hits longer paths harder is not used. “Supplier delay indicators” as an immediate action is also sloppy for a diagnosis-time model.

**Section 2.** This is the strongest section. The month-by-month split is clear and correctly refuses a single failure mode. Two real errors remain. First, October’s mean risk of 0.23 is described as the model “attempting to compensate for unseen risk.” That is invention; a likelier reading is mix (more V3 / Partner Z) plus residual miscalibration, and predicted risk cannot “see” the label backlog. Second, July is undersold. V3 mature breach is already 0.46 and parts wait is already 65h; July is the onset of supplier-driven deterioration, not a quiet prelude.

**Section 3.** Coverage of the required tests is mostly there, but one row is logically inverted. For April recomputation, H1 is “the drop was real” and H2 is “pipeline error,” yet the expected results are swapped: recovery after relabeling would support the *pipeline* hypothesis, not the “real drop” hypothesis. Duplicate cleaning is required in the test list and barely appears. Stratification is ECE-by-variant only; discrimination by source and month is thin. The lineage test chases Partner Z features and ignores the documented unrecomputed “events before diagnosis” cache.

**Section 4.** Containment (do not train on October) and refusing a global recalibration that swallows V3 are proportionate. The training-window call is wrong: July is labeled “pre-severe delay” and treated as safe, despite V3 breach 0.46 and a doubled parts-wait edge. June (mature, pipeline-ok, mix-shifted) is safer than July and is omitted. May is not conditioned on recomputing the cached feature. August–September are neither flagged as unsafe for the *old* concept nor valued as the right data for a *new* one. Rolling supplier SLA as a context feature is defensible; treating realized post-diagnosis delay as a training feature would leak.

**Section 5.** Maturity gating so a label-delay alarm does not auto-trigger retraining is the right dependency. Population/source monitoring is underbuilt (Partner Z is folded into variant share). The code sketch fails an explicit requirement: it never builds both an event-calendar cohort and a case-start cohort, and never states their different interpretations. `pm4py.filter_time_range(..., min_time=, max_time=)` does not match the usual API (positional datetimes + `mode`). `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is used as if it returned case `duration`; it does not. `discover_performance_dfg` is computed and then ignored, so the one edge the case actually hinges on (`Order Part → Part Received`) never enters the summary, nor do V1/V2/V3 shares.

**Section 6.** The four failure modes and mitigations are the cleanest part of the answer and match the requested pitfalls (immature labels, calibrating a pipeline bug, ignoring supplier concept drift, fairness claims without stratification).

**Net.** Strong narrative diagnosis, several concrete factual/logical mistakes, and a missing required code design. Under a strict bar that is a mid-6, not an 8–10.
