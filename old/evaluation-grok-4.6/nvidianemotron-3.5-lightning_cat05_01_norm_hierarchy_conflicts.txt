The formatted submission is only a partial answer. Sections 1–3 exist; section 4 breaks off in the first case row; sections 5–7 are not delivered. That alone bars a high score. The task required every case, alternative interpretations, evidential remediation, and a replacement exception clause. Those are not optional appendices.

What is present in sections 1–3 is directionally competent. The memo refuses a single total order, treats GPIP-4 as integrity baseline rather than automatically defeated by newer or louder text, confines ECD-2 to timing and onboarding, treats the CFO email as uncontrolled interpretive guidance, and treats the ERP outage as evidential weight rather than a waiver. Those strengths are undercut by recurring legal and table errors.

C5 is repeatedly described as an “express carve-out.” The source only records silence: ECD-2 does not mention segregation, Finance, Compliance, the €100,000 threshold, or the CRO. Silence is a reason not to infer a waiver. It is not an affirmative reservation. Building the hierarchy, the rule catalogue, and the case logic on “C5 excludes X” overstates the text and then uses that overstatement as if it were dispositive.

The hierarchy table also inverts its own columns on the GPIP-4 rows. “What it can override” is filled with defeaters of those clauses (A7, D1) rather than what those clauses prevail over. The ECD-2 rows get the polarity right; the GPIP-4 rows do not. That is a structural defect in the required artifact.

The rule catalogue is thin relative to the scenario.
