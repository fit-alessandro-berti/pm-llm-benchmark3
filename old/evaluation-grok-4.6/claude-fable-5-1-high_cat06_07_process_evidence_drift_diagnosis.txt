**Code in Section 5 would fail on the metric it exists to produce.** `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` returns a dict of `(activity1, activity2) → float` (seconds, default aggregation `mean`), not a nested `{ "median": ... }` object. `perf.get(PART_EDGE, {}).get("median", float("nan")) / 3600` therefore raises `AttributeError` as soon as the parts edge exists—the normal case. Median is also never requested (`aggregation_measure="median"`). That is not a cosmetic API nit; it is a crash in the required sketch, and it quietly substitutes the wrong statistic even if rewritten as a float. The instruction not to invent a non-existent PM4Py drift detector was followed; inventing a non-existent performance-DFG schema was not.

**Event-calendar DFGs drop the actual parts delays.** `filter_time_range(..., mode="events")` keeps only events inside the calendar month, so an `Order Part` on 30 August and `Part Received` on 5 September never form an edge in either month. With median lead times of 130–155 h this is common, and it is exactly the edge the audit treats as the leading indicator. The interpretation note correctly warns against `traces_contained` for case-start cohorts, then does not warn about this complementary bias. Joining event-month data-quality audit columns onto a case-start summary further mixes the two cohort definitions the text itself insists must be kept apart.

**October is described in incompatible ways.** The timeline correctly says parts-edge 155 h and V3 share 41% imply a true October rate at or above September’s, and that the 0.16 figure is selection, not recovery. The closing sentence then says October is “evidence of a label-pipeline problem and nothing else until it matures.” Process evidence in October is independent of label maturity. That sentence over-claims and undercuts the earlier, better analysis.

**The Partner Z residual is computed on the biased numbers the text already distrusts.** `0.58 × 0.61 + 0.42 × ~0.15 ≈ 0.42` against an observed 0.45 is a useful first-order mix check, but 0.61 is the pooled V3 rate (Partner Z already a third of V3), and Partner Z labels mature more slowly, so 0.45 is more downward-biased than 0.27. The residual “~0.03” is therefore not an estimate of a Partner-Z-specific effect; it is a lower bound on a contaminated difference. The table flags asymmetric maturation; the arithmetic then treats the gap as if it were mature.
