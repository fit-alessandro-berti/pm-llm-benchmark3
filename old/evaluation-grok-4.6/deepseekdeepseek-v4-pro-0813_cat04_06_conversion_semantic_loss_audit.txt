**7.3**

The write-up is a strong, well-structured response that hits the required nine-section format, treats the POWL source as authoritative, avoids inventing a POWL-to-BPMN API, and keeps conversion, visualization, and behavioral equivalence clearly separate. The code is complete and realistic, exception handling is present, markings are checked, and the bounded explorer’s limits are stated honestly. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

The most serious flaw is treating Petri→tree as `conversion failed` throughout the audit table without executing the converter or catching an exception. That term is an observed outcome, not a structural prediction. The allowed vocabulary already includes `not tested`; using `conversion failed` as a design judgment is an overclaim, and it sits poorly next to the careful use of `expected preserved` everywhere else.

The invariant table is mostly right, but the negative-witness column is inconsistent. For S1, S4, S6–S11 the negatives are source-rejected traces. For S2 and S3 they describe conversion defects (“rejecting either interleaving”, “legal is mandatory or impossible”) rather than traces the source itself refuses. That blurs the baseline the rest of the audit depends on.

Round-trip comparison is thinner than it looks. Footprints are reduced to start/end activity equality; reachability is only state/transition counts; firing ignores arc weights; visible-label handling is not quite the same in replay versus exploration. None of these is fatal for this model, but they weaken the claim that the comparison uses “as many methods as are defensible.”

Minor nits: unused `copy` / `_marking_key` in the conversion script, fragile `globals().get` wiring between the two code blocks, and no executed evidence that would justify any `validated preserved` cell. The visual-semantics, rejection, canonical-storage, and residual-uncertainty sections are solid and do not inflate the grade.
