## What it does well

Sections 1–2 are the strongest part of the paper. FEP-1 is decomposed into unilateral authority, error/duplication, bank-account integrity, commercial substance/receipt, collusion and credential misuse, recoverability, attribution, and emergency normalization. AV-2, MG-4, RA-7, and ED-1 are mapped to those threats by function, not by event name. The sufficiency test is structured (timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, collusion resistance) and correctly rejects “same expected loss” as the only standard.

Case reasoning is mostly disciplined:

- Same-user is not automatically `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` (AP-203, AP-207).
- Distinct-user is not automatically `OBJECTIVE_MET` (AP-204, AP-209).
- A completed control is not treated as a sufficient control (AP-202 override, AP-208 post-settlement ratification, AP-204 fabricated receipt).
- AP-205 is correctly separated from completed-payment FEP-1.
- AP-206 is correctly left `INDETERMINATE` on the token-timing fork.
- AP-208 is not rescued by an ex-post legitimate outcome.

Section 4’s hybrid treatment of post-release, pre-settlement MG-4 is coherent. Section 6 correctly treats repeated pairs, shared workstations, and overrides as alerts rather than proof.

## Material inaccuracies

**1. The facts table in the code inverts bank-change on the two cases where that flag matters most.**

Constructed row for AP-202 sets `bank_change_lt_7d=False`. The narrative is explicit: the bank account changed two hours earlier, and AV-2 warned on that change. The dedicated bank-change flag the prompt required would therefore be silent on the related-party fraud case.

Constructed row for AP-203 sets `bank_change_lt_7d=True`. The case facts say there was no bank change. Combined with `~bank_change_lt_7d` in `emergency_basic_eligibility`, the code would mark the strongest ED-1 case ineligible on a false bank-change violation.

That is not a style nit. It is a fact error in the artifact that is supposed to emit preliminary evidence.

**2. AP-203 settlement is omitted from the evidence join.**

The log has no `Bank_Settlement` event for AP-203; the facts place settlement at 12:00. The code special-cases only AP-201. `manager_before_settlement` therefore becomes false (NaT comparison), and `ed1_preliminary_observed_conditions` fails on the one case that should pass the observable ED-1 gate. The checker would contradict the qualitative verdict on the cleanest emergency package.

**3. AP-207 is internally inconsistent with the author’s own sufficiency test.**

Section 2 treats post-release, pre-settlement review as weaker than a true second releaser and, at best, partially equivalent. Section 4 calls that timing hybrid and inferior. AP-207 is nevertheless classified `OBJECTIVE_MET`. ED-1’s letter allows approval before settlement, but the paper itself says ED-1 does not make the four-eyes objective disappear. On the author’s own terms, AP-207 is a permitted, well-supported exception with a residual unilateral-release window, i.e. closer to `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET`. AP-203 (pre-release MG-4) is the case that actually approximates equivalence.

## Smaller but real flaws

- AP-201’s audit is called “outside the usual recall window” because it starts ~25 hours after *release*. Settlement is 10:00; audit starts 09:00 next day, inside a 24-hour window measured from settlement. The paper elsewhere treats settlement as economic irrevocability. The recall clock is applied inconsistently.
- AP-201 as “tolerable” sits uneasily next to objective 8 (do not normalize convenience single-operator processing). The residual-risk text sees the issue; the classification still leans on low amount and a clean outcome more than the author admits.
- AP-204 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` with **high** confidence is defensible on the fabricated receipt, but the paper also says collusion is unproven and recovery/settlement is unknown. High confidence overstates what the record can carry.
- AP-209 as `INDETERMINATE` is a thoughtful refusal to rubber-stamp distinct users, but it over-weights a cross-case pattern relative to a clean AV-2 pass, distinct resources, timely audit, and no defect. The author’s own Section 6 rule — pattern is an alert, not a finding — points more naturally to `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` with a pair-risk residual.
- The required `filter_four_eyes_principle` call is not exception-guarded, even though the write-up spends a paragraph on version drift. Semantic inversion is handled; an API or input-type failure is not. `format_dataframe` also never binds a resource key, which is a real four-eyes portability risk.
- Several fact fields are unused or one-off (`no_defect_reported`; a hardcoded AP-201 settlement) while the systematic facts frame is left incomplete (no settlement times, no manager independence, no shared-workstation flag).
