8.2

The final write-up is a strong, format-compliant solution: the nine required headings are present, the two languages are characterized correctly, the P2 abstraction proof is valid, and the counterexamples are genuine.

**What is right.** Model A couples one `Check Revision` to each loop iteration (parallel with `Analyze Revision`, then optional `Approve Revision`). Model B emits exactly one check, concurrent with the whole loop. That yields the right core verdicts: P1 not equivalent, P2 equivalent only under the stated dashboard map, P3 not equivalent. T-A1/T-A2 (two checks) and T-B1/T-B3 (two analyses, one check) are decisive; T-B2 (`Analyze, Approve, Check, Close`) correctly exploits the fact that A’s AND-join finishes before the XOR. Tree-node, depth, and leaf counts for A/B/simplified (12/12/11, depths 4/5/4) are exact. The simplified sequential loop is a defensible P1+ target and is actually smaller than both originals.

**What keeps it from a high score.** P1 is never written as a crisp equivalence (`M1 ≡ M2` iff …); it stays a compliance narrative. The P1 caveat is wrong: weakening P1 to “at least one case-level check” would **not** make the models equivalent, because P1 still treats approval **placement** as visible (T-B2). “Largely included” is sloppy: for one analysis, \(L(A)\subseteq L(B)\) exactly. The stochastic/EMD block is a non-working stub; a declared uniform distribution over the P3-bounded languages was plausible and was not given. Sections 6–7 are not self-contained, and `max_total_steps=80` is an unargued cutoff for silent-heavy process-tree nets.

The analysis of loop scope, silent skip, and bounded vs unbounded comparison is otherwise careful and should be trusted on the main equivalence claims.
