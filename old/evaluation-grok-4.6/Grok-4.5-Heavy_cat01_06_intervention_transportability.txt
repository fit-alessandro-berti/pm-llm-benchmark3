6.6

The answer is a competent transportability memo with the right headline recommendation, but it is not close to flawless. Several required artifacts are internally inconsistent, one process model is legally wrong, and the PM4Py object does not implement what the prompt (and the surrounding prose) claim.

**What is actually strong.** The piece correctly refuses a blanket copy of Site A, treats the −9 h median as unidentified, and segments on the statutory originals gate. Sections 1, 3, 4, and 9 show real analogical control: AND-join before Assessment is shared; originals, specialized pools, the single-owner lock, legal/supplement loops, and A’s co-interventions (API, +2 FTE, mix shift, huddles, better stamps) are effect modifiers rather than nuisances. The required median-up / p90-down pattern appears in both the bottleneck-shift and legal-congestion scenarios. The pilot’s mechanism-vs-implementation split (true overlap vs cycle time given overlap) is the right identification strategy.

**Section 1.** The table hits the mandated columns and the twelve required features, and most rows are accurate. It still understates a fact the later text gets right: Site B already allows `Identity Check ∥ Originals Receipt` after Register. Calling current concurrency merely “limited” and “Document mostly after Originals” collapses two different parallelisms and makes the status quo look more sequential than the DFG (`Register → Originals` 1,860; `Identity → Originals` 1,020). Assessment-capacity and data-independence rows are useful but thinner than the rest.

**Section 2.** All required node names appear, and the  S_*  devices are used. The essay is slightly over the 180–280 word cap (~300). The DAG is unsigned, so `originals_required → true_overlap` and `digital_documents → true_overlap` look like the same kind of arrow. `API_latency → true_overlap` is the wrong edge: latency belongs on identity service time / waiting, not on the overlap mediator. Missing and material: `treatment_parallelization` never loads `identity_capacity` or `document_capacity`; A-only co-interventions `queue_huddles` and `API_latency` have no selection nodes; nothing carries earlier verification completion into `legal_capacity`. “Similar diagram ≠ transport” is stated, but the graph does not actually encode the main interference path the rest of the memo relies on.

**Section 3.** Correct refusal to identify the 9-hour move; confidences are appropriately low. Weaker rows treat “no RCT” and “+2 FTE” as if they were the same kind of estimand. Transferable-component cells sometimes smuggle in hopes (“reduction in serial waiting when truly concurrent”) that the identification paragraph just denied. Fine as a critique of A; not a clean transport table.

**Section 4.** Coverage of the ten required modifiers is good. Several “beneficial signature” cells are empty or vacuous (“None for pure parallel”). Identity WIP and document WIP are near-duplicates. The lock row’s beneficial signature is “None if lock enforced,” which is a constraint, not an interaction. Serviceable, not tight.

**Section 5.** Five labeled scenarios, including the required tail-worsening cases, and an explicit discount of A’s −9 h. Then a direct contradiction: the optimistic row assumes 70–90% eligibility and “few cases require [originals],” which Site B cannot legislate away, while the paragraph under the table says parallelization is limited to ~38%. That is not an optimistic *structural* transfer; it is a counterfactual that deletes the main selection node. Eligible share for “digital-claim-only” treats “not originals-required” as identical to “digital packet at Register,” which the evidence does not say. Ranges are asserted more than derived from A’s service times (3.4 h ID, 8.1 h Doc, 18 h originals delay) and B’s pool sizes (4 / 7 / 3 / 2).

**Section 6.** The eligibility rule and the list of remaining statutory gates are the right intervention. The compact partial order for the *ineligible* stream is wrong: `Register ≺ (Identity Check ∥ Originals Receipt) ≺ Document Check` forces Identity to finish before Document Check. Site B’s rules and DFG do not. Identity may run with Originals; only Originals gates Document. That invented edge is exactly the kind of control-flow error the memo elsewhere warns against. WIP text is ambiguous: “identity reviewers ≤4–5 open” with a pool of four reads like a pool cap of ~1 task per person, not Site A’s per-reviewer limit of six.

**Section 7.** Design logic is the best part of the answer. Arithmetic is not. Baseline 3,400 / 6 months and ~38% eligible imply ~215 eligible cases per month, so a 3–4 month window yields ~650–860 eligible *total*, not 800–1,000 *per arm*. The table cites both numbers. Stop rules (true overlap <30%, p90 +10 h, rework/appeal +2 pp) are reasonable but unanchored. The prose is at or just over the 220-word cap.

**Section 8 — largest spec failure.** Site A trees, `convert_to_petri_net`, the AND-join via a nested `StrictPartialOrder`, conversion, soundness hooks, and the “cannot encode” comment all exist. The Site B object does not. `legal_loop_body` is built and then dropped. `xor_legal` is only `XOR(skip, Legal Review)`; `Request Supplement` never enters `top`. Same `Transition` instances are reused in the dead loop body, which would be illegal POWL if wired. Originals Receipt is absent. `play_out(..., no_traces=3)` does not match the usual `parameters` API and is likely to throw. The prompt asked for a POWL with an optional legal branch *and a supplement loop*, and forbade collapsing Site B to a convenience skeleton. Comments describe the loop; the net that is checked and played out does not contain it. That is a model-fidelity miss, not a style nit.

**Section 9.** `conditional go`, first wave = no-originals/digital, do not copy unsegmented task creation or A’s magnitude. Expansion/modify/abandon gates are coherent with Section 7. Residual softness: the 2–4 h overall median is restated as if it were an estimate rather than a scenario cell with confidence 55.

**Section 10.** Exactly eight rows in the required shape. Assumption 3 (lock can be relaxed for the pilot) sits uneasily next to “management cannot remove statutory constraints” and the legacy lock; it is a hope, not a tested design condition. Assumption 1’s 38% stability inherits the digital/no-originals conflation.

**Net.** The causal story is better than the artifacts that were supposed to pin it down. Under a normal rubric this would sit in the high 7s. Under the stated hypercritical bar — unique transport effect denied, partial-order fidelity, quantitative coherence, code that matches the spec — the false ineligible order, the self-contradictory optimistic/power numbers, the unsigned/incomplete DAG, the word-limit slips, and especially the unwired supplement loop keep it well short of excellent.
