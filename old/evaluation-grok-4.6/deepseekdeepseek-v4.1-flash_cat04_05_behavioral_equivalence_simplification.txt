**7.5**

The final write-up is strong on the parts that matter most: the two models are not the same object, equivalence is made conditional on an observation model, the four traces are valid, loop scope and silent `tau` are handled correctly, and bounded testing is not treated as a proof. Under a hypercritical reading it is still not nearly flawless.

**What is actually right.** The structural table is accurate: A puts `Check Revision` inside each loop iteration (parallel with `Analyze Revision`, then optional approval); B places a single `Check Revision` in parallel with the whole loop. That implies A has \(n\) checks for \(n\) analyses and B has exactly one. A1/A2 are in A and not B because they contain two checks. B1 is in B and not A because approval is sequenced after the `Analyze`/`Check` join in A, so `Approve` cannot precede `Check`. B2 is in B and not A because A must execute the first loop child again after `Rework Case`, and that child includes a check. P1 and P3 are therefore not equivalent; P2 is equivalent only after collapsing checks to a Boolean, hiding approvals/`tau`, and forgetting Analyze/Check interleaving. That P2 image is the same for both models: \(n_A \ge 1\), `hasCheck = true`, `hasRework \Leftrightarrow (n_A \ge 2)\). Node/depth counts for A (12 / 4) and B (12 / 5) are correct with root depth 0.

**P1 is not defined tightly enough.** Section 1 mixes a compliance filter with language equality (“same set of P1-compliant visible traces”) without an explicit projection. That leaves a real ambiguity the rest of the document never resolves: either P1 still sees full visible sequences (including check-before-analyze and approval placement), or it only cares that each revision block contains a check before the next rework/close. The verdict `not equivalent` is correct under both readings, but the notion itself is not “precise enough to guide a comparison.” The later caveat that A’s parallel may already violate a strict “check-after-analyze” reading is appropriate, and then underused.

**The simplification overclaims preservation.** The sequential tree is simpler than both originals (11 nodes, no `+`, depth 4 vs B’s 5), and the regression/negative traces are consistent with that tree. It is not an exact P1 preservation of A. It drops every A-trace in which `Check Revision` precedes `Analyze Revision`, and it is far from B. Calling that “exact / strengthened” is hedging, not a preservation argument. The quality bar asked for a simplification that preserves the semantics it claims to preserve; this one preserves a *stricter* P1 policy plus P2, and that should have been stated as restriction plus abstraction, not exactness.

**Code is plausible, not trustworthy as written.** The BFS design is the right one (silent moves, trace-sensitive visited set, visible-length and analyze bounds, final-marking test, contrast with `play_out`). Several details are still wrong or fragile: marking identity is keyed by `place.name` (collision-prone after tree-to-net conversion); `discover_footprints(net_a)` does not match the stated API of a tree or accepting net; `get_enabled_transitions` is fed a `Counter`, not necessarily a PM4Py `Marking`; expansion stops at the final marking without a supporting lemma that the converted net is a sound WF-net with no enabled continuation. None of this was executed, so Section 4 is hand-checked reasoning wearing a “bounded comparison” frame, and Petri-net simplicity is correctly withheld but then the comparison is incomplete.

**Smaller unclarities.** The complexity table includes the Section 8 model before that model exists. Section 6 correctly refuses invented transition weights and correctly says footprints/EMD cannot prove trace equivalence, but it never notes that these two languages already differ in support, so a well-formed EMD on the bounded languages would be strictly positive and would actually help. Duplicate-label policy is stated clearly for traces; structural “duplicate visible leaf labels = 0” is true and easy to misread as “no repeated labels in behavior.”

The verdicts and counterexamples would support a high score. The definitional slack around P1, the overstated simplification, and the unverified/API-loose comparison code are why it is not close to flawless.
