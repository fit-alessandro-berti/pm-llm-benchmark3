**6.4**

The write-up is complete, multi-hypothesis, and mostly well calibrated, but it mishandles the one question the prompt is built around, and a few of those mistakes are not cosmetic.

**The central contradiction is misread.** Handover edges are event-level frequencies; working-together rates are a share of *all* cases. Mira–Carla at 8% of the log is about 330 cases, i.e. roughly a third of Mira’s 905 cases, not evidence that “the majority of cases where Mira acts” have no other named resource. Working-together is case co-membership. Short routing events still count. “She does not linger, so WT is low” confuses a duration/role story with a metric that does not encode duration. The real reconciliation is mixed normalization plus coverage: she is on ~22% of cases, so she cannot look like Alice–Carla (27% of the whole log); she is a high-fan-out exception router on a minority stream, hence central in flow/subcontracting and small in a globally normalized collaboration graph. That point is never stated cleanly.

**SNA theory is inverted in Hypothesis 3.** High handover betweenness with low working-together/clustering is the usual signature of a broker on a structural hole. Using low WT as *contrary* evidence against the boundary-spanner hypothesis is backwards.

**Betweenness is overclaimed.** 0.77 does not mean a random pair of resources is likely to have its shortest handover path through Mira. Bot_Ingest→Bot_Rules, Alice↔Carla, Bruno↔Diego, and the team-lead→Vendor_Pay payment path all bypass her.

**Vendors and money controls are thin** relative to an explicit constraint. Vendor_Verify’s long service time and the Temp_1↔Vendor_Verify loop, and the fact that *settlement release to Vendor_Pay is a team-lead function Mira does not perform*, are first-order organizational facts. They barely appear.

**Format and internal consistency.** The executive summary is ~205 words against a 120–180 limit, and it lists queue-depth alerting as an immediate action after §5 placed it under monitoring.

**What still works.** All six required sections are present. Five hypotheses each have mechanism, support, limits, and log predictions. Risks stay structural rather than accusing. Absence data is used as a natural experiment, not as decoration. PM4Py proposals are concrete and cover every required family. Removal is not recommended without a substitute. Centrality is not treated as automatically good or bad.

Those strengths make this a solid organizational memo. They do not offset a wrong account of working-together, which is the analytic core of the task.
