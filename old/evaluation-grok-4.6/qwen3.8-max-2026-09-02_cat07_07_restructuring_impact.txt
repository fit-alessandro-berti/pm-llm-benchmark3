**7.5**

The final write-up matches the required seven-section structure and satisfies the scenario constraints: it does not treat the restructuring as inherently good or bad, it does not rest on average cycle time, it treats East as a distinct beneficiary/risk profile, it keeps fairness and learning in view, and it refuses full rollout without a pilot and guardrails. The 343 vs 359 “listed handover” comparison is a genuine, useful reading of the tables.

It is not close to flawless.

The optimistic scenario is internally inconsistent on the scarcest resources. Legal/Risk are already at 93%/91% utilization, and the proposed routing raises those handovers from 9 to 12 per 100 cases. That cannot coexist with “waits stay near 10–12h” unless capacity is added, which the optimistic path never states. The 85–88% utilization “safe zone” is also applied loosely next to resources already above it.

The PM4Py sketch has real errors, not just version caveats. `cycle_time` is a Series merged as if it were a column (`df.merge(cycle_time, on=CASE)`). Case-level KPIs (`cycle_time_days`, `rework_flag`) are then aggregated on the event table, so medians/p90s and fairness splits are weighted by number of events, not cases — exactly the bias the narrative warns against. Handover/working-together/subcontracting networks are treated as dicts of `(source, target)` weights; PM4Py typically returns a NetworkX graph. Extra kwargs on several discover/filter calls are likely invalid.

Subcontracting is listed as a core technique but is barely used in the mechanism map, even though a shared service doing work for local teams is the organizational pattern it is meant to capture. After centralization, `team` is no longer a clean case attribute (local plus central resources on one case); the filters never resolve originating team vs executing resource. Invented `sla_miss` / `enterprise` / `vulnerable` / `failed` values are acceptable only as sketch placeholders, but they are written as if they were in the log.

The rest is strong: mechanism table, three scenarios with the required dimensions, leading vs lagging indicators, tail/fairness/learning/accountability risks, East-first phased pilot with controls and rollback triggers, and a conditional “modify and pilot first” recommendation that correctly tells management not to trust early averages. Those strengths are why this is still a solid 7 rather than a 6; the capacity contradiction and the broken fairness metrics in code are why it is nowhere near a 9.
