**6.4**

The answer is complete, format-compliant, and gets the central scientific point right: the crude Orion penalty reverses inside every complexity stratum, so the 50% transfer is not justified by the overall means. Arithmetic is accurate, queueing is separated from review service, resource group is treated as a bundle rather than an atomic treatment, and the recommendation is safer than the proposed reassignment. It is not close to flawless.

**Causal overclaim.** “Orion is faster” in every stratum is treated as strong evidence, but High-Nova and Low-Orion each have two cases. The within-stratum gaps (~2–3.4 h) are smaller than the sampling noise those cells can produce. No standard error, interval, or even a qualitative “n is too small to read the sign” appears in the executive verdict. Confidence 70/100 is too high for an underdetermined design with empty day-medium and day-high Orion cells. The required “evidence for and against *that verdict*” is also mis-aimed: the text argues against a harmful Orion effect, not against “mainly confounded.” The obvious counter to that verdict—the consistent within-stratum Orion advantage, i.e. a possible beneficial group effect—is parked in the hypothesis table instead of weighing the stated conclusion.

**Stratification is handled well, then slightly oversold.** The tables correctly refuse to treat complexity adjustment as identification. The executive still presents the reversal as if it nearly settles the crude association. Residual shift, load, compliance, skill, and completion-selection are listed, but not allowed to pull the confidence down.

**Pilot does not match the policy.** Randomizing low/medium *day* cases tests a slice Orion almost never works today (one Low-Day case; zero Medium-Day). That cannot tell management what happens if half of Orion’s actual night/high/compliance load moves to Nova. The write-up notes eligibility and rollback, but never states this external-validity gap clearly enough.

**Code defect.** The PM4Py path builds illegal XES: case id is an XML `id` attribute, not a trace-level `concept:name`. `discover_dfg(..., case_id_key="case:concept:name")` is therefore likely to fail or collapse cases. A manual fallback hides the failure, which also violates “use PM4Py consistently.” DFG “comparison” is two printed edge lists, not a contrast. Trace-cell warnings will fire on almost every variant and add noise rather than diagnosis.

**Smaller issues.** Front-door is named without its identifying assumptions. `resource_group → unobserved_reviewer_skill` locks skill as a mediator in the DAG while the table admits it may confound routing. Appeals 1 vs 1 are used as evidence against harm. The 10% cycle-time rollback is arbitrary at this sample size.

The skeleton of a good committee memo is here. The grade is held down by overconfident reading of tiny strata, a policy-mismatched experiment, a broken process-mining implementation, and a verdict whose counter-evidence is not actually weighed.
