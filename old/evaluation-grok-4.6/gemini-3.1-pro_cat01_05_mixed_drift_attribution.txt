**5.4**

The answer is format-complete and gets the headline story right (March is mostly semantic; later movement is real and staggered). Under a strict reading it is still not a high-quality diagnosis. Quantitative claims are sloppy, several attributions are backwards or underspecified, and the monitoring design conditions away the phenomena it should detect.

**Temporal synthesis.** Six intervals meet the letter of the spec and miss the point of the timeline. September recovery is absent, so residual backlog cannot be separated from new drift. August is also absent, even though `supplier_z_pct` is already falling while `p90_cycle_h` is still rising to 110 — that is the cleanest aggregate clue that queueing/staffing, not contemporaneous supplier intensity, is dominating late summer. July is the worst row: `Assess->RequestInfo` is treated as a priority-rule signature. The priority rule changes queue discipline; it does not generate document-rework loops. That onset should have been mixed at most, with the rework peak kept on the supplier clock. Staffing is coded as performance drift rather than resource/organizational drift that *manifests* as queueing. Evidence strengths of 85–95 are too high for deliberately non-unique aggregates.

**Process-mining rigor.** The raw-versus-mapped writeup is the strongest section and stays inside the word limit, but it still undersells the one mapped-fitness fact that matters: after label repair, fitness still slides from 0.93 to 0.86. Mapping therefore *fails* to restore the log, which is exactly why post-May movement is not logging. Duplicate `Open Case` is described as “false loops or parallelisms”; the supplied DFG is a prefix insertion (`Open Case -> Register`), not a loop. The October `Assess->SeniorAssessment` jump is treated as seasonal population with “evidence against: None.” The baseline note that senior assessment was historically recorded inconsistently is a first-class competing logging hypothesis and is ignored in the timeline, the hypothesis table, and the attribution. `fast_variant_pct` is never used.

**Causal restraint.** Interaction of May rework with June capacity loss is correctly flagged, and the revision conditions in §9 are specific. Restraint collapses elsewhere. Measurement missingness lists “missingness is not random” as evidence *against* the hypothesis; non-random, activity-concentrated missingness is the predicted signature. Priority gets a supporting row that cites rising aggregates, which cannot support a redistribution hypothesis the author elsewhere admits is invisible in monthly totals. December is credited to overtime/campaign at strength 80 while the prompt itself says load fell at the same time. Cycle-time shares (>60 / ~25 / ~10 / <5) are presented as a partition without a defined contrast window and without admitting that rework *creates* queueing. If the target is Nov vs Jan, case mix is underweighted; if it is Jul vs Jan, October’s senior-assessment spike does not belong in the same decomposition.

**Quantitative reasoning.** This section should have been disqualifying on its own. March raw-fitness change vs January is \((0.55-0.92)/0.92 = -40.2\%\), not \(-39.1\%\) (that figure is September’s). MAE on the seven given rates vs January is \(3/7, 40/7, 59/7, 28/7, 64/7, 36/7\) \(\approx 0.43, 5.71, 8.43, 4.00, 9.14, 5.14\). Reported values \(5.43, 8.29, 3.71, 9.29, 5.00\) do not match the stated measure. The “omitted edges = 0” assumption is also inconsistent with computing MAE only over the seven listed relations. Two logging change-points are both `2026-03`; that does not satisfy “at least two” in any useful sense. Percentage changes on already-percentage metrics are reported to a tenth of a percent as if they were estimated effects.

**Monitoring usefulness.** Required columns and monitor count are met, and the seven decision rules are well-formed. The design still fails its own discrimination job. The pre/post-staffing row is `N/A` in every pattern column. Primary population is “standard complexity, non-Supplier Z,” which conditions on the two variables that carry the supplier shock, the priority rule, and the renewal season. That is not a reference process; it is selection bias, and the table already warns that Z is a non-random region/customer slice. The DFG monitor uses a fixed Jan–Feb baseline after the prose rejects universal thresholds and asks for seasonal references. Part A embeds a stripped monthly table (no complexity, supplier, median cycle, missing-resource, or duplicate-open-case fields). `discover_monthly_dfg` does not group by month. `filter_time_range(..., mode='events')` can split in-flight cases and is not discussed. Change-point logic is a single blunt OR on p90 and fitness gap, so it cannot separate logging, behavior, and performance — the distinction the rest of the memo depends on.

The skeleton is a competent exam answer. The numbers are not reproducible, one major mechanism is attached to the wrong signature, a stated logging confound is dropped, and the monitoring population is the one place the drift will not be seen. That is well below a nearly-flawless bar.
