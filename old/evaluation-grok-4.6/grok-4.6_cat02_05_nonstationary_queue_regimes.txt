This is a strong, well-structured process-mining writeup that hits the required headings, keeps estimands and assumptions explicit, refuses to treat recovery as a cruise regime, and generally respects uncertainty. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several numerical mistakes, one inconsistent data-audit claim, and a missed first-order stock–flow contradiction pull it well below the top of the scale.

## What is actually good

The estimand table is careful: opening-hours intensities, median service kept distinct from sojourn, \(\rho_{\mathrm{med}}\) labeled as an optimistic load *index*, completions-per-FTE treated as a descriptor rather than a quality metric. A2–A7 (WIP clock, missing 07:00 stock, contaminated 29 Apr, mix-as-label, hourly/daily peak conflict) are the right threats. Recovery (R4) is correctly ring-fenced as backlog liquidation; R5’s central fact — weekday outflow \(\approx\) R1 outflow while offered work, tail, and \(L\) jump — is the right diagnostic. The E1–E3 alternative is materially different, not a relabeling, and the flow-balance sketch is a legitimate third frame. Section 4’s composition biases and Simpson-style reversals (volume vs delay, flat productivity vs rising \(L\), recovery-as-capacity, intervention vs grand mean, mixture Fano) are on-point. Little’s Law is applied only where the author is willing to defend it, and median service is never substituted for \(W\). The Python block matches the spec and would, if run, correct some of the hand-copy errors in the prose.

## Substantive analytical flaws

**1. Stock–flow identity is never confronted.**  
Over 28 days, arrivals \(-\) completions \(= +110\). Daily `mean_wip` starts at 18 and ends at 19; in R5, net flow is \(-94\) while daily \(L\) only goes \(37\to 50\). If `mean_wip` is system size on the same population as the arrival/completion counts, that is a first-order contradiction, not a footnote about unknown 07:00 stock. Possible readings (L is active/visible work only; abandonments/transfers; snapshots miss a back office; definitions differ) all undermine using \(L/\lambda\) even in R1. The answer uses net flow as backlog change *and* \(L\) as Little’s \(L\) without reconciling them. A2 (night vs opening-hours snapshots) does not close a \(+110\) vs \(\Delta L \approx 0\) gap. This is the largest miss.

**2. Peak-window audit is internally inconsistent.**  
Bins are stated as 07–08, …, 18–19, so 08:00–09:59 is indices 1–2. Under that mapping *every* representative vector disagrees with `peak_08_10_pct` (07 Apr: \(17/101\approx 17\%\) vs 31; 15 Apr: \(\approx 18\%\) vs 32; 21 Apr: \(\approx 10\%\) vs 47; 25 Apr: \(\approx 12\%\) vs 22). The author declares 07 Apr “consistent” by summing **09–11** (\(12+20=32\)), then flags only 21 Apr as a conflict using the **correct** 08–10 bins. An off-by-one (09–11) actually reconciles three of four days and still leaves 21 Apr broken; that is the observation that should have been written down. Instead the ordinary-day vector is given a clean bill of health it does not deserve.

**3. End-of-R5 backlog figure is wrong.**  
Net through 28 Apr is \(-128\), not \(-110\). The \(-110\) already includes R7’s \(+18\). Section 7 then staffs a recovery plan off the wrong residual. Related slip in A3: if 06 Apr opened empty, unfinished work grew by **exactly** 110 (accounting identity), not a “lower bound.” A lower bound on *ending stock* under \(S_0\ge 0\) is 110; that is a different claim, and it is still only valid if there are no exits other than `completed`.

## Numerical and local errors

- **15 Apr completion Fano \(1.54\) / CV \(0.56\)** is false (population Fano \(\approx 2.06\), CV \(\approx 0.65\)). \(1.54\) is essentially the 07 Apr completion Fano. Arrival Fano \(3.85\) vs \(3.79\) is smaller but in the same table. The script would print the correct values; the report was not checked against it.
- **All-weekday `staff_fte` is \(6.15\), not \(6.20\)** (\(123/20\)). Downstream \(\rho_{\mathrm{med}}\) and \(L/c\) in that row are slightly off.
- Several hourly CVs are rounded in a way that looks like different estimators (21 Apr completions \(0.31\) vs \(0.33\); 25 Apr completions \(0.41\) vs \(0.45\)).
- Prose attributes “strong lag-1 autocorrelation” to weekend seasonality. Weekly seasonality is primarily lag-7; lag-1 in arrivals is a mix of weekday persistence and Fri–Sat drops. The code computes ACF; the narrative never reports it and slightly mis-describes the dependence.

## Softer issues (still charged under a strict bar)

R2’s \(W_{\mathrm{open}}\approx 4\) h is used as a sign-reversal against R1 after the matrix itself marks R2 as a weak “order of magnitude only” because of the Friday–Saturday inventory step — the reversal is directionally plausible and over-sold. Eight primary labels on 28 dependent days is acknowledged but still leaves R6/R8 too thin to carry “regime” estimates. \(\rho_{\mathrm{med}}\approx 0.93\) in R5 is correctly called optimistic; with \(S_{p90}\approx 83\) the understatement of offered load is not even roughly quantified. Daily Fano on \(n=7\) is treated as a precise underdispersion fact with no sampling caveat.
