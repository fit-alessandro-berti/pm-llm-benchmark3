The answer is format-complete and occasionally conceptually on-target, but it fails the core evaluation bar: feasible, minimally changed, temporally consistent counterfactuals. Several paths violate stated control-flow or resource rules, timestamps are invented rather than propagated, and later sections contradict earlier ones.

**Process feasibility (serious).** The partial order forces `Identity Check ≺ Document Check` in both the originals and no-originals branches. That is the opposite of the rules: the two checks may run concurrently when originals are not required, and originals—not identity—gate document check when they are. K-337’s table path is illegal: `Assessment2 → Decision → Compliance → Decision`. High-risk/high-value cases require compliance after the latest assessment and before any decision; suppressing a rework loop does not waive that. The derivation later puts compliance after Assessment 2, so the table and the narrative disagree. K-104 has C1 do “preliminary compliance in parallel with Assessment” and then final compliance immediately after; C1 is a singleton and cannot occupy two intervals at once.

**Temporal consistency (serious).** Queue snapshots are mostly unused. K-411 claims both checks finish “by ~12:30 instead of ~15:00.” Identity factually ends 13:20; D2 still needs a 4–8 h service after a morning queue of ~4 h, so document check cannot finish near midday. The writeup never separates earlier readiness of checks from earlier *completion* of the case—assessment still faces an overnight/queue wait. K-337’s 12–24 h saving ignores RMI2’s weekend wait (Fri 10:30–Mon 09:00, ~46 h) plus Monday–Wednesday work; a feasible path that still inserts compliance after Assessment 2 saves on the order of 2–4 calendar days, not half a day. It reorders a single certified reviewer; it does not reduce demand, and preliminary reading of incomplete packets can increase total C1 time. The 100-case writeup then books 100 C1 slots and calls the spillover a 4–8 h delay—understating collapse of a singleton pool. K-411 is said both to free assessment slots and to saturate assessment. C1 scarcity, D1/D2 substitution, and bottleneck migration are named, and the pilot table has the requested rows. That is why this is not a 1–3 answer. It is still far from a sound counterfactual analysis: illegal paths, impossible clocks, and a robustness ranking that rewards the most quality-destructive intervention.
