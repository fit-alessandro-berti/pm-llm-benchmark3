**6.0**

The write-up follows the required skeleton, cites well more than five artifacts, and offers three genuinely different interventions (policy/guardrails, evidence-loop digitization, repair-quote acceleration). That is the floor of a passing advisory note, not the ceiling. Under a strict reading it is repeatedly imprecise, causally sloppy, and incomplete in ways that would change a sponsor’s decision.

**Diagnosis.** The core contradiction is stated clearly and is tied to real objects: the 2,550/2,300 fast spine, 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen, 31%→9% cost erosion, Dispatch→complaint 96h/p90 180h, R1/R2 alignment deviations, Premium HV 22% vs 5%, 26% repeated evidence, 34h evidence→manual, 140h unclear-defect median, 68% fitting traces. That is real process-mining use, not Lean slogans.

It still under-uses the log. Fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces is not interpreted (many traces with small deviations, not a broken happy path). R3 is absent. The evidence↔manual ping-pong (780 + 890) is flattened into a single 34h wait. Escalation’s one daily 45-minute slot never appears. Fast approval→close without dispatch (350) is ignored. Resource math (7×36h, 82% queue threshold, 2 fraud specialists, Monday/campaign spikes) is atmosphere, not diagnosis. Those omissions matter because every later design collides with them.

**Redesign A — material error.** Forcing HV cases through evidence + fraud + a pre-dispatch gate is a defensible, non-generic move. Equating that with “96–120 hour reviewed/repair path territory” is not. Those medians are *end-to-end repair-path* times (quote, 30h customer approval, repair, QC). A still-replacement path with added gates is not that object. Repeating “96h+” in the table locks the error in. Two other holes: R2’s only exception is *Premium* low-risk accessories, yet Standard low-risk is left on ungated fast dispatch; and fraud→deny/repair (210/310) is collapsed into fraud→dispatch, so the decision set is silently rewritten. The 6–18h gate is cited without choosing the resource design that determines which end of that range you get.

**Redesign B — right target, thin mechanism.** Hitting the 22% unclear/missing-evidence mass and the 26% double-request loop is the correct scarce-capacity play. Trade-off on digital literacy/abandonment is honest. But routing *all* evidence returns into an auto-screen the prompt already calls outdated and non-segment-aware is untreated; the 220 evidence→fraud cases would be stripped of a risk step; 30–45% is a vendor-like *could*, not a designed yield; and “bypass the 34h manual wait” overclaims what a completeness portal can do for genuinely ambiguous defects.

**Redesign C — selection bias.** The 3.3% reopen rate is conditional on cases that *already survived* manual/fraud and a customer-approved repair. Treating it as a portable quality property of “the repair path,” then writing that into the table, is a causal mistake. The stated quote delay is part-price confirmation, not missing auto-text; “auto-generated quote” does not attack that wait. Bypassing manual eligibility for quote/repair on >EUR 400 work is an R1 hole. The 12.5% HV repair-rework rate and the 30h/p90 80h customer-approval wait are barely designed. “Right-first-time” is the one generic label in an otherwise specific set.

**Table.** Column polarity on “compliance risk” flips: Current/A use high = bad risk; C’s “High (avoids dispatch violations)” uses high = good compliance. That makes the Pareto object unreadable. “Dominated? Conditional (if Risk scales)” confuses feasibility with domination. A is not dominated if fraud is short-staffed; it is *infeasible* or *worse on cycle time/resource pressure*. Cost cells stay too vague even as ranges. Segment collapse into one row per design undoes the prompt’s main instruction.

**Failure modes.** Each design has two failures and one migration, so the rubric is met. They mostly restate the sacrifice paragraphs. Missed and more important: Premium service-credit exposure under A, auto-screen re-looping under B, R1/rework blow-up under C, and any migration onto the escalation board.

**Experiments.** Downstream checks (complaint→manual edge, QC→rework) are the strongest part. Sequencing logic is not argued. Starting with the high-complexity portal while ignoring the prompt’s two-week auto-screen update (and its 15–35% manual-inflow risk) is the wrong first information buy. “48 hours” and “abandonment +5%” are unanchored. There is no observation window despite a 180h complaint tail, no conformance KPIs (R1/R2 violation rate, fitting-trace share), and no holdout/segment-stratified read so you can tell prevention from delay.

**Net.** Format-complete, artifact-aware, and non-generic enough to be useful as a draft. Not sponsor-ready: one serious misread of the performance DFG, one causal leap on repair quality, a self-contradictory comparison table, incomplete R1/R2 handling, and an experiment plan that skips the cheapest test the data already offered. Those are not nits; they are the difference between mining-backed redesign and a polished narrative that overfits a few headline numbers.
