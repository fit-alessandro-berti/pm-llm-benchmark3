**5.3**

The write-up looks complete and professionally structured, but it fails the parts that matter most: hard eligibility rules, internal consistency between algorithm and assignments, and the explicit ban on presenting one policy as uniformly best.

**Hard-constraint failures in the assignment table**

C010 assigns **R8_Hugo** to Final Approval. Hugo’s Approval score is **0 (not allowed)**. The brief allows skill 1 only to prepare, not skill 0. HC4/HC5 and the skill matrix forbid this. Bruno already owns the case at skill 2 and could finalize; shielding his overtime does not justify an illegal assignee.

C015 assigns **R5_Eva** to Security Review. Eva’s Security score is **0**. Constraint 6 allows skill **1–2 under Gita/Hugo**, not skill 0. The queue note tempting Eva/Jonas does not override that rule. Valid supervised candidates would be Celine or Iris (skill 1), or keeping Hugo as owner.

Those two cases are then counted as training wins to claim **33% ≥ 20%**. Strike them and training share is **1/9 ≈ 11%**, under the stated target. C012 is labeled “suitable for trainee” and is instead given to a Quality specialist.

**Algorithm vs. table vs. constraints**

The score line includes **S_bt** (resilience) and never defines it. Supervised pairs are generated only for Security or normal/low work, so HC2’s “high-priority trainee + supervisor” path is unimplemented. The pair loop never rejects skill 0, which is exactly how Eva/Hugo slip through. `max(candidate_options)` on an empty list is unhandled, despite an “emergency override” in prose. Weights are defined by SLA bands but selected with `get_weights(case.priority)`. Fake numeric precision is never computed for C001–C016; the table is qualitative and sometimes contradicts the authors’ own penalties (especially SC3 on C003: reassignment already 2, complexity 0.66, diagnosis loop — they push it to 3).

**Capacity and “resilience”**

Hugo is loaded to the batch cap of 4, including urgent enterprise Security (3h, complexity 0.88), rework, supervision, and illegal Approval prep, with 5.5h left. Farid gets two urgent cases (2h and 4h) plus C010 sign-off. Eva gets two high-priority cases in 5h. Celine gets high-complexity rework on a 6h SLA plus another diagnosis plus mentoring. High-OT staff are zeroed out, which is coherent as a preference, but the work is dumped onto a new specialist bottleneck — the failure mode the text claims to prevent.

**Factual / framing errors**

C006 is described as an “inter-team handover … to Jonas.” Iris and Jonas are both Quality. Section 4 calls the policy **“Optimal & Sustainable,” “Minimal” SLA risk, “High & Balanced,” “Highly Resilient”** on every axis. The question required trade-off reasoning and forbade a single obvious optimum. There is no worked first-available or specialist-only roster for this queue, so the comparison is marketing, not evidence.

**Weaker but real issues**

Naming Bruno, David, and Iris as overtime risks in a team-wide note is poor socio-technical practice. Skill-mismatch audit only flags skill &lt; 2, so unsupervised Security by a skill-2 resource would pass. Training share is computed on events, not cases. PM4Py code audits a subset of indicators, does not **learn** weights, and omits `get_service_time`, `filter_case_performance`, and `view_sna`/`save_vis_sna`. Targets such as Gini ≤ 0.22 are arbitrary.

Sections 1, 5, and 6 are the strongest: objectives vs. hard/soft rules, a mostly human explanation, and a full indicator list. That is not enough. An auditable assignment policy that violates its own eligibility rules on Security and Approval, fabricates the training quota, and then declares itself optimal on every dimension is not near-flawless.
