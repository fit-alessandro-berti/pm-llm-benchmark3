**7.2**

The answer is a strong, format-compliant causal-policy writeup with accurate arithmetic and appropriately low epistemic confidence. It is not nearly flawless. Several conceptual and design choices are sloppy enough that a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” rejection is earned more by caution than by fully clean identification logic.

**What is done well.** All ten required headings appear. Tables use the specified columns. Word counts for §§1, 3, and 8 fall in range. Availability vs intensity, targeted staggered rollout, contemporaneous shocks, and CSAT nonresponse are distinguished. Median and p90 are never collapsed. Pre/post windows are transparent, control comparisons are labeled as arithmetic rather than causal, and the bounded ranges (−4.5h to +0.7h median; −6.3h to +9.7h p90) are internally consistent with the tables. I checked the main means and DiD gaps; they are correct. The threat list covers all mandated items. The recommendation (`expand with an embedded evaluation`) is the only defensible option and pairs a next branch, assignment mechanism, measurement plan, spillover monitoring, guardrails, and a scale-up rule. The claim ladder is correctly ordered and refuses the leadership one-liner.

**Format and estimand issues.** §1 never flags that `automated_fast_path_pct` is already 4–6% when `automation=0`. That is a measurement fact about “actual use,” not a minor omission. E3 puts median and p90 in one estimand. Treatment-version heterogeneity (Gamma after a readiness pilot vs Alpha/Beta as distressed high-volume sites) is mentioned only in passing. Interference is noted for the fraud desk, but the April routing campaign is treated almost entirely as a confounder to residualize rather than as a bundled co-treatment. The policy-relevant ITT of “do what was done to Alpha/Beta” includes the extra ~10% arrivals; the ceteris-paribus automation effect does not. Those are different estimands and the writeup blurs them.

**Identification is oversold relative to the design.** Augmented synthetic control is a reasonable *name*, but with two never-treated donors, outcome-based selection into treatment, and a systemwide May shock, the donor pool cannot support a credible synthetic match. Using Gamma as a donor only through June and dropping it thereafter is an ad hoc patch, not a design. The text admits the thin donor pool, then still ranks this first. A controlled ITS or an imputation event study with explicit “we cannot identify this” would have been more honest. Rejecting naïve TWFE in §3 and then estimating exactly that specification in §9, even with disclaimers, is inconsistent. Relative-time dummies are built and then used only for group means; the code never estimates the event-study contrast the identification section advertises.

**Quantitative discipline is good on arithmetic, weaker on interpretation.** Unweighted branch averages treat Alpha and Epsilon as equal despite very different volumes. Arrivals/FTE is not tabulated even though Alpha’s load rose sharply while FTE fell from 34 to 32—the central “capacity vs substitution” quantity. Raw intensity buckets show *worse* median and p90 at higher `automated_fast_path_pct`; the hand-computed branch-FE slopes without month effects (−0.02h and +0.23h per pp) are then offered as if they were additional evidence rather than a confounded residual. Escalation confidence of 65 is too high: the incremental DiD is about +0.9pp on top of a March systemwide +2pp fraud-desk jump. The late common-window DiD (median −1.3h, p90 −2.3h) is the most informative comparison and implies almost no remaining effect after the shared May shock; the “conditionally causal” ladder bullet still says automation “may produce a modest median reduction,” which overreaches the author’s own late-window numbers.

**Rollout and learning.** Choosing Delta first maximizes cleanliness and minimizes harm; it does not maximize learning about the target population (historically weak, high-complexity branches). Epsilon is closer to Alpha/Beta/Gamma on mix and CSAT. A 12-week pre/post window is short given a documented May regime shift. Case-level randomization is the right instrument; the decision rule’s 5% median threshold is arbitrary relative to the estimated range that includes zero.

**Code.** The CSV is complete and the validation/warnings are appropriate. Printing pivots satisfies “prints … without subplots” but is a minimal reading of “visualizes.” Clustered SEs with five clusters will be theater; the warning is present, which helps. Causal comments in the script are explicit. The script would run.

Net: a competent senior-analyst memo that gets the big questions right and does not fabricate an effect. Under a hypercritical bar it loses substantial credit for a poorly powered primary design, bundled-treatment confusion, a TWFE/code inconsistency, a missed measurement anomaly, and a claim ladder that is slightly stronger than the late-window evidence.
