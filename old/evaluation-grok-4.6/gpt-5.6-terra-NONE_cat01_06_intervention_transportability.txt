6.6

The answer is a strong transportability memo that correctly refuses a naive copy of Site A, treats the before/after contrast as confounded, and redesigns the intervention around statutory gates. It is not close to flawless. Several quantitative, structural, and modeling errors are material.

**What it gets right.** Sections 1–4 and 9–10 track the evaluation criteria. The comparison table covers the required features and the ones that actually matter (originals gate, legal loop, lock, mix shift, timestamp artifact). The graph states the shared mechanism as an AND-join on the verification pair, not “parallelism” as a primitive. Confidence scores stay low; the nine-hour median drop is not treated as an identified effect. The candidate design is receipt-aware and segmented. The pilot text correctly separates fidelity failure (no true overlap) from mechanism failure (overlap occurs, delay moves downstream). `conditional go` is the only defensible call.

**Process and causal structure.** The graph is mostly coherent but misspecified in places. `case_complexity -> originals_required` is a correlation, not a mechanism. Capacity and specialization should also point at `true_overlap`; they only hit waiting time. `treatment_parallelization -> rework` is asserted without a path (coordination vs. legal supplement). Site B’s DFG is almost unused. That is a serious miss. `Register -> Identity Check` is only 2,050/3,400; `Register -> Originals Receipt` is 1,860; `Identity Check -> Originals Receipt` is 1,020. Identity is already legally unlocked after registration, and a large share of cases already start it before or beside originals. For the 62% originals-required stratum, “start Identity with Originals Receipt” is partly the as-is process, so incremental overlap on the critical path is smaller than the write-up implies. `Legal Review -> Request Supplement` is 360/1,360 (~26.5%); that rate is never used to ground the congestion scenario.

**Quantitative scenarios.** The required “median up, tail down” case is present, and ranges are appropriately wide. They still over-anchor on Site A’s 9 h as an upper bound. At Site B, perfect overlap of service times saves about `min(3.4, 8.1) = 3.4` h plus some handoff wait—not 5–9 h—unless one also assumes large queue-discipline gains that are not the treatment. Optimistic eligible share 30–38% is the no-originals mass; it is inconsistent with later filters (digital and complete, spare legal capacity, low/moderate complexity). Receipt-aware “eligible share” is not a share. WIP caps set equal to pool size (4/7/3/2) are not controls; they just restate headcount.

**Pilot.** Mechanism-vs-implementation logic is the best part of the answer. The design itself is muddy: case-level 1:1 and “stepped rollout by week/team” are different experiments. n = 200/200 for a 3–4 h median shift in a process with median 44 h and p90 110 h is an unevidenced power claim. 12 weeks of intake at ~1,300 completions, with 20–30% digital-eligible, does not reliably yield 400 completed digital cases plus 90-day appeal follow-up.

**Section 8 is the clearest technical failure.** The Site A trees and the verification AND-join are fine. The legal/supplement fragment is not a sound optional loop. `supplement_path` is reused as both LOOP redo and a post-loop XOR branch; after the loop exits the model can take `Request Supplement -> Document Check -> Assessment` and then `Notify` with no `Approve`/`Reject`. That violates the required decision-before-notify structure and is likely unsound. Duplicate-label transitions are acceptable; the extra `POWL(...)` wrap, blanket `try/except`, and `noTraces` parameter are API hedging, not a working construction. Comments on what POWL cannot encode are accurate and should have been matched by a simpler `LOOP(Legal Review, Request Supplement -> Document Check -> Assessment)` then `XOR(Approve, Reject)`.

**Smaller defects.** Section 6’s textual POWL is an informal recursive sketch, not a well-formed operator tree. Section 2’s prose is within the word band but stays generic on interference through shared specialist queues. Several “transferable component” cells in Section 3 transfer a methodological lesson rather than a piece of the effect.

The analogical core is good enough that this is not a 5. The unused DFG, over-anchored hours, confused pilot, empty WIP rule, and broken legal POWL keep it well below 8.
