3.5

The write-up has the right skeleton and a recognizable qualitative story (do not pool; isolate the outage; something changes around case mix; staffing later changes the balance). Under a strict reading it is not a competent quantitative analysis. Multiple core numbers are wrong, the queueing objects are mis-defined, regimes overlap, several statistics appear invented, and the “reproducible” code cannot run.

**Format and estimands.** Headings match the spec. Opening hours as 12 h is a fair convention. The text correctly refuses to treat median/p90 service as mean flow time. That is the high-water mark. Symbols for λ, μ, and ρ are stripped into empty parentheses; units are written as `h¹` rather than h⁻¹. Estimands are never stated as such (what is being estimated, over which clock, at what precision). Five “threats” mix real inferential problems with plot points (the staffing start date is a data limitation, not Simpson’s paradox). The prompt’s independence warning and the open-vs-calendar clock for WIP snapshots are underplayed.

**Fatal data errors.** 2026-04-15 has 105 arrivals, not 112; 112 is the next day. The outage-day intensity 9.3 h⁻¹ is therefore false (105/12 ≈ 8.75). Weekend share is 8/28 ≈ 29% of days and 359/2519 ≈ 14% of arrivals, not “20% of days / 10% of arrivals.” Pooled open-hour intensities are about 7.50 (arrivals) and 7.17 (completions); the table swaps them (7.2 vs 7.5) and reports mean WIP 28.5 instead of ≈ 29.9. These are not rounding issues.

**Regimes are not a partition.** Primary ranges 06–12, 16–19, 20–28, 29 May-03 all contain weekends that are simultaneously labeled weekend regimes. “Weekend Post-Mix 25–03” also swallows three weekdays. Apr 13–14 is split out as “Pre-Outage” with service 25–29 / 48–60; those days are 25–26 / 48–50. Recovery as 16–19 is wrong: 16–17 are the catch-up days (129 and 112 completions, WIP 52 then 35); 18–19 are ordinary low-WIP weekends. Every cell is marked “High” confidence, including two-day slices. The mix shift that actually appears in the table is 20 Apr (complex_pct 23 → 41), not 13 Apr.

The alternative is not a different operating story. It is a coarser version of the same chronology, and it labels 15–19 “High-Complexity” when complex_pct is still 25–27%. A serious alternative would have been weekday/weekend first, backlog-state, burstiness, or “treat complex_pct as a coding change.”

**Queueing competence is weak.** Capacity pressure is treated as ρ = λ_arrivals / μ_completions, i.e. arrivals/completed. In any near-balanced window that quantity is ≈ 1 by construction; it is not utilization. Offered load vs FTE is never computed. The same false ρ (0.8–1.9) is then used for staffing advice.

Little’s Law is applied too freely. “Boundary stability = no outage” is not the LL boundary condition (start/end inventory vs throughput). Open-hours snapshot L does not give calendar sojourn when the shop is closed 19:00–07:00. Arrivals are used as the throughput proxy in every row, including the post-mix week where weekday inventory grows by 10–16 cases/day and the staffed window where inventory is falling. Marking baseline, weekend (n = 2), and staffed periods `applicable` is not defensible from these aggregates. The implied 2.25–2.63 h vs ~24 min median service (queueing plus overnight hold) is the interesting comparison and is not discussed. Verdicts should have been mostly `approximate only` / `not defensible`.

**Section 5 numbers do not survive a check.** For 07 Apr arrivals, Fano = Var/Mean is about 3.0 (population) or 3.2 (sample), not 1.8. For 21 Apr it is about 9.3 / 10.1, not 12.5. Fano is equated to CV²; those are equal only if the mean is 1. Index of dispersion is defined as a second copy of Fano. Lag-1 ACF 0.35 with p < 0.01 is not computed in the code; with n = 28, SE ≈ 1/√n, that p-value is not supported. CUSUM is claimed to put a p < 0.001 break on 13 Apr; the visible level shift is 20 Apr, and the code never implements a test that could produce that p. Four exemplar days are dismissed for the wrong reason (they do include weekday, outage, burst, and weekend); the real limit is that four profiles cannot identify a day-level stochastic process.

**Operations.** Peak smoothing and “collect opening backlog / hourly FTE” are sound. “Clear WIP = 47 before 07:00” is impossible: the center is closed. Staffing 7.5 FTE is backsolved from the bogus ρ. The pooled 6.5 FTE policy is a fair “looks good on the average” example, but it inherits the same utilization error.

**Python fails the contract.** Dates have length 28; every measure list has length 27, and different columns drop different days (arrivals lose 105 on 15 Apr, completed loses 46 on 18 Apr, etc.). `pd.DataFrame(...)` raises. Fano and “index of dispersion” are the same function; `acf` is imported and never called; `scipy.stats` is unused. Regime labels are assigned then overwritten, so printed summaries do not match Section 2. Little’s Law is a mean of daily L/λ, not L̄/λ̄ over a declared window. There is no pooled-vs-segmented comparison table. PM4Py remarks are comments only (acceptable) but add an extra invented API and never state, next to the constructors, that these frames are not an event log.

**What keeps it above a 2.** Correct heading set; outage isolated rather than treated as a high-μ regime in the narrative; median service not used as W; qualitative burstiness on 21 Apr; a named (if weak) alternative; a policy that pooling would mis-rank. That is a draft outline, not a finished analysis.

The score is 3.5 because the document would mislead an operations reader on intensities, utilization, mix-change timing, Little’s Law applicability, and every reported dispersion/p-value, and because the artifact that was supposed to make the numbers checkable does not execute.
