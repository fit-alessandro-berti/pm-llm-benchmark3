**7.0**

The final write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly grounded. It uses the normal vs peak mix shift, DFG branches, queue/utilization table, performance edges, and variant rules instead of chasing the historical average. It also obeys the hard constraints: no blanket capacity add, no simultaneous first-priority for every case type, and downstream overload is discussed for the main moves. It is not close to flawless.

**What is strong.** The four scenarios vary mix and disruption type, not just volume. Weekly demand (+43%) is separated from Urgent+Complex rising 31% → 49%, which is the right diagnosis. Bottleneck chains are sequential and evidence-based (pre-analytical 96% / 9.5h, accession 14h with only two specialists, assay 98% / 18h, tech val 101%, medical 22h, specialist 36h, hold→medical 18h→48h, recollection→receive 42h→60h, Urgent bypass 74%→58%). I1–I3 are correctly rejected as blanket rules and rewritten as lanes, adaptive batches, and conditional machine routing. I4/I5/I10 are trigger-gated. The trade-off table, redesign-induced residual risks, and leading vs lagging dashboard are in the required shape.

**Material flaws (these keep the score well below 9–10).**

1. **Process-mining error on technical-validation “exits.”** Peak `Assay → Technical validation` is 7,240, but peak tech-val outflows are 3,420+4,050+610 = **8,080**. Calling 4,050/7,240 “~56% of technical-validation exits” mislabels inflows as exits. Normal balances (15,890 = 15,890); peak does not. The mix point still stands, but the cited 74%→56% comparison is not a clean like-for-like rate.

2. **Recollection latency is conflated with the external loop.** `Pre-analytical → Request recollection` is 3h/7h. The 42h/60h edge is `Request recollection → Receive sample` (physician/courier/patient). “Aim for recollection decision within 4h, not after 42–60h” treats the long edge as decision time. I6 can start the loop sooner; it cannot turn a 60h recollection wait into a 4h lab decision.

3. **S1 off-trigger is inconsistent with the baseline mix.** Off requires Urgent+Complex **<25%**. Normal mix is already **31%**. After a wave, the policy may never turn off on mix grounds. That is a real control-design error.

4. **Protected slots are not reconciled with Complex-panel physics.** S1 reserves ~30% assay time for Urgent and 25% for Complex while Complex-panel needs **multiple assays** and machines are already at 98%. Twenty-five percent of slots for ~20–25% of cases that each consume more than one run is under-specified and may be infeasible. The package reallocates delay more than it creates capacity; the guardrails (80–90% Urgent ≤18h, Routine p90 96–120h, Complex p90 ≤180h) are not shown to be jointly feasible.

5. **“Acceptable” is soft where the contract is hard.** Allowing 10–20% Urgent breaches in stress, and setting Routine peak aims near the current peak (median 62h, p90 120h), is closer to “don’t collapse” than a redesigned service promise. Penalty exposure is not treated.

6. **Uneven use of DFG counts.** Comparing 8-week vs 3-week raw frequencies (e.g., batch-wait 760 vs 1,180) understates intensification. Weekly batch-wait starts are ~95 vs ~393. Some rates are handled correctly; this one is not.

7. **Interventions beyond the log are thinner than they look.** Auto-release of Routine normals and specialist tele-review are not in the candidate set and are only weakly constrained. “Few” leading indicators became ten.

The reasoning quality is well above a mid-range answer. The calculation slips, the recollection misread, the S1 hysteresis bug, and the untested slot/SLA arithmetic are enough that this is clearly not a 9+.
