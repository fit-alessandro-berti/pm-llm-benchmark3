**6.4**

The memo is structurally complete and strategically in the right family: it refuses national scale, refuses a full rollback, and treats Redesign R as a bundle of mechanisms with heterogeneous effects. That is the correct reading of the evidence. The five metric-conflict explanations are mostly sound, especially fitness vs. precision and median vs. tail. The evidence table meets the eight-item minimum and usually keeps facts, interpretations, and residual uncertainty in separate cells.

It is not close to flawless.

The final gates are broken on the page. “First-time-right 80%,” “complaints … 7%,” “manual activation 10%,” and “conformance … 95%” are missing the comparison operators. In a decision memo those numbers are the payload; as written they are not implementable.

The recommended action also blurs C and E. Pausing survey-skip and post-billing activation for everyone is the conservative control fix. Restricting coordinator ownership and the merged validate/schedule path to simple houses is a different choice, and it is not justified by the segment notes. Those notes blame skip-rule errors, missing permit checks, and manual activation—not ownership—for apartments, old buildings, and business customers. Rural is the one segment where fewer handovers may have hurt rescheduling, and it is almost ignored. The result is under-scaling the mechanism that looks beneficial and over-constraining the one that does not explain the damage.

Several other defects compound that:

- Low precision is attributed to coordinator ownership. The discovery/alignment evidence points to relaxed controls (skipped survey, repeated visits, billing before activation), not to single-case ownership.
- VOI ranks M1–M8 but rarely states how a result would flip A–E. M5 is ranked last while the memo still scales ownership; M7 is only “medium” even though the go/no-go rule depends on billing-before-activation conformance. That is inconsistent.
- “Second visits +11% to +18%” is wrong; the rates are 11% and 18%.
- An 85% utilization cap is invented and presented as an immediate order.
- “Proven gains” overclaims a 6-week, mix-shifted, incompletely instrumented pilot. The harder apartment/old-building mix is a first-order confounder and is used only as a table footnote.
- SLA (better for simple houses), NPS, business, and rural effects are left out of the decision logic.

Useful synthesis, incomplete discipline. The recommendation is directionally defensible and still too loose, too garbled, and too casually causal to score higher under a strict read.
