**7.3**

The final write-up is complete, correctly structured, and conceptually strong. It does not treat the restructuring as inherently good or bad, does not collapse success into average cycle time, treats East as structurally different from North/South, and keeps fairness and learning in view. The recommendation is appropriately “modify and pilot first,” with explicit warnings about what early averages cannot support. Those are the right conclusions.

It is not close to flawless.

**What holds up**

The mechanism map covers every required link (nominal handover reduction, central queues, utilization, rework, local expertise, role clarity, resilience, fairness) and ties them to log-visible measures. The three scenarios are internally consistent: optimistic is capacity-and-triage working; central is median improvement with a worse tail; adverse is a hidden bottleneck plus ownership loss. The observation that recorded handovers may *rise* even if local loops fall is the right correction to management’s story. Leading vs lagging indicators are separated properly. The eight “hidden by the average” risks include the required themes. The pilot (East first, North/South as controls, Legal/Risk held back because they are already at ~90%+ utilization, local liaison retained, guardrails and rollback) is the correct experimental design.

**What fails under strict review**

Section 7 would not run as written. `case_summary` renames `customer_segment` to `segment`, then later groups by `"customer_segment"` (`cycle_by_segment`, `fairness_by_team_segment`, `fairness_by_complexity_segment`) — that is a `KeyError`. `gb` is created before `resource_group` is added, then reused for `gb["resource_group"].shift(1)`; that is another likely `KeyError`. `filter_event_attribute_values` is called as `(log, values, attribute_key, ...)`; PM4Py’s signature is `(log, attribute_key, values, ...)`. Handover/working-together/subcontracting outputs are treated as `{(src, tgt): weight}` dicts; in current PM4Py they are typically NetworkX graphs, so `.items()` does not yield edges. `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` return tuples and are not unpacked. The `get_service_time` fallback uses the same column for start and end timestamps, which cannot recover activity duration.

There are smaller defects in the prose. The guardrail “rework within **X** events after TSS return” is an unresolved placeholder. “Cream-skicking” is a typo. Several mechanism-map edges lost their arrows (`local caseworker  Technical Shared Service`), so the claimed network change is harder to read. Temporal features are slightly oversold as a direct queue-WIP measure. Optimistic “early warning signs” are really exit criteria from the optimistic world, not properties of that world.

**Net**

This is a good forecasting memo with the right organizational and measurement logic. The required section on how one would actually compare before/after in PM4Py contains multiple executable and API errors, and the rest of the text has enough sloppiness that it cannot be scored as nearly flawless.
