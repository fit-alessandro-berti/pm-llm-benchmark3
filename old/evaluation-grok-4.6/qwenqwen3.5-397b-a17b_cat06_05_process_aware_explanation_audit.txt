The final audit is structured correctly, hits the main leakage classes, and gets the central distinctions right: full-trace / post-closure features are inadmissible; `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are kept; `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is treated as a temporally unavailable but process-legal pattern, unlike `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`; and SHAP importance is not sold as causation. Section 2’s DFG caveat and enabled / future-possible / impossible split, plus the SQL and nightly-snapshot lineage catches, are the strongest parts.

It is not close to flawless against the stated requirements.

**Completeness failure.** Section 1 must cover every local-only driver. `claimant_channel` (Case X) and `initial_reserve` (Case Y) are absent from the table. Case Z’s local explanation also uses `same_day_document_count_snapshot` and `calendar_week`; those are only audited globally, not as Z-specific explanation items.

**Process error on documents.** After the first `Coverage Check Completed`, evidence cycles have typically not started. Replacing `num_document_requests_total` with a t0 count of “0 or 1” treats an almost-always-zero prefix statistic as a repaired early-warning signal. That repeats the team’s “document rework is an early warning” mistake in weaker form.

**Lineage and internal contradiction.** `initial_reserve` is used in Case Y as a credible t0 driver with no lineage discussion; reserves are often revised after coverage check. Section 3 correctly parks `prior_claim_count_before_t0` as unusable until the SQL is fixed; Section 4 then lists it as a Case Y credible driver. Those cannot both stand.

**Case Z under-specified.** The prefix at t0 is only `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`. `Claim Closed` and `Damage Assessment Completed` are not merely mis-ordered; they have not occurred yet. The table labels the pattern only as process impossibility and underweights the same-timestamp / missing sequence-index artifact the prompt flags. The Z JSON note also omits the nightly document snapshot and week 52.

**Uneven severity and thin causal tagging.** Functionally similar full-trace edges are scored inconsistently (`Documents Requested -> Received` critical vs `Damage Assessment -> Settlement` high). Team claims such as “appeals cause breaches” and “the final team determines delay” are not systematically tagged as unsupported causal stories. The four standards (predictive validity, causation, actionability, fairness) are gestured at rather than separated.

**What remains solid.** Leakage on duration, appeal flags, final handler, full-trace variant, and `final_decision`; valid retention of congestion and prefix age; region/week kept as proxies rather than deleted; JSON narratives do not restated leaked outcomes; Section 5 covers the required validation agenda.

The result is a competent audit with several requirement misses and a few process/lineage contradictions. Under a strict reading, that is mid-6s, not a high score.
