**7.0**

This is a strong, process-aware audit that does the hard parts well: it separates t0-unavailable information from process-illegal orderings, keeps congestion and prefix-age signals, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as legitimate-but-future rather than impossible, and consistently refuses causal or interventional readings of SHAP. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several defects are material.

**What is solid.** Section 1 covers the required inventory and usually with the right defect class. Duration is correctly treated as near-label leakage; `full_trace_variant_id` and `appeal_seen_anywhere` as structurally unknowable at t0; `final_handler_team` as a closure overwrite whose 35% disagreement is the leakage mass; `prior_claim_count_before_t0` and `same_day_document_count_snapshot` as lineage bugs rather than inherently illegal features; `queue_length_at_t0`, `initial_damage_complexity`, and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` as keepable. The same-timestamp `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern is correctly called out as impossible *and* an ordering artifact *and* post-t0. Section 2’s prefix-recompute test, enabled/future-possible/impossible split, and DFG-is-not-normative caveat are the right validation design. Section 3 states the four standards (predictive, causal, actionable, fairness) without deleting every proxy. Section 5 is a coherent priority stack, including withdrawing already-issued explanations and treating the post-removal AUC drop as honest performance.

**Instruction failure in the revised narratives.** The task forbids reproducing leaked future outcomes in the revised narratives. Case Y’s narrative does so directly: it says the original score was dominated by a variant that revealed “the case’s actual future, including its appeal.” That restates a post-t0 outcome as a fact about this case. Case X is only slightly better: calling the 0.82 untrustworthy because its largest driver was “a future appeal indicator,” after the local explanation already set `appeal_seen_anywhere = 1`, tells the reader this claim appealed. The removed-feature list also reprints the full variant string `...DEC-APP-DEC-CLOSE`. An audit may name a dropped column; it should not narrate the hidden suffix.

**Technical inaccuracy in the DECLARE encoding.** The English constraints in 2(f) are right; the DECLARE arity is not. Standard `Precedence(A, B)` means A is a prerequisite of B. `Precedence(Settlement Decision Recorded[2nd], Appeal)` therefore means the opposite of the gloss “a second decision must be preceded by an appeal.” `Response` is used in the standard direction, so the section mixes conventions. In a process-mining audit that invokes DECLARE by name, that is a real error, not a wording quibble.

**Internal inconsistencies.** `prior_claim_count_before_t0` is labeled “mild future leakage” and **High** in the same row. Future sequence patterns that are equally unusable at t0 are split between **Critical** and **High** without a stable rule (process-valid future leakage is High except when it is Appeal, which is Critical). Section 2(e) says explanations may cite only enabled or already-observed activities; Section 4 then cites historically typical future paths, which is actually the better practice. The two sections are not reconciled.

**Over-trust in leftover attributions.** The answer correctly says you cannot salvage a leaked model by subtracting bad SHAP terms, then still promotes the remaining leaked-model directions (`queue_length`, complexity, channel, reserve, region) as “credible_drivers.” Those signs can move after retraining. Calling them provisional associations would have been tighter; treating them as the revised explanation overstates what the contaminated fit can still support. `initial_reserve` is also promoted for Case Y while its write-once status is unverified, which conflicts with Section 3’s “nothing until repaired” class.

**Smaller unclarities.** Section 4 was required as valid JSON; a methodological preamble sits in front of it. “These two admissible facts” in Case X follows four listed drivers. `calendar_week = 52` is a confounding/regime issue, not really a Case Z *ambiguity* on the same footing as the missing sequence index. Employee experience is extra coverage, not a miss, but it is given **Medium** while being absent from the explanation artifacts the table was asked to cover.

The piece would sit in the high 8s if the Case Y/X narratives had stayed prefix-only and the DECLARE predicates had matched the literature. Those are not polish issues; they are exactly the failure modes the rubric warned against.
