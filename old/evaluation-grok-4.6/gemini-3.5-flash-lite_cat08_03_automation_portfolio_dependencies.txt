**Material errors**

The closing trade-off attacks the wrong activity. Management’s high-frequency temptation is **Register application** (11,880, very high feasibility, 8 min, 1.5h wait), not **Receive application** (already 72% digital, 0.2h wait, 1% rework). A1 is auto-registration. Calling Receive the thing to refuse is a basic misread of both the table and the candidate list.

The same paragraph cites the **58h external valuation wait** as a governing constraint and then never puts A7 (or any valuation parallelization) in a recommended portfolio. That is the problem’s textbook synchronization delay (2,350 cases; required in 71% of equipment finance; an input to committee packaging). Rating A7 as “tertiary,” process-level Medium, and first-year Low is analytically wrong given the prompt’s own framing.

“Portfolio 2 augmented with A12” is **five** modules. The budget cap is four. The rollout quietly drops A10 and lands on four, but the recommendation language and the phased plan do not match. A10 is also rated High first-year while listed as depending on A9, which is not in the recommended set.

**Inverted or confused dependency logic**

A6 is described as a downstream risk that “can overload analysts.” The resource note says the opposite: clean inflow overloads analysts *unless* normalization is improved. A6 is 75 minutes of analyst work on 5,200 cases; it is the mitigator, even if faster normalization later enlarges the 110-minute credit-analysis queue. That second-order effect is never cleanly separated.

A3 is treated as something that will “paralyze” three specialists. A hit pre-classifier with escalation is meant to *reserve* those specialists for true hits. Entity-mismatch load (1,750, 55 min, 31h, 38% rework, 1,160-loop back to KYC) belongs to A4, not A3. The write-up repeatedly blurs A3 and A4.

A9 is casually described as “automated accounting ledger updates.” It is contract generation with control-rule checks. That is a domain error.

The matrix says A6 is a “prerequisite for *upstream* credit and committee tasks.” Wording is simply backwards.

Product and segment variants are almost unused: Micro / SME / High-exposure, working-capital vs equipment vs credit-line renewal, collateral incidence 71% vs 18%, weekly committee for high exposure, four-eyes only on high-exposure, 14% contract remediation on renewals. The matrix is in A1–A12 order, not a ranking. First-year ratings conflict with the final choice (A10 High then dropped; A7 Low on a weak argument; A2 Low despite 8% misroutes that change control path, collateral, and four-eyes).

**Net**

This is a competent consulting memo with a sensible eventual four-module set and a correct refusal of frequency-first thinking. It is not a rigorous process-mining recommendation. The headline activity is wrong, the largest wait is cited and then dropped, concurrency and variants are ignored after being flagged, module count contradicts the budget, and several resource arrows point the wrong way.
