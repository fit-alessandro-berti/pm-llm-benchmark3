**3.2**

The write-up looks organized and uses the required headings, but the core inference task is largely unsuccessful. Three of four primary labels are wrong, several “supporting” episodes are factually false, eligible sets are repeatedly incomplete, and basic scheduling definitions are confused. Structure cannot rescue that.

## 1. Queue diagnoses are mostly incorrect

**QA is not preemptive priority.** There is no interruption in any QA `service_segments`. A03 finishes 08:20–08:30; A02 runs 08:32–08:52 and completes; A04 starts only at 08:58 after A1’s recorded calibration. A04 never preempts A03 or A02. The section-1 “strongest supporting episode” (“A04 preempts A03”) and the section-2 claim (“A04 preempts A02 at 08:58”) are both invented, and they contradict each other.

Worse, priority is the rule QA most clearly *rejects*. At 08:20 the waiting set includes A04 (P1, arrived 08:13); the server takes A03 (appointment 08:20). At 08:32 it takes A02 (P3) while A04 (P1) is still waiting. At 09:05 it takes A05 (P3) while A08 (P1) is waiting. At 09:32 it takes A07 (P3) over A08 (P1). After the A03 appointment insertion, the sequence is ordinary FIFO. Calling this preemptive priority with confidence **85** is a serious misread.

**QB is not non-preemptive priority.** B02 interrupting B01 at 09:16 is the textbook definition of preemption. Using that event as support for a *non-preemptive* label is a definitional error. Non-preemptive priority also does not predict “resume the interrupted job.” When the server becomes free at 09:31, waiting jobs are B01 (P3, incomplete) and B03 (P2); a priority rule selects B03. The observed resume of B01 is FIFO / resume-preempted-job, and it *contradicts* priority. Later, at 10:14, B01 is resumed again over B06 (P2). The answer never reconstructs 09:48 (second stop, “dispatcher reassignment”), where B03 (P2) starts while B05 (P1) has already arrived.

**QC is not EDF.** The first two free-server decisions already kill EDF and simple priority:

- 10:10, present: C01 (dl 13:00), C02 (14:00), C03 (12:30), C04 (14:30). EDF → C03; priority → C03; SPT → C02. Observed: **C02**.
- 10:18, now including C05 (P1, dl 10:50). EDF and priority → C05; SPT → C03. Observed: **C03**.

The table also miscomputes EDF on its own truncated sets: among {C01, C02}, EDF is C01 (13:00 < 14:00), not C02; among {C01, C06}, EDF is C01 (13:00 < 15:00), not C06. The 11:40 choice of C01 (P3, dl 13:00, 36) over waiting C07 (P2, dl 12:00, 18) contradicts EDF, priority, *and* SPT at once. The note that C07’s deadline was “less urgent” than C01’s is backwards (12:00 is earlier than 13:00). EDF was not even in the candidate list; SPT/SEPT plus deferral of the long C01 is the obvious competitor and is never seriously tested.

**QD skill-based routing is the only broadly defensible primary label**, and even that is oversold. Skill constraints are resource feasibility, not by themselves a next-job rule. Within a feasible set the data look more like FIFO + exclusive-skill reservation + one honored appointment (D06 at 11:30) than “priority as secondary.” D-M at 11:25 selects D04 (P3) over D08 (P1). D-X at 11:48 selects exclusive-skill D07 (P3) over D10 (P1). Confidence **90** is too high once priority is treated as a real secondary component.

Three decision times per queue is the minimum asked for, and the chosen times are often the least informative (QA 08:02 with one job; QD 11:00 with two dedicated resources). Prediction columns are unreliable. “Priority → B01 (non-preemptive)” at 09:31 is not what priority predicts. QC’s EDF column is wrong twice. QD drops the appointment column even though D06 is an appointment job. Arrival-scan delay (±2 min) is mentioned in the scenario and almost never applied except as a hand-wave for B05.

## 3. Supporting / contradicting cases recycle the same errors

- QA “support” is a non-event. The real contradiction is not “A06 after A05 despite appointment”; that is FIFO plus a late appointment. The real contradiction to their own label is A02/A05/A07 running ahead of waiting P1 jobs.
- QB “support” is a preemption used to justify non-preemption.
- QC’s C07-after-C01 story treats a completion instant (12:16) as if C01 and C07 were both waiting. The live choice was 11:40.
- QD: “D08 starts after D07 at 11:35” is false. D08 is 11:35–11:43 on D-M; D07 is 11:48–12:16 on D-X. The offered excuse (D08 not yet eligible when D07 was selected) is impossible: D08 had already finished.

Apparent violations are not sorted into “explained by a known constraint” vs “needs an undocumented exception” with any care. D-F’s meeting, D-X’s 11:20 start, A1 calibration, C1’s 10:10 free time, and exclusive skills (D05 only D-F, D07 only D-X) are recorded constraints, not mysteries.

## 4. Counterfactuals do not discriminate

QB 09:31: the two “competitors” are a misnamed priority rule and a vague dispatcher override. A real fork is resume-interrupted vs priority vs SPT (remaining times ~31 vs 12). The proposed log field is fine in spirit, but the hypotheses are ill-posed.

QC 12:16: both EDF and priority predict the only remaining job, C07. An episode that both theories predict cannot separate them. The interesting fork is 10:10 (SPT vs EDF/priority) or 11:40 (FIFO vs everything else).

No additional observable is specified crisply enough (what field, at what timestamp, which branch predicts which value).

## 5–6. Exceptions and data needs are generic

Four exceptions appear, but they are not tailored to *these* traces and are mis-ranked. Appointment windows are called “low” plausibility even though A03 at 08:20 is the cleanest QA deviation from FIFO. Delayed scans are blamed for A06, which does not need that explanation. Missing, and more important:

- treating skill/availability as hard filters rather than as a “discipline”;
- resume-preempted-job as a first-class QB rule;
- frozen-batch SEPT on QC’s initial backlog (C02–C03–C04 by `est_min`, C01 deferred);
- C05 as an SLA-endangered policy switch (the prompt explicitly asks for this);
- D-F idle 11:36–12:00 because D05 would overrun the 12:00 meeting if started then, but *could* have finished if started at 11:21.

Section 6 omits several fields the prompt already flagged as necessary given these ambiguities: appointment tolerance, current remaining time (B01), logged eligibility set, and estimate revisions. “Policy version” is listed with no link to a suspected change-point.

## 7. Synthesis is only half right

QD as most identifiable is fair. QB as least identifiable is defensible (QC is a close second). The “most dangerous policy to infer” claim is inconsistent with the rest of the memo: the author *already* inferred preemptive priority on QA and EDF on QC from observationally inadequate evidence. Those are the dangerous inferences. Appointment scheduling is a secondary QA component they themselves under-used.
