The answer is analytically strong and structurally compliant, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it still contains judgment slips, an unverified (and possibly non-executable) PM4Py artifact, and a few category/consistency problems that keep it well short of 9+.

**What holds up.** Event-time role reasoning is disciplined: Eva is not treated as QM from the current master; aliases are normalized before SoD; `svc_qms` is never given a standing human role; D-IC-04 is correctly voided as prohibited subdelegation; D-QM-01 is not proven by the later QMS insert; RR6 is not converted into a collusion finding. The required headings, case table, alternative explanations, and control recommendations are complete. The main case calls on CHG-502, 507, 508, and 510 are right.

**Material defects.**

1. **Required “executable” code is not demonstrated and may be wrong.** The write-up invents a precise PM4Py release (“2.7.23.3 … July 17, 2026”) and asserts a `keep_violations` flag. Classic PM4Py four-eyes filters used `positive=True/False` (and often *keep compliant cases* by default). If `keep_violations` is not a real parameter, the block raises; if polarity is inverted, RR1 “violations” are the opposite set. Admitting the library was never run, then still advertising expected screening results, fails the task’s executability bar. The multi-column `df.loc[mask, cols] = [a, b, c]` assignments are also a known pandas footgun.

2. **CHG-504 as primary AUTHORIZED is too firm.** The brief itself says security cannot exclude credential sharing. VPN overlap plus a self-report is medium identity assurance, not authorization proved. Primary **INDETERMINATE** (or AUTHORIZED only as a thin secondary) would match the paper’s own “do not convert an identity gap into innocence” rule. Calling it authorized while tagging DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM is internally mixed.

3. **CHG-509 should not sit so comfortably in INDETERMINATE / possible VALID_SUBSTITUTION.** D-QM-02 requires principal absence. Leave ends 14 Aug; 15 Aug is merely a Saturday. In a control assessment the *proponent* of the delegation must prove the condition. Absence not on the record is a failed predicate, not a toss-up. Weekend-as-unavailability is an argument, not evidence. Secondary VALID_SUBSTITUTION overstates residual possibility.

4. **CHG-505’s primary UNAUTHORIZED bundles unlike failures.** Late/out-of-scope retrospective review is a clear RR4 break. The initial Farid approval is only conditionally authorized (no CI-44 link; safety rationale not in the log). Collapsing those into one UNAUTHORIZED row, then stacking two secondaries, blurs what is proved versus what is open. A split finding would have been cleaner.

5. **D-QM-01 is narrowed more than the instrument strictly requires.** Restricting it to `Approve_Change` only is a choice, not a given: “QM approval for production-equipment change controls” can be read to include retrospective QM review of those controls. Outcomes for 505/510 still fail on *cooling_system* scope, so the error is harmless here, but the formalization presents the narrow reading as if it were textual. The math also omits the CI-44 incident predicate that the code later treats as decisive.

6. **Identity handling is not fully consistent.** CHG-506 account use on sick leave is unresolved because Ben denies it. CHG-505 review uses the same account deeper into the same certified leave and is still attributed to Ben (“no event-specific denial”). Denial is relevant to *intent*, not to whether the human is bound. RR5 as written makes both events identity-impaired.

7. **Secondary labels are used loosely.** CHG-508’s DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM is not an identity defect; it is a successful alias resolution that *reveals* a real RR1 breach. That category is for unresolved or corrupted identity, not for “a naive string compare would have missed this.” CHG-501’s portfolio-level SUSPICIOUS_PATTERN is better confined to §4 than attached to a clean substitution.

8. **Asha’s 13 Aug VPN window is under-analyzed as a delegation condition.** D-QM-01 is “while Asha is absent.” At 09:15–09:40 she is on VPN and claims to have worked. The answer correctly says leave does not strip *her* standing QM power, but it is too quick to treat formal leave as satisfying the *delegate’s* absence predicate for the whole day. No Ben approval falls inside that window, so the miss does not change a case result; it does weaken the formal rule statement.

9. **Small unclarities.** Business-day counting is well specified and the 505 lateness result is robust, but “end of the second business day” is stipulated rather than sourced from the site procedure. Grace’s verification is correctly left open, yet the matrix’s claim that her high-risk *approval* authority “would be absent” is unused speculation. Eva’s near-monopoly on verification is a pattern signal that §4 barely develops. The dangling GitHub/PyPI footnotes do not earn the API claims.

**Net.** This is a high-end compliance memo with the right instincts on aliases, service accounts, stale masters, and “suspicion ≠ proof.” It is not near-flawless: the mandated PM4Py check is unrun and possibly API-incorrect, two identity/authority classifications are kinder than the record, and a few labels paper over remaining uncertainty. Those are enough, on a harsh scale, to stop in the mid-8s.
