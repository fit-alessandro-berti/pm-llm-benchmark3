**5.3**

The write-up is a complete, well-structured operations note that hits every required heading and the right qualitative tensions (specialization vs pooling, P1 reservation vs overflow, Validation as a merge bottleneck). It is not close to a high score. Under a strict reading it is a competent narrative wrapped around unauditable numbers and a non-computational “simulation.”

**What is actually solid.** Assumptions A1–A7 and the two objectives are the strongest part: ranges are converted with an explicit mixture-of-midpoints rule, ME/ES parallelism is a decision variable rather than a hidden constant, and O2 is defined on worst-case P1 SLA plus utilization spread rather than base-case mean delay. The three policies are implementable enough to audit (eligibility, P1 jump, overflow at queue>2 / wait>15, sequential P1 on a cross-skilled resource). Sections 6, 7, and 9 cover the requested secondary effects, event attributes, PM4Py uses, and a P1-protecting pilot. The recommendation is not “Hybrid because it is fastest in the base case.”

**Capacity arithmetic does not survive audit.** Block arrivals are copied correctly; almost everything built on them is not. Mechanical / electrical / software branch volumes are inflated (~5 / ~6 / ~4 cases per block vs the table’s ~3 / ~4 / ~2). U2 is mishandled: “E and ES demand doubles; proportions are renormalized” is *not* “electrical minutes ≈ 400 per 2h.” After renormalization, total arrivals are unchanged and ME is not doubled, so electrical offered load rises modestly, not to saturation-vs-456-minutes. Daily minutes, 2-hour slots, and “cases of capacity” are mixed. Farah’s 08:00–12:00 window is treated as if it still backs afternoon electrical/software. Emil is the only resource after 18:00 (Deepa ends at 18:00, overtime is 30 minutes) and that coverage hole is never turned into a load statement, even though P2’s 8-hour clock for 16:00–20:00 arrivals runs off-shift. The 90% fairness/fragility rule is invoked but never computed from consistent offered-load ÷ available minutes.

**Section 4 is disconnected from Section 2.** The policy×scenario table has the required columns, but the cells are labeled ±20% and then filled with round numbers that do not follow from the (already wrong) loads. P2 SLA staying in the 80–94% band is optimistic once shift end, 30-minute overtime, and evening single-coverage are taken seriously. Fairness and resilience are adjectives, not measures. That is allowed as “qualitative ranges,” but then the same invented levels reappear as if they were model output.

**The Python block fails the task’s hard requirements.** Demand-by-block and U1–U5 mechanics are not encoded. U1 surge, U2 renormalization, U3 Chen absence, and U5 intake outage are not implemented; only a tail factor is toggled, and U5 is incorrectly mapped to `u4_factor=2.0`. Comparison metrics come from `approx_metrics()` (`base = 85/65/55` times a scenario multiplier), not from the log or a capacity model. Resource contention, calendars, breaks, setup, and preemption are constants or comments. `RES[r_int]["Electrical"]` / `["Software"]` will hit `None` and blow up for Ben/Ada on ME/ES. Diagnostic work is collapsed to one activity named `"Diagnostic"`, so handover-of-work and skill-specific service analysis cannot recover Mechanical vs Electrical vs Software. `choose_resource("Electricity" if False else "Mechanical", ...)` is leftover junk. try/except around PM4Py is a shrug, not a demonstration. A scaffold is allowed only if omitted mechanisms are identified; fabricating the scoreboard and omitting the demand table are not disclosed as first-class gaps.

**Smaller but real defects.** Markdown ate several formulas (`(1p)`, stray `**`). Strict specialization still parks Deepa in both families. Cross-team +5 minutes is defined and then barely used. Preemption (at most once, 10% of remaining) never enters any delay bound. Complex-tail draws are means only; no range, seed, or interval is propagated into SLA statements that look like point estimates.

The piece reads like a good first design review, not like a finished, checkable analysis. The structure and policy language keep it above a failing score; the load errors, evening-coverage blind spot, and fake quantitative engine are why it cannot go much above the midpoint.
