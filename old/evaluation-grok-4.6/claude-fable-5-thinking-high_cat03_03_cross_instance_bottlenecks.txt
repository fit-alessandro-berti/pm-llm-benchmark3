The answer is complete, well structured, and largely faithful to the required sections and hard constraints. It still falls well short of a high score because several central causal claims are sloppy or wrong, a few quantities are misstated, and the ranking logic is not internally consistent.

**Critical-path error in the main cascade.** The centerpiece coupling story is that J3’s FIX-A hold delays J4’s machining, which delays J4’s pack, which delays S1 and therefore J1. That chain is not supported once the afternoon constraints are applied. J4 is batch-ready at 13:10; B2 still cannot start before RACK-7 is free (14:35) and J5 is ready (14:40). Removing the morning fixture wait would have left J4 waiting for J5/RACK-7 anyway. FIX-A delay is slack on the path to B2 and S1, not additive with the rack delay. The line that “J4’s ~1 h 50 min (FIX-A) plus ~1 h 25 min (RACK-7) largely explains” the 19:10 pack, and the later claim that FIX-A and RACK-7 “caused the J4 lateness that drove S1’s hold,” are therefore incorrect. The same error reappears in Narrative A and in the optional “rank tools first” rationale. This is not a wording quibble; it is the main object-centric punchline, and it fails a max() feasibility check the author already had the numbers to perform.

**Related over-attribution.** CNC-2 is idle only 10:05–10:47. The remaining wait to 11:45 is unconstrained in the record. Treating “up to 1 h 50 min” as fixture delay is a possible upper bound; treating machine capacity as ruled out for the whole interval is not. Afternoon, RACK-7 is free 5 minutes before J5; calling the rack the binder for B2 overstates a near-tie that J5 actually wins. The 14:50–19:10 interval after B2 starts (~4 h 20 min) is never decomposed, so the 19:10 pack time remains largely unexplained. Rework, which appears twice in the job DFG, is never considered as a reason J3 is late to B1 or J4 is late to pack.
