6.4

The write-up hits the required eight-section skeleton, assigns ASE and TSI correctly, and treats the requested borderline pairs and robustness perturbations in a way that is mostly internally consistent. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

The central design failure is that the “preferred” business-semantic relation is typed structural isomorphism after identifier stripping. Sections 3 and 4 therefore emit seven singleton-or-pair clusters, i.e. the finest object-aware partition. The prompt explicitly warned against treating that partition as automatically best and asked for a semantic relation built from multiplicities, externals, qualifiers, *or lifecycle motifs*. Motif-level grouping is only gestured at as an undefined fourth relation (LME) and then used in §§5, 7 and 8. So the design space does not actually contain three operationally distinct equivalences that are all used.

The canonical-signature scheme is not reproducible as stated. The prose promises renamed persistent IDs plus E2O incidence; the code counts `{type: n}` per event, contains a `pass`, derives types from ID prefixes (`O1` → `O`, not `Order`), and never puts `EXT-` flags or object identity into the returned tuple. That encoding cannot see that the same package (or a replacement package) persists across Pack/Dispatch, so it is not TSI and is weaker than the relation defined in §1. Table “signatures” are informal slogans, not the scheme from §2.

Other required items are thin or missing: `max_objs` is never discussed; `cluster_equivalent_ocel` is called without a lead type `Order`; BSE_5 and BSE_6 are interpreted jointly despite being separate preferred clusters. PM4Py remarks are directionally aligned with the question’s hints but stay generic on the actual key structure.

What remains strong—ASE/{X1,X2,X5,X6,X7} vs {X3}/{X4}/{X8}, X1≅X2 only under renaming, the X7 Dispatch E2O/`removed_before_dispatch` distinction, X5/X6 externals, and the four-purpose recommendation—keeps the answer clearly above a mid-fail, but the collapsed equivalences, broken signature, and skipped API points block anything near the top of the scale.
