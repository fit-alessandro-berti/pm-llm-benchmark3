**3.0**

The submission has the required skeleton and the right vocabulary (interval reasoning, certain/possible/indeterminate, midpoints as lossy). Almost every material number, conversion, and classification is wrong, and several sections contradict each other. Under a strict reading this is a formatted but unsafe analysis.

**Core model errors.** The required construction is: resolution first, then `true = recorded − offset` with `offset ∈ [a,b]`. The answer instead converts timezone, applies offset as a vague `±` band, then rounding, and treats HUB as exact points while still citing ±0.5 s. HUB should be about `[T−1.5 s, T+1.5 s]`, not a degenerate instant. Floor-to-minute is treated as a ~50–60 minute window (OPS-BER `08:14–09:04:59`; VENDOR-NY `08:00–08:59:59`). Displayed `10:14` / `04:00` means only that minute, then a few minutes of clock error—not an hour.

**Timezone and DST are inverted.** June CEST→UTC is handled inconsistently; T-402 `Containment_Complete` is a HUB/UTC `10:02:00` rewritten as `09:02:00`. For the 2025-10-26 Berlin fold the answer maps “pre-fold” to UTC+1 and “post-fold” to UTC+2. The opposite is true: first 02:xx is CEST (UTC+2), second is CET (UTC+1). Alert_Received is then collapsed to a single `[01:50, 02:49:59]` blob, against the instruction to keep disjoint unions.

**Section 2 and Section 3 do not cohere.** T-401 Investigation is tabled as `[08:14, 09:04:59]` UTC, then S1 is given as `[600, 1800]` s from “(10:14–08:00)”. Those cannot both be true. Even their own interval is 14–65 min, which is a **possible** S1 breach, not a certain one. Correctly normalized, T-401 start is roughly `08:12–08:16` UTC; S1 vs 15 min is **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION**, S2 vs 45 min is **CERTAIN_PASS**. They mark both S1 and S2 certain violations. That inverts the operational reading.

**Arithmetic and SLA constants are repeatedly wrong.** 36 h is written as 259200 s (72 h), not 129600 s. T-407 `14:17−14:00` becomes `[1700, 1800]` s instead of 1020 s. T-408 S2 `55 min` becomes 3900 s (65 min). T-403 S2 is 42 min (pass), reported as 70–80 min (certain fail). T-405 S2 is called CERTAIN_PASS while their own upper bound is 3600 s > 2700 s. T-406 S2 is given as negative durations even though `12:52` is after `12:14`.

**T-408 S3 is a clear miss.** Close at `2026-06-03 06:30` vs containment `2026-06-01 16:05` is ~38 h 25 min, a **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** of 36 h. They report 86400 s and CERTAIN_PASS. That is one of the few high-trust, same-system tests in the set.

**Inferences are sloppy.** T-404 start is computed from 10:35 (correct source) but the note says Containment_Complete; HUB uncertainty and the 18–25 min band are not propagated into S2 (`[10:10, 10:17]` → containment `10:55` is ~38–45 min, not `[1800, 2400]`). T-406 contradicts itself: entry delay “does not affect true occurrence” and then the occurrence interval is shifted by −5 min, while ±300 s clock and nearest-minute rounding are dropped. T-407 correctly flags that Sent ≠ Received, then scores S1 on Sent→Start.

**Coverage of required tests is thin.** Many testable profile pairs are omitted. The “Acceptance Region” column is labeled SLA even on profile rows. Negative durations are labeled CERTAIN_VIOLATION instead of internally inconsistent / INDETERMINATE. T-409 discards the positive part of a ~`[−8, +16]` min band and calls the rest indeterminate.

**Sensitivity analysis does not survive its own numbers.** T-404 S1 upper bound 1260 s still exceeds 15+2 min (1020 s) and 15+5 min (1200 s); they flip it to CERTAIN_PASS and talk about 1262/1265 s (seconds, not minutes). T-401 profile “fixes” at ζ=2 only because the duration interval is invented. No case is re-evaluated as an interval; the matrix is decorative.

**Section 5 is not the requested program.** It is a stub (`# Add remaining cases similarly`), with `data` as a dict then `data.extend` of rows, naive `fromisoformat` + `astimezone`, offset taken as `offset_bound[0]` “for simplicity,” datetime midpoint via `/ 2`, and the wrong PM4Py surface (`converters` / `algo.conformance.temporal` instead of `pm4py.format_dataframe` and `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile`). It would not run and does not implement interval classification or DST alternatives.

**Sections 6–7 are generic.** Several POSSIBLE/INDETERMINATE rows from §3 never appear; T-406 CERTAIN_VIOLATION is listed as if it were an uncertain result. Governance hits the requested keywords but the enforcement line (“require non-overlapping intervals for SLA violations”) is confused, and there is no serious policy on whether overlap alone is enough to charge a breach.

The writeup looks complete and would mislead a reviewer who does not recompute. Structure and taxonomy awareness are the only reasons this is not lower.
