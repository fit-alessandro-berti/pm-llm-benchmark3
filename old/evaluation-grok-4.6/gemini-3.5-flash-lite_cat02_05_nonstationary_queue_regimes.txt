**5.2**

The write-up has the right skeleton and several sound process-mining instincts (do not pool day types, do not treat median service as flow time, do not treat the outage or the catch-up day as steady state, Little’s Law needs a stable window, four hourly snapshots are not a stochastic model). Under a strict bar that is not enough. Material arithmetic errors, internally inconsistent segmentation, and several first-order analyses that were required by the prompt but never actually performed keep this far from a high score.

## What is actually solid
Section 1 defines the right objects and correctly refuses to treat the table as an event log. The five threats are real. Isolating 2026-04-15 as an exogenous shock, and calling 16–17 a transient drain rather than a high-capacity regime, is the correct operational reading. The pooled-staffing policy in §7 is a legitimate Simpson-style warning. Format compliance is fine: the eight headings are present and the Python block exists.

## Material numerical errors
These are not rounding noise.

- Pooled arrivals are \(2519/28 = 89.96\), not \(89.6\). Pooled mean WIP is \(836/28 = 29.86\), not \(31.5\). The headline Little’s Law figure \(31.5/89.6 \approx 4.2\) h is therefore computed from two wrong inputs.
- Baseline weekdays (6–10 and 13–14 Apr) are \(\lambda = 701/7 = 100.14\), \(\mu = 697/7 = 99.57\), \(L = 142/7 = 20.29\). The table sets \(\lambda = \mu \approx 100.4\) and §6 then uses \(L = 19.8\). Three different invented numbers for one window.
- “Weekday demand \(\approx 105.8\)” is wrong. All 20 weekdays average \(2160/20 = 108.0\).
- Intervention weekdays (29 Apr–1 May) complete at \(363/3 = 121.0\), not \(118.4\). That \(118.4\) is unexplained.
- Reported arrival variance \(971.4\) and Fano \(10.8\) do not match the table (sample variance \(\approx 902\), Fano \(\approx 10.0\); population Fano \(\approx 9.7\)).
- Surge \(L = 45.4\) is a range midpoint, not the mean of the days they appear to be using (\(310/7 = 44.29\)).

A process-mining answer that advertises “defensible precision” cannot publish this many free-floating statistics.

## Segmentation is inconsistent with itself
The primary table’s date bounds overlap. 18–19 Apr sit in both “Weekend Low-Load” and “Post-Outage Recovery”; 25–26 Apr sit in both Weekend and “Complex-Mix Surge.” Recovery is labeled `2026-04-16 to 2026-04-19` with \(\lambda \sim 107\), which is impossible if Saturday/Sunday are inside the window (\(112+99+48+39)/4 = 74.5\)). The estimates only make sense for Thu–Fri, which is not what the boundary says.

The Python `assign_regime` function does not implement the table: it peels weekends off first, so code and prose are answering different partitions. Weekend itself is not a single regime. Early weekends have median service \(28\)–\(30\) min and \(\sim 22\%\) complex; late weekends have \(34\)–\(36\) min and \(\sim 40\%\) complex. Pooling them reintroduces the same mix-shift the author criticizes.

The alternative segmentation is only a sketch. No dates, no \(\lambda/\mu/L\), no confidence. Type B and Type C are almost the same post-20 Apr days, so it is not a “materially different” operating story.

## First-order evidence that was ignored
Cumulative flow is the obvious backlog audit. Net arrivals − completions over 28 days is about \(+110\) cases. Mean WIP never leaves the teens-to-fifties, and the last Sunday still shows \(L = 19\). That identity fails unless WIP is not the full system, completions are not all exits, or the counts are dirty. The prompt already warned that 07:00 backlog is missing; the answer notes the missing \(B_0\) and then never checks flow balance. That is the single most important omitted observation.

WIP also *rises* from \(47\) on the outage day to \(52\) on 16 Apr. Calling 16–19 Apr “aggressive backlog draining” that “restores capacity” overstates a two-day partial catch-up that left a secular pile-up; the intervention later only nicks it (\(43 \to 32\) on weekdays) while cumulative inventory stays huge.

Weekday-only arrival Fano is \(\approx 0.66\) (underdispersed). The “massive” daily Fano \(\approx 10\) is almost entirely weekday/weekend mixture. Daily weekday demand is regular; the operational burstiness is *intraday*. The answer gestures at this and then leaves the decisive number uncomputed.

## Hourly vectors and §5 are below spec
The task asked for analysis of the representative hourly vectors *and* the daily sequence: Fano/IDI, CV, lag dependence, change points, and why four days are not a model. What appears is one wrong daily Fano, a hand-waved service CV \(> 0.6\) inferred from a late-period median/p90 with no distributional assumption labeled, a qualitative lag-1 claim, and a one-line dismissal of the four days. No hourly Fano/CV (ordinary day \(\approx 3\), 21 Apr \(\approx 9\)), no comment on the 11:00–14:00 completion zeros, no multimodal burst on 21 Apr (\(34\), then \(24\), then \(14\)).

The change-point “heuristic” is `rolling(5).mean().idxmax()` on `complex_pct`. That finds the *peak of the high-mix plateau*, not the break. A CUSUM or two-means split would put the jump at 20 Apr; their index will land near 27 Apr. Presenting that as a transparent detector is a methodological error, even with the disclaimer.

## Little’s Law section is thin and unit-confused
Only four windows, and two of them use the wrong \(L\) and \(\lambda\). Weekend, recovery, and intervention are missing — recovery is exactly where a “not defensible” verdict was required. \(W = 0.197\text{ days} \approx 2.36\text{ hours}\) silently multiplies by \(12\), not \(24\). That can be justified (open-hours sojourn, snapshots only 07:00–19:00, overnight hold excluded), but the table never states the convention, so the equivalence looks like an arithmetic slip. There is no discussion of whether \(\lambda\) or \(\mu\) is the right throughput proxy when inventory is trending, and no comparison of implied \(W\) to median service (which would have shown waiting dominating, *without* subtracting a median from a mean).

Capacity pressure is never quantified. A labeled bound such as \(\rho \gtrsim \lambda \cdot \mathrm{median}(S) / (C \cdot 720)\) would have shown baseline \(\sim 0.6\) versus late weekdays approaching \(1\) even before right-skew. That is the natural complement to the WIP story and is absent.

## Assumptions, mix, and code vs. spec
Assumptions are not labeled as such. The complex-share jump is treated as a real mix shift; the prompt’s coding-change alternative is barely mentioned, even though service and `complex_pct` moving together is also what a recoding would produce. §8 is missing a second coded dispersion statistic (only Fano + WIP autocorr), does not print hourly or three-way pooling comparison tables, and the PM4Py block is a name-drop, not a sketch of how `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` / arrival-finish rates would be built from a *case-level* log. The code’s regimes do not match §2.

## Net
This is a competent outline with the correct qualitative morals, damaged by wrong numbers, overlapping regimes, a flow-balance hole, unanalyzed hourly evidence, and a change-point gadget that cannot find a change point. On a lenient rubric it might sit around \(7\). Against the requested standard — nearly flawless required for a high mark, minor defects heavily penalized — it is a mid-fail on accuracy and consistency.
