**6.5**

The write-up is substantively strong on fairness framing and constraint adherence, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, format violations, several PM4Py errors that would produce the wrong objects, and a bottom line that outruns the evidence keep it well below the top band.

**What is done well.** Sections 1–6 follow the required agenda and mostly respect the scenario constraints. Raw approval gaps are not treated as discrimination; case-mix differences are not used as a blanket excuse; `risk_score_band` is treated as potentially contaminated; rights-preserving steps are treated as a source of burden rather than as steps to delete. The eight fairness dimensions are actually used, not merely listed. The analysis strategy correctly separates disparity, legitimate routing, disparate impact, and measurement uncertainty, and the sensitivity plan for suspect covariates (risk band, documentation completeness, prior-case count, endogenous channel) is the best part of the memo. Variant reading of V4/V5 versus V3 is accurate, and the capacity facts (7 bilingual workers, 5 specialists with dual roles) are applied to the right groups.

**Format.** The required contract is exact top-level headings `# 1` … `# 8`. The answer adds a title and a “Key Insight,” then demotes every required section to `##`. That is a direct miss of a stated output constraint.

**Section 3/8 overclaim.** Residual gaps after a limited adjustment are repeatedly converted into “implementation-level unfairness” and, in the bottom line, “the process is procedurally unfair.” The same memo then lists reasons the residuals could be biased covariates, unobserved need, or prudent extra review. Those caveats belong in the verdict. The warranted conclusion is closer to **indeterminate-to-concerning on procedure, not established unfairness**. The later walk-back helps, but the headline still overfits.

**PM4Py plan (largest technical deduction).** This section is the required operational core and contains errors that would break the analysis:

- `filter_variants` is called with a flat activity list. In PM4Py that is a collection of variants, so each string is a length-1 variant, not the intended path.
- `filter_case_performance(..., max_performance=30.0)` labeled “slow cases / right tail” keeps *short* cases if `min` defaults to 0. That inverts the forensic filter.
- `filter_paths_performance` is called without the required path set.
- `get_all_case_durations` is stored as `waiting`; total cycle time is not waiting time.
- Section 2 invents `get_service_waiting_time`, which is not in the allowed API.
- Log-skeleton / DECLARE cannot test “Accommodation Review ⇒ `disability_accommodation_requested`.” Those miners constrain activities, not case attributes, unless the attribute is first materialized as an event.
- `insert_case_service_waiting_time` results in the group loop are discarded.
- The code never implements the matching/stratification design from Section 2, so the plan and the script diverge.

**Smaller accuracy nits.** Groups B/C/D are called “minority groups”; only B is defined that way. Supervisor-review residuals are treated as evidence of unfairness in Section 3 after Section 4 correctly says the data cannot distinguish prudent oversight from less-trusting defaults.

**Net.** A competent policy memo with the right structure of concerns, spoiled by noncompliant headings, a too-strong verdict, and a PM4Py appendix that would not actually recover the intended comparisons. That is clearly above a mid answer, and clearly not near-flawless.
