## Decision rule is not deployable and ranks the wrong quantity

The eligibility rule is internally inconsistent. Repeated alerts require a risk increase of 0.05, but I-07’s `p_now` *falls* (0.75 → 0.72) and is still treated as eligible. Operators are missing throughout the rule and the priority formula, so the rule cannot be executed as written.

The stated score is some variant of `adjusted p × value − false-alarm burden − spillover`, while the task’s own EV object is `ARR × value − swarm cost − false-alarm burden`. Ranking by breach *exposure* rather than *intervention value* is the central conceptual error. It systematically overweights high-value, low-effect, long-horizon cases (I-08: ARR 0.05–0.15, FAB €5,000, 8 h left) and underweights high-effect, short-horizon cases (I-07). Spillover enters as a near-constant and does not change ranks. Time-to-SLA, stage-window closure, option value of waiting, and specialist interruption cost never enter the rule.

Calibration handling is a false precision. Midpoint additive shifts treat 0.04–0.09 and 0.05–0.12 as known constants. The NNLS `+0.10` is invented: the brief says the sample is small and *both* calibration and effects are uncertain, not that the model underpredicts. Uncertainty should widen intervals and raise the evidence bar, not inflate `p`.

The published scores do not match the stated formula. I-07, I-08, and I-10 are exactly unadjusted `p_now × value` (0.72×5,000=3,600; 0.43×100,000=43,000; 0.46×11,000=5,060). I-04’s €36,720 matches neither 0.48×60,000 nor 0.395×60,000. Adjustments and false-alarm penalties are described and then not used.

The portfolio is the value trap the prompt warns against: three Strategic Enterprise cases, including I-08 (tiny effect, largest FAB, long horizon, known overprediction; lower-bound EV is negative: `0.05×100,000 − 450 − 5,000 = −450`) and I-04 (10 h left, first alert—high option value of waiting). None of this is a consistent priority policy.

## Expected-impact table is not auditable and is quantitatively wrong

Upper-bound ARRs on the named four cases sum to `0.22+0.28+0.30+0.15 = 0.95` expected breaches, not 3. Midpoints sum to ~0.67, not 2. Adverse lower bounds, with one null effect, are ~0.2–0.4, not 1. The table treats probabilistic risk reductions as if whole cases were saved.

Spillover is self-contradictory. The parenthetical `4 × 6 × (0.01–0.03) × €100k` is €24k–€72k; the cells say €180–€420 / €360–€840 / €720–€1,680. Formula and numbers disagree by about two orders of magnitude. €100k is the *maximum* case value, not an average (~€29k). Optimistic “High” confidence violates the instruction not to treat the ranges as known causal effects.

I-05 vs I-07 is directionally right and is the strongest part of the answer. The rest does not hold together. A 4-hour cooldown after a non-intervened alert would expire I-07’s 1.5 h SLA. Post-swarm re-entry is not conditioned on that.

## Fairness safeguards are mostly non-operational

The “quota” for value < €10k fires only if *no* higher-value eligible case exists, so it never protects I-07/I-01/I-10 against I-08. That is the starvation pattern this portfolio already implements (3 SE, 0 SME, 0 NNLS). Label bias is reduced to “flag for retraining” and a generic synthetic control; there is no treatment indicator, untreated-only training rule, or logging of offer vs. take-up. Spillover review at €500/week is disconnected from §3’s own spillover scale. Public-sector / regulatory non-monetary duty is mentioned only as a reserve that was not actually left open.

## Monitoring is a checklist, not an identification plan

Weekly calibration and named triggers are fine as hygiene. Causal estimation via post-hoc DiD/synthetic control after value-based selection is confounded by design; there is no shadow mode, marginal-slot holdout, or logged propensity. Recalibration still talks about “the threshold” after claiming there isn’t one. Feature-freshness / imputation failure (the I-06 risk) is unmonitored. Stage-at-swarm (was the intervention still pre-Diagnosis Complete?) is unmonitored.

## Net

The answer knows the buzzwords—stage eligibility, cool-down, calibration, spillover, feedback loops—and then fails the things the rubric says separate strong from weak: effect-heterogeneous allocation, coherent capacity math, reproducible EV, and honest uncertainty. It is an outline of a policy, not a policy.
