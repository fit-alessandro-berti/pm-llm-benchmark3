## Defects (why this is not a 9.5–10)

**1. Subcontracting loops are compared on topology, not rate.**  
Gita–Carla–Gita (36 / ~126 ≈ 29%), Hugo–Dan–Hugo (29 / ~104 ≈ 28%), and Lena–Kim–Lena (11 / 44 ≈ 25%) are far denser than Aline–Elena–Aline (33 / 510 ≈ 6.5%). The risk table’s claim that “this topology is not unique” is technically true and mildly misleading. Bot_Match→Kim→Bot_Match is the fairer comparator (~25%); Aline/Elena is not. Section 4 later asks for normalization, so the error is incomplete use of the author’s own standard, not a contradiction of the data.

**2. Formal vs informal specialization is blurred.**  
Elena is labeled “Team A approver” and Felix “Team B approver.” Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix look like designed routing, not informal specialization. Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo look like emergency/role specialization. The answer’s benign table leads with “assigned team routing,” which is the better account for the high-volume pairs, but the lead thesis still folds everything into “specialization” and never cleanly separates formal assignment from informal rush channels.

**3. PM4Py API slip, even inside a caveat.**  
`filter_four_eyes_principle(..., retain_violations=True)` is not the usual signature; the pandas/log filter uses `keep_violations`. The text says to check the installed version, so this is not a hard factual error, but it is the one place the required tooling is named incorrectly. Several other calls (`reconcile_with_authoritative_audit`, `encode_declare_response`, object-style `.business_time` on a DataFrame group) are undisguised fiction. That is acceptable as pseudocode; it is still unclarity in a section that also presents importable `pm4py` calls as if they were adjacent.

**4. Small calibration drift between §1 and §6.**  
Section 1’s mixture includes “control weaknesses.” Section 6’s opening sentence lists only team assignments, emergency processing, and data-quality defects, then introduces Lena/Kim afterward. The report wording is slightly more exculpatory than the analysis. The residual five same-person justifications that are *not* in the 12-row quarter-close batch are also easier to miss than they should be.

**5. “High, if confirmed” slightly under-ranks already-flagged PO SoD exceptions.**  
Shared-account and bot-mapping contamination are real, so verification-first is justified. Still, 42 Create PO / Approve PO exceptions are an on-the-record control finding; parking them behind actor reconciliation without a parallel “confirmed-SoD, high-amount emergency” slice is a bit too deferential relative to the nine bank exceptions.

None of these are unsupported accusations, invented facts, or collapsed risk categories. They are the difference between an excellent evidential memo and a nearly flawless one.
