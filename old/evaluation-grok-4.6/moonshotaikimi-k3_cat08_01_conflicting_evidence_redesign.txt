**7.5**

The final write-up is a strong, non-generic executive redesign note: it uses the DFG, performance DFG, conformance/alignment, rework stats, segment outcomes, and resource constraints; it treats the fast path as partly a skipped-control artifact rather than a true efficiency frontier; and the three redesigns are different *levers* (routing/enabling conditions, evidence-and-fulfillment integrity, resource/commitment architecture), not parameter tweaks. Trade-offs are explicit, dominance is not overclaimed, and the experiment plan (holdouts, adjacent-edge watch, conformance as a guardrail against “faster by skipping work”) is better than most answers on this prompt.

It is not near-flawless, and the defects are real.

**Process-mining hygiene is sloppy in a place that matters.** Closing 3,015 + denied 220 + abandoned 390 = 3,625 is arithmetic on *edge frequencies*, not a trace-level statistic. Calling this “3,625 of 5,000 traces” and “~27% censored or unlogged endings” over-reads a DFG. Loops (complaint/reopen, rework, reshipment), possible multiple closes, and the fact that even the frequency graph does not fully balance (dispatch inflows 2,365 vs outflows 2,580) mean that gap cannot be interpreted as a case-count. Building Stage 0 around that number hardens the error. The same section also slides from “highest abandonment *rate* in the unclear cohort” to implying that the 320 evidence-stage abandonments *are* that cohort.

**One internal design contradiction.** Redesign C says the cell owns Premium claims end-to-end (Premium accessories 18% + Premium high-value 14% = 32%), while accessories stay on the 8h fast lane. Failure-mode economics then staff/risk the cell as if it served “only ~14% of volume.” That is a capacity-planning error, not a quibble.

**A few over-tight causal stories.** Summing selected mean edges (14+34+22+30h) as the decomposable 96–120h reviewed path is loose: those edges are not one serial spine, the 34h evidence return is customer wait (not something you “launch in parallel” away), and repair/QC time is mostly untouched by A. A and B also share a pre-dispatch quality gate, so the triad is less clean than advertised. The table’s cycle-time cell for A compares high-value mainly to the *reviewed* path and understates that many high-value cases today are on the 20–28h fast path, so cohort cycle time is mixed, not uniformly improved.

**What still keeps it well above a middling score:** segment-conditional logic (keep the highway for accessories; do not romanticize the slow path), the 31%→9% cost erosion used correctly, selection vs treatment on complaint rates, R1/R2 as enabling conditions rather than audits, QC slack vs the 82% manual-review cliff, and an experiment sequence that can detect relocated failure demand. Those are the pieces the rubric actually asks for. The misses are specific enough that a 9+ would be indefensible under a strict reading.
