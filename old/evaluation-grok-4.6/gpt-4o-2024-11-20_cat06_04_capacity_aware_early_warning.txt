4.3

The answer is structurally complete and avoids the most naive failure modes (it correctly shelves I-03, distinguishes I-05 from I-07, and does not rank solely by `p_now`). Under a strict decision-theoretic and operational standard, that is not enough. The policy contains a reversed calibration correction, an unauditable and physically impossible impact table, internal contradictions between the stated rule and the allocation, and a weakly justified use of scarce slots.

**Calibration is inverted.** Underprediction means the model’s `p_now` is too low and should be shifted up by about 0.04–0.09 for SME; overprediction means Strategic Enterprise probabilities should be shifted down by about 0.05–0.12. The answer does the opposite, and it states the wrong causal story explicitly rather than making a one-off arithmetic slip. That error would systematically over-select overpredicted Strategic Enterprise cases and under-select underpredicted SME cases. The adjustments are also collapsed to arbitrary point values (0.04 and 0.08) and then barely used in the table. This is a first-order failure of the robustness the prompt asked for.

**The 0.50 threshold is both forbidden as an organizing device and applied inconsistently.** The task warned against a single probability cutoff. The rule still leads with `p_now > 0.50`, then marks I-04 (0.48), I-08 (0.43), and I-10 (0.46) eligible anyway. I-05 is coded ineligible rather than eligible-but-suppressed, conflating stage eligibility with persistence policy. The promised expected-net-value score is never computed, so the “Moderate / High / Moderate-High” tiers cannot be audited.

**Portfolio choice is only partly defensible.** Rejecting I-03 is correct. Suppressing I-05 is correct. Reserving one slot is a reasonable reading of the management preference. The rest is weak. I-07’s midpoint direct surplus is on the order of \(0.26 \times 5000 - 450 - 50 \approx €800\), among the lowest in the book; I-04 still has 10 hours, a €2,000 false-alarm burden, and a segment that overpredicts; I-02’s effect band (0.08–0.22) is modest and sits in the overpredicted Strategic Enterprise segment. Meanwhile I-01 (acceptable Public Sector calibration, first alert, 0.18–0.35 effect) is deferred for “high uncertainty,” which is factually the wrong segment diagnosis. I-09 is parked for sparsity without triggering the manual review the fairness section later requires. Time-optional high-value cases are treated as intervene-now cases; option value of waiting is not used. Specialist interruption (two people, 45 minutes, diagnosis-queue delay of 8–20 minutes per slot) is never operationalized.

**Section 3 fails the auditability requirement and is numerically impossible.** Three selected cases have a maximum combined absolute risk reduction of \(0.22+0.28+0.32=0.82\). Reporting 1.3–2.1 breaches avoided in the optimistic scenario is not a rounding issue; it cannot occur under the supplied ranges. Midpoint effects sum to about 0.60, not 0.9–1.5. Direct economic benefit should be on the order of €9k–€24k before spillover, not €46k–€112k. No arithmetic is shown, so the inflation cannot be reconstructed. Spillover euros imply inconsistent average untreated values across scenarios. The optimistic net claim of “>€60,000” is an artifact of those errors. The prompt required ranges to be treated as uncertain, non-causal estimates; the table treats them as if they were known effects and then mis-multiplies them.

**Repeated-alert policy is directionally right and technically thin.** The I-05 / I-07 contrast is the strongest part of the memo. Cool-down “for 2 full cycles” is undefined (30-minute refresh? stage cycle?). The 10pp escalation trigger would not fire on I-07’s 0.75→0.72 move; the new log is doing all the work, which should have been the primary rule rather than a side clause. Re-entry on “two SLA thresholds” is meaningless. Re-swarming after an actual intervention is not specified, even though the process note forbids repeat swarms without a material new event.

**Fairness and monitoring are checklist-complete, not decision-ready.** The five required risks are named and paired with safeguards, which is better than a principle dump. The safeguards still collide with the live policy: the decision rule reserves capacity for Non-Native-Language Support, the noon allocation reserves the spare slot for a possible regulatory arrival, and Section 5 promises a 20% weekly quota for SME/Public Sector. Uncertainty is used as a reason to wait on I-09, which starves the sparse segment of the very outcomes needed to learn effects. “Separate model feeds” is a confused substitute for logging treatment, identifying a causal estimand, and using an experiment, staggered rollout, or proper observational identification. A random holdout of SLA-critical incidents is proposed without any ethical or operational constraint. There are no numeric triggers for changing the rule (calibration error band, negative rolling net value, segment disparity, spillover rate). Policy-induced label drift is mentioned, not designed against. Non-monetary public-sector obligations are essentially omitted except for the reserve-slot footnote.

**Net.** The memo looks like a policy because it has the six headings. It does not survive an audit of signs, arithmetic, or consistency. The two independently fatal defects are the reversed calibration correction and the impossible impact table; the inconsistent threshold, unshown EV scores, and I-07/I-04 slot use would already block a high mark. This is a mid-structure, low-rigor answer.
