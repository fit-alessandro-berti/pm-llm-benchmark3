## §1 Estimands — internally contradictory

Time origin at `Submit` is correct. Almost everything else is not.

- **Event and censoring are defined inconsistently.** “Event” is any terminal disposition, while “censoring” includes Transfer. Two sentences later Transfer is “not censored but [a] competing event with unknown outcomes.” Competing events have observed alternative exits. Unknown external resolution after Transfer is a missing outcome after an intermediate state, not both an event and a censoring rule.
- **Open cases with age ≥ 72 are omitted from the censoring definition** (“age < 72h”). S09 (92h), S11 (81h), S25 (79h), and S26 (104h) are already past the horizon.
- **Estimand 1 is malformed.** `P(open AND duration_or_age_h < 72)` is a snapshot of currently open *young* cases, not P(unresolved at 72). Cases still open before 72 have not reached the horizon. Then S09 (open at 92) is scored 1 “since 92 > 72,” which **contradicts the definition just given**.
- **The required contribution cases are not used.** The prompt asks for resolved-at-76, open-at-92, withdrawn-at-27, transferred-at-68. The answer substitutes S01 (resolved at 18). S03 (76h resolve) is the already-observed breach and is never classified.
- **Withdrawn-at-27 = 0 under “unresolved at 72” is undefended.** If the estimand is “not resolved by 72,” S06 is a 1 (it never resolves). If it is “still lacking any terminal disposition at 72,” it is a 0. The answer never separates those.
- **Transferred-at-68 scored as 1 under P(U72) and 0 under P_breach** is an undeclared change of assumption, not a contribution rule.
- **Estimand 2 is a crude sample proportion**, not a cumulative incidence. S09 is written as contributing 0 rather than remaining at risk / right-censored for late resolution.
- **Estimand 4 is a leftover fragment** (`_transfer`) and is a hazard, not an SLA tail-risk estimand.
- **Estimands are not mapped to operational decisions**, which the prompt required in this section.

A strong answer would have defined, at minimum: (i) $P(T_{\text{resolve}}>72)$ with competing exits handled explicitly; (ii) CIF of resolution after 72, or of any unsuccessful/unknown exit; (iii) $P(\text{still unterminated at }72)$. It would then say, for each, whether S03 is an observed late resolution, S09 is an observed sojourn past 72 plus residual censoring, S06 is a competing exit before 72, and S08 is a competing exit / informative removal at 68.

## §2 Encoding — table and prose disagree; labels reversed

- Resolved as competing-risk code 0 collides with the usual “0 = censored” convention.
- Open is censored only if `age < 72`. Late opens are undefined.
- The table’s three Transfer assumptions (censored / unknown-outcome event / 50% breach) **do not match** the four listed below (competing risk / “pessimistic = censored” / “optimistic = resolved” / interval-censored).
- **Pessimistic and optimistic are reversed.** Treating Transfer as ordinary censoring is *not* pessimistic; it assumes transferred cases would have followed the same resolution hazard as those who remain. Pessimistic treatments are: count Transfer as failure, bound the CIF, or assume no later internal resolve. Optimistic is treating Transfer as success. The 50% imputation in the table is arbitrary and not a standard sensitivity.

Three Transfer regimes worth encoding: (1) competing exit (CIF of Transfer); (2) IPCW/censoring at transfer time, with a discussion of informativeness; (3) worst/best bounds (never resolved vs resolved at transfer). Interval-censoring from transfer to cutoff invents a follow-up window the extract does not give.

## §3 Bias — right vocabulary, wrong cases

- Survivor bias is not only “ignoring withdraw/reject/transfer.” A completed-resolved analysis also drops **open** cases and keeps late successes (S03 76h, S13 110h, S20 74h, S28 88h), which **overstates** timely resolution.
- Informative Transfer is real for S08/S32 (P1, escalated, long), but S21 is a P1 transfer at **26h**. The “longer durations” claim is selective.
- “Open at 72h (S09, S24) vs open at cutoff (S26, S31)” is false. S09 is 92h, S24 is 67h, S26 is 104h, S31 is 23h. The needed contrast is: **open and aged < 72** (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) are right-censored before the SLA; **open and aged > 72** (S09, S11, S25, S26) are already-observed sojourns past the deadline, not ordinary pre-horizon censoring.
- Cohort imbalance is asserted with a weak pair (S18 vs S21) and no accounting of non-randomized rollout or incomplete compliance.

## §4 Analysis plan — a textbook estimator error

**“Cause-specific hazards (Fine-Gray model)” is wrong.** Fine–Gray is a *subdistribution* hazard model. Cause-specific Cox treats other exits as censored for that cause. Conflating them is a disqualifying methods error.

For *these* data the recommended primary is also unjustified: $n=32$, a handful of Transfers, and six cohort×priority cells. A Fine–Gray Cox model is not identified in any useful sense. The natural primary is **Aalen–Johansen / nonparametric CIF** (resolution; Transfer; withdraw/reject; still open), with KM only as a *biased contrast*. AJ is dismissed as “complex.” Landmarking and RMST/RMTL are waved off rather than tied to decisions (e.g., RMST to resolution for staffing; landmark at 24/48h given current stage and `last_event_gap_h`).

KM of time-to-resolution that censors withdraw/reject/transfer **overestimates** resolution probability. That is never stated cleanly.

## §5 Process features — leakage vs time-varying confused

Current stage, rework, and gap are legitimate **time-varying** state descriptors in a clock-reset or start–stop design. They are not “potential leakage” by default. Leakage is using a *future* prefix token as if it were known at $t=0$. Immortal time is required by name: coding `escalated=yes` as a baseline covariate credits waiting time before Escalate to the escalated group (S03, S13, S28). That discussion is missing.

The state diagram is not a competing-risk multi-state graph (no separate exits for Resolve / Withdraw / Reject / Transfer / cutoff; Rework is a dangling box). The §5 PM4Py snippet is invalid XML, the wrong importer, and a likely-wrong `get_variants` import.
