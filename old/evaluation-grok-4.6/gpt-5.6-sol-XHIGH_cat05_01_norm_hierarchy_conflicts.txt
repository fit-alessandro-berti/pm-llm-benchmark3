**8.0**

The answer is a strong, disciplined compliance analysis: headings match the spec, facts are separated from assumptions, rules are applied at the time of the act, late ERP stamps are not treated as conclusive, and post-hoc approvals are not treated as automatic cures. The case-by-case work is mostly legally coherent. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading there are material inconsistencies, one outcome-determinative stretch, and several places where the method stated in §1 is not the method used in §4–§6.

**The single most important analytical choice is also the weakest.** P-501 is the only non-`NONCOMPLIANT` result, and it stands on reading B2 as a completion duty once B4 reverses PO sequence. That reading is possible because B2 has no express “before commitment” clause, but it fights process purpose and B5 (Finance is *not* waived). Plant/Finance at 15:10/15:12 are exactly the post-commitment approvals the prompt says not to treat as curative. The alternative in §5 is the better textual reading; making the thinner reading *primary* looks like a device to avoid an all-`NONCOMPLIANT` table rather than the product of the same purposive method used to deny P-503 scope.

**GPIP wins every live dispute, contrary to the anti-automatic-hierarchy instruction.** The hierarchy table is issue-specific on paper. In application, A1–A3 are never displaced. ECD-2 and the CFO email never actually save a case. That can be defended (A7 reserved to the CRO; C5 silent; D3–D4), but the result is a de facto total order: board policy unless a CRO instrument exists. Prior overlay practice is acknowledged and then given almost no work to do. A stricter answer would have let D1 do *some* Finance work after 15 July, or would have used `INDETERMINATE` where that authority question is dispositive.

**`INDETERMINATE` is defined and then abandoned.** §1 says the label is for a balanced unresolved dispositive fact. Whether an Incident Command authorizer was a designated independent budget owner is exactly that fact for P-505, P-507, and P-508. The burden-of-proof rule converts every such gap into `NONCOMPLIANT`. That is a legitimate enforcement posture, but it is not the classification rule the answer announced, and it produces seven identical primary labels where the prompt asked not to hide ambiguity behind a single outcome.

**§6 collapses the distinctions it claims to draw.** P-505/507/508 are listed as both **policy breach** (“unproven A2 roles”) and **inability to prove compliance**. Those categories cannot both be true on the same element. If the roles are unproven, the honest label is inability to prove (and, under the author’s own taxonomy, `INDETERMINATE`). If the required act is known to be absent, it is a breach. The double-count is a real evidential-discipline failure, not a wording slip.

**Burden is applied asymmetrically.** A role-labeled Plant/Finance event on P-501 is presumed valid despite missing user IDs (so A1 is not tested). Missing A2 role evidence on P-505/507/508 is treated as a completed breach. P-506 is credited with a pre-commitment “Finance component” without establishing Finance Controller status — the same actor-qualification problem treated as fatal elsewhere.

**P-508’s primary row is soft on D1’s cap.** D1’s substitution of “all operational approvals” is only *up to* EUR 150,000. At EUR 155,000, D2 adds Legal; it does not obviously carry the D1 waiver forward. The answer knows this in §5 but in §4 still says D can “explain absence of a separate local event.” That blurs a clean threshold.

**P-504 SOP scope is undertheorized.** Whether a cafeteria freezer is “maintenance and production-support” is contestable. If the SOP is off, B1/B3 fall away and the case may turn only on whether the supplier was *new* for A3 — which is unknown. That alternative is not pressure-tested, unlike the weaker P-503 overlay alternative.

**Formalization is slightly sloppy.** G-50, L-25, and D-150 lost their inequality operators (`≥` / `≤`). In a table labeled “formalized,” that is not cosmetic. The CFO-email chronology sentence (“after P-502 but before P-503–P-508”) omits P-501, which is also pre-email.

**What remains genuinely good, and why this is not a 6–7.** Scope and time are handled with care: P-501 is correctly pre-ECD; P-502 is correctly pre-D; P-505 uses the 17:59:31 acknowledgment over the 18:06 ERP stamp; P-507 treats the 12:40 PDF as timely C3 evidence and the 14:30 ERP post as logging; P-506’s verbal extension is rejected. A6 is applied consistently to late Finance/Compliance. P-503’s dual A1 analysis (if joint IC authority is final approval, SOD fails; if it is not, required approval is missing) is the best single piece of reasoning in the paper. §5 names the exact premise that would flip the label. §7 actually answers the drafting request, including a non-trivial anti-normalization clause.

**Net.** This is a high-quality examiner answer with a coherent conservative theory of the case. Strictly graded, it is not model-answer clean: one classification rests on the least supported local reading, uncertainty is systematically re-labeled as breach, evidential categories are mixed, and the advertised multi-dimensional hierarchy collapses to GPIP in every case that matters. Those are structural flaws, not nits. Hence 8.0 rather than the 9+ reserved for a nearly airtight opinion.
