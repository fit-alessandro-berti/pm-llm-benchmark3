The write-up has the right skeleton and a mostly sound conservative posture, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it loses a lot of points to factual slips, self-contradictions, a missed hard requirement, and sloppy aggressive-merge analysis.

**What is actually solid.** Sections 1–4 get the main taxonomy right: exact technical dups, multi-system observations, abstraction-level summaries, harmonization collisions, concurrent same-label events, and quarantine. The conservative path (merge only e03/e04 and e11/e16, keep e05/e14, keep e06/e07 and e15, refine e08/e09, never order by `ingested_at`, keep the original timestamp) matches the facts and the “minimize semantic damage” brief. The 15-event count is arithmetically correct. The `have_common_object=True` warning for e13/e14 is the right idea. Failure modes and the provenance schema are complete enough.

**Material factual errors.** Fact numbers are wrong in the decision table (e03/e05 is Fact 2, not 5; e06/e07 is Fact 3, not 6). Worse, Section 5 claims e08/e09/**e10** all share activity `Dispatch Package`. They do not: e10 is `Handover to Carrier`. That error then contaminates the aggressive count. If those three really collapsed, the log would lose two events, not one, and 13 would be wrong; if e10 is *not* merged, the prose is wrong. Either way the scenario is internally inconsistent.

The mechanical-merge story is also incomplete in a way that matters. After harmonization, e06/e07 are the same label and share O1/P1; e13/e14/**e15** are all `Capture Payment` at 12:00 and share O1/V1. A `have_common_object` merge is more likely to produce ~10–12 events and to swallow e15 (and possibly e05, which also shares O1/P1 with e03) than the 13-event picture given. The answer stresses the e13/e14 trap and then fails to apply the same test to the other same-label, shared-object groups. Calling e13/e15 “different objects” is simply false: they overlap on O1 and V1.

**Implementation does not meet its own spec.** The prompt required `pm4py.ocel_drop_duplicates` in a justified scenario; it never appears. The “pre-check for candidate groups” is a comment, not a demonstration. `merge_verified_pair` drops `eid_b` from `e2o` and then tries to rewire rows that can no longer exist. Events are treated as if indexed by `eid`, and relations are called `e2o`; a PM4Py OCEL uses `ocel:eid` / `relations`. Original timestamp/activity/source/key are not actually preserved on every row, only sketched inside the merge helper, and `original_activity` is filled from the *discarded* row.

**Ordering talks one game and plays another.** The microseconds warning is correct, then the code assigns `e14` sort key `12.1` and runs `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`, which is exactly the false story it just forbade. Invoice→Auth and Release→Accept are asserted as if given; they are plausible, not stated. Within-source `source_seq` (WMS 100/110/120, Carrier 1/2) is underused after being listed as a rule.

**Sensitivity and ambiguity are thin.** OTG is effectively missing. E2O/ET–OT/lifecycle numbers are qualitative. e06/e07 is explicitly ambiguous in the source facts; only one scenario is given. Evidence-based “absorb e15 into e13/e14” is not an implementable merge.

So: good exam outline, several load-bearing mistakes. Not a 3, because the conservative design is still usable. Not a 7+, because required API use is missing, e10 is mislabeled, aggressive counts don’t add up, and the ordering section contradicts itself.
