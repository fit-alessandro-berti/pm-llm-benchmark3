**6.5**

The write-up is well structured, follows the required headings, and gets most of the hard identity/delegation calls right. It is not close to flawless. Several rule applications are incomplete, and the checking code does not implement the rules the narrative claims to apply.

**What is actually strong.** Alias merge of `u_bk`/`b.koch` before SoD is handled correctly and is decisive for CHG-508. Service-account treatment is appropriately split: CHG-503 stays unattributed; CHG-509 is attributed to Eva with an architectural caveat. Event-time roles are respected (Eva is not QM on 08-09; current master is not used). RR3/RR4/RR5/RR6 are formalized in the right spirit: a later QMS row cannot prove an earlier delegation; weekends are excluded from business-day counts; patterns are not treated as proof. CHG-501 as `VALID_SUBSTITUTION`, CHG-504 as authorized-with-integrity-caveat, CHG-506 as suspicious/indeterminate, and CHG-509 as indeterminate on weekend principal-absence are defensible. Section 4 generally supplies a benign and an adverse reading plus a discriminator. Section 6 is concrete.

**Material analytical misses.** CHG-510 is classified only as invalid IC subdelegation. Clara is both drafter and approver (`u_cs` / `u_cs`); that is a standalone RR1 breach and is omitted from the SoD column. D-QM-01 is scoped to *production-equipment QM approval*. The narrative never applies that scope limit to Ben’s cooling-system retrospectives on CHG-505 and CHG-510; it only asks whether “retrospective” counts as “approval.” CHG-505’s retrospective is also during Ben’s certified sick leave (08-14 16:00), the same identity conflict treated as central on CHG-506, and is not analyzed. Asha’s 08-13 VPN session is not used to test whether D-QM-01’s “while Asha is absent” condition still holds later that day. D-IC-03 is assumed to cover CHG-505 as CI-44 without stating that two same-day cooling emergencies exist and only one incident is in scope.

**Smaller but real inaccuracies.** Asha’s matrix row cites the signed PDF as *her* evidence; that artifact is D-QM-01’s, not the principal’s standing role. Diego is described as having subdelegated via D-IC-04; Farid did. Grace’s verification on CHG-507 is called “out of role,” but RR2 only requires implementer ≠ verifier. CHG-502 is called `UNAUTHORIZED` at high confidence while simultaneously floating a LOW-risk SOP carve-out. Executive arithmetic is inconsistent: “~3 of 10” genuine authority failures, then five cases (507, 510, 505, plus 508 and 502).

**Code vs. stated rules.** The script meets the mechanical checklist (StringIO, `format_dataframe`, alias replace, `filter_four_eyes_principle`, interval checks, handover/working-together, optional repeated-activity filter, no auto-collusion). The authority function does not. The `allows_subdelegation` branches are identical, so D-IC-04 would be accepted if the principal-absence test passed. Principal absence is required for *every* delegation, which would mis-score D-IC-03 (Diego need not be absent). CSV `emergency` remains the strings `"true"`/`"false"`; `"false"` is truthy, so the standing-IC branch is unsafe. `format_dataframe` runs *before* alias normalization, and PM4Py four-eyes is run on `org:resource` rather than `effective_resource`, so service-account rows are not the identity the SoD story uses. Those are not cosmetic.

Net: a competent investigation memo with the right uncertainty posture on the ambiguous identity cases, undermined by a missed same-case SoD breach, a missed delegation-scope constraint on emergency retrospectives, and checker logic that contradicts the legal analysis. That is well above a weak answer and well short of near-flawless.
