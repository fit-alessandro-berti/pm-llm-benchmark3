**6.5**

The submission is a competent process-aware forecast, not a DFG-mode copy. It keeps A/B from collapsing, respects mandatory investigation and supplier rules, treats missing events as non-diagnostic, and uses queues, batch timing, and feed delay. That is the right *kind* of answer. It is not close to flawless. Under strict scoring the local semantic errors, next-event vs eventual-event confusion, and `process_enabled` inconsistencies are enough to keep it well below the 8–10 band.

**What is actually strong**

C6-A vs C6-B is the decisive test and is handled correctly: same prefix, opposite obligation sets. High severity, reversible injury, two devices on one lot, and three similar complaints pull A into Tech + Reg + Supplier after review. B’s low severity, no injury, single device, 0.93 duplicate match, and 0.98 completeness shift mass toward `Close — No Safety Issue` without a mandatory fork. Fast Close is correctly withheld for B because an evidence cycle already occurred. C6-C correctly treats country-rule reportability as a Regulatory Assessment obligation even without injury, and correctly treats same-failure-mode / different-lot linkage as weaker than Rule 5. C6-D is the best single case: worklist open at 12:40 is used as evidence of in-progress Reg, migration is used to explain a source attachment with no evidence events, and absent events are not treated as absent work. C6-E correctly blocks easy closure via the six-complaint lot rule. Cross-case discussion of DFG mixing, completion-only logs, and the 16:00 batch is accurate.

Intervals are honestly wide and midpoints are roughly coherent as a partial top-three. That part meets the uncertainty brief.

**State-abstraction errors (not cosmetic)**

C6-A’s alternative “S2/S3 boundary” is wrong. S2 is defined as evidence requested but *not* received. Evidence was received at 12:10. The case is in S3. Residual re-request risk is a *future branch after review*, not a current S2 state. The better alternative was S4: review already finished and investigations opening, with a delayed completion event.

C6-B’s “S1-equivalent disposition shortcut” is also not a state in the given abstraction. The case has left S1. A serious alternative would be late S3 leaning S5, or low-probability S4 if review already escalated.

Those two alternatives show the state machine being used rhetorically rather than as the normative abstraction the prompt required.

**Next logged event vs later completion**

Several rank-2/3 items are plausible *later* outcomes sold as the *next* log line.

- C6-A rank 2, `Technical Investigation Completed` (0.12–0.28): median Tech after review is 8.5 h; Tech queue is 7; one of three engineers is out. Evidence arrived at 12:10. For Tech *completion* to be the next line, review completion must be missing *and* investigation must already have finished in a window that performance data make unlikely. High-severity preemption affects *start*, not a ~2 h finish. Ranking this above a review-driven re-request is a performance error.
- C6-E rank 2, `Supplier Analysis Completed` (0.08–0.24): typical duration 6–36 h. Same problem unless Supplier Analysis has already been running since the first cycle. That belongs in unknowns, not as the second most likely *next* event.
- C6-C rank 3, `Authority Notification` (0.06–0.18): Rule 6 and the DFG put notification *after* disposition; batches go at 16:00. This requires unseen Reg *and* unseen disposition *and* a reportable finding *and* making the batch. Residual mass is fine; putting it in the top three is not process-disciplined. `Close — No Safety Issue` or a hedged Supplier Analysis would have been more coherent.

C6-E also omits `Customer Evidence Requested` despite completeness 0.72, an explicit second cycle, and a DFG re-request edge of 101. That is the obvious competing next activity after a second review and should have displaced Tech or Supplier *completion*.

**`process_enabled` is applied inconsistently**

The prompt asked for a clean split between what the process can do now and what the log is likely to show. The flag is used, but not stably.

- C6-A/B mark `Customer Evidence Requested` as enabled *now*. A new request is created *by* Evidence Review (Rule 3). The currently enabled work is review. That is the same logical pattern used to mark Tech `false`, so the flag contradicts itself.
- C6-D marks `Evidence Received` enabled. If the source attachment means the cycle already happened, the task is done and the feed is late (`process_enabled` should be false). If the cycle never started, `Customer Evidence Requested` is the enabled act, not Received. Either reading makes `true` on Received sloppy.

So the observation-model language is better than the boolean discipline.

**Smaller but real defects**

C6-C’s 0.38–0.62 on Reg Completed is optimistic given 12 waiting, 2–14 h after assignment, and no worklist evidence that the case is assigned. The contradicting-evidence bullet knows this; the interval does not fully absorb it.

C6-A/B rank-2 items need an unobserved review completion despite a *normal* feed flag. The global 8–15% young-case delay can justify a thin tail, not a second-place forecast.

Several strings are broken where a connective was dropped (“no reg event  reg not started”, “ReceivedEvidence Review”, “RegisteredTriageEvidence…”). Intent is recoverable; as submitted they are unclear.

C6-C bundles “Evidence cycle” in `completed_obligations` instead of naming the logged steps. Minor sloppiness.

**Net**

This is clearly above a weak or generic answer: obligations, objects, queues, and data quality are in the reasoning, and A/B are not cloned. It is also clearly not near-flawless: two alternative states violate the given abstraction, several “next” events are long-horizon completions, `process_enabled` is inconsistent on the exact distinction the task asked for, C6-C’s third prediction is poorly sequenced, and C6-E misses the re-request that the completeness score and DFG both support. Those are structural issues, not nits. **6.5**.
