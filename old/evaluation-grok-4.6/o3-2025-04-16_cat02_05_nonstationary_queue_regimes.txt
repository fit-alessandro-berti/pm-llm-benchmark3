**4.6**

The write-up has the right skeleton and a mostly sensible qualitative regime story, but it is not reliable as analysis. Under a strict reading it fails on numerical accuracy, internal consistency (prose vs. formulas vs. code), and several queueing-identity checks the prompt specifically asked for.

**What is actually good.** Headings match the spec. Section 1 correctly treats opening hours as a 12-hour window, separates active service time from flow time, and lists real threats (non-stationary demand and capacity, mix shift, outage spillover, carry-over, weekday/weekend confounding). The primary story—baseline weekdays, weekends, outage-plus-recovery, high-complexity/high-WIP at unchanged 6 FTE, then intervention—is the right operational narrative, and recovery is *not* sold as a healthy high-throughput regime. Little’s Law is correctly refused on the outage day. The pooled-KPI staffing example in §7 is a legitimate “looks fine in aggregate, fails in segment” policy.

**Segmentation is sloppy and partly false.** R0’s window “2026-04-06 – 2026-04-14 (Mon–Tue)” is ambiguous and collides with Friday 5.5 FTE. Rw is mislabeled as “2026-04-06/07/08/09/10 Sat–Sun and 2026-04-11/12” (those first dates are weekdays). Intensities look like *all* weekends mixed together, including post-mix-shift Saturdays/Sundays with ~40% complex work, which is not a “baseline weekend.” R1 reports a 3-day arrival rate beside a single-day (16th) completion rate. “Backlog cleared 16th” is wrong: mean WIP *rises* 47 → 52 on the 16th and is still 35 on the 17th. R3’s “WIP 3219” is a typo, and 9.1/9.6 /h are not recoverable from the stated dates in a consistent weekday-vs-all-days way. Apr 18–19 disappear from the primary table. The alternative in §3 puts “weekends always” in the 6 FTE band; weekends are 2.5–3.0 FTE. That is a factual error, not a rounding issue.

**Utilization and Assumption A1 do not add up.** The Pareto-style formula as written does not yield “1.3 × median,” and the reported ρ values are not that formula anyway. R2’s 0.95 is approximately λ × *median* / (staff × 60), i.e. the substitution the prompt forbids. Weekend ρ = 0.55 is not reproducible from the stated λ, service times, and 2.5 FTE (the same recipe gives ~0.7+). Capacity pressure is therefore decorative.

**Section 4 misses a required comparison and mangles Simpson.** The prompt asked for weekday/weekend *composition* as a quantitative pooling failure. That table/contrast is not there; weekends are almost absent from §4. The “Simpson-like” claim that pooled data would show “service times improved” does not follow: R3 medians are *worse* than baseline and only slightly better than R2. That is confounding, not a reversal. Pooled weekday WIP ≈ 32 is also off (weekday mean WIP is about 35 if outage/recovery are included).

**Section 5’s headline statistics are wrong and partly fabricated.** For the four hourly arrival vectors, sample Fano factors are about 3.2, 4.1, 10.1, 1.3 (population ~3.0, 3.8, 9.3, 1.2), not 1.6, 2.4, 6.9, 1.2. CVs 0.38 / 1.12 / 1.80 / 0.30 are likewise not the CVs of those series. Lag-1 on the bursty 04-21 vector is not a convincing +0.42 (the 34 and 24 spikes are followed by troughs). Ljung-Box “p < 0.05” is asserted with no test in the text or in the code—on n = 28 serially structured days that is invented precision. Daily weekday arrivals in the high-complexity band are actually *underdispersed* relative to Poisson; hourly counts are wildly overdispersed. That aggregation contrast is the interesting point and is missed. Four representative days cannot support a stochastic model: that caveat is correct and is the strongest sentence in the section.

**Little’s Law is applied with broken units and a closed-stock problem that is never checked.** W = 20 / (8.3 × 12) = 0.20 “days” is only defensible as a fraction of an *opening* day (≈ 2.4 h). Labeled as flow time in days, it is easily read as 0.20 × 24 h = 4.8 h—a factor-of-two trap the write-up never disambiguates. Overnight holdover means 15-minute *open-hours* WIP is not the 24-hour time-average, so L and λ are not measured on the same clock. Verdicts of `applicable` for R0 and weekends are too strong given unknown 07:00 stock, mixed Fridays, and later weekends that are not the same mix.

Worse: over 28 days arrivals − completions ≈ +210, while mean WIP stays in a 12–52 band and ends near 19. That inventory identity does not close. Either `mean_wip` is not the stock that arrivals and completions increment, or there is unmeasured outflow. Until that is resolved, L/λ is not a flow-time estimator. A competent audit would have computed the cumulative imbalance and downgraded almost every window. The matrix never does.

The code then implements a *different* law: `ft_days = W/(lam*12)/24`, which would print ~0.008, not the 0.20 in the table. So §6 and §8 contradict each other.

**The Python block does not reproduce the paper.** `classify()` marks 2026-04-17 as `Weekend` (Friday, 6.5 FTE, post-outage recovery), dumps 04-16 into `HighStaff` with the intervention days, and never builds the prose R1 “outage & spillover” regime. Pooled-vs-segment numbers from §4 are not printed. The heuristic is a nest of date ifs, not a change-point procedure, which would be acceptable if it matched the narrative and were labeled as such; it does not match. PM4Py comments are present and correctly say this is not an event log.

**Smaller defects that still cost.** Greek estimands rendered as blank “  = arrival intensity.” Intervention start time on 04-29 is mentioned in the prompt and barely treated as a within-day uncertainty. Complex_pct is not allowed to be a coding change in the estimates. Representative hourly totals match daily arrivals, but 04-21’s 08–10 share is *not* 47% (the 34-count bin is 10–11); a careful read would have said so. “1 FTE cuts WIP 30%” over-attributes a confounded R2→R3 movement.

**Net.** This reads like a passable exam outline with numbers filled in by eye and a script that was not executed against the write-up. Qualitative process-mining instincts are above average; the quantitative layer would not survive a reviewer checking Fano, ρ, inventory balance, or running the code. That is well below a high score on a rubric that privileges consistency, defensible uncertainty, and use of the supplied evidence.
