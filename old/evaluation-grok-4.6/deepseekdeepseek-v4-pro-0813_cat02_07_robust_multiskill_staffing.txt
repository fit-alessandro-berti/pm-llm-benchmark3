**6.2**

The write-up has the right spine: labeled assumptions, auditable midpoint load math, a real finding that base demand exceeds shift capacity (especially after 16:00), three operational policies, and a robustness-based hybrid recommendation rather than a base-case mean-delay pick. Format compliance is good, and Sections 5–7 and 9 are competent.

It is not close to flawless.

The Section 4 table is not produced by the model in Section 8. Ranges such as hybrid base mean delay 3–5 h and P1 SLA 85–95% are narrative estimates. The Python uses different, arbitrary knobs (`capacity_factor` 0.85/0.95/0.90, SLA shifts, `wait = 18 × service` near ρ = 0.9). Those two artifacts will not agree. That breaks the required audit trail from evidence → metrics → recommendation.

Section 8 does not meet its own spec. Preemption is absent. Setup is hard-coded to 0. ME/ES is always sequential in the log. The event log ignores shifts, breaks, Chen’s absence, queues, and overtime; assignment is a static name map. Handover/setup/preemption are mostly hidden in a 15–25 min/case constant, which the prompt forbade. PM4Py use is try/except around APIs that may not exist; the log is not an output of the same engine that produced the comparison.

Capacity analysis has a concrete error: overtime is added as a daily lump (+180). Ada/Ben/Chen’s 30 minutes after 16:00 land on the worst block (16:00–18:00, load/cap ≈ 1.94). Missing that overstates evening collapse and understates how much OT can move P2. Coordination is also treated as resource-minutes without a clear owner.

Internal definitions drift. Section 2 omits handover from the 2,826-minute load; the code then adds 630–1,050 minutes of burden and uses that for SLAs. Fairness is defined on resources in the text and computed on skills in code (Intake utilization is always low, so the spread is partly an artifact). U2 is interpreted as a composition reshuffle with almost unchanged total load; that is disclosed, but it is the milder reading of “demand doubles,” so U2 barely stresses the comparison. P3’s 24-hour clock and next-day carryover are left vague even though unfinished work is the binding constraint.

The qualitative policy design and bottleneck-migration story are well above average. The quantitative comparison and the required reproducible engine are not. Under a strict bar, that caps the score in the low 6s.
