**8.0**

The final nine-section design is strong on the criteria that matter most: it refuses to treat omitted applications as non-default, defines a conditional-on-activation estimand instead of pretending default is observed for every case, reconstructs a complete 48,000-row cohort flow, separates ordinary censoring from delayed labels, selection, transfer, and MNAR partner feeds, and backs that with a leakage table and a temporal (not random) split. Those choices correctly avoid the failure modes the prompt flags. It is not near-flawless.

**What holds up.** The primary estimand \(P(\text{default}_{90}\mid X(\tau), A=1, \text{label observed})\) is well specified (unit, landmark \(\tau\), horizon, population, estimand). Alternatives A–C answer genuinely different questions and are labeled as such. Arithmetic on the 48,000 cases is correct (25,200 activated = 21,600 labeled + 3,600 immature/missing). The leakage audit covers every required field with the right keep/repair/drop calls. Validation by case start, maturity-aware Test A/B, and the four reasons a random split fails are all on target. The skeleton uses the requested PM4Py surface without inventing a survival API, truncates at \(\tau\), drops post-decision columns, and marks HOOK points.

**Statistical and design flaws.** The competing-risk state space is inconsistent: section 1 correctly nests default *after* activation, but the code hook flattens `{in-process, activated, default, rejected, withdrawn, transferred}`, which treats default as a sibling of activation and is process-wrong. Concept shift after 1 July 2025 is detected (DFG/PSI) but not modeled: `policy_period` is constant on the training window (starts through March 2025), so it cannot capture the stated change in both selection and the outcome relationship. Complete-case training that drops 3,600 of 25,200 activated accounts (~14%) is only a sensitivity away from a better primary (discrete-time hazard / IPCW). Calling April–June 2025 “fully matured” is slightly optimistic for slow activations. The claimed inference population (“in-flight applications that *will* be activated”) still conditions on a post-\(\tau\) event; the cascade is described, not specified.

**Implementation gaps.** `label_available_at` is never parsed with `pd.to_datetime`, so the maturity gate can fail or compare lexicographically. Repairs required by the design—as-of resource experience, event-sourced `documents_total`, `source_system` MNAR strata, elapsed-time prefixes—are absent from the code. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` and `pm4py.get_variants` are called and then largely unused (`pref_df` is only printed; variants go to `_`). `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is joined with a fragile index assumption. These are not polish nits: they are the difference between a design document and an implementable audit pipeline.

The result is a defensible, honest study design with a usable skeleton and several concrete errors and unfinished pieces. That is clearly above a mediocre answer and clearly below a near-flawless one.
