**6.2**

This is a well-structured, format-compliant memo with mostly accurate arithmetic and the right high-level instincts (endogenous staggered rollout, intensity ≠ availability, median ≠ tail, G=5, fraud-desk interference, “no single X%”). It is not close to flawless. Several claims that the task is designed to punish—identification overconfidence, process signatures that the panel does not actually show, and intensity treated as a dose—are stated as if they were established.

### Fatal and near-fatal problems

**Rework is not a clean, strongly identified step-function.** This is the worst error. Alpha rework is 13.2 pre, 11.2 in April, then 14.0–14.5 thereafter. Beta is 12.8 → 10.4 in April → 12.7 for four months. Gamma post-adoption 12.6 is *above* its Jan–Apr 12.3 and only looks like a 2 pp win if May–Jun (the complexity shock) is the baseline. Section 2 nevertheless marks `ITT_avail_rework` “Yes, strongly identified,” Section 4 says the drop “remain[s] stable despite subsequent volume shocks,” and Section 7 gives confidence 90 with “None in supplied data” as counterevidence. That directly contradicts the Section 5 pre/post table (Alpha rework **+0.54 pp**). A first-month dip that reverses is not a sustained automation effect.

**“Strongly identified” is the wrong epistemic status for this design.** Escalation’s +1 pp at go-live is more consistent than rework, but it still co-moves with the March fraud desk, May complexity, and the routing campaign. Calling both rework and escalation strongly identified, while marking p90 “No” for the *same* threats that apply to the median, is internally inconsistent. Later sections then treat p90 DiD as an estimated effect anyway.

**`automated_fast_path_pct` is not an exogenous dose.** It is a post-treatment process outcome (mix of take-up, algorithm thresholds, and case mix). The “monotonic dose-response” and the −0.1304 h per 1% coefficients are largely tautological: more cases on the fast path pull the mixture median down. Interpreting this as proof that “availability alone did not drive outcomes” is a mediator/collider error, not an intensity-of-treatment design.

**Section 5 labels a DiD “Causal Finding.”** The prompt asked to separate arithmetic from interpretation. −4.49 h / −4.17 h is a pair of two-way mean contrasts on five branches with co-timed routing, staffing, and case-mix shocks. Calling the match “identical” also depends on giving Gamma the May–Jun peak as pre-period. Full-pre Gamma median change is −3.38 h, not −8.45 h; versus Jan–Apr it is about −0.9 h. That is window-shopping, then using the shopped number as replication against regression to the mean.

### Identification and quantitative discipline

The Callaway–Sant’Anna / clean-control primary strategy is the right *name*. It is not implemented. There is no event-study table, no cohort ATT(g,τ), no lead coefficients. Section 9 estimates the TWFE the identification section just rejected, with G=5 clustered SEs reported to three decimals. Comments that “this does not prove causality” do not repair the mismatch.

Synthetic control is dismissed solely on level convex-hull grounds; trend or differenced SC is not considered. Fine as a limitation, overstated as mathematical impossibility.

Pre-trends over three months with noisy medians do **not** “strongly refute” regression to the mean. Alpha and Beta were selected on historically bad cycle times; Gamma’s July drop coincides with an organization-wide volume and cycle-time retreat. The memo’s own raw fact is the important one: Alpha/Beta **absolute** median and p90 worsen after April. Relative DiD improvement is entirely “controls deteriorated more,” which is exactly where fraud-desk spillovers, extra routed volume, and non-parallel summer stress would show up.

Unverifiable precision: −0.1304 (SE 0.0321), controlled −2.19 h, bounds [−5.0, −2.0] and [−7.5, −1.5]. Approximate calculation is allowed; four-decimal cluster SEs on five clusters, plus a −2.19 h model that is never specified in code, are not.

Mixture/aggregation: printing a warning about medians is not the same as using it. Fast-path substitution **mechanically** changes the case mix inside the monthly median. Without path-specific times, a 2–5 h median “effect” can be composition, not capacity creation. Tail ratios ~2.0–2.2 exist pre-treatment in every branch; treated-only “tail amplification” is oversold.

CSAT handling is appropriately skeptical in the estimand table and then still given a 0.00 to +0.10 interval as if that were an effect range rather than noise plus selection.

### Process and policy

Mechanisms coverage is the strongest part of the memo (substitution, specialist congestion, learning, routing, staffing, survey selection). Failures: unexplained 4–6% fast-path while `automation=0`; routing treated as SUTVA rather than a co-treatment; no path accounting (fast-path % + escalation % ≪ 100); Alpha’s May −2 FTE / p90 119.9 h episode underweighted relative to the tidy DiD story.

`expand with an embedded evaluation` is the correct bucket. The design is not. A two-week 50/50 case experiment cannot identify p90 or rework when cycle times are 40–120 h and rework is a loop. A four-week pre-period on a process that was measured monthly is not a serious baseline. A “statistically significant 3 h median drop” rule with two remaining branches is theater. Delta (easiest mix) as the default next site is the opposite of the external-validity problem the selection discussion just raised; Epsilon is the closer analogue to treated complexity. Circuit breakers are good; the scale-up rule is not identified by the evidence offered.

### What still works

Section 1 stays descriptive and hits availability vs use, purposive selection, co-shocks, and measurement limits. Estimand list is broad enough and correctly kills case-level LATE and complexity-stratum effects. Threat table includes the required items and the right bias directions. Claim ladder structure is right: the Observed bullet is largely fair; Not supported correctly rejects a single “reduces cycle time by X%.” Code is self-contained, validates the 40-row panel, prints trends without subplots, builds relative time for adopters, and warns about G=5 and aggregated medians.

Those are necessary, not sufficient. The memo repeatedly converts a fragile, confounded, five-unit descriptive pattern into “strongly identified,” “causal finding,” confidence 90, and a replicable −4.2 to −4.5 h median effect. Under the stated rubric—estimands, identification logic, process interpretation, quantitative discipline, uncertainty—that is a substantial failure, not a polish issue.
