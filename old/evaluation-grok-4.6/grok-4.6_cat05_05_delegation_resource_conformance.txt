The analysis is structurally complete and gets the hard conformance questions right in substance: alias collapse before SoD, event-time roles rather than current master data, `svc_qms` as a non-human, void subdelegation, leave as non-revocation of standing QM authority, and RR6 (density is not collusion). That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading. Several judgments are sharper than the evidence, the automated checks do not fully match the prose, and a few control points are asserted as if they were given rules.

**Over-crisp case labels.** CHG-505 is marked **UNAUTHORIZED** as the primary label, with only a parenthetical valid-IC caveat. That collapses three separate uncertainties into a verdict: whether the case is CI-44 (D-IC-03 is incident-scoped, not merely `cooling_system`), whether “within two business days” is Thursday EOD or some other site convention, and whether D-QM-01 can ever carry `Retrospective_QM_Review`. Primary **INDETERMINATE** with **UNAUTHORIZED** secondary would have matched the author’s own medium confidence. CHG-501 as **VALID_SUBSTITUTION** is similarly firm. The QMS row does not exist at 09:00 on 11 Aug; validity is entirely a bet on an unaudited PDF versus an unnamed system of record. Residual “data-recording gap” is noted, then underweighted. CHG-509 as **INDETERMINATE** is the right instinct, but the code path returns a hard absence failure, so the write-up and the checker disagree on the same condition.

**Absence logic is not applied at event time with equal care.** Section 2 correctly says a principal on leave is still QM, and that a short VPN window does not cancel the leave record for the rest of the day. It does not then test the other half of D-QM-01: if Asha was interactively present at 09:22 on 13 Aug, the instrument’s “while Asha is absent” condition is no longer a clean fact for later the same day (CHG-506 at 13:00). Treating recorded leave as sufficient for every timestamp that calendar-overlaps leave, after acknowledging a live principal session that morning, is a gap in the event-time test the author claimed to apply. Ben’s sick-leave denial is also stretched: the file records a denial of CHG-506, not of the Friday CHG-505 review. Grouping both as one credential-compromise story is investigative hypothesis, not evidence.

**Rule formalization overclaims.** RR2 is correctly read as a same-human ban, not a title check. RR4’s clock is then defined as if the SOP text contained “day 0 = event date, deadline = 23:59:59 UTC on the second following weekday.” That convention is plausible and does not change CHG-510, but it is not in the prompt, no alternative count is shown, and “safety rationale must be credible” is mentioned then not operationalized (both emergency cases have a flag and no rationale object). The claim that a later database insert cannot prove an earlier delegation is right; the further claim that the signed PDF *does* prove D-QM-01 for CHG-501 is the same class of leap in the other direction.

**SoD versus control objective is described, then applied unevenly.** Eva’s move from Independent Verifier to QE on 12 Aug is handled well as a weakened objective, not an RR2 fail. The same discipline is not applied to Diego implementing emergencies that his own IC delegation chain approved (CHG-505), or to Eva both approving and verifying CHG-509 after attribution. Those are not RR1/RR2 hits; they are the same kind of objective leak the author flags for Eva-as-QE. Calling them “outside the rules” is technically accurate and still leaves the control analysis incomplete. CHG-502’s **High** severity on a LOW-risk self-approval is a policy judgment presented as if it followed from RR1.

**Code does not fully implement the memo.** The required PM4Py surface is present and the non-collusion stance is explicit, which is the right design. Gaps: (1) `ben_sick_at_approval` inspects only `Approve_Change`, so the Friday CHG-505 retrospective during certified sick leave is invisible to the flag table that the narrative relies on; (2) `qm_authority` returns `False` for D-QM-02 when leave has ended, while the case write-up is **INDETERMINATE**; (3) D-QM-01 still returns `True` under sick leave (only a note), so CHG-506 looks conditionally authorized in the printout and unauthorized/identity-broken in section 3; (4) `sod_violations` is a brittle set comprehension around `filter_four_eyes_principle` and assumes that API returns conforming cases and preserves `case:concept:name` after `format_dataframe`—never demonstrated; (5) service-account attribution is a manual dict, as disclosed, but CHG-503 is left as a string inequality that the same code will treat as an RR2 pass, and that false pass is only narrated, not flagged as `FALSE_FOUR_EYES_PASS`. Networks are computed and not over-interpreted; that part is clean.

**Matrix and identity layer are strong, not tight.** Alias normalization (`b.koch` ≡ `u_bk`) and the two `svc_qms` sessions are handled correctly, and CHG-508 is the right test case. Attribution language for CHG-503 (“cannot pass”) versus CHG-509 (“stronger map to Eva”) matches the evidence table. Weaker points: Farid’s “principal-absence condition” is marked N/A without stating that D-IC-03 is *not* absence-conditioned, which matters because Diego is on-site implementing; Clara’s D-IC-04 row is good; Grace’s row correctly refuses to mint QM authority from a Site Director title. **Collusion section complies with RR6 and still over-aggregates.** Benign and adverse stories plus discriminators are supplied for the required indicator classes. The useful claim is that the Clara→Ben edge is expected under cell design plus D-QM-01, and that the signal is the intersection with alias self-approval, sick-leave use, and void IC paper. Reciprocal LOW-risk training approvals are discussed, then CHG-508 is correctly reclassified as self-approval via alias—so the reciprocal “pattern” in *this* log is partly an identity bug, which deserved more weight as an alternative to “you-approve-mine.”

**Control recommendations and conclusion are directionally right and slightly generic.** Delegation form, dual timestamps, alias-to-person key, step-up on shared terminals, RR4 clock in workflow, and “do not auto-open misconduct from edge weight” are the correct remedies. Pair rotation and “sampled second-QM review” are tacked on without saying they are organizational, not rule-required. The three investigations (Ben’s credential after 12:00 on 13 Aug, D-IC-04/CHG-510, CHG-503 `svc_qms`) are the right triage; CHG-507 is a confirmed standing-authority fail and is oddly demoted relative to open identity questions, which is defensible only if “investigation” means residual uncertainty. The systemic split (authority failure vs identity/data vs pattern risk) is the requested judgment and is not quantified even coarsely, so “how much” is answered only by case list.

**Net.** This is a competent compliance memo with the required headings, the right refusal to call collusion, and the right identity mechanics. It is not nearly flawless: it converts several open conditions into primary verdicts, its checker contradicts its classifications on absence and sick leave, and it treats a self-defined business-day clock and an unaudited PDF as if they were in the control text. Those are not cosmetic nits on a problem that asked for event-time reasoning and honest uncertainty.
