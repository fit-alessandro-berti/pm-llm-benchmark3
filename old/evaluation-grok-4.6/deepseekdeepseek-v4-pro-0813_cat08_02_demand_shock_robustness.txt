**7.3**

The final write-up is a complete, well-structured redesign that hits every required section and stays mostly grounded in the logs. The four scenarios vary mix and disruption type, not just volume. Bottleneck-migration is real rather than decorative: I8 → pre-analytical, I1 → medical/specialist WIP, I2 → assay and technical-validation bursts, I3 → M2 repeats, I4 → technical validation, I5 → specialist review, I10 → transport/handoff. I9 is correctly rejected. WIP caps and the dual trigger on I4 (accession queue high **and** technical validation below 95%) show the author understood that not every case type can be first and that I4 is unsafe at the observed 101% technical-validation utilization. Residual risks include at least one redesign-caused failure (over-triage, I4 stealing validators). Leading vs lagging metrics are distinguished.

It is not near-flawless. Repeat-after-failure is a named case type with the worst peak times (median 145h, p90 240h) and is barely treated as its own stressor; the trade-off table has no column for it. Performance-DFG hop times (Receive → Pre-an 2/9, Pre-an → Prep 4/13, Batch wait → Assay 9/22, Tech → Med 8/24, Hold → Med 18/48) are underused relative to utilization tables. Several dashboard “warning” bands are set at the already-observed peak medians (pre-an 9.5h, assay 18h, med 22h, specialist 36h), so they flag a crisis rather than lead it. Accession has no fallback when I4 cannot fire because technical validation is also saturated. A few S3 lines (slowing routine release; recollection rising if staff are pulled) are loose relative to the I9 rejection and the evidence. Unused signals include clerk error after 9h overtime and the peak rise in recollection-to-cancel. Operational cutovers (complex p90 150h/100h, specialist 36h/24h) are plausible but thinly derived.

Strong process reasoning with clear conflict handling; deductions are for incomplete case-type coverage, weaker process-mining use, and thresholds that are less leading than claimed.
