**6.1**

The answer is in the right family: it refuses A/B as control failures, keeps a second-officer review in the recommended path, tries to gather information in parallel, and does not treat rescreening, SAR, or self-certification as a substitute for independent review. Under a normal rubric that would be a high-6 / low-7. Under the required hypercritical standard, material arithmetic, labeling, and benefit-accounting errors keep it well short of strong.

**Fatal problem for the #1 plan: the fallback does not do what the table claims.** Action C’s own timings are review 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute. Starting that path at *t*+45 yields a finish of roughly 85–135 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. The continuation tree still writes “S2 Review (35m) → Decision at t=80m [SLA Kept]”—lower bound only, recording time dropped. Rank 1’s “€0 harm if approved on time” and “High” confidence are therefore unsupported. The same tree silently truncates S4 from 35–65 to 35–45 on the happy path and invents second-review durations (10–15, 20–30, 30 minutes) that nowhere appear in the case.

**Delayed S2 is worse than immediate S2 on both cases.** The +0.12–0.28 spillover is for interrupting P-901 *now* for ~45 minutes. P-901’s deadline is 70 minutes. Pulling S2 at *t*+45 leaves P-901 about 25 minutes of slack and is not the experiment those numbers describe. Waiting until 45 and then doing C is typically dominated by doing C immediately: worse P-884 SLA odds and worse P-901 harm. Section 5’s sensitivity (“if S4 is 65 minutes, trigger C at *t*+45”) therefore does not answer the question that was asked. If S4 is known to be late, rank 1 and rank 2 should flip or collapse into “D ∥ immediate S2,” not a late copy of C. The rerank trigger later says *t*+50, which also disagrees with the *t*+45 gate.

**Benefit ranges systematically assume the value of timely *execution*.** The €20k–€35k figure is the value of *paying* on time, not of finishing a review on time. Conformant approval is only 0.55–0.75, and those estimates are observational and may not transport. Rank 1–3 still book the full SLA band as the benefit of a timely second review. False-reject/hold harm is parked in a separate column rather than netted against that probability. The given “continue normal processing” SLA-breach band (0.48–0.64) and the non-transport warning are essentially unused.

**Conformance labels are sloppy in the exact place the template is strict.** D-in-parallel-with-S4/S2 and immediate independent S2 review are `CONFORMANT NOW` if the named officer actually reviews. `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS` is the F/G-style label; applying it to ranks 1–3 blurs the distinction the answer later draws well for F and G. Rank 1 never states *which* condition must hold.

**Process gaps a specialist answer should not miss.** The prefix ends at Evidence Validated; the prescribed cycle returns to Analyst Review before approve/execute. The write-up jumps to second officer as if that step were already complete. Rule 8 is never used: SAR/hold does not authorize execution—an important brake on G and on any “file and pay” fantasy. The risk score is 12 minutes stale and is never treated as a cheap refresh. Rule 11 is tabled but not applied to the customer-facing text of E/F.

**Smaller inconsistencies that add up.** Rank 1 harm ignores the fallback’s P-901 hit. Alternate screening is sold as “elimination of fuzzy-match ambiguity” in the table and only as a possibility in the prose. “Optimal” use of one of three API calls is asserted, not compared to saving the call or to running D in parallel with *immediate* S2. G’s operational risk is described reasonably, but 85% / “High” operational confidence is incompatible with S4’s 30-minute window, 35–75 minute reviews, 0.55–0.75 approval, and a non-transportable predictor. Section 2’s rank-1 approve path has no reject/hold/SAR branch.

**What remains creditworthy.** A and B are last, correctly tagged `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT`, and explicitly barred from winning on a favorable forecast. G is `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED` until director plus incident ID exist. F’s evidentiary caveat is right. The lexicographic argument—asymmetric regulatory/non-monetary tail dominates €20–35k operational loss—is the correct decision stance. Parallel rescreen plus a named second officer is the right *shape* of plan. The six required sections are present and on-template.

The recommendation is directionally expert and structurally complete, but the primary plan’s feasibility, ranking versus C, and monetization of “SLA met” do not survive the case’s own numbers. That is a 6.1, not an 8+.
