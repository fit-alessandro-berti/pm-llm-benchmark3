The answer gets the core diagnosis right: March is mostly semantic/provenance, later movement is real, change points are mechanism-specific, and supplier rework, staffing queues, priority redistribution, and seasonality are treated as distinct. Structure and coverage are strong. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several quantitative, causal, and requirement failures are material.

**Requirement misses.** Section 7 never monitors outcomes (approve/reject, decision mix, or quality), despite an explicit “outcomes” requirement. Part B never calls `pm4py.format_dataframe(...)`; it uses `dataframe_utils` plus manual renames. Section 2 is ~300 words against a 180–280 limit. Section 6 omits priority class and resource/team as segment levels even though section 5 uses them.

**Quantitative errors and manufactured precision.** The metric table mixes relative percent (cases) with percentage-point changes labeled `%` (complexity, supplier, variants). Claimed `r = 0.94` between Supplier Z share and rework is not supported by the monthly series; a Pearson calculation on the twelve points is about **0.81**, and Oct–Nov already break a tight supplier–rework lockstep. Peak `missing_resource_pct` is July 6.5, not June 6.2. The queue/service baseline “≈ 5.5” is not identified from the given numbers (Jan median cycle 31.5h vs Assess service ~4.8h). L1 on seven edges is computed correctly, but `Assess→RequestInfo` and `RequestInfo→CollectDocs` are near-duplicates, so the distance double-counts rework and the “omitted edges unchanged” assumption is stated rather than stress-tested.

**Causal overreach.** “Proved” / “conclusively proves” is too strong for aggregated, non-unique evidence. Section 2 uses the Oct Senior Assessment jump (4% → 22%) as proof that *post-May* movement is not logging; that edge is the seasonal/recording window, not the May supplier break. The problem states Senior Assessment was historically recorded inconsistently; October can mix true mix shift with **better recording**. That alternative is almost absent. The second logging change point at 2026-06-15 is a forced “need two CPs” artifact: missingness is described as concentrated in migrated activities, and a 5.5 → 6.2 move is not a second schema event. The June 15–30 row cites July cycle times the monthly grain cannot assign to two weeks.

**Decomposition and monitoring design.** The 15–20 / 25–30 / 5–10 / 45–50 split of the Jan→Nov +17.5h is directionally plausible and appropriately ranged, but Nov stacks seasonality, residual supplier load, missing assessors, and priority, so shares are not separately identified. Case-mix is likely understated without stratified cycle times. Primary monitoring on “standard, non-Z, post-map” is a useful *control*, not a primary operational population; it hides the cells where drift actually appears. Label (`>9`) and missing-resource (`>4%`) alerts would stay on for the rest of 2026. Invoking Benjamini–Hochberg on ten heuristic thresholds, not p-values, is cargo-cult multiple testing. Code computes relative changes and never prints them; change-point rules miss the October Senior Assessment break because they only watch rework.

**What still holds.** Mechanism-specific change points, the raw/mapped fitness reading for March, supplier↔rework and staffing↔queue (service almost flat), priority as a tail/median story, eight competing hypotheses with confounders, and the IF/THEN rules are the parts that would survive review. The failures are precision, restraint, a few invented numbers, and incomplete compliance—not a wrong overall story. That is a good answer with several disqualifying nits, not a near-ceiling one.
