**6.1**

The delivered write-up through §8 is unusually strong on the things this task actually tests: it treats metrics as evidence, reasons from language membership rather than score worship, separates exploration from enforcement, and designs a third model that is safer than S and more compositional than C. The probe matrix for B1–B8 is correct. The pathology ranking is mostly well judged. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading of the spec.

**Required format is not met.** The task demands one Markdown document with exactly eleven top-level headings. What is actually finished is §§1–8, a truncated §9 that dies inside `format_dataframe`, and **no §10 or §11 at all**. Governance recommendation and the dissenting view are not optional color; they are named deliverables and quality criteria. An answer that never says which original may be used, which should be retired, or what would overturn the recommendation is incomplete, however good the earlier analysis is.

**The third-model policy layer has a real bug.** `post_hoc_policy_scan` builds `pos` with `{a: i for i, a in enumerate(activities)}`, which keeps the *last* index of each label. R5 then asks whether both checks precede Expedite Review. After a correction/recheck the last Credit Check or Technical Review can sit *after* ER, so a trace the POWL model is designed to accept (including the authors’ own X3) is misclassified as a CRITICAL P4 violation. That is not a comment-quality issue; it breaks the hybrid story in the exact place they claim superiority over both inputs.

**Several metric readings are over-precise.** Fitness 0.973 is treated as proof that C’s training log contains traces outside the eight variants. Alignment fitness is not a coverage rate; the residual could be cost, normalization, silent moves, or a few badly deviant cases. The 1M-case runtime extrapolation from medians is arithmetic theater. The duplicate-label “33” audit (naive 39) is a fair comparability point; saying it matches *no* obvious definition is a bit too sure.

**§9 as delivered does not satisfy the validation contract.** The task asked for a concrete program: seeded splits, negative suite, perturbations, unseen combinations, cost sensitivity, soundness, disaggregated metrics, a comprehension protocol, and a reject-despite-good-aggregates rule, plus PM4Py skeletons. Almost none of that appears in the final text. Planning it earlier does not count if it never lands.

What remains is still clearly above a mid-pack process-mining essay: use-case framing, S/C language analysis, B3/B4/B8 handling, C’s spurious AA↔no-SR constraint, silent-pass of missing Supplier Review on C’s branches 1/2/8, and a generally well-motivated POWL-plus-severity design. Under the stated rubric—hypercritical, nearly flawless only for a high score, required headings mandatory—those strengths cannot carry a truncated, spec-incomplete submission.
