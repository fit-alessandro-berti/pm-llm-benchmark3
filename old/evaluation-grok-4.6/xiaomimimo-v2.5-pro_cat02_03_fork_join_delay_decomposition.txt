**4.5**

The write-up has the right fork-join *shape* (max-based `join_ready`, critical branch ≠ longest service, non-critical acceleration can be worthless) and the required seven-section skeleton. It is not close to a high score: the required case table, aggregates, counterfactuals, and Python all contain material errors, and several of those errors are exactly the distinctions the prompt asked the analyst not to miss.

**Case table and internal consistency.** Queue and service columns are mostly correct, as are critical-branch labels (A: F01/F11; C: F04/F08; B: the other eight). `join_ready` is not. F01 is fork-relative (50), most later rows are arrival-relative, and F08/F09 match neither convention (70 vs 75/55; 80 vs 85/65). Worked F02 text says “50 min elapsed from fork_release”; \(09{:}25-08{:}25=60\). Totals and downstream waits are wrong for F08 (105 vs 110), F09 (115 vs 120), F10 (ds 15 vs 20, total 110 vs 115), and F12 (ds 25 vs 10, total 140 vs 125). Those four flow times then contaminate the tail claim (F12 = 140) and the CF3 mean (~15.8 vs actual \(176/12 \approx 14.7\)).

**Aggregates.** C mean queue is \(125/12 \approx 10.42\), not 10.0. Mean synchronization waits are all wrong: A is 11.0 not 16.3; C is \(\approx 30.4\) not 33.1; B is \(\approx 4.3\) not 0. Reporting B’s mean sync as 0 treats “critical ⇒ sync = 0” as if it held on every case. That is a definitional failure, not a rounding issue.

**Counterfactuals (the slack test).** Reducing A by 20% does **not** cut F01 by 10 minutes: A moves from 09:00 to 08:50, B is already at 08:55, so realized join-ready saving is 5 minutes. That is the prompt’s central point—acceleration only helps until the next branch binds—and it is missed. CF2 states F12 saves 17.5 minutes; a 50% cut of a 30-minute queue is 15, and F05/F10 would not even capture the full cut (A binds). The claimed secondary effect on F01/F11 is backwards: those cases are already A-critical, so shrinking B’s queue cannot “force A to become” critical.

**Python vs. spec.** The log construction, `format_dataframe`, `discover_performance_dfg`, and the comment that DFG/directly-follows metrics do not recover synchronization waiting are fine. The block does **not** implement CF2 or CF3, does not print per-case savings, and leaves “additional counterfactuals” as a stub. Sync waits are never computed. That is a hard miss of items 4 and 8.

**Format and completeness.** Headings are `###` plus a persona preamble, not the specified top-level set. Elapsed fork-join time is never given as its own additive component. The double-counting argument is directionally right but not shown as a formula (`\(\sum_i \mathrm{sync}_{c,i} = 3\cdot\mathrm{join\_ready}_c - \sum_i i_{\mathrm{end},c}\)`, which is not an elapsed interval).

**What remains defensible.** Frequency ranking of B, refusal to treat A’s flat 50-minute service as dominance, explicit zero-benefit cases when another branch stays last, downstream masking, two redesigns aimed at F capacity and B queue, and a solid §7 on causality, hidden capacity, preemption, and queue vs. release policy. Those points keep the grade out of the failure band. The arithmetic, slack handling, and incomplete code keep it far from “nearly flawless.”
