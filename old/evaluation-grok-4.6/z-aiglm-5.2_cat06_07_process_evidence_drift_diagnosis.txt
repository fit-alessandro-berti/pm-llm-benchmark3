**8.0**

The final six-section writeup is a strong audit: it correctly treats April as a schema/pipeline incident, June as a routing/mix change that need not be concept drift, August–September as overlapping process/concept/population effects, and October as heavily confounded by label maturity. It does not collapse the year into one drift label or treat October’s 0.16 as comparable to mature months. Remediation is mostly proportionate. Under a hypercritical reading, several inaccuracies, sloppy classifications, and incomplete artifacts keep it well short of near-flawless.

**What is solid.** The timeline is the best part. January–March are treated as the reference; April’s AUROC/ECE collapse is tied to the rename, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, missing queue features, and unmapped variants rather than to a worse model; May is residual backfill, not a new regime; June’s per-variant rates (0.16 / 0.09 / 0.35) are used to reject concept drift; July–August V3 (0.46 → 0.64) is tied to the `Order Part → Part Received` edge (65h → 130h) while V1/V2 stay flat; September adds Partner Z and missing rural/device-age features; October is quarantined. Section 4’s staged containment / recalibrate-June / repair-features / retrain-later plan matches that story. Section 6 hits the required failure modes (immature-label retraining, calibrating a pipeline bug, ignoring supplier-driven V3 drift, fairness-washing Partner Z) with usable mitigations. Alert suppression so a maturity or data-quality red does not auto-trigger retraining is the right architecture.

**Classification is messier than the narrative.** June is simultaneously “covariate drift,” “process drift,” and “population shift.” The timeline later says the honest primary is a routing-policy mix shift. Putting April under covariate drift is also a stretch: corrupted or missing features after a rename are a pipeline failure, which the same table already covers. The concept-drift row then hedges that V3 may only be an unmodeled covariate (parts delay). That ambiguity is intellectually fair, but the table never names a *primary* mechanism per month, so the triangulation the rubric asks for is diluted.

**A real data-table slip.** The variant table is labeled *matured* breach rates; October is the only month marked unusable. August V3 = 0.64 and September V3 = 0.61 are therefore already matured estimates. The writeup repeatedly treats those figures as still censored by the 93% / 82% *overall* maturity column (“likely understated,” “potentially worse than observed”). Overall observed rates (0.28 / 0.31 / 0.16) *are* maturity-contaminated; the variant series is not in the same way. That conflation weakens the “separate label maturity from real performance” criterion even though the qualitative October call is correct.

**Overconfident or slightly wrong claims.** Risk 5 says June shows AUROC “appearing to decline”; June AUROC is 0.79 vs a 0.81 baseline — the live issue is ECE 0.060 from mix, not discrimination. Test 2’s “April AUROC returns to 0.78” is an invented target (why not ~0.81?). “October’s true rate will be substantially higher than 0.16” is directionally plausible given the API bias toward long paths, but it is stated as fact. Arbitrary gates (90%, 95%, 40-day delay) are presented as if they were given.

**Diagnostic tests are complete but uneven.** All required test families appear, and most have two competing expected results. Test 1’s falsification is circular: a matured October rate above 0.31 is said both to show extra genuine breaches *and* to confirm that delay was the dominant confound. Test 5’s missing-feature vs true-concept split is the sharpest test in the set; Test 6’s reweight/transport design is appropriate for Partner Z. Falsifiers are present but not uniformly crisp.

**Monitoring code is a sketch, not an audit extract.** It does the important things: `format_dataframe`, diagnosis-label normalization before features, deterministic `(case, activity, timestamp)` dedup, and an explicit event-calendar vs case-start split with the right interpretation note. It uses `filter_time_range`, `get_variants`, `discover_performance_dfg`, and `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, and it does not invent a PM4Py drift detector. Gaps: `discover_dfg` is never called despite being listed; `dfg.get(edge)` is stored raw instead of a median hour; temporal features contribute only `.shape`; variant mix is a nested dict, not monthly V1/V2/V3 shares. The function therefore does not actually “return auditable monthly summaries.” `mode="events"` is plausible but version-sensitive.

**Smaller nits.** Partner Z’s 0.45 vs 0.27 is used more firmly than the “Z labels mature more slowly” caveat allows. The ASCII dependency diagram is hard to parse. Section 2’s “is the model broken?” table is useful but flattens July–September into “yes for V3” without stating how much of the *overall* metric move is mix vs relationship change.

**Net.** This is a competent, well-structured strong answer on the rubric’s main discriminators. It is not nearly flawless: drift types are over-assigned, matured variant rates are mixed with cutoff maturity, a few numeric claims are invented or overstated, and the PM4Py block stops short of the summaries it promises. Those are enough, under a strict bar, to hold the score at 8.0 rather than the 9s this kind of writeup often receives.
