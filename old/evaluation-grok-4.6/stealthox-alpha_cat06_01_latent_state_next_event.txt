**6.2**

The answer is process-aware, well structured, and clearly stronger than a DFG-frequency baseline, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the task, it has a **systematic error** in next-event semantics and a **repeated contradiction** on `process_enabled`. Those are not cosmetic.

### What is actually strong
The obligation analysis is mostly right. C6-A and C6-B are not cloned despite isomorphic prefixes; severity, injury, object cardinality, lot clustering, and duplicate similarity are used as obligation drivers rather than as flavor text. Rule 5 is applied carefully (one complaint with two devices does not by itself mandate Supplier Analysis; six complaints on one lot does). Completion-only logging and rule 7 are used as an observation model, not as an afterthought. Queue load, risk-based RA priority, TI overtaking, the 16:00 notification batch, and the 48-hour delay window appear in the right places. The cross-case section on DFG limitations is the best part of the submission.

### Core logical failure: “next logged” vs “later on the path”
The task asks for the **next logged** activity or termination. Across C6-A, C6-B, and C6-E, rank 2/3 are mostly **successors of rank 1**, not competing next events.

- C6-A rank 2, `Customer Evidence Requested`, cannot appear until Evidence Review completes. The text admits this, then still scores it as the second most likely **next** event (0.12–0.25), above Technical Investigation Completed.
- That ranking is backwards. TI/RA completions are the only serious **concurrent** next-event alternatives, because starts are unlogged and high-severity work may already be running. The author even calls Regulatory Assessment Completed “a close substitute” inside the rank-3 rationale, then does not give it a slot.
- C6-B rank 2/3 (`Close — No Safety Issue`, `Fast Close`) have the same problem: both are gated on review completion.
- C6-E rank 3 (third evidence cycle) is again gated on the rank-1 review.

This is not a wording quibble. It collapses the distinction the prompt explicitly required.

### Direct contradiction on `process_enabled`
Requirement 6 is to distinguish a currently enabled action from the event that may be observed next. The submission understands this once (C6-D rank 2 is correctly `false`) and then violates it everywhere else.

- C6-C rank 3: reasoning says Disposition is **“Not enabled at the current instant”**; `process_enabled` is `true`.
- C6-D rank 3: reasoning says Disposition is **“Currently blocked”**; `process_enabled` is `true`.
- C6-A rank 2, C6-B ranks 2–3, C6-E rank 3 are not enabled now; all are `true`.

C6-A’s rank-1 rationale (“the only activity the current state enables next”) is also inconsistent with the stated S4 alternative and with rank 3.

### Other defects that should have been caught
- **C6-B Fast Close** after a completed evidence cycle is observationally unsupported (the DFG only has Triage → Fast Close) and sits poorly against the S1 fast-close abstraction. Using it as a top-3 next event, with 0.07–0.15, spends a slot that should have been a concurrent or review-gated **logged** alternative (further evidence request, or a delayed review completion).
- **C6-D rank 2** is two different activities glued with a slash. That is not a single next event.
- **C6-D completed_obligations** lists unlogged evidence as completed. That mixes inference with trace fact; the alternative state already covers the point.
- Rank-1 intervals are too tight relative to the author’s own alternatives. If TI/RA may already be running, migration can delay events, and C6-C’s RA assignment time is unknown in a 12-deep non-FIFO queue, 0.42–0.58 (C6-C) and 0.40–0.60 (C6-A) overclaim. C6-C/D top-three midpoint mass (~0.64–0.68) then dumps a large residual on unnamed outcomes without naming a time horizon, even while listing “undefined prediction horizon” as an ambiguity.
- Authority Notification’s 16:00 batch is discussed in the abstract and almost unused in the live cases where reportability is the live risk (especially C6-D after a confirmed RA start).

### Calibration
Uncertainty language is more honest than typical PPM point forecasts, and the residual-mass note is directionally right. That does not offset the fact that several quoted intervals are attached to events that cannot be next, and that `process_enabled` is internally false in two places.

This is a good process-semantics essay wrapped around an incorrect next-event estimator. Strictly, that is well above a DFG-copy answer and well below a reliable one.
