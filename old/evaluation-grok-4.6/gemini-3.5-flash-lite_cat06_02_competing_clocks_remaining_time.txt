**4.0**

The answer has the right *scaffold* (six required sections, scenario envelopes, `max` rather than a sum, some dependence language) and a central window that happens to sit on Monday–Tuesday. That is not enough. The judging criteria are temporal decomposition, calendars, parallelism, and honest uncertainty. On those, the write-up is systematically wrong, internally contradictory, and arithmetically unreliable.

**Incubation arithmetic is false, and the error is load-bearing.** Primary incubation is 46–54 h after Friday 14:00, i.e. **Sunday 12:00–Sunday 20:00**. The answer writes “Sunday 12:00 and Saturday 16:00,” then staffs a Saturday 15:00 on-call readout and an earliest terminal of **Saturday 19 Sep 17:00**. Saturday 16:00 is only ~26 h after Friday 14:00. A 46 h clock cannot produce a Saturday result. That single mistake invents a weekend-release path that the process cannot take.

**Saturday completion is impossible even if incubation were ignored.** QA Saturday hours end at 13:00; Sunday is closed; electronic release requires QA acceptance after *both* usable results. A Sunday-ready micro result, even with on-call readout, still meets a closed QA function until Monday 08:00. Section 3 actually says QA starts Monday ~08:00 and the smooth terminal is Monday ~12:00 — then Sections 2 and 4 advertise Saturday 17:00. The sections do not agree with each other.

**Weekend on-call is therefore mis-ranked.** Authorization (0.25–0.45) moves readout from Monday 08:00–09:30 to Sunday afternoon/evening. It does **not** open QA on Sunday. On a smooth path the terminal shift is on the order of an hour Monday morning, not “Saturday vs Monday.” Treating on-call as the main high-sensitivity fork is the wrong dominance story.

**Chemistry Friday finish is incompatible with the queue, the triangular service law, and the no-overtime rule.** At 15:30 there are 2.5 h to close, two busy analysts (0.4–0.9 h and 1.2–2.0 h left), one urgent plus three ordinary jobs, and service triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5). Even under risk-priority, B-417 at best takes the second free analyst ~16:42–17:30; remaining Friday capacity is then 0.5–1.3 h, below the 1.5 h minimum. FIFO (~55%) is worse. The text says start ~16:30, service ~2.5 h, finish 19:00 — then “completed safely before 18:00,” then “assume normal finish Friday 17:30,” plus “minor spillover/buffer.” That is three incompatible clocks plus unauthorized overtime. Chemistry is much more likely to **pause and finish Monday**, and under FIFO it can be the *slower* branch. The answer barely prices that 55% path.

**Confirmation timing is also wrong.** A contamination indication is observed at *readout*, not when the plate becomes technically ready. If there is no Sunday read, the extra 20–30 h starts Monday morning, so confirmatory readiness is **Tuesday**, not Monday ~14:00. Scenario 3 is therefore a day early.

**Remaining-hour bands do not match the stated clock times.** From Friday 15:30: Monday 12:00 is ~68.5 h, Tuesday 15:00 is ~95.5 h. The answer maps “54–72 hours” onto that window. 54 h is Sunday evening; 72 h is Monday 15:30. The P80 map (72–120 h → Tuesday–Thursday) is similarly off (120 h is Wednesday afternoon). Mixing “working/calendar hours” without a conversion rule makes the numeric forecast look quantitative when it is not even self-consistent.

**QA at the future join is asserted, not decomposed.** Five batches are already waiting; Friday has 4.5 reviewer-hours left; Saturday has 4; arrivals continue at 0.35/h with overdispersion; Monday opens with two reviewers. Residual work into Monday morning is plausible and should have been bounded. Instead the smooth path starts QA at Monday 08:00 with ~1.5–3.5 h service and almost no wait. The join-time queue, which the prompt required, is a driver in Section 5 and a ghost in Section 3.

**Smaller but still material defects.** (i) Scenario probabilities treat the raw rerun rate 10–20% as if it were P(rerun *dominates the terminal clock*); a Friday/Monday chemistry rerun often still loses to micro + QA. (ii) Smooth-path tail Monday 16:00 ignores ordinary QA congestion. (iii) Batch rejection at 10–15% is not supported: an indication (0.05–0.12) is not a confirmed critical defect. (iv) The secondary update trigger (“primary readout before Friday 18:00”) is physically impossible. (v) Dependence is named and given point bumps (0.35 / 0.20) but never carried through the scenario table as a correlated mixture.

What remains is a competent template wrapped around broken clocks. The Monday-centered *central* range is roughly in the right calendar neighborhood, largely because QA is closed when the author thought micro would finish Saturday. That is not a defensible forecast; it is a cancelled error. Under a rubric that punishes even minor issues, and given failures on the exact axes the task said it would grade, this is well below a passing expert answer.
