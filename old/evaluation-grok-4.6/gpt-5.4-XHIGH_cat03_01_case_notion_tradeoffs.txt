This is a well-structured, identifier-heavy answer that hits every required section and almost every mechanical constraint. It is not close to flawless. Two analytical mistakes are serious enough that a strict reading cannot treat the object-type comparison as reliable.

**The package-flattening claim is factually wrong.** The matrix states that package-level event duplication is “Low” because “each package-linked event belongs to one package case.” That is false. `e16` and `e24` are directly related to both `P2` and `P3`. It is a missed instance of the exact phenomenon the prompt told the author to track, and it sits in the required comparison matrix.

**“Convergence risk” is applied inconsistently with flattening semantics.** Even after granting the author’s own definition, the ratings do not measure a risk of the chosen case notion. `Delivery` is marked “Very High” and `Invoice` “High” because `D2` and `V2` are hubs where *other* objects meet.
