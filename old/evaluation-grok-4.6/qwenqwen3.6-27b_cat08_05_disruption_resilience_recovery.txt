**6.4**

The final seven-section writeup is complete, well structured, and mostly faithful to the required format. It uses the phase metrics, conformance scores, and most adaptation notes, and it correctly refuses to equate T4 operational recovery with compliance recovery. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

**Material accuracy and DFG grounding.** The timeline treats `Dispatch without scan` as 1,800 cases. In the T2 DFG that node has two inbound edges (1,800 + 1,450). Using only the larger edge understates the dominant failure mode the text itself calls the largest traceability gap. A few other frequencies are cited correctly, which makes the miss look like careless DFG reading rather than a one-off rounding issue.

**Resilience vs. concealed noncompliance is inconsistent.** Section 2 correctly flags that A sometimes bypassed authorization and should be modified. Section 3 then lists A as unqualified “true resilience” and claims those adaptations “preserved or substituting controls.” That collapses the exact distinction the prompt requires. Mission-aligned throughput with missing authorization is the ambiguous / risky case, not the clean resilience case. C/G are handled better; A is not.

**Temporal recovery is overstated.** T4 is described as cycle times recovered, with hospital median 8h as the exhibit. Wholesale-bulk is still 58h / 120h versus 36h / 72h baseline; pharmacy p90 is 74h versus 49h. Calling operational metrics “near baseline” hides the segment that never actually recovered and weakens the fairness argument the rest of the memo tries to make.

**Table decisions hedge the required choice.** Several rows are `Keep/Modify` or `Retire/Modify`. The prompt asked for a decision. G in particular needs a hard call; “retire the scan bypass, replace with a signed handoff log” is a modify-the-control, retire-the-bypass distinction the cell fudges.

**Internal design contradictions.** Section 4 allows a 48h backlog age for Priority 1/2. Section 5 sets P1 < 24h and P2 < 36h. For hospital-critical work whose baseline p90 is 15h, a 48h P1 backlog rule is also poorly calibrated. Invented thresholds are acceptable in a redesign; contradicting yourself is not.

**Smaller gaps.** Recall-sensitive product risk is almost absent from the A–L table. Spreadsheet invoice hold is a named T2 move-on-log deviation and barely appears. Leading “throughput” is implied via fairness and cycle-time lag rather than stated. None of these would sink a strong answer alone; stacked on the issues above they keep it well short of near-flawless.

The recovery model, indicator set, hidden-tail treatment of K/L, and the management trade-off paragraph are the strongest parts. The grade is held down by DFG miscount, a core misclassification of A, T4 cherry-picking, and conflicting SLAs.
