**6.0**

This is a structurally complete policy with the right skeleton (stage gate, calibration, evidence persistence, causal Δ not `p_now`, spillovers, reserve slot, I-03 exclusion, I-05 vs I-07). It is not decision-theoretically coherent enough, nor operationally realistic enough, to score higher. Several errors are in the actual allocation, which is the part that matters.

**The stated decision is internally inconsistent.** I-07 is labeled **Wait / backup** in the allocation table and then named as an **Intervene now** case in the selected portfolio. A deployable noon rule cannot give two actions for the same case. The hedge “if the 45-minute swarm does not fit in 1.5h, promote I-02” makes the third slot undefined rather than reserved.

**I-07 is the wrong third slot.** New evidence correctly *re-qualifies* I-07; it does not entitle it to scarce capacity. Midpoint direct EV is ~€839, the lowest among serious candidates, and the lower bound is only barely positive. The case is still in **Triage** with **1.5h** to deadline. After a 45-minute swarm the remaining path is still Diagnosis → Resolution → Validation → Closed. Pulling both seniors off the diagnosis queue for that is a poor use of the intervention’s effective window. I-02 is already at Diagnosis Started, has 3h left, ~€3k midpoint EV, and remains positive at the lower Δ bound even after the SE downshift. Deferring I-02 as “timing risky” while swarming a *tighter* triage case is self-contradictory.

Urgency ordering is inverted more generally: long-runway triage cases (I-04 at 10h, I-01 at 7h) are served immediately; short-fuse diagnosis-stage cases (I-02, I-09) are told to wait. That fights the constraint that the swarm is useless after Diagnosis Complete.

**Eligibility and prioritization are conflated.** Gate (c) is the right persistence rule. Using it as a ranking bonus is how I-07 jumps over higher-value, better-timed work. I-08’s weak Δ and €5k burden are good reasons to wait; they are not reasons to spend the slot on I-07 instead of I-02.

**Section 5 is violated by Section 2.** The fairness write-up says not to let NNL cases lose slots *purely* because intervals are wide, and to send them down a dedicated review path. I-09 is then parked as wait/do-not-yet solely for sparse calibration, with 2.5h left on-stage, Δ 0.18–0.30, and €20k at stake. That is exclusion by uncertainty, exactly what the safeguard forbids. “Review path” is not operationalized for noon.

**Section 3 is not an audit of the rule in Section 1.** Direct benefit is Δ × value only; swarm cost is separate; **false-alarm burden disappears** from net impact even though gate (d) charges `(1−p*)×burden`. For this portfolio that is ~€1.3k, enough to move the adverse row. “Upper half of the range” is implemented as the **upper endpoint**. Spillover is monetized at an unexplained ~€15k exposure with no sensitivity. The adverse “wrong-reason” zero is assigned to I-07, the cheapest way to protect the narrative; if the dud is I-04, net impact is largely negative. Specialist sequencing (two people, three 45-minute swarms, ordinary diagnosis stopped) is not in the spillover arithmetic.

Smaller issues that still matter under a strict read: Public Sector “widen the interval” is asserted and never shown; calibration is collapsed to point shifts (+0.065 / −0.085) and not carried as ranges into the tiering; spillover is a portfolio afterthought rather than a term in the case scores; non-monetary public-sector/regulatory duty is mostly the reserve-slot preference, not a scored obligation; I-06 “verify first” has no clock, so a real 0.20→0.52 jump can expire while the reserve sits.

**What is actually good, and why that is not enough.** I-03 is refused despite `p_now=0.82`. I-05 is suppressed as a 30-minute refresh with no new event; I-07 is not. Δ is not treated as observational `p`. Three scenarios exist. Fairness and monitoring (segment floors, treatment labels, hold-back, spillover-by-segment, versioned rule changes) are the strongest pages and are more careful than the live allocation they are supposed to govern.

The piece reads like a high-quality template with a broken third-slot decision, a table/narrative clash, and an impact table that does not implement the stated objective. That is a 6, not an 8.
