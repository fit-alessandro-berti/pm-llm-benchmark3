The answer is a strong control-objective analysis: it does not count activities, does not treat four-eyes form as the objective, and consistently uses timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, and tail exposure. Classifications for the hard cases (AP-202, AP-204, AP-205, AP-206, AP-208, AP-209) are mostly the ones a careful reviewer would defend. Remaining defects are real, even if none collapse the argument.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 decomposes FEP-1 into seven threat scenarios and maps each control’s prevent/detect/correct/fail behavior, including AV-2’s stated blind spots. Section 2’s equivalence test is the right instrument: capability and irrevocability first, amount as a severity scaler. The rejection of “same expected loss” is earned, and AP-208 is used correctly as the counterexample. Section 4’s hybrid semantics for post-release, pre-settlement manager action are precise and then applied consistently (AP-201/207 vs AP-208). Section 6 distinguishes alert from proof. The code meets the mechanical spec (StringIO, `format_dataframe`, four-eyes filter, blocked AP-205, facts join, labeled evidence flags) and is unusually honest about PM4Py version drift.

**Accuracy and overclaim.**

- ED-1 condition 6 requires an L3+ manager. Additional facts establish independence for m02/m11/m15/m04, not grade. Calling AP-203/AP-207 “all seven conditions evidenced” and scoring High-confidence `OBJECTIVE_MET` overstates the file. m21’s independence is not stated at all; the facts table still sets `manager_independent=True` for AP-208.
- T4 attributes “shared service accounts are not a second person” to ED-1. That rule is FEP-1.
- `Audit_Review` is a point event. Treating it as RA-7 *completion* within one business day is an assumption, not a fact.
- AP-208’s token is only “active,” with no pre-prepare timestamp. The narrative is careful; the code is not (`emergency_token_before_prepare=True`).

**Classification nits (not wrong, not tight enough).**

- AP-204 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` is defensible for payment integrity and pair-independence, but the write-up also says FEP-1 never claimed to stop collusion or fake receipts. That tension is acknowledged, then under-resolved: the row should have separated “SoD form held” from “integrity objective failed.”
- AP-201 as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is generous. There is no ED-1 envelope, the same-user act was convenience, and it is unknown whether m04 could stop the 10:00 settlement. The residual-risk text is better than the label.
- AP-209 as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is preferable to auto-MET, but given missing org-design documents the problem itself plants, `INDETERMINATE` was equally available. Confidence “low–medium” saves this more than the label does.

**Section 5.** Six cases, qualitative ranges, causal-chain framing: good. The AP-202 counterfactual still underweights that a second releaser who can be bypassed with m09’s credential does not restore independence. The AP-206 residual (“low if m11 was real”) is slightly too soothing while eligibility remains the open issue.

**Code (evidence-quality, not a verdict — correctly labeled).** Fragile `left_on=completed["preparer"]+"-"+...` merge; `format="ISO8601"` is pandas 2+ only and is not in the version caveats; “one business day” is implemented as `days=1, hours=16`; EventLog→`pd.DataFrame(kept)` is not a reliable column mapping even with the try/except. The flip heuristic for filter semantics is clever and still not a proof of what the installed function returns. None of this is fatal because the same-user join is the real detector.

**Net.** Control reasoning is well above a typical conformance dump, and the required structure is followed exactly. It is not nearly flawless: MG-4 eligibility is assumed, one control rule is mis-attributed, two MET cases are over-certified, and the checking code is more robust in commentary than in joins/heuristics. Under a strict rubric those are enough to keep it out of the 9s.
