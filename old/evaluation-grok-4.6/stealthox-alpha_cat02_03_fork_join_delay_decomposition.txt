## What is actually correct

The delay definitions are the right ones: `join_ready = max_i E_i`, branch queue after `fork_release`, sync wait as slack to that max, and elapsed time as `T = upstream + stage + downstream + final`. The no-double-count point is right in substance: `q_i + s_i + w_i` equals the same wall-clock stage for every branch, so summing the three paths counts the parallel window three times (F07: 225 vs 75). Case-level numbers for all 12 cases check out, including F02/F04/F07/F11. Critical labels A/B/C = 2/8/2 are right. Counterfactual *savings* for the three required designs (15 / 92.5 / 176) match the max-based logic, including F01’s 5-minute CF1 cap and F10’s 5-minute CF2 cap. Zero-benefit cases are identified. §7 is the best section: timestamps are not causes, `end − start` is not proven busy time, and `final_start − join_ready` is not proven queueing.

## Numerical and consistency failures (these are not nits)

The §3 comparison table is internally inconsistent on the exact quantity the prompt asked to use (queue + service, not service alone):

- Reported B queue mean **17.5** vs actual **19.17** (`230/12`).
- Reported B `q+s` mean **55.0** vs actual **56.7**, which is the same as their own “contribution to J” row. Those two rows must be identical; they are not.
- C queue **10.0** vs **10.42**; C `q+s` **30.2** vs **30.6** — same pattern, smaller size.

B is still the right top-ranked branch, but the headline ranking table does not add up.

Bottleneck-migration counts are wrong in two places. After CF2 (B queue −50%), A is critical in **6** cases (including two ties under their A-first rule), not “~8”. After the stronger R1 design (B queue → 0), A is critical in **10/12**, not “~8/12”. Their own Python would have printed the CF2 labels; the prose was not reconciled with it.

F-station utilization is stated as **≈57%** (240/424, correct for 09:08–12:40 × 2) and later as **≈0.67**. Those cannot both be the measured utilization. 0.67 is a steady-state offered-load sketch (`20 / 15 / 2`), not the log.

## Formula and code defects

Section 1 is supposed to give usable formulas. As written, operators are missing throughout (`FR_c  AR_c`, `i  {A,B,C}`, `_i w`, `77  75`). The algebra is recoverable from prose and the F07 example, but the required formulas are not actually written.

The Python `join_ready` reconstruction is buggy:

`fork_release + timedelta(join_ready_min)`

`join_ready_min` is minutes from midnight (e.g. 565), not minutes after fork. Displayed `join_ready` from the code therefore cannot reproduce the §2 clock times. Downstream math that uses `join_ready_min` as an absolute minute clock is fine; the printed PM table is not.

## Analytical misses

`final_resource` is a perfect F1/F2 alternation (odd cases F1, even F2). That is stronger evidence of **pre-assignment / two lanes** than of a shared two-server queue. They did notice that some waits line up with the assigned server freeing (F05/F08/F09/F11, 60 min) and that many waits do not, but they overstated the complement: “the other 8 cases the F-pool was already idle.” F10’s join at 11:20 has **both** F1 and F2 busy until 11:25, then another 15 minutes after F2 is free. CF3’s 176 minutes is correctly treated as an upper bound in §7, then still sold in §4–5 as the largest lever without keeping that bound in the recommendation’s headline numbers.

Including `w_i` inside `max_i(q+s+w)` is redundant (the sum is already equal across i). Not false, but it blurs that elapsed stage time is `max(q_i+s_i)` and sync wait is slack.

## Why not lower, and why not higher

The required structure is present, the 12-row decomposition is right, CF savings are right, and the main qualitative conclusion (B controls via queue+service; A’s 50-minute service is a floor; speeding a noncritical branch can save nothing; downstream may mask branch gains) is the conclusion the data support. That is real process-mining competence.

It is not a high score because the main aggregate table contradicts itself, migration frequencies are invented-looking round numbers, the formula block is not actually formulas, and the reproducible code would print a wrong `join_ready`. Those are exactly the failure modes a strict judge should not forgive in a delay-decomposition write-up.
