**7.2**

The memo is structurally complete and strategically sound: it refuses national scale and full rollback, treats R as a bundle of separable interventions, and recommends a hybrid (E) plus measurement (D). Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are mostly separated. The five metric conflicts are explained with the right process-mining artifacts (fitness vs precision, alignments, DFG, temporal profile, social networks). All of A–E and M1–M8 are covered.

It is not nearly flawless. Several decision thresholds are invented and then treated as if derived from the data. Simple-house FTR ≥85% assumes an unstated segment baseline of 82% (only a +3pp *change* is given). Simple-house all-in cost ≤ EUR 184 confuses overall *visible* cost with segment *all-in* cost. The memo requires complaints “no worse than baseline after 60 days,” but the given metric is 30-day complaints; that mixes M4’s cost window with a different KPI. Mix-adjustment “to the pilot mix” uses the biased sample as the reference population instead of baseline or intended national mix.

Coordinator scope is internally inconsistent: scale coordinators only for simple houses, yet also “pause broad coordinator ownership *without backup*,” which implies coordinators may continue elsewhere with backup. The memo overclaims causation (“survey skipping and post-billing activation produced paths”) that the evidence table correctly treats as uncertain. NPS is gated with “not materially worse” while other gates are numeric. Observation-window bias is ignored: a 6-week pilot reporting p95 of 62 days needs an explicit stance on follow-up and incomplete cases; that is a material process-mining caveat for tail comparisons.

VOI ranking is defensible on causal value but underrates cheap, fast tests (M6, M7) relative to a randomized skip trial, and does not weigh cost of information. These are enough real flaws that a top-band score is not warranted.
