**4.8**

The write-up looks complete and consultant-polished, but it is not reliable. It hits the required seven-section skeleton and repeats the right slogans (throughput is not resilience; after-the-fact audit is not real-time control). Underneath that, temporal attribution, metric reading, the resilience/noncompliance split, and the recovery design all break in material ways. On a hypercritical rubric this is an adequate outline with too many invented, inverted, or operationally impossible claims to score higher.

**Temporal reasoning is sloppy.** T1 is the morning outage, not the workaround regime; improvisation belongs to T2. Labeling T2 “partial automation” is simply wrong — T2 is the manual-workaround phase; partial system return is T3. J and K are evidenced in the stabilization DFG, not as T3 dominant variants. The claim that dispatch-without-scan “lingered in 27% of cases” in T4 has no support in the stabilization DFG (that graph has no `Dispatch without scan` edge). That number appears fabricated.

**Several “evidence” cells invert or invent the data.** A compares hospital fill rate (91%) to baseline median cycle time (8h) — a category error. B is said to have “reduced rework” while T2 rework rose from 3.5% to 12.5%. F is said to have reduced dual-control gaps “from 0.2% to 1.4%”; that is an increase, and the T2 figure is 2.9%, not 1.4%. K did not cut backorder waits “600 → 300”; those are different baseline activities, not a before/after for reconciliation. L did not restore baseline traceability at 2.2% (baseline is 0.8%). Pharmacy fill-rate “baseline 100%” and “T4 95%” are not in the case. Invented KPIs are disqualifying on a data-grounded task.

**The resilience vs. concealed-noncompliance distinction is asserted, then misapplied.** I (defer noncritical) is capacity allocation / graceful degradation, not concealed noncompliance; retiring it violates the instruction not to treat every baseline deviation as bad. D was explicitly reasonable early in T2 and should be time-boxed (snapshot TTL), not retired. L cannot be retired after missing scans, temperature records, and authorizations; the correct move is to modify it so operational recovery is not mistaken for compliance closure. A is treated as resilience with a footnote, but it is the textbook ambiguous case (mission protection plus authorization bypass). The section’s definitions are also wrong: resilience is not “temporary by nature,” and concealed noncompliance is not “permanent.”

**DFG evidence is underused where it would have mattered.** In T2, more packs went straight to `Dispatch without scan` (1,800) than through `Temporary release control` (1,650), and the “control” path still dispatched without scan (1,450). That is the core abductive finding — compensatory controls were optional and did not restore the scan. The answer never uses those frequencies.

**The recovery model fails the incident it claims to handle.** Triggers, triage, minimum controls, evidence, backlog, and remediation are all named, but the controls assume the broken substrate: mandatory barcode scans and e-signatures while WMS/scanning are down. Ambient is misclassified as high risk. The backlog score `(Criticality × Risk) / Cycle Time` prioritizes already-fast work, not aging or harm. Cancelling non-critical work at 48h ignores baseline bulk p90 of 72h. The “POWL-style” block is unlabeled ASCII, not POWL, and is not explained.

**Recommendations and the trade-off paragraph inherit the same errors.** “Blacklists” is the wrong object (the gap is standing authorization / whitelists, plus restriction lists). Residual risk “None” for scan/e-sign mandates is unserious after a cyber outage. The evidence-that-would-change-the-call is inverted: a shrinking remediation queue makes limited after-the-fact cleanup *more* acceptable, not less; good real-time metrics do not argue for *more* emergency controls.

**What remains creditworthy:** format compliance; correct use of some conformance figures (44% / 59% / 76% fitting traces); C and G correctly treated as high-risk shortcuts; J correctly tagged as modify-if-rules-are-informal; explicit recognition of L as a hidden post-recovery tail; and a management section that at least uses accept / do-not-accept language. That is enough for a mid-fail / low-pass, not a high score.
