**6.6**

The write-up is well structured, hits the required six sections, and gets the main “gotchas” right: I-03 is ineligible, I-05 is suppressed while I-07 is treated as new evidence, observational `p_now` is not treated as a causal effect, and spillover/reserve are inside the objective. It is not close to flawless. Several ranking and robustness claims are internally inconsistent, and a few operational constraints are under-modeled.

**What is solid**
- Stage gate, evidence gate, data-quality gate, and segment calibration adjustments are the right levers and match the case notes.
- The allocation table covers all ten cases with a real wait / do-not-intervene vocabulary, not a probability ranking.
- I-06 is held for imputation, I-08 is waited because the lower bound is negative after a €5k false-alarm burden, and one slot is reserved.
- The impact table is roughly auditable and correctly allows an adverse net loss.
- Repeated-alert logic and the I-05 vs I-07 contrast are deployable.
- Fairness/feedback and monitoring name the right failure modes (label contamination, spillover incidence, segment drift) and give triggers, not slogans.

**Decision-theoretic problems**
- The rule says a slot is justified only if *robust* net benefit beats spillover. I-02’s lower-bound direct net is about €950. After the answer’s own €1.2k–€3.6k spillover, I-02 fails that test, yet it is Tier 1 and selected. The rule is not the rule that was applied.
- I-08’s midpoint direct net (~€4.6k) is higher than I-02’s (~€2.7k). I-08 is waited for a negative lower bound and high disruption; I-02 is taken despite a similarly weak lower bound once spillover is included. That is an inconsistent robustness stance, not a reasoned risk attitude.
- I-09 is the case whose *effect* estimates are flagged as least reliable, and the adverse row assumes it may get zero benefit. It is still locked as a firm intervene / Tier 1 using the face-value 0.18–0.30 range. “Widen uncertainty” is stated, then ignored in the ranking. A coherent robust rule would make I-09 conditional on completed language corroboration, or shrink its ARR toward a skeptical prior before committing a scarce slot.
- Calibration is applied to `p` but not to ARR. Strategic overprediction of 0.05–0.12 is exactly the kind of bias that should shrink I-02/I-04 effect ranges. Leaving ARR untouched while advertising calibration robustness is incomplete.
- I-04 is the best EV case, but it is still in Triage with 10 hours left. Committing a slot at 12:00 throws away option value and reserve flexibility. Sequencing it last shows the author knows it is not time-critical; locking the slot anyway is not justified.
- Reserve “option value” is never quantified, so the “keep one slot unless the fourth case clears the hurdle” rule is not actually computable.
- Full false-alarm burden is subtracted from every intervention, including optimistic/base rows where the swarm is assumed to work. The prompt’s template invites that, and the author flags conservatism, but then uses it for ranking. That quietly punishes high-disruption cases (I-08) while still blessing I-04. The concept of a false alarm is not cleanly separated from a fixed coordination cost.

**Operational gaps**
- Two seniors *deliver* the swarm and stop ordinary diagnosis. Three 45-minute swarms are 2+ hours with both (or at least one) seniors off the queue. That is a larger capacity hit than a 0.01–0.03 probability increment on six unnamed cases, and it is not scheduled against I-09’s 2.5h and I-02’s 3.0h windows. Feasibility is asserted, not shown.
- All three SME cases are suppressed or waited; I-01 (Public Sector, complete data, large jump, good ARR) is also waited. The fairness section never audits this portfolio. The starvation risk is described in the abstract and then enacted without comment.
- Public-sector / regulatory non-monetary obligations are mostly reduced to the reserve preference. I-01 is treated as a smaller euro case, not as a possible non-substitutable duty.

**Smaller defects**
- Section 1’s utility formula is missing operators and is not a procedure a duty manager could run without re-deriving it.
- Optimistic ARR points (0.24 / 0.19 / 0.27) are reasonable as upper-half midpoints but are not labeled that way in the table.
- Adverse spillover (€8k–€16k) is a free scale-up, not derived from the stated 15–30 minute delay.
- “Priority score or tier” is only a coarse tier; that is allowed, but the tiers do not match the quantitative screen.

The answer would be in the high 7s or 8s if I-02 were wait/marginal, I-09 were conditional, I-04 were a timed option rather than an immediate commit, ARR were robustified for known miscalibration, and the fairness section scored the chosen book. As written, coverage is strong and the big traps are avoided, but the allocation does not faithfully implement the stated decision rule.
