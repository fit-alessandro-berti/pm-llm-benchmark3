7.2

The final write-up is a competent, format-compliant reconstruction that mostly reasons from decision sets rather than start order, and the four primary diagnoses are defensible. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several missed decision opportunities, incomplete competitor comparisons, and a few judgmental overreaches keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is solid.** The required seven headings and the section-1 schema are present. QA as FIFO plus non-preemptive appointment overlay is well supported (A03 at 08:20 vs A02/A04; A02 over waiting A04 at 08:30; A05–A08 in arrival order despite A08 being priority 1). QB is correctly refused a single stable rule; B02’s urgent preemption, B01’s 09:31 and 10:14 resumptions, B03 over B05 at 09:48, B05 over B04 at 10:00, and B04 over B06 at 10:28 are all used. QC’s early C02/C03/C04/C06 sequence is genuine SPT evidence, and C05 is treated as a deadline-risk exception rather than as proof of priority. QD’s skill matrix is handled carefully (D05 only D-F, D07 only D-X, D01/D02 in parallel). Competing-rule columns in section 2 are almost entirely numerically correct. Confidence scores, especially QB at 42, reflect ambiguity rather than rhetoric. Sections 4–6 are on-task.

**Factual/analytical gaps (not just style).**

1. **Missed high-value decision times.** When D-X becomes available at 11:20 it stays idle until D06 at 11:30. Eligible work then includes D07 (D-X only), D08 (priority 1, est 8 — would finish by 11:28, before the appointment), and D03. That idle interval is the cleanest evidence of appointment reservation in QD and is not given its own row. D-F’s idle window 11:36–12:00 is discussed in comments but is also not reconstructed as a decision: D10 (priority 1, electrical, est 12) could have finished on D-F before the 12:00 meeting.

2. **Knock-on effects not computed.** At 11:21, D05 (est 30) would have finished at 11:51, *before* D-F’s meeting, if started instead of D03. Choosing D03 is what made D05 unable to finish before 12:00. On QC at 11:40, starting C07 first would finish C07 at 11:58 (meet 12:00) and C01 at 12:34 (meet 13:00); starting C01 causes C07 to miss. The write-up says “all other rules predict C07” but never shows that the observed choice is strictly dominated on both deadlines.

3. **Preemption evidence underused on QA.** A04 (priority 1) arrives at 08:13 while A01 (priority 3) is running and is not preempted. A08 likewise never preempts. That is independent evidence against preemptive priority, distinct from the 08:30 selection of A02 over A04, and is not used. The 08:30–08:32 and 09:14–09:16 two-minute gaps sit exactly on the stated scan-delay bound and are only weakly discussed.

4. **Competing deadline rules are thin.** EDD appears in QC comments; least slack / remaining-time-to-deadline does not. Least slack would have picked C03 at 10:10 and C07 at 11:34, so it is actually a useful rejected alternative. Remaining times for B01 are treated as `est_min` minus elapsed without labeling that as an inference from a possibly noisy original estimate.

5. **Section 7 overclaims identifiability.** QA has almost no unexplained residual; QD still has the D10/D-F idle anomaly. Calling QD “most identifiable” is arguable if the claim is “skill constraints dominate,” but as a *discipline* inference QA is cleaner. QB’s primary cell also dumps the whole hybrid into `primary_discipline`, overlapping the secondary column.

**Uncertainty and equivalence.** These are relative strengths: FIFO vs appointment (QA 09:20), FIFO vs SPT (QA 09:32, QD 11:21), skill vs priority (QD 11:25/11:30), and the explicit 09:48 vs 10:00 split on QB. The answer does not treat every FIFO inversion as priority. That is why this is still a good answer rather than a mediocre one.

**Net.** Core queue labels are right, tables are mostly accurate, format is followed, and uncertainty is not fake-precise. The penalty is for incomplete decision-set coverage, missing quantitative dominance arguments, and a slightly wrong “most identifiable” call — the difference between a strong reconstruction and a near-complete one.
