**6.4**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly does the job the prompt asked for: mix-and-disruption scenarios, bottleneck migration, a standing package plus triggered policies, a full trade-off table, self-inflicted residual risk, and a leading/lagging dashboard. It also respects the hard constraints better than a typical “add staff and prioritize everything” answer. It is not close to flawless.

**What works**

Sections 2 and 4 are the strongest parts. Peak utilizations, the Hold→Medical edge (18h→48h), the Urgent bypass drop (74%→58%), M2’s repeat clustering, and the TV/MV feed relationship are used as real evidence, not decoration. Conflict hints are applied rather than restated: I1 is narrowed so Complex-panel is not starved with Routine; I2 is rejected in favor of typed batch sizes; I3 is treated as dangerous under machine failure; I5 is gated on TV queue; I10 is refused during courier disruption. Triggers and off-switches are specific enough to be operational. Residual risk includes harms created by the redesign (partner traceability, auto-reg metadata drift, silent WIP rejection). The dashboard is small and mostly actionable.

**Material errors and logic breaks**

1. **S1 is internally inconsistent.** Calling the winter wave “Volume +0%” while importing the historical peak mix (Urgent 18→29%, Complex 13→20%) mixes two different experiments. The logs are not mix-only: ~2,300 cases/week normal vs ~3,300/week peak. A clean design would either hold volume and change mix, or name the historical peak as volume-plus-mix. As written, later claims about “per-case intensity” sit on a false isolation.

2. **R6 reverses I4 and then makes the policy inert at the only time it matters.** I4 is “cross-train two *technical validators* to do *accession correction*” because accession has two people and a 14h peak queue. The package trains *accession* staff into TV, then keeps them on accession during peak. Peak TV stays at 101%; accession is unchanged; the 4-week training spend does nothing in S1/S3/S5. “Invert the trigger” is not the same as inverting the skill direction. This is a central flaw in the “robust” package, not a wording slip.

3. **The WIP-cap formula is confused.** “1.5× service rate × service time” collapses toward a constant (~1.5 pieces of WIP on a single server) and is not a Little’s-Law cap. The *idea* of typed WIP limits is sound; the stated rule is not implementable as written. Claiming “next bottleneck risk: none if caps well-set” also violates the prompt’s requirement to name the station that gets overloaded. Caps create upstream blocking and admission delay; that is the trade-off.

4. **P-S4 claims “next bottleneck: none.”** Moving clerks into triage and calling in pre-analytical staff dumps work onto prep/assay (already 89%/98%) and can reopen registration error/recollection loops. This is exactly the failure mode the prompt forbade papering over.

5. **Several quantities are sloppy.** Accession “420/8530 = 4.9%” uses the wrong denominator (420/9730 ≈ 4.3%). “One absence doubles load” is only true for a two-person pool; the log states that only for accession, not specialist review. Post-I1 batch-wait “~20h” and “>110%” specialist load are asserted, not derived. Completed-case Urgent p90 is listed as a *leading* indicator; without in-process age it is lagging.

**Lesser but real gaps**

R4’s failure mode is courier politics, not the downstream pre-analytical plateau that a flatter but still-high arrival stream would produce. P-S1b’s reserved M1 window is I3-lite and under-discusses Routine/repeat pressure on M2. P-S5 is an escalation slogan, not a policy. Effect cells in the table are often “same SLA / positive / neutral” where the logs would support directional magnitudes (Urgent p90 19→33h, Complex 118→210h, rework 7.5→11.8%). I9 is silently dropped rather than rejected on downstream grounds.

**Net**

This is a competent operations redesign with genuine bottleneck-migration reasoning, not average-chasing. Under a strict bar it cannot sit in the high band: one flagship robust intervention is specified backwards, the WIP rule is mathematically unclear, S1 misstates volume, and at least two rows deny a next bottleneck. Those are not cosmetic.
