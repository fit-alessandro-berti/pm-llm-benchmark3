**7.4**

The final write-up is a serious, process-aware assessment: it uses the required headings, refuses a single total ordering, applies rules at the time of commitment, does not treat ERP timestamps as conclusive against supplier acknowledgments or the signed PDF, and does not treat later approvals as curative. Classifications are mixed rather than uniformly noncompliant. That is the floor for a high score, not the ceiling. Under a strict reading, the piece is not nearly flawless.

**1. The required hierarchy table is internally reversed.**

Section 2’s columns are “What it can override” and “What it probably cannot override.” For ECD-2 those columns are used correctly (ECD can override B1/B3; it probably cannot override A1–A3). For GPIP-4 A1 and A2 the same columns are used in the opposite polarity: the cells describe what probably cannot override GPIP, not what GPIP cannot override. That is a logical error in a mandatory artifact, not a formatting nit. The issue-specific bullets under the table partly rescue the analysis, but the required table does not.

**2. INDETERMINATE is used to avoid a decision the author’s own assumptions already made.**

Assumption 7 states that Incident Command authorization is not automatically an independent budget owner under A2. On that interpretation, P-507 has a complete factual record of no Finance/budget-owner event before commitment, a live A2 trigger (EUR 110,000), no CRO exception, and only a non-controlled CFO email that the author elsewhere refuses to let waive GPIP core controls. That is a **NONCOMPLIANT** (or, if D1 is given full effect, a **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION**). It is not an evidential gap. The instructions require a primary classification under a stated interpretation; parking P-507 in INDETERMINATE hides a legal choice behind a residual-uncertainty label.

P-508 is treated more decisively on the EUR 150,000 threshold. The methodology is therefore inconsistent: the author will decide D1’s ceiling but will not decide A2’s budget-owner element, even after writing the latter down as an assumption.

**3. Burden of proof is not applied evenly.**

The author says Northbridge must prove compliance or a valid exception, and uses missing requester/committer identity to keep P-505 and P-507 indeterminate. P-501 is nevertheless classified **JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** even though Plant_Approve’s actor is unknown. If that approver was ENG-1 or SHIFT-1, A1 fails. The case also states, as a material fact, that the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate SHIFT-1. Calling lack of delegation “not fatal unless SHIFT-1 was final approver” is a generous inference, not something the burden-of-proof rule the author announced can support. P-501 should have been INDETERMINATE or NONCOMPLIANT on the author’s own standard.

**4. A few evidential overclaims.**

- P-505 lists “ERP clock offset evidence” as decisive. The file says the clock *may* have been nine minutes fast. That is a possible offset, not proven offset.
- The 12-minute recovery delay is an ON-44 fact tied to the 14 July outage. Using outage-recovery logic around the 18 July P-505 timestamps blurs a time-bounded evidence note.
- P-507’s seals are called “likely” within ECD scope. The case never states they are for the affected sterilizer, only that they are “critical” and needed within eight hours. The hedge is present, but the controlling interpretation still treats scope as established enough to build the rest of the row on.

**5. Pseudo-logic is cleaner than the case law it is supposed to encode.**

`unless(... or supported_by(CFO_email_for_finance_only))` treats the CFO email as a working defeater for A2’s Finance Controller limb. The case table then refuses to fire that defeater and drops P-507 to INDETERMINATE. The catalogue and the assessments do not implement the same rule.

**6. Smaller defects that still matter on a strict mark.**

- Thresholds in the hierarchy table lose the “≥” sense in several cells (“Purchases EUR 50,000”), which is exactly the kind of ambiguity this problem is built on.
- P-502’s local emergency-form rule (B6) is waved through as “ECD record” without a recorded emergency form in the file.
- Section 7 is serviceable but invents a 72-hour cap and a 24-hour CRO ratification device that are not derived from the sources; as a *recommendation* that is allowed, but it is presented with more certainty than the earlier caution about CRO-equivalent overlays would suggest.
- No case is classified COMPLIANT. That is allowed, but P-501 is the one purchase that can be argued as compliance *with* B4 rather than an exception *to* the regime. The label choice is defensible; the surrounding missing-actor problem is not.

**What remains strong (and why this is not a mid-score paper).**

P-502, P-503, P-504, and P-506 are correctly noncompliant on independent grounds (A3 after commitment; SoD plus missing compliance plus weak ECD scope; cafeteria/out-of-scope unapproved supplier; expired directive plus invalid verbal extension). P-508’s split from P-507 at EUR 150,000 is a real, usable distinction. Alternatives in Section 5 name the exact premise that would flip the label. Section 6 separates policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and inability to prove—this is the right typology. Post-hoc approval is never treated as a cure.

The paper fails a strict mark because the required hierarchy table is semantically broken, INDETERMINATE is used as a hedge against the author’s own A2 assumption, and burden of proof is applied to some identity gaps and not to P-501. Those are not polish issues. They are the issues the prompt told the analyst not to hide.
