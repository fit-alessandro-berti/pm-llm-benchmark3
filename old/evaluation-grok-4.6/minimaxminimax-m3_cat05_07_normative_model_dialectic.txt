**6.2**

The written briefs and case matrix are competent; the required model, several classifications, and one load-bearing consistency check are not. Under a strict reading of the rubric (dialectical fairness, model semantics, evidence traceability, scope/authority, and the quality of the model/data revision), this is a strong essay attached to a failed control-flow artifact.

**What holds up.** Sections 1–3 separate stipulated facts from authority/scope/logging questions and state an asymmetric burden of proof that is the right one: noncompliance requires an in-scope, in-force controlled rule; an exception requires contemporaneous, bounded authority. The prosecution is not a straw man (expiry, exception-normalization, SUP-703, the 14 ineligible Auto_Approve cases). The defense is not a straw man (M0 cannot express loops, identity, overlays, or label equivalence; 289/312 PD-3-eligible; 29% notification missingness). The matrix covers all 15 cases, including control-flow-fit / resource-rule fails (SUP-712, SUP-715), and the “what would reverse” column is usable. The layered architecture in §6 is the correct design: POWL for structure, DECLARE/identity for four-eyes and CoI, pandas for eligibility/amount/time, a versioned overlay registry with case-level snapshots, and explicit scope for rejected leads and reactivations. The event schema (stable `notification_id`, alias table, policy snapshot, immutable timestamps) matches the data defects in the file.

**Fatal model-semantics failures (§7 is a required deliverable).** The revised POWL does not implement the routes the prompt demanded.

1. `checks` forces `Quality_Review` *before* the XOR. PD-3 and FD-7 both allow quality *after* provisional activation. Comments claim the auto route encodes delayed quality; `auto_branch` is only `Auto_Approve → Activate_Supplier`. Quality never sits after auto-activation. The emergency branch adds a second `quality` *and* still inherits the pre-decision `checks`, so emergency activation cannot precede quality. That is not an approximation; it is the opposite of the overlay semantics.

2. Rejection is not a child of the decision. The XOR is `{standard, auto, emergency}`, all of which contain `Activate_Supplier`. `Reject_Supplier` appears only in `terminal_choice` *after* the loop. The model therefore requires an approval/activation path before it can reject. SUP-709 is inexpressible. Withdrawal has the same problem.

3. `LOOP(checks→decision, Request_More_Info)` re-executes sanctions, quality, *and* activation on every redo. That is not a defensible stand-in for SUP-707’s pre-decision info cycle. The prompt allowed an awkward LOOP only if the limitation was stated; the unsafe re-activation is not disclosed.

4. The same `Transition` objects (`quality`, `activate`, `notify`, `close`, `sanctions`, `standard_branch`) are planted in multiple parents. That is not a valid POWL. `M1` is constructed twice; the first graph is dead. Reactivation and pre-screen are separate disconnected objects, and pre-screen’s accept and reject both fall into `Close_Intake`.

So of the seven mandated behaviors, standard-route concurrency and notify-before-close are real; auto and emergency are mis-ordered; the info loop is unsafe; rejection is structurally wrong.

**Logic and taxonomy errors.** Section 1 says a lingering factual emergency is *not* authority. Section 5 then marks SUP-705 `MIXED` with dominant `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. That is the split-the-difference the prompt forbade, and it contradicts the brief’s own burden rule. `NONCOMPLIANT (pending)` is not in the allowed label set. SUP-708’s facts (activation 40 minutes before sanctions, no overlay) support `NONCOMPLIANT` with a confidence discount, not a private fourth status. The prosecution brief calls SUP-706 a resource/identity CS-9 breach; it is a notification/logging problem. SUP-715 is a missing CoI *disclosure*, not a prohibited relationship; “High” severity is overclaimed next to SUP-703.

**What this is not.** It is not empty, one-sided, or a mechanical 50/50. System-level “operations are materially noncompliant *and* M0 is obsolete” is the right synthesis, and the rejections of each side in §5 are mostly pointed. Those virtues are why this is not a 4–5. They do not rescue a non-executable-as-specified model, a self-contradiction on the hardest authority case, and off-menu labels. Nearly flawless was the bar for a high score. This is not that.
