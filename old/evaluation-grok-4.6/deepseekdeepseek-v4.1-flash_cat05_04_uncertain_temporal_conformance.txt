**7.8**

The final write-up is a strong interval analysis: offset direction, resolution-before-correction, DST fold filtering, inferred starts, and certain/possible distinctions are mostly handled correctly, and the SLA/profile numbers I checked (T-401–T-409 durations, zeta bands, +2/+5 minute thresholds) are internally consistent. It still falls well short of “nearly flawless” under a strict reading.

**What is right**
- Clock model `true = recorded − offset`, floor/round semantics, and HUB stacking of ±0.5 s with ±1 s are applied consistently.
- T-405 is the hardest case and is done well: two folds, ingestion order eliminating same-fold and reverse-fold triples, residual minute-scale uncertainty kept (not collapsed to a point).
- T-404 keeps the full 18–25 minute duration plus HUB error; T-406 states the entry-delay assumption and the record-only alternative; T-407 does not treat `Alert_Sent` as `Alert_Received`.
- Baseline vs sensitivity is separated; several classifications correctly flip on a few seconds (T-404 S2, T-408 I–C at ζ=2, T-403 A–I at ζ=3).
- Headings match the required template.

**Deductions (strict)**
- **Section 5 item 6 is not actually done.** The code calls `format_dataframe` / `conformance_temporal_profile` but never compares those midpoint diagnostics to the interval conclusions (e.g. T-401 S1: midpoint can pass 15 minutes while the interval is a possible violation). That was an explicit requirement.
- **The executable block is incomplete relative to the spec:** it only prints T-401, does not classify every case-rule pair, leaves T-404 `Investigation_Complete` in the midpoint log, and includes a no-op activity rewrite. DST filtering uses full-interval precedence (`end ≤ next.start`) rather than existence of an ordered triple; it happens to agree here, but it is the wrong constraint.
- **Notation error:** “EDT = UTC4” in §1 (others are written `UTC+2` / `UTC+1`). Conversions in the table are UTC−4, so this is a real documentation defect, not a math error.
- **INDETERMINATE is unused.** T-407 S1 depends on an unlogged causal assumption and an unacknowledged receipt; labeling that only as PV (medium confidence) is defensible, but the spec asked for INDETERMINATE when evidence is too weak. T-409’s weak NTP bound is treated the same way.
- **Small formal slips:** half-open floor intervals are treated as closed in duration arithmetic (classifications do not change); T-409 causal order is applied to S1 but the I–C table still uses the unconstrained I window; §3 labels concatenate pair names (`AlertInvestigation`), which hurts auditability.

The interval core is high quality. Missing comparison, incomplete PM4Py exercise, and the remaining semantic/documentation issues keep it clearly below a 9.
