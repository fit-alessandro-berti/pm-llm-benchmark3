**6.5**

The six-section write-up is methodologically on-target: it uses `max` rather than a sum at the AND-join, separates queue wait from service, respects chemistry/QA/micro staffing calendars, keeps path bands overlapping, and treats the temperature-excursion flag as a dependence shock rather than an independent coin-flip. That is the right skeleton. It is not close to flawless.

**Tail calibration is wrong, and the sections disagree with each other.** Lab investigation is specified as 3–7 *calendar* days after the join (Monday/Tuesday), so a P90-like window should sit near **~140–250 h** (Thu 24 Sep through about Mon 28 / Tue 29 Sep). The forecast instead gives **200–320 h** (Sat 26 night to Thu 1 Oct). The upper bound overruns the 7-day lab model by several days; the lower bound skips the 3–4 day lab cases that would finish Thursday–Friday. Section 2’s own lab-investigation central range (Thu 24 12:00–Mon 28 12:00) is more defensible than Section 4’s P90, which is an internal contradiction. P80-like **120–170 h** (Wed afternoon–Fri evening) is also shifted late and too wide: a large share of query and short documentation-investigation paths finish Tuesday, so Wednesday 15:30 is closer to a mid-delay outcome than to the start of an 80th-percentile band.

**Several scenario clocks are late relative to the working-hour arithmetic the answer itself sets up.** Documentation-only investigation adds 8–20 QA working hours (QA is 12 h on weekdays). From a typical Monday afternoon join, 8 h is Tuesday morning/afternoon and 20 h is Wednesday morning, *then* residual QA service. Central **Wed 12:00–Fri 18:00** is 1–2 days too late. QA-query central stretching to Wednesday 08:00 is likewise a tail, not a centre (4–12 working hours from Monday afternoon is mostly Tuesday). Smooth-path QA tail stopping at Tuesday 08:00 is the opposite error: overdispersed arrivals and a P80 service of 3.5 h with several jobs ahead can spill well into Tuesday.

**The smooth-path “dominant clock” is framed too narrowly, and it collides with later claims.** Weekend closure is the bulk of remaining *elapsed* time (~48–65 h of calendar idle before any Monday join). Microbiology staffing is what prevents a routine Sunday readout; chemistry FIFO then often wins the Monday `max()`. Calling the smooth dominant clock “chemistry FIFO spillover and QA queue” buries that floor. The same answer then ranks “weekend on-call authorization and contamination” as uncertainty driver #2, while Section 6 says on-call “mainly matters if chemistry is already complete.” Those three statements cannot all be right. On-call is a weak secondary trigger *by the answer’s own join logic*; chemistry start/discipline or the primary micro readout would move the forecast more.

**Smaller but real gaps.** Service times of the four samples *ahead* of B-417 are never given; Monday FIFO waves silently assume B-417-like (~2.5–2.8 h) jobs. Risk-priority chemistry completion **09:00–10:00** is a tight slice of the residual-work range. P50-like **72–96 h** has an optimistic lower edge (Monday 15:30 is more like the smooth mass, ~P30–P45, if smooth is only 30–45%). Sensitivity text that weekend/overtime moves completion “0.5–1 day” earlier is sloppy: QA is still closed Sunday, so Friday chemistry overtime does not buy a calendar day.

The piece would sit in the high 7s if the quantile bands matched the scenario clocks and the on-call/micro/chemistry story were consistent. Under a strict reading, those are not nits: they are the required forecast outputs and the required causal story.
