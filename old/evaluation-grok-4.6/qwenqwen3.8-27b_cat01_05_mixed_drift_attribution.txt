**What fails a strict reading**

For a required reproducibility section, that is a real defect, not a nit.

2. **Quantitative labels do not match the numbers.** “Jan–Feb to Jul–Aug, median +13.1h” uses July 44.2h, not the Jul–Aug average (~44.6h). “Oct–Nov, p90 +53h” is November-only (121−68); the two-month average is about +50.5h. Small, but the prompt asked not to manufacture tidy figures the aggregates do not support.

3. **Cycle-time shares are asserted, not derived.** The 20–35 / 25–40 / 5–10 / 35–50 split is only loosely tied to “Assess +0.6h” and doubled rework rates. No hours-per-rework or mix-adjusted residual is shown. Ranges plus a non-additivity caveat help, but this is still the weakest quantitative claim.

October is the second logging change point mainly because senior assessment becomes visible; July’s peak in missing resources and duplicate `Open Case` is the more direct logging/provenance candidate and is underused. Timeline “drift dimension” sometimes blends performance with organizational/resource drift instead of keeping those labels distinct.
