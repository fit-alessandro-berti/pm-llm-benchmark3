**6.3**

The write-up hits the required seven-heading scaffold and the main analytical constraints (no default verdict, not averages-only, East treated differently from North/South, fairness and learning present, no full rollout). That completeness is the main reason it is not a low score. It is far from nearly flawless.

**What works.** The mechanism table does connect structure to observable measures and covers the mandated items (nominal handover reduction, central queues, utilization, rework, local expertise, role clarity, absence resilience, fairness). The three scenarios are parallel and include early-warning signs. The hidden-risks section is the strongest part: tail latency, high-complexity concentration, local-knowledge erosion, fairness, skill atrophy, accountability diffusion, and rigid exception handling are all named and tied to log fields. The recommendation is appropriately conditional and correctly warns against reading early mean cycle time as success.

**Material flaws.**

1. **PM4Py sketch is technically wrong**, which is costly in a section whose job is PM4Py. `pm4py.format_dataframe` converts a raw DataFrame into an event log; it does not pretty-print networks, DFGs, roles, or service-time tables. Applying it to `discover_handover_of_work_network`, `discover_working_together_network`, `discover_subcontracting_network`, performance DFGs, and role objects is a factual API error. The sketch invents `get_p90_cycle_time`, `get_rework_rate`, and `get_attribute_value_frequency` inside the `pm4py` namespace without marking them as helpers. It analyzes one log rather than a before/after comparison, which the prompt required. It never filters on `customer_segment` and never operationalizes SLA misses, both explicitly requested. `filter_variants` is unused; variant/performance-DFG comparison stays decorative.

2. **The pilot does not test the intervention it claims to evaluate.** Sending only East into a “Technical Shared Service” while North and South stay local cannot produce the pooling/utilization effect management is selling. You are not merging N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech; you are relocating an already-overloaded East specialist into a queue that still shares Legal/Risk. The 15% utilization claim is therefore not identified. East is also the smallest book (520 vs 820/760), so even a clean East result would not identify North/South expertise loss. Guardrails (12% SLA, 12% rework) sit on top of East’s current 9.8% / 11.5% without saying whether they are relative or absolute, so they can fire immediately or never.

3. **Given numbers are underused.** The current handover table, team P90s, and management’s own expected waits (Technical SS 4.0h, approval 3.0h, Legal/Risk 10.5h, frequency up from 9 to 12) are not systematically stress-tested. Legal/Risk already sit at 93%/91% utilization; the design *increases* their inflow and dumps tech, legal, risk, and hard QC into one shared queue. That heterogeneous-server / priority-inversion problem is mentioned in passing, not modeled. Mixing those work types is a first-order queueing issue, not a footnote.

4. **Causal and measurement sloppiness.** Optimistic “early warning signs: None” is empty. Several scenario percentages (wait 5.5h, rework +25%, similarity <0.4) are free parameters. `local_expertise_flag` is treated as if it will automatically fall after centralization; that depends on how the flag is defined. Role-clarity “index” and “30% overlap” are unspecified. Cross-team cover’s existing 18% post-handover rework—the best current evidence on resilience—is barely used.

5. **Mechanism map is a list of arrows, not a map.** Missing are feedback loops that the narrative itself implies: informal local advice suppressing referrals; skill atrophy increasing future referrals; shared approval reducing ownership and raising rework; measurement of means hiding tail/fairness effects and producing false green lights.

A rigorous answer would have derived scenario waits from the given frequencies and utilizations, designed a pilot that actually pools at least two teams (or a specialty slice) with a holdout, and written a before/after PM4Py comparison that uses only the listed APIs correctly. This is a competent memo with a broken methods appendix and a pilot that cannot identify the main claimed benefit.
