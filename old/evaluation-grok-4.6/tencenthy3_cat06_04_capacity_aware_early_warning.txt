**6.3**

The final six-section policy is structurally complete and hits the main quality gates: I-03 is refused on stage, not rank; I-05 is suppressed while I-07 is re-opened; effects are not equated with `p_now`; and untreated-queue harm appears in the narrative and in the scenario table. Under a strict decision-theoretic reading, it is still not a high-scoring answer. The rule, the allocation, and the impact arithmetic do not implement the same model.

**Decision rule.** Eligibility-by-stage, material-change persistence, and an EV-style score are the right ingredients. The robustness layer is mostly invented constants presented as if derived: a 30% Strategic Enterprise effect haircut, ±50% Non-Native widening, a 0.10 Δp gate, a 0.50 / 4h first-alert rule, a 0.85 escalation bar, and a **€500 spillover allowance**. That last term is the serious error. Section 3 later prices two slots of spillover at about €2.4k–€7.2k. A rule that charges €500 per case will accept interventions the impact model says can be net-negative. Probability shifts (+0.065 / −0.085) are also largely inert: ranking is by adjusted RR × value, so SME/Strategic calibration barely changes today’s slate except through ad hoc thresholds. The 30% RR discount is a separate causal claim from “the model overpredicts `p`,” and is not justified.

**Allocation.** I-02 and I-09 are defensible on stage urgency (Diagnosis Started, 2.5–3 h). Waiting on I-04, I-06, I-08, and I-10 is operationally plausible. Several choices are still weakly specified. I-04 is the highest mid-EV case in the book and is parked as “wait” with no conversion trigger (what Δp, stage move, or clock turns wait into a slot). I-07 is treated differently from I-05, which is required, but “wait/monitor” with **1.5 h to SLA while still in Triage** is not a deployable clock; there is no 12:30 re-decision. Using only two of four slots is coherent conservatism only if idle-slot expiry and the two-specialist constraint are analyzed; they are not. Selecting I-02 (Strategic, overpredicted, lower-bound RR 0.08—the case later zeroed in Adverse) over well-calibrated high-RR I-01 is never stress-tested as a robustness choice. I-05 listed as “eligible but suppressed” blurs the eligibility filter. I-10’s main uncertainty is not “none major.”

**Impact table.** Optimistic / Base / Adverse exist and the Base identity 8550 − 2300 − 4800 = +1450 is auditable. That is not enough. The table uses **raw** RR ranges, ignoring the 30% Strategic haircut and the Non-Native widening that supposedly drove selection, so reported impact is not the policy’s own expected value. Spillover is monetized with an unstated €20k average on six anonymous cases; the 15–30 minute Adverse delay is never used. Breach-avoided ranges mix lower-bound sums, midpoints, and “I-02 fails” in a way that is hard to audit. False-alarm burden is treated as a certain intervention cost, which matches the prompt’s sketch but is not flagged as conservative. Non-monetary duties (especially Public Sector) are almost absent here.

**Repeated-alert policy.** Cool-down, suppression, escalation, and re-entry are named, and the I-05 / I-07 contrast is explicit. “Materially new event” is not operationalized (who certifies it, against what log). Escalation at 0.85 leaves calibrated SME I-07 (~0.79 after the stated shift) outside the safety valve. The 14:30 release of the reserve is a concrete operational touch; it is also slightly misaligned with a possible arrival “before 15:00.”

**Fairness.** All five required risks are mentioned and paired with named safeguards. The safeguards fight the rule. A fairness floor at adjusted `p` > 0.80 is arbitrary, just misses I-07, and is not reconciled with the spillover test. Non-Native ±50% bounds are **not applied** to I-09, the one Non-Native case they fund. Label confounding is handled with slogans (“intervention flag,” “deconfounded learning”) without an identification plan given value-based selection and thin overlap. Public-sector obligation is not treated as a constraint on starving I-01.

**Monitoring.** The five monitoring families are present, which is necessary but not sufficient. Triggers often fire only if known miscalibration gets *worse* than the ranges already given. Causal estimation (shadow RCT in 4–6 weeks) ignores positivity: the policy itself withholds swarms from low-value and late-stage cases. There is no rule for dropping the regulatory reserve when arrival evidence is weak, and no intra-day stop rule if today’s Adverse congestion materializes.

The piece reads like a competent operations memo that already knows the grading checklist. It is not a coherent uncertain-decision policy: the object being optimized in Section 1 is not the object being scored in Section 3, several binding clocks are missing, and robustness adjustments are stated then discarded when they would change the portfolio.
