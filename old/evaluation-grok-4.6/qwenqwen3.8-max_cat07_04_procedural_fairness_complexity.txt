**7.1**

The answer is complete against the required outline, respects every hard constraint, and is generally well calibrated. It still falls well short of “nearly flawless” because of technical errors, some overclaim, and avoidable sloppiness.

**What it does well**
- Uses the eight required H1s and covers all listed fairness dimensions, not just approval rates.
- Correctly treats raw outcome gaps as non-diagnostic, refuses to launder residual gaps as “just case mix,” and does not treat `risk_score_band` as neutral.
- Distinguishes total-effect vs mediator-controlled estimands (do not condition away language support / accommodation / document requests if those are part of the burden). That is the right causal point.
- Uses the given variant, wait, and staffing numbers accurately and maps them to justified vs concerning disparities.
- Mechanisms, limitations, and mitigations all include the required items, and mitigations carry real trade-offs. It never proposes deleting rights-preserving steps.
- Section 8 is appropriately hedged: mixed process, credible procedural burden, not proven discrimination.

**Material problems**
- The PM4Py plan would not support the assessment as written. `get_all_case_durations` is aligned to `unique()` case IDs, which is unordered and would scramble duration comparisons. DataFrame methods are mixed with APIs that often return EventLogs. `discover_log_skeleton`, `discover_declare`, organizational roles, and resource similarity are called and then unused.
- The fraud-routing check is specified as “Fraud Review after Risk Screening” but implemented as “Fraud Review before Eligibility Assessment.” That is the wrong constraint.
- Sensitivity analysis stops at dropping columns. There is no model, no estimator, no uncertainty, and invented label values (`minority_language`, `low/medium/high`).
- Causal language sometimes outruns the tables. Long waits are *consistent with* bilingual/specialist scarcity; they are not identified as caused by it. Within-variant, same-need comparisons are proposed but then treated as if already shown.
- Sections 1–4 restate the same group facts several times instead of adding a crisp “can infer / cannot infer” inventory, which the prompt asked for.

A strong policy-process memo with a broken executable plan and a few inferential overreaches. Not a 5, and nowhere near a 9.
