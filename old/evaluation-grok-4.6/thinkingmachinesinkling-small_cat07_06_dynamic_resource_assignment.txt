The write-up is complete, correctly headed, and does the socio-technical work the prompt asked for. It still has a hard constraint break, several skill/capacity errors, and a broken PM4Py sketch. That is well short of “nearly flawless.”

**What holds up**

Sections 1–2 separate safety rules from preferences and cover every required scoring block (eligibility, SLA, skill, continuity, workload/OT, training, resilience, ties/exceptions). The answer does not pretend there is a single optimum. Section 4’s comparison is the right shape: first-available wins waiting and loses mismatch/handovers; specialist-only wins immediate quality and creates Security/Approval queues. Section 5 is usable with workers. Section 6 lists every required audit indicator and ties them to log fields.

Most of the queue is defensible: C002/C011 stay with R6; C007 stays with R3 (reassignment 3, high handover, complexity 0.81); C016 stays with R5 (skill 2, high handover); Security stays on skill-3 Risk staff; Approval stays on skill 2–3; R4/R9 are largely protected from extra load.

**Material failures**

1. **C012 violates a constraint the answer itself marked hard.** R2 is overtime-risk *high*, the case is *low* priority with 48h left, and R10 already owns it (Quality 3, 4 open cases, 6.0h, OT low). Constraint 4 / C6 allows high-OT load only when SLA is urgent *and* no safer alternative exists. Neither condition holds. Calling this “training” does not repair it: training is allowed only if SLA *and* the overtime rule still hold. This is a direct stated-policy vs. table contradiction.

2. **C015’s training note is wrong.** R5 and R10 have Security = 0. Skill 0 is not allowed; supervision is only for skill 1–2 under R7/R8. Suggesting R8 can supervise R5/R10 on Security is a skill-matrix error.

3. **Hours remaining are almost unused, then the batch overloads the “good” people.** R3 gets C003 + C005 + C007 with 6.0h left, including a high-complexity rework on a 6h high-priority SLA. R6 gets two urgents (2h approval, 4h VIP negotiation) plus C010. R8 gets urgent Security plus two more cases on 5.5h. The policy scores open-case count far more than hours left, so the table is not actually feasible as a same-day plan. That is a core design hole, not a rounding issue.

4. **Ownership vs. performer is muddled.** C001/C005/C012 invent “retain owner, other person performs” without saying who is `org:resource`, who owns SLA, or how that is logged. For Security that can be made legal (R8 performs). For auditability it is sloppy.

- Eligibility filter includes `skill >= 0`, which admits “not allowed.”
- C005 (high, customer waiting) to a Negotiation-1 trainee is *allowed* with supervision, but it is an aggressive use of the exception and stacks on R3’s other load.

**Net**

This is a competent consulting-style design with the right headings and real trade-off language. Under a strict reading it is not a correct implementation of its own rules, and the current-queue table is the place that had to be right. A high score would need C012 (and any other OT/skill-0 slip) fixed, hours treated as a first-class capacity constraint, a clean performer/owner/supervisor model, and a PM4Py sketch that actually calls the APIs as specified.
