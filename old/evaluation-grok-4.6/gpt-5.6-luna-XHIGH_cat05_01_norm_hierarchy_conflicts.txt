**6.8**

The submission is a serious, well-structured compliance analysis. It uses the required headings, keeps an issue-specific hierarchy, refuses a single total ordering, and avoids the usual collapse-errors: newest document wins, all emergencies are compliant, post-hoc approval cures, ERP time is conclusive. Most primary classifications are defensible. Under a strict reading it is still not close to flawless.

**1. Timing discipline is uneven, including one direct miss of a stated quality constraint.**

The task requires rules to be applied at the time of the act. D1 is an ordinary email sent **2026-07-15 07:20**. P-502 was committed **2026-07-14 10:15**. The answer still says D1 “may address the separate Finance workflow” for P-502. That is retrospective use of a later, uncontrolled Finance interpretation. Even a charitable “clarification relates back” theory is never argued. Classification stays NONCOMPLIANT because A3 is independently fatal, so the outcome is lucky rather than earned. The same memo is temporally competent for P-503/P-505/P-507/P-508; the failure is the silent back-dating, not the existence of D1.

A smaller temporal slip: pseudo-logic line 8 uses `action_time < expiry`. ECD-2 runs *through* 18:00, which is inclusive. It does not change P-505, but it is sloppy given that case turns on minutes.

**2. Evidential use of ON-44 is inverted.**

ON-44 is a scoped outage: ERP down **14 July 08:30–13:00**, recovery stamps up to 12 minutes late. It is an evidential rule, not a standing reason to distrust ERP.

The answer applies it where it does **not** operate and under-uses it where it does:

- **P-505 (18 July)** is justified with “especially with ON-44 and the stated fast clock.” The 9-minute-fast clock is in the case file; ON-44 is not. That is a factual error. The acknowledgment at 17:59:31 can still support a pre-expiry commitment without the outage.
- **P-501** is the outage case. Request 08:42 and commit 08:50 sit *inside* ON-44, with no call recording or supplier acknowledgment. The answer treats those ERP times as hard facts and never walks the “ERP ≥ real time ⇒ still before 09:00” inference that would actually lock ECD-2 out. P-502’s 12:40/12:55 recovery entries get the same silence.

That is a real evidential-discipline failure, not a wording quibble.

**3. Classification method is internally inconsistent.**

Section 1 puts unresolved role/scope/timing questions in INDETERMINATE and confirmed breaches in NONCOMPLIANT. That is a coherent rule. It is not applied evenly.

- **P-501 → JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION** despite unknown Plant/Finance identities, unknown final-business-approver, and SHIFT-1 acting with a reachable, non-delegating Plant Manager. If missing role evidence is enough to block a clean finding elsewhere, P-501 should have been INDETERMINATE (or NONCOMPLIANT if B2 is treated as a gate). JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION overstates what was actually proved. The B4 reading on PO-before-commit is fair; using it as the *case* label is not.
- **P-505 / P-507 → INDETERMINATE** on the A2 budget-owner limb, even though the file already says Incident Command’s charter does **not** make members budget owners, and D3/D4 deny a Compliance/SoD waiver. Under the answer’s own burden-of-proof sentence, unproved pre-commitment A2 is closer to NONCOMPLIANT than to a tie. INDETERMINATE is available, but then P-501 cannot be a justified exception on thinner role evidence.
- **P-506** is correctly NONCOMPLIANT, but the justification says later Plant *and Finance* approvals do not cure “PO/A2 timing gaps.” Finance_Approve at 06:05 **precedes** commit at 06:20. The live A2 defect is the missing independent budget owner, not late Finance. That is a concrete misstatement of the record.

No primary label is obviously indefensible. The problem is unstable thresholds, not a wrong scorecard.

**4. Scope and authority are mostly well handled; a few provided facts are left idle.**

Strengths: GPIP as baseline; A4/A5/A6/A7 read carefully; ECD-2 confined to C1/C2/C3 and the window; C5 treated as a non-waiver signal; D1 capped at €150,000 and limited to Finance process; D2 read as additive in P-508; cafeteria and post-expiry purchases kept outside C6; oral extension rejected in P-506; late Compliance in P-502 not cured. That is the core of a defensible hierarchy.

Gaps:

- Past practice (“signed incident directives as temporary overlays, but not for new-supplier Compliance”) is supplied and almost unused. A strict analyst would say why that practice does *not* promote ECD-2 over A3, rather than leaving it in the unused-facts box.
- “Unapproved supplier” (P-504) is not the same phrase as “new supplier” (P-502 / A3). The answer asserts “no … A3 threshold” for P-504 without deciding whether register status and GPIP new-supplier status coincide. NONCOMPLIANT still holds on B1/B3, but the A3 question is ducked.
- P-503’s “Satisfied rules” cell includes “D1 could address Finance … if otherwise valid.” That is a hypothetical, not a satisfied rule.

**5. Catalogue, alternatives, remediation, and redraft are strong with residual softness.**

The rule table and 11-line defeasible logic match the assignment and do not launder conditionals into DECLARE rules. Section 5 is the best part: six real reversing premises, with a clear weak/strong call on each. Section 6 correctly splits policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and failure of proof. Section 7 hits authority, scope, thresholds, SoD, new suppliers, retrospective records, expiry, and a normalization brake.

Residual issues: B6 is repeatedly “not stated” rather than analyzed as a surviving local duty; P-505 still has no Create_PO in the file and that is under-weighted relative to the timing drama; “Medium-high” severity is undefined next to a High/Medium/Low legend; the policy redraft quietly adopts Internal Audit’s 24-hour test without saying it is a policy choice.

**Net.** This is a high-quality memo with professional structure and mostly right case outcomes. It is not near-flawless. The ON-44 inversion, silent D1 retroactivity on P-502, P-506 Finance-timing error, and JE/IND inconsistency are enough, on a strict rubric, to keep it well out of the 8–10 band.
