The answer looks expert-shaped and is better than a naïve DFG copy, but it has material process-semantic errors, a systematic miss on a required distinction, and several internal contradictions. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Required `process_enabled` distinction is unused.** All 15 predictions set `process_enabled: true`. The prompt explicitly asks to separate a currently enabled action from the next *logged* event (including in-progress work whose completion is not yet visible, and feed-delayed later events). After `Evidence Received`, another `Customer Evidence Requested` is not enabled until review completes; `Disposition Recorded` is not enabled in S4 while Regulatory Assessment is still open; `Authority Notification` is not enabled before a reportable RA outcome (and, in the DFG, not before disposition). Collapsing every forecast to `true` is not a formatting nit. It is a failed required reasoning step.

**C6-B is the clearest process error.** The case already left the Fast Close decision point: triage chose `Customer Evidence Requested` at 09:30. Fast Close is a S1 short-circuit (rule 2; DFG `Initial Triage -> Fast Close`). It is not a plausible post-evidence termination, and citing 286/972 *direct* triage-to-Fast-Close cases is exactly the undisciplined DFG use the rubric warns against. The historically and normatively appropriate non-investigation close after review is `Close — No Safety Issue` (68 DFG cases from review). Treating Fast Close as an “obligation” and putting S5 as the alternative state is also wrong: S5 is investigation-complete disposition-ready, not “review maybe done on a no-investigation file.”

**C6-E rank 3 contradicts the answer’s own evidence.** `Supplier Analysis Completed` is forecast at 0.05–0.20 while contradicting evidence says a 6–36 h supplier queue makes completion today unlikely. Receipt was 13:20; only ~40 minutes have elapsed. Mandatory *opening* of supplier analysis is correct (six same-lot complaints). Predicting its *completion* as the next log event is not. A better third candidate is a delayed/parallel `Technical Investigation Completed` after the first review, or a later-cycle evidence request after review—not a same-afternoon supplier close.

**Notification / disposition ordering is sloppy in C6-C and C6-D.** Rule 6 requires notification before *closure*, not before disposition. The DFG is `Disposition Recorded -> Authority Notification` (44). Ranking `Authority Notification` ahead of (C6-C) or instead of a coherent disposition step, and marking it enabled, ignores both the normative rule and the observational order. The 16:00 batch constraint makes this worse: C6-C’s own 5 h TI→RA median from 11:45 points to ~16:45, *after* today’s batch.

**Timing arguments are internally inconsistent.** C6-A rank 3 says TI median completion is ~18:00, then still offers `Technical Investigation Completed` as the *next* event at 14:00, in front of a review that the same write-up says is 1–5 h from a light queue. C6-C rank 1 (0.45–0.75 for RA soon) underweights a 12-item risk-prioritized queue and treats TI completion as assignment time. Those intervals are too high for the performance data the answer itself cites.

**C6-D underuses the observation model it advertises.** This is the migration-affected case with a source-system attachment and a worklist open of R-R2. The right process-aware move is to treat missing evidence-cycle completions as *plausible next log events* (rule 7), not only as a caveat. Instead, evidence never appears in obligations or in the top three. Extra non-schema keys (`migration_flag`, C6-E `note`) are minor, but they sit next to a real analytical omission.

**C6-A is the strongest case and still overreaches.** Primary S3, lot-triggered supplier obligation, and exclusion of Fast Close are correct. Rank 1 review completion is reasonable. Rank 2’s 0.15–0.40 on another evidence request applies the pooled 101/356 loop rate to a high-severity injury / multi-device / reportable-leaning file that the same answer says must go to TI, RA, *and* supplier analysis. Different lots correctly keep Supplier Analysis off the mandatory list. The technical note (“root cause plausible but not confirmed”) and the country reportability rule are barely used: they should move residual probability toward a non-clean close / possible second investigation, not toward a lightly supported same-day notification.

**C6-E reportability is dismissed too cleanly.** “No regulatory assessment required” does not follow from low severity plus no injury when six complaints share a lot. Rule 4 also covers a *possible reportability/safety signal*. That belongs in unknowns or open obligations, not a closing note.

**Uncertainty presentation is only partly disciplined.** Interval widths are appropriately coarse and midpoints are not fake-precise. The calibration note is not accurate: widths are not “0.25 in most cases,” and midpoint sums (~0.88–1.00) are not “80–90% mass.” Overlapping intervals are allowed; claiming they were designed to a coverage target they do not meet is extra noise.

**What is actually good, and why that is not enough.** Primaries for A–E are in the right region. A vs B is not treated as the same prefix. Cross-case discussion of attributes vs. control flow, DFG blindness to mandatory rare events, parallelism, and feed delay is the best section. Those strengths would support a much higher score if Fast Close were not recycled after an evidence path, if `process_enabled` were used, if E’s rank 3 did not refute itself, and if D’s missing evidence were treated as a predictive hypothesis rather than a footnote.

The submission is a coherent mid-tier expert draft with several disqualifying local failures. It is not a weak frequency dump. It is also not a carefully constrained process-aware forecast.
