**4.3**

The answer is long, structured, and conceptually aware of several intended points (K-337 is a decision-policy change; four-eyes is preserved; queues are forecasts; local gains can displace other cases). Under a strict reading of the rubric, that is not enough. The core deliverable is a *minimal-change, temporally consistent* counterfactual. On that standard the write-up repeatedly contradicts itself, invents rules, mishandles scarce capacity, and fails explicit format constraints.

**Fatal consistency errors (largest deduction).** Cycle-time claims do not add up, including inside the same case.

- **K-104:** Section 2 says “2.5–5 h faster (Tue 22:30 vs. Wed 18:30).” That interval is ~20 hours, not 2.5–5 hours. Section 3 then reports a 1.25-day best-case gain, a 1.1-day base gain, and a 0.25-day worst-case gain, while treating the factual cycle as “4 days.” Factual Register→Notify is Mon 08:10→Wed 18:40 (~2.5 calendar days). Three different magnitudes for one intervention.
- **K-219:** Section 2’s best case finishes Tue 19:00 and claims a “~2.5 day improvement.” Factual Notify is Wed 09:00. The factual cycle is ~48 hours; a 2.5-day improvement is impossible. Section 3’s best-case row ends **Wed 08:50**—essentially the factual finish—while still claiming “~1.5–2 days earlier (Tue evening instead of Wed morning).” The cells contradict the parenthetical. Same subsection: “net gain ~12.5 h” and then “expected net: 4–7 h.”
- **K-411:** Section 2 has D2 **Tue 11:30–16:00** and “1.5–2.5 h saved.” A 4.5 h document check moved from 15:00–19:30 to 11:30–16:00 is a 3.5 h document-completion shift, and cycle time is not document-completion time. Section 3 instead has D2 **Tue 15:30–20:00**, “essentially the same” as factual 19:30, then a base case “~10 min *worse*” because A2 is somehow faster on Wednesday. The two sections are different counterfactuals. The “no, wait” cell is draft residue, not analysis.
- **K-219 worst case** is chronologically broken: rework “Wed 08:00” then “Tue 18:00 rework Document Check.”

An evaluator asked for temporal consistency cannot treat this as a small editing problem. The numeric story is unstable.

**Control-flow and feasibility errors.**

- The partial order states `Identity Check → Document Check → Assessment`. That is false. Both checks precede Assessment; Identity need not precede Document. This is a first-order process error in the feasibility model.
- K-104 invents a rule that “escalation advice precedes assessment.” No such rule exists. Escalation is advisory and may overlap.
- K-337 says M1’s decision “is not dependent on C1’s final sign-off,” then later requires compliance before decision. For a high-risk/high-value case this is not a wording slip; it is a constraint violation in one paragraph and a correction in the next.
- C1 is treated as free for a Tue 06:00–08:00 preliminary screen and a Tue 20:00–22:30 final review while snapshots show **C1: 20h / 18h / 16h**. A reservation can jump the queue only by displacing others; the path still writes those C1 intervals as if they occur. The reserved Tue 08:00–12:00 block also cannot serve as the *final* review, because Assessment does not finish until 19:10. The claimed acceleration therefore depends on a *second* unexplained C1 jump that night.
- K-219’s D2 start at Tue 08:00 ignores Tue 08:00 **D2: 4 h**. Adding this case to D2 does not make D2 idle at open. Handwritten work on D2 is also not a free 5 h draw from a 4–8 h “standard” band.
- K-411’s “both start Tue 11:30” likewise ignores that creating a task is not the same as resource availability (Tue 08:00 D2: 4 h). Section 3 overcorrects into almost no gain; Section 2 under-corrects into simultaneous starts.

**Minimal-change discipline is uneven.** K-104 keeps identity/document/assessment times fixed and only moves compliance—good in spirit—then adds an unearned overnight decision slip and an available C1. K-219 moves Decision to Tuesday evening with no M1 availability evidence (factual decision is Wednesday morning). K-337’s compression to Friday is the right *kind* of story (drop the weekend-external loop), but the joint M1/C1 construction is looser than the rules allow and the cycle-length labels bounce between 3.5, 5–6, and 9→3.5 days.

**Format and instruction failures (not cosmetic).**

- Required top-level headings only; the answer adds a title, extra `###` blocks, and `## END OF ANALYSIS`.
- Section 6 must be **one** table with columns `pilot element | intervention 1 | intervention 2`. It is two tables. Rows are present, structure is not.
- Pilots must be the **two highest-ranked** interventions. Rank 1–2 are K-337 and K-104; the pilots are K-337 and **K-219** (rank 3).
- The K-219 pilot eligibility line is “Non-originals… Exclude… originals.” Case K-219 is `originals required: yes`. The pilot excludes the case it is supposed to test.
- Section 4’s 100-case write-up and Section 7 both overrun the stated word bands by a wide margin.

**Capacity math is directionally aware and locally wrong.** Mixing C1’s 20 h *stock* with 60 h of weekly reservations and 40 h of weekly capacity is a stock–flow error. D2 “60 h inflow” is both “20 h backlog” and “8–10 days” in the same paragraph. Qualitative interference (C1 reservation, D1↔D2 substitution, rework amplification, assessment bunching, weekend RTT, timestamp ≠ concurrency) is the strongest part of the paper and is still undermined by these numbers.

**Ranking fights the rubric.** Rankings must weigh quality/compliance and spillovers, not cycle time alone. K-337 is scored “high robustness” and #1 because it frees hours. It is also the only intervention that changes the allowed decision and can raise loss, appeal, and defensibility risk. Calling it the most robust, then calling K-411 the most dangerous “despite potential speed” after arguing K-411 saves ~0 h, is incoherent. K-411 is a repair of a forbidden sequentialization (Rule 3 already allows concurrency); its real risk is logging, not process law. K-337 is the intervention that is fast *and* dangerous. The synthesis says K-219 is least transportable, which is reasonable, then adds an extra “least transportable systemic assumption” the prompt did not ask for.

**What remains creditable:** explicit paths rather than slogans; acknowledgment that K-337 cannot silently ignore the gap; four-eyes A1≠C1; preliminary compliance ≠ final review; best/base/worst plus falsifiers; interference table columns as specified; eight labeled assumptions.

Those are the features of a competent draft, not a finished analysis. The contradictions on durations, the illegal Identity≺Document order, the C1-availability fiction, the wrong pilot pair/format, and the K-219 originals error are enough, under the requested strictness, to keep the score well below the middle of the scale.
