**6.2**

The answer is complete, correctly structured, and conceptually on-side: it does not treat every deviation as failure, does not equate throughput with resilience, and does not pretend post-hoc audit work is real-time compliance. Keep/modify/retire calls for A–L are mostly defensible. Those strengths are not enough for a high score under a strict reading, because the analysis repeatedly overclaims, misuses numbers, and leaves supplied evidence on the table.

**Material inaccuracies**
- Section 5 invents a “baseline” hospital-to-pharmacy fill-rate ratio of **0.35**. Baseline fill rates are not in the data. 8/23 ≈ 0.35 is the *baseline median cycle-time* ratio, not a fill-rate ratio. The target “0.95–1.05 of 0.35” is therefore meaningless. This is a category error, not a rounding issue.
- T4 is described as “near baseline.” That is true only for hospital-critical median (8h). Pharmacy median is 28h vs 23h; wholesale median is 58h vs 36h (+61%); pharmacy p90 is 74h vs 49h; wholesale p90 is 120h vs 72h. Stabilization still has a long tail and a non-critical service gap. Calling that recovered misstates the after-period.
- Several quantities are stated as if measured: G’s time “lost” at “2–3×,” remediation cut “~60%,” failover preserving “baseline-level cycle times for 70% of orders,” H “contributed to” the T4 0.5% controlled-substance gap, C “contributed to” the 9.5% exception rate. Those are attributions or inventions, not findings.

**Incomplete use of the given evidence**
- T2/T3 conformance (fitness 0.71 / 44% traces; 0.82 / 59%) is almost unused in the timeline, despite being the cleanest “during” quality signal.
- DFG frequencies are thinly used. The T2 split is the core abductive evidence: skip-auth 1,600 vs manual auth 2,500 vs fast lane 1,000; manual pack → dispatch-without-scan 1,800 vs temporary release 1,650; phone PoD 2,600. The table talks about patterns in the abstract and barely uses volumes.
- Specified move-on-log / move-on-model deviations and the three temporal-profile deviations are only partly absorbed. Spreadsheet invoice hold is essentially dropped. Variable delay on dispatch-without-scan → phone PoD is not used.
- T3 (systems back, scanning/sync unreliable) is the highest-leverage mixed-mode window for stale snapshots and reconstructed scans. It is summarized, then under-analyzed.
- Recall-sensitive product is in the process context and almost absent. The hospital-critical × controlled-substance cell is not treated as a distinct control problem.

**Logical and clarity issues**
- Pattern I is classed as both a risky shortcut and an ambiguous case. That can be nuanced; here it is just inconsistent.
- “Evidence of resilience” for F leans on the prompt’s gloss that F “reduced controlled-substance errors,” while the phase rate is 2.9% vs 0.2% baseline. Without a no-F counterfactual, that is circular.
- A’s hidden-risk cell (“authorization gap for non-ambient/non-controlled products”) does not match the stated failure mode: A is a *timing* bypass of electronic authorization for hospital-critical work, not a product-class gap.
- Recovery-model thresholds (15 min, 95% availability, 70% capacity, 4h decay, 10-point scores, 48h WMS, etc.) are a legitimate design proposal, but they are not derived from the log and are written with the same certainty as the metrics.
- Sloppiness: “Hospitalcritical” concatenation; “triary matrix” in recommendation 7.

**What still works**
Sections 3 and 7 have the right decision logic: A/B/F/J as bounded resilience; C/G/E/H as control disappearance; K/L as hidden post-recovery load, not proof of compliance. The recovery model includes the required ingredients (trigger, triage, minima by risk, evidence capture, backlog rules, reconciliation/audit flow). Recommendations are prioritized and decision-usable.

The piece reads like a strong first draft of an advisory memo, not a nearly flawless reading of this log. Fabricated ratios, overstated T4 recovery, and weak DFG/conformance grounding are enough to keep it well below the top of the scale.
