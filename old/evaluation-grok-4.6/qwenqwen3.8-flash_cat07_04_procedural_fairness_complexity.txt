**7.2**

The final write-up is complete, on-format, and constraint-compliant, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has several substantive analytical slips, a weaker PM4Py plan than the surrounding prose, and a few interpretation gaps that matter for a fairness assessment.

**What it gets right.** The eight required headings are present and used for the intended content. Raw approval gaps are not treated as discrimination. Case-mix differences are not used to wave away remaining process burden. `risk_score_band` is treated as potentially contaminated. Rights-preserving steps are analyzed as sources of delay and handover, not as noise to be removed. Mitigations keep language support and accommodation review in place. The bottom line is appropriately calibrated: concerning procedural disparities, not a finding of intentional discrimination.

**Main analytical problems.**

1. **Unadjusted waits are treated too much like residual group effects.** The waiting-time table is descriptive. Group B’s longer Submit → Identity Verification wait (1.4d vs 0.8d) and Supervisor Review wait (1.9d vs 1.0d) can be explained in large part by in-person share (38% vs 9%) and by backlog in two regions. The preliminary model controls for channel and region on some outcomes, but the path section still presents these waits as Group B/C disparities. The language-support and accommodation queues *are* group-specific and should have been separated more sharply from channel/region mix.

2. **Support-need coverage versus variant share is missed.** Case mix says 57% of Group B needs language support, but V4 is only 24%. Group C has 62% accommodation requests, but V5 is only 31%. That is one of the better available tests of routing consistency and accessibility: either support is absorbed into other variants, or many requested supports never appear as a dedicated path. Section 1 lists consistency of routing and quality/accessibility of support, then does not use this gap.

3. **The given “adjusted” estimates still include language and accommodation.** The preliminary model does not control for `language_preference` or `disability_accommodation_requested`. Nested models in section 2 acknowledge this, but sections 3 and 8 sometimes read +8.0 pp supervisor review and +9.5 pp document requests as extra scrutiny after complexity is held fixed. Those coefficients can still be the uncontrolled support process itself. That does not make them irrelevant to fairness, but it changes what they identify.

**PM4Py plan.** The function coverage is aligned with the prompt, but the code/pseudocode is not a clean analysis plan. `case_attrs` never creates `cycle_time`, `supervisor_review`, or `request_missing_documents`, yet the regressions use them. `case_outcome` is used as a logit outcome without binarization. Group filters use `"A"`/`"B"` while the scenario labels are `"Group A"`/`"Group B"`. Language-preference values are invented. `get_all_case_durations` is stored and not joined to groups. Handover conversion assumes a NetworkX edge API. Matching and waiting-time decomposition are described in section 2 and only weakly implemented in section 7.

**Smaller issues.** “Instrumenting” risk score is named with no instrument. Several handover phrases lose the direction markers, which makes the resource story harder to read. Daily workload limits are discussed well; queue length and applicant-versus-internal wait are discussed, then not operationalized beyond a pandas shift.

The piece would be in the high 8s or low 9s if the wait-time claims were explicitly unadjusted, the 57%/24% and 62%/31% coverage gaps were used, the adjusted-model controls were not over-read, and the PM4Py block constructed the case-level features it later models. Those are not cosmetic nits; they affect what can be inferred after controlling for complexity.
