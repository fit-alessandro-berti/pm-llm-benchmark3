**7.6**

The final write-up is a strong, structured dialectic: it uses the required headings, argues both sides at real strength, keeps authority/expiry bright-line (especially 704 vs 705 and 702 vs 714), and mostly classifies from evidence rather than splitting differences. Section 1’s stipulated/disputed/unproved split and dual burden of proof are unusually clean. The matrix is complete, including M0-fitting non-control-flow cases (712, 715). Those virtues are real; they are not enough for a top score under a strict reading.

The decisive deductions are in **model semantics and the revised POWL**, which the prompt weights heavily.

M1 cannot replay a normal full-review rejection (`Register → Sanctions ∥ Quality → Reject → Notify → Close`). Reject is an XOR sibling of the checked routes, so checks-then-reject is not in the language. The text then partly hides this by saying rejection is “governed by scope rules, not by this POWL,” even though `Reject_Supplier` *is* in the model. That violates the instruction to state awkward encodings instead of concealing them.

The auto route makes `Master_Data_Validation` a mandatory POWL step. Eligible historical pilot cases such as SUP-702 have no such event, so they would fail the new model. MDV belongs in the data/overlay layer (where the code already lists it). Putting it in the net is inconsistent layering and makes the “executable revision” unfit for the log it is supposed to govern.

PD-3/FD-7 say quality/approval **may** follow provisional activation. M1 forces them after `Activate_Supplier`. Stricter, CS-9-like orders on those routes are therefore excluded. A partial order that *permits* post-activation completion would have been the defensible approximation.

Smaller faults, each enough to block a high-9 under the stated rubric:

- SUP-715 as `NONCOMPLIANT` overreads a disclosure duty the materials say does not prohibit the relationship; `MIXED` or a scoped residual finding would have been tighter.
- SUP-713 as `COMPLIANT` is slightly generous given a mapping approved after the fact (the evidentiary-vs-normative distinction is good, the label is still optimistic).
- Defense slightly overclaims that M0 “contradicts” CS-9 on rejects; CS-9 is silent on whether those leads are M0 cases.
- System-level prose says “5 noncompliant” and then lists six cases.

Classifications that *are* well judged: 701, 703, 704, 705, 706, 707, 708, 709, 711, 712, 714, and the 702/714 authority split. The overlay table, snapshot/alias/notification schema, and inline authority checks are the right *kind* of data proposal. They do not repair the control-flow errors above.

Net: excellent legal and evidential reasoning; not nearly flawless as a process-aware model revision.
