**5.5**

The answer is correctly structured and not vacuous: it uses three scenarios, warns against averages, treats East as different in places, and recommends a guarded pilot. Under a strict reading of the prompt, that is the floor of a passing response, not evidence of a strong one. The analysis is repeatedly one-sided, internally inconsistent, and sloppy with the given data and with the PM4Py contract.

**Section 1 is a claim list, not a mechanism map.** Required labels appear, but the causal chains are asserted rather than derived. Local-expertise loss is mapped to *higher* cross-location handovers even though the proposed routing is designed to cut ad hoc cross-team transfers (6 → 3 per 100). The observable signature of lost local knowledge is more plausibly extra specialist referrals, higher `rework_flag` on `local_expertise_flag=1` cases, longer early-stage service times, and more `handover_reason` values of the form “need specialist / unclear.” Informal advice is also largely off-log; that observability gap is ignored.

Worse, the map *assumes* the restructuring is harmful: role clarity “declines,” resilience “falls,” rework “rises.” The prompt forbade a default valence. Pooling three tech specialists can *raise* absence resilience relative to today’s already-bad cover path (8.9h, 18% rework). Shared queues can *clarify* ownership if `org:resource` and queue IDs are well designed. Those counter-mechanisms belong in the map; they are missing. Legal/Risk at 93%/91% utilization is the single most important queueing fact in the brief and is barely used. Routing 9 → 12 specialty cases per 100 onto already-saturated servers is a nonlinear wait-time mechanism, not a footnote.

**Section 2 contradicts Section 1 and mishandles East.** Optimistic “role clarity increases because queues make ownership explicit” is the opposite of the map, with no mechanism that would flip the sign. Optimistic “early warning signs are minimal variance in service time” are *good* signs, not warnings. The adverse scenario then puts SLA misses “concentrated in East.” That is backwards. East is the pre-loaded team (E_Tech 82%, 8.6d / 22.8d, 9.8% SLA miss). An adverse shared-service outcome should hit North/South (loss of local loops and regional knowledge) and high-complexity / legal-risk tails. East is the team most likely to *improve* if the only change is unloading E_Tech. Collapsing “duplicated approvals” is rhetoric, not a reading of the design. Learning is almost absent from the scenarios even though the prompt named it as a first-class outcome.

The scenarios also underuse the supplied tables. Management’s own estimates already lengthen several waits (tech 3.2→4.0h, return 1.6→2.2h, approval 2.4→3.0h, legal 9.6→10.5h) while raising specialist and approval frequencies. A competent central case would treat those numbers as the *stated* mean and then apply utilization/variance effects, not invent a loosely related 10–20% story.

**Section 3 is a poorly paired two-column dump with at least one API error.** `pm4py.get_service_time` does not measure waiting time on “new shared edges.” Waiting time is `insert_case_service_waiting_time` (or a performance DFG / sojourn decomposition). Resource-similarity drift is not a days-scale leading indicator. Missing, despite being named in the prompt: working-together and subcontracting networks, performance DFG, `extract_temporal_features_dataframe`, utilization from service time, WIP/queue length, `handover_reason`, and a real SLA definition. Pairing “handover edge weights” with “median cycle time by complexity” is not a leading/lagging design; it is two leftover rows.

**Section 4 hits the six required themes but stays thin and recycles the same weak cross-team story.** The important hidden risks that are *not* developed: (i) mix/censoring bias (easy cases finish, tails still open, mean looks better); (ii) East gains cancel North/South losses in the pooled mean; (iii) Legal/Risk saturation producing tail explosions that never move the mean; (iv) Kingman-style wait growth once the merged tech+legal+difficult-QC queue is a single high-utilization server. Those are the process-mining-relevant invisibilities. “Six numbered clauses” is not analysis.

**Section 5 is the largest design failure.** Treating North *and* East while holding South out confounds the heterogeneous effect the prompt insisted on. East is the expected winner; North is the expected loser. After 12 weeks you cannot say which mechanism operated. A 30% skim of hard cases also does **not** test the proposal. The proposal *merges* N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech away and centralizes QC and approvals. Leaving embedded specialists in place for 70% of work tests a different organization. The 85% utilization guardrail is already violated in the baseline (Legal 93%, Risk 91%, E_Tech 82%). Twelve weeks is shorter than a reliable read on 90th-percentile cycle time (East already 22.8d), learning, or expertise atrophy. No difference-in-differences, no case-mix adjustment, no isolation of the Legal/Risk change from the tech-pool change. Rollback on two noisy weeks, given 1–3 week cycle times, will either never fire or fire on incomplete cases.

**Section 6 is the least-wrong section**, and that is not high praise. “Pilot first” and “do not trust early averages” are the answers the prompt already demanded. The recommendation inherits the North+East confound and never says the obvious conditional policy: if East improves and North/South degrade, do not scale a uniform shared service—keep embedded tech in high-expertise teams and only pool the overloaded node. It also never conditions on *not* increasing Legal/Risk intake until capacity exists.

**Section 7 fails the specification it was given.** Working-together and subcontracting networks are required and absent. `get_service_time(df_post, ['Technical Shared Service'])` is not the PM4Py signature. `filter_event_attribute_values` is used as a case-level filter on `case_complexity` and `team`, which can mutilate traces. The fairness loop discards its return value and iterates South on a frame already filtered to North/East high-complexity, so South is empty. There is no temporal pre/post split, no control-group comparison, no SLA code, no `customer_segment` / `priority` / `handover_reason` analysis, no post rework call, no variant comparison. Calling it a “sketch” does not excuse omitting named analyses or writing code that cannot run as a comparison.

**Net:** competent exam-template coverage with several errors that change the forecast (East in the adverse tail, utilization guardrail vs. 91–93% baseline, pilot that cannot identify the East vs North/South effect, one-sided mechanisms, broken PM4Py comparison). That is not near-flawless. It is a mid-pack answer that would mislead a manager who trusted the adverse East story or the 85% guardrail.
