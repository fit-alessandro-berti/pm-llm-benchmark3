The answer hits the required seven headings, respects the constraints (no default verdict, not average-cycle-time-only, East treated separately, fairness/learning included, no unmonitored full rollout), and the organizational reasoning is mostly sound. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several material defects keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is done well.** The mechanism map correctly treats “fewer local loops” as distinct from fewer total handovers, and it ties pooling, queue formation, role blur, and expertise loss to concrete mining objects (handover, subcontracting, performance DFG, waiting time, roles). The three scenarios are differentiated on the right axes, and the central case is anchored to management’s own 4.0h / 10.5h / 12-per-100 figures. Hidden-average risks cover all mandated themes. The recommendation is appropriately conditional. East’s overload versus North/South local strength is used, not ignored.

**Mechanism map.** Several cells are unreadable because arrows were dropped (“local caseworker  local tech”, “local team  central service”). That is not a cosmetic issue in a mechanism map: the causal links are the deliverable. The map also under-uses the supplied tables. Management’s expected frequencies already imply *more* specialist and approval handoffs (41+36+49+12 versus 36+31+42+9), not a nominal reduction; that audit is only gestured at. Pooling math for utilization (71% / 68% / 82% plus Legal/Risk at 93%/91%) is never made explicit.

**Scenarios.** Structure matches the spec. Optimistic “early warning signs” are mostly falsifiers of optimism, not leading signs that the optimistic path is occurring. Adverse and central scenarios repeat the same bullets at different intensity rather than specifying distinct handover topologies or variant signatures. Fairness and learning are present but stay qualitative.

**Indicators.** The leading/lagging split is reasonable and uses named PM4Py measures. Weak points: role discovery and resource similarity are not “days” metrics; they need stable volumes. SLA miss is listed as a log measure though no SLA timestamp exists. Customer satisfaction is extra-log (acceptable if labeled; here it sits in a PM table). `handover_reason` and `priority`—columns that would separate planned routing from absence cover and would support fairness/SLA analysis—are barely used.

**Pilot.** Required elements are named, but several incompatible designs are stacked (East-first, matched North/South subset, same-team BAU, team-week cluster randomization). The pilot also tests a *modified* model (local tech retained for low/medium work; legal/risk expansion deferred) without stating that this is not a test of the proposed full merge. Windows are asserted without a sample-size or tail-power argument for East high-complexity or customer-segment gaps. Guardrail thresholds (20%, 15%, 2pp) are arbitrary.

**PM4Py sketch.** This is the largest technical failure. The sketch treats “Technical Shared Service” and “Shared approval” as `concept:name` values and filters variants with `"Technical Shared Service" in act`. In the given process those are organizational queues/resources; activities remain Specialist Review, Quality Check, Supervisor Approval, etc. That mistake would make the before/after DFG/variant comparison miss the actual change, which lives in `org:resource`, `team`, and handover/subcontracting networks. Further issues: `get_service_time` is never called; `format_dataframe` never binds the resource key, so org-network discovery may not use `org:resource`; `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is applied to a single-timestamp log without discussing that service versus wait is not identified; `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="case")` is used on attributes that may not be case-constant; `filter_variants` is fed a fragile `get_variants` key type; networks are discovered and never differenced (no edge-frequency deltas, centralization, or betweenness). Invented filter values (`language_accommodation`, `"true"`) are not marked as placeholders.

**Net.** Strong exam-style process-mining forecast with the right recommendation and the right risk set, undermined by an unreadable map, an incoherent pilot, unused quantitative evidence, and a sketch that confuses resources with activities. That is a competent answer with clear, grade-relevant flaws, not a near-flawless one.
