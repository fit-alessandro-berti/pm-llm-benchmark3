**What is done well**

The mechanism table is the right object: expected signature, supporting evidence, limiters, and a test. Learning is not equated with “gets faster”; fatigue is allowed to look like speeding up if verification is dropped; circadian effects are treated as poorly identified; queue pressure is the only mechanism with a within-band speed–quality signature; case-mix is grounded in the monotonic complexity/priority decline; batching and Bot_Risk are not collapsed into fatigue. Section 2 correctly splits an early mix/warm-up regime from a late pressure regime, states that high-WIP service is ~25–26% shorter inside each complexity band, and notes that later low-WIP error rates stay near early rates for seniors, mids, and night staff. Interventions 6 and 8 are refused on the right grounds. The dashboard exceeds the eight-indicator floor and insists on denominators, review coverage, and pending seven-day cohorts.

**Gaps that keep it well below a top score**

1. **Pooled sequence bins are still treated as if they were mostly within-activity performance.** The position table mixes all human activities. End-of-shift Quality Check bursts, Junior_H/I Document Review runs, Rework Correction, and bot-exception repairs will mechanically shorten recorded service and raise late error/rework *even if the same activity at the same complexity did not get worse*. Section 2 mentions delayed detection and batching, but never states this composition problem as a first-order confounder of the headline 10.8 min / 7.9% / 13.7% row. That is the largest interpretive hole.

2. **Learning is listed as “supported” by unadjusted 18.8→13.6 min and 3.4%→2.5%.** Those moves coincide with complexity 0.72→0.54 and high-priority 41%→28%. The “against” cell admits the confound, but the “supporting” cell still uses the confounded aggregates as if they were a learning signature. A stricter table would have put the raw drop under “compatible with,” not evidence for, until matched activity/complexity/WIP comparisons exist.

3. **No even qualitative dominance ordering by phase in the table, and no use of the WIP table to bound the early speed drop.** The narrative says “part of” 18.8→12.9 may be easier work, then stops. Given high vs. low complexity at low WIP (22.4 vs. 12.7 min), a large share of the early decline is *consistent with mix alone*. Omitting that bound makes warm-up look more necessary than the numbers require. (A fake numerical decomposition without the band cutoff would have been worse; a qualitative bound would have been enough.)

4. **Kitchen-sink model vs. earlier identifiability warning.** Section 3 first says to fit elapsed time, clock time, and WIP separately and jointly only with independent variation; then it writes a single `log(duration)` formula containing resource, shift, calendar, complexity, priority, elapsed minutes, WIP, cyclic clock, and “selected interactions.” With 13 humans, overlapping shifts, and WIP generated by prior speed/rework, that equation is not identified in the sense the same section describes. The caution is present; the illustrative specification walks it back.

5. **Circadian row is thinner than the data allow.** Night last-two-hour service 15.2 min vs. Day 10.9 min, with *lower* late errors than Day/Late, is used well as a limiter. The overlapping Early/Day and Day/Late windows (06:00–14:00 vs. 09:00–17:00 vs. 14:00–22:00) are a natural clock-vs-elapsed contrast and appear only later, in the analysis plan, not as a test in the table.

6. **Minor unclarities.** “Through positions 16–30” is a poor way to say “from bins 1–5 to 16–30.” “Later events” in the experience table is not the same as late clock time or high elapsed minutes, yet it is used to weaken fatigue; the text eventually flags mix differences, but the table cell is cleaner than the comparison. That matches “pseudocode,” but `cluster_log` is named in prose and unused, and `view_dotted_chart(..., attributes=...)` may not match the installed API.

**Constraint check (all passed)**

Fatigue is not treated as the sole cause. Early complexity/carryover is central. Late speed is attributed primarily to queue pressure, batching, and measurement, not learning. Punishment on raw late error rates is rejected. Quality checks are not to be stripped for throughput.
