**5.5**

The write-up is well structured and conceptually stronger than it is numerically reliable. It follows the required heading scheme, labels assumptions, separates wait from service, keeps OPEN/MISSING/blank distinct, and treats completed-case sojourn, right-censoring bias, and a time-varying bottleneck with real process-mining judgment. Those strengths do not offset multiple audit-level errors in the quantities the task actually asked to reconstruct.

**Case-level work is mostly right, then breaks on the incomplete cases.** Fully observed waits, services, and the P01–P14 sojourns (mean ≈146, median 145) check out. P16–P22 elapsed waits and OPEN lower bounds are correct. The failures cluster on missingness:

- P15’s S2 start and S1 end are both observed (13:30 and 12:15). S2 wait is **75 minutes**, not 75–110 (central 92.5). The range is what you get if S1 *start* is mistaken for S2 queue entry. That same confusion is repeated in the scenario table (late-start S2 wait 110, early-start 75). This is not a rounding caveat; it is the wrong event.
- A6/A7 say missing starts are bounded by prior/next work on the same resource, then the bounds do not do that. P07 on I1 should be tightened by P05’s 09:25 end (feasible start ≈09:25–10:00, not 08:45–10:00). P05 on C1 should be tightened by P03’s 09:45 end. For P11 on C2, P08 occupies 11:15–12:00 and P11 ends at 12:25, so a non-overlapping start cannot be before 12:00. The claim that “the queue-entry constraint 11:05 dominates the lower bound” inverts the resource constraint. Loose bounds are defensible only if labeled as ignoring the calendar; here they contradict the stated method.

**The required queue/WIP picture is not a reconstruction of this log.** Under A4, S1 queue is arrived-not-started. At 10:00 that is about **6** (P09–P14), at 11:00 about **6–7**, at 12:00 still about **6** under the central P15 start—not “about 2” at 09:00–12:00. Mid-window system WIP is about **11–12**, not 7–9. The 14:00 census (S1: 1+1, S2: 4+2, system 8) is exact and well explained; calling the late S1 queue of 1–3 the *peak* is therefore not a rounding issue. It means the time path was sketched from the tails, not swept. That is the core deliverable.

**Station-level arithmetic does not survive a recount.** Eighteen fully timestamped S1 services sum to **505** minutes, not “about 540.” Twelve fully timestamped S2 services sum to **480**, not “about 545.” The 540/545 figures look like completed counts times a typical service, or midpoints smuggled into a line that claims to exclude missing intervals. The 570–680 / 580–680 busy ranges and the 79–94% / 82–96% utilizations inherit that inflation. A defensible occupancy window is closer to S1 **535–645 / 720** and S2 **515–645 / 705**, and even that still depends on the overly wide missing-start intervals.

**Subperiod flows and Little’s Law are sloppy in the same way.** P17 arrives at 10:45, so 08:00–10:55 has **17** external arrivals, not 16. P07’s S1 *end* is observed at 10:00; S1 completions in that bucket cannot “depend on P07 timing.” The text later admits this, so the table and the prose disagree. 11:10–12:00 has **3** arrivals (P19–P21), not 4, and 2 or 3 S1 completions depending on whether 12:00 is closed—not arrivals 4 and completions 3 under one convention. The Little interval 11:15–13:30 has **six** S2 completions (P08–P13), not “roughly 4–5,” so \(X_{S2}\approx 1.8\)–\(2.2\) per hour is too low. The caution about nonstationarity is appropriate; the check is not.

**Internal consistency is weaker than the tone suggests.** Optimistic/central/pessimistic logic is carefully framed as wait-versus-service tradeoff, which is the right idea, but the scenario table is already wrong for P15 S2 and uses infeasible resource windows for P07/P05/P11. Section 4’s WIP snapshots cannot be what the Section 7 event sweep would print, so the “reproducible” code was not used to police the narrative. Wrapping the required PM4Py calls in `try/except` further weakens the reproducibility claim: the task asked to demonstrate those APIs, not to guard against them.

**What remains genuinely good** is the qualitative bottleneck reading (S1 load-builds first; S2 is the cutoff bottleneck; C2’s 15-minute outage aggravates rather than creates the backlog), the refusal to treat the 146-minute completed-case mean as a population sojourn, and the explicit elapsed-versus-final split for OPEN work. Those points are mostly robust to the numerical errors. They are not enough. A strict reading of this task is that queue length, busy minutes, rates, missing-start intervals, and Little’s Law have to be *computed from the table*, not approximated in a way that fails a line-by-line check.

The answer looks like a strong method note attached to an unaudited reconstruction. That is a mid score, not a high one.
