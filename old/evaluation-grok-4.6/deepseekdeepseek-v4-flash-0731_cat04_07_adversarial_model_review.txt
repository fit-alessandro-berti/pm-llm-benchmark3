**7.2**

The final document is a strong board-ready review: it follows the required heading contract, refuses to collapse metrics into a winner, reasons from concrete traces, and proposes a third design that is actually safer than S and more maintainable than C. It is not nearly flawless. Several technical slips and incomplete required items keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What holds up.** Use-case framing in §1 is correct and is used consistently later. The §2 table treats every reported number as evidence, not a scoreboard, and the comparability caveats (seeds, costs, positive-log precision, undocumented duplicate-label definition) are the right ones. Probe reasoning for S and C in §5 is accurate if “accepts” means language membership: S’s `+(X(a,τ),…)` block really does admit B3/B4; C’s eight-sequence XOR really does reject B2, B5, and T-only B6. The third model’s backbone — mandatory `par(C,T)`, exclusive approval, Expedite after both checks, correction only via a `Correct`-headed loop — is a genuine alternative, not a blend of the two submissions. A1–A4 distinguish it from both inputs. The dissent is a real counter-argument (fail-closed C vs. attribute-open third), not a token paragraph, and it is inside the 150–250 word window.

**Material errors.** The worst factual slip is in §7: P7 is *not* left to the external checker. After `par(C,T)`, the only additional C/T labels sit inside a body that starts with `Correct Dossier`, so B8 is already outside the process-tree language. Calling the Python scan the enforcement mechanism overstates the hybrid and understates the tree. Related looseness is undersold: putting `RepairLoop` in parallel with the S/E block lets Expedite sit between a correction and its rechecks, and the tree accepts `Correct` with an empty recheck set. Both are defensible as “unknown attribute” choices; they are not called out as probes the way A4 is. The §10 line that the third model can be used for simulation “immediately after soundness checks” sits badly next to optional Supplier and optional rechecks: play-out will generate cases that are only legal if missing attributes happen to match.

**Required-item gaps.** §9 does not fully deliver the validation program it was asked for. Duplicate-event perturbation is essentially missing (only deletion is sketched). Attribute-stratified holdout collapses to variant stratification plus a comment that attributes are absent. Stakeholder comprehension is a deployment condition in §10, not a documented protocol in §9. The 0.90 holdout-fitness reject rule reintroduces the metric worship §1–§2 correctly refused. The negative-suite / alignment skeleton is also sloppy as runnable PM4Py: raw dicts in `Trace(...)` and an assumed `d["cost"]` key are version-fragile; the n=1 split rule parks rare variants only in train and weakens the holdout it is meant to protect.

**Net.** This is a competent skeptical review with a defensible third model. Hypercritically, the P7 misstatement, the under-specified parallel/empty-recheck semantics, and the incomplete validation checklist are enough to keep it out of the 8–10 band.
