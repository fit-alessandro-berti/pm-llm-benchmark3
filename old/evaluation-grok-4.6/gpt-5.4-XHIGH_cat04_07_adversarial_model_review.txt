**7.0**

The write-up is well organized, follows the required headings, and is strongest where it treats metrics as evidence rather than scores to maximize. Use-case separation, the probe matrix, and the dissenting view are the best parts. It is not close to flawless. The third artifact — the part the prompt most needs to be right — is internally inconsistent and, as written, does not implement the policy it claims to implement.

**What is actually good.** Model S and Model C are read with standard process-tree semantics, not from the leaderboard. The claim that S optionalizes the four middle activities, and that C is an eight-trace whitelist, is correct. B1–B8 are classified correctly under language membership. The metric table refuses aggregation and correctly flags positive-only precision, optionalized mandatory work, missing seeds, and undocumented duplicate-label counting. The dissent is a real fail-closed argument for C, not a rhetorical shrug. Governance (S exploration-only; neither original for enforcement) follows from F1, B3, B4, B2, and B5 rather than from “S has higher fitness.”

**Section 7 is the decisive failure.** `model_third` is only the POWL backbone. It has no `Correct Dossier`, no redo, and no loop. Play-out, alignment, and `evaluate_model(model_third)` therefore reject every correction trace, including B6 and A2. The audit table still says the third alternative “accepts” those traces if `affected_checks` is set. That is not a hybrid; it is two acceptors that do not compose. A backbone-plus-policy design is allowed, but then the behavior audit, fitness discussion, and validation program must evaluate the **same** decision procedure. They do not. Section 9 converts `model` to a Petri net and never calls `classify_trace`. The advertised P6/P7 semantics are invisible to the proposed experiments.

**`classify_trace` is not a correct policy oracle.** Mandatory checks are accumulated over `labels[1:-1]`, which includes events **after** approval. `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Credit Check, Technical Review, Release Order` therefore passes the checker (one approval; both checks seen). So does a check split across the approval boundary. Supplier is tested for presence, not position, so a post-approval `Supplier Review` can satisfy the external rule. After the first `Correct Dossier`, `pending_scope` is never returned to `None`; an empty list remains `is not None`, so a later initial check is misread as a recheck. Multiple Expedite/Supplier events are ignored. Unknown labels are ignored. The Expedite block uses first-occurrence `index`, which is only safe because missing checks return earlier — a fragile ordering, not a specified invariant.

The conjunction “POWL AND policy” only papers over some of this. The POWL does force both checks before approval and Expedite after both checks, so the worst standalone policy bugs are not in the backbone language. That does not rescue the write-up: correction traces live only in the buggy checker, and the checker is what section 8 uses to claim superiority on P6, B6, and A2.

**Smaller technical defects.** `stratified_split` sends every singleton bucket entirely to holdout (`k = max(1, …)`), so rare signatures never appear in training and the “reproducible stratified holdout” is biased against C-like rigidity. Alignment-cost sensitivity is an empty-parameter stub. Internal cases with an extra Supplier Review are auto-passed and never discussed. Simulation is listed as a use case, then left with a generator that cannot emit corrections. “Little semantic uncertainty” in §5 is fair for S and C; it is not fair for the third design, whose acceptance depends on which artifact you run.

**Overclaim vs. evidence.** Calling the third model the only honest treatment of P3/P6 is true only in prose. The executable `model_third` is a reasonably good partial order for the no-correction subset (mandatory checks, optional supplier/expedite, Expedite after both checks, XOR approval). That **is** safer than S and less brittle than C on B2/B5/A1/A3. It is not a complete replacement for P6/P7, and the paper writes as if it were.

**What keeps it at 7 rather than lower.** The review of S and C, the pathology ranking, the refusal to crown a winner by average score, and the deployment conditions (attributes, negative suite, no silent optionalizing) are competent board-level work. The grade is capped because the required third model does not do what the surrounding narrative says it does.
