The write-up is complete, well structured, and generally reasons from objectives, timing, independence, coverage, and recoverability rather than raw four-eyes counts. The hardest cases (AP-202, AP-204, AP-205, AP-208) are directionally right. It is not close to flawless.

**Case judgments.** AP-202 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` and AP-205 as `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION` are correct. AP-204 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` despite formal four-eyes is the right non-mechanical call given the fabricated receipt. AP-208 as `OBJECTIVE_FAILED` is right, but the rationale omits the decisive ED-1 amount breach (EUR 900,000 ≫ 100,000) and treats “payment was legitimate” as almost incidental to an authorization-control failure. AP-201’s `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is defensible, but the recall claim is wrong: settlement is 10:00 on 4 May and audit is 09:00 on 5 May, so the review is still inside a 24-hour settlement/recall window; measuring 25 hours from *release* and treating recall as already lost is a concrete timing error. AP-206 should have been `INDETERMINATE` (or at least presented as the leading alternative). The prompt itself says the late token may be a logging delay or an ED-1 precondition failure; locking `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` papers over that. Never using `INDETERMINATE` at all is a classification-scheme miss. AP-209 as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` over-weights a possible dedicated two-person cell that the facts say is unproven.

**Instruction breaches.** Section 5 invents precise probabilities (~85%, &lt;10%, ~20%, ~60%, ~70%, ~15%, ~40%, ~5%) without labeling them illustrative, which the prompt forbade. “Same expected loss” is explained adequately; the sufficiency matrix is schematic but usable.

**Code.** The block is self-contained and labeled as evidence, but it does not do what was asked. `filter_four_eyes_principle` is assumed to return a list of violating case IDs; if it returns a filtered log (normal PM4Py pattern), `pd.DataFrame(violating_cases, columns=[...])` is invalid. Facts are joined only to those violators, so AP-204 and AP-209 never get flags. Hard-coded `audit_timing_hrs` for AP-208 (96) does not match the log.

**Smaller defects.** Section 1 treats ED-1 as a permission rather than mapping prevent/detect/correct/fail as cleanly as the other three controls. Post-release / pre-settlement MG-4 is correctly called hybrid in the note, but the comparison table still lists it as simply “Medium preventive,” which is inconsistent. AP-204’s “past recall” assumes a settlement the event log never records.

Strong exam-style analysis with several material timing, taxonomy, and implementation errors. Not a high score under a strict reading.
