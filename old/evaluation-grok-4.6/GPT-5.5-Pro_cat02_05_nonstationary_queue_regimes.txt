**7.6**

The answer is complete, well structured, and mostly numerically careful. It meets the required heading contract, separates demand / mix / capacity / backlog, refuses to treat outage recovery as steady capacity, and is appropriately conservative on Little’s Law. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several concrete defects keep it well below the top of the scale.

**Material inaccuracy.** Section 4 states that the pooled median service time is “around 32.5 minutes” and then repeats “Pooled median service is 32.5 min.” That is wrong. The 28 daily medians sort to a median of **30.5** minutes; their mean is about **31.0**. The same section’s own Python would print `median_service_med = 30.5` for `all_days_pooled`. So the featured pooled-versus-segmented comparison contradicts both the data and the accompanying code. The qualitative contrast (≈25 vs ≈35) still holds, but a required quantitative exhibit is incorrect.

**Autocorrelation is over-interpreted.** Lag-1 values of ~0.87–0.93 on service and `complex_pct` are largely what a single mid-sample level shift produces. The answer reports them as evidence of serial dependence without decomposing within-regime persistence from the April 20 mix/service jump. Lag-7 ≈ 0.99 on arrivals is plausible given weekday/weekend separation, but it is mostly calendar structure plus trend, not a deep stochastic finding. This is a real inferential slip, not just a wording nit.

**The Section 4 “Simpson” weekend comparison is contaminated.** Absolute weekend WIP 17.5 vs weekday 36.5, and WIP/FTE 6.61 vs 5.66, mix baseline, outage, recovery, and high-complex days into one weekday average. The cleaner within-regime contrast already appears in Section 2 (baseline ~5.5 vs ~3.4). Using the mixed weekday figure makes the reversal look weaker and less clean than it is.

**A few smaller analytical gaps.**
- Recovery service “median 31 / p90 66” is just the two-day midpoint; that should have been labeled as such.
- Baseline completions are 697/7 = 99.57, rounded to “100/day” beside a more precise 8.30/h. Harmless, but sloppy next to an otherwise precise table.
- Implied opening-hour sojourn (~2.44 h baseline) is never contrasted with median active service (25 min). That gap is the most direct demonstration that service time is not flow time.
- The peak-column vs hourly-vector mismatch is correctly flagged under the stated 08:00–09:59 convention, but the audit stops too early: for three of four representative days, a one-bin shift (09:00–10:59) nearly reproduces `peak_08_10_pct`. The bursty 21 April vector still fails. That is worth one extra sentence.
- Threat 10 (net +110 vs falling late WIP) is a good audit point, but mean snapshot WIP is not start/end inventory, so the inconsistency is slightly overstated.

**What remains strong.** Estimands and threats in Section 1 are excellent, especially carry-over, service ≠ flow, outage contamination, non-independence, and the closed-flow check. The primary segmentation is operationally right: weekdays/weekends split, outage isolated, Apr 16–17 treated as catch-up rather than capacity, Apr 20–28 as overload, Apr 29 as a mixed implementation day. Almost all regime rates, nets, WIP/FTE, and FTE-hour productivities check out. The alternative state-pressure cut is genuinely different and the tradeoff with calendar staffing is clear. Little’s Law is applied only as an opening-hour ratio, with completions used when A ≈ C, and the verdicts (`not defensible` / `approximate only`) are the correct ones. The “staff to 90/day and drop temp FTE because productivity is stable” policy is a sharp pooled-vs-segmented implication. The Python block satisfies the seven technical requirements and does not pretend the daily table is a PM4Py log.

**Net.** This is a strong process-mining writeup with one definite published number wrong, one oversold dependence statistic, and a few avoidable imprecisions. That is too many defects for a top-band score under the requested standard.
