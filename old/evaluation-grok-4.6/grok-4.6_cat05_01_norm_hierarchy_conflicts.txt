**7.6**

This is a strong, assignment-faithful analysis with real process discipline. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several interpretive stretches, one structural tension, and a handful of inconsistent evidence calls keep it well out of the top band.

## What it does well

The answer follows the required skeleton exactly and, more importantly, uses it. Facts and assumptions are separated. Authority is treated as issue-specific rather than “newest / strongest / global wins.” Rules are applied at commitment time. A6 is actually used. ERP stamps are not treated as the act. Emergency status is not a blanket cure. Those are the core quality constraints, and they are met.

The hierarchy table is the right shape: GPIP-4 integrity controls (A1, A3, and the existence of A2) sit above ECD-2; ECD-2 is confined to local timing and the local register; D1 is an uncontrolled Finance interpretation with no retroactivity. The formalized rules plus the `normally` / `unless` / `overrides_for` fragment are genuine defeasible logic, not decorative DECLARE statements. Scope and clock work on P-501 (pre-09:00), P-502 (D1 not yet live), P-503 (backup / three-day stock), P-504 (cafeteria), P-506 (verbal extension), and P-508 (150k cap) is careful and usually right.

Section 5 is the best part of the paper. The alternatives name the exact premise that would have to move, and several of them (especially P-502’s C4-as-A7 reading and P-508’s de minimis 5k reading) are the ones a serious reviewer would actually run.

## Material flaws

**1. D1 is asked to do more work than the hierarchy allows.**  
This is the central defect. Section 2 limits the CFO email to *how Finance operational approval may be evidenced*, and says it probably cannot touch A1, A3, or the substance of GPIP-4 money control. Section 1 then lets dual Incident Command authorization stand in for *both* the Finance Controller *and* the independent budget owner, even though the file says IC members are not budget owners and D3/D4 warn against stripping integrity controls. That is how P-505 and especially P-507 become `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` with no Finance event on the record. That is not a small hedge. It converts an uncontrolled email into a temporary waiver of a board-level dual-control rule, which is exactly the move the opening method claimed it would refuse. Using `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` instead of `COMPLIANT` acknowledges the discomfort; it does not resolve the contradiction.

**2. P-501 is too clean.**  
`COMPLIANT` at medium-high confidence overstates the file. B2/B5 still require Plant and Finance on a ≥25k emergency buy; those approvals arrive six hours after commitment. The answer treats that as harmless because A2 is off, which makes B2 timing secretly data-conditional (“pre-commit only when A2 also fires”). That doctrine is never justified. Separately, A1 is listed as satisfied while the 15:10 Plant approver is unknown. If that person is ENG-1 or SHIFT-1, the case flips. Missing identity is not a satisfied segregation rule. `COMPLIANT` with a loud residual, or a lower confidence, would have been defensible. The current cell is not.

**3. P-505 is not held to the same recording standard as P-502 and P-507.**  
For C3 the paper is otherwise meticulous: verbal authorization is valid only if recorded within four hours, and PDF beats ERP. P-505 is given “C3 met at 17:50” with no recording timestamp at all. On a razor-edge expiry case that already depends on a supplier acknowledgment versus an 18:06 ERP stamp, that is an evidential lapse. This was also the one natural `INDETERMINATE` file (clock plus D1 authority). The label is defined, then never used.

**4. Severity is poorly calibrated at the top of the money range.**  
P-507 (EUR 110k, off-register, no Finance event, D1 doing all the work) is marked **Low**. That is not a serious residual-risk judgment. P-508’s missing A2 at EUR 155k is only **Medium**, which is at least argued, but the 110k call is simply soft. Classification and severity are supposed to carry different information; here severity is being used to flatter the preferred overlay reading.

## Lesser issues

- **P-504 at High confidence** sits badly next to the paper’s own note that cafeteria spend may fall outside SOP scope. If B1/B3 do not attach, the violation set shrinks and the right label is closer to a weaker noncompliance or an indeterminate SOP-jurisdiction question. The purpose/scope failure is still real; the confidence is not.
- **“Unapproved” versus “new” supplier** is a disciplined assumption, but it is never stress-tested against A3’s wording. It only matters for P-504, which makes the silence cheaper than it looks.
- **“C1 (in substance)”** on P-505 is vague. Either a PO exists and is missing from the extract (logging defect) or the PO duty was never discharged (remaining SOP breach). The paper cannot have both.
- A few hierarchy phrases (“local convenience, emergency haste, IC custom”) are slack relative to the rest of the instrument.
- Section 7 is operationally good — written CRO instrument, 24-hour purpose test, SoD ineligibility, clocks, post-expiry standing rules, third-directive brake — but it quietly hardens the same D1-as-Finance-substitute theory the hierarchy was unwilling to fully own.

## Bottom line

The answer is consistent, time-aware, and unusually honest about alternatives. It would survive a competent second review. It would not survive a hostile one without a rewrite of the D1/A2 relationship, a downgrade or reopen of P-501, a real C3 recording analysis on P-505, and a reset of P-507 severity. That is a 7.6, not a 9.
