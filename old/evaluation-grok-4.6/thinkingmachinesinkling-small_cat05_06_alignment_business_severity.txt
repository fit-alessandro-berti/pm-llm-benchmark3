**6.4**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and conceptually strong on the assignment’s core point: uniform alignment cost is not business risk. Sections 1 and 6 are the best parts. Silent `T(tau)` is correctly treated as fitting, zero-cost Auto Approve paths are correctly treated as policy failures, missing events are not treated as proof of non-occurrence, and the required deviation taxonomy is used. All 12 cases appear, headings match, and the remediation/hypothesis sections are usable.

It is not close to flawless.

**Policy errors.** CL-610 is scored as a P1-only Auto Approve breach. P2 independently requires Fraud Screen and Manual Approve for any recent beneficiary-bank change, so this is a dual P1/P2 failure, not a milder cousin of CL-609. Ranking CL-609 (18k, MEDIUM, no bank change) at 5 and CL-610 at 4 inverts the overlay. CL-602 invokes P3 even though Pay follows Manual Approve; P3 is sequence (pay before applicable approval), not weak evidence behind an approval that already happened. CL-604’s overlay line (“fraud/approval missing”) is wrong: Manual Approve is present and only late.

**Severity discipline.** Three very different failures—480k undocumented fraud path, 90k irreversible pay-before-approve, 18k data-guard miss—are all 5 with little differentiation by exposure or reversibility. That undercuts the framework the answer itself defines.

**Required remediation type omitted.** The portfolio has workflow redesign, not data-aware conformance checking: alignments plus amount/risk/bank-change predicates that would surface the four fully synchronous P1/P2 violations. Those are not the same control.

**Section 7 is not a faithful executable reproduction.** `conformance_diagnostics_alignments` is called as `(net, im, fm, log)`; the public API is log-first. The log omits CL-606/608/611/612. Worse, CL-604 is given a `Fraud Screen` event even though the authoritative alignment is `M(Fraud Screen)`. That contradicts the instruction to treat the supplied alignments as diagnostic ground truth. Unused import, string timestamps without `format_dataframe`, and a try/except that merely prints failure mean the block is unlikely to run as specified.

Strong PM semantics, several concrete overlay and implementation mistakes. Under a strict bar that is a mid-6, not an 8+.
