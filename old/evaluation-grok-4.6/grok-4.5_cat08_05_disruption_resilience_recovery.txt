The answer is complete, well structured, and generally well grounded in the DFGs, phase metrics, conformance results, and segment/product risk. It does the four things the task actually tests: it tracks before/during/after state, it interprets adaptations abductively rather than treating every deviation as failure, it separates mission-protective adaptation from concealed control loss, and it proposes a concrete recovery model. Under a normal rubric this would score higher. Under the required strict standard it is not nearly flawless.

**Most serious factual problem.** Pattern F is described as evidence of resilience because dual-control missing was “only 2.9% (not worse).” Baseline dual-control missing was 0.2%. T2 is more than 14× worse, not “not worse.” The same phase also shows cold-chain documentation missing at 4.8% versus 0.4%. F can still be read as a *partial* substitute that may have prevented a worse controlled-substance collapse, but the write-up treats a large control degradation as if it were contained success. That is a material misread of the only quantitative evidence offered for F, and it then contaminates Section 3, where F is listed as “true resilience.”

**Related DFG underuse.** In the T2 DFG, `Manual pack → Dispatch without scan` is 1,800 and `Temporary release control` is only 1,650 (of which 1,450 still dispatch without scan). A large share of flow bypassed F entirely. The table and Section 3 talk about sampling and cold-chain weakness, but they do not use this frequency split. That leaves the resilience claim for F stronger than the process graph supports.

**Smaller accuracy and inference issues.**
- T0 is labeled “Day 30 to Day 1.” The source window is Day −30 to Day −1.
- C is said to be a “high contribution to 9.5% traceability exceptions.” The log supports that C is a major *authorization* deviation (1,600 skips), but the 9.5% figure is an aggregate; G, missing scans, missing PoD, and missing temperature records are at least as plausible drivers. The table correctly marks them *Modify* because authorization was sometimes skipped, deferral rules look informal, and pharmacy fill fell to 61%. The later text quietly substitutes the *redesigned* versions for the *observed* ones. The ambiguity subsection partly repairs this, but the “true resilience” list is cleaner than the evidence.
- “Hospital-critical fill rate remained 91%” is taken as strong proof of A. The prompt uses “remained,” so the reading is allowed, but baseline fill rate is not in the metric table. The answer never flags that limitation.

**Format and clarity defects in the required artifacts.** The seven sections are present and in order, but the recovery model and indicator tables lose comparison operators and arrows (`snapshot age  T_fresh`, `Bare skip  0`, `No   Normative path`, `Fail gate  HOLD`, `w5·DeferCount` with the penalty sign dropped).
