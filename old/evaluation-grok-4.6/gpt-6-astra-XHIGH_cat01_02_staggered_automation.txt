8.3

This is a strong, format-complete causal-policy analysis with unusually careful arithmetic and a clear distinction among availability, uptake, package effects, and software effects. It is not flawless. Under a strict reading, several unclarities, small specification problems, and decision-rule gaps keep it well below a top score.

**What is done well.** Section 1 stays descriptive and correctly flags the 4–6% pre-availability fast-path share, targeted selection, staggered exposure, and the fact that averages of monthly quantiles are not pooled case quantiles. The estimand table is the strongest part of the answer: E1 is defined as a delivered rollout package rather than “the software,” E2 rejects completed-fast-path conditioning as an assignment rule, E9/E10 separate component and network effects, and interference through the fraud desk is treated as a design object rather than a footnote. The primary design (cohort-time contrasts vs never-treated donors, already-treated units kept out of the comparison) is the right one for this panel; naïve TWFE is demoted and explained. Quantitative work I could check is accurate to the reported rounding: pre/post means, donor DiDs, g−1 event-study averages, load changes, separate-donor envelopes, Gamma baseline sensitivity, and the CSAT selection illustration. Median and p90 are never collapsed. The claim ladder is correctly ordered.

**Strict problems.**

1. **Dose-response evidence is weaker than advertised.** The April→August intensity comparisons for Alpha/Beta confound calendar time, complexity, staffing, and learning. The Gamma July–August check is worse: both Gamma and donors are recovering from the May–June peak, so a 7 pp intensity rise with a −0.15/−0.20 hour relative change is not a meaningful dose test. The FE intensity coefficients are at least labeled descriptive, but they are still presented as “quantitative evidence” of intensity without showing identification within the treated post period. Completed-path share remains a collider/mediator; that caveat is stated, then underweighted.

2. **Staffing is placed in contradictory roles.** E1 puts “unrelated … staffing schedules” in both counterfactuals, while later text treats Alpha −2 / Beta +1 / Gamma −1 / Delta +1 as threats that can mask or mimic benefit. If staffing responded to expected automation, it is a mediator of the package, not a covariate to hold fixed. The answer never resolves which contrast leadership is being offered.

3. **The leading alternative explanation is underplayed.** Donors’ deterioration is the entire source of the “relative improvement” story: Alpha/Beta raw medians are mixed (−0.4 h, +1.86 h) and raw p90 averages rise ~10%. A product-complexity shock that interacts with baseline risk (Epsilon tracks Alpha more than Delta does) can produce exactly this DiD pattern without any automation benefit. Leave-one-out donor checks do not address a common shock with heterogeneous incidence. The ±5 h / ±8 h drift scenarios are honest, but they are assumed, not identified, and then reused as if they were the uncertainty interval for Section 7.

4. **Section 7 confidence scores overstate directional knowledge.** After correctly saying the data do not establish even the causal sign without extra restrictions, the table assigns 40/30/45/55 to operational outcomes. Escalation at 55 is a confidence in a *routing change*, not in welfare; that is easy to misread as “we know automation increases escalations and that is the most solid finding.” CSAT at 15 is appropriate; the others are not calibrated to the paper’s own identifying caveats.

5. **The rollout recommendation leaves current operations unaddressed and uses arbitrary gates.** “Expand with an embedded evaluation” is the right class of decision, and Delta-first with an Epsilon safety gate is reasonable. Missing: whether Alpha/Beta should keep raising `automated_fast_path_pct` while tails are worse in levels. The 5% median / 5% productivity scale-up rule and the 10% p90 / 0.15 CSAT tripwires are invented; Alpha’s own post-window p90 increase is already ~9.8%, so the tail guardrail is almost calibrated to ignore the observed early-adopter pattern. Within-Delta unit randomization is also only a partial fix while the shared fraud desk is already loaded by existing adopters.

6. **Small presentation and specification nits.** Section 8 sits at or slightly over the 400-word cap. Event-time dummies are built in code and then unused in estimation (the arithmetic event study is better, but the indicators are ornamental). Equal-weight averaging of Alpha/Beta five-month windows with Gamma’s two-month window is flagged, then still used as the headline D for sensitivity and CSAT bounds. E5/E6 say “No causal identification” while E3/E4 are only “not established,” even though all four are the same branch-month DiD with different outcomes.

**Bottom line.** The answer understands the scientific problem: endogenous staggered rollout, intensity ≠ availability, substitution versus capacity, tail versus median, small clusters, and donor contamination. It refuses the “automation reduces cycle time by X%” sentence for the right reasons. What keeps the score well short of the top of the scale is residual over-interpretation of relative DiDs that are driven by untreated deterioration, an unclean intensity analysis, unresolved treatment-definition issues around staffing and routing, and a scale-up rule that is more precise than the evidence.
