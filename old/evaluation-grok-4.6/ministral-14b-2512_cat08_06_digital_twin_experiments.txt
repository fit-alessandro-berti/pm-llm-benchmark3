**4.0**

The write-up has the right section skeleton and names many of the right process objects, but it fails several hard constraints, fabricates PM4Py, and then implements an independent-case loop that does not actually twin the system it describes.

**Process-mining grounding is the clearest failure.** The brief required `pm4py.format_dataframe` on the excerpt and `pm4py.parse_process_tree` (or an explicit caveat about the tree string). The skeleton does neither. It never materializes the given log excerpt, never builds a performance DFG, never computes case-arrival average, and never derives service/waiting columns. Instead it invents APIs that do not exist: `discover_arrival_distribution`, `discover_performance_schema`, `discover_waiting_times`, `discover_service_times`, `pm4py.algo.discovery.process_tree`, `performance_schema.apply`, `arrival_distribution.apply`, `variants.apply`. Importing Alpha Miner next to “arrival discovery” is cargo-cult PM. Calendars are not encoded by `xml_parser`. This is not a small naming slip; a judge told to check PM4Py conventions has to treat the estimation plan and the code as untrustworthy.

**The simulation is not the model claimed in prose.** Section 1 lists architect bursts, district batches, related cases, and calendars. `simulate()` is a monthly outer loop that draws a Poisson count, enqueues independent cases, pops queues, samples a service time, then jumps 30 days. There is no event calendar, no shared capacity, no working-hours clock, no AND-join after planning/fire/environment/notice/inspection, no batching state, no travel, no no-shows, no council-week shutdown. `generate_arrival` ignores the stated 1.7× seasonal and 2.2× Monday multipliers. Experiments reuse one simulator without reset. Several snippets are not valid Python (`["A01", "A02", ...]`). That directly violates “do not design a simple independent-case simulation only” and “do not ignore resource calendars.”

**Waiting vs service is collapsed, then dressed up as inference.** The log is completion-heavy. The plan says: if start is missing, infer service from waiting, and define waiting as next-start minus this-completion. That mixes residual sojourn, calendar idle, and the next queue, then uses the mixture as “service.” The limitation is not explained in a way that would let anyone estimate occupancy. The engine then samples only `service_time` and never waits at all.

**Missing objections are treated as almost observed.** The brief forbids treating missing objection events as observed. The answer still writes Bernoulli \(p=0.15\), and `duration > 30d ⇒ objection` with \(p=0.7\). Public-notice length is a censored proxy, not a labeled event. Using it as a classifier without a missing-data model, identifiability discussion, or sensitivity prior is exactly the error that was ruled out.

**Non-stationarity is stated, then analyzed as if the system had a steady state.** Discarding three months “until queues stabilize” is the wrong warm-up story for seasonal, calendar-driven, heavy-tailed work. Complex-variance p90 is 260 days; an 18-month run after a 3-month throwaway leaves a thin completed sample in the tail. Thirty replications “because CV < 10%” is asserted, not justified. Kruskal–Wallis on seeds is a non sequitur. No common random numbers, no paired comparison, no cyclic/terminating design, no discussion of whether means exist under shape \(<1\) Weibulls.

**Experiment design looks complete and is softer than it looks.** D1–D10 appear, which is good. Effect sizes (40% fewer missing-doc loops, 30% less travel) are hardcoded as if known. Stress 3 is “turn D4 off,” not a demand, calendar, or data-quality shock. There is no factor for start-time uncertainty, objection non-identifiability, or architect-burst intensity. Combined policy is only D10.

**Scope and decision sections are the strongest parts and still leak inventions.** Entities and FTE calendars are mostly taken from the brief. Inventing Monday priority at completeness (the log shows a Monday *volume* spike, not a priority rule) quietly changes the twin. Decision-drafting synchronization, inspector batch state, and council-week capacity are under-specified as state. Decision metrics cover median, tail, SLA-by-type, fairness, utilization, rework, and backlog—good—but 15%/20% reduction targets and 30/60/120-day SLAs are presented as criteria rather than stakeholder-elicited, and the 30-day Simple SLA is already in tension with the missing-doc variant (median 22d, p90 45d).

**Validation is a checklist with the wrong tools.** “PM4Py replay / fitness / precision” validates a control-flow model against a log, not a stochastic digital twin. Claiming planners/inspectors should sit at ~80% utilization invents an empirical target. Holdout of the last six months is mentioned without saying how seasonal alignment or incomplete long cases will be handled. There is no plan to validate inter-case dependence or calendar closures, which are the features that distinguish this twin from a replay.

**Net.** Structure and vocabulary are competent enough that this can look like a 7. Under a strict reading it is a 4: the required PM4Py path is missing, several named functions are fictional, objections and sojourn times are over-identified, and the executable sketch does not implement non-stationary demand, calendars, or cross-case coupling.
