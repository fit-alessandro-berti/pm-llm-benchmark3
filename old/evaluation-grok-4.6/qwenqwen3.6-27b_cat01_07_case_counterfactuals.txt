**5.4**

The write-up is well structured and conceptually on-theme: it separates timing, routing, and decision-policy interventions, keeps final compliance after assessment, flags four-eyes, and treats a deleted rework loop as a policy change rather than a free time cut. Format compliance is mostly solid. The quantitative counterfactuals are not.

**Fatal temporal errors.** K-337’s factual trace runs Monday week 1 through the weekend and ends Wednesday week 2 (~214 h). The answer repeatedly treats it as a same-week ~66 h case. The derivation then argues with itself (“~168 h? Wait… same week”), never resolves the calendar, and still reports −36 to −48 h in the table versus −24 to −30 h in the text. That is a core failure on temporal consistency.

**K-104 arithmetic does not match its own path.** Reserving C1 is modeled as extra Monday prep, but final review still starts Wed 13:10. Cutting 4.5 h to 3–4 h implies ~1–1.5 h earlier notify, not the claimed −4 to −8 h. A reserved C1 slot is also not used to move final-review start time, so the stated intervention is only half-applied.

**Queue snapshots are treated as optional color.** D2 at Mon 16:00 has 5 h remaining, yet K-219 starts D2 at 18:30. K-411 starts D2 at 10:00–10:30 despite a 4 h D2 queue at Tue 08:00, then lets A2 start Wed 08:00 despite a 5 h A2 queue — earlier readiness is confused with queue-jumping. Snapshots are forecasts, but they are not licenses to invent free servers.

**Smaller but real defects.** The base partial order smuggles in K-104-only prelim compliance. Best/base/worst bands are asserted more than derived from predecessor completion plus uncertain waits. Section 7 is thin on why a local K-104/K-219 win fails at 100 cases beyond restating bottlenecks. Assumption 7’s “exactly 48 h” weekend delay is over-precise and inconsistent with the K-337 log.

What remains good — interference table, policy-vs-timing distinction, K-337 quality risk, ranking K-337 last, usable pilot skeleton — is not enough. On a strict bar, material calendar and queue-propagation errors cap this well below “strong.”
