5.2

The write-up is well structured and conceptually stronger than its numbers. It follows the required heading scheme, treats `MISSING` / `OPEN` / blank as distinct, keeps station sojourn and WIP invariant to unobserved starts, and does not drop right-censored cases. Those are the right process-mining instincts. The execution is not close to flawless.

**Case-level reconstruction is mostly sound; aggregates are not.** Fully observed waits and services for P01–P04, P06, P08–P10, and P12–P22 match the log. The same-resource bounds for P05, P07, P11, and P15 are correctly derived. That competence does not carry over to Section 4–5.

S2 peak WIP is reported as 7 (and as a Phase-3 peak of 7). Under the author’s own occupancy definition, at most six cases are in S2 at once; the 14:00 snapshot is two in service plus four waiting. S1 Phase-1 peak WIP is not 8 at 09:25. At 09:25 S1 holds about six cases; Phase 1 ([08:00, 10:00)) peaks at 7; S1 first reaches 8 around 10:10. Phase-2/3 mean WIP is also wrong: S1 should be about 7.58 and 4.92, not 6.92 and 4.54; S2 should be about 3.88 and 5.38, not 3.58 and 5.77. Full-window means (~5.79 / ~3.72) are only approximately recovered, and the 361-point grid quietly over-counts the 14:00 instant.

**Phase utilizations are not supported by the resource calendars.** I2 in 10:00–12:00 is not 100% busy (observed busy time is 105 of 120 minutes; the 5-minute gaps are real). C2 in 12:00–14:00 is not 100% (P11–P13–P15 leave idle gaps; even the ASAP upper bound is 105/120). S1 Phase-2 “97.9%” and S2 Phase-3 “95.8%” are inflated. Full-window busy-time ranges [535, 605] and [515, 570] are internally consistent with the 0-service ALAP extreme, but that extreme is then treated as if it were an operational utilization band.

**Causal and Little’s-Law claims overreach.** P08’s 55-minute S2 wait is not “specifically because” C2 was in maintenance when P08 became ready at 10:20. C2 was still on P06 until 10:50; the outage explains roughly 15 of the 55 minutes, plus a 5-minute post-outage gap. Little’s Law is correctly refused for the full window, but the 09:00–13:00 check is broken: S1 occupancy is 1670 case-minutes (mean 6.96), WIP starts and ends at 5 not “5 to 6”, and the empirical sojourn 106.8 min is not a defined average of any stated cohort. Calling a 4–12% gap “remarkably tight” is rhetoric on top of an undefined statistic. Section 2’s λ ≈ 3.6 also disagrees with Section 5’s 3.75 / 3.50.

**Uncertainty handling is incomplete.** Midpoint vs ASAP vs ALAP is the right skeleton, and the invariance of completed sojourns and \(L(t)\) is the best insight in the document. But ALAP is allowed to be zero service without a realistic floor from neighboring cycle times; rounding is given a variance formula and then ignored in every reported interval; FIFO is declared “nominal” so that P08 starting at 09:50 while P07 may still be waiting is never used to tighten P07/P15. “Bufferless transfer” is the wrong name for immediate queue entry after S1.

**Section 7 does not redeem Section 4.** The script embeds the table, distinguishes statuses, builds an interval frame, calls `format_dataframe`, and labels PM4Py waiting/arrival fields as case-level. It also hard-codes ASAP starts into the PM4Py log (a silent single imputation), hard-codes busy-time arithmetic instead of deriving phase metrics, never prints the Section 4 phase table, and calls `pm4py.convert_log_to_time_intervals` as if that were a settled public API. Those outputs cannot audit the incorrect station table.

The bottleneck story—S1 as a morning arrival-gate, S2 as the slower two-server constraint, maintenance as a shock rather than the only cause—is directionally defensible. It is not earned by the phase metrics, peaks, or utilization path used to argue it. For a required reconstruction exercise, that is a central failure, not a rounding footnote.
