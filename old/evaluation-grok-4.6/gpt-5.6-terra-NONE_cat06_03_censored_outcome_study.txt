**7.2**

The write-up is one of the stronger designs this prompt can produce: it refuses the lender’s “every application / rejected = non-default” framing, separates selection from outcome, and mostly keeps an as-of discipline. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading there are real estimand slips, a maturity rule that fights itself, a temporal scheme that does not do what it claims for the migration test, and a skeleton whose own leakage assert cannot pass.

## What is actually good

The primary target is the only honest binary target available in this warehouse: 90-day default **after activation**, among cases with a mature observed feed. Rejected, withdrawn, open, transferred, and immature activated cases are not coded as \(Y=0\). The multi-state alternative and the labeled counterfactual \(Y^\pi\) are the right contrast set, and the untestable list in §3 (potential outcomes under activation, MNAR feeds, unlinked transfers) is correct.

The cohort arithmetic does reconcile to 48,000. The leakage table in §6 hits every mandated field with the right keep/repair/drop calls. Random splits are rejected for the right reasons (policy, migration, maturity, transfer twins). Deployment feedback in §8 (acceptance policy, intervention, routing) is the right boundary language.

## Target and estimand are not as clean as they look

Section 1 defines the **population** as applications that “would eventually be activated” and the **estimand** as

\[
P(Y=1 \mid X_t,\ A=1,\ \text{outcome observed and mature}).
\]

That is three different objects glued together.

- “Would be activated” is a potential-outcome statement. At prefix time \(A\) is unknown. The training sample is cases with **observed** \(A=1\), not the operational scoring population (all in-process files). The write-up later admits the model is an activated-account association, but the population sentence still reads as if the deployment cohort is identifiable as-of \(t\). It is not.
- Conditioning on “outcome observed and mature” (\(R=1\)) makes the estimand the **observation process**, not default risk among activated accounts. Section 3 then writes the assumption \(P(Y \mid X, A=1, R=1) \approx P(Y \mid X, A=1)\). That is the actual estimand they want. Section 1 already conditions it away. This is not cosmetic: partner-originated MNAR is the one missingness mechanism the prompt flags, and folding \(R=1\) into the scientific target hides it.
- Prediction time is a menu (first Credit Review **or** \(k \in \{3,5,7\}\)), not a single protocol. Reporting “separately by prefix” does not fix that the unit of analysis, the at-risk set, and the meaning of “early” change with the menu.

Complete-case restriction to the 21,600 is defensible **only** as an estimator of the selected estimand. Making it the **primary** analysis, with IPW/bounds as mere sensitivities, is the weakest choice they themselves justify. Given partner MNAR, complete-case is the analysis most likely to look well-calibrated and be wrong.

## Cohort and censoring: lumped buckets, conservative-and-inconsistent maturity

The flow table is a status inventory, not a cohort flow. It never shows: started → has prefix before decision → still open at \(t\) → later activated → mature label. The 3,600 “not yet mature” row collapses three mechanisms the rest of the memo insists on separating: 90 days not elapsed, feed delayed, feed permanently missing. No count was given for permanent missing, but the code path still cannot distinguish them.

Maturity is stated twice and implemented a third way.

- Prose (preferred): `activation + 90 ≤ C` **and** `label_available_at ≤ C`.
- Conservative fallback: `activation + 90 + 35 ≤ C`.
- Code: **both** `activation + 125 ≤ C` **and** a non-missing label.

The AND of max delay and `label_available_at` discards activated cases whose feed already arrived inside the 7–35 day window. With extraction on 15 Feb 2026, that is most of late 2025. Combined with the Test-2 start window (below), the design talks about a migration test it has almost no mature labels for.

Defaults recorded on day 40 are still forced to wait 125 days. Harmless but sloppy; the 90-day **non-default** horizon and the **default event time** are not the same maturity problem.

## Prefixes and features: the leakage hole the table does not close

§4 is mostly right (no padding, keep genuine rework, normalize names, do not collapse semantic changes). Two gaps matter.

1. Post-decision activities are removed by a **closed name list**. Any post-activation event not in `{Accepted, Rejected, …, Default Recorded}` remains in the stream and can enter a \(k\)-event prefix. The feature join never requires `prefix_time < activation_time`. That is exactly the leakage the memo says it prevents.
2. Dedup is “exact key match” in code and “narrow tolerance” in prose. Migration duplicates with jittered timestamps survive.

§5 is a reasonable feature menu, but “historical approval/default rates by channel/region/resource” is target leakage unless the window is strictly prior and excludes the index case. The caveat “training-only” is one sentence; the code never builds it. `case:requested_amount` is discussed and then omitted from the skeleton.

## Validation: the split does not isolate what it names

Start-date bins:

- train 2024
- valid Jan–Jun 2025 (pre-policy **starts**)
- test 1 Jul–14 Oct 2025
- test 2 15 Oct 2025+

Problems:

- Policy is applied at **decision**, not at start. A May 2025 start decided in August is a post-policy case sitting in the “pre-policy” validation fold.
- Migration hits **in-flight** traces. August starts that cross 15 Oct 2025 are scored as “policy-shift,” not “migration.” Test 2 is almost empty under a 90+35 maturity rule at 15 Feb 2026 (an Oct 15 activation matures ~17 Feb). The memo notes late-2025 immaturity and then still presents Test 2 as the migration evaluation.
- There is no same-regime validation slice for a model that will be used after 1 Jul 2025. Tuning on pre-policy data and then announcing post-policy AUC is a drift **detector**, not a model-selection design. Rolling-origin is mentioned, not specified (cut dates, which labels are frozen, how prefixes that straddle a cutoff are handled).
- Transfer twins are “grouped” in one sentence; no rule for which ID keeps the start date, or what happens to the 40% unlinked.
- Required optional mention of `pm4py.split_train_test` as a **bad** baseline is absent.

Metrics for the chosen binary target are fine (PR-AUC at 12.5% prevalence is the right flag). Calibration-by-subgroup is listed without any sample-size or multiplicity caution beyond a clause on ECE.

## Implementation skeleton: fails its own audit

Approximate length ~230+ lines against an 80–160 request. That would be forgivable if the extra lines were correct. They are not.

**The leakage assert is dead on arrival.** `features` is `primary_cases` merged with prefix times. `primary_cases` contains `activation_time`, `terminal_time`, `rejected_time`, `withdrawn_time`, `transferred_time`. Those names are in `FORBIDDEN_MODEL_COLUMNS`. `assert not present_forbidden` raises every run. `default_90d_label` is **not** in the forbidden set, so the actual label column would have survived even if the assert were rewritten — the check is both crashing and incomplete.

**`risk_score_last_prefix` is not as-of last.** After `merge`, row order is not timestamp order. `groupby.agg(..., "last")` is last in memory, not last in time.

**`get_prefixes_from_log` is theatre.** The object is unused. Feature construction is a manual `cumcount`. Depending on PM4Py version the call may also expect an `EventLog`, not a DataFrame — they note version risk and then do nothing with the result.

**`discover_dfg` / `get_variants` on the full untruncated `log_df`** includes Contract Signed, Account Activated, Collections, Default Recorded, and future test cases. Unused variables do not make this a justified API use. It is the opposite of the training-only reference rule in §5.

Other concrete defects:

- No `prefix_time < activation_time` guard (see above).
- Maturity in code is 90+35 **and** label present, contradicting §2.
- `int(r["default_90d_label"])` will throw if a non-numeric or NaN slips the filter.
- `pd.Series([...], dtype="datetime64[ns, UTC]")` is brittle once `format_dataframe` has or has not stripped tz.
- `safe.get("risk_score")` is not a safe missing-column pattern.
- Channel/region taken from `iloc[0]` of the **full** case group, i.e. first row after whatever sort `format_dataframe` applied — usually start, not guaranteed if static attributes were backfilled later.
- Partner-missing and transfer-linkage strategies from §3 are comments, not code paths.
- Elapsed-time prefixes from §4 are absent.
- `documents_total` is audited in §6 and never touched.

The comments about connecting a survival library later are appropriate; inventing a PM4Py survival API is avoided. That is the floor, not a virtue large enough to offset a self-failing audit.

## Writing and small factual nits

“Counts sum to 48,300” immediately followed by the correct 48,000 sum is careless. The activity map is three toy aliases for a migration that “renamed several activities.” Resource experience is handled well in prose and is not in the CSV schema, so the code omission is acceptable; presenting the skeleton as covering the leakage audit is not.

## Strict scoring

Design sections are roughly 8s with recurring precision problems (estimand, population at \(t\), maturity, validation folds). The skeleton is a mid-5: it demonstrates the right libraries in the wrong order on the wrong population and then asserts a property the table violates. Averaging those under the instruction to treat even minor issues as expensive lands at **7.2**, not in the 9s. A near-flawless answer would have a single operational prediction time, an estimand that does not condition on \(R\), a maturity rule that matches the code, a temporal backtest that can actually contain mature post-migration labels, prefixes clipped by activation, and a feature table that passes the audit it prints.
