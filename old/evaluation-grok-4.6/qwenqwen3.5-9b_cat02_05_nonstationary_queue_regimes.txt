**4.0**

The write-up has the required eight headings, the right high-level caution (medians are not flow times; outage recovery is not a steady high-throughput regime; daily rows are not independent), and a genuinely different weekday/weekend alternative. That is not enough. Under a strict reading the analysis is empirically unreliable: several headline numbers contradict the table, the primary regimes mis-group the data, Little’s Law arithmetic does not match the stated formula, and the Python block does not do what the spec requires.

**Empirical errors that break the argument**

Pooled “all 28 days” figures are not pooled. Arrivals average about **93.5**, not 104; completions about **86**, not 99; FTE about **5.14**, not 6.0; `complex_pct` about **32.9%**, not 38%. Those look like weekday-ish guesses relabeled as full-sample means. Section 4’s three “pooling obscures X” stories therefore rest on the wrong baseline, including the claim that pooled arrivals (104) sit near a weekday mean of 108 (weekday arrivals are about **113**).

The case-mix break is dated to **2026-04-16** and a jump “25% → 33%.” The table is 25, **27**, 25, 24, 23, then **41** on 04-20. April 16–19 is still the mid-20s, overlapping the pre-outage drift. Treating 04-16 as a mix-shift day **confounds outage recovery with case mix**, which is exactly the separation the quality bar asks for.

Primary segmentation is internally inconsistent:

- **04-25–04-28** is called “Stable Baseline” with “low `complex_pct` (18–26%).” Those days are 40–47% complex, medians 35–36, and Mon–Tue WIP **49–50**. They belong with the stress regime, not baseline.
- **04-17–04-19** are omitted entirely, even though 04-17 is still recovery (112 completed vs 99 arrivals, WIP 35, FTE 6.5).
- Date ranges include weekends while the numeric estimates (~100, ~115, ~118) are weekday-only. A range and its estimates must match.

**Little’s Law**

The matrix structure and the “not defensible / approximate only” verdicts are reasonable. The numbers are not. Stated formula is \(W=L/\lambda\). With their own pooled inputs that is \(29/104=0.279\) days (~6.7 calendar hours, or ~3.3 opening hours if \(\lambda\) is per open hour), **not 0.99 days (23.8 h)**. Recovery \(52/129=0.40\) days, not 0.86. Baseline 0.13 days is close to using the *minimum* WIP, not the window mean. Units slide between 12-hour opening time and 24-hour calendar time without a consistent throughput definition. Computing \(W\) on windows they mark non-defensible is acceptable if labeled; publishing values that contradict \(L/\lambda\) is not.

**Burstiness and dependence**

Overdispersion relative to Poisson is directionally right; the Fano numbers are not. For 04-07, mean is \(101/12\approx8.42\), variance ~25 (population) / ~27 (sample), Fano about **3**, not mean 9, variance 45, Fano 5. For 04-21, Fano is about **9–10**, not 18. Coefficient of variation is required and essentially absent. Lag-1 dependence is asserted from five visually trending WIP days rather than a computed series diagnostic (weekends would matter). The 04-21 vector is misread: the spike is the **10–11** bin (34), not “08:00–09:00 (8).” A careful audit would also have flagged that this vector cannot produce `peak_08_10_pct=47` (08:00–09:59 is \(3+8=11\) arrivals, ~10%). That data conflict is ignored.

**Other process/queueing slips**

Inventory identity is stated as arrivals − completed + start − end, which is identically ~0 under conservation; net change is arrivals − completed. “1 FTE ≈ 1 case/hour” fights the 24–36 minute service times (~1.7–2.5 cases/hour if busy). Capacity pressure is never bounded (e.g., load \(\approx\) arrivals × service / (FTE × 720 minutes)). Simpson’s paradox is named, not shown—no reversal is computed. Utilization, demand, mix, capacity, and backlog are discussed in prose but not kept apart in the regime cut.

**Section 8**

The code embeds mostly the right arrival/completion series (so it would *print* ~93.5 / ~86 and contradict §4). `complex_pct` is still wrong (04-08 is 18, not 20; 04-20 is 41, not 42). `DataFrame.from_dict(..., columns=['arrivals_by_hour'])` does not fit 12-length lists and would not run as written. There is **no** change-point/segmentation heuristic—only hardcoded date masks. Only pooled \(L/\lambda\) is computed, not the §6 windows. A second dispersion measure (CV, etc.) is missing. Prints are not compact comparison tables. PM4Py comments are the one spec item done correctly.

**What remains creditworthy**

Format compliance; refusal to substitute median service for sojourn; outage/recovery singled out as transient; carry-over and overnight closure noted; weekday/weekend alternative is a real competing story; Little’s Law applicability is not claimed for the pooled series; four hourly snapshots are not oversold as a full arrival process.

Those are the bones of a competent answer. The flesh—the change point, the regime membership, the pooled statistics, the Fano and \(W\) calculations, and the required reproducible analysis—is too often wrong or unimplemented to score higher than low-mediocre under a strict bar.
