**4.8**

The answer has the right skeleton and catches the crude-versus-stratified reversal, but it is not a reliable causal or quantitative analysis. Under a strict reading it fails several required distinctions, misstates the data in material ways, and ships non-runnable diagnostics.

**Causal verdict is conceptually confused.** The headline pattern is compositional: Orion is slower in aggregate (21.96 vs 15.24) and faster in every complexity stratum. That is primarily evidence that the crude Orion penalty is confounded by case mix and the routing rule, not that assignment to Orion “partly causes” longer times through mechanisms. Complexity is fixed at Classify, before routing, so it is a confounder (and a driver of compliance), not a mediator of `resource_group`. Night coverage and compliance are better treated as a treatment bundle / policy descendants of routing, not as proof of a harmful Orion effect. Confidence 65 is too high given n=2 cells, almost no Orion-Day overlap, and completed-case selection. `mainly confounded` or `not identifiable from the supplied evidence` would have been more disciplined. The required split among total, direct, and mediated estimands is named but never defined.

**Internal contradiction in the “strongest evidence.”** The memo says Orion has “longer queue waits” and then cites high-complexity waits of 19.47 vs 22.75 — those are *shorter*. Overall Orion waits are longer only because of mix. That error is fatal to the mediation story as written.

**Material arithmetic and probability errors.** Night is 17 cases (~21.7h), day is 13 (~14.0h), not “19.5h (n=12) vs 14.5h (n=18).” Compliance-required is 13 cases (~26.8h) vs 17 (~12.0h), not “23.5h (n=12) vs 10.0h (n=18).” Orion mean wait is ~12.8h, not 11.5h. Day is ~92% Nova, not 75%. The text also confuses \(P(\text{night}\mid\text{Orion})=92.9\%\) with \(P(\text{Orion}\mid\text{night})\). The wait/service ratios are then used to claim Orion is “queue-driven” even though the (already wrong) ratios are almost identical. High-complexity service 9.17 vs 9.3 is presented as skill evidence; that is noise.

**DAG and role table have several wrong arrows/roles.** `resource_group → compliance_required` contradicts the policy fact that compliance is required by case type. `shift → arrival_load` reverses the likely time-of-day structure; arrival load is an intake condition, not a consequence of the assigned group’s shift. `shift → queue_wait` is missing despite being central to the narrative. `complexity` as mediator of the group effect is temporally impossible. Calling `shift` a collider is inconsistent with the author’s own chain `resource_group → shift → arrival_load`. `case_completed → quality_outcome` treats a selection filter as a causal parent of quality. Arrival load is barely analyzed despite being required.

**Section 4 overclaims identification.** “Orion may reduce cycle time” is not allowed from 6 vs 2 high-complexity cases. Stratification is treated as almost identifying, with unobserved risk mentioned only as a residual caveat — the opposite of the requested discipline.

**Hypotheses and tests are uneven.** The set covers the required themes, but rank 1 (Orion delays mediated by compliance/night) is already in tension with the within-stratum reversal. There is no clean “Orion is genuinely slower” hypothesis. The appeal-rate “negative control” is not a negative control: appeals are a downstream outcome, not a variable that the confounder should affect and the treatment should not. “Same reviewer on Orion vs Nova cases” is mostly infeasible if reviewers *are* the groups. Overlap/positivity is not used as a first-class falsification test even though several cells have 0–2 cases.

**Intervention plan is safer than 50% reassignment, but incoherent in places.** Stratifying a group-randomization pilot on `shift` fights the operating model (Orion *is* the night cover). The memo understates the central operational implication: if the stratified pattern is even approximately real, moving half of Orion’s hard/night work onto Nova is more likely to backfire than to harvest the crude gap. Arbitrary ±10–20% stop rules are untethered from baseline variance (one extra appeal already swings the quality metric).

**Section 7 would not execute and does not meet the spec.** `value_counts` on lists is a TypeError. `pm4py.convert_to_event_log` on an unmapped dataframe with an ad-hoc `events` column is not a valid event log; the prompt’s `parse_event_log_string` path is ignored. The “standardized contrast” computes only a Nova-weighted Orion mean and never reports the contrast to Nova (~12.9 vs 15.2, about −2.3h). Warnings are generic rather than comparison-specific. DFGs are not actually comparable process diagnostics.

**Format nits.** Section 1 overruns the 180–300 word cap and spends that space on a self-contradictory mediation claim. Several required process factors are only gestured at.

What remains creditworthy is structural completeness, detection of Simpson’s reversal, attention to selection and the group-as-bundle problem, and a phased plan that refuses blind 50% reassignment. That is a mid-stack draft, not a committee-ready causal analysis.
