**6.4**

The answer follows the required seven-heading structure, respects the constraints (no default verdict, East treated as a distinct case, fairness/learning included, no unmonitored full rollout), and is analytically stronger than a generic shared-services writeup. The utilization math, the East-vs-North/South split, and the warning about averages are the best parts. Under a strict reading it is still not close to flawless.

**Mechanism map.** This is a list of effects, not a map from the six restructuring actions to process-mining observables. “Nominal handover reduction” is handled loosely: management’s own table *increases* caseworker→specialist and caseworker→approval frequencies (36→41, 42→49) and mainly cuts cross-team cover (6→3). That contradiction is not stated clearly. Resilience is mentioned, but the single-point-of-failure vs. better-absence-cover tradeoff is not tied to a concrete log measure (e.g., wait after `handover_reason` = absence, or concentration of `org:resource` on the shared queue).

**Scenario forecasts.** The three-way split and required dimensions are present. The central and adverse stories are coherent. The optimistic case is thin and mostly restates management’s table. Several numbers (6.5h / 12h Monday waits, 28-day tails, 2% rework) are asserted without linking back to the given wait, utilization, or volume figures. Fairness and learning appear, but East’s improvement vs. North/South degradation is not quantified scenario-by-scenario.

**Indicators.** The leading/lagging split is sensible and uses real log fields. Gaps: no handover-network or performance-DFG metrics; fairness is not operationalized (e.g., team- or segment-level cycle-time or SLA gaps); `handover_reason`, `priority`, and `local_expertise_flag` are underused. SLA misses are later treated as `outcome == 'SLA_Miss'`, which is not in the stated schema.

**Hidden risks.** Six items cover the requested themes, but tail latency and high-complexity cases are collapsed into one bullet. Resilience/exception paths are only partly separated. The masking logic is generally sound.

**Pilot design (largest flaw).** Making East the only pilot team cannot test the *proposed* mechanism. The redesign is a three-specialist pool plus shared legal/risk/approval queues. An East-only pilot either (a) is just East’s old specialist behind a new queue name, or (b) quietly borrows North/South capacity and contaminates the controls. Staffing, whether legal/risk and supervisor centralization are in scope, and how North/South remain comparable are unspecified. Guardrails are also tight relative to noise (90th percentile 22.8d → 25d). Eight weeks will not show learning or expertise loss, which the rest of the memo treats as first-order.

**Recommendation.** “Pilot first” matches the brief, and the warning about early averages is correct. It does not say what would justify proceed vs. modify vs. stop after the pilot, and it does not entertain the obvious modification (overflow-only pooling, keep North/South embeds). “Benefits are heavily outweighed by queuing risks” is a stronger prior than the evidence supports.

**PM4Py sketch.** It names most requested APIs and is usable as a sketch. It does not actually filter by `required_specialty` or `customer_segment`; it omits `discover_dfg` and `filter_variants`; it never builds the North/South control comparison the pilot depends on; it does not compare handover-network edge weights or waits. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` then `get_service_time` is redundant/confused. Networks are computed and then unused.

Net: complete and on-brief, with one structural hole in the pilot and several missing required analysis pieces. Not a 7+ under a strict bar.
