**7.8**

This is a conceptually strong SLA/survival write-up that does the hard things well: estimands before estimators, competing events vs right-censoring vs already-observed 72h facts, transfer as a selected path rather than casual dropout, and a real leakage/immortal-time discussion. It is not nearly flawless. Several encoding, sensitivity, and spec issues are precise enough to keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually strong.** Time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72h, and the 5 under-horizon opens (S10, S15, S24, S29, S31) are identified correctly. Estimand A as the resolve CIF \(F_R(72)\) and Estimand B as residual internal occupancy are operationally distinct and usable. The four worked examples (S03, S09, S06, S08) are encoded the right way under the primary reading. The identified bounds \([9/32, 14/32]\) for both \(\theta_A\) and \(\theta_B\) match the table. The completed-case \(4/13\) vs \(18/27\) contrast is the right attack on survivor bias, and the S13-vs-S11 and S24-vs-S26 contrasts are exact. P1 accounting (\(3/8\) transfer, \(2/8\) late-open, \(2/8\) on-time, \(1\) unknown) is correct. Landmarking as the process-aware secondary method, and the refusal to put `escalated` / `rework_count` in a from-submit model, are the right design choices for these data.

**Material flaws.**

1. **T-fail is not a distinct 72h analysis.** In the code, T-fail only relabels transfer as cause 5 and recodes *late* resolves (all \(t>72\)) as cause 5. Risk-set evolution through hour 72 is identical to T-compete, so \(\hat F_R(72)\) and \(\hat S(72)\) do not change. The “three transfer treatments” therefore collapse to two for both advertised estimands. The printed `bound_theta_A_*` columns make this worse: they are computed from raw `observed_status`, so under T-censor they still treat S08/S21 as known non-resolves. Section 2 says those two cases become unknown; Section 8’s bounds ignore that. That is an internal inconsistency, not a rounding quibble.

2. **T-censor \(\theta_B\) is mislabeled.** Estimand B is internal occupancy; transfer ends internal observation. After recoding transfer to censoring, `theta_B_S_all` is no longer \(\Pr(T_{\mathrm{term}}>72)\). The comment admits this, then still prints it as \(\theta_B\). A sensitivity that changes the estimand while keeping the name is worse than a missing sensitivity.

3. **Requirement 6 is violated.** The PM4Py path invents `1970-01-01 00:00:{i:02d}` timestamps. Calling them “ordinal ranks” does not make them supplied event times. The survival analysis does not use those clocks, and an `EventLog` fallback exists, so this is not fatal — but it is a direct miss of an explicit constraint.

4. **The 27-case “known bit” silently assumes T-compete / A3.** Section 3 states that 18/27 cases with a known 72h internal-resolution bit are non-resolves. Under T-censor that denominator is 25 and S08/S21 are unknown. The assumption is labeled in Section 1 and then treated as a fact when arguing bias. That overstates how much of the table is identified.

**Smaller process and design nits.**

- The multi-state diagram includes `AutoCheck --> Resolve` as a “rare short path.” No supplied prefix contains that edge. Control-flow proposals should not add unobserved transitions without marking them as hypothetical (as was done correctly for `Stalled`).
- Landmarking is described carefully, then `usable_escalated` is still filled from the full prefix for every case with \(t>48\). The text says escalate-vs-48 is unidentified; the table treats it as a usable 48h covariate anyway.
- Fast reject (S05, S23) is discussed as possibly desirable, but Estimand A still folds every reject into \(1-\theta_A\). That is a defensible choice only if the SLA is intake-level internal resolve; the operational section later admits an 80% target is then unattainable. The estimand/decision mapping should have split “SLA breach” from “not resolved because correctly screened out” more cleanly, rather than leaving that to Section 7.
- The AJ helper contains dead snapshot logic (`if ut in want or ut >= max(want): pass`). Harmless, but it is the function that supposedly grounds the numerical claims.

**What does not get extra credit.** Depth and case-ID density are high, and the format matches the required nine headings with a single code block. That is the assignment being done properly, not a reason to overlook a no-op sensitivity, bounds that do not follow the transfer assumptions, and fabricated timestamps. A nearly flawless answer would have made T-censor widen the identified interval, made T-fail a real composite-at-72 (or a different named estimand), and built the PM4Py log from sequence indices only.
