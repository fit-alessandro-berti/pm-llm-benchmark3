**6.3**

The answer is conceptually strong on transportability and correctly refuses a naive copy of Site A, but it is not close to flawless. Required headings are present and the causal story is mostly right; quantitative scenario analysis and the Site B model—the two axes the prompt weights hardest—contain definite errors, internal contradictions, and a few silent assumptions treated as facts.

## What is actually good

Site A’s 9h median movement is not treated as an identified treatment effect. Co-interventions (API, +2 FTE, huddles, mix, timestamp capture) are named, and transport confidence is kept low (mostly 15–30). The core design change is right: distinguish task *creation* from lawful *execution*; do not start Document Check before Originals Receipt; run Identity during the originals wait; restrict true AND-concurrency to digital cases; protect Assessment/Legal with WIP and release throttling. The bottleneck-shift scenario (median down, p90 up) is present, as required. The T1/T2/C logic for separating implementation failure from mechanism failure is the best part of the write-up. The assumption register is usable.

## Section-by-section defects

**1. Process-structural comparison.** All twelve mandated features appear, and the extra co-intervention / rollout rows are useful. Two rework rows overlap. Mix and tail dump before/after into the “before” cell and leave “after” blank. Several cells lost operators (`+`, `→`, `≠`), so “Task **creation** task **executability**” and “Identity Document” have to be inferred. That is sloppy on a table whose job is structural precision.

**2. Transportability graph.** Required nodes are all there, and the 255-word gloss is inside the 180–280 window. Two graph problems: (i) `S_measurement -> true_overlap -> waiting_before_assessment` puts a recording artifact on the *causal* path to cycle time, so measurement bias is modeled as if it shortens waits; (ii) “the chain is structurally identical at both sites” is immediately qualified, but the graph still has `treatment_parallelization -> true_overlap` unconditional on `originals_required`. Shared vs site-specific vs modifiers is otherwise well judged, and the point that isomorphic control-flow is not transport is correct.

**3. Evidence transfer table.** Complete coverage of metrics and co-interventions; the “not identified” stance on the 9h is explicit. Confidence scores are invented but directionally sensible. The “theoretical bound ~3h” is the right *kind* of object (an upper bound from `min(service)`, not an estimate of A’s effect). It is not carried consistently into later sections.

**4. Effect modifiers.** Ten-plus rows, all mandated constraints covered, mitigations mostly operational. Several “beneficial signature” cells are empty or “Minimal,” which hides whether the modifier can help at all. Originals-delay burstiness and the missing WIP cap are good extras.

**5. Quantitative scenarios — material failures.** This section does not survive strict checking.

- Mix shift “8pp × ~25h ≈ 2h” treats the **median as a linear mixture** and invents the 25h complexity gap. Medians do not decompose that way.
- Identity 0.4h plus document 0.7h is added as if both service cuts sit on the post-parallel critical path. After AND-join, only `max(id, doc)` does.
- The site-wide ceiling is computed as `0.38×3.4 + 0.62×2 ≈ 2.6h`, then immediately inflated to “2 to 4h,” then **S1 is 3 to 5h — above the author’s own ceiling**.
- S2 treated-stratum **3 to 6h exceeds `min(3.4, 8.1) = 3.4h`**, the bound they just used at A (3.2h). Handoff/queue slack could justify a little more; 6h is not justified.
- S2 eligible share is **38% for “no originals *and* no legal.”** Digital is 38%; legal is 40%. That 38% is valid only if legal ⊆ originals, which is never stated or tested against the DFG. Independent intersection is ~23%. Section 9 then says “35–38% digital, non-legal,” baking in the same hidden assumption.
- S3 p90 “2 to +6h” has an ambiguous sign next to rows that mark worsening with `+`.
- DFG structure is underused: `Identity Check -> Originals Receipt` (1,020) vs `Register -> Originals` (1,860) vs `Register -> Identity` (2,050) shows identity is *not* already overlapping the originals wait for everyone; the “1–3h handoff only” originals effect may be low.

S4 (mean better, tail worse) and S5 (lock failure) are the right qualitative scenarios; the numbers attached to them are not trustworthy.

**6. Candidate intervention.** Eligibility, statutory guards, pending-vs-released queues, supplement priority, and server-side logging are the correct modification of the proposal. The textual POWL set includes `Assessment → Outcome`. Symbols for `≺`/`‖` were stripped, so the spec is unreadable without the comments; the comment on identity vs originals is needed to know they meant concurrency, not precedence. If that line were precedence, digital cases would be serialized and originals receipt could not start until identity finished — the opposite of the design.

**7. Pilot.** Design elements are all named. Word limit is **~238 > 220**. “Indicting” for “indicating.” Power arithmetic is wrong: 3,400/6 ≈ 567 cases/month, S1 at 38% ≈ 215/month; 10 weeks is ~2.3 months → ~250 per arm in a **two**-arm split, not “430 per arm.” A three-arm T1/T2/C split makes this worse, and that is not in the power line. Duration is 12 weeks in one row and 10 weeks in another. Individual-case randomization, day-cluster randomization, and reviewer pods are stacked; 3 assessors and 2 legal reviewers cannot be podded, so downstream SUTVA is not controlled. The *verbal* account of mechanism vs implementation vs absorption is clear and would have been enough without the broken sample-size claim.

**8. PM4Py — model fidelity miss.** Site A trees, `parse_process_tree`, `convert_to_petri_net`, POWL imports, LOOP with two children, explicit `identity → assessment` and `document → assessment`, soundness/workflow checks, playout, and the limitations comment all match the prompt. The Site B net is still **wrong**: `outcome` is in the root partial order with **no** `assessment → outcome` edge, so Legal Review / Approve / Reject / Notify are minimal activities and can fire at case start, concurrent with Register. That contradicts both the candidate spec and Site B rules 5–8. Duplicate `Approve`/`Reject`/`Notify` transitions are fine; `Document Check (re)` as a distinct label is a smaller fidelity hit. `play_out` on an unbounded LOOP with no `no_traces`/length cap is fragile. They never mention running the code; playout of this net would have shown the bug.

**9. Recommendation.** `conditional go` is the only defensible choice. Transfer the mechanism, not the 9h or blanket AND-split; start with digital non-legal cases; expand/modify/abandon triggers are specific. Word count is in band. It inherits the 35–38% and 3–6h overstatements, so the operational recommendation is slightly too bullish relative to the author’s own ceiling.

**10. Assumptions.** Exactly eight, correct template, right content (creation vs execution, statutory opinion, identity data independence, downstream slack, timestamps, A’s overlap not pure artifact, mix stability, floaters). No issue here beyond not listing the legal⊆originals share assumption they actually used.

## Scoring rationale (strict)

A near-flawless response on this rubric would need: (a) internally consistent bounds, (b) an executable POWL net that preserves Assessment-before-decision and the originals gate, (c) an eligible-share calculation that intersects originals and legal without a hidden inclusion, (d) a pilot N that follows from 3,400/6 months. This answer fails (a)–(d) while succeeding on analogical structure, confounding, legal non-start, bottleneck/interference, and segmented learning. Under a rule that minor defects move the score a lot and high scores are reserved for near-flawless work, that is a mid-6, not a 7–8.
