**4.0**

The write-up is complete and correctly recognizes that the control flow is not block-structured, but the two models and the witness table do not support the claims made about them. Several “exact” statements are false, and one central encoding error is treated as an unavoidable limit of process trees.

**Process tree — wrong loop, likely invalid API, coarser than necessary**

The intended trial cycle is the standard do/redo form: always `Execute Trial` then `Inspect Trial`, and `Adjust Parameters` only as the redo. In PM4Py that is `LOOP(SEQ(Execute Trial, Inspect Trial), Adjust Parameters)`. That already encodes “repeat until a pass” without data labels: exiting the loop is the pass; the redo child is the fail path. Putting `OPT(Adjust Parameters)` inside the body, then looping with `SKIP`, is not a forced approximation. It admits adjustment after the last inspection and admits a new cycle with no adjustment. Calling this “necessary representational loss since process trees lack data-dependent loop guards” is incorrect.

`Operator.OPTIONAL` is not a PM4Py process-tree operator. The task lists `SEQUENCE`, `XOR`, `PARALLEL`, and `LOOP`. Optional work is `XOR(τ, a)`. `ProcessTree(label="", operator=None)` is also the wrong silent node; a skip should be `label=None`. An empty string can show up as a visible empty activity.

The two-phase skeleton (`Register` → initial `PAR` → second `PAR` of trial/approvals/supplier → `Release` → `Archive`) is a defensible conservative policy, but it is not the most faithful tree. Approvals and supplier are allowed to start after `Analyze Root Cause` and to overlap design and verification planning. This tree delays both until `Plan Verification` and `Design Corrective Action` have finished. That loss is at least disclosed. Parent back-pointers are never set.

**POWL — the distinctive model is not a model of the narrative**

`powl` is a single `StrictPartialOrder` over every visible activity plus two unused silent transitions. Edges force `Supervisor Review`, `Quality Review`, `Regulatory Review`, `Consult Supplier`, and `Adjust Parameters` on every run. There is no implemented XOR, no optional supplier, and no loop (`OperatorPOWL` for risk is allocated and then left disconnected). High-risk is also wrong even as a choice: `XOR(SR, QR, RR)` would pick one review, not `SR` vs `QR ∥ RR`.

`tau_split` and `tau_merge` are nodes of the partial order with no incident edges, so they are free concurrent silent steps. That is worse than omitting them.

The compact POWL text (`IT -> AP (optional)`, `Loop: ET -> IT -> [AP] -> ET`) does not match the code (hard `IT → AP`, no back-edge, no `LOOP`). `Operator` imported from `pm4py.objects.powl.obj` is not the documented import.

A faithful POWL is straightforward: `LOOP` for the trial, `XOR(SR, AND(QR, RR))` for risk, `XOR(τ, CS)` for supplier, then partial-order edges for the cross-branch constraints (`Contain → Plan → trial`, `Analyze → Design → trial`, approvals and optional supplier into `Release`). That is what the task asked for. It was not delivered.

**Witnesses do not match the artifacts**

“Intended verdict” is the narrative verdict. T5 is placed in the five allowed rows but is narratively illegal (adjust after the last inspect). That breaks the required 5+3 split.

POWL verdicts are inconsistent with the constructed DAG:

- T1–T3 cannot be produced if CS, AP, and all three reviews are mandatory.
- T4 cannot be produced: there is only one `Execute Trial` node and no loop.
- F3 is claimed forbidden for POWL because of a “missing cycle”; the code has `IT → AP` and no order between `Supervisor Review` and `AP`, so F3 satisfies the edges.

T5 does show a tree over-approximation, but only because the loop was encoded incorrectly. F1/F2 are reasonable negative traces; F2 is a real cross-branch sync violation.

**Comparison table overclaims fidelity**

Optional supplier, risk XOR, and release synchronization are not “exact” in the POWL. The tree’s approval/trial overlap is not merely a start-time under-approximation; supplier and reviews also cannot overlap planning/design. Duplicate-label risk is correctly avoided.

**Validation and self-critique**

The validation plan lists the right ingredients (net conversion, soundness, playout, replay, silent/duplicate check, limits) but was not executed, so it never would have caught the unused taus, the uncomposed `OperatorPOWL`, or the witness mismatches.

The self-critique is partly honest about POWL composition and the dangling XOR, but it repeats the false claim that repeat-until cannot be expressed in a process tree and never corrects the models or the table.

**Net**

Structured, explicit about some tradeoffs, and conceptually aware that POWL should carry the non-block edges. As executable semantics it fails: illegal/misused operators, a process tree that botches the one construct trees handle well, a POWL that makes every alternative mandatory, and witnesses that do not describe either object. That is well below a strong answer on this rubric.
