**6.6**

The write-up is well structured, format-compliant, and strong on identification language (staggered adoption, intensity vs availability, SUTVA, TWFE contamination, small clusters, CSAT nonresponse). It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, a serious quantitative-interpretation error plus several process and consistency lapses keep it well below the top of the scale.

**The main scientific failure is asymmetric, window-mismatched use of controls.** Section 5 subtracts a June→August rebound in never-treated branches (~−7% to −16% median; the arithmetic is closer to −8% to −17%) from Gamma’s full-pre vs post contrast (−8.3%). Those windows are not comparable: Gamma’s pre-mean is pulled down by low-complexity January–April months, while the control rebound is measured from the May–June peak. That alone makes the “automation-attributable median ≈ −3% to +2%” bound an artifact of mixed windows, not a transparent DID.

Worse, the same secular evidence is not applied even-handedly to Alpha/Beta. Calendar-aligned contrasts (Jan–Mar vs Apr–Aug, or Apr–Jun vs Gamma-as-not-yet-treated) show never-treated and not-yet-treated medians and p90s deteriorating more during the May complexity shock than the April adopters did. A naive control-adjusted reading is therefore a *relative improvement*, not a null. The authors correctly note that complexity, routing, and Alpha’s FTE loss bias raw post-period outcomes toward harm, then ignore that implication when they center the median range on zero. Confounders are used to dismiss Gamma’s improvement and not used to reinterpret Alpha/Beta holding roughly steady under extra load. That is a logical inconsistency, not mere conservatism.

A process-mining assessment should have computed arrivals per FTE. Alpha’s load rises from ~30 cases/FTE in January to ~41–43 in May–June while median cycle time stays near 48–53h. In a queueing system that is not a null; it is the signature of possible capacity creation, which the rubric explicitly cares about. Treating “median did not fall” as “no operational benefit” collapses substitution and capacity into one number and is the largest process-interpretation miss.

**Other quantitative and causal nits**

- Gamma escalation pre-mean is 8.7, not 8.4. Small, but this is the section that claims arithmetic discipline.
- Escalation: March +2pp is org-wide, but adopters add a further ~1pp at switch-on, and the prompt states that automated triage sent more borderline cases to the fraud desk. Section 7’s claim that escalation is “not attributable to automation” overreaches.
- p90 “tail worsening” is the unadjusted Alpha/Beta pre/post (+10%). Controls’ tails rose more over the same calendar window. The mechanisms table notes the ambiguity; the effect table does not.
- Pre-period `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% while `automation=0` is never interpreted (measurement, residual path, or leakage).
- E2 as “CACE-like” is a stretch: there is no instrumented binary compliance contrast, only an endogenous dose slope.
- The “Conditionally causal” ladder item is a hypothetical future design, not a claim supported by these data.

**Identification vs implementation gap**

The primary strategy is a group-time/event-study ATT, with an explicit (correct) warning not to trust naïve TWFE. Section 9 then estimates exactly that TWFE (`median ~ post + controls + branch/month FE`), builds relative-time dummies, and never uses them. Dead event-time indicators plus a model the prose rejected is a real credibility leak, even though the code meets the letter of the spec and the cluster/aggregation warnings are good.

**What still works**

Sections 1–2, 4, 6, and 8 are mostly high quality: availability vs dose, selection on weak units, fraud-desk interference, measurement limits, required estimands (including tail, CSAT, dynamic/heterogeneous), a full mechanism table, ≥10 threats, and a policy that improves identifiability (stepped-wedge of Delta vs Epsilon, freeze routing, case-level and fraud-desk queues, p90/CSAT guardrails). Rejecting “automation reduces cycle time by X%” is the right leadership claim. `expand with an embedded evaluation` is the right discrete choice.

Even that policy block has a self-contradiction: Alpha’s p90 would have tripped the proposed “+15% for two consecutive months” rollback (June +22% vs pre, with May–June both elevated), yet the narrative says there is “no clear customer harm.”

**Net.** Strong causal vocabulary and the correct rollout posture, undermined by control-window mixing, one-sided confounding, missing load/queue analysis, an unused event study, and a few over-precise “indistinguishable from zero” statements. That is a solid mid-pack answer, not a near-flawless one.
