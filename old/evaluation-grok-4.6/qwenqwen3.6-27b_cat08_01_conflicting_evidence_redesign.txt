**6.5**

The write-up is competent, on-format, and clearly above a generic Lean dump: it uses the DFG, performance DFG, conformance rules, rework stats, and segment outcomes; it names the fast-path cost illusion; and A/B/C are at least thematically different. It is not close to flawless. Several evidence mistakes and design inconsistencies are material under a strict reading.

**Diagnosis.** The contradiction is stated well (2,300 dispatch-from-fast cases, 14.1% vs 3.3% complaints, 31% → 9% cost erosion, 72h/96h complaint tails, R1/R2 deviations, 68% fitting traces). Two claims are wrong or sloppy. “Saves ~EUR 30 per case” is invented; the prompt gives only relative percentages. The 34h `Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review` edge is mostly customer wait, not proof that “the manual queue is already impacted.” The 2,550 `Auto → Fast approval` edge is over-read as high-value R1 breach; that edge is mixed-segment. Fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces, low model precision, and the Premium temporal deviation on `Manual → Repair quote` are cited lightly or not interpreted.

**Redesign A.** Segment-aware routing is the right idea (keep Premium low-risk on the R2 exception; pull high-value off blind fast approval). Execution is a combo platter, not a single design: smart router + auto-rule rewrite + pre-dispatch gate + evidence portal. That last piece is Redesign C, so A is not a clean alternative. The gate is aimed at **Standard low-risk**, where wrong-replacement cost is EUR 95 and median fast time is 13h, so a 6–18h gate is a large penalty for a cheap error. The EUR 410 error and “incorrect replacement part selection” tail sit on **high-value dispatch**, which A does not gate. Complexity is labeled a “2-week rule update” while the same design also adds a gate and a portal — internally inconsistent. Forcing high-value to review is treated as a 15–35% manual inflow bump (the prompt’s generic rule-update range) without checking whether that understates a full high-value divert. Saying 20h → 96h for high-value is “acceptable” sits poorly next to Premium “strict service expectations.”

**Redesign B.** Repair-first for Standard high-value is a real second mechanism and uses the 3.3% complaint path. Two evidence errors hurt it. The 72h / “visibility” tolerance is a **Standard low-risk accessory** fact; it is applied to Standard high-value, whose reviewed/repair median is 120h. “Repair provides status updates” is not in the data. The design ignores the actual quote delay (part-price confirmation), the 12.5% high-value repair-rework rate, and spare QC capacity (“underused except after repair campaigns”). Volume math (“~390 → triple”) treats 1,050 segment cases as incremental repairs and ignores that some Standard high-value already repair, abandon, or deny.

**Redesign C.** Narrow and defensible as an evidence/abandonment play (26% repeat requests, 320 abandonments, 140h unclear-defect median, 30–45% portal effect, literacy risk). It is still partly nested inside A. The 30–45% repeat-request cut is blurred with the 34h first-pass wait. Recovery bots are extra invention. Standing alone it correctly admits it does not fix R1/R2 — which makes the A/C overlap more awkward, not less.

**Cross-cutting gaps.** None of the three redesigns really touch the complaint/reopen loop (9.2% of cases), R3 (reopen must pass Manual before close/deny), the escalation board (high CSAT, one daily slot), or `Customer complaint/reopen after close`. Those are first-class PM artifacts, not optional color. Fraud Monday/campaign peaks are almost unused.

**Table, failures, experiments.** Required columns are present and mostly qualitative, as asked. “Dominated?” for B is muddled (B does not put Premium on repair, yet the note says it is dominated on Premium speed). Failure modes are concrete and tied to 82% utilization, 3 technicians, and portal literacy. The experiment plan has the right ingredients (pilot, metrics, stop/go, downstream checks) but treats A→C→B as a stack to implement rather than a choice among alternatives, and Pilot 1 (10% of Standard high-value) does not test A’s distinctive low-risk gate.

Net: a strong structured draft with real PM texture, undermined by one fabricated cost figure, one clear segment mix-up, a misplaced quality gate, A subsuming C, and several unused conformance/rework artifacts that should have changed the designs. That is well short of near-flawless.
