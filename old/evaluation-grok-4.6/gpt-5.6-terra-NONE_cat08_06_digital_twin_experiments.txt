**7.1**

This is a strong, well-structured design note that hits the required section contract and gets the big modeling judgments right: DES rather than replay, queues rather than sampled waits, calendars as first-class constraints, objections as latent rather than observed, service/wait not collapsed, and no false claim of optimality. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless. The architecture and the executable skeleton diverge on the exact points the prompt forbids treating casually.

**What is actually good.** Scope, entities, and state are specific enough to build from. Cross-case structure (architect bursts, district bundles with a max-hold, neighborhood-period latent pressure, shared pools) is real, not a slogan. Parameter estimation correctly refuses to treat inter-event elapsed time as service, routes waiting through contention, and keeps PM4Py in the log/model lane. Assumptions are partitioned as requested. The experiment table is better than the minimum: B0, D1–D10, interaction packages, and six stresses, with paired streams and incremental contrasts. Uncertainty treatment names the right objects (CRN, parameter outer loop, tail summaries, censoring in the *historical* log). Validation is staged and does not rest on mean lead time. Decision rules are multi-criteria and explicitly non-optimizing. The Python preface follows the stated conventions: `pandas.read_csv` + `pm4py.format_dataframe`, parse-tree try/except, DFG / performance DFG / arrival average, and an honest NaN service/wait split.

**Material design errors.** A 6-month warm-up is too short for a process whose Complex-variance p90 is 260 days; cyclic year-long warm-up is mentioned and then not used as the primary design. End-of-horizon open cases are dropped in `collect_case_metrics`, which downward-biases tails—the cases the twin is supposed to protect. CRN is prescribed, then undermined: one shared `Generator` is consumed by arrivals, routing, delays, and rework, so D1/D2/D3 change draw counts and desynchronize pairs. The prose seed `hash((scenario_id, replication))` is not stable under Python hash randomization. Peak demand is implemented as `1.7 × overall mean`, not `1.7 × Jan–Feb`, and the same weekday multiplier is applied on weekends, which will invent a large Saturday/Sunday load the log does not support. D7 multiplies Monday λ by 0.80 after commenting that demand must be conserved.

**Process-model and policy gaps.** The tree places public notice *after* the planning/fire/environment AND-block; `next_activities_after_completeness` puts it in parallel with those reviews. The tree is parsed and then never used for routing. D5, D6, and D8 exist only as `PolicyConfig` flags. Monday clerk reservation, Simple-only planners, inspection no-shows, legal urgent-week exceptions, and scheduler capacity are specified in text and absent from the resource construction. `InspectionBundler.release_bundles` and `travel_time_hours` are dead code; aged leftovers are dumped as one bundle.

**The constraint violation that matters most.** The prompt forbids an independent-case twin. The *write-up* is not independent-case. The *run loop* is: a daily Poisson draw, a few independent delay samples, a lognormal completion, always “Issue permit,” no seize/release, no working-time calendar, no queue, no bundle release. Resource pools are built and ignored. That is more than an unfinished stub; it is a different model than Sections 1–5 describe. `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` is also a weaker, version-sensitive choice than `pm4py.get_variants`.

**Net.** Excellent advisory structure and mostly sound stochastic-process reasoning, with enough specification, sequencing, identification, and implementation breaks that it should not be treated as a near-final digital-twin design.
