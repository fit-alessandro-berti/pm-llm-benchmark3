8.4

This is a strong, professionally structured digital-twin design that satisfies the required section contract and the hard constraints (cross-case dependence, calendars, service/wait separation, latent objections, no `pm4py.read_csv`, no optimality claim). It is not nearly flawless. Several unclarities, small inconsistencies, and unresolved modeling ambiguities keep it well below a 9 under a strict reading.

## What is actually good

The core simulation design is the right class of model: calendar-aware discrete-event simulation with shared queues, non-stationary clustered arrivals, latent objection episodes, inspection tours, and an AND-join before decision drafting. The dual (then triple) clocks are the correct way to stop D1/D7 from looking good by pushing delay upstream. Parameter estimation is process-mining literate: PM4Py for log/model/conformance, a separate engine for resources and calendars, historical waiting used as a calibration target rather than resampled on top of simulated queues, and objections treated as latent rather than observed. The experiment matrix, nested uncertainty plan, validation stack, and constrained Pareto decision rules are all in the right methodological family. The skeleton is modular and mostly faithful to the stated PM4Py conventions.

That is the ceiling. The defects below are why it is not a 9 or 10.

## Material defects

**1. Writing error in the first design object.** Section 1 says “Two clocks should be reported” and then lists three. That is not a typo in an appendix; it is the boundary definition. A reader cannot tell whether municipal-controllable time is a required estimand or an optional footnote.

**2. Experiment-matrix notation collides with itself.** S4 says “D3/D5 travel +25%”. In this document D3 and D5 are interventions (batching; legal pre-check) *and* districts with long travel. The trade-off column (“especially in D3/D5”) implies districts, but a required experiment table cannot reuse the same IDs for two different factor systems. That is an unclarity in a deliverable whose job is to be executable by a study team.

**3. FTE versus headcount is unresolved for every part-week pool.** “2 FTE equivalent, Tuesday–Thursday only” is not the same as `capacity=2` on a Tue–Thu calendar. Two people working three days is about 1.2 five-day FTE; two FTE compressed into three days is a different roster. The same issue applies to inspectors (Tue–Fri) and, less severely, to legal blackout weeks. The narrative is careful everywhere else and then silently picks a headcount interpretation in code. That choice changes D4, S2, and every utilization metric.

**4. The resource map does not implement the resource story.** Inspection scheduling has a 3-day median wait and a named `scheduler_1` in the log. The text says to estimate a scheduler pool. The template gives that activity to nobody: clerks, planners, inspectors, legal, and system do not own `Inspection scheduling`. If the skeleton were filled in as written, scheduling is either unschedulable or silently infinite. Likewise, `available_fraction=0.60` is attached to the environmental calendar after the text correctly rejects “just multiply service time,” but `is_open()` never reads `available_fraction`, and Monday `weekday_capacity_override` is also inert. The code therefore contradicts the two calendar rules the design spent the most ink defending.

**5. “Object-centric” is used loosely.** What is specified is a multi-entity DES (cases, architects, objection episodes, tours). That is the right idea. It is not an object-centric process model, not OCEL, and not PM4Py object-centric discovery. Overstating the formalism matters because the log is explicitly flattened and the data gap is exactly the missing object links.

## Section-by-section

**1. Scope.** Entities, state, queues, calendars, and dependencies are mostly complete. Gaps: the two/three-clock error; no explicit state for council-week urgency exceptions; appeals are brought inside the boundary despite unstructured/unreliable log support, with only a general exception-subprocess remark; the normative tree’s `*(completeness, 'Completeness rework')` redo activity is flagged but never given a state-machine mapping that matches P001 (check → request → receive → check, no rework label).

**2. Estimation.** This is the best section. Residual issues: week and architect-week bootstrap are both named without a nesting scheme, so dependence could be double-counted; Hawkes is mentioned for 18 months / 1.5 seasons of data without a complexity warning; building-area and environmental-sensitivity latents are correctly treated as unidentified, but there is no identification strategy beyond “scenario ranges.” The identifiability warning on service effort versus calendar capacity is correct and necessary.

**3. Assumptions.** Cleanly partitioned and mostly honest. The fire/inspector FTE assumption is implicit rather than stated. “No additional cross-case precedence is invented” sits next to generating relatedness from architect+district+week; that is an invented grouping rule and should have been labeled as such more sharply. Missingness-not-at-random is acknowledged, but no concrete MNAR mechanism (e.g., objections missing more often when informal) is specified.

**4. Experiments.** Coverage of B0, D1–D10, packages, and five stresses exceeds the brief. Factor levels for D1/D8/D9 are invented effect sizes presented as design points, which is acceptable only because the preamble says so. The staged design and the “cross B0, D1–D10 with S1” language are in tension: one is a screen, the other is a large crossed design. S4’s label collision is the serious issue. There is no cost or staffing-feasibility factor except a qualitative “cost” column, despite D4/D10 being capacity policies.

**5. Uncertainty.** Methodologically ambitious and mostly right (WIP-initialized implementation estimand vs seasonal long-run, CRN, entity-keyed streams, separate aleatory/epistemic intervals, no p90 truncation). Weaknesses: 100 parameter draws × 20 inner replications × ~20 policies/stresses is asserted as “practical” with no budget, runtime, or reduction design; the 2% / 5% half-width rules are arbitrary relative to decision thresholds; 12-month warm-up is short relative to Complex p90 ≈ 260 days even with the “extend if needed” caveat.

**6. Validation.** The six required modes are all present, with sensible replay/distributional/utilization/bottleneck checks and an explicit warning that a performance DFG is not a service-time proof. The 12-fit / 6-holdout split does not guarantee the holdout contains both peak seasons; rolling-origin is mentioned but not specified tightly enough to fix that. Numeric tolerances (2 pp variants, 10% median, 10–15% p90) are stated without linking them to decision-threshold materiality.

**7. Decision criteria.** Correctly refuses a single optimum and includes fairness, applicant burden, and D2/D8 safety non-inferiority. Many numeric gates (5% median, 1.25 district ratio, 80–90% of parameter draws, 70–85% utilization) are presented as “proposed governance thresholds” but still function as if they were reasoned. Utilization 70–85% is a generic textbook band, not derived from these calendars (fire is inherently idle Mon/Fri). Safety for D2 is correctly called conditional, then the rest of the framework still ranks D10 as if D2 were an ordinary throughput lever.

**8. Code.** Hits the checklist: pandas load, `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree` with a real semantic caveat, variants, DFG, performance DFG, arrival average, timing columns that refuse to mislabel completion-to-completion gaps. Separation from the engine is clean. Failures: scheduler pool absent; `available_fraction` and Monday override unused; system calendar `time(23,59,59)` is exclusive in `is_open`, so the last second of every day is closed; no conformance call despite a `convert_tree_for_conformance` helper; no even-toy event loop, which would have exposed the scheduling-skill hole. API names are plausible. The excerpt-as-demo disclaimer is correct.
