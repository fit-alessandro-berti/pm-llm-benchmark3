**5.0**

The final write-up has the right skeleton and a correct case-level table, but the required aggregate and counterfactual work is sloppy, internally inconsistent, and in places simply wrong. That is disqualifying for a high score under a strict rubric.

**What is actually good.** Section 1 states the right max-based identities: \(T_{join}=\max_i(\mathrm{end}_i)-\mathrm{fork}\), \(T_{total}=T_{up}+T_{join}+T_{down}+S_F\), and a usable explanation that \(\sum_i W_i\) overlaps and is not additive elapsed time. Section 2’s 12-row table and the four worked cases (F02, F04, F07, F11) match the timestamps. Case-level critical branches are right: A on F01/F11, C on F04/F08, B on the other eight. The qualitative ranking “do not treat mean service as influence” is the correct idea. Section 7’s caveats (policy vs queue, unidentified capacity, preemption, \(n=12\)) are appropriate.

**Critical-path frequencies are wrong, and the text contradicts itself.** The comparison table reports B \(9/12\) (75%) and C \(1/12\). The same document’s case table has C critical on **both** F04 and F08, and Section 4 lists four zero-benefit cases for the B-queue lever (F01, F04, F08, F11), which implies B is critical in **8/12**, not 9. Section 3 even names F04 and F08 as C-critical while still printing \(1/12\). That is a core deliverable error, not a rounding issue.

**Almost every aggregate statistic is incorrect.**
- Mean B service is \(37.5\), not \(38.3\); mean C service is \(20.17\), not \(19.5\).
- Mean B queue is \(19.17\), not \(18.8\); mean C queue is \(10.42\), not \(8.6\).
- Mean fork–join time is \(61.0\), not \(56.7\).
- Mean downstream queue is \(176/12=14.67\), not \(14.25\) (and total downstream minutes are \(176\), not \(171\)).
- “Mean sync wait (when not critical)” of \(10.0 / 0.0 / 35.0\) is F02’s case vector, not a mean. Actual non-critical means are about \(13.2 / 13.0 / 36.5\). Reporting B’s non-critical sync wait as \(0\) is incompatible with F01, F04, F08, and F11.

**Counterfactuals do not re-apply the join.** The task required checking cases where another branch remains critical *after* the change. The write-up only gates on the *original* critical branch and then subtracts the full lever.
- A \(-20\%\) on F01: A-path \(50\to40\), B-path stays \(45\), so join falls by **5**, not 10, and criticality moves to B. F11 is a \(40\)–\(40\) tie. Claimed uniform 10-minute savings on both A-critical cases is false.
- B queue \(-50\%\) is likewise not always a full half-queue cut: F05 and F10 become A-bound (savings \(10\) and \(5\), not \(12.5\) and \(10\)). The “\(\approx 85\) minutes” figure uses the wrong critical count and the naive subtraction.
- Downstream\(\to 0\) is treated as if extra F capacity erases every \(T_{down}\) minute. The F1/F2 intervals show a mix of genuine blocking (F05, F08, F09, F11) and residual gaps after the peer resource is free (F07, F10, F12). Section 7 mentions policy vs queue, but Section 4 still sells a uniform 171-minute win.

The Python implements the same naive CFs (`-= 0.20 * A_service` / `-= 0.50 * B_queue` on originally critical rows) and prints `new.sum() - original.sum()` as “Total_savings” (wrong sign).

**The required code would not run and misses the PM4Py spec.**
- `for col in df.columns: df[col] = df[col].apply(to_min)` is applied to `case` and `final_resource` and will throw.
- Critical-branch code (`where` twice) is not the stated A\(\succ\)B\(\succ\)C rule; it overwrites an A-critical row with C whenever \(B<C\). It happens not to fire on this sample.
- The interval log is two rows per case (synthetic `ForkJoinStage` + F), not “one row per branch/final activity.”
- Timestamps are integers from midnight, never datetimes. `pm4py.format_dataframe(..., format="log")` is not the real API (`case_id` / `activity_key` / `timestamp_key`).
- Compact case-decomposition tables are never printed.

**Other nits that still cost points.** Tie-breaking is declared but never exercised; “90th percentile” is asserted without a computation; redesigns repeat the 75% figure; assumptions are labeled but then ignored when numbers are presented as exact.

The piece reads like a competent outline with a correct micro-table and an incorrect summary layer. Under a hypercritical standard—wrong critical counts, fabricated means, non-recomputed counterfactuals, and a non-executable required script—this cannot sit in the upper half of the scale.
