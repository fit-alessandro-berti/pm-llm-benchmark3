The submission is complete, well-structured, and conceptually on-target: it defines two distinct objectives, treats robustness as performance under U1–U5 rather than base-case mean delay, and correctly identifies the specialization–pooling tension, the thin software bench, electrical load, the 16:00 cliff, and Chen-absence as the binding stresses. That is not enough for a high score under a strict bar. The quantitative spine, the policy specifications, and the Python artifact all have defects that would fail an audit.

**Arithmetic in the load analysis is wrong.** Midpoint expectations are not optional decoration; they are the claimed audit trail. Correct values are Intake 6.94, Mechanical 26.30, Electrical 32.82, Software 38.50, Validation 15.74. The write-up reports 6.90, 26.15, 32.49, 37.50, and 15.42. Software is a full minute off; Electrical and Validation are systematically low. Those errors propagate into the 473 / 786 / 488 / 290 / 648 load totals. Block-weighted type counts are also slightly misstated (ME ≈ 8.25 not 8.1; ES ≈ 4.99 not 5.2; E ≈ 11.06 not 10.9). Section 2 then stays mostly qualitative: there is no multiplier-adjusted, time-block capacity-versus-load table, so the “show enough work to audit” requirement is only partly met.

**O1 is mis-scaled.** The objective is sold as SLA-risk, but \(0.01 \times p90\) in minutes dominates the unitless SLA-gap terms (a U5 p90 of ~700 contributes ~7, while a 20-point P1 miss contributes 1.0). Under that formula the ranking can be driven by tail minutes rather than the SLA shortfalls the text emphasizes.

**P-Spec is not one policy.** Eligibility is restated three ways in the same subsection (Ada/Emil barred from Electrical; Ada allowed if the mechanical queue is empty; Ada allowed after a 15-minute wait). Operators could not implement this as written. Hybrid is clearer, but several of its advertised rules are not what the code does.

**The Python block would not run as printed, and the DES does not implement the model it claims.** `@dataclass` followed by `order: bool = True` then `class ActivityExec` is invalid Python; `ActivityExec(...)` therefore has no generated constructor. Even if that line is repaired, material mechanisms are wrong or omitted while the narrative treats the table as simulation-informed:

- `_preempt_rem` is written and never read; victims are re-drawn at full service, so the 10% remaining-time penalty is not in the dynamics.
- Handover and setup advance a local clock and are logged, but they do not occupy the resource in `util_busy` / `busy_until`.
- Parallelization ignores the stated critical-path test and just sets `parallel=True` by priority.
- Hybrid overflow uses *case age since arrival*, not stage wait, so P1 overflow fires almost immediately after Intake.
- If no primary is free, Hybrid silently becomes full pooling, collapsing the contrast with P-Pool.
- Unfinished cases are censored at `DAY_END+120`, which fabricates sojourn times and can still “pass” P2/P3.

Section 4 therefore presents wide ranges “from capacity logic + simulation” without a single executed seed mean. That is a credibility problem: a working scaffold should have populated the table. The PM4Py calls are present and correctly caveated as case-level versus stage-level, which is one of the stronger parts of the artifact, but they sit behind a synthetic fallback log that further suggests the DES was not trusted.

**Smaller consistency issues.** Encoding dropouts (`≤`, `→`) appear throughout. Farah’s role, Ada-on-Electrical, and P-Spec overflow differ between prose and code. U2 is a defensible mix-reweight reading of “demand doubles; proportions renormalized,” but the alternative (doubling E/ES arrival *volume*) is not discussed. Intake/Validation non-preemption is actually in the brief, not merely a tractability assumption.

**What still works.** Assumptions are labeled. Parallel versus sequential, reservation, sticky assignment, and Emergency-Pool triggers are operationally meaningful. Bottleneck migration under U1–U5 is directionally right. Section 6 covers gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, preemption thrash, Validation as a new bottleneck, and interpretability. Section 7’s attribute list and PM4Py drift design match the brief. Section 9’s shadow / day-randomized A/B / P1 stop-loss plan is responsible. The recommendation is not “pool because base mean delay is lowest.”

Those strengths make this a competent incomplete analysis, not a near-miss on excellence. Wrong expected-service numbers, an internally contradictory specialization rule, a broken/non-faithful simulator, and an SLA objective that is not actually SLA-weighted are each independently disqualifying for a high score. Combined, they cap the grade at 6.0.
