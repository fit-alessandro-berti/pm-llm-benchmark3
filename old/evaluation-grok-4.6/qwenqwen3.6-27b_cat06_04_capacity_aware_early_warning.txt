**5.5**

The write-up has the right *shape* for this task: stage eligibility, segment calibration, I-03 rejected, I-05 suppressed, I-07 re-opened on new evidence, a reserve slot, spillover in the impact table, and concrete fairness/monitoring safeguards. It is not a `p_now` ranking exercise. Under a strict reading, that is not enough, because the core decision calculus is wrong and is not applied consistently.

**The objective function contradicts the prompt.** The allowed calculation is `absolute risk reduction × breach value − swarm cost − false-alarm burden`. The supplied ranges are already absolute risk reductions. The answer instead uses `p_adj × eff_mid × value`. That double-counts probability, systematically understates benefit, and is the main reason I-08 looks like a −€2,000 case. Under the stated formula, I-08’s midpoint is about `0.10 × €100,000 − €450 − €5,000 = +€4,550`; even the lower bound is only about breakeven. Excluding a €100k Strategic case on that basis is a material allocation error, not a rounding issue.

**The stated rule is not the rule used.** Section 1 includes a spillover penalty and a €300 uncertainty penalty. The Section 2 scores match the formula *without* spillover (I-01 ≈ €750 is `0.61×0.265×9000 − 450 − 250`). If the advertised spillover penalty had been applied at the base rate used later (`3 × 6 × 0.02 × €15,000` scale), I-01 would be near or below zero. I-09 is listed at ~€1,800 and still deferred, while I-01 at ~€750 is taken; the €300 NNLS penalty would not flip that ranking. A deployable rule that is not the scoring rule is not decision-theoretically coherent.

**Eligibility is internally inconsistent.** The gate is “time to SLA ≥ 2.0 hours,” but I-07 (1.5 h) is marked eligible. Either the gate is real, in which case the table is wrong, or the table is real, in which case the rule is not deployable as written. The 2-hour cutoff is also underspecified relative to a 45-minute swarm: the binding constraint is stage, not an arbitrary remaining-time floor.

**The impact table inherits both problems and is sloppy even on its own terms.** “Optimistic” is defined as the upper half of each effect range, but the avoided-breach figures are `p_adj × effect`, and the effects mixed into that row are not uniformly upper-half (I-02 looks mid, I-01 looks like `0.61×~0.30`). Adverse treatment of I-04 (near-zero effect) is the right idea; the arithmetic is not auditable as causal impact. Using €15,000 as the untreated breach value is a reasonable placeholder, but it is not stress-tested, and the 8–20 minute delay channel is barely used except as atmosphere in the adverse row.

**Case judgments are mixed.** I-03 is correctly refused. I-05 vs I-07 is the required distinction and is handled clearly. Waiting on I-06 because of imputation is defensible. Waiting on I-09 because of a thin NNLS sample is defensible *as a robustness choice*, but then the policy should have said “uncertainty dominates score,” not posted a higher ENB and ignored it. Immediate swarm on I-04 (10 h, triage, first alert, €2,000 disruption) spends option value and a scarce slot that the reserve preference was meant to protect; that trade is not argued. Two specialists delivering three swarms, and the fact that a used specialist stops ordinary diagnosis, are not operationalized.

**Sections 5–6 are the strongest part** and are why this is not a 3–4. Value starvation, NNLS uncertainty, segment miscalibration, label leakage, and spillover incidence are all named, and the safeguards are concrete (caps, shrinkage, causal logging/IPW, concurrent-swarm limits, calibration triggers). Even here there is drift: the fairness quota (“minimum one SME/Public slot when ENB > 0”) is not what the chosen book does with I-10/I-07, and matching-based ATT after a value-and-score policy will have overlap problems that are not mentioned.

Net: a readable operating policy that hits the qualitative traps in the rubric, built on an incorrect expected-value identity, a rule/score mismatch, and at least one consequential mis-ranking. That is competent mid-pack work, not near-flawless.
