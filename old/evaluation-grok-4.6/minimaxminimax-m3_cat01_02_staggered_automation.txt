**6.5**

The answer is conceptually strong and format-compliant, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, quantitative errors, a false final claim, and a few identification overreaches keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is strong.** Sections 1–4, 6, and 8 are the real assets. Availability vs intensity, non-random selection, the March fraud-desk shock, routing/FTE/case-mix co-movements, and CSAT nonresponse are separated cleanly, with no causal claim in §1. The estimand table covers ITT, dose, median, tail, rework/escalation, CSAT, dynamics, and heterogeneity, and it flags SUTVA/treatment-version problems. The primary design is a staggered event study with not-yet-treated/never-treated controls, plus synthetic control and a triple-difference alternative; naïve TWFE is correctly rejected. The mechanism table is process-aware (fast-path substitution vs specialist congestion, learning, mix, staffing, rework displacement, tail amplification, survey selection). The threat table is complete and decision-relevant. The rollout choice (`expand with an embedded evaluation`) is the right one: Delta first, Epsilon deferred, pre/post windows, queue and fraud-desk instrumentation, spillover stops, and a joint median/p90/escalation scale-up rule. The Python block meets the spec: embedded CSV, validation, printed (not subplot) trends, pre/post contrasts, relative time `g`, labeled descriptive TWFE plus intensity model, clustered SEs, and explicit non-causal comments.

**Quantitative discipline is not at the same level.** Several numbers are wrong or internally inconsistent, which matters because the rubric weights arithmetic facts vs interpretation.

- Escalation “Jan–Feb” averages are actually Jan–Mar (Alpha 8.5 vs 7.8; Beta 8.2 vs 7.5; Delta 7.5 vs 6.8). The footnote contradicts the arithmetic.
- Delta Apr–Aug p90 is given as 92.9; the correct mean is 89.3. The 92.9 figure matches a slip that mixes Alpha’s April p90 (97.3) into Delta’s series. That error feeds the p90 ratio DiD (1.30 vs ~1.25).
- Post-period windows are not held fixed inside a single table: Delta/Epsilon rework and some complexity cells use May–Aug while the header says Apr–Aug. Alpha post complexity is 33.8, not 34.4; Gamma pre complexity is ~23.7, not 24.5; Epsilon post arrivals are 914.6, not 894.8.
- Minus signs disappear in the headline bounds (“10% to +2%”, “8% vs Delta”), so the intervals are ill-formed as written.
- §5’s “1 to 2 hours per 10pp” intensity result is asserted, not computed. The code is supplied but those coefficients are invented.

**A final-claim error is worse than an early slip.** §5 correctly records Beta’s median *rising* (+1.9 h full post; +0.85 h late post). §7 lists that as counterevidence. §10 then states as an *Observed* fact that late-post medians “ended lower” at Beta by 0.9 h. That is a sign error in the claim ladder. Late-window cherry-picking for Alpha (−3.3 h vs −0.4 h full post) is presented as if it were the natural contrast.

**Identification is good, not tight.** Ratio-DiD (subtracting post/pre ratios across branches with very different baselines) is treated as “the most defensible single number” (−18 to −22 pp). Level DiD is much smaller (Alpha vs Delta about −6 h; Gamma vs Delta about −8 h) and is not shown. Gamma is called the clean case, but Gamma’s complexity *did* jump 22→27 in May, it has only two post months, lost an FTE in June, and received the July routing bump. “Fraud-desk ramp-down” in Jul–Aug is inferred, not measured. Within-Delta 30% case randomization is proposed without confronting intra-branch interference, which is inconsistent with the otherwise careful SUTVA discussion. Relative-time `g` is built in code but never turned into an event-study specification, so §3 and §9 are only loosely linked.

**Net.** This is a serious causal-policy memo with the right estimands, the right primary design, the right median-vs-tail split, and a rollout that buys learning rather than a fake X%. It is not a 9–10: too many checkable numbers are wrong, the final Observed bullet misstates Beta, and a few “defensible” magnitudes are oversold. A 6.5 reflects strong identification/process reasoning pulled down hard by quantitative and terminal-claim flaws.
