**6.4**

The final write-up is diagnostically strong on the timeline the rubric cares about, then loses a good deal of credit to a required-but-broken monitoring sketch, a real mix-up in the covariate-drift row, and several internal inconsistencies.

**What is actually good.** Sections 2, 4 (in outline), and 6 get the main story right: April is a schema/pipeline incident, not model decay; June is a routing/mix change with stable within-variant rates; July–September stack supplier performance, V3-conditional risk change, and then Partner Z; October is not a comparable observed-breach month. That is the triangulation the prompt asks for. The table’s contradicting-evidence column, the stratified-test design, and the five failure modes (including June over-reaction and Partner Z “unfairness”) are the right shape.

**Section 1 has a material logic error.** The covariate-drift row treats Jun–Oct as one phenomenon and claims mean predicted risk “diverges from observed breach due to mix.” That is defensible for June (more V2, observed 0.15 vs predicted 0.20, AUROC still ~0.79). It is the wrong mechanism for August–September: V3 share and V3 matured breach both jump, but mean predicted risk only creeps 0.18 → 0.19 → 0.21 while observed breach goes 0.19 → 0.28 → 0.31. If this were mainly covariate drift with a stable \(P(y \mid x)\), scores should have moved with the mix. They did not. That pattern is concept/process change (and, in October, censoring). Parking October on the covariate row is worse: 55% maturity plus a long-path outcome backlog makes the 0.16 vs 0.23 gap unusable as mix evidence. Label-delay is also over-implicated in July (98% mature).

**Falsification is only half-done.** The task asked to separate observed evidence from hypotheses and state what would falsify each explanation. Section 3 does this implicitly via “expected result under two hypotheses.” Section 1 does not. A reader still has to infer the kill-tests for each row.

**Section 4 contradicts itself.** Immediate policy says do not train on Sep/Oct; later, Jul–Oct are “unsafe until labels mature 95%,” while July is already at 98%. June is correctly called recalibration-not-retrain in one paragraph and then only “preferably” added to the safe set. That is sloppy enough to matter for an audit recommendation.

**Section 5’s code fails the spec and would not run as written.**

- Required `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is never used.
- Cohort *interpretations* are not noted (event-calendar vs case-start), only two loops.
- `format_dataframe` renames to XES columns; the next lines still use `activity` / `case_id` and will break or silently miss the rename.
- `discover_performance_dfg` returns a `(dfg, start, end)` tuple of mean times, not `perf_dfg[edge]['median']`.
- `month_start + MonthBegin(2) - 1 day` from the first of the month is a **two-month** window (1 Jan → 28 Feb), which wrecks the monthly audit.
- Unused `log_converter` import.

For a section whose job is “auditable monthly process evidence,” those are not nits.

**Smaller cuts.** Alert dependencies are two sentences. Predictive metrics (Brier especially) are underused once the table is left behind. Process vs concept for supplier delay is acknowledged, then blurred again in the covariate row. Confidence “High for routing, Medium for supplier performance” is crammed into one cell.

The reasoning core would sit in the high 8s if the covariate row, July/training consistency, falsifiers, and the PM4Py sketch were clean. Under a strict reading they are not, so the score cannot stay there.
