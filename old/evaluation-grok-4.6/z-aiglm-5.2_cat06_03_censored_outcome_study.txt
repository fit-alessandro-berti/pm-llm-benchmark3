**5.5**

The write-up is structurally complete and avoids the worst failure modes (rejects-as-non-default, training only on the 21,600 rows as if they were a random sample, random case splits, and using `final_limit` / `collection_status` as features). It is not close to a high score under a strict reading of the brief.

**What is actually solid.** The primary estimand is explicitly $P(T>t\mid X_{\text{prefix}},\text{activated})$, rejected/withdrawn/open cases are not coded as non-default, and the alternative multi-state target is framed as a different question. The 48,000-row flow adds up. The leakage table hits the required fields with the right keep/repair/drop calls. Temporal splitting is justified by the July policy change and October migration. Section 8 correctly separates association, selection, and counterfactual default, and the three deployment DGP shifts are plausible. The skeleton uses `read_csv`, `format_dataframe`, a start-time split, `get_prefixes_from_log`, and a leakage column drop, and it does not invent a PM4Py survival API.

**Material statistical errors.**

1. **Validation is incompatible with label maturity.** The test window is starts from 15 Oct–31 Dec 2025 with extraction on 15 Feb 2026. A 90-day post-activation outcome, plus a 7–35 day feed lag, is not observable for most of that window (and often not even for late validation starts). Fast-to-activate cases would dominate any complete-case test metric. The brief required maturity to be designed into backtesting; a start-date cut alone does not do that.

2. **Delayed labels are not ordinary right censoring.** Treating “feed not yet in the warehouse” as survival censoring at extraction is wrong when the 90-day horizon has already elapsed. That is late event reporting. Censoring at $t>90$ while a default may already have occurred biases the hazard down. A maturity lag (e.g., only treat a 90-day outcome as complete ~35 days after the horizon, using `label_available_at`) is the honest rule; it is missing from both the design and the code.

3. **Still-open cases are misclassified.** The 5,100 open applications are administrative censoring of an unfinished process, not a competing terminal outcome. Some can still activate. Excluding them from the primary activated-only model is defensible; calling them competing is not.

4. **Selective non-observation is underspecified.** Unlinked transfers (~40%) and partner accounts with permanently missing feeds are not separated in the flow table from ordinary competing exits. Treating MNAR partner feeds as simple right censoring is a weak primary rule; the 60% linkage table is unused even as a sensitivity.

**Process and target gaps.**

5. Prefixes are not constrained to pre-decision / pre-activation events. A 3-event or 48-hour prefix can already contain Accept, Contract Signed, or Account Activated. That leaks selection into an “early warning” feature set. Short-case `<PAD>` is also a back-door length/termination signal.

6. Cohort membership uses *eventual* activation, i.e. future information. That is valid for a selected-sample estimand and is disclosed in §8, but it is never reconciled with scoring “every application” at event 3. IPTW is a different causal estimand, not a sensitivity of the primary one.

7. Several as-of rules stay verbal: same-calendar-day `documents_total`, extraction-time resource experience, and `risk_score` LOCF are not operationalized beyond the table.

**Section 9 is a sketch, not an audit pipeline.** It never builds terminal states (reject / withdraw / transfer / open), never applies `label_available_at` or a feed-lag maturity rule, never restricts training rows to activated-and-at-risk cases, never implements elapsed-time prefixes, and never *checks* leakage (no assertion that forbidden columns or post-prefix events are absent). `discover_dfg` is called on the full training log and unused. `get_prefixes_from_log` on a dataframe, and whether it returns one prefix per case or all lengths $1..k$, is left unexamined; the latter would induce repeated-case dependence. Activity-name mapping after the migration is described in prose and omitted from code.

**Net.** Competent PPM hygiene and an honest activated-only survival target, undermined by a non-mature test design, a real mistake on delayed labels, a sloppy competing-risk typology, and a skeleton that does not implement the cohort or audit logic the prompt asked for. That is a mid-range design, not a rigorous one.
