**6.2**

The final write-up follows the required ten-section format, rejects a blanket copy of Site A, and gets the main causal point right: Site A’s before/after contrast is confounded, and only *true* overlap that shortens the join before Assessment can travel. That is a competent transportability analysis, not a near-flawless one.

**What is solid.** The process table covers the mandated features and ties each to design. Statutory order is respected: Document Check is not started before Originals Receipt when originals are required. Co-interventions (API, added reviewers, mix shift, huddles, timestamping) are treated as non-identified. There is a scenario in which the median improves and the tail worsens. The candidate intervention is segmented (digital vs originals-required), keeps Assessment after both checks, and the pilot text correctly separates mechanism failure (overlap without waiting reduction) from implementation failure (no true overlap). The recommendation `conditional go` matches the evidence.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Pilot design is internally inconsistent.** Phase 1 eligibility is ~12–25% of ~567 completions/month, then further capped at 30–50% treated. That cannot yield 500 treated and 500 control cases in 8–12 weeks. The power target and the duration cannot both be true.

2. **Site B frequencies are almost unused.** The DFG counts (e.g. 2,108 Originals→Document vs 1,160 Identity→Document; 360 supplements; 1,360 legal) and the 18h originals delay vs 3.4h identity service are the best local evidence that identity is rarely on the critical path for 62% of cases. The quantitative ranges are asserted, not derived from those numbers or from A’s 18→12h waiting change after stripping mix/staff/API.

3. **The transport graph under-models confounding.** Huddles, API latency, and added document capacity appear mainly as modifiers of the parallelization path, not as alternative causes of the same outcomes that moved with the “treatment” at A. That weakens the transportability claim the graph is supposed to support.

4. **POWL/legal-loop fidelity is poor.** `LOOP(legal_once, supplement_rework)` with Approve/Reject/Supplement inside the body does not encode “supplement ⇒ rework Document/Assessment, then return; approve/reject ⇒ exit.” Approve can still redo; Supplement need not trigger rework. Duplicate labels and a large try/except wrapper around unknown constructors further reduce model fidelity relative to a manual-faithful construction.

5. **Scenario confidence is not a single estimand.** The IT-lock row scores confidence that the *failure story is true* (~65); other rows score confidence in an *effect range*. Same column, two meanings.

6. **Smaller slips.** Assumption 1’s “if false” clause is vacuous (“effect may be smaller or larger”). Throughput per verification FTE at A is essentially unchanged (4,800/18 vs 5,200/20) and is not used. WIP caps are 6×headcount with little queueing justification. Digital/legal/complexity independence is assumed without checking the DFG.

The piece is structured, cautious, and operationally usable. Under a strict bar it is a mid-tier expert memo: the right decision and the right constraints, with broken arithmetic in the pilot, unused local process data, a misspecified loop model, and a DAG that does not fully represent Site A’s confounding.
