5.7

The answer is structurally complete and directionally competent, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the rubric—temporal reasoning, abductive use of the DFGs/conformance evidence, resilience vs. concealed noncompliance, and a concrete recovery redesign—it leaves too much of the case unused and makes several avoidable analytical errors.

**Format compliance.** All seven required sections and the A–L table columns are present. That is the main strength. Completeness of headings is not the same as completeness of reasoning.

**Section 1 is thinner than the prompt requires.** T0–T4 are named correctly, and the exception-rate jumps (0.8% → 9.5% → 5.4% → 2.2%) are accurate. The prompt asked to refer to before/during/after metrics. Cycle times, p90s, fill rates, rework, cancellations, and conformance fitness are mostly absent. Those are the numbers that show the real temporal shape of the incident: hospital-critical median almost holds (8h → 10h → 9h → 8h) while pharmacy and wholesale collapse and do not fully recover (pharmacy 23h → 54h → 38h → 28h; wholesale 36h → 96h → 84h → 58h). T4 is described as “compliance control recovery nearly complete.” That overstates the data. Traceability is still 2.75× baseline, cold-chain 0.9% vs 0.4%, dual-control 0.5% vs 0.2%, rework 5.8% vs 3.5%, and an audit-remediation tail remains. T1 is also over-dramatized: the material control failure is T2, not the morning outage itself. Dominant variants are named impressionistically rather than from the DFGs (e.g., 1,800 pack→dispatch-without-scan plus 1,450 temporary-release→dispatch-without-scan; 1,600 skip-auth; 2,300 invoice-close→audit-remediation).

**Section 2 is the best part and still uneven.** Most keep/modify/retire calls are defensible, and the table correctly treats L as not equivalent to real-time control. Several cells are imprecise:
- **B** is not limited to controlled substances; the case describes offline customer/product eligibility checks. Narrowing the affected segment is wrong.
- **C** is mis-described as an expedient for “non-urgent” orders. The given observation is ambient product plus assumed standing authorization, not urgency.
- **F** already covered all controlled-substance orders; the documented miss is cold-chain temperature evidence. Requiring a “100% CS audit at T3/T4” as the main fix answers the wrong gap.
- **G** is labeled Retire while the rationale is a Modify (handwritten/manual identity capture). That is an internal contradiction. Given the “largest traceability gap” finding, a hard ban on *unrecorded* dispatch is right; a ban on any degraded dispatch is not.
- **C = Retire** is too absolute against the explicit instruction not to treat every baseline deviation as bad. A time-boxed, ambient-only, documented standing-auth path is the more faithful reading.
Frequencies, move-on-log/move-on-model deviations, and fitness scores (0.96/0.71/0.82/0.90; 91%/44%/59%/76% fitting traces) are almost unused. Without them the table is commentary, not process evidence.

**Section 3 fails a scored criterion.** The conceptual distinction is asserted rather than demonstrated. Only B is called true resilience; only C and G are called concealed noncompliance; only A and J are called ambiguous. That taxonomy is too small and partly wrong. I (capacity protection for hospitals), early D, F for controlled substances, and J with explicit rules are resilient. E, G, H, late D, and especially **K/L** are the concealment story: operational cycle time looks recovered while 1,400 manual-reservation reconciles, 1,200 POD remediations, and 2,300 audit-remediation cases carry the real control debt. The answer notices hidden workload in the table and then drops it in the section that was supposed to explain it. Throughput-as-false-resilience is mentioned, but not pinned to the 91% hospital fill vs 61% pharmacy fill split, nor to the T4 “recovered” dashboard vs the audit queue.

**Section 4 is a checklist, not a recovery process.** The required headings exist, but the design is generic. The 30% order-impact trigger is invented and ungrounded. There is no degraded-mode state machine aligned to T1–T4, no named forbidden variants (skip-auth on recall-sensitive/CS/cold-chain; dispatch with no identity artifact; invoice close with no evidence or queued exception), no dual-control pairing rule, no snapshot time-box, no scan-reconstruction protocol, and no rule that invoice close ≠ compliance close. “100% manual compliance check” vs “minimum manual form completion” is not an operational control matrix. This is the other scored weakness: the redesign is not concrete enough to run.

**Section 5 misclassifies the indicators it was asked to define.** Fill rate, cycle-time ratios, and orders processed in T2/T3 are concurrent/lagging outcomes, not leading indicators. Leading measures would be snapshot age, time-to-declare degraded mode, % of T2 dispatches with a contemporaneous evidence pack, trained manual-compliance headcount, and dual-control roster coverage. There are no thresholds tied to baseline (hospital fill, exception-rate ceilings, backlog age, audit-queue aging). Fairness is reduced to a hospital/pharmacy cycle-time ratio and ignores the much sharper 91% vs 61% fill-rate and cancellation shock. Conformance fitness, inventory-sync trust, and audit-remediation queue age—the hidden-workload indicators the case is built around—are missing.

**Section 6 is thin and the residual-risk column is misused.** Four generic recommendations, no rank, and the first “residual risk” cell restates a benefit. The high-value, case-specific actions are absent: forbid unrecorded dispatch; time-box snapshots; stand up a parallel evidence team so T4 ops metrics cannot mask the compliance tail; write an explicit pharmacy fairness rule; cold-chain logger fallback; CS dual-control pairing. Effort/benefit labels are plausible but unconnected to DFG volumes.

**Section 7 has the right three-part shape and the wrong hardness.** “Should not be accepted under any circumstances” for skip-auth and scanless dispatch flattens the required nuance. Management *should* accept hospital fast-lane and deferral of bulk if minimum product controls stay in force; it should *not* accept declaring recovery when hospital medians are back and 2,300 cases remain in audit remediation, or treating after-the-fact reconstruction as contemporaneous GDP/controlled-substance compliance. The “evidence that would change the recommendation” sentence is circular and does not specify a decision threshold.

**Net.** Correct skeleton, mostly right instincts on A/C/G/L, and an honest nod to hidden T4 work. Those are not enough. The answer underuses the DFGs and conformance findings, flattens the resilience/noncompliance distinction, overstates T4 recovery, contradicts itself on G, and offers a generic SOP plus mislabeled KPIs. That is a mid-pack response, not a high-scoring one.
