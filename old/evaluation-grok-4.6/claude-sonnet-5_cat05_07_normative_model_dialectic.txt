**6.0**

The legal-dialectical core is strong; the required process-model revision is not. Under a strict reading that weights model semantics, evidence completeness, and internal consistency as heavily as rhetoric, this is a good brief attached to a broken control-flow design.

**What holds up.** Section 1 separates stipulated facts from authority/scope disputes and states a usable burden of proof: noncompliance needs a real deviation plus no valid overlay; exceptions need competent authority, window, eligibility, and contemporaneous evidence; scope ambiguity is not convicted as noncompliance. The two briefs are actually adversarial. Prosecution is sharp on form-of-authority, automatic expiry, normalization of lapsed instruments, and the fact that a trace can fit M0 while still breaking CS-9’s resource rule. Defense correctly reads `checks` as unordered, treats the missing loop as a model gap, and distinguishes logging artifacts from misconduct. The required concessions appear on the defense side (703, 708, 705, 714, 712). Case calls for 701, 703, 704, 705, 706, 708, 709, 711, 712, and 714 are mostly right, and the system-level judgment does not merely split the difference.

**Fatal model error (Section 7).** M1 puts `checks = {Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review}` *above* the XOR that is supposed to contain the auto-approval and emergency routes. Every path, including PD-3 and FD-7, must therefore finish Quality_Review before a route can even be chosen. That is M0’s constraint, not the overlay semantics the rest of the memo argues for. The same `sanctions` / `quality` objects are then reused inside `auto_branch` and `emergency_branch`, so the “relaxed” routes both require a second copy of activities the top level already forced. The revised POWL therefore cannot represent the two overlay routes it claims to add. A defensible approximation is obvious: put concurrent checks *inside* the standard branch only; factor `Sanctions_Check` (and only that) before activation on the overlay branches; leave post-activation Quality_Review / retrospective approval unordered or weakly ordered on those branches. That is not what was coded.

**Loop placement cannot replay the motivating case.** `Request_More_Info` is modeled as `LOOP(silent, Request_More_Info)` *after both checks*. SUP-707 is Register → Sanctions → Request_More_Info ×2 → Quality_Review → …. The proposed M1 cannot replay the one desirable loop the memo spends pages defending. Calling it a “bounded” loop in §6 is also false: POWL `LOOP` is unbounded; the limitation note admits this, then the architecture still says “bounded.”

**New activities break replay without an approximation.** `Automated_MasterData_Validation` and `Provisional_Activation_Emergency` do not occur in any observed trace. PD-3 does require master-data validation, but encoding it as a mandatory visible transition means SUP-702 cannot replay. FD-7 traces go Sanctions → Activate, not through a new provisional-activation label. The prompt asked for a stated limitation plus the most defensible approximation (silent transitions, optional skip, or log-label mapping). The code adds log-absent mandatory nodes instead.

**Prosecution does not do what the prompt required.** Defense concedes real risk. Prosecution never clearly concedes genuine model/data defects. It treats Request_More_Info as ultra vires even though CS-9 does not forbid extra pre-decision work, and it treats CRM missingness as a live notification failure rather than acknowledging N4 as a collection break. The burden-of-proof paragraph is more honest than the brief that was supposed to carry the concession.

**Evidence misses and overclaiming.** SUP-702’s listed trace has no `Notify_Requester`; N4/CRM is the only notification evidence, and PD-3’s master-data step is absent. The matrix says “Missing evidence: None” and the call is JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at High confidence. That is too clean, especially beside the overlay table’s own `ratification_status: monitored_exception_only` and the observer’s retraction. High confidence on 702 is inconsistent with the memo’s authority analysis. The 14 ineligible + 9 unknown = “7.4% control failure” line conflates unproved with noncompliant; under the prosecution’s own “PD-3 is invalid” theory the denominator case is 312, not 23. SUP-715 as MIXED is allowed but sits poorly next to SUP-712: both are M0-fitting resource/ethics failures; only 712 is called NONCOMPLIANT. SUP-710 as a pure scope defect under-weights CS-9’s “every activation” language; MIXED or a lower-confidence NONCOMPLIANT was more precise.

**Smaller nits that still matter at this bar.** Defense invents a prosecution argument about order *inside* `checks` that the prosecution brief does not make. “Nine times FD-7’s cap” on SUP-703 is rhetoric; FD-7 does not apply. Layer 2 hard-codes PD-3’s expired window while Layer 1 promotes auto-approval to a standing route, so it is unclear whether the revision *legalizes* low-risk auto-approve or only time-boxes the dead pilot. Commented pandas is fine; the policy table and event schema (immutable `notification_id`, eligibility snapshot, alias-resolved actor ids, overlay registry) are the best part of §6–7.

**Net.** Sections 1–5 would score well on fairness, burden, and most case calls. Sections 6–7 were the test of whether the author can encode the process they just reasoned about. The POWL they shipped reimposes M0’s Quality_Review-before-decision constraint on the very exceptions the judgment treats as first-class, and it cannot replay SUP-707. That is not a polish issue. It is a failure of the artifact the prompt asked to be judged on most harshly.
