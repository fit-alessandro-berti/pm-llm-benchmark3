**5.5**

The write-up follows the required skeleton and shows real command of intervention *type* (timing/reservation vs routing vs decision policy vs concurrency), interference, and uncertainty ranges. Under a strict reading of the spec, that is not enough. Several counterfactuals violate mandatory control-flow, misuse the queue snapshots, and contradict themselves.

**Process feasibility (largest failure).** K-337 is high-risk and high-value. Suppressing the second RFI changes *decision policy*; it does not waive Rule 5. After Assessment 2 the path must still be `Compliance Review → Decision → Notify`. The table path `Register -> ... -> Assessment 2 -> Decision` is both vague (the forbidden ellipsis) and illegal. That also makes the “Decision moves to Friday / −72h to −96h” claim an artifact of dropping C1 and M1 timing, not a feasible compression. Notify is missing from all four paths. K-104’s specified action is a packet to **S1** plus a provisional **C1** slot; S1 almost disappears, and “Prelim Compliance” is treated as if C1 can work in parallel with I/D despite Rule 11 and a 16–20 h C1 backlog.

**Temporal consistency.** Queue snapshots are used as decoration more than constraints. K-219 asserts D2 starts “immediately” at Originals Receipt (Mon 17:30) *and* cites D2’s 5 h queue at 16:00; those cannot both be base-case facts, and overnight vs business-hours service is never resolved, so “16 h earlier” is not earned. K-411’s Tue 15:00 document finish assumes D2 starts with I2 around 11:30, but Tue 08:00 D2 already has ~4 h of work. K-104’s reserved C1 start Tue 20:00 is the optimistic reservation story stated as the central timeline, not as one branch. The 100-case note that K-337 saves **300–500 h for 100 cases** (~3–5 h each) cannot be reconciled with the case-level **72–96 h** range.

**What is actually good.** Section 1 correctly separates policy (K-337) from routing (K-219) and does not smash all cases into one sequence. Best/base/worst blocks exist. K-411’s derivation *does* notice that earlier readiness need not mean earlier completion when Assessment is saturated. The interference table and 100-case prose hit reservation, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment peaks, weekends, and false concurrency. Word counts and most table schemas match. The ranking (K-411 > K-219 > K-104 > K-337) is defensible on robustness, not just speed.

**Remaining spec nicks.** K-219’s pilot assignment (`D2 if D1 queue > 4 h`) is a different intervention than “assign D2 as soon as originals arrive.” Section 7 then oversells K-411 as “immediate cycle time relief without shifting burden,” undoing the better queue analysis. Invented point rates (15% rework, 5% error) are not labeled as scenarios. Partial order omits escalation, prelim vs final compliance, and Identity-before-Originals.

Format-compliant and conceptually oriented, but not a trustworthy counterfactual: the one case that most tests prerequisites is infeasible, and several clocks ignore the snapshots the prompt supplied.
