**7.6**

The final write-up is complete, well structured, and hits every required heading and constraint. It does not treat the restructuring as inherently good or bad, keeps East distinct from North/South, treats fairness and learning as first-class effects, and refuses a full rollout without a gated pilot. The mechanism map, three-scenario forecasts, indicator split, hidden-average risks, and recommendation are internally coherent and clearly tied to event-log observables.

It is not close to flawless.

The centerpiece quantitative claim — handover gap-wait rising from 588 h to 722 h per 100 cases (+23%) — multiplies **median** waits by frequencies and reports the product as total wait hours. That is not an expectation. Queue waits are right-skewed, so the mean exceeds the median; the figure is a median-based index, not hours. The answer never flags this. Directionally the arithmetic is useful (343→359 events is correct; round-trip 4.8 h→6.2 h is correct), but the headline hour totals and “+1.3 h/case” are overstated in precision.

Legal/Risk is treated as “arithmetically infeasible” and an “unbounded queue.” +33% case volume on 91–93% utilization is a serious warning if service time per case is unchanged and no capacity is added. Utilization already mixes case and non-case work; new cases need not be equally heavy. “Unbounded” is stronger than the evidence.

Approval capacity is inconsistent: one place says load per remaining approver rises “roughly +50–60%,” another says it “roughly doubles.” With 3→2 supervisors and volume 39→43, load per FTE scales by about 1.65×. “Doubles” is wrong.

The scenario table assigns false precision to quantities the log never gives (baseline high-complexity P90 ≈26 d, several post-change P90s). Labeling some cells “est.” is not enough when they are then compared as if measured.

The PM4Py sketch covers every requested analysis family and is usable as a design, but several calls are wrong for current PM4Py, not just stylized:
- `discover_performance_dfg` returns three values, not four.
- `filter_variants(..., top_k_variants=5)` is not the public API (`filter_variants_top_k` is).
- `get_variants` values are typically case lists, not counts; summing/sorting them as frequencies fails.
- `insert_case_service_waiting_time` does not create `case:service` / `case:waiting`.

For a required “sketch / pseudocode” that is a moderate defect, not a fatal one. Subcontracting is computed and barely interpreted.

Strengths that keep the score from falling further: East-as-beneficiary vs N/S slack transfer; right-censoring of early post-change averages; informal-consult measurement gap; Bot→intake as a null-control edge; utilization framed as a congestion signal rather than a benefit; stepped-wedge isolation of tech pooling from legal/risk and supervisor removal; named-approver and liaison modifications. Those are the right process-mining connections.

A near-flawless answer would caveat the median×frequency index, state Legal/Risk risk without claiming infeasibility, keep approval math consistent, and use the actual PM4Py signatures. This is a strong consulting forecast with a few real analytical and API errors.
