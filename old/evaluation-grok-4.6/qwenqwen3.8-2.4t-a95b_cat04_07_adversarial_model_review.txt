**7.4**

The review is substantively strong: it separates exploration from enforcement, treats the reported numbers as evidence rather than a ranking, reasons from concrete traces, designs a third model that is actually safer than S and less brittle than C, and ends with a real dissent rather than a polite hedge. Probe calls on B1–B8 match the process-tree languages. The Petri-net backbone (mandatory parallel checks, gated correction with Credit-only / Technical-only / both redo, Expedite only after checks complete, XOR approval, reused labeled transitions) is the right semantic move.

It is not near-flawless.

The “compact formal description” is not compact and not formal. Parallel and choice operators are missing or rendered as blank space (`(C  T)`, `C  T  (C  T)`), so Section 7.1 cannot reconstruct the net. That is a required deliverable, not decoration.

The PM4Py artifacts would not do what the text claims. `im`/`fm` are plain dicts, not `Marking`. `pm4py.fitness_alignments` returns an aggregate dict, but `hard_negative_review` zips the log against it as if it were per-case diagnostics. Language membership is then approximated by `fitness >= 0.999`, which is the wrong test for policy probes (use replay / `conformance_diagnostics_alignments` and inspect log/model moves). For a task that demanded self-contained PM4Py assigned to `model_third` plus metric skeletons, that is a real defect, not a sketch-level nit.

Smaller cuts: Expedite-after-partial-redo is left underspecified (does “follow both mandatory checks” mean historically or after the last correction?). Supplier-before-checks is allowed and documented, but that is an extra permissiveness the governance section does not treat as a hard gate. Validation code is not executable as written.

The board-level argument still holds. The implementation and formalization do not.
