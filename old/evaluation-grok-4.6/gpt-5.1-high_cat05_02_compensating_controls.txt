The answer is a strong, well-structured control review: it uses the required headings, does not treat four-eyes form as the objective, and generally reasons from timing, independence, coverage, recoverability, and case risk. Classifications are mostly defensible and non-mechanical (especially AP-204, AP-205, AP-208, AP-209). It is not nearly flawless. Several factual, logical, and implementation defects remain.

**What is done well**
- FEP-1 is decomposed into real threat scenarios (error, unilateral fraud, collusion, bank-change/new-vendor risk, recall window, emergency loophole, structural pairing/outages), with AV-2 / MG-4 / RA-7 / ED-1 mapped to prevent / detect / correct / fail-to-address.
- Sufficiency is not reduced to “a control occurred” or to realized loss. AP-208 is correctly failed despite a legitimate payment; AP-204 is correctly failed despite formal four-eyes; AP-205 is correctly `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`.
- ED-1 is mostly treated as a conditional permission. AP-206 is rightly `INDETERMINATE` rather than forced into MET/FAILED.
- Post-release, pre-settlement manager review is discussed as hybrid rather than casually labeled “preventive.”
- Monitoring recommendations separate alerts from proof, and cover the requested signals.
- The Python sketch meets the requested pipeline shape (StringIO → `format_dataframe` → four-eyes filter → blocked attempts → joined facts → flags) and labels output as evidence, not verdict.

**Material defects**

1. **Internal contradiction on AP-207 / equivalence.** Section 2 treats AP-203/AP-207 as “fully equivalent (or stronger than four-eyes).” Section 4 then says post-release, pre-settlement MG-4 is structurally weaker and “should not be given full equivalence by default.” AP-207 is exactly that pattern. Calling the ED-1 package “textbook” and `OBJECTIVE_MET` with high confidence overstates what the problem allows: ED-1 does not make the four-eyes objective disappear.

2. **Wrong recall-window arithmetic for AP-201.** Settlement is 10:00 on 4 May; audit is 09:00 on 5 May (~23 hours after settlement). The answer treats RA-7 as after the recall window and mainly non-corrective. The 25-hour figure in the case table is from *release*, not settlement. That error is then baked into the facts table as `post_settlement_>24h`.

3. **AP-208 ED-1 conditions are incompletely cited.** Amount EUR 900,000 exceeds the EUR 100,000 cap; bank change three days earlier violates the seven-day rule; AV-2 was down; manager was post-settlement; audit was four days late. The write-up stresses amount, newish vendor, outage, and lateness, but never states the hard ED-1 amount breach, and “relatively new vendor” blurs an 8-month tenure (which *clears* the 180-day rule) with actual failed conditions.

4. **Section 1 vs Section 4 semantics drift.** Post-release/pre-settlement MG-4 is “hybrid preventive/corrective” in §1 and “hybrid detective–corrective” / “not truly preventive” in §4. The later formulation is better; the earlier one inflates preventive credit.

**Code issues (strict)**

- `audit_after_recall_window_flag` uses `.str.contains(">>24h")` while AP-201 is encoded `>24h`. The flag would miss the one case the narrative calls late.
- “Emergency eligibility” is not actually computed (token-before-prepare, amount &lt; 100k, vendor tenure, no 7-day bank change, AV-2 pass without override, MG-4 before settlement, RA-7 within one business day). A boolean `emergency_token` plus `emergency_high_risk = token & bank_change` is not an eligibility test.
- `filter_four_eyes_principle` polarity is assumed (same resource = violation) and only caveated. Across PM4Py versions the helper has been documented both as keeping *satisfying* traces and as keeping *violating* traces. If it keeps compliant cases, AP-204/AP-209 would be mislabeled as violators and same-user cases dropped. The code never checks the complement or completed-payment filter explicitly.
- Manager/audit timing and bank-change flags are copied from a hand-built table rather than derived from timestamps, so the “checking code” does not independently verify the judgments it is supposed to support.
- `format_dataframe` is called without `timest_format` / `resource_key`; that is version-fragile, which is mentioned but not guarded.

**Smaller nits**
- Shared service accounts (explicit in FEP-1) are barely treated.
- Severity/confidence scales are undefined.
- AP-203 is upgraded from the source hedge (“mostly satisfied”) to “all ED-1 conditions appear satisfied” without new evidence that audit *checked* those conditions.
- Residual-risk language is qualitative as required, but AP-202’s “near-worst-case” and AP-208’s “extremely high at execution time” are slightly theatrical relative to the evidence standard the rest of the answer sets.

**Case-table verdict (substance, not form)**  
AP-202 failed, AP-205 prevented, AP-204 failed despite two names, AP-208 failed despite no loss, AP-209 only partial because of the u12/u13 pattern: these are the right calls. AP-201 as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is reasonable (convenience same-user, but independent pre-settlement MG-4 and low amount). AP-203 as `OBJECTIVE_MET` is acceptable; AP-207 as `OBJECTIVE_MET` is the stretch, given manager-after-release and the paper’s own warning about ED-1.

Net: high-quality control reasoning with a few real inconsistencies and a checking script that would not fully reproduce the narrative. That is well above a mediocre answer and well short of near-flawless.
