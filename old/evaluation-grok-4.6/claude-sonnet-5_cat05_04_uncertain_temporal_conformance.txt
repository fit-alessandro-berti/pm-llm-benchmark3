**3.5**

The fragment is methodologically stronger than most incomplete attempts, and then it fails the assignment on completeness, arithmetic, and the findings that matter most.

**What is actually good.** Section 1 is the high-water mark. Offset direction is stated correctly (`true = recorded − offset`, so `[r_lo − b, r_hi − a]`), resolution is applied before clock correction, midpoints are withheld from the primary analysis, T-404 keeps the full 18–25 minute band, T-405 treats the fold as a union and prunes with ingestion order rather than inventing a point time, T-406’s entry-delay reading is labeled as a substantive choice, and T-407 correctly refuses to equate `Alert_Sent` with `Alert_Received`. The UTC table in Section 2 is, for the rows that exist, mostly right: HUB `±1.5 s`, OPS-BER floor-plus-`[-90, +120]`, VENDOR-NY, SENSOR stacking, the inferred T-404 start `[10:09:58.5, 10:17:01.5]`, the single surviving T-405 branch `(CEST, CET, CET)`, and the T-409 weak `±12 min` substitution are all defensible.

**Then the deliverable collapses.** The required contract is seven exact headings. Sections 4–7 are absent. Section 3 dies mid-row at T-405. That is not a trim; it deletes the cases the prompt flagged as interpretive tests (T-406 delay semantics, T-407 missing receipt, T-409 non-metrological offset) and the only clean double SLA breach, T-408. On HUB-only stamps, T-408 S2 is about `[3297, 3303] s` against 2700 and T-408 S3 is about `[138297, 138303] s` against 129600 — both **CERTAIN_VIOLATION**. An answer that never reports those is not a borderline write-up. It missed the easiest certain findings in the set.

**Duration arithmetic is not trustworthy even where rows exist.** Interval construction and duration construction disagree.

- T-401 S1 is listed as `[719.5, 991.5]`; from the Section 2 intervals it is `[718.5, 991.5]`.
- T-401 AR–CC is listed as `[3058.5, 3321.5]`. The same table implies roughly `[3058.5, 3081.5]`. The upper bound is ~240 s too wide.
- T-402 S1 lower bound is `938.5` vs `958.5`.
- T-402 S2 is the serious error: `[1288.5, 2761.5]` vs `[2488.5, 2761.5]`. Twenty minutes. Classification luckily stays POSSIBLE_VIOLATION for S2, but the profile story is now false: the write-up implies containment can be *too fast* relative to the band, when every feasible duration is *slow* and only the upper tail is in doubt.

That last error is not a rounding quibble. It is a broken duration interval used as if it were evidence.

**Smaller defects that still count under a strict rubric.** Section 2 drops explicit ISO datetimes after T-401 (the spec asked for them). Minus signs in the offset algebra are eaten (`recorded  true`, `[r_lo  b, r_hi  a]`), so the one section that must be unambiguous is typographically damaged. Headings are `#` rather than the required `##`. T-403’s z=3 near-miss (`1141.5 > 1140`) is a nice catch; it does not offset missing sensitivity to SLA tolerances of 0/2/5 minutes, which would actually flip several calls (T-401 S1 becomes a pass at +2 min; T-404 S2’s 3-second overage disappears immediately; T-402 S1 is still certain at +2 min and only possible at +5 min). There is no midpoint-vs-interval comparison, no `pm4py` reconstruction, and no evidential-sufficiency or governance close.

**Net.** Strong assumptions and a mostly correct UTC normalization of the early cases are real work. They do not compensate for an unfinished findings table, a 1200-second duration error, silence on T-408’s certain SLA violations, and four missing required sections. This is an incomplete technical draft, not a finished evaluation.
