7.4

This is a strong, well-structured design that hits every required section and avoids the worst constraint violations in the prose. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several design-to-implementation gaps, a few internal contradictions, and some overconfident invented quantities keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually good.** The digital-twin boundary is right: activity-level DES, not replay and not independent-case Monte Carlo. Non-stationary arrivals, HR calendars, separate municipal service / queue wait / external delay, latent objections, architect bursts, inspection bundling, and AND-join before decision drafting are all named as first-class mechanisms. PM4Py is correctly scoped as log/model handling. The excerpt is loaded with pandas, not a nonexistent `pm4py.read_csv`. Waiting and service are not collapsed without comment. Objections are treated as latent. Optimality is not claimed. The experiment table includes baseline, D1–D10, combinations, and more than three stress cases. Warm-up is seasonal rather than naive steady-state. Validation covers face, replay, distributions, utilization, bottlenecks, and hold-out. Decision criteria are multi-objective and include tails, fairness, rework, backlog, and robustness.

**Model scope is slightly over-specified and under-operationalized.** Entities such as `InspectionBundle` and `NeighborhoodObjectionCluster` are introduced as if they will carry state, then never given clocks, membership rules, or release conditions. The join state before decision drafting is listed as a case variable but never defined as an enabling predicate (which reviews are required, what happens if a review is skipped by policy, how late fire/environment results reopen drafting). Queue discipline is “scenario-dependent” without a default priority, preemption, or aging rule except a vague nod to D6. Time granularity, whether travel is a resource hold, and how council-meeting weeks interact with an already-started legal check are left open.

**Parameter estimation is conceptually right and operationally thin.** The three-layer timing split and the warning that performance-DFG / inter-completion times are not service times are correct, but the plan never says how start times will be imputed when missing (regression on duration covariates, utilization inversion, expert priors, interval censoring). “Approximate Bayesian calibration or simulation-based optimization” is named without parameters, discrepancy function, weights on median vs p90 vs utilization, or a statement that 31,200 cases will make unweighted likelihoods dominate operationally irrelevant residuals. Architect-burst and objection-cluster estimation from a flattened case log is asserted, not designed (what window, what matching key, how to avoid double-counting shared-resource delay as “relatedness”). Routing probabilities for latent fire-area and environmental-sensitivity flags are not identified; the log already has `requires_*` columns, so the latent layer needs an explicit measurement model.

**Assumptions are mostly honest, with a few silent inventions.** The weekday model in code (Monday 2.2, Wednesday 1.0, every other day 1.2, including Saturday/Sunday) is not justified in Section 3, even though the excerpt contains a Saturday-evening submission. Case-type mix is treated as constant across months and architects after the text said marks should be conditional. Legal-check and objection base rates in `RoutingPolicy` are fabricated and not labeled as placeholders in the assumptions list. D7 is assumed to preserve demand, then implemented as a Monday rate cut with no compensatory mass on other days—so the intervention secretly destroys volume. D2 eligibility depends on `prior_violations is False`, but cases are generated with `prior_violations=None`, so the no-inspection rule never fires unless something else sets the flag.

**Experiment matrix is rich on paper and inconsistent with the runner.** E11–E13, S4, and S5 appear in the table and vanish from `build_scenarios()`. S2 is an empty stub. Factor levels (20/40/60% loop reduction, 2/5/10-day batch windows, 50/70/90% detection) are reasonable screening ideas, but there is no power calculation, no statement of which metric is primary during screening, and no control for multiple comparisons. D10 is described as “best conservative levels from E1–E4” and then hard-coded to a single central combination. Stress S1 multiplies an already-seasonal peak rather than replacing it; that is easy to misread as 1.7 × 2.3.

**Uncertainty treatment is directionally correct and numerically arbitrary.** Observed-WIP vs 6–12 month seasonal warm-up is the right fork. “100 then 300–500 replications” is not tied to the width of a paired interval on p90 for Complex variance (8% of ~1,700 cases/year after warm-up is a small tail sample). First- vs second-order uncertainty is mentioned and not implemented. Heavy tails get quantiles and mixture talk, but no explicit extreme-value or semiparametric tail model, and the sampler is a single lognormal around the median. Computational cost of a calendar-aware DES at that replication count is ignored.

**Validation is complete as a checklist, weak as a protocol.** Replay-with-exogenous-arrivals is the right idea; the text still never says how to score a match when start times are missing and objections are latent. Conformance to the process tree (alignments, remaining-token issues around `Completeness rework` / `tau`, which are not log activities) is omitted even though PM4Py is already in the stack. The 12-vs-6-month split is short for two seasonal peaks. Utilization validation correctly notes that completion-only logs cannot identify busy time, then offers “HR plus interviews” without a quantitative acceptance band.

**Decision criteria overfit invented thresholds.** “−5–10% median, −10–15% p90, no district SLA drop beyond 2–3 points, 70–85% utilization” are presented as a selection rule despite the prompt giving no municipal SLA. That is a policy claim, not a measurement plan. Fairness and spillover are well motivated; `summarize_case_durations` only reports by case type, so the stated criteria are not even computed in the skeleton.

**Python skeleton: PM4Py conventions are mostly respected; the “simulator” violates the problem’s hardest constraint.** `format_dataframe`, `parse_process_tree` with a documented fallback, `get_variants`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, and `get_case_arrival_average` match the requested API. Timing columns correctly stay NaN when starts are absent. That part is faithful.

The executable path is an independent-case stopwatch: activities are appended in a fixed sequence, service hours are added as wall-clock sojourn, calendars are never queried, queues never form, resources are never seized, parallel reviews are serialized, rejection never occurs, inspection bundles do not exist, architect IDs are i.i.d., D3/D4/D5/D6/D8 flags have no effect on event times, D4 adds an FTE that is not time-varying and is unused, and D9 applies an arbitrary 25% cut to a 60-day constant. Running `run_experiment_matrix` would therefore rank policies almost at random relative to the mechanisms in Sections 1–4. The comments admit this, but the prompt required the skeleton to be coherent with a non-independent, calendar-aware twin—not a sequential delay generator that happens to sit next to unused `WorkingCalendar` classes. Unused `import random`, hardcoded fire/environment/notice by case type, and `base_daily_rate=50` (inconsistent with 31,200 cases / 18 months) are smaller but real sloppiness.

**Net.** Excellent advisory structure and process-mining hygiene; not a calibrated, internally consistent experimental instrument. The prose would support a high score. The contradictions (D7 demand, unused calendars, experiment table vs code, selection thresholds as if known) and the independent-case engine that cannot actually implement the twin it describes are enough to keep the grade well below “nearly flawless.”
