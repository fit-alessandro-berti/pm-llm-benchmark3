**6.8**

This is a strong, well-structured pipeline design that hits the required section contract, keeps the conservative path actually conservative, and gets the hard constraints right (do not drop e05/e14, do not treat equal activity+timestamp as identity, do not make ingestion the business order, preserve original timestamps, do not treat a total order as physical truth). The event/relation arithmetic is also correct (17/44 → 15/39 → 12/32 → 10/28). It is not nearly flawless. Several cross-section contradictions and implementation gaps are real, not cosmetic.

### What is actually good
The decision tree is the right shape: drop only exact technical duplicates, merge only externally verified multi-system observations, retain abstraction-level pairs, refine harmonization collisions, and treat disjoint finest-grained objects as a veto. That is the correct conservatism for this log.

Event-level calls are mostly well justified. e03/e04 and e11/e16 as verified T2 merges, e03/e05 and e13/e14 as retain-unchanged, e08/e09 as a label collision rather than a duplicate, and e06/e07 plus e13/e15 as abstraction pairs with an explicit aggressive alternative, all match the known facts. The `have_common_object=True` argument is the one the prompt asked for: O1/V1 are phase-level objects and cannot distinguish M1 from M2.

Ordering philosophy is also right in prose: semantic constraints first, `source_seq` only within source, ingestion as a flagged deterministic leftover, original timestamp never rewritten, and a clear explanation that pre-assigning microseconds launders a tie-break into a fake measurement.

### Material accuracy problems
The sensitivity analysis contradicts the conservative pipeline. Section 4 refines e07 and e15, so conservative **cannot** still exhibit a Pack→Pack self-loop. Capture→Capture **does** remain, but because e13 and e14 are concurrent, not because e15 is an abstraction duplicate. Worse, Scenario B does **not** erase Pick→Pick or Capture→Capture: it keeps e03/e05 and e13/e14. Only mechanical merge (10 events) kills those loops. The opening of section 7 therefore misstates both conservative and evidence-based DFGs, which is exactly the comparison the section exists to make.

OTG is undersold in the same place. Aggressive fusion of e13/e14/e15 does not leave object-to-object topology “stable”; it fabricates an M1—M2 co-occurrence through a single capture event. That is one of the more important semantic damages and is treated as a footnote.

### Implementation does not match the stated policy
`compute_order_keys` never uses `source_seq` (R3), despite R3 being listed as a distinct rule and despite fact 9 warning that ingestion is not business order. The Kahn ready-set is ordered by `ingested_at`, then eid. That collapses R3 into R5 and makes the “semantic vs deterministic” distinction weaker in code than in prose.

`DOMAIN_ORDER` is a hardcoded eid list, not a derived lifecycle/object rule. After Scenario B merges e09 into e08, the e09→e10 edge disappears and is **not** rewritten as e08→e10. Handover-after-acceptance then survives only because ingestion times happen to agree. The same transitivity hole appears when e15 is folded into e13. A pipeline that claims R1–R4 are semantic constraints cannot encode them as brittle pre-merge identifiers.

`validate_integrity(agg)` will fail as written: the default protected set still includes e05 and e14, which Scenario C deletes. That is an executable error, not a style issue.

Policy/code drift on merges: section 2 promises attribute-level authority for e11/e16 (ERP financials vs billing-specific fields); the helper is a single source rank, so billing-authoritative non-null fields lose whenever ERP already has a value. T2 says both `source_event_key`s are kept on the canonical event; the helper only records them in `AUDIT` and then drops the donor row. `refine_label` never writes an audit row, so section 8’s reversibility story is stronger than the code.

Minor but real: e15 is relabeled `Capture Payment (ERP invoice summary)`. Fact 8 is an invoice-level balance update with no Payment object. Keeping the Capture prefix continues to pollute payment-flattened views even in the “minimum-change” path. A qualifier that leaves the ET name in the Capture family is not a free refinement.

### Smaller issues
The tree is sound, but T1’s “lowest eid” canonicalization is an unargued deterministic choice. Scenario B’s merge of e08/e09 is labeled “evidence-based” while fact 4 is explicitly a harmonization collision; that is aggressive reinterpretation, not extra evidence, and should have been named as such more sharply. The intended 12:00 order lists e13↔e14 as the only admissible swap; e12 (M1 authorize) and e14 (M2 capture) are also semantically unordered. Candidate-group pre-check and the scoped `ocel_drop_duplicates` no-op are used correctly; `ocel_merge_duplicates` is correctly confined to a warned scenario.

### Net
Conceptually this is a high-quality answer: conservative default, verified-pair exceptions, provenance, and the right warnings. Strictly, it loses a substantial amount for a sensitivity section that misstates which loops survive, an ordering implementation that ignores its own R3 and breaks under merge, a validation call that cannot succeed on the aggressive object, and merge/authority behavior that does not implement the rules advertised in sections 1–2 and 8. Those are not polish nits; they are the difference between a coherent pipeline and a set of mostly-correct essays sitting on top of a one-shot script.
