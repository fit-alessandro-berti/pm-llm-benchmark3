**7.0**

This is a serious, process-aware design that correctly refuses the main failure modes (rejected/withdrawn/open = non-default, train-only-on-21,600, post-outcome fields, random split). It is not nearly flawless. Several definitional, identification, and implementation errors are material.

**What holds up.** The core diagnosis is right: `default_90d` is defined only after activation, and coding non-activated cases as 0 quietly changes the estimand into a policy-embedded “did not default under our approvals” quantity. The primary target is labeled as factual and policy-embedded; the activation-conditional target is correctly treated as a different question with collider/selection risk; the “activate everyone” quantity is correctly marked unidentified and only bounded. The 48,000-row flow table, the five-way missingness taxonomy, the 13 April 2025 maturity implication (no complete-case post-policy data), the leakage table, and the case against a random split are all at the required level. The skeleton uses real PM4Py entry points and, in prose, justifies not calling `insert_case_service_waiting_time`.

**Target and estimand (material).** The write-up overclaims that the target is “well defined for every one of the 48,000 applications,” then excludes early terminals from the landmark risk set. The displayed CIF identity also drops the “at risk at \(L\)” conditioner on the right-hand side. Calling the object a “cause-specific cumulative incidence function” blurs CIF and cause-specific hazard. The recovery formula \(P(\text{default}\mid\text{activated})=F_{\text{default}}/(F_{\text{default}}+F_{\text{activated, no-default}})\) is not generally the activation-conditional risk once transfers, late non-activation, and censoring exist. More importantly, default is only reachable *after* activation, so a reduced-form competing-risk model from the landmark (direct transition to default) is a coarse stand-in for the multi-state process the answer itself describes in (D). The 180-day activation window is a free parameter with little justification.

**Risk-set error (most serious statistical flaw).** After 180 days without activation, \(E\) is impossible. Those cases should leave the risk set for the composite (competing “not activated,” or an explicit intermediate state). The design leaves still-open and late-reject cases as ordinary censoring, which keeps them at risk for activate-and-default. The code does the same: initial `event_type="censored"`, competing terminals only if `terminal_time <= act_deadline`, no absorbing “no activation by 180d” outcome. That is inconsistent with the stated estimand.

**Validation vs headline estimand.** The 270-day CIF plus 35-day feed cannot be observed for any July–December 2025 start by 15 February 2026. Using that window as the “headline” test, even with censoring-aware metrics, evaluates a shorter, activation-speed-selected functional—not \(F(270)\). The prequential \(C=d\) story in the text is the right one; the implemented `temporal_split` is a single retrospective cut on features/labels built at the final cutoff, and applicant/linkage blocking is never coded.

**Outcome semantics.** The warehouse target is `default_90d` gated by `label_available_at`. The pipeline ignores both for labeling and keys off a `Default Recorded` activity that the schema does not guarantee. That is a different outcome, and it undercuts the delayed-feed discussion. Open cases with all-NaT terminal times are assigned via `idxmin`, which is version-dependent and can fabricate a terminal state.

**Code contradictions with the design.** `FUTURE_ACTS` includes `Accepted` and `Contract Signed`. Those can occur before 72 hours without activation; such cases are still at risk under the author’s own terminal set (`Account Activated` / Reject / Withdraw / Transfer). The assertion would reject valid prefixes. Section 2 says `final_limit` / `decision_reason` / `collection_status` are dropped at load; they are not. The §4 `data_extract_time` same-instant rule is not implemented. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is computed and discarded. Historical encodings and IPCW are comments only—acceptable in a skeleton, but then the audit does not actually “fail the build if a feature has no tag.”

**Smaller issues.** Manski bounds are correctly wide but presented as “the most useful single sentence” while remaining almost uninformative. Transfer-as-competing is coherent for “default on our books”; the 60% linkage limitation is handled in prose only. Policy/migration flags will be collinear with calendar time and maturity. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` on a DataFrame is version-fragile. The block is at or past the 160-line cap.

The design would be in the high 8s–9s if the risk set matched the composite event, outcomes used the warehouse label with a maturity gate, the test window could identify the stated horizon, and the skeleton did not contradict the prefix/terminal rules. Those are not nits.
