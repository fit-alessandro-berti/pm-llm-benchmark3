US cannot both activate and not activate R1. That is not a wording quibble; it is the central foreign-high-value rule, applied to three similar cases three different ways.

**TX-301 / TX-309 are right by accident.** After denying R1, the author invents a free-floating “best practice” duty to re-review any large increase. Section 4 then admits TX-301 is “COMPLIANT under strict rule logic” and that they chose NONCOMPLIANT anyway. That is the opposite of applying the given policy.
