**6.4**

The memo chooses a defensible option (E, not A or B), keeps facts and interpretations mostly separate, and covers all six required sections. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several concrete errors, PM-concept slips, and incomplete decision logic keep it well below the top of the scale.

**Factual and quantitative errors.** These are serious in an executive memo. Simple houses are 34% of the pilot, not “the majority of straightforward orders.” Apartments (29%) + old buildings (18%) = 47%; adding “or business customers” (8%) makes 55%, so the 47% claim is wrong. Coordinator utilization of 93% was reported in *two* pilot regions, not “pilot regions” as a whole. The memo and final section disagree on timing (10 weeks vs 8 weeks). “Worsing” is a sloppy typo in a decision document. “Installed but not working” became *a* shifted complaint type, not clearly “the dominant” one.

**Facts vs interpretations.** The evidence table exists and meets the eight-item floor, but it is thin on the artifacts the prompt required: generalization (0.70 → 0.56), temporal-profile deviations, DFG edge shifts, permit-check move-on-model, and the handover-of-work vs working-together network. Those findings are mostly left out of the table and only loosely implied later. The first “assumption” row is poorly framed (a hypothesis about mix confounding, worded as if it were being adopted). The memo itself states causal claims—“created a fragile system,” “systematically failed”—as if settled, which blurs the table’s own fact/interpretation split.

**Process-mining concepts.** High fitness / low precision is explained only partly correctly. Low precision means the *model allows* too many unobserved traces (skipped survey × manual activation × second visit × billing-start combinations), not that “the log has many unique paths.” Generalization is never used. Alignments are cited once (billing before activation) and the other frequent alignments are underused. The social-network finding (simpler handover network, more centralized working-together network, loss of resilience) is reduced to “coordinator absence,” which is incomplete.

**Metric conflicts.** The five required tensions are all named and the segment / cost / tail stories are directionally right. The handover–resilience and visible-vs-downstream cost explanations are the strongest parts of the answer. The precision paragraph and the over-attribution of all tail risk to “shortcuts for simple cases” are weaker; the evidence also implicates coordinator overload, delayed second visits, missing permits, and billing-before-activation.

**Alternatives A–E.** A is correctly rejected. B is not chosen merely because some KPIs worsened. C is undersold and poorly distinguished from E. Coordinator ownership plus suspension of survey-skip and post-billing activation (C) could be given to apartments and old buildings without exporting the two most harmful design choices; the memo never explains why those segments should be denied the coordinator model. D is called “always reasonable” without weighing delay against the value already visible in the simple-house segment. That C/E gap is the main strategic hole.

**Value of information.** There is a ranking, which is good, but most items do not state a decision-flip: what result would move management from E to C, back toward a limited skip rule, or to rollback. Ranking and action disagree: M7 is 7th in VoI yet implemented immediately; M5 is last even though the recommendation *scales coordinators* and the log already shows stalling on absence and 93% utilization; M8 is ranked 4th and then dropped. M6’s 95% precision bar is arbitrary and mixes up the original skip target (medium-risk addresses) with “low-risk apartments.”

**Final recommendation.** Immediate / pause / scale / measure / threshold is present and avoids national scale of the harmful pieces. Thresholds are invented without being labeled as judgment calls, and they do not cover SLA, permit conformance, coordinator-absence resilience, or NPS/CX decomposition. The package is usable as a draft; it is not a tight, artifact-complete, numerically accurate decision memo.
