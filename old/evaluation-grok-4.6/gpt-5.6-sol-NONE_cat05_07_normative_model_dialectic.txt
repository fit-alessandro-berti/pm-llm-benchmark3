**7.4**

The answer is unusually well structured and, on the legal/process side, often excellent. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has a real POWL-semantics defect, a burden-of-proof inconsistency, and several smaller category and modeling slippages that a specialist reviewer should not wave through.

**What is actually strong.** Sections 1–3 meet the dialectical brief. Established facts are separated from authority, scope, identity, and logging assumptions. The four-part noncompliance test and the stricter production burden for exceptions are the right standard. The prosecution concedes loops, CRM loss, duplicates, and label drift instead of pretending M0 is complete. The defense concedes SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-712, SUP-714, and the 14 ineligible pilots instead of treating every replay failure as obsolescence. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including M0-fitting resource/COI problems. Classifications for 701, 703, 704, 705, 706, 707, 708, 709, 712, and 714 are well supported. The layered architecture (POWL for route shape, DECLARE for cross-cutting obligations, snapshot data checks for eligibility/time/amount, org controls for identity/COI, governed overlays with automatic expiry) is the right design, and the data proposal is specific enough to be operational.

**Model semantics are not correct as written.** The revised POWL reuses the same `Transition` objects (`sanctions`, `quality`, `activate`, `manual_approval`) inside mutually exclusive XOR branches. In POWL those leaves are identified by object identity. Sharing them across `standard_route`, `auto_route`, and `emergency_route` yields overlapping children, contradictory order constraints, and an invalid exclusive choice. The required repair is trivial and standard: a fresh `Transition(label=...)` per occurrence. That this was not done is a serious miss on a scored criterion, even though `print(M1)` would still run.

The `Request_More_Info` approximation is also weaker than the answer admits. The only observed desirable loop is SUP-707: `Register → Sanctions → Request_More_Info² → Quality → …`. M1 places the loop *before* the entire decision block, so that case still would not replay. Acknowledging the limitation does not make the approximation adequate when the motivating trace is exactly the one the loop fails to accept. Putting a LOOP around the check/decision fragment, or stating that replay must use DECLARE rather than this POWL, would have been more honest.

The emergency branch overfits the log in the opposite direction: it requires `Provisional_Activate_Supplier` and a single `Daily_Transaction_Monitoring` token that the observed emergency case does not contain. Cardinality/calendar constraints cannot live in POWL; encoding them as mandatory activities invites false negatives.

**Authority handling is slightly inconsistent with the answer’s own rule.** Section 1 says an exception is not established by informal practice and that the operator must prove contemporaneous authority. PD-3 was never incorporated into CS-9; Compliance later denied approving a weakening. Under that standard SUP-702 is at best MIXED or a low-severity technical breach with strong mitigation—not a clean `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`. Medium confidence does not dissolve the contradiction. The prosecution brief also slightly strawmans the other side (“implication that formal M0 fitness is a sufficient compliance test”); the prosecution already disclaimed that claim.

**Category imprecision.** SUP-711 is not indeterminate: same source transaction ID is strong evidence of a duplicate ingest. After dedup the case is COMPLIANT; `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` misuses the label. SUP-713 is closer to COMPLIANT-with-late-change-control than a scope defect. SUP-715 as MIXED is analytically explained, but the dominant finding is simpler: control-flow COMPLIANT, separate disclosure breach, prohibited-relationship claim unproved. PD-3’s required automated master-data validation is added to the model but never applied to 702/703/714 at case level.

**Net.** This is a high-quality review memo with a broken executable model and a few classification/consistency nits. It is clearly above a mediocre split-the-difference essay and clearly below a near-flawless specialist opinion. 7.4 reflects that gap.
