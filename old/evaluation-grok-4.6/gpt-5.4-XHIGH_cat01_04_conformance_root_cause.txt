The later variant tables are exhaustive (650 and 620). Two structural facts are therefore in the data and never enter the ledger, ranking, or conclusion:

- `Request Info` is 40/600 in April–May and **zero** thereafter. None of the dated logging changes would delete it. That is either a real behavioral change or an unlisted capture break, and it directly bears on hypotheses 1, 3, and 5.
- The enhanced-track share is ~30% → ~26% → **~43.5%**. Combined with 60 `Standard Review → Supervisor Sign-off` cases, this is the most interesting policy-implementation signal in the file. A high-value rule that fires on the standard path is not what a naive “enhanced = high risk” story predicts. The answer repeatedly asks for value stratification but never notices the pattern that makes stratification urgent.

A third quantitative tension is ignored: after the stated Aug–Sep repairs, the published variant mix cannot produce 66% perfectly fitting traces against the unextended model (sign-off alone is 210/620). These are not nits. They are process-mining observations sitting on the same tables the answer otherwise mines carefully.

Collapsing June and August into one posterior ranking makes “real process change” look like a weak fifth-place leftover (68) even though, after repairs, it is the main Aug–Sep story. The text understands the period split; the table does not encode it.

A few ledger rows over-attribute. A strong April–May baseline supports “something changed after May,” not specifically labels, technical events, *and* timestamps.
