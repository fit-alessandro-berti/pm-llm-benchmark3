**7.2**

The deliverable is substantively strong: it refuses to crown a winner by averaging metrics, separates exploration from enforcement, reads S and C from the process-tree expressions rather than the scoreboard, and proposes a third model that is actually safer than S and more maintainable than C. Under a normal seminar rubric this would sit in the high 8s. Under the required hypercritical bar it does not.

**What is right.** Sections 1–2 and 6 are the best parts. The fitness/precision trade-off is use-case-relative; precision-on-a-positive-log, optionalized mandatory checks, silent-τ absorption, and duplicate-label alignment artifacts are ranked by decision impact rather than listed as equal nits. The B1–B8 matrix for S and C is almost entirely correct: S’s `+(X(a,τ),…)` block can empty out (B3) and can put Expedite before the checks (B4); C’s eight-branch XOR misses `TR→CC→AA` (B2) and every Supplier+Auto combination (B5); neither handles partial rework (B6). The third tree

`→(Register, *( +(CC, TR), CD ), +(X(SR,τ), X(ER,τ)), X(MA, AA), Release)`

enforces P1, P2, P4 (for Expedite), P5, and P7 without making the mandatory checks optional, and the dissent is a real operational argument rather than a token caveat.

**Requirement misses.** Section 3 asks for at least five *admitted* undesirable or policy-ambiguous traces. Only four are actually in the language of S (skip both checks; skip one check; Expedite before both checks; Expedite between the checks). Item 5 is a behavior S *rejects* (no second Credit Check, no Correct Dossier). Items 6–7 are either attribute-conditional or policy-legal. That is a direct miss, not a wording quibble.

Section 8 requires P1–P7 *and* B1–B8 in a four-column table including the third model. B1–B8 are deferred to §5, which has no third-model column. The three extra adversarial traces are well chosen (A2 in particular), but the required comparison is incomplete.

**Modeling gaps that are under-sold.** The third model cannot fire Correct Dossier after Supplier or Expedite Review, and it cannot interleave Supplier Review with the mandatory checks. P3 does not place Supplier Review after both checks; P6 does not restrict *when* a correction may occur. Hard-wiring “always re-run both checks, and only before the optional reviews” is a design choice, not a consequence of the missing attributes. Calling it merely “conservative” hides a class of legitimate traces the model will fail. A DECLARE/data layer, or a loop whose body includes the optional reviews, would have been the honest way to represent that uncertainty. The post-hoc `if external and SR missing` check is the right *kind* of honesty, but it is not in the model and is one misconfiguration away from approving the violation the board cares about.

**Technical sloppiness.** The compact tree in §7 drops `tau` (`X('Supplier Review', )`), so it is not a parseable artifact. The validation skeleton will not run: a PM4Py `EventLog` has no `.to_csv`, and `sklearn.train_test_split(..., stratify=case_labels)` is not a documented way to split an event log. Those are labeled “skeletons,” but they are the reproducibility program the section claims to specify. Minor: `holout`, missing hyphens, informal 1–5 scores treated carefully in §2 and then allowed back in as a deployment gate in §10 without a protocol stronger than “recruit five analysts.”

**Net.** The reasoning quality is high and the third alternative is defensible. The grade is held down because the answer fails two explicit format/content mandates, miscategorizes an S *rejection* as an admission, and leaves a real control-flow restriction (correction and Supplier Review timing) less examined than the metrics that were supposed to be treated as evidence, not ground truth.
