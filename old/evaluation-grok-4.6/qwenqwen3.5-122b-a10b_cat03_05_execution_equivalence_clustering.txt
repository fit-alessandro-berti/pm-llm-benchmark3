**5.0**

The write-up has the required eight-section shape, three named relations, a table covering X1–X8, and some sound high-level instincts (X1≡X2; activity traces of X1/X2/X5/X6/X7 coincide; X8 is a distinct return motif). It is not a reliable analysis. Definitions, signatures, and assignments contradict each other in several places that matter for this problem.

**Internal contradiction (serious).** Section 3 puts X4 in C2 (multi-package logistics) and X8 in C4 (lifecycle exception). Section 5 then says business-semantic equivalence “groups them in C4.” That is not a borderline disagreement between *relations*; it is the same relation assigned two ways. The X4–X8 paragraph also says treating them identically is risky immediately after claiming \(\equiv_{biz}\) identifies them.

**Typed-structural assignments violate the stated definition.** \(\equiv_{struc}\) is defined to keep object cardinalities and E2O incidence, while discarding only local IDs, qualifiers, and EXT *flags*. Under that definition X1 is not structural-equivalent to:
- X5 — `Pack`/`Dispatch` involve two orders and three items, not one order and two items;
- X6 — `Issue Invoice`/`Record Payment` involve two orders;
- X7 — `Dispatch` is incident to an `Item`, unlike X1.

The table still dumps X1, X2, X5, X6, X7 into one structural cluster. That is the main object-centric distinction the prompt asked for, and it is handled incorrectly.

**Canonical signature is not rename-invariant.** Objects are numbered by first appearance, then listed by *sorting the original IDs*. On X1, `Pack` sees `{I11,I12} → L1,L2`. On X2, items are introduced as I22 then I21, so `Pack` sees `{I21,I22} → L2,L1`. The tokens differ; `assert sig_x1 == sig_x2` is false. The f-string is also syntactically broken. The scheme was required to be reproducible and invariant to local renaming. It is neither.

**Preferred signature and preferred clusters do not match.** Section 2’s token stream (per-event renamed IDs, qualifiers, EXT) would separate X3, X4, X5, X6, X7, and X8. Section 3 then merges X3–X4, X5–X6, and X7–X8. There is no second aggregation rule that turns those signatures into those clusters, so the “preferred” relation is not operational.

**Semantic clusters blur distinctions the log was built to expose.** Grouping X5 with X6 as “Cross-Order **Logistics**” is a naming and conceptual error: X6 is shared *billing*, and the O2O notes stress that M61 does not allocate at order level. Grouping X7 with X8 as generic “exceptions” is allowed by the prompt’s pluralism, but it is never given a crisp invariant (what must match: a non-linear item lifecycle? any qualifier? any extra pack?). \(\equiv_{biz}\) is defined as “same operational intent and risk profile,” which is not tight enough to be reflexive/symmetric/transitive in a checkable way; the X4/X8 slip shows transitivity was not under control.

**Weaker but still material issues.**
- Activity-sequence clustering is the one part that is clean; concurrency is discussed, though the data have no same-time events.
- Section 6 does not specify PM4Py’s actual key (typically an abstracted execution/isomorphism key around a chosen object type, not raw IDs as first-class constants). The snippet uses a likely-wrong argument (`lead_object_type`), a non-existent `has_external_objects` field, and a post-process keyed on `len(members)`, which does not canonicalize anything.
- The partial-order perturbation is misread as “allow Pack after Dispatch.” The relevant test is whether independent pack/dispatch branches may be reordered, which is exactly the X3 vs X4 interleaving question.
- Compliance is assigned to typed-structural equivalence after that relation was defined to drop EXT and qualifiers — the features most relevant to shared invoices, substitutions, and incomplete settlement. That recommendation fights the rest of the essay.

Completeness of the template and a correct activity-label partition keep this out of the failure band. Definition–assignment inconsistency, a non-canonical “canonical” signature, and the X4/X8 self-contradiction block anything higher.
