The answer gets the *strategic* call right—do not copy Site A, do not treat the 9-hour drop as an identified effect, do not start Document Check before originals when the law forbids it, and do not expand past a digital-only slice without a pilot. That is not enough. Under a strict reading of the rubric, the piece is repeatedly sloppy in the places the evaluator was told to weigh: the transport graph, the numbers, the PM4Py model, and several design details that quietly reintroduce the same confounding the author diagnoses at Site A.

### What is actually good

Co-interventions are handled seriously. The API upgrade, the two extra document reviewers, the marketing mix shift (48% → 40% high-complexity), daily huddles, the FTE increase, and the overlap jump (8% → 71%) as a possible timestamp artifact are all treated as threats to identification, not as decoration. Transport confidences in Section 3 are appropriately low (often 12–35). The statutory gate on originals, role specialization (4/7/3/2), the legal-supplement loop (360/345 in the DFG), and the legacy case lock are used as real effect modifiers rather than footnotes. “Conditional go” with a digital-first slice is the defensible recommendation.

That is the ceiling. The rest of the score is lost to errors that are not cosmetic.

### Process comparison (Section 1)

The required twelve features are present and the table schema matches. Several cells are still wrong or invented. Site A rework is described as “sequential re-entry to Document Check if appeal”; the prompt never says that, and appeals (4.1%) are not the same object as rework (11.8%). The complexity row claims “Complexity did not decline at Site A” in the same breath as 48% → 40%. Queue discipline at A is “not explicitly described” even though the prompt states a six-open-task cap—then Section 4 rediscovers that cap. “Sequential IdentityDocument” is garbled. These are small, but they are exactly the kind of local unclarity the rubric said to punish.

### Transportability graph (Section 2) — fail as a graph

The required object is a directed adjacency list over a specified node set, plus selection nodes. What appears first is a single spurious chain:

`treatment_parallelization → true_overlap → digital_documents → originals_required → … → appeal`

That is not a causal graph. Digital documents are not caused by overlap; originals-required is not a descendant of digital documents; appeal is not the end of that path. The main mechanism edge the rest of the memo argues for—`true_overlap → waiting_before_assessment → cycle_time`—is missing from the proper edge list and only shows up in informal `--modulate-->` syntax that is not an adjacency list.

Selection structure is equally loose. `S_IT_case_lock → IT_case_lock` and `S_queue_huddles → queue_huddles` are near-tautologies. `S_legal_capacity → assessment_capacity` is unmotivated. `digital_documents` is never attached as a selection/modifier node even though the entire recommendation hangs on it.

The prose that follows is also over the 180–280 word cap by a wide margin (roughly 450–500 words). The ideas in the prose are better than the graph; the graph is what was asked for.

### Quantitative scenarios (Section 5) — arithmetic and eligibility errors

This section is supposed to *show the mapping* from A’s evidence and B’s constraints to ranges. It does not survive a calculator.

- Optimistic transfer: “6 to 9 hours (25% to 40% reduction from 44 h).” 6/44 ≈ 14%, 9/44 ≈ 20%. The percentages contradict the hours. The text then says this is “based on Site A’s 22% reduction, adjusted” for a heavier tail—and then quotes a *larger* percentage band than A’s.
- Eligible share is 38%, but the 6–9 h reduction is applied to the **overall** 44 h baseline. If 62% of cases are gated by an 18 h originals delay, the site-level median cannot move 6–9 hours just because the digital slice is parallelized. The later “high originals-delay” row quietly admits this (4–6 h overall) and thereby contradicts the optimistic row.
- Digital-only baselines of ~38 h median and ~75 h p90 are invented. No decomposition is given.
- Theoretical overlap savings are bounded by the shorter verification service (~3.4 h identity) plus some queue compression. Claiming 6–11 h median cuts for B, after insisting A’s 9 h is confounded, is internally inconsistent.
- Bottleneck-shift is the only candidate for “average improves, tail worsens.” It is specified as median **−1 to +3** and p90 **+2 to +8**—i.e., average is allowed to worsen. The required signature is never cleanly produced.
- Legal/rework congestion: DFG has 360 supplements, not “40 cases or so.” The same table later says “40 of 3,400, or 1.2%,” which is a different and wrong quantity.
- Assessment “already 80%+ utilized” is asserted from headcount (3 assessors, 3,400 cases) with **no assessment service time** in the data. That is not a scenario assumption; it is a fabricated utilization fact.

Confidence 68 on the kitchen-sink optimistic row (IT fix + huddles + 0.5 legal FTE + 0.5 assessor FTE + WIP + phased rollout) is especially hard to defend: that is no longer a transport of A’s treatment.

### Effect modifiers and intervention spec

Section 4 is the strongest table, but it overreaches. “Cross-train one assessor to do document triage” spends the scarcest pool. Holding verification *completion* when the assessment queue is long is an odd WIP rule (finished work is not allowed to finish). Cohort sizes drift across sections (22–28%, 25%, 38%) and Cohort C (“high-complexity or legal-flagged”) can exclude most of the book (48% complex, 40% legal) without a joint distribution.

The DFG is underused. Site B already shows `Register → Identity` (2,050) and `Register → Originals` (1,860) plus `Identity → Originals` (1,020). Cohort B’s “parallelize identity and originals receipt” is largely the *status quo*, not a transport of A. That should have been said plainly.

### Pilot (Section 7)

The table is complete against the required rows, and the mechanism-vs-implementation distinction is conceptually right. Three design problems remain first-order.

1. **SUTVA.** Four identity, seven document, three assessors, two legal. Case-level 1:1 randomization on a shared floor means treated cases steal capacity from controls. Interference is mentioned, then not designed around (no stepped-wedge, no team/week clusters as the primary assignment unit).
2. **Re-confounding.** Section 9 wants huddles + WIP + parallelization shipped together, which recreates Site A’s identification problem. The pilot table randomizes only parallel vs sequential; the recommendation text does not.
3. **Stratum 3.** “50% receive treatment C (sequential, control only)” randomizes sequential vs sequential.

The 220-word cap is missed. Power (d = 0.35, n ≈ 100, ~70%) is decorative.

### PM4Py (Section 8) — the clearest technical fail

The prompt asked for `pm4py.parse_process_tree`, `pm4py.convert_to_petri_net`, `pm4py.objects.powl.obj` used as in the manual, explicit order edges so Assessment waits for both checks, `check_is_workflow_net` / `check_soundness`, optional `play_out`, and **no sequential Site B model as a simplification**.

The code does not do that.

- Imports are invented (`pm4py.objects.process_tree.utils.parse`, `pm4py.conversion.process_tree_to_petri_net`, `pm4py.verification.workflow_net_property`, `Sequence`/`Choice`/`Loop`/`Parallel`/`Tau`/`POWL(name=...)`). Standard POWL leaves are `Transition`/`SilentTransition`; `StrictPartialOrder` is `StrictPartialOrder(nodes=...)` plus `order.add_edge`, not `order=[(a,b), ...]`.
- Site B is a single `StrictPartialOrder` that puts **Assessment → Legal, Assessment → Approve, and Assessment → Reject** in the order relation at once. That is AND, not XOR: a valid linear extension must execute legal *and* approve *and* reject.
- `Legal → SupplementDoc → SupplementAssessment → Legal` is a **cycle**. A strict partial order is acyclic. The supplement loop had to be an `OperatorPOWL` LOOP (or equivalent), with XOR for legal vs skip and approve vs reject.
- On constructor failure the code **falls back to a sequential `Sequence(...)`**, which the prompt explicitly forbids.
- The “representative output” (place/transition counts, soundness `True`, three traces including a legal loop) is not produced by this program. It is fiction attached to non-runnable constructors.

The limitations comment is fine. It does not rescue a model that cannot encode the control flow the rest of the memo specifies.

### Recommendation and assumptions (Sections 9–10)

Conditional go is correct. The 250–400 word cap is blown through (roughly 2–3×). The extra “FINAL SUMMARY” repeats the same claims with another confidence band (45–58%) that does not match Section 5’s 68. The eight assumptions are the right list and are testable; this is one of the cleaner sections.

### Bottom line

This is a competent consulting memo with a broken formal core. It would be a mid-to-high score if the rubric only rewarded qualitative transport instincts. The rubric also required a real selection diagram, internally consistent scenario arithmetic, a non-cyclic POWL that preserves XOR and the Assessment join, and a pilot that isolates the mechanism. Those are not nits, and they are not close. **5.6**.
