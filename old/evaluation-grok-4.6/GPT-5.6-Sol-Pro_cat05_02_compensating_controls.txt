The answer is a strong, structured control assessment that generally reasons from objectives, timing, independence, coverage, recoverability, and evidence quality rather than activity counts or amounts. It still has a few material judgment problems that keep it well short of near-flawless.

**What it gets right.** Section 1 decomposes FEP-1 into real threat scenarios, not a restatement of the rule, and is accurate on AV-2’s narrow coverage, override weakness, and inability to prove receipt or collusion. Section 2’s sufficiency vector is the right test, and the rejection of “same expected loss” as the only standard is precise. Most case calls are defensible: AP-202 and AP-208 are clear failures; AP-205 is correctly treated as a blocked incomplete payment; AP-206 is correctly left open because the token/precondition fact is unresolved; AP-203/AP-207 are distinguished from convenience same-user cases; post-release/pre-settlement manager action is correctly labeled hybrid. The monitoring section properly treats pair concentration and shared workstations as alerts, not proof. The code meets the specification, reconstructs the log in-memory, separates AP-205, joins case facts, emits preliminary flags, and documents PM4Py polarity/`keep_violations` version risk.

**Main defect: AP-204.** Classifying a paid fabricated receipt as `INDETERMINATE` confuses “collusion is unproven” with “the control objective was not shown to fail.” The package did not cover commercial substance; RA-7 found the defect after the recall window; residual risk materialized. Under the answer’s own test, that is ineffective or at best partial, not indeterminate. The caution about not proving one person controlled both actions is fair, but it is the wrong decisive question for sufficiency.

**Related hedging errors.** AP-209 is over-hedged the same way. No defect was found, four-eyes form and timely audit exist, and the pair/workstation pattern is systemic residual risk. `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` would match the evidence better than treating missing org-design documents as blocking classification. AP-203/AP-207 as `OBJECTIVE_MET` is slightly generous while L3 status and, for AP-207, actual hold/cancel authority remain unverified. AP-201 as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is arguable as “nonconforming but tolerable,” but the answer underweights that ED-1 is the only permitted same-user path and the token was absent by convenience.

**Smaller issues.** AP-205’s severity cell mixes realized loss with a control signal. The narrative sometimes slides between FEP-1’s segregation objective and broader payment-integrity objectives without saying which label is being applied. The 24-hour recall proxy is well flagged in code, but Section 3 still leans on it as if it were the RA-7 business-day rule.

The piece is unusually complete and mostly disciplined. It is not a 9+ answer because the AP-204/AP-209 classifications fail the sufficiency test the author themselves defined.
