**7.4**

The answer is a strong, structured control-objective analysis that generally does what the prompt asked: it does not treat four-eyes form as the objective, it does not treat “a control fired” as “the control was enough,” and it does not grade cases by amount alone. Sections 1, 2, 4, and 6 are the best parts. The grade is held well below the top of the scale because of one overconfident classification, an internal contradiction in control typing, a likely non-executable PM4Py call, and several precise overclaims.

**What is actually good.** FEP-1 is decomposed into real threat scenarios (unilateral fraud, clerical error, BEC/account takeover, fictitious performance, collusion/shared credentials, emergency-privilege creep), and AV-2 / MG-4 / RA-7 / ED-1 are mapped by what they can and cannot cover. The AV-2 blind spots (no receipt test, no collusion test, no commercial reasonableness, no compromised-account attribution) are used later, not just recited. The sufficiency test is multidimensional (timing, independence, coverage, overrideability, recoverability, evidence quality, collusion resistance), and the rejection of “same expected loss” as the sole standard is the right argument: tail risk, normalization of deviance, adaptive adversaries, design effectiveness versus outcome luck. AP-204 and AP-209 are the decisive tests of the prompt, and both are handled correctly: distinct users plus a fabricated receipt is `OBJECTIVE_FAILED`; the same pair with a clean audit and missing org-design evidence is `INDETERMINATE`. AP-205 is correctly `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`. AP-202 and AP-208 are correctly failed even though AP-208’s payment was legitimate. Pre-settlement / post-release manager review is given a coherent hybrid reading (workflow-detective, cash-outflow-preventive), which is what the prompt asked for.

**AP-206 is the main substantive error.** ED-1 condition 1 is explicit: the emergency token must be active *before* `Prepare_Payment`. The token is timestamped at 13:02; prepare is at 13:00. The case file itself offers two readings (logging delay versus failed precondition). The answer picks the convenient reading, labels the case `OBJECTIVE_MET`, and assigns **High** confidence. That is not available on this record. The same row then calls the severity “procedural nonconformance,” which contradicts `OBJECTIVE_MET`. AP-201, which also has independent pre-settlement review and a clean AV-2 pass, is only `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` because the bypass was unjustified. If token-before-prepare is a real preventive gate rather than paperwork, AP-206 cannot be a clean meet. `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` or `INDETERMINATE` with medium confidence is the defensible call.

**The case table fights Section 4.** After arguing that manager action after `Release_Payment` but before settlement is financially preventive / hybrid, the findings table parks those reviews under detective controls for AP-201 and AP-207. That is not a wording quibble: the whole point of the assignment is timing semantics. If the intercept can still stop settlement, it does not belong in the same bucket as a next-day audit.

**Other case nits.** AP-201’s audit is ~25 hours after release. RA-7’s *audit* SLA is one business day; the 24-hour clock is the *recall* window. Treating 24.9 hours as a control defect conflates those clocks; 5 May 09:00 can still be next-business-day. Settlement timestamps for AP-202 and AP-204 are stated as facts but are not in the log (only inferred from “funds not recovered” / late audit). For AP-202 that inference is fair; writing “Bank settlement completed May 4” as if logged is not. “Rubber-stamped” for m09 is an inference. m21’s independence is assumed; the fact table never certifies it. AP-205’s counterfactual claim that AV-2 drives failure probability to **zero** is absolute in a way the rest of the answer knows better than to be (invoice-number variants, duplicate-detection gaps). The AP-202 counterfactual mixes a realized outcome (loss already happened) with a prior probability.

**Section 7 is complete in shape and weak in the one API the prompt named.** The pipeline does the required seven things: in-memory CSV, UTC parse, `format_dataframe`, four-eyes filtering, blocked-attempt isolation, joined case facts, evidence flags, and an explicit “not a verdict” banner. Two defects keep it from being clean. First, `keep_violations=` is not a documented argument to `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`; current high-level signatures take the two activities and return traces that *satisfy* four-eyes. This will typically raise `TypeError`, which means the code as written is not “executable.” The correct, version-stable pattern is to take the filter output as the conforming set and compute violators as the complement of completed Prepare+Release cases. Documenting version drift does not make an invented kwarg run. Second, repeated-pair and shared-workstation flags are copied from a hand-built facts table rather than derived from the log (u12/u13 appear together twice; that was computable). AP-206’s ED-1 status is a hardcoded special case instead of a derived partial-eligibility rule. `numpy` is unused.

**What keeps the score from collapsing.** The answer never maps “same user ⇒ fail” or “distinct users ⇒ pass.” It does not treat AV-2 clean-pass as proof of receipt. It does not excuse AP-208 because the vendor was honest. Collusion resistance, overrideability, and recall-window recoverability actually drive the classifications. Monitoring recommendations correctly separate alerts (pair concentration, shared workstation, late ratification) from proof of misconduct. Those are the hard parts of the task, and they are mostly right.

**Net.** This is a high-quality review with a real control-objective spine, not a checklist dump. It is not near-flawless: one ED-1 precondition is waved through at high confidence, preventive/detective labels are inconsistent with the author’s own timing theory, and the mandated PM4Py check is written against an API that likely does not exist. Under a strict reading those are enough to keep the grade in the mid-7s.
