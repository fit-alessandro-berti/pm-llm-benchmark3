The write-up has the required seven-heading skeleton and some real process-mining vocabulary (resource-feasible missing-start intervals, elapsed vs final service, three imputation scenarios). Quantitatively it is not reliable. Under a strict reading that is disqualifying.

That is not rounding noise. Several required rows contradict the same row’s resource note (P15) or the cutoff definition (P16/P18/P19/P21/P22). Completed-case **total sojourn** is never tabulated, even though it is identified for every completed case once S2 end and arrival are known (P01=60 … P14=230).

**Missing-start logic is only half-done.**  
P05 / P07 / P11 resource windows are reasonable under FIFO + non-overlap. The answer then treats 0-minute service as a live endpoint without a plausibility bound from the rest of the I1/C1/C2 sample (~20–50 min). It also never uses the invariance that, when queue-entry and completion are observed, **station sojourn is known** and only the wait/service split is uncertain. Section 6 first says P05/P07/P11/P15 sojourns “shift by 10–20 min,” then says missing starts “do not alter total sojourn bounds significantly.” Both cannot stand; the first is false for those four cases.

**Station metrics (Section 4) do not match the clock.**  
“08:00–10:00 / 10:00–12:00 / 12:00–14:00” arrivals 10 / 6 / 6 sum to 22 and line up with **case-ID blocks** (P01–P10, P11–P16, P17–P22), not timestamps. External S1 arrivals in 08:00–10:00 are 13 (P01–P13), not 10; 10:00–12:00 are 7–8, not 6; 12:00–14:00 is essentially P22, not 6. Completions and WIP inherit that binning.  

Utilization is worse: busy minutes in a **2-hour** slice are divided by **360** (or 345) minutes (e.g. 185/360 = 51.4%, 85/345 = 24.6%). The correct denominators are on the order of 240 resource-minutes (minus the C2 outage in the relevant window). Reported utilizations are therefore about half of a coherent occupancy rate and cannot support a bottleneck claim.

**WIP, Little’s law, resources.**  
No event-ordered WIP(t) path is given, despite the required simultaneous-event convention. S2 “queue length grows to 9 by 14:00” is false: at 14:00, S2 has **4 waiting** (P16, P18–P20) and **2 in service** (P15, P17); system WIP is **8** (plus those). Little’s law is named in §2 and given a residual “0.8–1.4” in §6 with **no** λ, L, or W. Per-resource busy time (I1/I2/C1/C2) is not produced. C2 down 10:55–11:10 is “25% of S2 capacity”; it is **50%**.

**Bottleneck story is only directionally fair.**  
S1 is initially overloaded and S2 is the slower two-server station; P08 starting at 11:15 after the C2 outage is a good observation. A1 asserts FIFO, but **P09 overtakes P08** onto C1 at 11:00 while P08 has been ready since 10:20 — unmentioned. Late S1 waits are systematically understated (P20–P22), so continued S1 congestion at 14:00 is underplayed. The qualitative “S1 warmup then S2 bottleneck” can survive, but the numbers attached to it do not.

**Python (§7) does not implement the analysis.**  
`MISSING`, `OPEN`, and blank are all parsed to `NaT` — the opposite of the spec. S2 wait is coded as `s2_start - arrival_s1`, not `s2_start - s1_end`. Missing starts are dropped rather than bounded. No WIP path and no optimistic/central/pessimistic scenarios are computed. `pm4py.OBJECT_PARSER.Variants.INTER_EVENT_LOG` and the `pm4py.algo.analysis.service_waiting_time` / `arrival_finish_rate` imports are not the required `pm4py.insert_*` surface; failures are swallowed by `try/except`. The script cannot reproduce Sections 3–6.

**What remains credit-worthy:** labeled A1–A8, rounding as ±2.5 / differences ±5, OPEN treated as elapsed lower bounds rather than dropped, a valid-projection caveat for case-level PM4Py semantics, and a reasonable comment that `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is a poor fit under censoring. That is a template, not a reconstruction.

The document reads finished and is not internally calculable from the given table. On a rubric that punishes even small quantitative defects, this is a low-to-mid score.
