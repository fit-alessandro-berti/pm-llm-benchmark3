**8.2**

This is a strong, well-structured audit that hits the intended month-level story and mostly keeps evidence, hypothesis, and action apart. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several conceptual slippages, a few overconfident operational calls, and a code sketch that would not actually produce the evidence the rest of the memo relies on keep it well short of a top score.

## What is actually right

The central chronology is correct and is the main reason the score stays high. April is treated as a schema/lineage break, not organic model decay. June is a routing-mix change with a stable remote-fix edge. July is an early V3/supplier warning. August–September are overlapping process, deployed-concept, and (from September) population effects. October is an evaluation-validity problem, and the observed 0.16 breach rate is not treated as recovery. Falsifiers are stated. Alert dependencies correctly stop a maturity alarm from becoming a retrain. Those are the things the prompt is actually grading.

That is the floor, not a reason to forgive the rest.

## Conceptual problems

**1. Covariate drift is overloaded until it stops being a diagnosis.**  
June’s V2 jump is filed under covariate drift, process drift, and “path/mix drift.” At the stated prediction time — immediately after diagnosis — realized V1/V2/V3 is mostly still in the future. A downstream routing-policy change is process drift; if those path indicators are not legitimate diagnosis-time features, it is also a change in \(P(Y \mid X)\). Calling it covariate drift is the wrong box. The memo later warns against leaking realized variant into training, which quietly contradicts the table. April’s pipeline-induced missingness is also parked under covariate drift “although caused by pipeline failure.” That is double-counting and blurs the one month that should be cleanly labeled a data-pipeline failure.

**2. “Need not be concept drift” in June is only half-argued.**  
Within-path V2 timing and breach look stable, so *path-conditional* concept drift is not required. The deployed model, however, scores before the path is chosen. A policy that sends a larger share of the same diagnosis-time cases through a safer treatment changes \(P(Y \mid X_{\text{diagnosis}})\) unless intended route is already in \(X\). The June pattern — prevalence 0.15, mean prediction 0.20, AUROC still 0.79, ECE 0.060 — is exactly what that looks like: ranking mostly intact, base rate wrong. The memo treats this as “old aggregate calibration on a safer mix,” which is operationally fair, but it never states the information-set condition that would make “not concept drift” true. Section 4 notices the issue; Section 2 does not. That is a real hole, not a wording nit.

**3. Concept-drift timing is inconsistent across sections.**  
The table implicates July–September. The narrative correctly calls July a transition and concentrates the deployed-concept claim in August. July’s aggregate calibration is almost fine (0.18 vs 0.19) while V3 breach moves 0.35 → 0.46. That is an early stratum shift, not yet the same claim as August (0.19 vs 0.28, AUROC 0.68, V3 0.64). “High in Aug for the deployed feature set” is the right sentence; “Jul–Sep” in the table is sloppy.

**4. Process drift in October is asserted more firmly than the memo’s own censoring caveat allows.**  
155 h on `Order Part → Part Received` is directionally consistent with July–September, but the same paragraph says October edge estimates may be distorted by event availability. “High” confidence for October process drift is too high relative to that caveat. The supplier story does not need October.

**5. Population vs mediation is described, then under-used.**  
The memo says Partner Z is more rural, older-device, and 58% V3, and that labels mature more slowly. It still lets “population shift” sit as a peer of concept/process drift for September without saying how much residual source effect would remain after variant × geography × device × vintage. “High for population shift; medium for its independent performance effect” is the right hedge; the September narrative still reads as if Partner Z is a fourth distinct engine rather than mostly a mixer of V3, unmodeled covariates, and slower labels.

## Evidence and metric handling

Numbers are generally cited accurately. The problems are inferential.

- April’s 18% unmapped share is correctly called an instrumentation artifact, but the covariate-drift row still treats April missingness as an input-distribution change. Pick one.
- May is used as a natural experiment that confirms the April pipeline story (unknowns 17% → 2%, AUROC 0.67 → 0.78). That is good. Calling May “generally usable” for training is not. The audit says the cached pre-diagnosis count was not fully recomputed and duplicate removal was only applied after 20 April. May can still carry stale lineage. “Usable after verifying cache version” buries the stronger claim: May is *conditionally* usable, and the condition is not shown to be met.
- August at 93% maturity is treated as almost decisive. That is probably right for the 130 h edge and the V3 rate jump, but “censoring cannot plausibly account for the magnitude” is stronger than the data. A 7-point maturity hole that is biased toward long V3 paths can move aggregate breach and ECE. The direction of the diagnosis survives; the claim that maturity is nearly irrelevant does not.
- September’s 0.45 vs 0.27 Partner Z gap is presented with a maturity caveat and then used anyway as population evidence. Without an equal-vintage comparison, it is not yet evidence of a source effect.
- ECE is later described as unsafe under prevalence change, which is correct, and then used in the table as if it were a stable drift detector across June (base rate 0.15) and August (0.28). The memo knows better than its own table.

## Section 3: tests

This is the second-best section, and still not tight enough.

The required tests are all present, and most have two competing predicted results. Several are over-specified relative to what the data can support. Propensity/transport for Partner Z in the first September–October window, with 82%/55% maturity and a new source at 24–27%, will be identification theater unless overlap and vintage are shown first. The memo mentions overlap checks, then proceeds as if the transport estimator is ready to adjudicate.

The same-path test is the right test and is the one place concept vs covariate can actually be separated. It is buried in a wide table and never ranked above PSI/JS “feature drift” tests, which cannot separate mix from a changed conditional.

Supplier interrupted time series is a good addition. Outcome-definition audit is slightly ornamental given “no major outcome-definition change was documented,” unless the point is to verify the October API path specifically.

Missing: a concrete diagnosis-time information freeze — rebuild features with a hard cutoff at the normalized diagnosis event and refuse any post-diagnosis path or lead-time field. The leakage risk is discussed in Section 6; it is not a first-class test in Section 3.

## Section 4: decisions that run ahead of the evidence

Containment is proportionate: quarantine April and October, fix lineage, do not globally cut thresholds off September/October. That part is good.

The weaker calls:

- A temporary V3 recalibrator on mature July–August data treats a regime *change* as a calibration problem. July is the start of the break, August is the new severe regime. Pooling them is mixing pre- and post-break intercepts. If AUROC inside V3 is the thing that fell, recalibration is already admitted to be insufficient. Proposing it as the short-term default anyway is a hedge that can freeze in a wrong intercept.
- June is “safe and useful” for training. It is useful as a *policy-mix* month. It is not automatically safe as an unweighted training draw if later production is V3-heavy; it will down-weight the failure mode that matters from August.
- July as a labeled-regime training month is optimistic. Transition months are where a single model learns an average of two conditionals.
- Separate models are correctly postponed. Hierarchical interactions are asserted as preferable “at first” with no sample-size or knowability check beyond a paragraph of caveats. Fine as a preference, not as a decision.

Training-month safety is directionally right (Jan–Mar safe; April unsafe; October unsafe). May/July/June are graded too generously.

## Section 5: architecture vs the code

The monitor stack and the dependency graph are the right design. Label-maturity and schema alarms gate performance pages and retraining. That is what was asked for.

The Python sketch does not support the memo above it.

- After `format_dataframe`, PM4Py typically exposes XES names (`case:concept:name`, `concept:name`, `time:timestamp`). Subsequent `groupby(CASE)` / `filter_time_range(..., case_id_key=CASE)` with `CASE = "case_id"` is version-fragile and likely wrong. A one-line caveat does not make the sketch executable.
- `discover_performance_dfg` values are often a statistic map, not a scalar number of seconds. `float(perf_dfg[edge]) / 3600` will fail or silently misread. The entire qualitative case uses **medians**; the code emits a **mean** and then admits, in prose, that a mean must not replace the median. Then it still returns the mean.
- There is no computation of the median `Order Part → Part Received` duration from consecutive events, which is the one process statistic the diagnosis actually uses.
- `get_variants` is dumped as raw variant strings. The audit’s object of interest is V1/V2/V3/unmapped. The code never maps traces onto those four buckets, so it cannot reproduce the variant table that drives June and August–September.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is called and then reduced to a row count. That is cargo-cult compliance with the function list.
- `duplicate_rows_removed_total` is a global constant repeated on every monthly row. It is not a monthly quality metric.
- Event-calendar vs case-start is explained well in the docstring and then not reflected in how the part-edge should be interpreted under right-censoring. October’s exact problem is left to the reader.

The prompt asked for an auditable monthly extract, not a model. This is a function-name checklist with the wrong summaries.

## Section 6: risks

The four requested failure modes are covered, plus several more. Most mitigations are generic but pointed in the right direction. Two problems:

- Risk 4 (do not retrain on June) is cleaner than Section 4, which called June a safe training month. The memo disagrees with itself.
- Risk 5 correctly refuses a raw fairness reading of Partner Z, then does not say what residual gap would count as a real disparity. The mitigation is a method list, not a decision rule.

## Strictness summary

The piece would be in the high 9s if diagnosis-time information sets were kept clean, drift types were not reused as synonyms for “something changed,” May/July training advice were actually conditional, September Partner Z were not treated as mature evidence, and the code computed median part-edge times plus V1/V2/V3 shares on both cohort definitions.

It is not that memo. It is a very good walkthrough with a few real category errors, some overconfident month-safety calls, and a monitoring snippet that would not generate the evidence the argument needs. That is an 8.2, not a 9.
