**3.2**

The write-up has the required nine headings and some usable operational language, but it is not internally consistent, does not do the requested capacity work, and does not deliver the specified Python. Under a strict reading it fails the quality bar.

**Internal contradiction that collapses the analysis.** Section 2 computes about 294 + 659 + 473 + 754 + 539 = **2,719 minutes** of base work, then asserts “~1300 mins of pure service time” and “systemic utilization around 60%.” That is not a rounding issue; it is an arithmetic break with the author’s own lines. Available time is also wrong: five 8-hour shifts minus 30-minute breaks plus Farah’s 4 hours is **41.5 hours (2,490 min)**, not “roughly 36 hours (2160 mins).” Offered load before multipliers is already near or above staffed minutes; after 1.25–1.40 multipliers, handovers, setup, and coordination it is tighter still. Every later utilization, SLA, and “manageable base case” claim is therefore unearned.

**Offered-load work is not what was asked.** The task requires time-block/skill analysis under base and **U1–U5**. The answer averages mix percentages (unweighted, not arrival-weighted), never builds a block-by-skill table, never converts shifts into skill-hours (Farah gone after 12:00, Deepa from 10:00, Emil from 12:00), and never applies speed multipliers to load. U2’s “double then renormalize” is not computed. U4 is a one-line variance remark. U5 is qualitative. Midpoints of uniform ranges are fine as means, but they are then treated as if they support SLA percentages.

**ME/ES sequential default is a process error.** Sequential ME adds the second branch to elapsed time (order ~26–33 minutes of extra critical path). Parallel pays 8 minutes coordination (and possibly 7 minutes handover). Defaulting Hybrid to sequential “to avoid 8+7” is usually the wrong SLA trade unless the second resource is unavailable. The text never compares those magnitudes. Preemption’s 10% remaining-time restart, the one-preemption cap, overtime ≤30 minutes, and the extra 5-minute team-crossing delay are mostly absent from both math and code.

**Objectives are misspecified.** Objective 1 caps each term at 0.90, so there is no gain past the SLA floor and no extra P1 weight, contradicting “strictly penalizing P1 violations heavily.” Objective 2 is bare min-max utilization; minimizing \(\max U_i\) alone rewards idle equalization, not service. Decision variables are a list, not a mapping into the policies.

**Section 4 does not meet the table contract.** Required columns include `P2_SLA`, `utilization_fairness`, and `handover_or_setup_burden`. P2/P3 are fused; handover burden is missing. U4 is omitted entirely. U2 and U3 omit Policy B; U5 omits Policy A. Cells are invented ranges (e.g., base P1 92/98/96%, U2-A P1 50%) with no link to Section 2. P3’s clock is 24 hours; reporting P3 SLA of 75–85% as if it were a same-shift target is not explained. “Estimates” is an honest label, not a substitute for a scale, a bound, or a method.

**Policies are implementable in outline, not in audit terms.** Specialization at multiplier ≤1.10 plus Farah-only Intake/Validation leaves Software as essentially Emil (Chen is 1.25). That single-server implication is never used in the load section. Pooling’s “SERPT” is actually assign-to-earliest-finish. Hybrid’s “queue > 3,” “60% of SLA,” and holding Deepa idle above 85% utilization are underspecified and, for Deepa, operationally costly. These are still the strongest pages in the document.

**Recommendation overclaims.** Policy C is a defensible *class* of rule, and not choosing Policy B only for base mean delay is correct. Calling C “most robust across all tested uncertainties” is not supported: U4 is untested, U5 still shows 75% P1 and “P3 complete starvation,” and B’s U5 “thrashing” is a story, not a result. The fallback (freeze P3, force sequential when max util >90% for 60 minutes) can lengthen P1 critical path under the same shock it is meant to absorb.

**Monitoring section misses the required attribute set.** Queue entry, assignment decision, eligible set, planned vs actual service, pause/resume, setup, policy version, and SLA clock are largely absent. Putting lifecycle states in `concept:name` in the prose would break discovery; the code does the opposite. That inconsistency is not resolved.

**Section 8 fails the explicit checklist.** Shifts, breaks, block demand, and U1–U5 tables are not encoded. Only a Policy C stub exists: independent cases, random arrivals over 480 minutes, Ada/Deepa|Chen/Ben hardcoded, no contention, no preemption, no parallel path, no three-policy runner, no scenario loop, no seeds/uncertainty, no comparison table. `start_timestamp` is not emitted. `pm4py.get_service_time` is never called. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is wrapped in `try/except` and not labeled as case-level (and would be misleading even if it ran, because there is no queueing). A scaffold is allowed only if omitted mechanisms are identified; here they are mostly just omitted.

**Section 9 is the second-best page**, but the “off-peak” ring-fence of 12:00–16:00 ES work is the peak ES window (and the U2 window). Limits about bursts and unmodeled fatigue are fair; they do not repair the unused demand table or the 1,300-vs-2,719 break.

**What remains creditworthy:** uniform + Bernoulli service assumption; some correct activity means; Software ineligibility of Ada/Ben/Deepa; qualitative U2/U3 bottleneck direction; three named dispatch ideas; refusal to pick a winner on base average delay alone; shadow-mode / P1 bypass as a safety idea.

That is a structured sketch with a fatal load error, a missing U4 comparison, a wrong sequential default, and non-compliant code. It is far from a near-flawless robust-policy analysis.
