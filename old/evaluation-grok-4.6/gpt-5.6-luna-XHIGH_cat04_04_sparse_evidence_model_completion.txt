## Material defects

**1. The permissive model’s extra gates are language-redundant, and the answer does not notice.**  
Once `g = R` is added, the constraints are only `R ≺ S ≺ A`. That language already contains every linear extension of the `C`-gate and `E`-gate branches. The “eight alternatives” are therefore six balanced branches plus one new branch, with two dead duplicates. The text says the `g = R` branch is “the only behavior that is not in the balanced candidate,” which is true of *new traces* and false as a claim about *structure*. A rigorous answer would have written the permissive language as the balanced language plus a single early-`S` PO, or would have replaced the gate XOR with one PO. Copying the balanced constructor and adding `R` to the gate list is how this happened. It makes the pair look more like a patched variant than a separately designed hypothesis.

**2. S5 is not reconciled with the observed skip rate.**  
Seven of 30 cases lack `S` (23%). S5 says roughly 5–10% of supplier events may be missing. Even under a generous reading, missingness cannot explain the whole no-`S` mass; several cases must be genuine skips if S5 is in the right ballpark. The answer repeatedly says absence “conflates” stocked and missing, which is true and insufficient. The quantitative mismatch is the strongest argument that a real no-`S` route is required, and it is unused.

**3. The balanced supplier gate over-symmetrizes asymmetric data.**  
`R C S E …` occurs twice. `R E S C …` occurs zero times. D5’s wording supports symmetry; the log does not. The answer admits `R E S C A P H` as “plausible, but not demonstrated,” then still makes the symmetric `E`-gate a first-class balanced construct rather than a sensitivity branch. A stricter balanced model would have treated “`S` may follow `C` before `E`” as evidenced and “`S` may follow `E` before `C`” as unobserved generalization, i.e., closer to what the permissive slot is for. S1 (credit before engineering) and the one-sided V5 pattern point the same way. The answer sees the pieces and still builds a symmetric gate.

**4. “Underfitting risks” for the permissive model mixes the opposite error.**  
“The early-`S` branch may be too permissive” is over-generalization, not underfitting. The required heading is underfitting; the content needed to say so and then separately list precision/over-generalization risks. Retries are a real underfit, but they are shared by all three models and do not characterize the permissive candidate.
