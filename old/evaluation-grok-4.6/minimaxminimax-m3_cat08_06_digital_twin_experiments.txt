**6.2**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly aligned with the required eight sections. It correctly refuses an independent-case DES, keeps calendars first-class, refuses to treat objections as observed events, and does not claim optimality. Under a strict reading, that is the floor for a passing design, not the ceiling. Several design, statistical, and PM4Py errors are material.

**What is actually strong.** Cross-case structure is named and scoped: architect bursts, district inspection bundles, district-level objection shocks, and a shared legal pool. Service versus waiting is not silently collapsed; the missing-start-timestamp problem is stated and carried into the skeleton. The experiment table covers B0, D1–D10, and four stress cells. Uncertainty treatment includes replication, bootstrap intervals for tails, Morris/Sobol, CRN, and a data-gap sweep. Decision criteria include median, tails, SLA by type, utilization, rework, backlog, and district/architect equity, and the Pareto framing is the right refusal of a single “winner.”

**Simulation-design flaws.** The 90-day warm-up is too short for the stated process. Complex-variance medians are 140 days and p90 is 260 days; discarding one quarter does not flush those trajectories or stabilize long-horizon queues, and “one seasonal quarter” also fails to cover the dual March–May / September–November peaks. Section 3.8 then randomizes the seasonal calendar across replications. That fights the actual non-stationarity (fixed calendar multipliers, Monday 2.2×, council weeks, D4 peak-month inspector). Shifting seasonality misaligns demand with calendars and with D4, so the intervention comparison is no longer ceteris paribus. Calling the matrix a “full-factorial” is simply wrong: D1–D9 are one-at-a-time cells plus one packaged bundle. Inspection no-shows, applicant readiness, and the log’s synthetic loop labels (`Completeness rework`, `Inspection rework`) are under-specified relative to the brief. D1’s “~50% fewer missing-doc loops” is an invented effect size presented as if it were a calibrated lever.

**Process-mining and code defects.** `pm4py.organize_logs` is not a real convenience API. `pm4py.discover_tree_inductive` is the old name; current PM4Py uses `discover_process_tree_inductive`. After `convert_to_event_log`, `event_log['case:concept:name'].nunique()` is invalid: an `EventLog` is not a DataFrame, so the “sanity” block crashes. `TOP_20_ARCHITECTS` / `TOP20_BURST_MULTIPLIER` are referenced and never defined. The skeleton has arrival, routing, calendar, and experiment placeholders, but almost no executable structure for bundles, objection processes, or shared case state—the part the prompt forbade treating as an afterthought. Inter-event gaps minus a sampled service time is circular if that service distribution is itself inferred from the same gaps; the text admits the limitation and then still uses that loop as the estimation plan.

**Validation and decision-rule looseness.** KS \(p>0.05\) on 80% of distributions is a poor acceptance rule (sample size, multiple testing, and practical significance are ignored). Deterministic replay with frozen timestamps tests bookkeeping, not the stochastic digital twin. Utilization ±15% and out-of-sample ±10% are arbitrary, and a chronological 12/6 split is not guaranteed to contain both seasonal peaks. `fairness_district` is defined as a CV and then used as if “increase” unambiguously means “worse equity,” which is easy to misread in a decision table.

**Net.** This is a competent consulting outline with the right headings and the right refusals, not a nearly flawless twin design. The warm-up/seasonality contradiction, the EventLog-as-DataFrame bug, fabricated or stale PM4Py names, and underspecified cross-case machinery are enough to keep it well below the top band.
