**5.0**

The write-up follows the required outline and gets the main AS/TS assignments right, but it is not close to a high score under a strict reading. Several definitions are sloppy, one robustness claim is factually wrong, the “preferred” relation does not actually refine TS on this set, and the pm4py / recommendation sections contradict both the library and the paper’s own TS definition.

**What is solid.**  
AS clusters are correct: `{X1,X2,X5,X6,X7}`, `{X3}`, `{X4}`, `{X8}`. TS correctly merges only X1–X2 (rename + swapped `Add Item` order) and splits X5/X6 on `EXT-*`, X7 on Dispatch–Item incidence, and X3/X4/X8 on package/delivery/payment/return structure. Local IDs are not treated as meaningful. The six required pairwise comparisons appear. The table is complete.

**Equivalence design is weaker than it looks.**  
AS is said to discard “repeated events”; a label sequence keeps those repeats (two `Add Item`s). Tie-breaking is both “arbitrary” and “lexicographic.” The TS bijection is left unnamed and the “equal up to” clauses are not fully specified. BS is not an independent relation: it is “same `sig`,” and on X1–X8 that `sig` induces **the same partition as TS**. The claim that BS “separates X7 further” does not change any assignment. The problem asked for equivalences that can disagree; here only AS vs everything else disagrees. Mentioning a fourth partial-order relation without defining it does not help. The preferred clustering is also nearly discrete (only X1=X2 merge), which sits poorly next to “do not assume the finest partition is best” and next to unused, more interesting BS coarsenings (isolated vs shared; split logistics; exception/return).

**Signatures are not reproducible as used.**  
Section 2’s function is a reasonable sketch (per-event type/ext counts + motif flags + qualifier *set*), but the table’s “Defining signature” column is informal prose, not that function’s value. O2O is reduced to an unsorted set of qualifier names, so `packs`/`carries`/`partially_settles`/`removed_before_dispatch`/`replaces` are not located on edges. Identity of items across events is not threaded; only per-event cardinalities are. That is a legitimate design, but then it should be applied uniformly in the table.

**Section 4 is thin and generic.**  
Risks are one-liners. “Traditional flattening” is never named (case projection? activity DFG? one object type?). Stability remarks are often tautological for singleton clusters.

**Section 5 is mostly right, with slips.**  
X1–X5/X6/X7 and X3–X4 are the right disagreement patterns. X1–X2 barely shows a disagreement at all. X4–X8 claims that collapsing payment multiplicity could make X4 “look like baseline with two dispatches”; that is still not the baseline (two packs, two deliveries).

**Section 6 is factually shaky.**  
`cluster_equivalent_ocel` is the Adams et al. equivalent-execution construction; the designed key is *not* “keep raw object identifiers.” Over-fine clusters can happen from neighborhood encoding / timestamps / implementation details, but stating that the key “preserves exact object identifiers” inverts the point of the method. The call `pm4py.cluster_equivalent_ocel(ocel, parameters={"lead_object_type": lead_type})` does not match the usual `(ocel, object_type, parameters=None)` API. `clusters.items()` is treated as lists of executions rather than sub-OCELs. `max_objs` commentary is directionally fine.

**Section 7 contains a clear error.**  
X3 has **two packs and one dispatch**, not “two packs and two dispatches with `Pack_i < Dispatch_i`.” That mistake undermines the partial-order perturbation. “Ignore qualifiers ⇒ X7 merges into SEM-BASELINE under AS” is vacuous: AS already merged them. Under the stated perturbation alone, X7 should remain separate in TS/BS because Dispatch still carries an Item.

**Section 8 fights itself.**  
Compliance is assigned “TS with exact identifiers.” TS was defined to *discard* local identifiers. Exact IDs would shatter monitoring variants; compliance usually wants a stable typed/semantic variant, not object identity. AS for reporting is defensible; the “why no single notion” paragraph is too short to carry the section.

**Scope.**  
The required 1,700–2,600 words is missed by a wide margin. Compression here is not elegance: formal clauses, flattening, pm4py, and borderline disagreements are under-argued. No missing events are invented, and the AS/TS table would still be usable, but the number of inaccuracies, the collapse of BS into TS, and the X3 dispatch error block anything above the mid range.
