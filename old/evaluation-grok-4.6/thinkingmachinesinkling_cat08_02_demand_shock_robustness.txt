6.2

The write-up is complete, on-format, and substantially better than a generic capacity-add memo. It still falls well short of “nearly flawless.” The process story is mostly coherent, but evidence hygiene, a few load-migration contradictions, and a simultaneous-priority collision are material.

**What is actually strong.** All seven required sections exist and are used for their intended jobs. The four scenarios vary mix *and* disruption type, not just volume. Bottleneck migration is argued as a sequence, not a slogan, and is tied to utilization, queues, DFG loops, and the given conflict hints. The package correctly refuses static I1/I2/I3, puts starvation and machine-failure gates on batching, keeps I10 off Urgent (18 h), and treats I5/I10 as valves. The waterbed residual—upstream queues looking healthy while assay/technical validation silently fill—is the best insight in the piece. The dashboard at least separates leading from lagging and bands thresholds off the peak numbers (pre-analytical failure ~8%, technical validation 101%, specialist ~36 h).

**Factual and conceptual slips.** Peak recollection destinations are taken from the *normal* DFG: 540 / 290 instead of 520 / 370. Loop arithmetic in S1 does not add up (1,100 recollections vs ~800 + ~450). Repeat-after-failure (a case type, 8–9%), in-process Repeat assay (1,140 / 1,260), and Request recollection are treated as interchangeable in S2 and S4; putting Repeat-after-failure at 15% because samples aged is the wrong object. Performance edges that actually dominate delay—*Request recollection → Receive sample* 42/60 h, *Hold → Medical* 18/48 h, *Batch wait → Assay* 9/22 h—are underused relative to frequency counts. The longest edge is why I6 matters; that is never made quantitative.

**Logic flaws that change the design.** The I4 guardrail (accession >12 h *and* technical validation <8 h / <85%) cannot fire in the observed peak, where both queues are already 14 h and 16 h. The intervention is therefore unavailable in the state it is meant to fix, and that is not acknowledged. S2 then claims I4 would collapse technical validation under assay degradation; if assay output falls, arrivals to technical validation should fall, so I4 is *more* plausible in S2 than in S1. Those two claims cannot both stand. M1 is reserved for Urgent, Complex-panel, *and* repeats at once. That violates the constraint that not every class can be prioritized simultaneously, and S2’s 30% M1 loss is never resolved among those three claimants. P-Surge’s 24 h starvation timer and Layer B’s 36 h timer are left inconsistent. I8’s “upstream-to-downstream” wording is simply backwards.

**Smaller misses that still count under a strict rubric.** The dashboard is not “few” (eleven leading signals). I7 is declared permanent without a downstream overload story beyond “smoother intake.” Registration overtime error after 9 h, the 22%→54% long-queue KPI, and I9 are essentially unused. Bypass-slot caps are unspecified. I4’s four-week training is listed as burden but the package treats cross-trained staff as already on the floor during a three-week peak.

**Net.** This is a competent, data-aware redesign with the right architecture (loop-breaking, guarded flexibility, scenario switches). It is not tight enough on the log, not consistent on I4/M1, and not sparse enough on monitoring to score in the high band.
