**6.4**

This is a competent, decision-oriented memo that chooses the right family of action (gated hybrid, not national scale or wholesale rollback) and hits every required section. It is not close to flawless. Several factual slips, overclaims, and missing process-mining artifacts would matter in an executive setting.

**What works.** Alternative E is the defensible call given the constraints. The memo states a real trade-off (speed/accountability on the easy path vs. tail, rework, and control failures on the hard path) rather than averaging to a fake win. The evidence table’s three-column design is the right structure. All five requested metric conflicts are addressed. A–E each get advantages, risks, and conditions. M1–M8 are ranked, and the final section includes immediate action, pause, scale, measurement, and graduation thresholds.

**Factual errors.** Simple houses are **34% of pilot volume, not “the majority.”** That error appears in §3 (“large, easy majority”) and §4B (“simple houses (the majority)”). It inflates the cost of rollback and flattens the segment story. Second visits **11% → 18% is not “roughly doubled”** (manual activation 7% → 15% is). Alternative C is misread: C suspends survey skipping and post-billing activation, **not** the validate/schedule merge, so it is wrong to say C “doesn’t yet exploit” the merge. Sparse start timestamps affect **activity-level waiting vs. processing**, not automatically the case-level median (first-to-last completion can still be valid). Treating the median win as “partly an artifact” of missing start times overstates the data defect. Complaint **rate** is a reported core metric; **cost** of complaint handling is what is truncated after week 4. Item 3 of the table conflates those.

**Logical flaws.** The median-vs-tail explanation is too simple. Median improved in **four of five segments**; p90 improved only for simple houses and worsened for apartments, old buildings, business, and rural. R did not mainly “help the majority and hurt a minority.” It sped typical cases broadly and broke the tail/quality of everything except the easy stratum. That is a different, and more important, synthesis. Ranking M4 first because “cost sign determines go/no-go” contradicts the memo’s own multi-metric logic (tail, FTR, complaints, billing-before-activation, missing permits). VoI is also mixed up with **implementation order**: M2/M3/M7 are enablers; they are not automatically higher-value than a randomized skip trial. “Proven beneficiary” for simple houses sits uneasily next to acknowledged case-mix confounding and missing pre-week-3 attributes.

**Missing evidence the prompt required you not to ignore.** SLA (13% → 16%, better for simple houses) and NPS (+21 → +17) never enter the memo or table. The complaint-mix shift (“waiting for appointment” → “installed but not working” / “billing before activation”) is the strongest qualitative corroboration of the activation/billing mechanism and is unused. **Generalization** 0.70 → 0.56 is omitted; fitness/precision without it is incomplete. The handover-of-work vs. **working-together** network centralization is reduced to utilization/absence. Field-survey utilization 78% → 52% (idle capacity while second-visit teams hit 92%) is the resource counterpart of “cost moved downstream” and is barely used. Temporal profile is thin: visit → activation worsening for apartments and failed-visit → second-visit delay under high coordinator load are largely dropped. Business (billing disputes) and rural (rescheduling) are in the data and almost absent from the decision.

**Smaller issues.** “Everything is allowed, so everything fits” is a sloppy precision explanation. The ~1pp FTR bar is arbitrary with no sample-error caveat. Scaling coordinator ownership only to 34% of volume while claiming to take “the safe core of C” is internally muddled (C is a national ownership scale). An extra H1 sits above the required six top-level sections.

The recommendation is still directionally right. The write-up is not reliable enough on quantities, alternative definitions, or the full process-mining record to be an executive memo you could send as-is.
