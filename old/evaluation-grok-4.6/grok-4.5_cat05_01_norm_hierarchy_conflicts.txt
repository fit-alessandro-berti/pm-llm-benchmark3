**6.6**

The answer is a capable, well-structured compliance analysis that gets several hard things right: it separates facts from assumptions, refuses a single total ordering, applies rules at the time of commitment, treats ON-44 as evidential rather than normative, and does not let ECD-2’s strong wording or the CFO email automatically defeat GPIP-4. Sections 1–3 and 7 are substantially better than the casework they are supposed to control. The grade is held down because the case section does not apply the author’s own hierarchy consistently, mis-fires a core trigger, and uses `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` in a way that collapses the classification scheme.

### 1. The hinge error: D1 / A2 is applied three different ways

The author’s stated rule is clear: ECD-2 overlays PO timing and supplier-register mechanics inside its window and subject scope; it is not an A7 CRO exception; D1 may interpret the Finance *channel* during the window but cannot delete A2’s independent-budget-owner element; IC members are not assumed to be budget owners; A6 means later approval is not a cure.

That rule is not applied uniformly.

- **P-502** (EUR 62k, dual IC before commit, Finance after): A2 is treated as an unjustified, still-required pre-commitment failure.
- **P-505** (EUR 88k, dual IC before commit, Finance after): D1 is treated as a valid Finance operational channel and the residual budget-owner gap is demoted to a “secondary concern,” producing `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`.
- **P-507 / P-508** (dual IC before commit, no Finance event): the same A2 gap is treated as fatal, producing `NONCOMPLIANT`.

P-502 can remain `NONCOMPLIANT` on A3 alone, so the classification happens to survive. P-505 versus P-507 does not. Both are inside the window, both are ≤ EUR 150,000, both have timely dual IC authorization, and neither has a demonstrated independent budget owner *before commitment*. P-505’s later `Finance_Approve` at 19:00 cannot be the difference: it is after both commitment *and* ECD-2 expiry, and the author elsewhere correctly invokes A6. If D1 + dual IC satisfies the Finance channel, P-507 should move with P-505. If the independence element still fails, P-505 should not be `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION`.

This is not a close judgment call at the margin. It is the central interpretive question in the file, and the author answers it differently depending on how complete the packet looks.

### 2. `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` is conceptually misused

A justified exception means the deviation is authorized by a valid exception path. The P-505 write-up simultaneously says the A2 tension is “not [a] full cure,” that the budget-owner element is “imperfect,” and that the primary class is still `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` with “Low-moderate residual” severity. That is a mitigated breach, not a justified exception.

The closing summary then defends P-505 as “the clearest JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION on evidential timing plus pre-commit Compliance/IC.” Compliance is doing rhetorical work it is not entitled to do (see below). Timing evidence can put the *act* inside ECD-2; it cannot convert an acknowledged A2 residual into an exception.

P-508 is handled more honestly (“nearest to justified exception, primary remains NONCOMPLIANT”). That is the classification P-505 should have received under the author’s own premises.

### 3. Clear trigger error on P-505 / A3

A3 fires on amount **> EUR 100,000** or a **new supplier**. P-505 is EUR 88,000 from an approved supplier. Compliance clearance was obtained but was not required.

The assessment still lists `R-A3 Compliance before commit` under satisfied rules and then uses that “pre-commit Compliance” as a reason the case is the cleanest justified exception. That is a rule-catalogue error, not a harmless extra control noted in passing. It inflates both the compliance picture and the classification.

### 4. Plant / B2 overlay is also unstable

The author generally treats ECD-2 dual IC authorization as overlaying ordinary local Plant approval (P-502 and P-505 record no `Plant_Approve` and do not treat that as a deviation). Then:

- **P-508**, which is in-scope and otherwise the cleanest ECD-2 packet, is charged with “no Plant_Approve.”
- **P-503** is charged with no Plant, which is fair *if* ECD-2 scope fails, but the Plant point is not conditioned that way with any precision.
- **P-507** under-discusses Plant relative to Finance even though B2 is conjunctive.

Same source, same clause, four treatments.

### 5. P-501 over-imports GPIP pre-commitment timing onto local B2

At EUR 38,000 from an approved supplier, A2 and A3 are off. The live local issues are B4 (4-hour stoppage; commit-before-PO allowed; Shift Lead substitution only if the Plant Manager is absent *and* actively delegated) and B2 (Plant + Finance at ≥ EUR 25,000, with **no explicit pre-commitment clock**).

The unreachable-delegation point is right: the Plant Manager was reachable and did not delegate, so SHIFT-1 cannot stand in as Plant. That is a real defect. What does not follow, without more argument, is that post-commit Plant/Finance the same afternoon is a GPIP-style uncured pre-commitment breach. A6 is a GPIP rule about missing *pre-commitment* approvals (A2/A3). Using it to score local B2 at EUR 38,000 as if it were A2 is a silent promotion of a local control into a global temporal rule.

`NONCOMPLIANT` is still defensible on the failed substitution plus commitment without Plant authority. Confidence of “medium-high” and the Finance-timing theory are overstated. `INDETERMINATE` or `NONCOMPLIANT` at lower confidence would have been tighter. The answer also does not use ON-44 on a case whose request/commit/PO stamps all sit inside the outage window, even though the real-world sequence is still almost certainly pre-09:00.

### 6. `INDETERMINATE` is left unused in a problem built around it

The prompt supplies four primary classes and repeatedly requires uncertainty to be visible. The author adopts a burden rule that integrity-critical ambiguity blocks a *clean COMPLIANT* finding. That is a legitimate audit stance. It is not a license to convert every unproven control into `NONCOMPLIANT` and never use `INDETERMINATE`.

Cases that actually needed it:

- **P-503**: scope (Ops vs Audit), whether PROC-3 is an IC member, and whether joint IC authorization is “final business approval” for A1. Multiple independent failures make `NONCOMPLIANT` survivable, but medium confidence plus no primary `INDETERMINATE` undersells the scope dispute the source text itself stages.
- **P-505**: commitment time and whether D1 can carry A2 are genuine normative/evidential forks, not failed checkboxes.
- **P-507 / P-508**: if D1’s status is the open legal question the author says it is, the Finance holding should have been framed as interpretation-dependent, not as a simple missing event.

A1 on P-503 is also left mushy (“at risk,” “weakens”). If IC authorization is the final business approval, PROC-3 is requester, committer, *and* a necessary joint approver of their own purchase. That is an A1 breach, not a vibe.

### 7. Smaller defects that still matter under a strict read

- Severity is never defined, yet P-504 (EUR 24k cafeteria freezer) is “High,” the same band as P-502/P-507/P-508. The *process* breach is real (out-of-scope ECD-2 use, unapproved supplier, A3). Equating it with a EUR 140k–155k production commitment flattens the scale.
- Rule-table triggers lose their comparators (“Amount EUR 50,000,” “Amount EUR 25,000”), which is sloppy in a formalized catalogue.
- P-506’s possible continuing B4 necessity (contamination-recovery chemicals) is dismissed as “without emergency defeater” rather than tested against the 6-hour/safety definition.
- P-503’s `Emergency_Authorize` at 10:55 is contemporaneous with commitment at 11:05 and is a better C3 record than the 4-hour write-up the author treats as the model; that packet is not given that credit.
- Section 5 is the strongest part of the casework: the steel-men for P-502 (COO+IC as A7-equivalent), P-505 (system-of-record finality), and P-508 (IC+Legal exhaust operational controls) identify the exact premise that has to move. That quality makes the inconsistent *use* of those premises in Section 4 more conspicuous, not less.

### What remains genuinely good

Section 1’s multi-dimensional authority model (rank × functional ownership × specificity × time) is the right method for a file that has no canonical hierarchy. Section 2’s issue-specific orderings — onboarding/timing versus Compliance versus Finance versus SoD — are the correct refusal of a single stack. Cabining C4 to “ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding” rather than reading it as a general GPIP override is the single best interpretive move in the paper. The rule catalogue and defeasible logic are in the right shape (`normally` / `unless` / `overrides_for` / `supported_by`). Section 6 distinguishes policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and inability to prove compliance, and the remediations follow that split. Section 7 actually answers the policy problem: CRO as the waiver authority, a time-boxed COO+CFO+IC bridge when the CRO is dark, clause-by-clause waiver listing, rebuttable timestamps, no automatic cure, and a concrete anti-normalization tripwire.

Those strengths are why this is not a mid-5. They are also why the case-table failures are inexcusable: the author wrote a hierarchy precise enough to decide P-505, P-507, and P-508 the same way, and then did not.

### Net

This is a strong process memo with a broken implementation layer. The architecture would support a high-8 or low-9 answer. The delivered casework contains one decisive consistency failure, one false trigger, a misused classification label, an over-import of A2 timing onto B2, and a systematic refusal to use `INDETERMINATE` on a problem that advertises ambiguity. Under a lenient rubric this is still a good paper. Under the stated hypercritical standard it is not nearly flawless.
