The final write-up is a strong, format-compliant causal assessment: it catches the crude-versus-stratified reversal, refuses to treat stratification as identification, separates queueing from service, distinguishes total/direct/mediated effects, and recommends a safer learning design than a 50% reassignment. Arithmetic on the supplied tables is accurate. It is not near-flawless.

**What holds up.** Verdict `mainly confounded` (65) is defensible for the crude +6.7h gap. Standardization to the pooled complexity mix (~17.1 vs ~19.6) is computed and labeled as descriptive, not an ATE. The DAG, role table, and five hypotheses cover treatment vs proxy vs bundle, selection on completed cases, and tests with predicted observations. Phases 2–3 match the required pilot and capacity interventions.

**Material deductions.** (1) The policy estimand is closer to an Orion-mix / ATT contrast than the pooled standardization they lead with; they never say so. (2) The randomized pilot is aimed at low/medium day non-compliance cases—historically almost empty for Orion (one day case)—so it does not identify effects on the night/high/compliance work management actually wants to move; that limitation is under-stated. (3) `cycle_time` is not wait + service (residual ~2.1h Orion vs ~1.4h Nova) and that leftover process time is never flagged. (4) Arrival load is reasoned about but never decomposed, despite being nearly collinear with Orion. (5) Section 2’s prose drops the arrows (`routing_ruleresource_group`), hypothesis “rank” puts the least-favored story first, and `quality_outcome` is loosely called a collider. (6) The PM4Py path is a minimal XES blob behind `try/except`; the diagnostic still works via the manual DFG, but it is not a clean PM4Py analysis.

Those are several real nits plus two estimand/design gaps. Under a strict bar that reserves 9+ for nearly flawless work, this lands in the high-7s.
