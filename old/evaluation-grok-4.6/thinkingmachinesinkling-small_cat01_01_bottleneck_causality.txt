**6.2**

The final write-up is substantively above average on the core puzzle and then loses a lot of points to a missing required section, a broken word limit, a policy-relevant identification error, and several DAG/process inconsistencies. It is not close to flawless.

**What is actually strong.** The answer detects the crude-versus-stratified reversal, does the arithmetic carefully, and does not treat stratification as identification. Crude means (21.96 vs 15.24), stratum means, wait vs service (12.80/7.04 vs 8.79/5.04), compliance splits, day/night splits, and rework splits all check out. It distinguishes direct, mediated, and confounded readings; treats `resource_group` as a bundled proxy for routing/skill/shift rather than a clean treatment; flags completed-case selection; and rejects a blind 50% move in favor of a learning plan that includes both a routing pilot and a capacity intervention. Those are the evaluation-emphasis items, and they are mostly met.

**Required-format failures.** Section 1 is roughly 450 words against a hard 180–300 limit. Section 8 is simply absent: no five numbered residual uncertainties, no “most valuable additional field” for each. On a specified eight-heading deliverable, that is a major completeness miss, not a cosmetic one.

**Causal-graph and role errors.** `resource_group → shift` is the wrong arrow for the decision. Night is a coverage/arrival condition that selects Orion; reassigning the label does not turn a night case into a day case. Encoding shift as a mediator implies the 50% move would change operating period, which is operationally false. `complexity → unobserved_case_risk` is backwards: residual risk should not be a child of the observed severity measure. The table then calls `compliance_required` a mediator of the group effect, while the prose correctly says compliance is policy-driven by complexity, not by group. That is an internal contradiction, not a subtlety.

**Wrong standardization for the actual proposal.** Combined-sample weights (Low 0.40 / Med 0.333 / High 0.267) are clearly stated and correctly computed (~17.1 vs ~19.7). They are not the policy-relevant weights. Management wants to move *Orion* cases. On Orion’s mix (≈14% Low, 43% Med, 43% High), Nova’s predicted mean is about 24.7 h versus Orion’s 22.0 h. The pooled standardisation understates how badly the proposal can fail if stratum gaps persist. Calling the gap an “ecological fallacy” is also the wrong term; this is Simpson’s paradox / confounding on case-level data.

**Process-mining and code.** Queue vs service is handled well; variant/DFG comparison in prose is thin. The code ignores the specified `pm4py.parse_event_log_string` path, fabricates timestamps, and treats `discover_dfg` as if `dfg[0]`/`dfg[1]` were node/edge sets. Manual DFG pairs and overlap warnings are fine; the PM4Py block is not a reliable diagnostic.

**Intervention gaps.** Phase 1 randomises low/medium cases only, so it cannot identify the effect of moving Orion’s actual mix (43% high, 93% night). Inventing “~3 cases/day” and “10% of volume” adds facts the prompt does not contain. “Almost certainly confounded” in the recommendation is louder than the 60% “mainly confounded” verdict. The stop rules are otherwise concrete and the Phase 2 capacity lever is the right safer experiment.

Net: disciplined on the reversal and on not equating association with causation, but too sloppy on format, DAG direction, policy-relevant weighting, and the required residual-uncertainty section to score higher under a strict rubric.
