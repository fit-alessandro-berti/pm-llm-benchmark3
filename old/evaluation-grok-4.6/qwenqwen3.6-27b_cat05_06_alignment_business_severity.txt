**6.7**

The write-up is structured correctly, covers all 12 cases, and gets the central thesis right: uniform edit distance is not business risk, silent `T(tau)` moves are fitting, and zero-cost traces can still be serious policy failures. The taxonomy (noise vs label mismatch vs scope vs missing evidence vs true control failure vs data-aware policy breach) is the right one. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

**Policy overlay is misstated in the two most important silent-failure cases.** P1 forbids Auto Approve except when amount ≤ €5,000 **and** LOW **and** no 7-day bank change. P2 *separately* mandates Fraud Screen + Manual Approve only for >€50,000, HIGH, or a recent bank change. CL-609 (€18k, MEDIUM, no bank change) violates **P1 only**. P2 is not triggered. Calling this a “P1/P2” failure and implying MEDIUM must take the fraud/manual path invents a rule the overlay does not state. CL-610 (€4.5k, LOW, bank change one hour earlier) violates **both** P1 and P2; the table records only P1. Those are not wording nits. They are the difference between “wrong branch of an XOR” and “missing a mandated dual control.”

**Severity is flattened where the framework promised exposure-sensitivity.** CL-602 (€480k, HIGH, bank change, no fraud evidence, verbal check only) is scored 4, the same as CL-609 (€18k MEDIUM Auto Approve). CL-610’s bank-change Auto Approve is also 4. The framework lists amount, reversibility, and fraud relevance, then barely uses them. Immediate actions that say “halt/block payment” on already-paid cases are sloppy.

**Section 6 does not do what it was asked to do.** It needs cost-function design, scope, **hidden moves**, **data conditions**, **activity granularity**, duplicated telemetry, **missing-but-external evidence**, and irreversibility. Hidden moves (CL-605), granularity (Assess Damage vs Assess Loss; UI vs business acts), and external evidence (adjuster report, CRM/portal message) are largely absent from that section even though they appear elsewhere. Numbering eight cases as “five” is careless.

**The PM4Py block would not run as written and misbuilds a key trace.** `format_dataframe(..., timestamp_key=None)` with no timestamp column is invalid in normal PM4Py. `conformance_diagnostics_alignments(net, im, fm, log)` is the wrong argument order (log first). The fallback import `pm4py.algo.conformance.alignment` is not the real module path. `(2 if risk_table["Policy_Violation"] else 1)` will raise on a Series. CL-604’s reconstructed log contains **two** `Pay Claim` events; the case has one early payment, and `L(Pay)` + later `M(Pay)` is a *timing* repair, not a double payment. After warning not to replace narrative judgment with a formula, the code sorts on `Risk_Priority_Score`. Alignments are computed and then ignored, which is philosophically fine, but then the reproduction claim is hollow.

**What remains good:** CL-601/603 as P5 noise; CL-604 as P3 (severity 5) rather than “cost 3”; CL-605 as a fitting silent move; CL-607/612 as scope (P6), not operational failure; CL-608’s mutable CRM link as evidence quality, not automatic non-notification; CL-611 as mapping lag; the pattern table’s use of the 74/9/4 monthly frequencies; and the six hypothesis/test pairs, which correctly refuse to treat a missing event as proof of non-occurrence.

Those strengths keep it well above a failing answer. The policy errors, incomplete §6, and non-executable/wrong-log code are material. It is a solid B, not near-flawless.
