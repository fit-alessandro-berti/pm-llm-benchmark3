**6.8**

This is a strong causal-policy writeup with careful arithmetic, the right estimands, and a rollout that tries to buy both operations and identification. It is not nearly flawless. Explicit format rules are broken, the reproducible analysis does not implement the design the text recommends, and a few process claims overreach the numbers.

**Format and instruction compliance.** Section 1 is well over the required 150–250 words (roughly 300+). Section 8 is over the required 250–400 words (roughly 450+). The identification section asks for a table *per strategy*; all four strategies are collapsed into one table. Extra outcome rows in §7 are harmless; the word-count misses are not. The Python block is self-contained and hits the eight required elements on paper, but it is not the analysis advertised in §3.

**What is done well.** Section 1 stays descriptive and correctly separates availability, intensity, selection, co-occurring shocks, and coarse measurement (including the frozen `rework_pct`/`escalation_pct` plateaus). The estimand table is the best part of the answer: ITT-as-delivered bundle, endogenous intensity/treatment version, median vs tail, composition vs true rework, CSAT under unknown *n*, dynamics, heterogeneity, fraud-desk interference, and a case-level effect that the aggregates cannot identify. The primary design (clean-control stacked event study, not naive TWFE) is the right one for two cohorts, never-treated units, and a not-yet-treated Gamma. Mechanisms are process-literate (fast-path skimming, specialist congestion, mechanical loop bypass, survey selection). Pre/post windows are stated; never-treated and not-yet-treated contrasts are both used; intensity is not treated as a valid instrument; median and p90 are never collapsed; the X% slogan is rejected; the claim ladder is properly ordered.

**Quantitative discipline is mostly high, with important exceptions.** Branch-window means, DiDs, load-per-FTE, March +2 pp escalation, adoption-month steps (+0.8 to +1.0 pp escalation; −2.0 to −2.4 pp recorded rework), Gamma −7.5 h at k=0, and the Alpha within-branch correlations (about −0.39 / +0.20) check out. The three untreated series really do move together in Apr–Jun (+5.7 to +6.8 h median; +15.9 to +17.9 h p90), which is the best parallel-trends exhibit in the file.

The tail-ratio claim does not. Section 4 presents p90/median widening as an Alpha/Beta vs control pattern (2.01–2.02 → 2.20–2.34 vs 2.05–2.08 → 2.24–2.26). August ratios: Alpha 2.34, Beta **2.20**, Delta 2.26, Epsilon 2.24. Beta’s change (~+0.18) matches Delta and is slightly *smaller* than Epsilon. Only Alpha clearly fans out. Section 7 prints the numbers more honestly, then still says adopters show “somewhat larger widening.” That is exactly the median-vs-tail conflation the rubric penalizes, just one level up (ratio instead of a single cycle-time effect).

Other nits: “~7 points per month” ignores the Aug plateau (+2). The 45 pp intensity scaling treats the 4–6% floor as zero. The survey argument that a 20–45% response-rate swing “can move a 5-point mean by more than 0.1” is asserted, not derived. E8 says Delta *and* Epsilon look like Gamma; §8 correctly treats Epsilon as Alpha-like. Those two sections contradict each other on transportability, which is the policy-relevant heterogeneity.

**Identification vs code.** The text forbids already-treated controls and wants k = −3…+4, with mature effects at k = 3, 4. The code is ordinary TWFE: `post + C(branch) + C(month)` and a cohort-agnostic event study that **clips lags at +2**, so Alpha/Beta’s recovery months (Jul–Aug) are dumped into the same dummy as June—the worst month in the panel (Alpha median 53.5 h, p90 119.9 h). That specification cannot speak to E3 and biases the “late” coefficient toward harm. `axvline(midx[a])` also plots integer positions on a string month index, so the figures would be misaligned. Reporting TWFE (−4.2 h / −6.5 h) next to the DiDs as if leave-one-out “agreement” were independent confirmation is circular: those estimators share parallel trends and most of the same variation. The honest robustness check is the untreated co-movement, which they do have.

**Uncertainty and decision.** The −1 to −7 h median bound is a reasonable sensitivity envelope; p90 −10 to +3 h correctly leaves the sign open; escalation is the only sharply identified operational change. Confidence 55 on the median is high relative to their own point that a ±1 h/month pre-trend, unrejectable with three points, is ±5 h over five months—the size of the effect. `expand with an embedded evaluation` is the right family of decision. Case-level 50/50 plus a randomized stepped wedge, frozen routing/FTE/thresholds, fraud-desk monitoring, and a dual (case-level and branch-level) scale-up rule are the correct learning architecture. It is internally inconsistent on timing: a two-month gap between branches vs three months of case randomization vs “≥3 months at ≥40% fast-path share” (Gamma only reached 32% in two months). Guardrails that need a “control counterfactual” for p90 in real time are not operational as written.

**Bottom line.** The scientific skeleton is well above typical answers: endogenous rollout, intensity vs flag, median vs tail, composition vs capacity, SUTVA via the shared desk, five-cluster humility, and a design that does not pretend a TWFE coefficient is a policy. Hypercritically, the required length caps are missed, the code implements a different and worse event-time contrast than the one defended, and the tail-amplification story is over-generalized from Alpha. Those are enough to keep it well short of the top of the scale.
