**4.2**

The answer has the right *shape* of a design (activation-conditional target, no fake non-defaults, temporal split, leakage table, PM4Py-shaped pipeline). Under a strict reading it is not a defensible study. Cohort arithmetic does not close, prediction time is inconsistent with the estimand and with the proposed features, validation collapses, and the code would not implement the design it claims to implement.

## 1. Target definition — conceptually acceptable, operationally incoherent

Conditioning on eventual activation is a valid primary estimand and is correctly distinguished from a competing-risk target and from unidentifiable reject inference. That is the strongest part of the answer.

The implementation of that estimand is not. Prediction time is “immediately after the first Credit Review exit,” which is *before* the selection event the estimand conditions on. A model trained only on cases that later activate cannot be applied, at first review, to “every credit application” without a train/serve support mismatch the text never names. If the operational use is instead post-activation early warning, the prediction time is simply in the wrong place.

Further gaps:
- Horizon is 90 days *after activation*, not after prediction. Time from first review to outcome is therefore random and policy-dependent; this is never treated as a variable-horizon or two-time-scale problem.
- Population wording (“started … through 15-Feb-2026”) contradicts the stated event window (through 31 Dec 2025) and the maturity rule.
- “Policy environment at decision time” is stuffed into the estimand without defining whether that is a conditioning set, a stratum, or a transportability caveat.

The alternatives are named clearly. The primary target is not “statistically defensible” as specified.

## 2. Cohort construction — does not account for 48,000 cases

Required: a flow that consumes every application. Produced: inconsistent invented counts and corrupted text (`19 , …  minerals`).

Reconciliation:
- 48,000 − 2,700 = 45,300 after a start-date filter.
- Activated is then given as 25,200, which is the *unfiltered* total (21,600 + 3,600). Those 2,700 cannot have been removed and also left activation counts untouched.
- Listed non-activated: 9,100 + 5,700 + 2,900 + 1,600 = 19,300.
- 25,200 + 19,300 + 2,700 = **47,200**. Eight hundred cases disappear.

The 2,700 “starts after 15-Nov-2025” figure is not in the snapshot. Maturity was applied to **start** date rather than **activation + 90 + feed delay**. 15 Feb 2026 − 125 days is ~13 Oct 2025, not 15 Nov 2025. Section 2 uses 15 Nov; section 3 uses 23 Oct; neither is the right as-of rule. Still-open cases are put through an activation-maturity filter that does not apply to them (5,100 → 1,600 with no justified subtraction).

Inclusion/exclusion/as-of rules are not stated as rules; they are a broken table. The one correct decision — not relabeling rejects/withdrawals/opens as non-default — does not salvage the accounting.

## 3. Censoring and missing labels — mechanisms listed, strategy contradicts itself

The five mechanisms are named, which is necessary but not sufficient.

Primary analysis is a complete-case binary classifier on the 21,600 labeled activations. That can be legitimate *for the activation-conditional estimand*, but delayed labels are merely held out rather than modeled; permanently missing partner feeds are first declared MNAR and then handled with IPCW (a MAR method). Those cannot both be the maintained assumption.

Other problems:
- “~2% of partner accounts” is fabricated.
- Transfer is called a competing event in a design whose analysis set is already restricted to on-platform activations. Pre-activation transfer is exclusion from the primary population, not a competing risk for 90-day default after activation. Post-activation transfer vs never-activated transfer is never separated. The 60% linkage table is mentioned and then unused.
- “Ordinary right censoring = activations after 23-Oct-2025” restates the wrong date.
- Testable vs untestable is thin: MAR for feed arrival is “partially testable,” transfer exchangeability is “untestable,” and nothing is said about the untestable implication of scoring at a time when activation is still future.

S1–S3 are generic bounds/IPCW, not a coherent primary-plus-sensitivity plan.

## 4. Prefix extraction — fights the feature list and the process

Fixed-length and elapsed-time prefixes are both mentioned. The actual rule is `min(5 events, first Credit Review exit)`. In the stated process that is usually event 3, so \(k=5\) is mostly dead. Cases shorter than 5 after clipping would then be dropped by a true `get_prefixes_from_log(..., 5)` or padded with empty tokens — both bad, and mutually inconsistent with section 9.

Worse: cutting at the *first* Credit Review makes document-loop and rework features almost structurally zero, yet section 5 sells those features. Activity names `Credit Review – Accept/Reject/Request Docs` are invented; the process description does not guarantee them. Duplicate drop on `(case, activity, timestamp)` can delete legitimate simultaneous events. Same-day `documents_total` is not handled here at all.

## 5. Process-aware features — categories are right, several features are invalid at \(t\)

Static / prefix / historical is the correct split. Usable ideas: counts, inter-event time, weekday/business hours, policy and migration indicators.

Invalid or leaking:
- Request-docs loop count and “max loop KYC → Credit Review” at first-review prediction time. KYC → Credit Review is the happy path, not rework.
- Rolling default rate of the “last 100 activated cases before prefix time.” Those labels are generally **not** mature at prefix time; this uses future outcomes. A valid as-of rate needs accounts whose 90-day *and* feed-delay window had already closed before \(t\).
- Officer experience is a “pre-collected snapshot” here and a calendar-year subtraction in section 6.

## 6. Leakage audit — best section, still sloppy

Required fields are all present, and the keep/repair/drop calls for `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, and `label_available_at` are right.

Defects:
- Table text is garbled (“set only after Accept  unavailable”).
- `documents_total` “EoD-1” / “00:00 of pred-day” is conservative if actually implemented; it is not implemented in the code.
- Experience repair (`(extract − event).years`) is not how tenure works and is not how pandas timedeltas work.
- No row for `default_90d` itself, `data_extract_time`, or the Accept event as a leak of the selection you conditioned on.
- Updated `risk_score` rule is correct in words only.

## 7. Validation — temporal in slogan, empty in practice

A random split is correctly rejected. The proposed split is not a serious backtest.

Test starts are **15–23 Oct 2025** (eight days) “post-migration, labels mature.” With a 90+35 maturity gap, the last activation that can be fully labeled by 15 Feb 2026 is ~13 Oct 2025 — *before* the migration. In the code, `test_mask` is `start ∈ [15 Oct 2025, 15 Feb 2026 − 125d] ≈ [15 Oct, 13 Oct]`, i.e. **the empty set**. Even under a 90-day-only rule the test slice is tiny and only partially mature. Late validation starts have the same problem.

There is no walk-forward origin, no evaluation in the pre-policy regime, no explicit handling of scarce post-migration labeled data, and no sample-size/power discussion. AUROC/Brier/ECE/IPCW are fine *metrics* attached to an unusable split. Training only on pre-1 Jul 2025 also means the policy indicator in section 5 has no variation in train.

## 8. Limitations — adequate, incomplete

Correct: no claim about rejected applicants’ counterfactual default; selection shift under a new policy; three real deployment feedbacks (collections intervention, score-dependent underwriting, new activity vocabulary).

Missing the limitation the design itself creates: scores at first Credit Review are not identified for cases that will not activate, which is exactly when an “every application” warning would be used. Predictive association vs selection vs counterfactual is distinguished in prose more cleanly than in the actual target/prediction-time pair.

## 9. Code — a sketch that would not run and would not audit

Approximate length is in range and the *intent* of the pipeline is visible. That is not enough.

Concrete failures:
- `pm4py.format_dataframe(...)` is not assigned.
- `attribute_filter` is imported and unused.
- `activated_time` via `g.agg` + `g.get_group(x.name)` is not a valid aggregation; `x` is a timestamp Series and `x.name` is not the case id. This block fails.
- Cohort flags only produce three buckets; rejected / withdrawn / transferred / still-open are never recovered, so the required cohort audit is not produced.
- `default_90d = g["default_90d"].max()` ignores `label_available_at` and the extract-time as-of.
- Splits are defined and never applied; the test window is empty (above).
- Clip uses first `str.contains("Credit Review")`, not first CR *exit*, and throws on cases with no such event.
- `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(df=analysis_log, prefix_length=...)` is the wrong calling convention (`log`, not `df=`), and after clipping to ~3 events a length-5 prefix extractor drops or empties the log.
- DFG and variants are computed and discarded — decoration, not a justified use.
- Leakage “audit” is `if col in prefix_log.columns: raise`. Those columns are in the stated schema, so this aborts on every honest input. It does not test post-cutoff non-nulls. Leak columns are never dropped.
- No as-of `risk_score`, no `documents_total` cutoff, no elapsed-time prefixes, no competing-status table.
- Survival/IPCW is a trailing comment, not a hook with a label/censor matrix.

## Overall

The answer avoids the most cartoonish failure mode (calling omitted cases non-default and randomly splitting 21,600 rows). It then fails the actual assignment: honest cohort construction, as-of semantics, a target that matches a prediction time, a validation scheme that can exist under the maturity calendar, and a skeleton that implements those rules. Invented counts, contradictory dates, MNAR-vs-IPCW, loop features that cannot exist at \(t\), a leaking “historical default rate,” an empty test set, and non-executable PM4Py glue are not minor nits. They are the difference between a study design and an outline of one.
