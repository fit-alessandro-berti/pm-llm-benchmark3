The final write-up is a strong, well-structured compliance analysis: all required headings are present, event-time reconstruction is handled correctly, and the eleven case calls are the right ones (or well-defended where the rules are genuinely under-specified). It is not near-flawless. Several concrete defects in formalization and in the code/diagnostics keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is solid**

Temporal semantics are the best part of the answer. Blank cells are treated as “no update,” forward-fill never copies the future backward, `event_index` is the same-timestamp tie-break, and event / effective / record / evidence time are distinguished with the right priority (signing certificate over a displayed backdate over an interested witness). That framework is applied consistently to TX-302, TX-305, TX-310, and TX-311.

The ambiguity section does the required work on TX-302 / 305 / 310 / 311 and usefully adds TX-306. Section 6 gives more than six distinct control-flow failure modes, including the ones the prompt named. Section 7 hits the requested logging fixes.

**Where it loses marks**

1. **Section 5 sample output does not match the code.** That is the largest defect. The code only sets `mat_increase_after_enh` / `mat_increase_pct` when an Enhanced_Review exists, so TX-301 would print `False` / `0.0`, not `True` / `56.2`. For TX-305, `r1_triggered` is `False` (short-circuit on unknown amount), not `None`; `r2_triggered` is likewise `False`, not `None`. The table is presented as program output. It was not produced by this program.

2. **R4 tracking is incomplete in the checker.** `last_std` is computed and never used. Material increase after Standard_Review is therefore invisible in the diagnostics except insofar as R1 already fails for lack of Enhanced_Review. The prompt asked for that event to be tracked and used.

3. **The formalization is not well-formed.** `R1_satisfied` is defined as obligation ∧ discharge, which conflates applicability with satisfaction. `latest_material_change` is `max` of a set that can be empty. `R3_satisfied` refers to an unbound `upd`. These are not cosmetic; they are the artifacts Section 2 is supposed to get right.

4. **Smaller judgment nits.** TX-311’s jurisdiction/index fact is given in the prompt; MEDIUM confidence is too low. Severity invents a CRITICAL/HIGH scale the rubric never defined. Exemption evidence quality is baked into the register as STRONG/WEAK rather than derived from timestamps in the checker, so TX-310 is decided by a hand-set flag as much as by R5 logic.

**Bottom line**

The legal/temporal reading of the log is high quality and would survive a careful review of the cases. The formal layer and the “executable” checker are sloppier than the narrative: unused state, a fabricated results table, and logic that would not type-check. Under a strict bar that reserves 9+ for near-flawless work, that is a clear 7.5, not an 8 or 9.
