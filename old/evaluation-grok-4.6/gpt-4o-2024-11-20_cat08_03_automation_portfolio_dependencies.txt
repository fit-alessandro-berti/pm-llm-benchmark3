5.4

The answer is structurally complete and directionally aware that frequency is a poor ranking key, but it is not a defensible process-mining recommendation. It leaves the most important artifacts unused, violates an explicit first-wave constraint, and contains several rating and sequencing errors.

**What is acceptable.** All twelve candidates appear, ratings are qualitative, three portfolios are named, A11 is not pushed ahead of controls, and the recommended set (A1/A4/A5/A6) is at least pointed at loops rather than raw volume. The A5–A6 pairing is the one genuinely good design idea: document completeness before analyst extraction.

**Matrix is not a ranking and several cells are wrong.** The table is listed A1–A12 with no rank order, so it fails the required “ranking” task. A7 is the clearest miss: external valuation has a 58h median wait, 19% lateness, and is a named synchronization point required in 71% of equipment cases — process-level benefit of “Medium” and first-year suitability of “Medium” treat it as optional. A4’s “downstream bottleneck risk: High” is confused (faster mismatch clearance unblocks the path; the risk is specialist capacity and dirty outflow, not a new downstream queue created by the bot). A6’s prerequisite “KYC and all documents” is inaccurate: KYC and financial review are concurrent, and the IS sequential recording is explicitly not to be trusted. A12 as “High” process-level benefit overstates a 650-case remediation path relative to the 31h/58h waits.

**Dependency map is generic and incomplete.** It restates obvious A1→A2 and A5→A6 links and never builds the actual join: committee packaging waits on KYC status, normalized statements, collateral if required, and credit notes. It omits the legal hard stop (KYC before signing; 6.5% provisional signing), A11’s “controls incomplete” gate, four-eyes on high-exposure analysis vs packaging, and the 3-FTE / two-senior-specialist concentration. Core-banking comments name A1/A9/A11 without justifying why those two of the first-six-months slots should be spent that way.

**Portfolios fail the problem’s own constraints.** Compliance requires at least one first-wave module that strengthens controls, not only labor. The quick-win set (A1/A2/A5/A10) does not do that; A2 can *worsen* control-path routing. The “bottleneck/synchronization” set ignores the longest wait in the log (A7) and the four-way committee join (A8). That is not a synchronization portfolio; it is a mid-process labor portfolio with A1 bolted on. Compliance-first then puts A9 next to A3/A4/A12 without A5/A6, i.e., contract generation on still-dirty artifacts — exactly the garbage-in risk the candidate note flags, and exactly what already produces 8.5% / 14% contract remediation.

**Section 4 does not do what was asked.** Effects are adjectives (“moderate,” “significant,” “some indirect gains”) with almost no activities, variants, or DFG edges. Required unused facts include: Resolve→KYC 1160 vs Resolve→Contract 380; Review→Missing docs 3300 and the 2800 return loop; 82% critical-path share for entity mismatch; concurrency of KYC and financial review; weekly committee cadence; Micro/SME/High-exposure and WC/Equipment/Renewal differences. Portfolio 2’s “significant reduction in KYC” is an overclaim: A4 touches 1,750 of 11,500 KYC cases. “Expanded capacity” for three specialists is not what a medium-feasibility assistant with limited coverage delivers. Portfolio 3’s “no immediate cycle-time reduction” contradicts A4’s 31h wait.

**Resource-network evidence is almost unused, which breaks the A1 story.** Back office is the visible queue but falls below 65% after 15:00 — automating registration first harvests idle afternoon clerk time and then increases arrival rate into classify (8% error), KYC (4h wait, 11% exception), and document review (18h wait, 24% exception). Credit analysts are the working-together hub; A1+A5 without A6 already live is the overload path the brief warns about. The rollout still does A1 in months 1–6 and A6 in months 7–12, i.e., it implements the failure mode it claims to avoid. It also spends only one of two allowed deep integrations in H1.

**Controls are named, not operationalized.** Four-eyes (5.8% month-end), provisional signing (6.5% / 1.1%), and contract–decision mismatch are barely connected to module choice. There is no SoD enforcement design, no KYC hard-stop before A10, no sampling/QA on A6 PDFs, no dual check that A9 terms match the committee record, no false-negative SLA for A3. Model drift is a one-line “update the rule library.” RM-triggered incomplete instructions are mentioned in the source data and then dropped.

**Final rec is only half an argument.** Choosing the bottleneck portfolio over a frequency-only wave is the right *slogan*, but the plan still automates the highest-frequency activity first, does not explain why that is safe given afternoon under-utilization and downstream queues, and never says clearly whether A1 should wait. The political A1 inclusion is understandable; leading with it is not, given the brief.

The piece reads like a competent template filled with plausible labels. It is not grounded in the log, the DFG, the resource graph, or the control findings tightly enough to advise a bank.
