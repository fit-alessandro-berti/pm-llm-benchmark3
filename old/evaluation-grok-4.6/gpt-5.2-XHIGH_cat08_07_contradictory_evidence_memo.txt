7.2

The memo is well structured, decision-oriented, and correctly refuses both national scale-up and a full rollback. It uses segment heterogeneity, conformance deviations, and incomplete cost data to justify a guardrailed hybrid. Facts, interpretations, and uncertainties are labeled. That is the ceiling; under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Analytical errors**

The case-mix question is pointed the wrong way. Pilot regions had *more* apartments and older buildings—the segments that deteriorated. Adverse mix would suppress the median gain and inflate the tail, not the reverse. Asking whether the median improvement is “due to case-mix” treats composition as a threat to the good news and understates how informative the median still is.

Fitness/precision are control-flow properties of traces. Sparse start timestamps affect waiting-versus-processing diagnosis (M2), not model precision. Folding them into the precision explanation is a process-mining mistake. Generalization (0.70 → 0.56) is omitted entirely; that metric is the one most relevant to whether a pilot model will hold in new regions.

A 6-week pilot cannot cleanly support a p95 of 62 days. Right-censoring of in-flight cases typically *understates* tails. The memo never flags this, so the tail deterioration—already the main reason not to scale—may be worse than reported, and the observation window is too short for the claim as stated.

**Internal inconsistency**

M4 (60-day cost after billing start) is ranked first, and 30-day complaints are a rollout gate, yet the decision is time-boxed at 6–10 weeks with “≥4 weeks” of sustained thresholds. Those clocks cannot all be true. The rollout criteria also omit total cost, despite cost being treated as the highest-value unknown.

M5 is ranked 7th while coordinator ownership is scaled immediately. The prompt’s absence-stalling and 93% utilization findings make resilience a scaling constraint, not a later diagnostic.

**Scope and evidence gaps**

SLA breach (13% → 16%, better for simple houses) is unused. Temporal-profile edges and the handover-of-work versus working-together distinction are collapsed into slogans. Combining E with D is reasonable but blurs the alternative set: it is never stated whether coordinators stay on *all* in-flight segments in pilot regions or only on simple houses.

The memo is a strong executive artifact with a defensible choice. It is not a rigorous synthesis of the mining artifacts or the data-generating process.
