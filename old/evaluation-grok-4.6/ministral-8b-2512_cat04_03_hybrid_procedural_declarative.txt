## 2. The mapping table encodes several requirements as their opposites

R2 is mapped to unary `exactly_one` on **both** `Manager Approval` and `Compliance Approval`. In DECLARE that means both activities occur once. The requirement is the exclusive split: exactly one in an accepted case, neither in a rejected case. The gloss “plus `absence` when Reject exists” is not a DECLARE encoding.

R3 is mapped to `chainresponse` plus `nonchainsuccession` to classification/approvals. Chain response requires the receive to be the *immediate* successor of every request. The scenario allows intervening work and multiple cycles; the right family is alternate response / alternate precedence, or response plus a “no outcome until unmatched request is closed” check. `nonchainsuccession` only forbids an immediate pair, so it does not implement “before any final outcome.”

R4 is mapped to `precedence(Sanctions Escalation, Obtain Legal Opinion)`. That is the wrong template. “If escalation occurs, the opinion must eventually occur” is `response`. Precedence is “the opinion may occur only if escalation already occurred,” which is closer to a converse safety property and is not R4. R10 is mapped to `activ_freq` `{1}` for reject, activate, and first payment. The text is “at most once,” i.e. `{0, 1}`. `{1}` rejects every accepted case (no reject) and every rejected case (no activate / no payment).

## 5. The log skeleton is the wrong orientation and the wrong relations

Under PM4Py:

- `always_before(A, B)` means if A occurs, B already occurred.
- `always_after(A, B)` means if A occurs, B occurs later.
- `never_together` is case-level exclusion.
- `directly_follows(A, B)` means A is always immediately followed by B.

Almost every `always_before` pair is written as (earlier, later), i.e. inverted. `always_after` puts `Close Onboarding` first, which is inverted and also not true on both terminals.

`never_together` is used for Request/Classify, Request/Activate, Receive/Classify, etc. That asserts those activities cannot share a case. T2 is then illegal. Ordering is not exclusion.

`equivalence` of `Verify Bank Account` with itself is meaningless.

`activ_freq` `{1}` on the three terminals again fails T1 and T3. The comment “unbounded” next to `{0,1,2,3}` is self-contradictory.

`directly_follows` lists three mandatory immediate successors of `Receive Onboarding Request` and two of `Activate Vendor`. A single event cannot always be directly followed by three distinct activities. T1 would fail this skeleton immediately.

The “overlap” paragraph then says the skeleton *allows* Request then Classify without Receive, while `never_together` forbids them together. The layer does not know what it enforces.
