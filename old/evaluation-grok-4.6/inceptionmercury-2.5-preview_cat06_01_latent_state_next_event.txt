**4.2**

The JSON is well-formed and the write-up is not a naive DFG copy, but it repeatedly violates the supplied state machine, misuses obligations as next events, and contains at least one factual mix-up. Under a strict reading that is not a high-quality process-aware answer.

**Normative states are mishandled.** S2 is defined as evidence requested with **no** Evidence Received event. C6-A and C6-B both have Evidence Received logged (12:10 / 12:16), so the S2 alternatives are definitionally impossible. That is not a minor wording issue: it shows the abstraction was not applied. Fast Close is specified as an S1 option after triage, and the historical path is `Initial Triage → Fast Close` (286). Ranking Fast Close as C6-B’s next logged event after an already-started evidence cycle is control-flow wrong. Duplicate/low-severity context can change the **eventual** outcome; it does not rewind the case to triage.

**`process_enabled` is unused as a distinction.** Every prediction is `true`. The task required separating what the process allows from what the log will show next. Evidence Review can already be in progress at 14:00; Technical Investigation / Regulatory Assessment / Supplier Analysis can be obligated but not the next completion; Authority Notification is not enabled until a reportable RA conclusion; Disposition is not enabled while mandatory investigations are open. Marking all of these enabled erases the observation model the scenario is built around.

**Several “next events” skip mandatory intermediates.**
- C6-A ranks Tech Inv / RA completions as next after Evidence Received. The cycle is Requested → Received → Review; review may open another cycle. High severity does not license jumping the logged review. A coherent #2 is another `Customer Evidence Requested` (completeness 0.86; DFG review→request = 101), not RA completion.
- C6-C and C6-D rank `Authority Notification` as a next log event. Rule 6 and the DFG place it after a reportable assessment (and typically after `Disposition Recorded`), in a **16:00 batch**. At 14:00, with RA not completed, this is not a plausible next observation. The batch constraint is almost unused.
- C6-E ranks `Disposition Recorded` while the second review is outstanding and Supplier Analysis is mandatory (lot linked to six complaints). Disposition is not enabled. Completeness 0.72 after the second package is treated as a contradiction but never turned into the obvious competing next event: another evidence request.

**Case-specific errors.**
- **C6-B:** R-T4 “usually handles potentially reportable cases” is left as a soft unknown instead of **contradicting** a Fast Close-first story. Open obligations list “Fast Close,” which is a terminal outcome, not an obligation. `Disposition Recorded` is a poor third outcome versus `Close — No Safety Issue` after review.
- **C6-C:** Completed obligations omit the entire logged evidence cycle. Supporting evidence cites “Similar complaints in prior 30 days (3),” which belongs to **C6-A**, not C6-C (four complaints, same failure mode, **different lots**). That is a fabricated attribute. The S5 alternative leans on migration despite a **normal** feed flag. Supplier Analysis is correctly omitted (different lots), which is one of the few precise object-centric calls.
- **C6-D:** Worklist use for RA-in-progress is the strongest piece in the set. The source-system attachment with missing evidence events is then largely ignored in the forecast, even though the feed is migration-affected and delayed completions are the scenario’s explicit observation risk. Contradicting evidence restates the lag model rather than opposing the ranking. S5 (RA already complete) is weak given assignment at 12:40, 2–14 h completion, and only ~80 minutes elapsed.
- **C6-E:** Primary S3 is right; S4 as alternative assumes an investigation already open with no such signal. Rank 2 `Supplier Analysis Completed` as the **next log line** ignores review remaining and 6–36 h supplier times.

**Uncertainty is only partly honest.** Intervals are wide, which is appropriate, but midpoints are not a coherent top-three next-event distribution: mass is piled on downstream obligations that cannot fire until unseen completions occur. Queue and clock facts (review 1–5 h, RA 12 waiting, 16:00 notification batch, 8–15% / 6 h lag) are mentioned more than used.

**Cross-case text is better than the case bodies.** A vs B should not share forecasts, and DFG-as-frequency not cause is correctly stated. That does not repair S2, Fast Close-from-S3, the C6-C attribute copy, or the collapsed enabled/observed distinction.

The answer has the right genre (obligations, objects, lag) and the wrong semantics often enough that it should not be scored as a strong process-aware solution.
