**6.5**

The final JSON is a strong process-monitoring writeup, not a DFG lookup. States, obligations, queues, and the completion-only log are used with real case-by-case differentiation. It is not nearly flawless. Several process-model slips and calibration stretches keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually good**

- **C6-A vs C6-B** is the right contrast: same prefix, opposite obligation sets. Fast Close is correctly treated as an S1 privilege, not a live option after an evidence cycle. C6-B’s path to `Close — No Safety Issue` and C6-A’s mandatory Tech / RA / Supplier fork are reasoned from rules 2, 4, and 5, not from successor counts.
- **C6-D** is the best observation-model case. The worklist snapshot is treated as independent of the migration feed; the source-system attachment is used as evidence that evidence events likely occurred; missing events are not treated as proof of absence.
- **C6-E** correctly withholds a low-severity close because rule 5 forces Supplier Analysis once the lot has six complaints.
- **C6-C** correctly treats Regulatory Assessment as mandatory on a repeated-malfunction reportability signal, and correctly leaves Supplier Analysis *uncertain* (same failure mode, different lots).
- Intervals are wide, midpoints are ordered, and they are not forced to sum to 1. `process_enabled` is usually used to separate an in-progress task from a later logged completion.
- The cross-case section on DFG limitations (no attribute conditioning, current queues, non-FIFO RA, migration) matches the scenario’s own warning.

**Defects that matter**

1. **C6-C rank 3 is the largest process error.** `Authority Notification` is not a plausible *next* logged event. Rule 6 requires notification before *closure*; the DFG places it after `Disposition Recorded`. Getting AN next requires an unobserved RA completion *and* an unobserved disposition, then a 16:00 batch. The writeup talks about “today’s batch” as if same-day possibility were next-event probability. The 0.05–0.18 band is too high for a two-hop, time-gated outcome.

2. **C6-D’s alternative state is inconsistent with the abstraction.** After `Technical Investigation Completed`, S3 `EVIDENCE_REVIEW` is the wrong fallback. Delayed evidence events would still sit inside an investigation fork (S4), or the case would be S5 if RA had already finished. Putting the case back in S3 after a completed investigation fights the state machine they otherwise use.

3. **`process_enabled` is blurred on C6-D rank 2.** A migration-recovered `Customer Evidence Requested` is a late observation of a past completion, not a currently enabled task. The required distinction is in-progress work vs next log line. Marking that prediction `true` collapses the two.

4. **C6-A rank 3 is weakly chosen and weakly timed.** They correctly open Supplier Analysis as mandatory (same lot, related complaints), then predict `Technical Investigation Completed` instead. Five hours after triage, median 9 h, seven items waiting, one of three engineers out: completion as the *next* event is a stretch even at 0.08–0.18. The more distinctive open obligation is almost unused in the top three.

5. **They criticize unconditioned DFG use, then do it.** C6-C cites `Technical Investigation Completed → Disposition Recorded` (119, 2.6 h) as support for rank 2 while arguing RA is still mandatory. That edge is exactly the population that skipped or already finished RA. Same class of mistake they flag in `dfg_limitations`.

6. **Timing calibration is uneven.** C6-E’s second receipt is only 40 minutes old, with four reviews queued, yet `Evidence Review Completed` is 0.28–0.48 (almost C6-A’s 0.30–0.50 after ~2 h) and a third `Customer Evidence Requested` is 0.12–0.25 — which still needs the second review to have already finished. C6-E rank 3 (`Supplier Analysis Completed`, typical 6–36 h) is at least down-weighted, but it is still an odd “next event.”

7. **Smaller factual looseness.** DFG shares use the wrong denominators (e.g. 126/972, 101/356). C6-C is still inside the 48 h migration window (~23 h old); “beyond the most migration-sensitive window” is overstated even with a normal feed flag.

**Bottom line**

This is expert-shaped reasoning with the right architecture: obligations, objects, queues, feed delay, and prefix-vs-state. Hypercritically, one next-event is process-illegal, one alternative state is illegal, `process_enabled` is misapplied once, and several rank-3 / DFG moves are the exact shortcuts the rubric penalizes. That is clearly above a mechanical DFG answer and clearly not near-flawless.
