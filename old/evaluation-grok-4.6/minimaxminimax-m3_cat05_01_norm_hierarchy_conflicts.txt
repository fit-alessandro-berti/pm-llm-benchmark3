**6.3**

The answer is formally complete and methodologically serious: it uses the required headings, keeps a dimension-specific hierarchy, separates facts from assumptions, applies rules at commitment time, and does not let ECD-2 or the CFO email automatically defeat GPIP-4. That scaffolding is well above average. Under a strict reading, however, several **core timing and threshold mistakes** undercut the very criteria the task rewards—treatment of time, evidential discipline, and consistency.

**What is strong**

- Authority is handled as institutional standing plus scope and time, not as “newest / loudest wins.” A7 is correctly treated as the only clean GPIP-4 waiver path; historical “directive as overlay” practice is noted and not inflated into a CRO exception.
- A4/A6 are applied with the right grain: emergency waives tendering, not the rest; late approval mitigates, it does not cure.
- Evidence ranking (recordings / signed PDFs / supplier acks over outage-affected ERP) is the right rule, and it is used well on P-507’s queued PDF and P-505’s supplier ack.
- P-503, P-504, and P-508 are the best case rows. P-503 remains noncompliant even if “critical contingency” is read generously (A1 + A3). P-504 is cleanly out of scope. P-508 correctly reads D2’s “as well” as adding Legal, not replacing Finance, above EUR 150,000.
- Section 7 is the high point: it pins waivers to the CRO, confines incident directives to timing / register / default authorizers, defines “critical,” blocks silent renewal, and adds a real anti-normalization brake.

**Material errors (these drive the score down)**

1. **P-502 C3 is internally false.** Verbal authorization 10:10, commit 10:15, record 12:55 is **2 hours 40–45 minutes**, inside C3’s four-hour recording window. The same row says both “exceeds 4h” and “recorded 2h 40m late.” That is an arithmetic contradiction on a rule the catalogue itself states correctly.
2. **P-501 B6 is met, not failed.** Commit 07-14 08:50 → form 07-15 07:50 is **23 hours**, which satisfies “within 24 hours.” Calling it unmet / “borderline,” then padding the row with a garbled remark about the PO sequence, is a clear misapplication of a bright-line temporal rule. The delegation point is also overstated: B4’s Shift Lead substitution is for an **absent** Plant Manager’s plant approval, not a general ban on SHIFT-1 committing. With A2/A3 untriggered and B4’s 4-hour stoppage met, **NONCOMPLIANT** as the primary label is too blunt; JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION or a split (compliant emergency timing / residual local-approval timing) was available and unused.
3. **P-506 inverts Finance vs. commit.** Finance_Approve 06:05 is **before** Commit 06:20. The assessment states the opposite and then lists “Satisfied rules: None.” The case is still noncompliant (expired ECD-2, no recorded extension, Plant_Approve after commit, no budget-owner), but the decisive A2 timing claim is wrong.
4. **P-505 mishandles A2.** EUR 88,000 triggers A2 (≥ 50,000), not the EUR 100,000 A3 line. Finance_Approve at 19:00 is after any plausible commit time. The primary row treats the case as timing-indeterminate and “leaning COMPLIANT on mechanics,” which only works if D1 silently substitutes for Finance—an argument the primary row does not make. Section 5 then explicitly says A2 is not triggered below EUR 100,000. That is a threshold error on a central GPIP-4 control.
5. **D3 is misread when convenient.** D3 *grants* the CFO Finance-interpretation authority and *withholds* Compliance and SoD waiver. Section 2 correctly says D can move some Finance expectations; several case rows then use D3 as if the CFO cannot touch Finance at all. That is hierarchy/case inconsistency. P-507 in particular is much closer to a live D1 question (EUR 110k, IC auth, Compliance already obtained) than the write-up admits.

**Smaller but real defects**

- Almost every primary label is NONCOMPLIANT (one INDETERMINATE). The task warned against treating all deviations as noncompliant. Expressly authorized moves (B4 commit-before-PO; C1/C2/C3 inside scope) are often folded into a single adverse verdict instead of being marked satisfied with residual defects carved out.
- B2 Plant_Approve is under-worked on P-502, P-505, P-507, and P-508.
- Catalogue inequalities are stripped (“Purchase  EUR 50,000”), and some “Pre-commit” timings are asserted for B2 although the SOP text is silent on sequence.
- The Satisfied-rules column is sometimes used for deviations (P-502).
- Retroactive Compliance/Finance paperwork in §6 sits awkwardly next to a firm A6 stance unless clearly framed as mitigation only.

**Net**

This is a coherent, process-aware brief with a defensible hierarchy and a good reform clause, not a shallow recitation. It is not near-flawless. Three timestamp/window mistakes, one A2 threshold mistake, and a shifting account of the CFO’s Finance power are exactly the failures a time-and-evidence-sensitive assessment cannot afford. Those errors keep the score in the mid-6s rather than the 8–9 range the structure otherwise aspires to.
