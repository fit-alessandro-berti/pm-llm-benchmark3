**8.2**

The answer is a strong transportability analysis: it refuses to treat Site A’s nine-hour median drop as an identified effect, grounds the mechanism in partial order rather than task labels, refuses to start Document Check before legally required originals, and designs a segmented, instrumented pilot. It is not near-flawless. Several modeling, identification, and design gaps remain.

## What is done well

The structural table hits the required features and adds the right extras (input independence, waiting, appeals). The DFG audit is one of the best pieces: Register successors 2,050 + 1,860 = 3,910 > 3,400, so the frequencies cannot be naive directly-follows counts of one Register per case; 2,108 Originals→Document matches 62% only numerically; 360 vs 345 supplement edges are not 15 proven violations. That is real process-mining discipline.

The DAG correctly marks calendar period as a common cause of treatment and co-interventions, splits true vs observed overlap, and treats overlap and waiting as mediators rather than things to adjust away. Quantitative scenarios use the right structural identities: concurrent ready work saves \(\min(I,D)\), not \(I+D\); if originals receipt already starts at registration, extra parallelization of identity with documents can save **zero** on a representative path. S3 actually constructs a mixture in which mean and median improve while p90 worsens. The intervention specification keeps statutory gates, versions the supplement loop, and does not import A’s six-task cap. The PM4Py sketch has the right AND-join edges, a nested LOOP with language `do (redo do)*`, and honest comments on unguarded XOR, resources, locks, and time.

Conditional go, first on ready digital / non-original / non-legal / lower-complexity cases, is the correct decision.

## Defects that keep this well below a top score

**1. Transportability graph is incomplete for a required node.**  
`S_legal_capacity -> legal_capacity` appears, then `legal_capacity` has no outgoing edge. `legal_wait` is driven by `legal_required`, `release_burst`, `arrival_load`, and `repeat_workload`, not by legal headcount. With two legal reviewers and 40% legal cases, that omitted arrow is exactly the bottleneck-shift mechanism the prose emphasizes. Required capacities for identity, document, and assessment *are* wired; legal is not. That is a fidelity failure, not a cosmetic one.

**2. “Transport confidence” is not a coherent quantity.**  
The table mixes (a) confidence that a confounder lesson transfers (mix 90, FTE 90, no RCT 90, timestamps 95) with (b) confidence that a numeric change is a treatment effect (median 25, p90 20). The footnote admits this, which does not fix the column. High scores on “A was confounded” are not evidence-transfer scores.

**3. Completed-case mix is under-analyzed.**  
48% → 40% high-complexity is treated mainly as a marketing/intake shift. Completed-cohort complexity is also a selected statistic: if easy digital cases finish faster, the completed mix looks simpler even with a stable arrival mix. Throughput is flagged as selected; complexity is not given the same collider treatment.

**4. Case-level randomization is a weak primary design given 4/7/3/2 pools.**  
SUTVA is the core threat, not a secondary “expansion experiment.” Quotas inside four identity and two legal people will leak through queues, huddles, and borrowing. The saturation/cluster arm should have been co-primary, not an afterthought “after initial safety.” The 500-per-arm floor is also thinly justified for a heavy-tailed 44 h / 110 h process; a 2 h median contrast is likely underpowered if \(\sigma\) is on the order of the upper tail, and 50 upper-decile points per arm is not a serious p90 design. The quality-sample caveat is better than the timeliness-sample claim.

**5. Site B POWL is an envelope, not the stated candidate.**  
Unguarded XOR can skip originals when they are required and take originals when they are not. Supplement iterations are relabeled (`Document Check (supplement)`, `Assessment (supplement)`) and an extra `Supplement Receipt` activity is invented, so the net will not replay Site B’s actual labels (`Request Supplement` → `Document Check`). Transitive closure is computed by hand; that is redundant and version-sensitive. Claiming local soundness on PM4Py 2.7.23.8 cannot be verified here; even if true, it only certifies the unguarded envelope.

**6. S1 still smuggles A’s tail into B.**  
Representative direct savings are 0–3.4 h. Overall −4 to −8 h median and −8 to −18 h p90 require large internal-queue effects and treat A’s −18 h p90 as a “stress-test endpoint.” If the median and p90 cases are originals/legal cases—the likely situation given 62% originals, 40% legal, and a heavier tail—those overall ranges are not “optimistic structural transfer”; they are the wrong quantile carriers. S2 states this clearly; S1 does not apply it.

**7. Smaller unclarities.**  
Who sits at B’s median is never identified. Independence of non-original and non-legal (22.8%) is labeled illustrative but still used in the duration calc; positive correlation would shrink \(e\). `case_complexity -> originals_required, legal_required` muddies statute vs severity. Information independence is correctly flagged as unverified, then the first-wave eligibility rule assumes it can be certified before enrollment without saying how in operational time. Assumption 3 notes unknown joint support; the recommendation still talks as if that stratum is the natural first customer.
