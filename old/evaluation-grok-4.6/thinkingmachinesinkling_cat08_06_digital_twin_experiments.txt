**6.2**

The write-up is complete on paper: all eight required sections exist, the major constraints are acknowledged, and the design is not a naive independent-case replay. That is why it is not a mid-fail. It is also not close to excellent. Under a strict reading it is a competent but leaky design note with several errors that matter for this exact problem.

**What is actually good.** Cross-case structure is treated as first-class (district inspection bundles, architect submission clusters, a district-level objection *proxy* rather than fake observed objection events). Resource calendars are in the entity model, not a footnote. Service time and waiting time are not silently collapsed; the missing-start-timestamp limitation is stated. D1–D10 plus three stress cases are tabulated with plausible trade-offs. There is a real (if generic) validation stack and an explicit “do not claim optimality” caveat. The code split between PM4Py analysis and a simulation engine is the right architecture.

**Simulation design is not tight enough.** The warm-up recipe is the largest conceptual mistake. The process is defined as non-stationary (season, weekday, architect bursts, council weeks), yet warm-up is “run until queue CV < 0.1 / utilization reaches steady state.” That is a stationary DES habit applied to a system that is not supposed to have a single steady state. For this twin, warm-up should be a full seasonal cycle, a loaded initial condition drawn from the log, or deletion of an initial transient *plus* reporting by season—not a stability test that the arrival process will keep violating. Related gaps: no common random numbers for paired intervention comparison; heavy tails are first treated carefully, then casually truncated above 30 days, which undercuts the p90/p99 metrics the decision section depends on; D5 is a control-flow change (legal currently sits after decision drafting) but is never specified as a tree rewrite or AND-join change. Decision-drafting’s “wait for all applicable reviews” is a stated business rule and is not a first-class state variable (`reviews_complete`, tokens on a join, etc.).

**Process-mining grounding is thinner than it looks.** Arrival, branching, and rework estimation are described in prose, not as a reproducible PM4Py-to-parameter pipeline. Calendar reconstruction from HR is accepted almost as ground truth; there is no plan to confront those calendars with actual event timestamps. The 85% variant-reproduction target and the weighted decision score are invented, not elicited. Hawkes-style architect clustering is named without an identification plan from the flattened log.

**The Python skeleton fails several stated conventions.** The question asked for `pm4py.parse_process_tree` (or a clear limitation) and for **case arrival average**. The code uses `pm4py.parse.process_tree` and never calls `pm4py.get_case_arrival_average`. `pm4py.statistics.variants.get_variants` is treated as a DataFrame with a `count` column; in the public PM4Py API this is typically a dict (or you use `pm4py.get_variants`). `len(dfg)` is described as node count; a DFG dict is edges. Service/waiting columns are only computed if a `start_timestamp` column exists—which the given excerpt does not—so the code does not implement the inference procedure advertised in Section 2. Arrival generation, routing, calendars, batching, and experiment execution are empty stubs. That is allowed in principle, but the stubs do not encode the dependencies the rest of the document insists on. The skeleton is coherent as a folder layout, not as a faithful PM4Py analysis script.

**Experiment and decision sections are usable but overconfident in their precision.** The matrix is the strongest part of the answer. The 10%/15%/5pp thresholds, the 60% synergy rule for D10, and the 0.3/0.2/0.15 weights are arbitrary and presented as if they were decision policy. Robustness language is good; the quantification is not.

Net: a solid outline that a municipality could start from, with one serious methodological contradiction (steady-state warm-up under non-stationary demand), several PM4Py/API misses against the spec, and a skeleton that does not carry the design. That is a mid-6, not an 8+.
