**5.6**

The memo hits the required scaffold and gets the big causal-policy call right: no single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, availability ≠ use, median ≠ tail, endogenous staggered rollout, and `expand with an embedded evaluation`. That is the floor of a competent answer, not the ceiling. Under a strict reading it is not close to flawless.

**Format and presentation.** Headings, tables, and most word-count bands are in place. Section 1 is slightly long. The damaging defect is systematic loss of minus signs and change arrows. Section 1 reports “Alpha 2” and “Gamma 1” FTE changes, which read as gains; Alpha lost two FTEs and Gamma lost one. Mechanism evidence collapses to unreadable tokens (`448`, `2532`, `2836`, `13.214.5`). Section 5’s bounded median effect is written “6% to +3%”; the claim ladder’s “conditionally causal” bullet says “0.1 to 0.3 h in median cycle,” which is the opposite of the intended reduction. A quantitative policy memo that reverses signs in the final ladder fails a basic accuracy bar.

**Estimands and identification.** The seven estimands cover the required contrasts, and the event-study vs synthetic control vs ITS comparison is conceptually sound. Identifiability is lazy: almost every row is “Partially.” The primary design is over-specified for five branches (relative time + month FE + covariates). The code does not implement that design at all: it fits a static `automation` dummy and an intensity OLS with branch FE, after constructing unused `rel_time`. That is a real strategy–evidence gap.

**Quantitative discipline.** The Jan–Feb vs Jul–Aug table is arithmetically correct and the intensity slopes are directionally plausible. Interpretation does not stay faithful to that table. Relative to Delta/Epsilon, adopter p90 rose *less* (+7.4 to +9.9h vs +12.5 to +18.5h), so a same-window control comparison points to relative tail *improvement*, or at least less deterioration. Section 7 still says p90 is “likely no improvement, possible worsening” with a range of “0% to +15% above expected trend,” which contradicts Section 5’s own bound and the control contrasts. Escalation is called a “clear increase” at confidence 60 even though controls also rose ~2.5–2.8pp versus ~3.3–3.8pp for adopters. “All branches drifting upward” in the pre-period is overstated (Alpha and Epsilon are not). Load (arrivals per FTE) is never computed; Alpha’s post load is about 31% higher, which a process-mining lead should have treated as a first-class covariate.

**Process and uncertainty.** Mechanism coverage is the right list, but garbled evidence cells weaken it. The threat table is complete. The rollout choice is correct; the scale-up rule is not: a cluster-robust CI excluding zero after treating one additional branch is not an identified decision statistic.

**Net.** Strong outline, honest refusal of the leadership one-liner, and a usable control table, undercut by sign corruption, a p90 conclusion that fights its own numbers, unused event-study machinery, and a statistically empty stop/go rule. That is a mid-pack expert draft, not a high-reliability assessment.
