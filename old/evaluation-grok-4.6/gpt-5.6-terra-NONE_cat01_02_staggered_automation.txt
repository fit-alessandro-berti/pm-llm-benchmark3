**6.4**

The causal framing is substantially better than a typical operational write-up, but this is not close to a high score under a strict standard. The task is judged on estimands, identification, process interpretation, **quantitative discipline**, and uncertainty. The first three are strong; quantitative discipline is not, and several later claims inherit the arithmetic.

### What is done well
Section 1 correctly separates availability, intensity, targeted selection, co-interventions, and measurement limits, and it avoids causal claims. The estimand table is the strongest part of the memo: ITT vs dose vs case-level TOT, median vs tail, process vs CSAT, dynamics, heterogeneity, interference, and treatment-version issues are all named, and most are honestly marked as not identified. The identification discussion correctly rejects naive TWFE, prefers cohort-specific not-yet-treated contrasts, and warns against conditioning on post-treatment mediators. The mechanism table is process-aware (fast-path substitution, shared fraud-desk congestion, fidelity, mix, staffing, rework displacement, tail amplification, survey selection). The rollout choice, `expand with an embedded evaluation`, is the right policy class. The claim ladder is appropriately conservative. The Python block meets the mechanical requirements and, importantly, would **not** reproduce the written pre/post table.

### Material quantitative errors
These are not rounding nits.

1. **Alpha rework is wrong, and the sign is wrong.** Post-period rework is \((11.2+14.0+14.5+14.5+14.5)/5=13.74\), not \(13.1\). Versus a pre mean of \(13.2\), the change is about **\(+0.5\) pp, not \(-0.1\)**. Alpha therefore does **not** show a post-period rework reduction. That error feeds Section 5 (“modest reduction”), Section 7 (“small observed reduction”; “did not increase”), and the safer-claim language.

2. **The adopter change table systematically drops minus signs.** Alpha FTE \(-1.6\) is written `1.6`; Alpha median \(-0.4\)h is `0.4 h`; Gamma median \(-3.4\)h is `3.4 h`; Gamma p90 \(-0.9\)h is `0.9 h`; Beta CSAT \(-0.02\) is `0.02`. Positives are marked with `+`, negatives are unmarked. In a required “transparent” table this is a serious presentation failure: the same row can be read as improvement or deterioration.

3. **Alpha pre-period `automated_fast_path_pct` is \(4.0\), not \(4.7\).**

4. **The untreated comparator does not reproduce.** Delta+Epsilon Jan–Mar median is about \(37.4\)h, but Apr–Aug is about **\(42.6\)h, not \(43.8\)h** (\(+5.2\)h, not \(+6.4\)h). Combined p90 is about **\(93.2\)h, not \(94.3\)h**. The qualitative “common deterioration” story survives; the “transparent” relative comparison does not.

5. **Section 5 never implements the primary design from Section 3.** The advocated object is a cohort-specific event study against *not-yet-treated* units (Gamma/Delta/Epsilon for the April cohort). The memo instead averages full post windows, including months after Gamma is treated and after the common Jul–Aug volume decline. Relative-time dummies are built in code and then unused; the estimated model is the same two-way FE specification the prose warns against.

### Narrower but real flaws
Gamma is overused as the “strongest” median evidence. Jul–Aug also improved among never-treated branches after the May–June complexity shock; a same-window comparison is only modestly favorable and rests on two post months. Escalation confidence of \(60\) is too high: every branch jumps \(\approx 2\) pp in March (fraud desk) and again in May (product change); adoption adds roughly one extra point. The \(0\)–\(8\)h “relative improvement” interval is asserted rather than derived from displayed DiD arithmetic. Load/FTE, the most obvious capacity confounder, is computed in code and omitted from the evidence table (Alpha rises from \(\approx 31\) to \(\approx 40\) cases per FTE). A two-branch waitlist RCT is described as converting the next rollout into evidence that can separate substitution from capacity creation; with \(n=2\) clusters it cannot. Case-level randomized encouragement is the actually informative design and is treated as a fallback.

### Scoring logic
A nearly flawless answer would compute the windows correctly, keep signs, report not-yet-treated DiD separately for median and p90, keep rework and escalation from collapsing into a quality win, and estimate the event-study it recommends. This memo has the right scientific posture and the wrong arithmetic in the one place leadership will read as “the numbers.” That is disqualifying for anything in the 8–10 band.
