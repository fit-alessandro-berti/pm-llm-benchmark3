**7.2**

The final write-up is a competent SLA survival SAP: estimands are defined before estimators, competing risks are not silently treated as ordinary censoring, immortal-time/leakage on `Escalate`/`Rework` is handled, all required headings are present, and the 32-row table is encoded without dropping open cases. It is not nearly flawless.

**What is actually wrong or unclear**

1. **Unidentified tail probability.** \(P(\text{Resolve in }(72,\infty])=\mathrm{CIF}(\infty)-\mathrm{CIF}(72)\) is not identified under right censoring. The prose never states a restricted horizon; the code quietly substitutes max observed time. That is a real competing-risk error, not a wording quibble.

2. **State diagram misses observed transitions.** Prefixes include `Screen>Escalate` (S04, S11) and `Rework>Resolve` (S27). The diagram only allows `Screen → AutoCheck/Review/Reject/Withdraw` and `Rework → Review`. That is a process-mining mistake on the supplied log.

3. **Duplicate “transfer” scenario.** `conservative_failure` and `competing` produce the same Resolve CIF and the same no-terminal KM. Listing four coded transfer treatments overstates distinct identifying assumptions. The three *distinct* ones are competing, censoring, and optimistic recoding as Resolve.

4. **Estimand vs estimator blur on open cases <72h.** Section 1 presents \(1-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(72)\) as the *actual* probability of not resolving by 72. With S10/S15/S24/S29/S31 still censored before 72, AJ is an estimate under non-informative censoring, not a fully observed empirical risk. Sections 2–3 know this; section 1 does not keep the distinction tight.

5. **Evidence used as IDs, not as estimates.** The data are fully available, yet the narrative never reports \(\widehat{\mathrm{CIF}}(72)\), at-risk counts at 72, or stratified numbers. Case IDs for bias are correct (P1: 3/15 vs 5/17; open >72h: S09, S11, S25, S26), but the analysis never *uses* the table to rank estimands or show which sensitivity actually moves.

6. **Plan/code mismatch.** Bootstrap CIs, RMST, and a printed risk-set table are promised or required in spirit; the code implements only a point AJ/KM, does not print the encoded `(time, cause)` dataset, and does not compute RMST or calibration. `parse_event_log_string` is skipped in favor of a manual `EventLog` (allowed), but `get_variants` return types are handled by a catch-all `except`, so a PM4Py API mismatch silently drops to string splits.

7. **Smaller slips.** “Age 72” vs “age ≥72”; Transfer called “semi-absorbing”; Fine-Gray dismissed too generically; landmark CIF after 72 is a restricted-time CIF among those still at risk, not “eventual resolution.”

**What holds up**

Time origin at `Submit`, horizon 72, `Resolve` at 72 as success; E1 vs KM-with-censoring; S03/S09/S06/S08 mapped under each estimand; transfer treated as competing / censoring / optimistic external success; open <72h vs already-observed non-resolution ≥72h; cohort non-randomization and leakage on post-baseline path features; primary AJ, secondary landmark/multi-state, with a justification tied to n=32 rather than a method dump.

Those strengths are why this is not in the 5–6 range. The CIF(∞) claim, the missing transitions, the duplicate transfer coding, and the failure to compute/discuss any numeric estimand from the given cases are why it is well below a high score under a strict bar.
