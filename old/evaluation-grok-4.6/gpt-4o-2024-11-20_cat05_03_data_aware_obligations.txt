## Temporal semantics

Section 1 is directionally right on blanks-as-no-update, no retroactive final state, and `event_index` as a same-timestamp tie-break. It is still too thin for the prompt.

- It never operationalizes **latest material data change** for R1 versus R4’s 20% amount rule. A jurisdiction change is not automatically an R4 invalidation; the text blurs them.
- Event / effective / record / evidence time are named, not applied. The whole point of TX-302 vs TX-310 is that a late *record* can still be good *evidence* if a qualified signature predates the obligation, whereas a displayed backdated field plus a post-release certificate cannot. That distinction is not stated cleanly.
- It does not say how a blank is treated at the *first* event of a case (unknown, not zero), which is decisive for TX-305.

## Formalization quality

The rule table is imprecise: snapshot is “at each event” instead of the release-time (or trigger-time) snapshot; R3’s trigger omits “list update after the latest screen and before release”; R5 treats `Record_Exemption` as the required event, which the policy explicitly rejects.

The Prolog is not a formalization of conditional obligations. Writing

`release_funds(Case) :- amount >= 100000, J != DE, enhanced_review, not valid_exemption`

inverts the rule. A valid exemption is a *defeater*, not a blocker of release. R3 is worse: it makes “jurisdiction was added to the sanctions list” a *prerequisite* of a legal release. The conditionals were encoded as unconditional permission clauses. Temporal direction is only half-specified (`T1 > last_change` vs `occurs_before`), and R5 omits expiry, scope match, and “evidence that approval existed at the relevant time.”

That is a failing formalization, not a minor notation issue.
