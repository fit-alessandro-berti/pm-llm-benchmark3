6.2

The answer is complete in outline and often well organized, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has requirement misses, several semantic errors, and repeated overclaiming.

**Structure and role-fulfillment.** The seven required headings are present and the matrix covers all 15 cases, including control-flow-fit cases with resource problems (SUP-712, SUP-715). That is the strongest part of the submission. It still fails an explicit brief constraint: the prosecution must acknowledge genuine model and data defects. Section 2 treats CRM missingness, `Request_More_Info`, and label drift as operator failures or illegitimate shadow process. It never concedes that M0 cannot express a pre-decision information loop, that N4 broke notification completeness, or that rejected-lead and reactivation scope are undefined. The defense concedes SUP-703, SUP-708, and SUP-712, but it still uses those failures mainly to indict M0, and it does not clearly accept the limit on informal continuation after PD-3 and FD-7 expired.

**Facts versus advocacy.** Section 1 is mostly careful, then the briefs inflate it. SUP-708 is called a “catastrophic international sanctions breach,” an AML violation, and a source of strict-liability fines and unlawful contractual binding. The record supports only activation 40 minutes before sanctions, with no intervening transactions and no cited legal regime. SUP-712 is “willful evasion” and “internal control fraud” in the judgment, with very high confidence, even though the matrix itself admits the aliases may be misconfiguration. “Criminalize” in the synthesis is rhetoric, not analysis. PD-3 is described both as an unauthorized departure affecting all 312 auto-approvals and, later, as a valid monitored exception; the prosecution may argue the first, but the stipulated-facts section already treats the pilot window and eligibility rules as established, so the 289 eligible cases are not an “uncontrolled departure” in the same sense as SUP-703.

**Authority and scope.** The expiry holdings on SUP-705 and SUP-714 are coherent and not a bland split. The rejection of a post-hoc Compliance disclaimer as a retroactive voiding of PD-3 is also reasoned. Confidence is too high where the file is explicitly ambiguous. SUP-709 is `MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT` at very high confidence, yet N1 and the case note leave open whether rejected pre-screen leads are in M0 at all. If they are out of scope, the right label is scope exclusion, not a defect in M0’s `checks` block. SUP-710 is treated as a lifecycle omission with only low residual risk. CS-9 says every onboarding/activation needs `Quality_Review` before activation; a still-valid prior certificate is an evidentiary claim, not a written substitute rule. That case is at least `MIXED` or a qualified noncompliance under CS-9. SUP-715 is labeled process `NONCOMPLIANT` even though no rule bars the spousal relationship and the only proved failure is missing disclosure. SUP-711 is `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` while the same text says the physical process was fully compliant at very high confidence; those cannot both be the dominant finding.

**Model semantics.** This is the largest technical failure.

- DECLARE is used incorrectly. `Response(Sanctions_Check, Activate_Supplier)` does not mean sanctions must precede activation; it means if sanctions occur, activation must occur later, which would forbid rejection after a failed screen. The intended constraint is precedence (or a similar existence/order rule). `Response(Activate_Supplier, Quality_Review)` is the right direction for retrospective QA, but as a global invariant it fails the standard path, where quality already precedes activation.
- The POWL loop is in the wrong place. `Request_More_Info` is modeled only after `Register_Supplier` and before the route XOR. SUP-707 is `Register_Supplier → Sanctions_Check → Request_More_Info → Request_More_Info → Quality_Review → …`. M1 cannot replay the case used to justify the loop. The brief required stating awkward POWL limits and choosing a defensible approximation; the text instead claims the loop is natively incorporated.
- `skip_intake` silently drops registration and then allows every operational child, including standard, auto, and emergency tracks. That is broader than reactivation and is not fenced in the control-flow layer.
- Duplicate same-label transitions across tracks are an understandable encoding, but the limitation is not discussed.
- Layer 4 omits FD-7’s daily monitoring. Section 6 also never gives scope rules for withdrawals, which the prompt required alongside rejected leads and reactivations.

**Revision quality.** The five-layer split (POWL / DECLARE / overlay registry / attribute checks / IAM-COI) is the right architecture, and the code is self-contained and uses the requested PM4Py types. The overlay table and event schema are directionally good (stable `notification_id`, actor alias plus master person, effective vs recorded time, eligibility snapshot, overlay id). They are undermined by invented normative numbers presented as if registered policy: PD-3 never stated a EUR 50,000 cap; CS-9 never defined a 12-month / EUR 100,000 reactivation instrument. Those belong in a clearly marked proposal, not in a “registry” with fake signature IDs.

**Net.** Dialectical coverage and case-level discipline are well above a mediocre answer, but the grade is capped by a missed prosecution concession, several evidence overclaims, inconsistent classifications, a DECLARE polarity error, and a revised POWL that does not actually accept the missing loop it was written to restore.
