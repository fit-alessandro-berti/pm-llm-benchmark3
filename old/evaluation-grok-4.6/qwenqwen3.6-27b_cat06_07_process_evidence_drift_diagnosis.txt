**7.2**

The final six sections get the *main* diagnosis right: April is a schema/pipeline incident, June is a routing/mix change that need not be concept drift, August–September is overlapping process/concept/population deterioration, and October is not comparable because of immature labels. That is the core of the rubric, and the answer does not collapse ten months into one drift type or treat October’s 0.16 as a mature breach rate.

It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several analytical shortcuts and a failed required implementation keep it well out of the 8–10 band.

**What holds up**

The drift table is complete, uses the required columns, and mostly separates evidence from overclaim. April’s 17% unknown activity, 14% missing queue feature, AUROC 0.67 and ECE 0.110 are correctly treated as pipeline, not process. June’s V2 jump to 52% is correctly read against a *lower* observed breach (0.15) and stable V2 matured rates (~0.09). The V3 matured-rate jump (about 0.33 → 0.64) plus the `Order Part → Part Received` edge (28h → 130–155h) is the right evidence for path-specific concept/process change. Partner Z is correctly dated to September, contrasted with the August V3 spike that precedes Z, and qualified by slower label maturity. October’s 55% maturity, 52-day delay, high predicted risk (0.23) vs low observed rate (0.16), and the note that long paths are disproportionately unfinished, are the right censoring story.

Section 3 hits every mandated test family and gives two-hypothesis predictions. Section 4 is staged (contain → recalibrate → repair features → retrain) and names unsafe vintages. Section 5’s *alert dependency* (label-maturity suppresses retrain) is the correct architectural point. Section 6 uses the four failure modes the prompt asked for and pairs each with a real mitigation.

**Material problems**

The required code does not implement the two cohorts it claims. Case-start is computed from `filter_time_range` output, i.e. events *already* restricted to the month. The min timestamp of those events is in-month by construction, so the second filter is tautological. The “case-start” set is cases *active* in the month, not cases that started then. After `format_dataframe`, grouping on `case_id` / `timestamp` is also the wrong key convention (`case:concept:name`, `time:timestamp`). Event-calendar vs case-start is exactly the distinction the monthly metrics table depends on; getting it wrong is not cosmetic.

`pm4py.filter_time_range(..., lower_bound=, upper_bound=)` does not match the public signature (`dt1`, `dt2`, `mode=`). `discover_performance_dfg` is treated as a list of `(src, dst, stats)` triples; current pm4py returns `(dfg_dict, start_activities, end_activities)`. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is assumed to expose `case_duration`, which is not a documented column of that helper. Unused imports and a `discover_dfg` result that never enters the summary add sloppiness. The comments do not actually explain the different *interpretations* of the two cohorts (in-progress performance vs start-vintage predictive risk).

**Analytical blunt edges**

July is merged into the August–September story and then called “safe” for training. That is inconsistent with the evidence the answer itself cites: July V3 matured breach is already 0.46 (vs 0.33–0.35) and part wait is already 65h. July is an early regime change, not a clean pre-drift month.

“Queue times lengthen” is listed as covariate drift without facing the prediction time. The model scores *immediately after diagnosis*; later part delay is not a diagnosis-time covariate unless it is already in X. The interesting claim is that \(P(Y \mid X_{\text{diagnosis}})\) changed on V3 — concept drift — while mix/Partner Z change the distribution of X. The table blurs that.

September’s 0.31 observed rate is treated as essentially real despite 82% maturity and slower Z labels. A strict write-up would say September is *directionally* high but still downward-biased. April’s cached feature is named as a “queue” feature; the audit specified “number of events before diagnosis.” The unused finding that no outcome definition changed is never used to rule out label-definition drift.

Section 1’s “what would falsify” is only a contradicting-evidence column; explicit falsifiers live in Section 3 and are not mapped one-to-one onto each drift type. Immediate actions and confidence labels are generic (“High”) rather than tied to a decision threshold.

**Net**

Strong, correctly structured audit of the *process* evidence; clearly above a one-label or October-naive answer. Not excellent: a specifically required cohort construction is wrong, several pm4py calls are invented or mis-shaped, and July is over-cleaned for training. That is a solid 7, not an 8.
