The final write-up is a strong, complete design: exact required headings, all policy components, all 16 cases, both baselines actually simulated, trade-offs stated, and socio-technical constraints treated as first-class. It is not nearly flawless.

**What is solid.** Hard vs soft constraints are separated correctly (Security/Approval skill floors, no unsupervised high-priority trainees, cap of 4). The owner–performer split is a legitimate reading of `current_owner` vs `org:resource`, and it is flagged as a testable bet rather than a hidden trick. C015 is handled well: Eva/Jonas have Security skill 0, so the queue note is rejected and a shadow-only path is used. High-priority work stays at skill ≥ 2 (C001 Hugo, C002/C011 Farid, C007 Celine, C008 Gita, C016 Eva). Specialist-only and first-available traces match the snapshot (Hugo stacked on all three Security items; C016 falling through to David; Jonas taking C002 as a skill-1 finalizer). Section 5 is non-punitive. Audit indicators cover every required item; A13 (owner-change vs performer-only penalty) is the right check on the central design assumption.

**Material assignment flaws.** C012 is the weakest call. Bruno is overtime-risk **high**; Jonas already owns the case, is Quality skill 3, has the lightest load, and SLA remaining is 48h. The overtime rule’s exception is “urgent SLA and no safer alternative.” Neither holds. Time-shifting the work to “tomorrow” does not satisfy the rule, and refusing to count C015 shadowing toward the 20% training share then forcing this exception is inconsistent. C001’s retained-owner briefing on a 3h enterprise Security SLA adds coordination delay exactly where Rule 5’s escape clause (owner skill 1, supervision not feasible) points to a clean specialist reassignment. Giving David the C009 review reintroduces work to the most overloaded high-OT resource after C003/C014 were moved off him for that reason. Celine’s stack (C007 + C003 + C001 briefing + C016 consult in 6h) is acknowledged but still operationally tight.

**Section 7 inaccuracies.** `filter_case_performance(..., min_duration=24h)` is not an SLA-miss detector; deadlines are per-case `sla_deadline`. `get_service_time`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, and `discover_performance_dfg` generally need interval (start/complete) logs; the given schema has a single timestamp, and that limitation is only half-addressed. `filter_event_attribute_values` is never actually called. Helper functions (`urgency`, `skill_match`, `schedulable`, etc.) stay unspecified, which is acceptable in a sketch but leaves the policy under-determined.

**Lesser issues.** Batching C006 with C012 across two people stretches Rule 8. Score weights are arbitrary aside from “governance-tunable.” Section 5 stays generic rather than explaining this batch’s actual moves. Throughput/SLA comparisons stay qualitative (appropriate given no engine, but still estimates).

The piece reasons about trade-offs and does not claim a single optimum. The remaining defects are localized design contradictions and PM4Py audit errors, not a collapsed policy. That caps it well below a high-9 under strict grading.
