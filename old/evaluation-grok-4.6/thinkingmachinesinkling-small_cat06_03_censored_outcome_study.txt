**6.2**

The write-up avoids the main failure modes (coding rejects/withdrawals/opens as non-default, training on the 21,600 with no selection story, random splits, post-outcome fields as features). The nine-section structure is complete and the leakage/limitations discussion is mostly the right kind of argument. It is not close to flawless. Several design and implementation errors are material, not cosmetic.

**What is actually strong.** The primary estimand is correctly restricted to mature activated accounts, with rejected/withdrawn/open treated as competing or undefined rather than \(y=0\). The 48,000-row flow table adds up and separates mature labels, immature activations, competing exits, transfers, and still-open cases. The five missingness mechanisms are named and distinguished. The leakage table covers the required fields with the right keep/repair/drop instincts (`collection_status` and post-activation events especially). Temporal validation is motivated well, and the decision-use boundary (association vs selection vs counterfactual; three deployment feedback loops) is honest.

**Target and prediction time are underspecified.** “First Credit Review or 3rd event, whichever occurs first / whichever is more process-meaningful” is not one rule. The 90-day clock starts at activation, so from the prediction instant the horizon is random (time-to-activation + 90 days). That is a real semantic gap for an “early warning from the first few events” product; a time-to-default-after-activation formulation is listed in the prompt and then barely used to discipline the primary binary target.

**Label maturity is defined incorrectly, twice.** `label_available_at` is already the time the label exists. Adding another 35 days double-counts feed delay. The complementary calendar rule is also wrong: 15 Feb 2026 minus 90 minus 35 is about 13 Oct 2025, not “31 Oct” or “~8 Nov.” A sensitivity cohort that “guarantees maturity” with those cutoffs does not.

**The temporal split fights the snapshot.** Train / val / test by start date around 1 Jul and 15 Oct 2025 puts the official test window (and much of validation) into the region where 90-day outcomes plus 7–35 day feeds cannot have matured by 15 Feb 2026. Evaluating only on the mature subset then selects the fastest-to-activate cases. Quarterly backtesting is a better idea and is left as a sketch. That is a serious validation flaw for this exact calendar.

**Censoring strategy is conceptually right, operationally thin.** The primary analysis is still a calibrated classifier on the 21,600. That is defensible *as a conditional estimand*, but IPW is described as a way to “approximate the full 48,000,” which is not what IPW identifies. Transfer linkage (60%) and permanently missing partner feeds are discussed, not designed (no explicit linked/unlinked estimand, no missingness model).

**Code is a comment-heavy outline, not the required pipeline.** `pm4py.format_dataframe`, a start-time split, and `get_prefixes_from_log` appear, but prefixes are never used. There is no per-case as-of filter that drops events after the prediction time *before* prefixing, so a 3-event prefix can include `Account Activated`. `pm4py.filter_log(log, id_list)` is not a trustworthy PM4Py API. The feature table is a placeholder (`prefix_len=3`, start month, region). Full 48k cohort counts are not printed. Same-day `documents_total` and as-of `risk_score` are not implemented. Elapsed-time prefixes are specified in §4 and absent in §9. `label_available_at + 35 days` is baked into the skeleton.

**Smaller defects that still count.** Historical region×amount default rates are outcome-derived and need a stricter as-of construction. Dedup on a 1-minute key can delete legitimate events. Comparison operators are missing in places. “OMOVED” and unused leak-column lists signal unfinished audit logic.

Net: statistically literate and process-aware, with the right refusal to fake labels — but a broken maturity rule, a test window that cannot support the 90-day target, an inconsistent prediction time, and a skeleton that does not enforce the as-of discipline the prose insists on. Under a nearly-flawless bar, that is a mid-6, not an 8–10.
