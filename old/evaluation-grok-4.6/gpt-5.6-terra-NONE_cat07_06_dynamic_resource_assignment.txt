## What is actually strong

The audit catalog is broader than the minimum list and is mostly well motivated (handover, reassignment, mismatch, overtime exposure, SLA near-misses, supervision compliance, specialist concentration, policy deviation).

That is not enough. The question asks for a policy that can be executed and audited on *this* queue. The queue table is where the answer breaks.

## Critical assignment errors

**C015 is a compliance miss.** Jonas has Security = 0. Skill 0 is ineligible, including under supervision. The rule allows skill 1–2 only with Gita/Hugo oversight; it does not create an exception for skill 0. The case note naming Eva/Jonas is a trap: Eva is also Security 0. The hedge (“preparing/observing… otherwise assign R8 alone”) shows the author saw the problem and still put Jonas in the assignment cell. For an auditable Security control, that is disqualifying.

**Overtime is treated as a hard constraint, then violated.** The answer says high-OT resources get work only if the case is urgent *and* no safer alternative exists. Then:

- C003 (normal, 18h) stays with David (high OT, 14 cases, 3h left). Continuity is only medium-handover / 0.66 complexity. Celine is Diagnosis 3 with spare capacity.
- C006 (normal, 36h, batchable) stays with Iris (high OT). Jonas and Hugo are Quality 3 with lower risk.
- C014 (normal, 25h, medium handover) stays with David again.

None of these meet the answer’s own exception test. “We accepted the trade-off” is not a defense when the rule was declared non-overridable.

**The 20% training rule is not met.** Normal/low cases are the pool. The answer claims C010 and C015.

- C010: Bruno is Approval **2**, not a trainee, and his training priority is **quality**, not approval. Preparation-by-a-competent-approver is not a documented training-priority assignment.
- C015: invalid (skill 0).

Meanwhile C009 is labeled “training opportunity,” owner Amal is Diagnosis 1, and Amal’s stated priority *is* diagnosis. The answer takes it away from Amal and gives it to Celine (already Diagnosis 3; her priority is negotiation), then mislabels that as development. That is the opposite of the constraint.

**Hours remaining are displayed and then ignored.** David cannot absorb a diagnosis loop, a rework case, plus two specialist consults on 3 remaining hours. Farid is given C002 (urgent, 2h), C011 (urgent VIP, 4h), and C010 finalization on 6.5h. Gita is given C001 (urgent, 3h, complexity 0.88) despite Hugo having more hours, fewer cases, and lower OT. A policy that scores capacity but does not use it is not operational.

## Policy / algorithm defects

The scoring function is decorative. No weights, no thresholds, no worked example, so two auditors would not reproduce the same ranking. That undercuts the “auditable” claim.

The pseudocode is inconsistent with the prose:

- After the Security branch it `continue`s, skipping the overtime filter.
- `urgent_exception(case, eligible)` consults `eligible` while that set is still being built.
- SLA “scoring” is mostly case classification; it does not use candidate-specific expected service time even though the text later says estimates should be checked.

Quality batching is allowed for C006 and C012 and then not used: the two batchable checks go to different people.

Section 4 never instantiates first-available or specialist-only on C001–C016. The comparison stays generic, so the reader cannot see where the proposed batch actually differs.

## PM4Py sketch

Useful as an audit notebook, not as a learning pipeline. It names most requested APIs and caveats version drift. Gaps that matter at this standard:

- `discover_batches` is in the provided function list and is directly relevant to Quality Check; it is unused.
- The sketch audits after the fact; it does not show how service times, handover networks, or role/similarity discovery would *set* scores or eligibility.
- Several calls are left as “conceptual” (`filter_case_performance` units, `insert_case_service_waiting_time` arguments). Acceptable as a sketch, not tight.

## Net

This reads like a strong essay wrapped around an unreliable dispatcher. The framework, worker text, and indicator list would support a much higher score if the queue had obeyed the stated guards. It does not: skill-0 Security, repeated high-OT loading on non-urgent work, a missed on-label training case, and invented training credit. Those are logical and compliance flaws in the main deliverable. A nearly flawless answer is not in reach.
