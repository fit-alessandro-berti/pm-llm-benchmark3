7.4

The final write-up (sections 1–8) is a strong, constraint-compliant fairness assessment, but it is not nearly flawless. The substantive argument is better than the technical and conceptual execution.

**What it gets right.** It uses the required headings, covers all listed fairness dimensions, and does not treat raw approval gaps as discrimination or treat case mix as a full excuse. The two-estimand design (total burden vs. within-need comparison), the warning against post-treatment controls, and the treatment of language/accommodation steps as rights-preserving rather than defects are the right conceptual moves. Mechanisms, limitations, mitigations-with-tradeoffs, and the calibrated bottom line (procedural concern, not proven discrimination) match the evidence.

**Material problems.**

1. **PM4Py plan has real technical errors, not just version caveats.** `filter_case_performance(..., 21.0, ...)` and `filter_paths_performance(..., 2.0, ...)` treat thresholds as days. In PM4Py those APIs work in seconds, so the filters as written would not select the intended cases. Assumed columns `service_time` / `waiting_time` after `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are not the actual attribute names. `get_variants` return type is handled inconsistently (`len(cases)` vs. counts). `filter_paths_performance` and `discover_declare` signatures are guessed. Labeling one call “pseudocode” does not cover the rest of the block, which is presented as runnable.

2. **Declarative/log-skeleton checks are not actually used for fairness.** The prompt asked for skeleton/DECLARE checks *to support the assessment*. The code only discovers models and then lists expected rules as comments. There is no group-wise conformance, violation rate, or constraint-deviation comparison. Handover, working-together, resource-similarity, and role discovery are similarly computed and then left unused.

3. **Strategy and code disagree on the key covariates.** Section 2 correctly insists on stratifying by `language_preference`, accommodation request, and month. The PM4Py strata omit all three and group on raw `prior_case_count` without coarsening, which will shatter the matching design the text just recommended.

4. **One subgroup test is conceptually muddled.** “Among minority-language applicants, do Group B applicants still wait longer than other minority-language applicants?” sits badly with the scenario definition of Group B (minority language, mixed channel). That comparison is either nearly empty or undefined. The analogous Group C check is better, but the write-up never notes that Group C is only 62% accommodation-requesting and that other groups also request accommodations.

5. **Presentation and small over/under-claims.** Several waiting-time labels lost their arrows (`Submit  Identity Verification`), which makes the tables harder to read. Variant-level fairness in code computes only duration, not approval, appeal, or rework, despite the text treating those as central. Residual approval gaps (−5.5pp for B) are correctly reported, then somewhat sidelined in the bottom line as “procedural rather than purely outcome-based.”

The fairness reasoning would support a higher score. The PM4Py section, the strategy/code mismatch, and the Group B language comparison keep it well below “nearly flawless.”
