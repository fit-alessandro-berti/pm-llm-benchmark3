**7.3**

The final 10-section writeup is strong on temporal synthesis, causal restraint, and monitoring design, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the instructions, format misses and one real process-mining error keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is done well.** The timeline correctly splits artifacts by date: March is treated as semantic/provenance drift (raw fitness collapse, labels, duplicates, missing resources) while mapped flow and cycle time stay essentially flat; May is rework; June is queueing with nearly flat rework; July–August is tail inflation consistent with priority redistribution; October is seasonal mix; December is left confounded. That is the intended decomposition. DFG L1 figures vs January (3 / 40 / 59 / 28 / 64 / 36) and consecutive distances check out. The raw-vs-mapped discussion is conceptually right: mapping recovers conformance without proving the renamed check is the same activity, and post-May rework/queue growth cannot be logging. Competing hypotheses, segmentation, class-wise discrimination, and the monitor family (schema gating performance alerts, seasonal bands, no universal threshold) are useful and specific. The seven IF/THEN/BECAUSE rules match the mechanisms. Causal language is mostly disciplined (“cannot be split,” “plausible only,” Z-share jump unexplained).

**Format failures.** Section 2 is ~320 words against a hard 180–280 limit. Section 9 is ~450–470 words against 250–400. Those were explicit constraints, not suggestions.

**Substantive error: senior assessment.** The answer treats October’s `Assess→SeniorAssessment` jump (4→22 per 100) as tracking complexity “nearly one-to-one” (21%→41%) and therefore mostly population drift, dismissing recording change because the label count stayed at 11. That does not follow. Senior Assessment already existed; “historically recorded inconsistently” means under-use of an existing label, which would not add a new distinct label. Conditional on high-complexity share, recorded senior intensity goes from roughly 4/21 ≈ 19% to 22/41 ≈ 54% — a large behavioral or logging change, not a mix-only effect. The problem planted that inconsistency; the answer used the wrong contrast (unconditional rate vs mix share).

**Smaller quantitative/logic nits.**
- Main-path leakage (`CollectDocs→Assess` 95→89, eligibility→docs 98→95) is under-read as a supplier/stuck-case signature.
- July is a weak second *behavioral* change point; rework was already elevated in May–June. The dated event is a queue policy.
- Correlation is reported as `r ≈ 0.94` rather than `r ≈ -0.94`.
- “Volume +13%” for the Oct–Nov surge is only Sep→Oct (670→760); Nov is +18% vs September.
- Mapped-fitness deltas are rounded inconsistently (March −0.025 becomes −0.02).
- Loop cost 8–16h and many 0–100 “evidence/confidence” figures are invented; they are labeled as such, but still more decimal-looking than the aggregates support.
- Fast-variant “~20 points” in Section 2 is a loose baseline-to-trough figure, not a post-May step.

**Code.** Part A is coherent and the caveats are honest. Part B method names are in the right PM4Py neighborhood; a few wrappers (`discover_dfg` return tuple, dataframe vs EventLog for `filter_trace_attribute_values`) are slightly underspecified. Persistence is promised in comments and not enforced in the flagging rule.

Net: a high-quality diagnostic that would be a clear 8.5–9 if word limits were hit and senior assessment had been conditioned on complexity. Strictly graded, those misses plus the smaller inaccuracies land it at **7.3**.
