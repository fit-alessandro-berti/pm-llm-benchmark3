## Conceptual and identification flaws

E1 and E6 are the same contrast (P0 vs P1 on cycle time for all cases). The list therefore does not actually deliver seven distinct estimands. Mediation is not isolated: E3 is the total effect of P2, which jointly changes review occurrence, shared-queue load, and escalation volume. E4/E5 are policy effects of adding capacity or changing escalation rules, not decompositions of the flag’s effect through waiting. Calling E2 a controlled direct effect of P0 vs P2 on triage is mislabeled: P2 is designed to hold priority triage fixed. E5’s population (“escalated cases”) is post-treatment; P4 changes who is escalated.

P1 is internally contradictory. Section 1 treats P0 vs P1 as a simultaneous change in lane and routing. Section 6 says P1 has no triage, queue, or cycle-time effect and “strong” evidence against adoption. P1 is “suppress display,” not “zero the stored bit and freeze downstream automation.” Whether display suppression changes routing is the whole point of distinguishing P1 from P2. Declaring a null effect with strong evidence from a world where the flag is always shown is unjustified.

The framework sentence lumps controlled, interventional, and separable effects together. Rejecting natural direct/indirect effects is the right instinct; the estimands are not specified precisely enough to inherit that virtue.
