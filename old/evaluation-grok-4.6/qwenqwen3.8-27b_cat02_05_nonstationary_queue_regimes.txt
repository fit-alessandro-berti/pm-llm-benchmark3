**What is actually good (and why it is not enough).** The intended analysis is competent process-mining work. Regimes are not forced into one cut. The planned primary split (baseline weekday/weekend, outage, recovery, high-complex pre-intervention, intervention) is defensible. Recovery is correctly *not* treated as a normal high-throughput regime. Median service is not substituted for mean flow time. Little’s Law is treated as a windowed claim with boundary, proxy, and stationarity conditions, and the intended verdicts (`approximate only` / `not defensible`, none fully `applicable`) match the data limitations. Dispersion is decomposed into between-regime vs within-regime variation. Carry-over, coding-vs-mix ambiguity, and the unlogged Apr 29 start are labeled. Those are the right instincts.

**Format and reproducibility failures (decisive).**
- No `# 1`–`# 8` document.
- No executable Python: tables are not constructed in code, summaries are not printed, Fano/CV/lag-1 and the change-point heuristic are only designed, Little’s Law is not computed in a script, and the required PM4Py comments are not in a code block.
- Claims of the form “the code computes…” are unsupported. Nothing was run.

**Analytical and numerical issues that remain even in the notes.**
- Several figures were wrong on the first pass (weekend WIP 16.92 vs 16.33; WIP Fano ~5.86 vs ~5.75; weekend capacity minutes 1800 vs 2160). Later self-corrections are not penalized, but they show the arithmetic was never locked into a single reproducible table. A finished answer would have one consistent number for each estimand.
