**7.3**

The final write-up hits the required structure and the main scoring axes (artifact use, process-mining reasoning, trade-offs, non-generic mechanisms, conflicting-evidence synthesis). It is still not near-flawless. Several analytical gaps, unsupported numbers, and design overlaps keep it well below a high score under a strict rubric.

**What it does well**

Diagnosis is the strongest section. It uses the frequency DFG (3,600 auto, 2,550 fast approval, 2,300 dispatch), the performance DFG (0.4 / 1.8 / 3.2 hour medians versus the 70 / 180 hour complaint tail), rework economics (31% cheaper before rework, 9% after; 14.1% versus 3.3% reopen), conformance (fitness 0.91 versus 68% fitting traces, R1–R3, low precision), segment outcomes (22% / 5% and 18% / 6%), and resource limits (7 reviewers, 82% threshold, 2 fraud specialists, underused QC). It states the contradiction directly and does **not** treat the slow path as universally better.

A / B / C are materially different in mechanism: routing authority, information acquisition, fulfillment error-proofing. Each names targets, a causal mechanism, and explicit sacrifices. The experiment plan is better than generic: staged, control-aware, landed-cost and post-close window tied to the 50–70 / 150–180 hour complaint lag, and a real “shifted downstream” test.

**Defects that cost points**

1. **Unsupported quantification.** The prompt allows qualitative ranges, not new effect sizes. “25–40%” cycle-time cut for evidence cases is not implied by a 30–45% drop in *repeated requests*. Fraud p90 “20–30% worse,” abandonment “+10–15% relative,” and high-value p90 “150–180 hours” as a stop line are invented. That is a real accuracy problem, not just style.

2. **Missed process-mining artifacts.** START = 5,000 and END = 3,015 + 220 + 390 = 3,625. About 1,375 cases never close in the DFG. A serious diagnosis should treat incomplete traces / missing close events, especially next to 68% fitting traces and “complaint after close.” Fast approval → Quality/compliance close (350) is also unused; it is a distinct skip-dispatch variant. DFG in/out imbalances (repair quote, complaint/reopen) are ignored.

3. **A and B are not cleanly separated.** A’s “evidence-deferred lane” is B’s core object. That makes A a partial bundle rather than a standalone alternative, and it weakens the “three materially different redesigns” claim even though the table still treats them as alternatives.

4. **Dominance column is uninformative.** All four rows are “Conditional.” The constraint forbids claiming *exact* Pareto dominance; it does not forbid a directional call. Current is reasonably dominated for high-value / unclear work on total cost, quality, and compliance. Leaving every cell Conditional avoids a required comparative judgment.

5. **Stage 1 go-criterion is partly tautological.** Forcing high-value work off the fast path should move reopen rates toward the reviewed-path 5–6% *by construction* if those rates are path properties. The real test is net outcome (cycle, credits, utilization, landed cost), which is mentioned but not made the primary gate.

6. **Ungrounded add-ons.** Loaner / expedite in C, and A’s post-dispatch audit for Premium accessories, are plausible operations ideas but are not in the artifacts. Mid-risk handling is asserted without mid-risk outcome data.

7. **R3 is under-specified.** Complaint/reopen → Escalation board is 120 versus 310 to manual review. That is a concrete R3 exposure (possible close/deny without mandatory manual review). C gestures at it; diagnosis does not pin it.

8. **Complementarity vs. alternatives.** The body often sells A+B+C as a stacked program. The question asked for three competing redesigns plus a comparison. The sequence can stack them later; the designs themselves should stand alone more cleanly.

**Bottom line:** Strong applied process-mining memo with real trade-off handling and usable pilots. Too many extra numbers, missed log-structure clues, and blurred alternative boundaries for a top-band score.
