The audit is substantively strong: it catches both obvious complete-trace leaks and the subtler lineage defects (extraction-date SQL, nightly snapshot refresh, untimestamped mutable risk score, closure-populated team, last-role-from-full-case), preserves `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and correctly treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as process-legitimate-but-future while treating `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as order-infeasible plus same-timestamp ambiguity. Predictive vs causal vs actionable vs fairness standards are kept distinct, and correlated fields such as region are not blindly deleted.

The main factual overreach is in the Case Y JSON: after flagging `prior_claim_count_before_t0` as extraction-date leakage, the revised explanation still treats the displayed value `3` as the repaired count. That number is not known until recomputation. The same section is slightly too generous in listing `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` as credible drivers when lineage was never established; those belong in “retain only after point-in-time verification,” not in the defensible-at-`t0` list.

Smaller gaps: the table never states that the published global ranking itself is not a valid early-warning ranking (ranks 1–4 and 7 are future-derived); team causal glosses (“appeals cause breaches,” “the final team determines delay”) are rejected implicitly rather than tagged as unsupported causal stories; and `queue_length_at_t0` at “low” understates the ±5 minute snapshot risk. None of these overturn the audit, but they keep it short of near-flawless.
