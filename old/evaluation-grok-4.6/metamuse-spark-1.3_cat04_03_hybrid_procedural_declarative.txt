**6.0**

This is a structurally complete, conceptually ambitious answer that does the hard policy work well and then loses a lot of credit on executable coherence. It is not near-flawless. Under a strict reading, several core artifacts would not do what the narrative claims.

## What is actually strong

The R5/R6 treatment is the best part of the submission. The two resolutions are incompatible on activity+timestamp data, Resolution A is chosen for an explicit reason (R6’s enabling condition is not in the log), and the compliance risk is stated in both directions. T6 is evaluated under both policies, and Resolution B is correctly left `policy-dependent` rather than silently passed. That is the main thing the task asked models not to get wrong.

The requirement-to-formalism mapping is also mostly right: backbone concurrency/choice in a process tree; binary exclusion and eventuality in DECLARE; counts in the log skeleton; R12 as warnings; SoD and the emergency condition outside activity-only models. The DECLARE commentary correctly distinguishes response vs precedence, altresponse vs chain response, and noncoexistence vs nonsuccession. Temporal arithmetic is correct (seconds, `zeta = 2.0`, T7’s 34h escalation→opinion warning, T1/T2 payment delays inside 72±72h). Vacuity, missing attributes, and the non-normative status of support/confidence are discussed in the right places.

That is a solid skeleton of a hybrid design. The grade is low because the artifacts that are supposed to *implement* that design are not internally coherent.

## Major faults

**1. The log skeleton’s `always_after` relation is not a well-defined constraint set.**

The text says `always_after(A,B)` means “whenever B occurs, A occurs after B,” with `(Classify Risk, Receive Onboarding Request)` as the example. Under that reading, `(Activate Vendor, Classify Risk)` and `(Reject Vendor, Classify Risk)` are false: classification does not always lead to activation, and it does not always lead to rejection. Under the other reading (“whenever A occurs, it is after B”), those two pairs become ordinary precedence constraints, but then the worked example is wrong. The four pairs cannot be justified by one semantics.

That is not a documentation nit. `always_after` is one of six required keys, and two of its four members are unjustifiable on the author’s own definition. Relative to the usual PM4Py orientation — `(A,B)` meaning B always follows A when A occurs — the tuples are also inverted. An executable skeleton would therefore not match the Section 8 “pass” cells.

The justification for empty `equivalence` is a second formalism error: equivalence is equal occurrence counts, not coexistence. Receive/Classify/Close (and the three mandatory checks) *are* count-equivalent on the backbone. The reason given for leaving the set empty does not describe the relation.

**2. The process tree and the procedural verdicts do not describe the same checker.**

The tree contains only the happy-path backbone. Clarification, escalation/opinion, and post-activation repair are excluded, which is a defensible *modeling* choice. It is not a defensible *conformance* choice unless the log is projected onto backbone labels first. PM4Py tree alignments treat extra labels as log moves. T2, T5, T6, and T7 all contain activities that are not in `procedural_model`, yet all four are marked procedural `pass`. T5’s explanation (“Procedural ignores clarification”) admits projection semantics that are never specified in Section 3 or 10 and are not implemented in the code. As written, the Python tree is not an artifact that would reproduce the matrix.

Excluding optional behavior is fine. Marking traces that exhibit that behavior as tree-conforming, without a projection or silent/optional operator, is not.

**3. The four-eyes check is inverted relative to the API the task named.**

`pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, a, b)` keeps traces that *satisfy* four-eyes (different resources). The answer treats the return value as the violating set. If that code were run, T4 — the only SoD failure — would be classified backwards. For an “executable-style” resource layer, that is a decisive bug, not a comment-quality issue. The surrounding prose about “applicable approval” and missing `org:resource` → `not evaluated` is correct; the call that is supposed to implement R9 is not.

## Medium faults

- **T8 vs `activ_freq`.** Activate once and Reject once each sit inside `{0,1}`. Frequency constraints do not fail T8. `never_together` does. The matrix explanation overclaims.
- **Section 7 is not executable where it claims to be.** `check_R3_deadline` iterates a `groupby().apply(...)` result incorrectly and then `pass`es. `check_R6_tolerant` is `pass`. Pseudocode in comments does not rescue functions presented as the resource/data layer.
- **Overall cell vocabulary.** T7’s overall verdict is `pass with warning`. The task restricted cells to `pass`, `fail`, `warning`, `not evaluated`, `policy-dependent`. The right overall value is `warning`.
- **Near-vacuous R11 approximation.** `precedence(Verify Bank Account, Release First Payment)` is already implied by the backbone: every payment is preceded by the initial verification. It does not encode “another Verify between Change Bank Details and a later payment.” The text calls it weak (`confidence` 0.8) but still puts it in the model as if it contributed R11 coverage. The external check is the only encoding that actually matches R11; the DECLARE rule is decorative.

## Minor faults (still charged, per the scoring bar)

- “mean ±2” is written as if `zeta` were an additive constant; the numeric bounds used are actually `mean ± 2σ`.
- Unused empty DECLARE template keys are harmless but sloppy given “use only templates that you can explain.”
- Process-tree construction never shows parent linking; acceptable as sketch code, not as the “self-contained PM4Py” artifact requested.
- T7’s 2h activate→pay interval is `|2−72|=70 < 72` and therefore a pass, but it is a hair’s-breadth statistical pass the matrix treats as unremarkable.

## Test matrix, after correcting the above

Independent of the broken artifacts, the *intended* layered story is mostly right: T1–T3 pass; T4 is a resource-only fail; T5 is unanswered clarification; T6 fails strictly on R5 and is policy-dependent under R6; T7 is legally ordered and temporally late; T8 is a control-flow contradiction. That is why this is not a 3–4. The deductions are for claiming that the dictionaries and code implement that story when they do not.

## Net

A high score required coherent, runnable encodings and a matrix that those encodings would actually produce. This submission has the right architecture and the right conflict analysis, then ships a self-contradictory log skeleton, a process tree that cannot justify its own pass/fail column, and a four-eyes call with the wrong polarity. Those are not presentation issues; they are the hybrid model. **6.0.**
