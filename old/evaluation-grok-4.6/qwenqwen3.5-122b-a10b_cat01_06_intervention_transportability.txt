**4.6**

The write-up looks complete and lands on the right *class* of recommendation, but it is not close to a high score under a strict rubric. Several required constraints are violated, the central Site B partial-order opportunity is missed, Site A’s before/after contrast is still treated as an identified effect, and the PM4Py section is not a faithful model.

### Format and compliance
The ten headings and most table schemas are present, which is the only reason the score is not lower. Two hard word-count constraints fail: the transportability write-up is below 180 words, and the recommendation is below 250. Section 2 invents a node, `NOT true_overlap`, instead of the required vocabulary plus `S_` selection nodes. Only one selection node appears; site differences in digital share, role specialization, IT lock, legal intensity, and capacity should have been `S_` nodes. That is not a cosmetic graph issue. It is the transport formalization the prompt asked for.

### Process structure
The most important analogical point is underplayed or stated incorrectly. At Site B, `Identity Check` may start after `Register` for **every** case. `Document Check` cannot start before originals when originals are required. Those are different constraints. The DFG already shows `Register -> Identity Check` (2,050) and `Register -> Originals Receipt` (1,860), with a median originals delay of 18h. The high-value intervention is therefore: run identity against the originals wait on the 62% statutory path, and run identity against document check only when originals are not required.

The answer instead treats physical cases as having no legal concurrency, equates “originals required” with “not digital,” and copies a digital-only eligibility rule into the design, the scenarios, and the recommendation. That collapses a three-way distinction (digital/no-originals, originals-required with identity overlap, illegal document-check start) into a single 38% share. The naive Site A copy is rightly rejected, but the modified design is still too coarse.

There is also a factual error: Site A does **not** require a separate legal review for high-value cases. The comparison table states the opposite for both A-before and A-after.

### Causal transportability
Co-interventions are listed, which is necessary and not sufficient. The evidence table still interprets the median 41→32h move as “parallelism reduced waiting time,” and waiting-before-assessment as a “direct effect,” with “none specific” in the non-transferable column. That directly contradicts the instruction not to treat the nine-hour reduction as an identified treatment effect. Mix shift (48%→40% high-complexity), +2 verification FTE, API latency, huddles, and better timestamps can account for most of the observed change. Productive FTE is not given its own evidence row. Identity service-time improvement is scored at high transfer confidence even though an API upgrade is an explicit co-intervention.

The graph does not encode the main Site B mechanism (`identity_capacity` overlapping `originals_required` delay) and mis-wires several others (`API_latency -> identity_capacity`, `legal_capacity -> assessment_capacity`).

### Quantitative scenarios
The required “mean better, tail worse” case is present, which is a plus. The rest is weakly identified. It is never clear whether cycle-time changes apply to eligible cases or the whole book. A 10% median reduction on a 38% subgroup cannot move the portfolio median by 10% without a heroic within-stratum effect. Site A’s 9h is never decomposed into the parts that could possibly travel (roughly: wait-before-assessment 18→12; sequential verification 3.5+7.2 vs parallel `max`; mix; headcount). DFG loop mass (360 supplements, 345 returning to document check) is unused. Eligible-share labels wander between “digital 38%,” “legal 40%,” and “IT lock 0%.”

### Intervention, POWL, and PM4Py
The candidate process still fails to encode the statutory originals gate in the partial order. Legal review is written as a free choice with approve/reject rather than a case-type-mandatory branch. `Notify` disappears. WIP=6 is copied from Site A despite different specialized pools (4 / 7 / 3 / 2). Resource responsibilities omit assessors and legal, the two likely downstream bottlenecks.

Section 8 is a hard fail on model fidelity. `POWL`, `POWLNode`, `POWLEdge`, and `RelationType` are not the PM4Py POWL constructors (`Transition`, `StrictPartialOrder`, `OperatorPOWL`, `order.add_edge`). Activity names with spaces are unquoted in `parse_process_tree`. The unused import is wrong. Soundness/playout calls do not pass the needed marking objects. Most damaging: the POWL-to-net conversion is commented out and replaced with Site A’s after tree. That is exactly the substitution the prompt forbade.

### Pilot and recommendation
`conditional go` is the correct bucket, and the pilot at least names randomization, stop rules, and mechanism vs implementation. The design is still sloppy. Physical cases are used as a randomization stratum even though they are declared ineligible. Case-level assignment with four tiny shared pools is a SUTVA problem; “separate queues” is not a credible safeguard at this staffing. If assignment is randomized, a propensity score is the wrong transport tool. The pilot never measures the quantities that distinguish mechanism failure from implementation failure in this setting: true overlap versus back-entered timestamps, illegal `Document Check` starts, lock-workaround rate, originals-wait / identity overlap, and assessor/legal utilization.

The recommendation then repeats the overstatement that parallelism is impossible on physical claims, undershoots the word count, and does not specify modification triggers as distinct from expand/abandon.

### Net
This is a structured, partly informed memo, not a nearly correct transport analysis. The right instinct (do not copy Site A onto every Site B case) is repeatedly implemented with the wrong process cut, the wrong identification claims, and a non-executable model section.
