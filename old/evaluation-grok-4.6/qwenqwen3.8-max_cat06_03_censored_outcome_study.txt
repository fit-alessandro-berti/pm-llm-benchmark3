**6.4**

The final nine-section writeup is a competent competing-risk design, not a weak “train on the 21,600 and call everyone else non-default” answer. It still falls well short of nearly flawless. Several issues are design errors, not nits.

**What is actually good.** The primary estimand is the right kind of object: activated-default under the observed process, with rejected / withdrawn / transferred / open cases kept as competing or censored states rather than fake non-defaults. The 48,000-row cohort table adds up and assigns each bucket a status. The five missingness mechanisms are distinguished. The leakage table covers every required field. Validation is temporal, and the code is a real as-of pipeline (`format_dataframe`, cutoff filtering, prefix time, forbidden columns, start-time split, `get_prefixes_from_log`) rather than a random-split notebook.

**Validation design is the largest statistical miss.** The headline split trains only through 30 June 2025 (entirely pre-policy) and tests 15 October–31 December 2025. By 15 February 2026 almost no test case can have a matured non-default: activation + 90 days + a 7–35 day feed delay lands after extraction. The writeup notices the maturity problem and mentions rolling backtests, then still presents this as the primary scheme. For an operational “current policy” warning model, training only on the obsolete regime is inverted. A maturity-gated split (train/validate on start cohorts whose `activation+90+35` is known by the cutoff, with post-policy and post-migration treated as shift/canary folds) should have been the main design, not a caveat.

**Implementation does not match the leakage story.** The problem flags that `risk_score` is updated across events. Warehouse extracts commonly stamp the *latest* score on every row. Filtering `timestamp <= T0` then keeps a future score. The table says “last value with valid-from ≤ T0”; the code never reconstructs an as-of score and never treats `risk_score` as a forbidden/denormalized column. That is exactly the leak the study claims to prevent. `documents_total` is “repair if snapshot time ≤ T0” in the audit and unconditionally dropped in the skeleton. `eligible_for_early_warning` is computed and then not applied to the feature table.

**The skeleton is not spec-clean.** The request was ~80–160 lines; the block is ~200+ with blanks/comments. `dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log)` does not match current PM4Py (`discover_dfg` returns the DFG dict). That will crash. Naive vs UTC cutoff comparisons are another footgun. These are not polish issues on a required “concrete enough to implement” artifact.

**Smaller but real conceptual slack.** Partner missingness is described as MNAR, then the primary strategy still leads with IPCW (a MAR tool). Fine-Gray is named without the usual prediction caveats. The optional 180-day window from `T0` quietly changes the estimand. “Uncertainty-aware” is a conformal one-liner, not a design (what is reported to the lender, how intervals are calibrated under censoring, aleatoric vs selection uncertainty). Excluding pre-prefix terminals is defensible, but it walks away from “every application” without a second-stage disposition model.

The statistical core is clearly above a failing or naive answer. Under the stated bar—hypercritical, high scores only if nearly flawless—the validation fold, `risk_score` as-of hole, API/`n` spec failures, and MAR/MNAR slip keep it in the mid-6s.
