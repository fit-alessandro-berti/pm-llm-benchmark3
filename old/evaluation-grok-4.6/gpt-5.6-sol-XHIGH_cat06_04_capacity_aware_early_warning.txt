**7.0**

The answer is structurally complete, arithmetically mostly careful, and clearly above a threshold-only or `p_now`-ranking response. It correctly excludes I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05 from I-07, refuses to treat `p_now` as an interventional probability, and treats capacity as a portfolio problem with externalities. Under a strict standard it is not nearly flawless. Several of the flaws are in the decision core, not in prose.

**Decision rule: coherent, not deployable as written.**  
The two-estimate setup (`p_i^0` vs uncertain `d_i`) and the five eligibility gates are the right architecture. The calibration adjustments for SME and Strategic Enterprise are applied in the right direction and the worked examples are correct. After that the rule frays.

Last-safe-start is made lexicographically second, yet it is never defined. Gate 2 is circular: the swarm must finish before last-safe-start, which is itself the time when the swarm must start. There is no assumed remaining-work model (resolution + validation after a 45-minute swarm), so a reviewer cannot reproduce who is at last-safe-start. Public Sector is told to carry a “sampling interval” that is never specified or used. Non-Native-Language Support is told not to take a point correction, then I-09 is scored with the raw 0.18–0.30 band as if it were as reliable as any other range.

The portfolio utility `U(A,s)` is announced and then abandoned. Allocation is a five-step lexicographic heuristic. No alternative sets are scored. That is a decision-theoretic gap, not a presentation choice: the answer tells the reader to compare portfolios and then ranks cases.

The spillover charge `q_t ≈ 6 s_t V̄_U + cost of 8–20 queue-minutes` likely double-counts the same delay process as both a probability shock and a separate minute-cost. Deducting the full listed false-alarm burden in `D_i` is flagged as conservative, but it is still the number used for gating. Burden that occurs only on unnecessary interventions should not be subtracted at 100% when the case is a true high-risk diagnosis.

**I-07 is the largest operational error.**  
I-07 is Triage, 1.5 h to a *resolution* SLA, €5k, lower-bound direct value €500. A joint 45-minute swarm leaves about 45 minutes for whatever remains of diagnosis, resolution work, and customer validation. That is a serious fit problem for the stated intervention, and it sits in tension with the author’s own gate that the swarm must leave time for resolution work. Calling this “now-or-never” and putting it first (A1) does not make it feasible. After one slot’s spillover (0.06–0.18 extra expected breaches on the untreated diagnosis queue), I-07 is often net-negative on the author’s own `U(A)`. Lexicographic urgency is allowed to override that without showing that a diagnostic swarm can still change the SLA outcome.

A strict reading is that I-07 should have been wait/do-not-intervene or a non-swarm escalation, with the slot held for I-09/I-02/I-06-if-validated or for option value on I-04.

**Allocation table: right exclusions, weak comparative justification.**  
I-03 is handled correctly, including a resolution-stage alternative. I-05 is correctly suppressed rather than treated as three independent warnings. I-06’s imputation hold is right; the 10-minute verify-or-else path is not. If the live queue field stays down, there is no fallback (treat as invalid, impute conservatively, or escalate manually).

Selecting I-07, I-09, I-02 plus a 15:00 reserve is internally consistent with the lexicographic rule and with management’s reserve preference. It is not shown to be the best of the feasible sets. I-09’s nominal €2.35k/€3.55k is allowed to drive A2 despite the note that both calibration and effect estimates are sparse; a zero-effect branch appears only later, in the adverse row. I-02’s Strategic overprediction is mentioned, then the unadjusted effect range is still used to clear the “positive lower bound” gate. I-04 wait is reasonable on option value; I-08 wait is reasonable on negative `D^L` and burden. Those waits would be more convincing if a two-slot plan had been compared with the three-slot plan the adverse scenario later calls likely harmful.

The reserve logic is incomplete on a binding constraint the author already stated: both specialists run the swarm jointly, so slots are sequential, not parallel. A reserved fourth *count* does not create a reserved *team* if both people are inside a swarm when the regulatory case arrives. Pre-emption, abort, or delayed start is not specified. “Tier BA” is undefined. The Strategic formula is broken in the LaTeX (`p_{\text{{now}}`).

**Expected-impact table: auditable arithmetic, sloppy accounting.**  
The I-07 / I-09 / I-02 building blocks and most scenario sums check out:

- Optimistic 0.65–0.84 avoided, gross €9.85k–€13.10k, after €1.45k burden €8.40k–€11.65k  
- Base midpoints 0.26+0.24+0.15 = 0.65, about €8.40k after burden  
- Adverse one-zero-effect band 0.26–0.38 and €3.0k–€5.6k gross  

Intervention cost €1,350 is correct. The €13k break-even on `V̄_U` is correct for 0.54 induced breaches.

Problems: full burden is deducted in every scenario, including optimistic, where a true-positive swarm should not pay the false-alarm charge. Optimistic spillover 0.01–0.015 is invented rather than “near the low end.” `V̄_U` is left symbolic even though the narrative later needs it for the claim that base can still be favorable. Adverse congestion is 15–30 minutes per slot; mapping that into “0.54 or more” expected breaches is asserted, not derived. Confidence “Low–moderate” is attached to Optimistic and Base alike, so the column adds little. Non-monetary terms are correctly left out of the euros and then not brought back as constraints in the table interpretation.

The deeper issue is that Section 3 assumes all three slots fire, while Section 1’s stop rules and the Adverse row say the third slot is the one that can flip the portfolio negative. The planned allocation and the impact table are not the same policy.

**Repeated-alert policy is the strongest section.**  
Stateful candidate, 90-minute cool-down, material-event re-entry, and post-swarm suppression are operationally usable. The I-05 vs I-07 contrast is exactly what the rubric asks for: unchanged refresh is not evidence; a new customer log is, even if `p` falls slightly. One residual unclarity: I-07 is “subject to intervention-history check,” but no prior swarm is stated, so the caveat is ornamental.

**Fairness: required topics covered; two of the safeguards fight the allocation.**  
Value starvation, NNL exclusion-by-uncertainty, segment calibration, treatment-contaminated labels, and spillover incidence are all present, and the safeguards are concrete (duty lane, value cap, logged assignment, no treated-as-untreated labels, congestion pause, matched NNL vs native rates). Two inconsistencies: (i) a 1-in-4 duty lane *plus* a separate regulatory reserve is two reserved slots, but the live plan spends three economic/urgency slots and one reserve; (ii) the €50k ranking cap is proposed in Section 5 and not used in Section 2. The 1.25 disparity trigger is arbitrary.

**Monitoring is wide and mostly serious.**  
Operational stop rules, segment calibration triggers, capacity-margin randomization, saturation experiments under interference, sentinel untreated labels, shadow-mode rule changes, and stratified fairness reporting are the right monitoring stack. It is slightly idealized for a two-specialist, four-slot shop, and it does not say how a 0.05 residual-calibration trigger interacts with the already-known SME / Strategic biases. Policy-induced drift is described correctly.

**Net.**  
This is a strong policy memo with real decision structure, not a probability ranking. Strictly graded, it still spends a scarce slot on the weakest process-fit case, leaves its central timing object undefined, does not compute the portfolio objective it writes down, scores the most uncertain case as if the effect range were solid, and reports impact for a three-swarm book it simultaneously warns may be harmful. Those are not polish issues. They keep it well short of a reference answer.
