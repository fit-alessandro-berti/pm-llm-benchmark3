**6.4**

The memo is in the right family: it refuses I-03, splits I-05 from I-07, prices spillovers, and does not rank on `p_now`. It is not close to flawless. The formal rule and the live allocation are not the same rule, several stated robustness adjustments are never used in the numbers, and the I-08 exclusion is decision-theoretically brittle.

**What is solid.** Stage eligibility is correct. I-03 is excluded because the swarm is inert after Diagnosis Complete, not because risk is low. I-05 is suppressed as a stale refresh; I-07 is re-admitted on the new log and then held for value/spillover, which is the right distinction. The three scenarios are present, the arithmetic that *is* shown is auditable, and Sections 5–6 name the required failure modes (label contamination, segment calibration, NNLS sparsity, spillover incidence on untreated groups) with operational rather than purely hortatory controls.

**The allocation rule is not coherent.** Section 1 imposes a maximin gate: lower-bound direct benefit must be positive. That knocks out I-08 by **€450** on a €100,000 case (`0.05 × 100000 − 450 − 5000 = −450`). Midpoint I-08 after a €1,800 spillover charge is about **€2,750**, which outranks I-09 (~€1,750) and I-02 (~€900). The memo never justifies why maximin applies *before* spillover and only as a binary gate. After high spillover, I-02’s own lower bound is about `950 − 2700 = −1,750` and I-09’s is about `2350 − 2700 = −350`; both are selected anyway. So robustness is used to kill one high-EV case and then abandoned for the cases that were kept. That is not a robustness principle. It is an ad hoc filter.

A coherent treatment of I-08 is available and only half-used: 8 hours left, Strategic overprediction, €5,000 disruption, and low ARR make *waiting* rational because the option value of more evidence is high. That is an option-value argument, not a −€450 hard gate. The memo mentions urgency in the table and then pretends the gate did the work.

**Stated adjustments are decorative.** Calibration intervals are specified (SME +0.04–0.09, Strategic −0.05–0.12, NNLS widened) and then do not enter a single priority number. Ranking is `midpoint ARR × value − costs − flat spillover`. For NNLS the text promises conservative effect assumptions; the impact table still uses the supplied 0.18–0.30 band at face value. Observational `p` is correctly distinguished from interventional effect in prose and then ignored in the scoring model. If calibration and effect uncertainty do not change ranks, they are commentary.

**Optimistic scenario is mis-specified.** The brief asked for the *upper half* of each ARR range, not the upper endpoint. Upper half of I-04’s `[0.10, 0.28]` is `[0.19, 0.28]`, not 0.28. Using the max inflates optimistic avoided breaches (0.80) and the +€20.9k net. Midpoints of ranges are also treated as expectations without a distributional assumption.

**Spillover is a flat tax.** One charge (€900 / €1,800 / €2,700) is applied regardless of who is treated. Two of three selected cases (I-02, I-09) are already in Diagnosis Started, which is exactly where the two seniors are pulled off ordinary diagnosis work. The memo never asks whether those two slots hit the same congested queue harder than one triage swarm (I-04) plus reserve. The €15k “average untreated diagnosis-stage value” is an unstated modeling choice that drives every net figure; sensitivity to that assumption is absent. The 8–20 / 15–30 minute delay channel is not used except as already folded into 0.01–0.03.

**False-alarm burden is taken as a certain cost of intervening.** That follows the suggested formula, but the brief required discussion of what that formula gets wrong. For I-08 the entire €5,000 is subtracted even on paths where the case would have breached. For a Strategic case whose *calibrated* risk is ~0.31–0.38, the opposite error also matters: unnecessary disruption is *more* likely, which is a reason to wait—but that is not the analysis that was written. The dual definition of “direct benefit” (gate includes swarm cost; Section 3 table does not) makes the two tables hard to reconcile without re-deriving them.

**Reserve and backup timing do not fit the clocks.** Reserving one slot for a possible regulatory arrival is a defensible preference. Naming I-06 as first backup “after 15:00” is not: I-02 and I-09 have 3.0h and 2.5h to SLA at 12:00. A 15:00 reallocation is mostly too late for diagnosis-stage leftovers. There is no protocol for how fast I-06’s imputed live-queue field is validated against a 4-hour clock.

**Fairness does not constrain the live book.** The selected set is two Strategic plus one sparse NNLS case. Public Sector I-01 is well calibrated, first alert, complete data, ARR 0.18–0.35; base net after spillover is essentially zero (−€115). It is parked. The “max two Strategic slots” cap is set at a level that the chosen book already satisfies, so it does not bite. The fairness override is defined and then not applied or explicitly declined.

**Precision and residual gaps.** Nets are reported as +€20.9k / +€9.8k / −€1.25k as if the inputs were known cash flows. Time-feasibility of a 45-minute swarm from Triage with 1.5h left (I-07) is not discussed. Non-monetary public/regulatory duties are mostly reduced to the reserved slot. Causal-effect monitoring is the right idea; “randomize slots where ethical” is asserted without a feasible design under four daily slots and a prohibition on repeat swarms.

The piece would survive a first operational review. It would not survive a consistency check of the rule against the recommended book. That is why it is a mid-6, not an 8.
