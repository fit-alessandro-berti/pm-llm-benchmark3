**8.1**

This is a strong, internally consistent reconstruction with real process-mining competence: missing starts are constrained by resource non-overlap (especially P11 after P08, not at queue entry or maintenance end), OPEN/waiting cases stay in exposure, service is not confused with sojourn, utilization is not computed from completed cases alone, and Little’s Law is correctly refused as a stationary claim in favor of a finite-window occupancy identity. Most tabulated numbers check out against the supplied log (case sojourns, missing-start intervals, 2-hour counts, WIP area 2085/1340, busy-time ranges, snapshots, C2 15-minute availability adjustment). Format compliance is complete.

It is not nearly flawless. Deductions below are for concrete gaps, not taste.

**Reproducibility.** The narrative’s best system-level occupancy claim—total WIP peaking at 13 in 11:30–11:40 and 11:50–12:00—is not produced by the Python. Hourly snapshots never show 13, and `summarize` only peaks station WIP. Several Section 5 figures (subperiod mean queues 3.35/0.71/3.29/3.73, ~80%/89% utilization) are asserted in prose and only partly recoverable from printed tables. `pm4py.format_dataframe` / `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are called without explicit column-key arguments; `start_timestamp_key` is version-fragile. `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is only a comment, which the prompt allows, but the script is not a full check on every headline number.

**Bounds that overstate uncertainty.** Zero service is allowed because “no positive minimum is supplied.” That is a mathematical limit, not an operational one: timestamps are on a 5-minute grid and these are inspection/calibration tasks. The 0-minute pole drives S1 busy time down to 535 minutes and the rounding envelope to 61.5–96.9% utilization, ranges the text itself calls uninformative. A 5-minute floor, labeled as such, would have been the defensible sensitivity bound.

**Work-conserving evidence underused.** Non-FIFO is observed (P09 before P08; P17 before P16) and correctly not imposed. Same-resource 5–10 minute gaps while queues are nonempty (C2 10:50–11:15 beyond maintenance; C2 13:20–13:30; C1 13:00–13:05 and 13:50–13:55) are mostly folded into rounding/dispatch remarks. Those idle-with-queue intervals are direct evidence that full-window utilization is not a pure capacity measure; they should have been quantified in the bottleneck section rather than left implicit.

**Minor inconsistencies and presentation nits.** Section 1 opens with the bottleneck conclusion before stating assumptions. Completed S2 service mean 36.25 is reported as 36.3 in a write-up that warns against unjustified precision. The illustrative 4.3 vs 3.0 services/hour capacity argument uses the same recorded closed-interval means the authors elsewhere treat as selected/biased. Extreme rounding envelopes are coupled via interval unions (good) after Section 2 first gives independent ±5 minute duration envelopes (easy to misread as the operational range).

**What remains stable and well done.** Distinguishing wait vs service vs sojourn under missing starts (sum fixed, extrema not additive); keeping censored occupancy in Little-style checks (5.79 vs 5.13, 3.72 vs 2.72); treating the 14-case mean 146.4 minutes as a completed-cohort statistic only; documenting that PM4Py case sojourn starts at S1 *start* and omits S1 queueing; not attributing all of P08’s 25-minute C2 gap to maintenance. Those choices are correct and should be preserved in any revision.

The analysis is well above a competent pass. Under a strict bar it loses material credit for unverified headline occupancy, an unrealistic zero-service pole, and incomplete use of idle-with-queue evidence.
