**6.5**

The final nine-section design is substantively on the right problem: activation is treated as selective observation, rejected/withdrawn/open cases are not recoded as non-default, the 48,000-case flow is internally consistent, ordinary right-censoring is separated from undefined outcomes, and the primary split is temporal rather than random. Those are the failure modes the prompt is written to catch, and the answer avoids them. It is not close to flawless.

The most serious statistical flaw is that the “survival” target is not implemented as time-to-default. In the skeleton, every *observed* activated case—default and non-default—gets `t_end = activation + 90d`. That collapses the survival framing to a binary label with identical event times, which makes Harrell’s C and any duration model poorly defined. The comment “use the exact default timestamp in production” does not repair the estimand the rest of the write-up claims to evaluate.

There are several factual and internal contradictions. The test window is specified as 1 Jul–30 Sep 2025 and described as “partially post-migration,” but the migration is 15 Oct 2025, so that window is entirely pre-migration. The same section recommends training across regimes with a policy indicator (Option B), while the actual split trains only on 2024; under that split `pre_policy` has no training variation and is a pure period dummy. The leakage table says `documents_total` should be repaired to an event-time count; `_LEAK` drops it. Prose restricts test to Jul–Sep 2025; the code sends every start after Jun 2025 to test, including the immature late-2025 mass.

Process-aware prefix rules are incomplete. Prediction is defined as “first *K* events before the activation decision,” but *K* = 5/7 is never hard-stopped at Accept/activation, so longer prefixes can ingest post-decision events. Loop features at *K* = 3 are nearly vacuous for the stated control flow. Queue/workload features are almost absent. Transfers are excluded, but the write-up does not treat the realistic dependence problem that linked re-appearances may already sit inside the 21,600 labeled rows.

The skeleton is usable as data-engineering pseudocode, but it misses an explicit requirement: `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is only mentioned, not called. Case classification ignores `label_available_at` even though Section 2 says to use it when present. The block is also well over the requested ~80–160 lines. `discover_dfg` / `get_variants` are used reasonably; avoiding full-log waiting-time features is the correct leakage instinct.

Limitations and decision-use language are among the stronger parts (association vs selection vs counterfactual; three deployment feedback channels). They do not offset the survival-time error, the migration-date mistake, or the design/code contradictions. Under a strict reading, this is a good study outline with several real inaccuracies, not a near-flawless one.
