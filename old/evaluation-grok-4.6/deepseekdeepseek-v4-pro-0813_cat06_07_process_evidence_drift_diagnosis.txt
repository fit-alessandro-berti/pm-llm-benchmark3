The final six-section writeup matches the intended diagnosis: April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a routing/mix change without conditional-risk change, August–September is overlapping process, concept, and population shift, and October is not comparable because of immature labels. That is the core of a strong answer. The remaining gaps are real, and they keep it well short of nearly flawless.

**What is solid.** The diagnosis table covers all six drift types, separates supporting from contradicting evidence, and does not collapse ten months into one label. Variant-weighted reconstructions for August (~0.31 vs observed 0.28) and September (~0.34 vs 0.31) are the right way to show that those months are mostly real V3 deterioration, not censoring. October is handled correctly: 55% maturity, 52-day delay, backlog on long paths, and an implausible drop to 0.16 while parts waits and V3 share stay high. The staged response is proportionate: freeze, do not fit to October’s 0.16, clean April before any April use, recalibrate mix-only June, do not treat August–September as a calibration-only problem, and withhold September–October from training. Alert gating (maturity/data-quality suppress retraining) is specified. The five failure modes are the ones that actually matter.

**Where it is imprecise.** June is listed as a primary covariate-drift month. Predictions are made immediately after diagnosis, before Dispatch / Remote Fix / Order Part, so the V2 share jump is process/policy mix after the prediction point, not a clean change in \(X\). Covariate drift is much clearer for September–October (rural distance, device age, Partner Z) and for April’s broken features. Population-shift and covariate-shift rows also recycle the same Partner Z facts instead of splitting “who arrives” from “which inputs the model sees.”

October’s “expected ~0.34” is then used as supporting evidence for censoring. That is circular: October V3 rates are explicitly unusable. The direct evidence is maturity, delay, backlog, and process metrics that did not improve. The hypothesized rate belongs in the narrative as a check, not in the evidence column.

The prompt asked what would *falsify* each explanation. That is mostly deferred to Section 3. The table’s “contradicting” column is not the same thing.

**Code is a sketch with a methodological bug.** `get_variants` is run on an event-calendar slice (`mode="events"`). That fragments traces at month boundaries and will not recover the V1/V2/V3 mix the rest of the memo depends on. Case-start IDs are computed, then unused for variant/DFG extraction. `extract_temporal_features_dataframe(...).to_dict()` dumps a case-feature table into each monthly summary; that is not an auditable KPI. `DIAG_ASSESS_COMPLETED` is invented. Performance-DFG on in-month events will miss `Order Part → Part Received` waits that cross months—the exact edge used as process evidence.

Those are not cosmetic. They do not overturn the diagnosis, but under a strict rubric they are enough to leave the answer in the high-7s to 8.0 range rather than 9+.
