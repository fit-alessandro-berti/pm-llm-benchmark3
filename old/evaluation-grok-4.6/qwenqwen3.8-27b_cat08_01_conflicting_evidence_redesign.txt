**7.0**

The final write-up is a strong, well-structured process-mining brief: it uses the DFG, performance DFG, replay/alignment diagnostics, rework stats, segment outcomes, and resource data; it states the fast-path contradiction clearly; the three mechanisms are different; and it does not pretend the slow path is always better or that exact ROI/Pareto dominance is knowable. It is not nearly flawless.

**What works**
- Diagnosis is the best part. Frequencies (3,600 / 2,550 / 2,300), fast-path medians, 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen, 31%→9% cost erosion, fitness 0.91 / 68% fitting traces, R1–R3, and the 22%/18% vs 5%/6% high-value split are used as *arguments*, not decoration.
- Redesigns are non-generic: segment-aware auto-screen + pre-dispatch gate; evidence portal with two-strike triage and digital-literacy fallback; parallel reviewed lane plus part-price SLA and escalation pre-review. Each names a real sacrifice.
- Experiment design is serious: holdouts, 30/60-day reopen, utilization thresholds, and explicit tests for downstream shift (complaints, queues, abandonment, fraud wait).

**Material flaws**
1. **Unsupported numbers in the comparison table and stop/go rules.** The prompt allows qualitative ranges, not invented effects. Gate delay of 6–18h is given. “+20–50% cycle time” for B and “−20–40% median/p90” for C are not. Same for several pilot targets (e.g. high-value reopen “toward 12–15% / 10–12%”). That violates the “do not pretend to calculate values the data do not support” constraint.
2. **The three redesigns are one stacked operating model, not three alternative balances.** A routes leftovers to B/C; B assumes A’s gate and C’s lane; C assumes A/B already exist. They are complementary workstreams, so the Pareto table’s “current vs A vs B vs C” comparison is partly apples-to-oranges.
3. **Redesign C misreads R1 and the fraud bottleneck.** R1 requires *either* manual *or* fraud before Fast approval, not both on every high-value case. Forcing parallel fraud onto ~35% of volume (vs ~980 current fraud edges) against two specialists is the opposite of what the DFG and resource notes imply. Failure modes mention overload; the design itself does not use selective fraud.
4. **Capacity for A’s gate is under-argued.** Two quality inspectors are “underused except after repair campaigns,” not spare capacity for ~2,000 low-risk dispatches. “Automated + sampled” is stated, then the text still treats inspectors as the conversion of idle capacity.

**Smaller issues**
- Digital literacy is treated as an intake attribute; the prompt only flags it as a portal risk.
- “Human triage” in B is unstaffed (new role vs the seven reviewers).
- Premium R2 + post-dispatch evidence still ships first and recovers later—the same failure mode the diagnosis attacks—without costing the recall.
- Manual inflows 1,400+890+310 are summed as if the DFG were flow-conserving; Manual and Fraud edges do not balance (selected/incomplete DFG). A careful miner would have said so.

Accurate artifact use and real trade-off logic keep this well above a generic ops essay. The fabricated percentages, stacked-not-alternative framing, and the R1/fraud design error are enough that a high score is not justified.
