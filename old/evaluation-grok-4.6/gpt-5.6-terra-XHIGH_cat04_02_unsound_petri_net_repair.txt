## Defects that keep the score down

**1. Repair B is mischaracterized (most serious).**
Adding `p_analysis → t_waive_review` does not make analysis “optional on the waiver route.” It makes analysis and waiver mutually exclusive. If `t_analyze` fires first, waiver is disabled and the case can finish only via standard review. If waiver fires first, analysis is impossible, not optional. “Optional” would mean the waiver path can complete with or without `t_analyze`. This net cannot do both. The task explicitly grades clarity of any policy change; calling this “optional analysis” is the wrong policy statement, even though the later clause about ordering is operationally true.

**2. Repair A’s “new risk” is not new.**
Two silent joins already feed `p_decision` in the original net. Adding one arc does not introduce that. The real residual risk of A is different: `p_analysis_done` becomes a shared preset of two joins. That is safe only because `p_review_done` and `p_waived` are mutually exclusive. The answer never states that invariant, so deadlock-freedom of the repair is assumed rather than shown.

**3. Completeness gap on the repair’s correctness.**
A cost-1 claim is only as good as the proof that the edit restores proper completion on *both* routes and does not create a new deadlock. The standard-review route is checked on the original net, and a waiver completion sequence is given in the diagnostic script, but Section 4 never shows why `t_join_normal` and `t_join_waived` cannot both become enabled, or why `fm` is now reachable after waiver. For a marking-based assignment, that omitted step is material.

**4. Smaller unclarities.**
- Section 3’s second sequence is the same defect with a different interleaving, not an additional defect; the text never says there is only one defect class.
- “Globally minimal” is slightly overstated without ruling out other cost-1 policy-preserving rewires (the authorize-side alternative is easy to invent and would break the normal join; it should have been excluded explicitly).
- Section 6’s qualitative soundness claim is reasonable but still predictive (“should be sound”) with no marking proof that every reachable marking of Repair A can reach `fm`.
- The fire helper reimplements semantics by hand. That is allowed, but it is another place an enabledness/`Marking` mismatch could hide, and it is not needed to make the argument.

The firing table and the choice of Repair A as the deployment fix are good enough that this is clearly above a middling answer.
