**6.1**

The write-up is a competent, on-format advisory memo: it keeps the seven required sections, distinguishes segments and slack, refuses a blanket ban on air/vans, and treats rework and obsolescence as first-class issues. Under a strict reading it is not close to excellent. Several hard requirements are missed, several mechanisms are misstated, and a few numbers are invented.

**Requirement misses.** Section 3 analyzes seven options (S4, S2, S3, S6, S5, S7, S9), not the required eight. S1 and S12 appear only later as packaging glue, without the mandated target-path / mechanism / service-quality-resilience / monitoring treatment. The effects table inherits the same shortfall.

**Invented or overstated quantities.** The prompt forbids precise lifecycle numbers that are not in the evidence. Section 6’s “30–40% transport / 20% energy” package claim is unsupported. S4’s “4–6× per order” is true only for orders actually switched off air, not for the Standard-stock Service-contract segment as a whole (16% mix, 7.8 vs 4.2 kgCO2e). S2’s “up to 50%” energy cut copies the 2.8→1.3 kWh batch-size range as if it were the intervention effect versus the current mix, which already routes most MTO through batch wait (3020/3780). The packaging rebound invents a “>50%” return drop; the log says 40%. Several KPI targets (OTD >90%, MTO rework <10%, late +2%) are arbitrary relative to the segment baselines (MTO Regular is already 18% late).

**Path grounding is selective.** Strong use of SP1 (22%), air vs ground, CNC_A/B rework (9% vs 18%), and consolidation wait. Weak or absent use of the DFG’s high-signal exceptions: partial shipment → backorder (1010 / 220 / 24% late, second shipment), emergency production (760), dedicated-van empty returns (52 kgCO2e path), heat-treatment batch wait (1.7× late risk), inspection→rework production (470), and cancel-due-to-delay (530). S8, S10, and S11 are the natural treatments for those paths and are not analyzed. R1 is reduced to “don’t ban air,” which is not the same as keeping an alternative route under carrier disruption.

**Mechanism and multi-criteria errors.** S2’s material column is labeled Neutral while obsolescence is the material risk. Versus today’s already-batched flow, capped dynamic batching is mixed on cycle time, not a clean increase. S3/S7 energy should fall if rework loops shrink (the CNC_B rework variant is 32 kWh vs 16 kWh). S6 conflates Q1 (reinforced pack for >8 kg) with packaging–route compatibility (geometry / `route_mode` mismatches that drive rework packaging). Adding an inspection “pause” without capacity can worsen the rushed-inspection loop the log already blames on queues. Resilience risk for S4/S5 is marked Low, which understates a ground-heavy book during disruption.

**Rebounds and validation.** Six named risks hit the required themes, but several are thin: consolidation “displacing capacity to urgent orders” is confused; the obvious expedite/air rebound from tighter ground SLAs is missing; CNC_A overload, heat-treat late risk, and empty-van displacement are missing. Validation’s SP1 check (air and whether lead time was later exceeded) is the wrong operationalization of “meet promised LT with probability >85%.” `weight>8kg` is not an observed attribute. Low model precision from tangled emergency/partial variants is never designed around. No phased or segment-control evaluation plan.

**What still works.** Boundary is mostly gate-to-gate and honest about the flattened log. The package (slack-aware routing + capped batching + CNC_A/quality gate + exception log) is coherent and does not sacrifice Critical-downtime service for the lowest-emission option. That is why this is not a failing answer—but the missing eighth intervention, invented package-level percentages, unused high-impact variants, and several effect-direction mistakes keep it well below a high score.
