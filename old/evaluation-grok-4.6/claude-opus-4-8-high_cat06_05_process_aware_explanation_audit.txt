**7.0**

The audit is substantively strong: it catches both obvious full-trace leakage and the subtler lineage bugs, keeps congestion and prefix-age as real `t0` signals, treats `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` as semantically legitimate but temporally inadmissible, and keeps predictive / causal / actionable / fairness standards distinct. Under a strict reading of the spec, it is not nearly flawless.

**Missing required coverage.** Section 1 was required to cover every sequence pattern. `Damage Assessment Completed → Settlement Decision Recorded` (−0.09) is absent. That pattern is the natural counterpart of the appeal pattern: process-legal, post-`t0`, and a hindsight “happy path.” Omitting it is a real completeness failure, not a formatting nit.

**Severity and terminology errors.** `appeal_seen_anywhere` is correctly marked critical, but the almost-equivalent sequence `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` is only high. Local Case Z shows these sequence features fire with value 1 on a `t0` prefix, so they are the same class of leak. `Coverage Check Completed → Fraud Investigation Opened` is labeled “future-possible, **not enabled** at `t0`.” After coverage check, fraud investigation is a parallel, trigger-gated next activity; “not enabled” contradicts the section 2 taxonomy (occurred / impossible / future-possible). `final_handler_team` is also under-rated: the served value is the closure field, so it is critical leakage that happens to correlate with the `t0` team ~65% of the time.

**Process-awareness gaps.** Replacing `full_trace_variant_id` with `prefix_variant_id` is incomplete: at this `t0` every case has the same prefix `Claim Registered → Coverage Check Completed`, so the prefix variant has no variance. `document_requests_before_t0` is correctly noted as ~0; the same point should have been made for prefix variant.

**Causal-story audit is thinner than the leakage audit.** The team line “Appeals cause breaches” is rejected as impossible at `t0`, but not as reverse causality even post hoc (appeals follow Partial/Deny and often occur after the 10-day clock is already lost). “More documents reduce uncertainty” is never challenged on the merits. Complexity is treated as a non-lever; in this process it is also the explicit fast-track routing criterion, which is a different operational claim than “it causes breach.”

**Section 4 issues.** Case Y’s narrative still talks about an “elevated score” after stripping `full_trace_variant_id` (+0.31 of 0.71). After that removal the remaining mass is ~0.40; breach status is unknown until re-fit. Putting `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` in `credible_drivers` treats a post-repair value as known. `initial_reserve` is given “low / legitimate” status with no lineage in the prompt; the hedge is appropriate, the severity is not. Case Z’s uncertainty note omits `same_day_document_count_snapshot = 6`, a local driver whose nightly 23:00 refresh is already flagged globally.

**What remains good, and why this is not lower.** Subtle lineage is handled well (`<= extraction_date`, 23:00 snapshot, employee experience as-of extraction, missing `feature_valid_from`, ±5 min queue join). Region/channel/`calendar_week` are retained as proxies rather than deleted. Section 2 is a credible validation suite and correctly states that a frequent DFG edge is not a normative rule. The JSON does not replay leaked outcomes in the narratives. Section 5 hits every required workstream and is sensibly ordered.

The piece reads like a strong specialist audit with one required item dropped and several precision errors. That is well above a weak answer that trusts full-trace SHAP, and well short of a 9–10.
