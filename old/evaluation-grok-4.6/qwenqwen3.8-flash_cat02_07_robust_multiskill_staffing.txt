**4.5**

The write-up has the right shape (specialization vs pooling, robustness rather than base-case mean delay, labeled assumptions, two real objective functions) and several sections are competent. It is not close to a high score. Required deliverables are incomplete, the core capacity math is wrong in a way that drives the bottleneck story, and the scenario table is presented as simulation output that was never produced.

**Format and completeness (severe).** The spec requires nine top-level headings. `# 9. Limits and validation plan` is missing. Section 8’s Python block is truncated mid-literal (`"Validation`), so it cannot encode the remaining tables, run three policies, emit an event log, call PM4Py, or print comparison tables. That is not a “well-specified scaffold with omitted mechanisms identified”; it is an unfinished artifact. Claiming “3-seed scaffold estimates” in §4 while the simulator does not exist is a false precision problem, even though some surrounding language says the numbers are uncertain.

**Offered-load analysis (severe).** Morning Electrical and Software capacity omit Chen, who is on shift 08:00–16:00 and qualified for both (multipliers 1.00 and 1.25). Base software 10:00–12:00 is therefore written as ~1.01 (Farah only) instead of roughly 2.25 × 38.5 against Chen+Farah (~0.48). Electrical 08:00–10:00 is written as ~0.72 (Ada+Farah) instead of Ada+Chen+Farah (~0.43). The same omission inflates U1 and U4 morning ratios (e.g. U1 software 10:00–12:00 ~1.72 is not defensible unless Chen is absent). U3/U5, where Chen is actually gone, are closer to correct—which shows the error is inconsistent, not a deliberate scenario assumption. Late mechanical after 18:00 and 16:00–18:00 software (Chen off, Emil on break) are the parts that hold.

**Policy design (mixed, sometimes strawman).** Pooling vs hybrid vs specialization is the right comparison, and hybrid-for-robustness is the right kind of recommendation. Strict eligibility is stacked: Mechanical = Ada/Ben only, Software = Emil only. Emil starts at 12:00, so morning software has **zero** dedicated staff by construction. That makes Strict fail for a modeling choice, not only for the data. A fair “fixed queues” policy would still assign Chen/Farah to software. Hybrid reservation (Ben / Deepa / Emil) and overflow/preemption triggers are implementable; adding handover+setup+cross-team all onto resource busy time is conservative and is at least labeled.

**Scenario table and metrics.** Required columns are present and the qualitative failure modes often match the (flawed) load story. `max_resource_utilization` capped at 1.00 collapses overloaded states into the same number. Ranges such as P1 SLA 92–96% in base pooling sit poorly next to a claimed software load ratio of 1.01. Resilience labels are mostly restatements of the SLA columns. P2 evening failure given no overnight staff is a real structural point and is handled better than the morning bottlenecks.

**What remains solid.** §1 mixture-of-uniforms service model, two objectives (squared P1 miss; worst-case P1 plus utilization CV and 90% fragility), parallel vs sequential coordination trade-off, U2 dual interpretation (renormalized mix vs doubled counts), and §5 refusal to pick pooling just because it wins on base mean delay. §6 (gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, preemption, Validation as a second bottleneck) and §7 (case-level vs stage-level waiting, handover network, shock flags, SLA clock) are process-mining competent. Those do not offset missing §9, a non-runnable §8, or the Chen-shaped hole in the capacity table that the rest of the argument leans on.
