**8.0**

The diagnosis is substantively right: April is a schema/pipeline break with healthy labels, June is a mix/routing shift without within-variant outcome change, July is offsetting errors, August is genuine V3/supplier drift with Partner Z ruled out, September is stacked process/concept/population/censoring layers, and October is not a comparable cohort. That is the intended triangulation, and the answer generally separates observation, inference, and falsifiers.

It is not near-flawless. Recurring missing comparators make the primary table and several later claims hard to read as written (`V2 share 3452%`, `V3 share 2042%`, `AUROC 0.760.66`, `ECE  ~0.05 and AUROC  ~0.78`). That is a real defect in an audit artifact, not a cosmetic one.

**Section 1.** The six-row structure is the right taxonomy, and the April vs October split is correctly not collapsed into one “model decay” story. Strong points: April’s 100% maturity plus 34 d delay as evidence that labels are fine while features are not; June’s “calibration moves, AUROC holds” mix signature; August’s 0% Partner Z as a clean population-shift falsifier; October’s 0.16 observed rate treated as selection, not recovery.

Nits that should have been caught:

- V3 mix is attributed to “Jul–Oct.” July is 22%, essentially baseline. The mix jump is August–September (35% → 42%), coinciding with the supplier shock and then Partner Z. That timing error blurs covariate drift vs process/population drift.
- October is placed in both pipeline failure and label delay. The overlap is acknowledged, but the pipeline row’s “immediate action” then bundles schema-diff gates with “fix outcome API,” which muddies containment.
- Section 1 quarantines Apr–May; Section 4 rehabilitates May after recompute. Pick one rule.
- “Supplier issues start 10 Jul” is not a serious contradiction of residual May drift. Incomplete backfill is the actual May evidence.
- V3 predicted risk ≈ 0.2 in later sections is not in the data. Only the pooled mean prediction is observed.

**Section 2.** This is the strongest section. July as two errors that cancel in the aggregate, August as understated by 93% maturity, and October as long-path labels withheld are the right narrative. The closing point—that aggregate metrics actively mislead—is the correct operational moral.

Residual issues: the September Brier “zero skill” line (`0.31×0.69=0.214`) is arithmetically correct for the *observed* rate, but that rate is 82% mature and downward-biased. Matching a constant predictor of a biased label is not the same as demonstrating zero skill against the true risk. Also, at diagnosis time the model cannot see realized part delay; the answer sometimes treats that as proven concept drift rather than “p(y|x) changed for the deployed feature set, ranking-vs-base-rate still untested.”

**Section 3.** Coverage of the required tests is complete (T1–T7 match the spec; T8 is a useful extra). Competing-hypothesis predictions are mostly well posed, especially T2 (April replay), T3 (mix vs within-stratum), T5 (same-path month effect), and T6 (transportability).

Gaps: T1’s “climbs toward 0.28–0.35” is a reasonable back-of-envelope, not a derived number, and should be labeled as such. T5/T3 never ask the sharper question of whether V3 deterioration is a uniform rate shift (calibration/segment intercept) or a ranking change inside V3. That distinction is exactly what decides recalibration vs retrain, and it is underplayed.

**Section 4.** The staging is proportionate: quarantine pipeline months, maturity-gate evaluation, mix-aware calibration, feature repair, then retrain. Training-window hygiene is mostly right (April unsafe despite mature labels; October unsafe despite looking “better”). The one-model-plus-stratified-calibrators default, with a Partner Z or V3 head only if T6/T5 fail, is the correct default given sample sizes.

The acceptance gate is the main conceptual smear. Stratified calibrators cannot restore AUROC. If the gate means “if discrimination is already ~0.78 and ECE is the only problem, skip retrain,” that is a good diagnostic. As written it reads as if post-calibration AUROC is an outcome of Stage 1. Also missing: whether the supplier shock is a regime to learn or a transitory disruption to avoid baking into weights. Including July as “real signal” assumes the former without stating the assumption.

**Section 5.** Monitor layering and alert dependencies are the right architecture: `data_quality_ok` / `maturity_ok` suppress predictive alarms and block auto-retrain; performance-DFG and population monitors are label-independent early warning. That meets the spec.

The code sketch hits the required APIs and, importantly, documents event-calendar vs case-start cohorts. It is still sloppy as executable audit code:

- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is almost certainly the wrong tool for median case duration (time-bucketed temporal aggregates, not per-case duration). The NaN fallback hides the mistake.
- `_median_hours` assumes a nested dict. In several PM4Py versions the performance DFG maps edges to a scalar; `k in rec` then TypeErrors.
- `closed_share` is used as a maturity proxy. Breach labels can mature at the SLA instant, before `Case Closed`; that is a different quantity from the table’s label-maturity column.
- Calendar-month DFGs on `mode="events"` fragments traces; treating `dfg_edges_calendar_month` as a structural-change hint without noting spurious cut-trace edges is a small overclaim.

These are sketch-level faults, but the prompt asked for appropriate use of those functions, not decorative imports.

**Section 6.** The six failure modes match the requested set and the mitigations are actionable. This section is clean. The only extra that would have helped: treating a uniform V3 rate jump as “concept drift” and launching a full retrain, when a V3 intercept plus a supplier-latency feature would have been enough.

**Net.** This is a strong PPM audit: it does not pick one drift label for the whole year, it does not treat October’s 0.16 as a real recovery, and it keeps remediation proportional. It loses a near-top score on unreadable operators in the diagnosis table, a few timing/attribution slips (V3 mix in July; assumed V3 predicted risk), the recalibration/AUROC gate, and PM4Py details that would fail a literal replay. Under a strict bar that is an 8.0, not a 9.
