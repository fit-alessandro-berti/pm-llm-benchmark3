**6.0**

The design reasoning is competent and mostly constraint-compliant, but the answer is not close to flawless. Several required pieces are thin, imprecise, or internally inconsistent, and the implementation section contains errors that would undo the conservative policy the prose defends.

**What holds up.** The six-way classification matches the required taxonomy, and the terminal actions (drop / merge / refine / retain / quarantine) are the right ones. The pair table is the strongest part: e03/e04 and e11/e16 are treated as multi-system observations of one fact; e03/e05 and e13/e14 are left untouched; e06/e07 and e15 are kept as abstraction-level events; e08/e09 is correctly called a harmonization collision rather than a duplicate; e09/e10 is ordered, not merged. Hard constraints are respected in the *design*: e05 and e14 are not dropped, ingestion is not the business clock, original timestamps are kept, and a total order is not sold as physical truth. The `have_common_object=True` warning is exactly right: e13 and e14 share O1/V1 while remaining distinct payments. The microsecond argument is also right.

**Material flaws.**

1. **The “decision tree” is a flat checklist**, not a branching procedure. There is no test order for overlapping diagnoses (e.g., same timestamp + shared objects + different payload could be 2, 3, 4, or 5). Ambiguous pairs are not given alternate rows in §2 even though the prompt invited that.

2. **§6 does not implement §3–4.** `order_key` is never built, so `ocel_sort_by_additional_column` and `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta` sort/offset a missing column. `preserve_originals` fails for `original_activity` (`ev.get(col)` looks up a column that does not exist). `merge_verified_pair` filters only the dropped eid, then *appends* a merged row, leaving the original keep event in place—so the helper duplicates rather than merges. E2O union is a comment, not a spec. Referential checks ignore objects and relation cardinality.

3. **Conservative code contradicts conservative policy.** After the two verified merges, `ocel_drop_duplicates` on `(activity, timestamp)` is applied to the whole log. That is the global, uninspected pass §9 itself lists as a failure mode, and it can collapse e05 with the surviving pick and e13/e14/e15 with each other. The prompt asked for a *justified* use of that function, not a second, broader wipe.

4. **Sensitivity is partly wrong and thin.** Under a *merge* (union relations), I1 and I2 would still both have a Pick; “Item-flattened DFG loses I2 pick” describes `drop_duplicates`, not `merge_duplicates`. Raw E2O is ~44, not ~41. OTG, ET–OT, and lifecycle/performance are named more than analyzed. Six edges are listed, but several are restatements of the same “self-loop vs. two activities” point.

5. **Coverage vs. target.** Audit trail and reconstruction are a schema dump. Conservative and evidence-based pipelines are short on operational steps (conflict rules, object-equality grain, who may refine labels). The write-up is well under the requested 1,900–2,900 words excluding code; the missing length is missing argument, not padding.

**Net.** Strong duplicate *judgments* and a correct multi-pipeline frame, undermined by a non-working ordering/merge implementation, an unsafe library call in the conservative path, and sloppy DFG claims. That is a mid-range answer under a strict rubric, not a high one.
