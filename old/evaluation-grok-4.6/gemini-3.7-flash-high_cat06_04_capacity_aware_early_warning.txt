6.3

The write-up is complete, hits the required gotchas (I-03 ineligible; I-05 suppressed vs I-07 re-entered; spillover can dominate), and is more decision-theoretic than a `p_now` ranking. It is not nearly flawless. Several stated rules are not the rules actually used, several numbers are invented and then treated as calculated, and a few choices are internally inconsistent.

**Decision rule.** The five-gate structure is deployable and correctly separates stage eligibility from value. The hard ban after `Diagnosis Complete` is right. Segment recalibration is directionally correct. Then the formal NEV is not the NEV that is computed. The displayed formula subtracts `(1-p)C_fa` and a per-case spillover term; every case score in §2 subtracts the full false-alarm burden and **zero** spillover. Spillover is a portfolio externality, so putting it inside a per-case rank is already the wrong object; writing it in and then dropping it is worse. The 6 h / 4 h runway cutoffs are arbitrary and are allowed to override NEV without a stated lexicographic order, which makes the “decision-theoretic” metric decorative. Reserving **exactly** one slot is written as a hard constraint even though the brief calls it a preference. NNLS is told to shrink effects to the lower bound in §1 and to use UCB optimism in §5; the live choice follows neither consistently.

**Allocation.** I-03 is correctly refused. I-05 is correctly refused. I-02 / I-09 / I-07 plus a reserve is a coherent reading of the urgency filter, not of the NEV table they printed. That is a problem they created: I-01’s printed NEV exceeds I-07’s, I-04’s paper NEV is the largest in the book, and I-06’s midpoint NEV is competitive with I-02 if the jump is real. Parking I-06 for a 10-minute check without a contingent fourth-slot rule is operationally incomplete: after they lock three swarms and freeze the reserve, a confirmed I-06 has nowhere to go. I-07 is the case the brief wants distinguished from I-05, but treating 0.20–0.32 as still available at 1.5 h **in Triage** is optimistic to the point of implausibility (45 minutes of swarm, then diagnosis complete, resolution, and validation). I-02 and I-09 are second alerts with no stated new event; under the authors’ own persistence rule they look more like I-05 than like first-alert candidates. Precise NEVs (€1,685, €2,700, €3,550) are false precision on midpoints of wide, non-causal ranges.

**Impact table.** The three-scenario skeleton and the arithmetic that **is** shown for `Δp × V` add up. Around that core the table is sloppy. Optimistic confidence is marked higher than base, which is backwards. False-alarm adders (€150 / €350 / €550) are not derived from the three `C_fa` values. Untreated queue value is asserted as €18k, then quietly switched to €15k in the optimistic row. Adverse spillover 0.035 is outside the stated 0.01–0.03 band. I-09’s adverse effect is cut below its supplied lower bound while I-07’s optimistic effect is left at 0.30, so uncertainty is applied asymmetrically. The idle reserve’s opportunity cost is omitted. “€50,000 value at risk” is just the sum of contract values, not risk-weighted.

**Temporal policy.** The I-05 vs I-07 contrast is the strongest page and matches the brief. The 120-minute cool-down is concrete. It is then undermined by a `t_SLA ≤ 3 h` re-entry that can revive a static alert with no new evidence, which reopens the I-05 failure mode they just closed. Claiming I-07’s small `p` drop “reflects log processing” is not in the data.

**Fairness.** All five required risks are named and the safeguards are more specific than slogans, which is good. Several safeguards contradict the noon decision: a `Δp ≥ 0.15` hurdle would have blocked I-02 on its lower bound; a €30k value cap is not applied to the NEVs they report; NNLS UCB vs lower-bound shrinkage is unresolved. Dedicated swarm shifts would change the spillover model used in §3; that implication is not carried through.

**Monitoring.** Cadence, ECE/Brier, imputation breaker, and trigger table are operationally serious. Causal identification is the weak joint. RDD on a “slot cutoff” assumes a threshold policy they do not run. A 3–5% holdout on **non-critical borderline** cases will not identify effects on the urgent, high-value cases they actually swarm. IPW/DML is named, not specified (propensity features, overlap, what is logged when a swarm is refused). Policy-induced label bias is correctly diagnosed; the fix is not yet an estimator.

Net: a strong consulting memo with the right architecture and the right refusals, damaged by formula/computation mismatch, unused ranking math, an over-rigid reserve, uneven uncertainty handling, and invented constants presented as audit-grade. That is well short of near-flawless under a strict reading.
