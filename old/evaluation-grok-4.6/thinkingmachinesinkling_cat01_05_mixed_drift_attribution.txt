**6.8**

The core diagnosis is substantively right: March is mostly semantic/provenance, later months are real multi-mechanism drift, and artifacts do not share one change point. That is necessary but not sufficient. Under a strict reading the write-up is not nearly flawless.

**Format.** Sections 2 and 9 ignore hard word limits (~180–280 and ~250–400). Both run roughly 1.5–2× long. That is not a cosmetic miss; the spec asked for compressed, judgeable synthesis.

**Quantitative errors.** Two arithmetic mistakes in the required decomposition:
- July `missing_resource_pct` delta is \(6.5-1.05=+5.45\), not \(+5.15\).
- January–December DFG L1 on the stated seven rates is **36**, not **32** (\(|97-98|+|93-95|+|21-12|+|20-11|+|14-4|+|78-83|\)).

Those are exactly the numbers a second LLM is supposed to trust. Invented precision also leaks in: mix “4–6 h,” queue alert “>15 h,” service “~1–2 h,” none of which are identified in the aggregates. The prompt forbade manufacturing precision the tables cannot support.

**Causal overreach.** Section 9 claims the aggregate Assess→Request Info jump implies rework rose “not just within Z cases.” A 18%→43% Z-share shift plus a pre-existing Z complexity premium can produce that DFG movement with no within-group behavioral change. Section 4 admits the selection problem; section 9 then overclaims. CollectDocs→Assess *frequency* is also treated at times as if it measured queueing. It does not; waiting is a temporal quantity.

**Process-mining / code rigor.** Part A’s change-point rule (`fitness_recovery>0.30` or month-to-month mapped drop \(>0.05\)) never fires on May, June, July, or October in *this* series. The code therefore fails to detect the behavioral and performance breaks the prose insists on. Part B has API errors: `format_dataframe(..., resource_key=...)` is not a standard PM4Py signature; `filter_time_range` is applied to an unformatted frame with a nonstandard `timestamp_column` kwarg; DECLARE is hand-waved. Filter-then-format is the wrong order for the high-level API.

**Monitoring design.** The 11-monitor table and seven IF/THEN rules are the strongest part: different references, seasonality, FDR, owners. The “primary population” choice is inverted. Restricting the main monitor to post-migration, non-Z, standard-complexity, complete-ID cases hides the highest-risk mass (Z, seasonal high-complexity, incomplete provenance). That segment is a **control**, not the book. Thresholds such as mapped fitness `<0.85` would have missed the actual mapped trough (0.86). Ranking eight complementary mechanisms 1–8 treats a stack of causes as a horse race.

**What remains solid.** Timeline onset types (sudden / gradual / seasonal / mixed), raw-vs-mapped interpretation, mechanism-specific change points, supplier↔rework and staff↔queue links, priority as redistribution not reduction, December as season plus intervention, and the decision-rule set. Those meet the stated evaluation emphasis. The grade is held down by preventable arithmetic, a causal claim the aggregates cannot carry, a detector that does not detect, API slippage, and two ignored length constraints.
