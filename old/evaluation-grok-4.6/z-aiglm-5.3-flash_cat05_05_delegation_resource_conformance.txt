The final report is a strong investigative write-up on event-time roles, delegation scope, SoD, and alternative explanations, but it is not nearly flawless. Required executable checking is broken, several classifications overreach on ambiguous identity evidence, and a few load-bearing assumptions are treated as findings.

**What is solid.** Event-time reasoning is consistently correct: Eva is not QM on 2026-08-09 and is only a QE from 2026-08-12; Ben has no standing QM authority; Farid is IC only under D-IC-03; current master data is not used. Delegation conditions are mostly well formalized: D-QM-01 production-equipment scope vs cooling retrospectives, D-IC-03 no-subdelegation voiding D-IC-04, signed PDF vs later QMS migration row, and RR4 business-day counting (Tue 11 Aug → deadline Thu 13 Aug) for CHG-505 vs CHG-510. Section 4 respects RR6: repeated Clara–Ben pairing and reciprocity stay indicators, with benign and adverse accounts and discriminating tests. Headings match the spec; the report does not ask questions or declare collusion.

**Material defects.**

1. **Section 5 is not executable as written.** `pm4py_verdict` contains a line-broken ternary that is a `SyntaxError`, so the whole block fails to parse. Even if that dead function were removed, `emergency` is read from CSV as `"true"`/`"false"` strings; both are truthy, so `not ev["emergency"]` never selects the HIGH non-emergency RR3 path and the RR4 loop is applied to the wrong case set. Authority evaluation would therefore contradict the case table. Handover/working-together unpacking assumes a tuple/dict of `(a,b)→weight`; pm4py typically returns a graph, so the network print is unlikely to work. D-IC-04 is discussed in comments but never inserted into `delegs`. Unresolved `svc_qms` is baked in as a distinct resource, so four-eyes can **PASS** CHG-503 even though the narrative claims fail-closed. Sick-leave and credential-sharing facts never enter the coded checks. A required deliverable that cannot be run, and that would mis-score HIGH approvals if it did, is a major miss.

2. **Overconfident labels on ambiguous identity.** The prompt states there is no canonical answer for ambiguous identity evidence. CHG-506 is recorded as Ben during certified sick leave, office IP, with Ben denying the act — that is primarily DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM / INDETERMINATE until forensics land. Calling it UNAUTHORIZED as the primary class asserts a perpetrator. CHG-504 is formally a standing-QM approval inside a VPN window, but security cannot exclude credential sharing; AUTHORIZED as primary is defensible only with a louder identity caveat. CHG-509’s D-QM-02 absence condition is not established: Asha’s leave ends 14 Aug, the event is 15 Aug (weekend), and “principal must be absent” is assumed, not evidenced. VALID_SUBSTITUTION is generous; INDETERMINATE is the tighter call.

3. **CI-44 is assumed, not shown.** D-IC-03 is scoped to Cooling Incident CI-44 only. Neither CHG-505 nor CHG-510 is labeled CI-44. Mapping CHG-505 → CI-44 and then scoring a VALID_SUBSTITUTION emergency leg is a hypothesis. It should not be presented as established event-time authority.

4. **Invented control rules stated as if they were given.** The ±30-minute “reaction-window” for Asha’s VPN, “Critical” as a severity tier, and treating weekend silence as satisfying a written absence condition are analyst conventions. They needed to be labeled as such more sharply in the findings table, not only in the code comments.

5. **Technical vs control-objective split is uneven.** The report is good on CHG-501 (PDF vs late DB) and on Eva both approving and verifying CHG-509. It is thinner on whether a QE on sick leave can discharge Retrospective_QM_Review at all, and on whether Grace’s verification of CHG-507 meets the independence objective even though RR2 is literally satisfied.

**Net.** Core PM reasoning — who had which role at t, which instrument covered which scope, alias/service-account handling, and refusal to equate pair concentration with collusion — is clearly above average. Strictly, the broken PM4Py/pandas program, the CSV boolean trap, the CI-44 leap, and UNAUTHORIZED on unresolved account use keep this well short of a high score.
