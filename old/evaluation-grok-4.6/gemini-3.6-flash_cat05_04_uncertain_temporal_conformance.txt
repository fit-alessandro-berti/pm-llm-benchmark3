**6.1**

The analytical core is substantially better than the score suggests: clock-offset direction, resolution-before-correction, CEST/EDT/BST conversions, DST-fold elimination, T-404 interval propagation, and most SLA/profile classifications are correct. Under the required strictness, that is not enough. The write-up has material specification gaps, a few concrete numerical errors, and a Section 5 artifact that does not do what was asked.

### What is actually strong
Offset semantics are stated and applied correctly: `true = recorded − offset`, resolution first. HUB `[T−1.5s, T+1.5s]`, OPS-BER floor-plus-`[-90, +120]`, SENSOR `±10s`, VENDOR-NY, and the T-405 surviving fold triple are right. The eight-combination DST argument is the best part of the paper: ingestion order leaves only Alert in fold 0 and Start/Containment in fold 1, and the leftover minute+offset uncertainty is kept. T-404 does not collapse 18–25 minutes to a midpoint. T-406 states the entry-delay assumption instead of burying it. T-407 is shown with and without the unlogged causal cut. Endpoint arithmetic for the hard cases (T-401 S1, T-402 S1/S2/P3, T-403, T-408, the ζ=2/ζ=3 knife-edges at 960s, 1140s, 3300s) is careful. Midpoints are confined to Section 5.

### Classification and semantics
**INDETERMINATE is never used.** The rubric defines it for weak, unbounded, inconsistent, or untestable evidence. T-407 (`Alert_Sent` ≠ `Alert_Received`, no ack, causal rule not logged), T-409 (order not even established under a ±12 min guess), and T-406 (user text plus a second delay model) are the cases that exist to force that label. Treating all of them as `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` plus a confidence adjective flattens the required distinction. Negative durations are left in T-407 P1 and T-409 rather than being handled uniformly as causal truncation, a union, or non-testability.

Same-clock dependence is ignored. HUB–HUB and LEGACY–BER pairs are differenced as if independent. It does not flip the headline calls, but it is an unstated independence assumption on the only high-trust source.

T-407’s Section 2 note (“unconstrained upper bound”) contradicts the 20-minute construction actually used.

### Numerical and sensitivity defects
T-401 S3 is wrong: feasible close-minus-containment is about `[86918.5, 86941.5]s`, not `[86917, 86943]s`. Harmless for the call, not harmless in a timing paper.

The +5 min S3 limit is **130100s**. It must be `129600 + 300 = 129900s`. T-408 is still a certain miss, so the matrix does not silently change, but the header is simply false.

T-409’s start end-point is written `16:17:00.000Z` rather than `16:16:59.999Z`.

Section 4’s instruction was to discuss every classification change. The matrices are good; the prose covers four clusters and skips T-403/T-405/T-406/T-407/T-409 SLA flips and almost every ζ shift, including the interesting T-408 P2 overlap at 3300s and T-403 P1 still open at ζ=3.

Section 6 does not walk every `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` row.

### Section 5 (required code) is the largest miss
The prompt required one executable block that (4) derives duration intervals and interval classifications, (5) builds an explicitly lossy midpoint log and runs `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` at ζ=1,2,3, and (6) compares those diagnostics to the interval conclusions.

The script stops after building intervals. It never computes durations or CERTAIN/POSSIBLE/INDETERMINATE labels. It never installs the **supplied** temporal profile. The call is `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile(df_pm4py, zeta=z)`. Current PM4Py requires the profile object; this call is not a complete conformance check, and discovering a profile from these nine messy cases would have been the wrong substitute anyway. DST is hardcoded to the already-chosen fold rather than represented as alternative intervals and then filtered. Causal truncation for T-407 is not in the code. The “comparison” is a comment about types, not a side-by-side of diagnostics. Isolated version-skew handling does not excuse omitting the profile.

### Net
Sections 1–3 are a competent interval analysis with one unused official class and a few sloppy numbers. Sections 4 and 6 are incomplete relative to their own specs. Section 5 fails a mandatory deliverable. That is well above a shallow midpoint write-up and well short of near-flawless. **6.1.**
