**7.4**

The answer is substantively strong on the rubric’s main diagnostic targets—April as a schema/pipeline incident, June as mix/routing rather than concept drift, August–September as overlapping process/concept/population change, October as label-maturity confounding—and it does not collapse ten months into one drift label. Under a hypercritical standard it is not nearly flawless. Taxonomy slips, at least one real conceptual error, incomplete falsification language, and a code sketch that would not run as written keep it well below the top of the scale.

## What is actually good

The six-section contract is followed. The table, timeline, tests, staged remediation, alert gating, and failure-mode section all exist and are mostly aligned with the evidence.

The April reading is the right one: mature labels, no documented outcome-definition change, 17% `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, 14% missing queue, 18% unmapped variants, mean risk 0.28 against an observed 0.19, AUROC 0.67 / ECE 0.110. Treating that as prefix construction failure rather than concept drift is correct. May is properly a recovery/validation month, not a clean baseline.

June is handled well. V2 rising to 52%, overall breach falling to 0.15 while mean prediction stays ~0.20, V2 mature breach at 0.09, and a stable diagnosis→`Remote Fix` edge are used to argue mixture/calibration shift without a within-path concept change. That is the distinction the prompt is testing.

August–September are not flattened into one mechanism. Parts-edge medians (65h → 130h → 148h), V3 mature rates (0.46 / 0.64 / 0.61), Partner Z share and V3 concentration, and the 0.45 vs 0.27 source gap are kept separate from one another, and the source gap is correctly treated as confounded. October’s 0.16 is not treated as a comparable breach rate. Alert dependencies that stop a maturity alarm from auto-retraining are the right architecture.

Section 4’s training-window rules are mostly proportionate: quarantine April, treat October as unsafe, do not fit calibration on a 55%-mature cohort, and do not add realized `Order Part → Part Received` duration as a score-time feature.

## Material problems

**1. April is listed under process drift.**  
The process-drift row says “April as a data representation incident; June–September as real operational drift.” Data representation is not process drift. The contradicting-evidence cell walks it back, but the months column still implicates April. That is a taxonomy error in the one place the answer is supposed to be crisp.

**2. Omitted variables are mislabeled as covariate drift.**  
Rural distance and device age “were not present in the original model.” A change in features the model does not use is not covariate drift of \(P(X)\). It is population shift that can induce apparent concept drift in the deployed feature space. The table puts those variables in the covariate-drift row and then also in population shift, so the two rows are not conceptually separated even though the prompt asked for that separation. June is likewise dumped into covariate, process, *and* population without a primary assignment. Overlap is allowed; this is mush, not disciplined overlap.

**3. Concept drift is under-specified from the model’s point of view.**  
The V3 mature rate moving 0.30 → 0.46 → 0.64 while mean predicted risk stays ~0.19 is, *conditional on the original diagnosis-time feature set*, strong evidence that \(P(Y\mid X_{\text{model}})\) changed. Calling this only “medium / low-to-medium” and repeatedly folding it back into “also process drift” blurs a distinction the tests later rely on. Process change in the world and concept change for the scorer can be simultaneously true; the answer never states that cleanly. It also never notes that September’s matured V3 rate (0.61) is slightly *below* August (0.64), which weakens any story of a still-worsening conditional relationship.

**4. Falsifiers are implied, not stated.**  
The prompt required distinguishing observed evidence from hypotheses *and stating what would falsify each explanation*. Section 1 has “contradicting or insufficient evidence,” which is not the same thing. Section 3 does the real falsification work via competing expected results, but the required table never says, for example, “April-as-concept-drift is falsified if replay restores AUROC/ECE on mature labels,” or “Partner-Z concept drift is falsified if transport closes the gap.” That is a direct miss of a stated requirement.

**5. October’s direction is left soft.**  
“Partial ascertainment” is right; the mechanistic implication is not spelled out. Backlog that hits long paths should *depress* the observed rate and can make a still-high-risk cohort look like 0.16 against a 0.23 mean score. The answer says not to compare October with mature months, but it does not say the 0.16 is biased *downward* and therefore cannot be read as improvement or as overprediction.

## Section-by-section defects

**Table.** Immediate actions are generally sensible, but concept-drift’s action (“recalibrate only after clean labels; retrain only if the conditional relationship remains changed”) already contradicts Section 4’s later claim that recalibration will not fix material AUROC loss. Confidence labels are hedged so hard they become hard to use. “High for June–September process change; medium for its complete contribution” is two different claims jammed into one cell.

**Timeline.** January–March as reference regime is right. The July mid-month caveat (supplier from 10 July) is a genuine plus. The narrative is still slightly too tidy about May: residual 2% unknown / 3% missing queue and AUROC 0.78 could be leftover pipeline, leftover mix, or both; the answer leans “residual contamination” without a test that would separate those. September is called a “joint process, population, covariate, and possible concept-drift month,” which restates the pile-up instead of ranking what the *mature V3* numbers can already support without Partner Z.

**Tests.** The required battery is present, and most items have two competing expected results. That is the strongest section. Remaining issues: inverse-probability-of-observation weights are proposed without saying that the observation model must be conditioned on path/source (the prompt already says missingness is path-dependent; a misspecified IPW will manufacture “concept drift”). The conditional-relationship test allows “known-at-score-time route/part indicators” but does not define how much of the July–August supplier state is actually known at `Initial Diagnosis Completed`. Date-discontinuity checks are good; they should have been used to *demote* month-level averages in July rather than only mentioned.

**Recalibration vs retraining.** Containment and the unsafe-month list are sound. “June … can be used for … training” is too loose: June is a different *routing* regime with a *lower* base rate; using it as a positive training slice without explicit variant/regime indicators is how a model learns the policy mix. “Matured, explicitly selected August–September cases can be used in a weighted, time-aware analysis” is correct but underspecified (selected how? on V3 only? on age ≥ \(k\)?). The default of one shared model plus variant calibration is justified; saying fully separate V2/V3 models need “sufficient mature volume” never checks whether August–September V3 volume actually meets that bar.

**Monitoring + code.** Architecture and gate order are good. The code is not.

- `pm4py.discover_dfg` and `pm4py.discover_performance_dfg` return `(dfg, start_activities, end_activities)` in current PM4Py. `_edges(dfg)` will raise `AttributeError` on a tuple. A sketch that cannot execute is not a small cosmetic issue in a required code block.
- Performance-DFG values are typically mean/std structures, not the *median* the case is built around, and the sketch never pulls `Order Part → Part Received`. Dumping the whole graph misses the one edge the audit must track.
- `feature_missingness` is `log.isna().mean()` inside the month loop—the full log, every month. That is the opposite of a monthly lineage audit.
- `get_variants(...)` return types vary (list of cases vs counts); `len(v)` is not robust.
- Case-start cohorts in September–October are right-censored; the code still emits DFGs/variants/temporal features with no incomplete-trace flag. The prose knows October is immature; the code does not.
- Normalization + exact-duplicate drop + ambiguous-key flag is the one part that matches the pipeline story.

**Risks.** The four required failure modes are covered and the extras (June-as-concept, leakage of post-diagnosis duration, global threshold) are relevant. Mitigations are generic in places (“explicit fairness metrics within relevant strata”) and do not specify a decision rule. The Partner Z item correctly refuses an unstratified unfairness claim; it still does not say what *would* justify a source-specific intervention.

## Net

This is a competent, well-structured triangulation that would pass a normal rubric for “strong.” It is not a 9–10 under the stated standard. The April/June/August–September/October plot is right, but the drift table contaminates process drift with a pipeline incident, misclassifies omitted Partner Z covariates as covariate drift, never writes explicit falsifiers in the required table, leaves October’s bias direction underspecified, and ships a PM4Py sketch that would crash and that computes the same global missingness every month. Those are not polish nits on an otherwise flawless audit; they are exactly the class of unclarity the scoring instruction says should move the grade down substantially.
