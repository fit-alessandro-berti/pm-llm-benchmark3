**6.5**

The write-up is a competent, correctly structured audit that catches the obvious full-trace leaks and keeps the legitimate congestion/prefix-age signals. It is not close to flawless. Several required items are missing, one highlighted distinction is only half-done, and the revised cases still treat contaminated SHAP and leaked probabilities as if they were still usable.

**Section 1 — incomplete coverage and blurred process semantics**

The table gets the big leaks right: `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_handler_team`, the SQL cutoff on `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and the 23:00 `same_day_document_count_snapshot`. It also correctly keeps `queue_length_at_t0`, `initial_damage_complexity`, and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`. That is the core of a good audit.

It fails a hard instruction: cover every local-only feature for X, Y, and Z. `claimant_channel` (Case X) and `initial_reserve` (Case Y) never appear in the table. Those are not optional extras.

The quality criteria explicitly require treating `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` differently from `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`. The finding-type labels differ (`Temporal leakage` vs `Process impossibility`), which is a start, but the prose still collapses both into “impossible at t0.” That is the wrong distinction. Appeal → later settlement is **normatively legal** on a complete trace and almost tautological with breach; it is future information, not an illegal order. Closed → DA is **not a legal order at all** and is the same-timestamp / missing-sequence-index artifact. A strong audit would say: one is leakage of a valid later path; the other is process-inconsistent even as a full-trace feature and should not be mined.

Several other patterns are over-called as impossible rather than **future-possible**:
- `Documents Requested -> Documents Received` is the ordinary evidence cycle after coverage check. It cannot be in this prefix; it is not process-illegal.
- `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` is allowed (fraud may run in parallel after a trigger). “Fraud at t0 is impossible” is false. What is invalid is treating an unobserved later opening as if it were already in the prefix.
- `Damage Assessment Completed -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is the normal later path (and the negative sign is consistent with a completed happy path). Unavailable at t0, not impossible.

`final_handler_team` is under-analyzed. The 65% match to the t0 team is the dangerous part: the feature looks “almost available,” while the 35% of reassignments are systematically the long/hard cases. That is label-correlated mutation, not a generic warehouse inconvenience. Severity “High” is acceptable; the mechanism is incomplete.

**Section 2 — adequate suite, sloppy enabled-set language**

The six checks map to the prompt (prefix availability, order feasibility, same-timestamp index, complete-trace flags, enabled vs future-possible vs impossible, repeated settlement after appeal). The DFG caveat is correct: a frequent edge is not a normative rule.

The enabled-set paragraph again flattens “not yet occurred” into “impossible,” and it is slightly too absolute on fraud. Alignment/declarative checks are named more than specified (what constraint set, what happens on a same-timestamp pair with no sequence index, how a sequence explainer trained on full traces is blocked). Usable, not rigorous.

**Section 3 — right taxonomy, weak standards**

The five classes are present and mostly well populated. Not deleting `claimant_region` / `calendar_week` is correct. The prompt also asked for the **different standards** — predictive validity vs causal interpretation vs operational actionability vs fairness review — as standards, not as one-line “claims.” Queue length is the one clearly actionable congestion signal; complexity is predictive but already frozen; region is a bundled proxy (network, language, channel, SES) that can stay in a scorer only with fairness/robustness review. That hierarchy is only sketched.

`claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` are again unclassified. Reserve has no lineage note and can be revised after t0; putting it in “defensible” later without a repair caveat is inconsistent.

**Section 4 — structure is valid; substance is not fully credible**

JSON shape matches the spec. Leaked full paths are not copied into the narratives. Case Z is correctly sent to invalidate/recompute, and the same-timestamp / mutable score / last-role issues are listed.

Defects:
- Case X/Y still narrate **0.82 and 0.71** after `prediction_status: recompute`. Those scores were driven by leaked features (`appeal` +0.26; `full_trace_variant_id` +0.31). You cannot keep the leaked-model probability.
- Remaining SHAP values are treated as reusable. They are not. Attributions from a model that included full-trace features are contaminated; signs may be hypothesized, magnitudes must be recomputed on a prefix-only model.
- `claimant_channel` disappears from Case X. It is neither a credible driver nor a removed driver.
- Case Y asserts `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` “recomputed with fixed SQL.” That value is unknown until the cutoff is actually `< t0`. Listing the same leaked count as a repaired driver is fabricated certainty.
- `initial_reserve` is used as if lineage-clean.
- Case Z omits week 52 as a surviving calendar/holiday proxy (the one local signal that is actually known at t0).

**Section 5 — complete checklist, generic execution**

All eight requested themes appear in priority order. Missing the sharper points that separate a process-aware plan from a generic ML feature-store memo: prefix-only training of sequence explainers (not just prefix recomputation of tabular features); mandatory sequence index before DFG/pattern mining; point-in-time joins for handler/risk/experience; and a rule that explanations from the leaked model must not be served even for the “valid” subset of features.

**Net:** a solid pass on the obvious leaks and on preserving queue/elapsed/complexity, with a real miss on required local features, an incomplete Appeal-vs-Closed distinction, several future-possible patterns mislabeled as impossible, and revised cases that still lean on leaked scores, unrecomputed SHAP, and an invented repaired count. That is clearly above a weak accept-the-SHAP answer and clearly below a near-flawless audit.
