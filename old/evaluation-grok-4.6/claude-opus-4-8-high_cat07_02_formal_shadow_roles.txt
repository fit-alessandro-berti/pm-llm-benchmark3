This is a strong, complete answer that hits every required heading, respects most of the stated constraints, and is unusually careful about automation, temps, and control-gate activities. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has a real evidence error, a self-contradiction in the proposed structure, and several methodological slips in the code.

**Factual distortion of the week-5 evidence.** The log says Mateo performed 21 preliminary A9 events and that 13 were later *rechecked* by Hana. Section 1 and 2 quote this correctly. Sections 3 and 4 upgrade it to *overturned* / *approval overturns* / “Hana over Mateo.” Recheck is dual control, audit, or confirmation; overturn is a competence and authority finding. That substitution is then used as the main proof that Mateo has no decision rights and that interpretation B (junior supervisor) is weak. The conclusion may still be right; the evidential step is not. This is the single most serious flaw, because the task is to infer authority from the log without over-reading it.

**The proposed structure does the merge it warns against.** Chen and Dina are the benchmark’s clearest “similar profile ≠ same role” pair (0.94 cosine; medians 0.71 vs 0.48; 42 vs 9 critical; Chen’s A6 = 24 vs Dina’s 6). Section 4 later offers the seniority split as an alternative, which makes section 1 look like the naïve clustering the prompt told the author not to submit.

**Mateo’s activity ownership is overstated as empty.** “A-none-owned” is false on the matrix: he leads A13 (42 vs Hana’s 24), is second on A9 (44), and has the widest spread. The bridging story is right; the claim that he owns nothing is a convenient simplification that hides reopen coordination as a real core activity.

**Other evidence nits.** Chen’s 24 security reviews are ~20% of A6, not “rare” or merely “adjacent.” Kai/Lina have 2 and 4 high-complexity cases, not “very-low only.” Fatima’s absence coinciding with 38→74 minute pricing duration is treated in §2 as confirmation she is the competence center, which is the causal overread the constraints forbid; §3 then correctly refuses to treat substitution as competence. Treating `discover_organizational_roles` as an activity grouping rather than an org chart is exactly the right warning. The answer reads like an 8–9 that repeatedly spends its own best insights and then slightly misstates the log. Strictly, that is a mid-6.
