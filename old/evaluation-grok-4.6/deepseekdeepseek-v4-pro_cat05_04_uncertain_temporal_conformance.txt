**6.3**

The final write-up follows the required headings, does real interval arithmetic instead of silent midpoints, and gets most SLA/profile classifications right. That is not enough for a high score under a strict rubric: the answer still has specification misses, a few methodological slippages, and a Section 5 that does not do what was asked.

### What is actually solid
Clock-offset direction, resolution-before-offset, OPS-BER / SENSOR / VENDOR-NY window construction, and the T-405 fold enumeration are correct. Only `A=CEST, S=CET, C=CET` survives ingestion order; collapsing to a single ordered triple is justified. T-404 keeps the 18–25 minute work-record range instead of a point start. T-407 treats `Alert_Sent` as distinct from `Alert_Received`. T-408 S2/S3 certain violations are numerically right (~55 min and ~38h25m). Sensitivity catches real knife-edges (T-404 S1 at +2 min still possible because 1023 > 1020; T-408 S2 still certain at +5 min).

### Material defects
**Section 5 fails the spec.** The code hardcodes already-normalized UTC intervals. It does not implement timezone conversion, floor/round semantics, or DST alternatives with pandas/`zoneinfo`. `pytz` is imported and unused. `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` is run against a profile *discovered from these nine midpoint traces*, not the supplied `(600,180)`, `(2100,600)`, `(2700,720)`, `(86400,21600)` profile. That makes the PM4Py comparison the wrong experiment. There is no table comparing midpoint labels to interval labels (the exact place midpoints would falsely pass T-401 S1 or T-404 S2). A try/except plus a disclaimer is not a comparison.

**T-409 mixes inference into the evidence table.** The source window is `[15:52:00, 16:17:00]`. The causal clip to `[15:59:58.5, 16:17:00]` belongs in duration reasoning, not as if the clock produced it. Classifications happen not to flip, but the interval claim is overstated.

**T-407 is over-certain.** Receipt is unacknowledged and the “received ≤ start” rule is explicitly unlogged. Bounding R and calling S1 a `POSSIBLE_VIOLATION` is one defensible semantics; `INDETERMINATE` is the other, and the write-up does not treat that fork as first-class despite the problem’s warning about weak evidence.

**T-406 entry delay is one-sided without a rival case.** Shifting occurrence earlier by `[0,5]` min is stated, but the equally reasonable reading (user typed the observed event time; delay is only bookkeeping) is not carried through sensitivity. That choice drives how wide S1/S2 are.

### Smaller issues that still cost points
Section 1 writes `true time ∈ recorded_clock – b, recorded_clock – a` without brackets. HUB `±0.5s` plus `±1s` is applied as a linear sum with no note that those layers might overlap. T-405 endpoints are closed as if the floor window were `[T,T+60]` rather than `[T,T+60)`. Severity/confidence are impressionistic. Several Section 2 intervals are written as loose “A – B” strings rather than explicit unions/intervals.

Net: the interval analysis is competent and mostly accurate, but the answer is not nearly flawless. The required executable normalization/DST/PM4Py pipeline is a stub, and a few weak-evidence cases are presented as more settled than they are.
