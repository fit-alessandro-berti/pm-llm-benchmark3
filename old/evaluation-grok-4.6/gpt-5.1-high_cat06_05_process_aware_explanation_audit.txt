**7.3**

This is a strong, well-structured audit that hits the required five-section form, covers all 22 mandated items, preserves the legitimate `t0` signals, and generally separates leakage, process impossibility, lineage bugs, proxies, and causality. It is not nearly flawless. Several process-aware mistakes and one internal contradiction keep it well below a high score under strict grading.

**What it gets right.**  
Obvious complete-trace leakage is identified at critical severity (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `num_document_requests_total`, `final_decision`, last-role / current risk score, all five sequence patterns). Subtler lineage defects are also caught: the `<= extraction_date` SQL, the 23:00 same-day snapshot, closure-populated `final_handler_team` with the 65/35 split, ±5 minute queue join, and missing sequence index on tied timestamps. `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are correctly kept. `claimant_region` and `claimant_channel` are not deleted; they are retained with fairness constraints. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is typed as temporally leaked but process-legal, while `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is typed as order-infeasible plus leakage. Section 2 states that a frequent DFG edge is not a normative rule. Section 3’s five-way classification and Section 5’s checklist match the spec. Cases X and Y do not replay leaked outcomes in the narratives.

**Material flaws.**

1. **Case Y contradicts itself.** `prior_claim_count_before_t0 = 3` is listed as a credible driver and is stated as fact in the narrative, while the same object is listed as unrepaired because the SQL can count claims created after `t0`. Until recomputation, the value 3 is not an admissible fact. A repaired-history feature may survive; this explanation treats the contaminated value as known.

2. **Missed degeneracy at this `t0`.** Immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`, the observed prefix is essentially `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed` for every case. `prefix_variant_id_at_t0` and `num_document_requests_up_to_t0` are therefore near-constants, not drop-in replacements. Recommending them without saying they carry no discrimination at this prediction point is a process-mining miss, not a wording nit.

3. **Case Z is only half-audited.** `calendar_week = 52` is temporally valid (holiday/policy proxy) and is the one listed driver that could be salvaged; it is omitted. `same_day_document_count_snapshot = 6` is a documented partial leak and is also omitted from the ambiguity list. The note also never says the **0.64 probability itself** is unusable, only that the explanation is.

4. **Causal language leaks back in.** Calling appeals the result of a “typically delayed or contentious” first decision imports the team’s causal story. The pattern is inadmissible because it is future information, not because appeals “mean” delay. Region’s local interpretation (“likely due to repair-network coverage, language or local processes”) similarly over-explains a correlation the rest of the answer correctly refuses to causalize.

5. **Enabled vs occurred is blurred.** Damage assessment and document requests are *enabled* next steps at `t0`, not in the same bucket as appeal/closure, which are not yet enabled. Grouping “appeals, final decisions, closure, damage assessment” as activities explanations must not refer to is too coarse: they must not be treated as *observed*, but they can be discussed as possible futures.

**Smaller issues that still cost.**  
`initial_reserve` has no lineage note; reserves are often revised, and the JSON treats it as a known `t0` fact. “Appeal possible by policy” at coverage time is nearly vacuous (almost every claim can later be denied/partial). Elapsed time is grouped with congestion as “actionable”; queue length is a lever, slack already consumed is not. Employee-experience as-of-extraction, though not in the top-12, is an offered lineage warning that is ignored. Prefix variants are awkwardly filed under drift/calendar.

**Section quality.**  
§1 is the strongest piece (complete, mostly well-severitied). §2 is credible as a validation suite. §3 is conceptually right but double-lists features without always saying which *implementation* is meant. §4 is the weakest: X is good; Y is internally inconsistent; Z is incomplete. §5 is a competent MLOps list, not a distinguished one.

The answer would be in the high 8s if Case Y did not assert an unrepaired count, if prefix repair had noted zero variance at this `t0`, and if Case Z had kept week 52 and dropped the snapshot. Under the requested standard those are not cosmetic.
