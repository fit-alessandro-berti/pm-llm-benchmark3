## The algorithm does not implement the policy it claims

Section 2 lists the right ingredient names, then encodes them incorrectly.

- Skill is treated as a **single scalar** (`resource.required_skill`) instead of a per-activity matrix lookup. The Security filter does not implement “skill 1–2 only under Gita/Hugo”.
- SLA term is `urgency * sla / sla`, i.e. a constant 1 for urgent/high. Tighter deadlines are not prioritized.
- `skill_match = resource_skill - case["required_skill"]` subtracts a **string** from a number. The comment then treats a negative result as “too high”; the inequality is backwards.
- `resource["overtime_risk"]` is an undefined name inside a function that uses `candidate_resource`. The overtime predicate is a tangle of double negatives.
- The “max 4” rule compares **current open cases** to `batch_size=4`. Almost everyone already has more than 4 open cases, so the stated cap is not the cap in the prompt (≤4 **new** assignments in this batch).
- Cases are taken in table order with no urgency sort, so routine work can consume people needed for C001/C002/C011.
- Supervision pairs, Quality batching (rule 8), hours-remaining feasibility, and bottleneck/resilience scoring are advertised and then mostly absent.
- Tie-break “lower remaining hours” is described and not coded.

A policy that cannot be executed, and that is inconsistent with its own assignment table, is not auditable.

## Weaker but still material issues

**Objectives.** Multi-objective framing is the right idea, and hard vs soft is attempted. “Aim for 2 reassignments” points the wrong way (history says **&gt;2** is harmful). Safety/compliance (Security, Approval, no high-priority trainee without a supervisor) is mixed with preferences (20% training) less cleanly than the prompt asked.

**Baselines.** The comparison axes are the ones requested, and the answer correctly refuses a single optimum. The numbers (“~90% of first-available”, “cut error rate 30%”, “2 resources per skill”) are invented. Neither baseline is actually run on C001–C016, so the trade-off discussion is generic.

**Worker explanation.** Tone is the strongest part: not punitive, continuity and fairness are humanized. It still oversells a score engine and asserts overtime/training behavior the table does not follow. Socio-technical content is present mainly as prose, not as a designed exception/appeal path.

**Audit indicators.** The **names** match the required list. The **computations** do not: `filter_event_attribute_values` is not a handover metric; `get_workload_snapshot`, `compute_handover_frequency`, `get_case_metric`, and `compute_skill_mismatch` are not the listed PM4Py tools; `filter_case_performance(..., 'sla_missed')` is not how that API works; training priority is a resource attribute, not a case filter. Handover-of-work, working-together, activity-based similarity, batches, performance DFG, and variants are barely or never used.

**PM4Py sketch.** `read_dataframe`, `format_dataframe(..., nominal_attributes=...)`, `save_dataframe`, and most “audit” helpers are fabricated or mis-specified. `view_sna` is aimed at organizational roles rather than SNA from handover/working-together/similarity. The sketch does not show learning thresholds from the log (service/wait times, rework after &gt;2 handovers, specialist queues). It therefore misses the prompt’s explicit function list.

## What keeps it above a collapse score

Headings are complete. The problem is treated as multi-objective rather than throughput-only. A few destinations happen to be reasonable (C004 owner stay, C007 stay, C011 Farid, C016 Eva stay). Section 5 would be usable as a communication draft if the underlying assignments were fixed.

That is not enough. A policy that assigns Security to skill 0, high-priority Negotiation to skill 1 without a supervisor, and urgent Approval away from an available specialist owner is not deployable, not explainable in good faith, and not a near-miss. The polish of the write-up conceals a broken model of the data.
