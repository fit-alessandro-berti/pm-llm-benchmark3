**6.4**

The answer is well-structured, hits every required heading, and correctly avoids the classic failure modes (I-03 by \(p_{now}\), I-05 treated like I-07, ranking only on probability, treating \(\Delta p\) as a known causal effect). That is necessary, not sufficient. Under a strict decision-theoretic and operational standard it has several material coherence failures, not just nits.

**Decision rule vs. own allocation.** Gate 5 requires robust direct value to exceed labor, false-alarm burden, *and* allocated spillover. The stated charge is \(6 \times 0.02 \times \bar V\). With any plausible untreated-queue value (~€12–25k) that is ~€1.4–3k per slot. I-07 fails this test across its entire effect range; I-01 fails except near the top of its range. Both are then labeled Tier A and deployed. Calling spillover a “guardrail not an invoice” does not repair a rule that was written as an eligibility constraint and then ignored. The robust-priority formula is advertised and never computed, so the tier table is not auditable against the rule that supposedly produced it.

**I-07 is over-selected.** Own timing rule: 1.5h cases need an explicit “can the swarm still change diagnosis in time?” check. That check is not passed; it is waved. The case is still in Triage, not Diagnosis Started; a 45-minute swarm with 90 minutes left leaves almost no path through diagnosis *and* resolution. Direct EV is modest (~€0.5–1.1k before spillover). Section 4 correctly says review the new log and deploy *if* it is actionable; Section 2 just says Intervene. That is an internal contradiction, not a nuance.

**Capacity is physically mis-specified.** Two specialists deliver *the* swarm. Slots are sequential over the day, not three parallel 12:00 deployments. There is no sequencing, no statement that I-07 must go first or not at all, and no recognition that starting I-01 or I-04 first expires I-07’s window. “Deploy now” on three cases at noon is not an implementable instruction.

**Early-stage “now” vs. wait is under-argued.** I-01 (7h, Triage) and I-04 (10h, Triage) consume scarce specialist time while diagnosis has not started. I-04 also carries a €2k disruption cost and Strategic over-prediction. A coherent policy would default those to “hold until Diagnosis Started or a concrete bottleneck is named,” especially while a reserve is being kept for 15:00. The answer notes the uncertainty and deploys anyway.

**Calibration is conflated with treatment effect.** Segment bias is about \(p_{now}\), not about the supplied \(\Delta p\) ranges. The Base impact row discounts I-04’s *effect* because Strategic risk is over-predicted. That is a category error unless an explicit contamination argument is made; it is not. SME/Strategic adjustments are otherwise directionally correct.

**I-09 vs. stated fairness language.** I-09 is at the effective stage, has 2.5h, and has a strong nominal EV (~€2.4–4.8k). The text says not to treat NNLS uncertainty as low value, then ranks it behind I-07. Conservative exposure limits are defensible; pairing them with an immediate low-EV, tight-timing SME swarm is not.

**I-05 suppression is too blunt.** Repeat *swarms* without a new event should be forbidden. I-05 has never received a swarm; it has only been re-scored. A refresh is not new evidence, but a never-reviewed live case should remain in the eligible queue, not be sent to Tier D. The answer keeps a monitoring record and still chooses “do not intervene,” which can starve a stable high-risk SME case for the rest of the day.

**Impact table is only partly honest.** Arithmetic on the raw \(\Delta p \times V\) endpoints is correct. Folding the full false-alarm burden into “intervention cost” in every scenario (including Optimistic) blurs a cost that should scale with unnecessary interruption. The invented untreated \(\bar V\) of €12–25k ignores the three *known* diagnosis-stage values (I-02/I-06/I-09 = €25k/€15k/€20k). Base net is described as “break-even to €6k” while the same ranges allow a negative lower bound. Adverse spillover (€9–18k) is a wide gesture, not a worked mapping from 15–30 minutes of delay. Confidence labels are reasonable; the Base narrative is slightly salesy.

**Reserve option value is not analyzed.** Management preference is correctly labeled non-binding, then treated as binding until 15:00. There is no \(P(\text{regulatory arrival}) \times\) value-of-a-free-slot versus I-09/validated I-06. Replacement order (I-06 → I-09 → I-02 → I-08) is sensible as a qualitative list.

**What is actually good.** Hard exclusion of I-03; I-08’s negative lower-bound EV and high disruption cost correctly block automatic selection; I-06 held for imputation validation; observational \(p\) distinguished from treated risk; I-05 vs I-07 comparison on *information change* rather than score direction; suppression ≠ “nobody owns the case”; escalation off the swarm path for late-stage work; treatment-label leakage, segment calibration, NNLS under-service, and spillover incidence on untreated groups all get concrete controls (logged exposure set, congestion stop rule, separate untreated-risk model, near-tie encouragement). Monitoring is the strongest section.

**Net.** This is a competent policy memo that would look strong in a loose rubric and does not survive a coherence audit. The governing rule is not the rule that selects the portfolio; the 12:00 plan is not feasible with two specialists; I-07 is promoted past the author’s own tests. Those are not presentation issues. A near-flawless answer would compute the robust score for all ten cases, sequence the two specialists, apply spillover at portfolio level without violating the written gate, keep I-04/I-01 on a short leash until diagnosis is actually the bottleneck, and put I-07 in “immediate review, swarm only if the log is usable *and* the slot can start now.” This answer gestures at all of that and then does something else.
