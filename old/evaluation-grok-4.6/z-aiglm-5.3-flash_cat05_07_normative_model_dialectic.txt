**6.5**

The dialectical core (sections 1–5) is strong: both briefs argue at full strength, concessions are real rather than cosmetic, the case table is complete, and the judgment does not split the difference (704 vs 705, 702 vs 714). The required executable model, however, is broken, and it misses an explicit minimum.

**What holds up**

Section 1 separates stipulated facts, disputed authority/scope questions, and unproved claims, and the burden rules are usable: scope ambiguity does not convict; exception proponents must show instrument, window, scope, and safeguards; mitigations cut severity, not authorization. That is the right frame for this file.

The prosecution brief is the strongest available CS-9 case: PD-3 is not a controlled amendment, FD-7’s sunset is evidence that expiry was meant to bind, M0-fitting traces still hide SoD/COI (712, 715), and 703/708 are correctly treated as core failures. The 14 ineligible auto-approvals and the August cluster are used as drift evidence, not anecdotes.

The defense brief is equally specific: M0 forces quality onto reject paths, cannot express the 47 Request_More_Info cases, treats CRM drop-out as process failure, and cannot represent PD-3/FD-7 as first-class routes. Concessions on 703, 708, and 714 prevent the brief from collapsing into “obsolescence = license.”

Case calls are mostly right and motivated:
- 701 compliant; 703/708 critical noncompliant
- 704 justified under live FD-7; 705 noncompliant after automatic expiry
- 707/709/710/713 as model or scope defects
- 706/711 as data issues
- 712/715 as non-control-flow breaches inside M0-fitting traces
- 714 noncompliant despite objective eligibility

Rejecting wholesale PD-3 nullification for in-window eligible work, while still refusing post-expiry self-help, is a reasoned authority position, not a mushy average.

Layered architecture (POWL vs DECLARE vs pandas vs instrument registry) matches what the sources actually need: amount/date/identity/authority cannot live in POWL.

**Defects that cap the score**

1. **The POWL is not executable as written.** In `route_standard` and `route_auto`, `t("Quality_Review")` is constructed again in `add_edge`. PM4Py `Transition` identity is object identity, not label. Those edges do not attach to the nodes in the partial order and will raise or silently omit the quality→activate / activate→quality constraints. The emergency route binds variables first and is fine; the two main productive routes are not. That is a hard miss on “one executable Python code block.”

2. **Required concurrency is dropped.** The task requires concurrent sanctions and quality on the standard route. M1 completes `screening` (sanctions ∥ info-loop) *before* any quality. A CS-9-valid order `Quality_Review` then `Sanctions_Check` then manual approval—legal under M0 and CS-9—is not in the language of M1. Sequencing is a design choice, not an acknowledged approximation of concurrency.

3. **Log labels vs model labels.** Observed `Auto_Approve` and emergency `Activate_Supplier` become `Auto_Approve_LowRisk` and `Provisional_Activate_Emergency`. A vocabulary layer is mentioned, but no mapping is given in the code/overlay, so replay against the given traces is undefined.

4. **Overlay predicates are unreadable.** Logical connectives were stripped (`quality_done  manual_done`, `risk=LOW  country=DE`, `resolve(approver)  resolve(requester)`). The intended AND/≠ constraints are reconstructible from context, but a normative rule table that cannot be parsed is a real defect in the revision the judge was told to score.

5. **Small analytical slips.** Calling 312 auto-approvals the “most common route” is unsupported (no denominator). Classifying 702 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION at “high” confidence sits uneasily next to listing PD-3 authority as disputed, even though the later “reject the prosecution” paragraph explains the choice. 711 is not really *indeterminate* once the shared transaction ID is stipulated; it is a resolved duplicate.

**Net**

Sections 1–5 would support a high score on fairness, traceability, and authority. Section 7 is a required artifact, and it fails both executability and a stated control-flow minimum. Under a strict reading, that combination cannot be “nearly flawless.”
