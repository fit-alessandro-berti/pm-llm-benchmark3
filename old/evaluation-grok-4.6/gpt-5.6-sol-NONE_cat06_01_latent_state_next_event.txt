**7.1**

The answer is process-aware and clearly above a DFG-copying baseline, but it is not close to flawless. Several probability assignments fight the evidence the author themselves cites, a few state/enablement labels are internally inconsistent, and the “next logged event” target is repeatedly stretched into a near-term path forecast.

**What is actually strong.** Control-flow obligations are applied instead of raw frequencies: Fast Close is correctly withheld from C6-B after an evidence cycle; C6-A/C6-D get dual investigation from severity/injury; C6-C treats repeated malfunction as a *possible* reportability signal, not a proven one; C6-E invokes the lot-cluster supplier rule without inventing a shared component. Missing events are not treated as proof of non-occurrence, especially in C6-D. The A vs B prefix contrast is the right contrast. DFG limitations are stated accurately (no denominators, no conditioning, poor concurrency semantics). Interval *widths* are not fake-precise.

**Next-event semantics are sloppy.** The task asks for the next logged activity, not the next important obligation. For C6-A/B/E the visible prefix ends at Evidence Received, the feed is normal, and Evidence Review is the enabled completion. Ranks 2–3 are mostly *later* activities (RA/TI, Close — No Safety Issue, a further evidence request, Supplier Analysis Completed) given 8–28% mass. That is only coherent if review already finished and was omitted. A normal feed does not support that. The author’s own caveat — hidden completions are weaker when the feed flag is normal — is then ignored in the numbers. Under a sequential reading, P(next = Evidence Review Completed) should dominate far more, and the skip-ahead alternatives should be thin tails, not a second tier.

**C6-A is the clearest calibration miss.** Combined 0.08–0.24 and 0.07–0.21 that RA or TI appears *before* any review completion implies a large hidden-S4 probability on a normal feed. That is not disciplined. A further evidence request after an incomplete package is at least as plausible a *later* branch as an investigation completion leaping the log, but it is omitted from the top three.

**C6-C and C6-D are over-cloned.** RA 0.48–0.70 vs 0.48–0.69 and Disposition 0.16–0.34 vs 0.16–0.34 treat two different observation models as interchangeable. C6-D has a 12:40 worklist open *and* a migration flag; C6-C has neither, plus a normal feed. Those should move both the RA interval and the disposition-as-next interval. Worse: RA duration is 2–14 h after assignment. If 12:40 was assignment, then at 14:00 the case is still below the stated minimum service time, which undercuts alternative S5 and a 16–34% chance that Disposition is the next *observed* event. The author lists the “opened vs assigned” ambiguity but does not let it widen or shift the numbers.

**Enablement and state labels leak.** C6-E’s primary state is S3, yet Supplier Analysis Completed is `process_enabled: true`. C6-D marks Evidence Review enabled despite Rule 3’s Requested → Received → Review chain never appearing; an attachment in the source system is not the same as an enabled review. C6-B’s “alternative” is still S3 with extra commentary, not a second state. C6-D puts “possible evidence handling not reliably represented” in `completed_obligations`, which mixes a guess into a completed set.

**C6-E rank 2 is poorly timed.** Supplier Analysis is the right *obligation*, not a credible *next completion*. Typical 6–36 h with three waiting, last evidence at 13:20, prediction instant 14:00. Completing next is possible only if SA has already been running for many hours. That story is never evidenced; 0.11–0.28 is generous. Ranking it over a third evidence request is defensible only via the already-open-SA story, which again fights the primary S3 label.

**Shared resources are ignored.** C6-A (12:10), C6-B (12:16), and C6-E (13:20) all sit on the same 4-waiting / 2-reviewer desk. The snapshot’s four waiting items may *be* these cases. Treating each review delay as an independent 1–5 h draw is a process-operational omission.

**Probability hygiene is only half-done.** Midpoints of the top three sum to ~0.94–1.00 while the calibration note appeals to leftover mass for delayed events, extra evidence cycles, and escalations. Those cannot both be true. Interval endpoints also recur (0.16–0.34, 0.48–0.69) across unlike cases, which looks like template reuse rather than case-specific uncertainty.

**Smaller defects.** No prediction that C6-D’s next *feed* item is a late Evidence Requested/Received (migration delay of already-occurred completions, up to 6 h). Cross-case text never uses the 16:00 batch except as a generic ambiguity. C6-A never asks whether the three similar complaints share the lot *in the ranking*, only in unknowns, while still floating RA/TI as if the investigation fork were already the thing being timed.

The skeleton is expert-shaped. The probabilities and some enablement bits are not. That gap is exactly what this rubric punishes.
