**2.4**

The write-up has the required heading skeleton and a few early fully observed cases (P01–P04, P06, P08) computed correctly, but the analytical core is not a reconstruction of the supplied log. It is a rewritten log, then a set of metrics and a bottleneck story about that fiction.

**Invented observations (disqualifying).** The prompt forbids inventing observations. The answer “corrects” P09, P10, and P13 as data-entry errors and then silently shifts P12, P14–P21 onto the same invented clock (P09 S1 09:10–09:40 instead of 10:05–10:35; P10 09:25–09:55 instead of 10:25–10:55; P13 10:00–10:30 instead of 11:10–11:40). Those I1/I2 sequences in the real table are already feasible: I1 is P01–P03–P05–P07–P09–P11–P13–P15–P17–P19–P21 with ~5-minute handoffs; I2 is equally regular. Nothing in the data supports the rewrite. Once those times move, almost every later wait, WIP path, subperiod rate, and the S1-vs-S2 bottleneck claim is computed on the wrong process.

**Missing-start bounds ignore the stated constraints.** Required logic is resource occupancy, queue entry, completion, and nonnegative service. The answer’s own A4 writes `max(arrival, previous_end)` but the table does not use it.

- P07 on I1: P05 ends 09:25, P07 ends 10:00, P09 starts 10:05. Start ∈ ~[09:25, 10:00], wait ~[40, 75] min, service ~[0, 35] min. Reported: wait [0, 15], service [35, 60] from arrival 08:45.
- P15 on I1: P13 ends 11:40, P15 ends 12:15, P17 starts 12:20. Start ∈ ~[11:40, 12:15], wait ~[90, 125] min. Reported: wait [0, 25], service [35, 75].
- P05 on C1: P03 ends 09:45, P05 ends 10:15, P07 starts 10:20. Start ∈ ~[09:45, 10:15], wait ~[20, 50] min, service ~[0, 30] min. Reported: wait [0, 10] and **service 50 min = 10:15 − 09:25**, i.e. S2 sojourn, not service.
- P11 on C2: P08 occupies C2 11:15–12:00 (after the 10:55–11:10 outage), P11 ends 12:25, P13 starts 12:30. Start ∈ ~[12:00, 12:25], wait ~[55, 80] min. Reported: wait [0, 15], service 55 min (again sojourn), with an inference window of 10:05–11:00 taken from the rewritten S1 end.

That is exactly the failure mode the quality bar names: active service confused with station elapsed time, and missing starts replaced by unconstrained or single-point stories.

**Timestamp semantics are violated.** Blank S2 on P16/P18–P20 is defined as waiting for S2. The table flags them `OPEN_IN_S2`, invents S2 start times, and puts OPEN service on a station that has not started. P21 is given dummy S2 OPEN (14:00–14:00). P22 is given OPEN S1 service even though S1 never started. P16’s real S1 is 12:05–12:35 (wait 100 min); the table uses 10:25–11:25.

**Station counts and subperiods are wrong even as arithmetic on the real log.** S1 completions are 20 (P01–P20), not 18. S2 completions are 14 (P01–P14), not 12. S2 queue arrivals are 20, not 18. In 08:00–10:00, S1 arrivals are 13–14 and S1 completions are 7 (through P07 at 10:00), not 10 and 10. Later hourly WIP in the code is `groupby(s1_start.hour).size()`, which is starts-per-hour, not WIP.

**Bottleneck section is inverted and internally inconsistent.** At 14:00 the real snapshot is: 4 cases waiting for S2 (P16, P18–P20), 2 in S2 (P15, P17), 1 in S1 (P21), 1 waiting for S1 (P22). Downstream congestion is the dominant end-of-window fact. S1 waits do grow through the morning (~5 min → ~100 min) because the two inspectors run ~25–30 min jobs against a faster arrival stream, but S2 waits also grow and the unfinished pile is at S2. The answer claims S2 is the mid-morning bottleneck because of C2, then **S1** is the late bottleneck “as it cannot keep up,” while also saying “S1 feeds cases faster than S2 can process.” Those cannot both be the conclusion.

The C2 outage claim is factually false for P06: P06 is 10:10–10:50 on C2, **before** 10:55–11:10. The 25 min P06 wait is the gap after P04 (ends 10:05), not maintenance. The outage sits between P06 and P08 and is a 15-minute capacity hole in an already loading S2, not the cause of P06’s wait.

**Little’s Law is only gestured at.** A5 restricts the law to intervals with no OPEN/MISSING and constant capacity, then no interval is actually checked with λ, L, and W. The formula text is missing the arrival rate (“WIP * J”). Rounding is stated as ±2.5 min but never propagated on fully observed cases; simultaneous-event WIP convention is “arbitrary order,” which is too weak for a 5-minute grid.

**Section 6 is qualitative cover, not sensitivity.** Optimistic / central / pessimistic are named, but they inherit the rewritten times and the wrong P07/P15/P05/P11 bounds. “OPEN service = 35 / 55 / 75” for P15 S1 is not a resource-feasible set. No scenario table recomputes waits, utilization, or peak queue. Claimed stability of an S2→S1 bottleneck shift is stability of an error.

**Python fails the mandated contract and would not support the narrative.**

1. `parse_time` maps MISSING, OPEN, and blank to the same `None` (explicitly forbidden).
2. `fillna` silently point-imputes missing starts (explicitly forbidden).
3. `(lower + upper) / 2` on `datetime` is a `TypeError`.
4. `pm4py.format_dataframe` is never called.
5. OPEN/blank rows are dropped from the interval log, so open cases never enter busy-time or WIP.
6. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are run on that projection and then averaged by `concept:name` as if they were **station** waits; they are not labeled case-level-only.
7. No comment on why `convert_log_to_time_intervals` is unsafe here; it is called on a raw DataFrame, not a formatted log.
8. Utilization uses only completed intervals over 360 min — exactly “utilization from completed cases alone.”
9. C2 outage is not a constraint in imputation. No three-scenario computation is implemented. Printed output does not reproduce §§3–4.

**What is salvageable:** headings; A2 capacity; the idea of resource-bounded imputation (stated, not used); treating OPEN elapsed time as a lower bound (stated, then applied to the wrong starts); C2’s 15 min outage as a utilization adjustment; P01–P04/P06/P08 arithmetic.

Those do not offset rewriting the event log, mis-bounding every MISSING start, calling S2 sojourn “service,” mis-tagging the S2 queue as in-service, inverting the bottleneck, skipping Little’s Law, and shipping non-compliant code. Under a strict reading this is far from a defensible reconstruction.
