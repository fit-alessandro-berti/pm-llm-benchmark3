**6.0**

The legal and evidentiary half of this review is competent; the process-model half is not. Under a strict reading of the rubric—dialectical fairness, model semantics, evidence traceability, scope/authority, and the quality of the proposed revision—the last two technical deliverables have material errors that a “nearly flawless” answer cannot have.

**What is actually strong.** Sections 1–5 get the authority hierarchy right: CS-9 is the only formally approved standard; PD-3 is an unincorporated, time-boxed steering-committee exception whose own compliance observer later denied having weakened CS-9; FD-7 expires automatically. The burden-of-proof rule (prosecution must prove in-scope deviation without a matching overlay; defense must produce overlay identity, window, and eligibility snapshot) is the correct one, and the write-up does not merely split the difference. Case calls are mostly right: SUP-701/713 compliant, SUP-702/704 justified exceptions, SUP-703/708/714 noncompliant, SUP-707/709/710 model-or-scope defects, SUP-706/711 data-quality indeterminate. The matrix covers all fifteen cases, including resource-rule cases that fit M0’s control flow. Defense is required to concede real risk and does so on SUP-703 and SUP-714; prosecution is required to concede model/data defects and does so on RMI, CRM linkage, rejected-lead scope, and reactivation.

**Where the dialectic is still not tight enough.** The prosecution brief under-specifies M0 deviations on the auto-approve traces: SUP-702, SUP-703, and SUP-714 are not only “manual replaced by Auto_Approve.” They also invert M0’s `checks ≺ decision` order by putting Quality_Review after Activate_Supplier, and SUP-702 additionally lacks Notify in the onboarding log. A process-aware prosecution would have led with that partial-order violation. The defense’s “possible sequence logging error” on SUP-708 is speculation against the stipulated facts (HIGH-risk, no overlay, activation 40 minutes before sanctions, Internal Audit already flagging it). SUP-713 as COMPLIANT with *high* confidence overstates a mapping approved a week after the case; equivalent content makes COMPLIANT defensible, not confident. SUP-712’s matrix still lists “alias mapping at time” as missing evidence even though the case narrative already resolves the two aliases to one human. Section 1’s “2%29%” is a sloppy transcription of the 2%→29% notification gap. PD-3’s required automated master-data validation before activation is never treated as a missing activity in either the briefs or the revision.

**The revised POWL fails explicit minimum requirements.** The prompt required concurrent Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review on the *standard* route. The constructed model sequences `sanctions → rmi_loop → (quality → manual → activate)`. That is not concurrency and is not a documented, defensible approximation of it—section 6 even claims concurrency is “represented per branch where lawful,” which is false for the standard branch.

The LOOP encoding is backwards. In PM4Py/process-tree semantics, `LOOP(Request_More_Info, silent)` executes the do-part first, so Request_More_Info is mandatory. Almost every stipulated trace—including the clean standard path, both auto paths, both emergency paths, and the reject path—has zero RMI events and would not fit `M_rev`. The comment only discusses a non-data-guided exit; it never states that the first iteration is required. Section 6 calls the loop optional; section 7 makes it obligatory. That is an internal contradiction, not a clearly explained approximation.

Reusing the same `quality`, `activate`, and `manual` Transition objects as nodes inside exclusive XOR children is not a valid POWL construction. Exclusive branches must be disjoint submodels; shared node identity collapses the XOR. The code also never imports `SilentTransition` and calls `Transition(label="", silent=True)`, which is not the POWL Transition constructor. The block is therefore not reliably executable, despite the prompt requiring one executable model.

**Net.** Scope, authority, and most case judgments would sit in the high 7s to low 8s. Model semantics and the revision—the artifacts the prompt specified in the most detail—are well below that. The result is a persuasive policy memo attached to a control-flow model that cannot replay the compliant majority of the log and does not implement the one concurrency constraint it was told it must represent.
