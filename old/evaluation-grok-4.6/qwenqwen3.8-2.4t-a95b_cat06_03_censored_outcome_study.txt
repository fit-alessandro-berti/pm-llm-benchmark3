**6.7**

The final nine-section writeup is a strong process-aware design: it refuses to treat omitted applications as non-default, treats activation as selection rather than a free complete-case filter, keeps an as-of cutoff, and makes temporal validation the primary scheme. Those are the hard requirements. It is not nearly flawless. Several target, maturity, and implementation inconsistencies are material.

**What is solid.** The primary estimand is an observed-process cumulative incidence of default that can only occur after activation, with rejected/withdrawn as non-activation states rather than zeros. The activation-conditional and counterfactual alternatives are correctly described as different questions. The cohort table sums to 48,000 and assigns each export bucket a status (labeled, competing, delayed, censored, unresolved transfer). Delayed labels, activation selection, unlinked transfers, and permanently missing partner feeds are separated. The leakage table covers every required field with keep/repair/drop rules. Random splits are rejected for the right reasons (policy change, migration, immature labels). The skeleton uses the requested PM4Py entry points, filters to pre-decision events, and does not invent a survival API.

**Target and horizon are internally inconsistent.** Section 1 defines default only if activation occurs within 180 days, then 90 days post-activation, plus a 35-day feed lag. That composite is never enforced in cohort labels: any activated matured account is coded `activated_matured_default` / `nodefault` regardless of time to activation. The 180-day window appears only as a late `analysis_end_time` comment. So the stated estimand and the constructed outcome are not the same quantity.

**Validation cannot identify the stated estimand.** Full observation of start → 180d activation → 90d default → 35d feed requires about 305 days. With `AS_OF = 2026-01-31`, starts after roughly late April 2025 cannot complete that window. Validation through 2025-06-30 and Test A through 2025-09-30 are therefore mostly right-censored for the primary CIF; Test B is almost entirely unidentified. The text says Test A is “partially matured” and Test B is monitoring-only, but it still presents those slices as the policy-shift and migration-shift tests of the primary target. That overstates what the extract can support. A design this careful should have set training/evaluation cutoffs from the maturity calendar, or dropped the 180-day bolt-on and defined a shorter, actually observable horizon.

**Prefix rules do not match the code.** The prose promises two schemes (fixed event count and elapsed time). The implemented rule is `min(kth pre-decision event, start+7d)`, then `get_prefixes_from_log(..., 3)` on an already-truncated log. Elapsed-time prefixes longer than three events are cut back to three; short cases are handled twice (clip before decision, then prefix-length flag). That is a third, underspecified hybrid, not two audited prefix definitions.

**Implementation leakage and label-as-of bugs.** `feature_df` includes `cohort_status`, which is built from activation, default, reject, withdraw, and transfer. The leakage assert only checks a forbidden-column set, so the “feature table” would pass the audit while carrying the target pathway. Labels with missing `label_available_at` are treated as already known (`isna() | <= AS_OF`), which can backfill delayed warehouse fields onto historical events. `default_within_90` can also fire from a `Default Recorded` timestamp even when the matured `default_90d` flag is absent, then get overwritten by the immature status—so the default indicator and the analysis status can disagree.

**Smaller but real statistical sloppiness.** Fine-Gray, cause-specific hazards, and an activation-as-intermediate multi-state model are listed as interchangeable options; they are not. Transfer handling oscillates between “competing event” and “missing follow-up” in the primary analysis. Inclusion criterion 3 (“usable pre-decision prefix”) conflicts with retaining all short prefixes. Partner permanently missing feeds are discussed but never placed inside the 21,600 / 3,600 counts. Historical default rates by prefix variant are allowed as features; even with a training-only as-of rule that is one step from target leakage. The 2025-10-01–10-14 band falls into `other`. Queue-backlog features are proposed without a point-in-time reconstruction rule.

**Code quality vs. the spec.** Length and the required calls are in range. `discover_dfg` unpacked as a 3-tuple is version-dependent. The rename map invents activity names. `documents_total` and extraction-time resource experience are audited in the table and then absent from the pipeline. Survival times and event codes are comments only, which is acceptable, but then `cohort_status` should not have been written into the feature frame.

Net: this is a competent, honest study design that would not make the classic biased-cohort mistakes. Under a strict reading it still has a broken horizon/maturity story, a target–code mismatch, and a leakage hole in the skeleton. That is well above a weak answer and well short of a clean one.
