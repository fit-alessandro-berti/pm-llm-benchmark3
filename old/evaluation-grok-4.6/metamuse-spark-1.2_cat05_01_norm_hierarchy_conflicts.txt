**6.4**

The submission is structurally complete and often sophisticated, but it is not close to flawless. It states sound method and then violates that method in several case-level applications. Under a strict reading those are not cosmetic nits; they are errors of time, evidence, and rule trigger.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 separates facts from defeasible assumptions and does not smuggle a single total order into the analysis. The issue-specific hierarchy in Section 2 is the right architecture: GPIP-4 integrity controls (A1/A3, and A2 unless interpreted) sit above ECD-2; ECD-2 can reach timing and the supplier register only inside its window and stated scope; the CFO email is interpretive and uncontrolled; ON-44 is evidential, not normative. The formal catalogue and the `overrides_for` / `supported_by` sketch match the assigned task. A6 is applied as a non-cure rule. Alternatives in Section 5 are mostly well posed (premise change, why credible, why weak). Section 6 distinguishes policy breach, logging defect, late evidence of a timely act, and inability to prove. The draft clause in Section 7 covers the required topics and includes a real anti-normalization safeguard.

Those virtues do not offset the analytical defects below.

**1. Direct contradiction of the answer’s own time-of-act rule (P-502).**  
D1 is sent **2026-07-15 07:20**. P-502 is committed **2026-07-14 10:15**. The answer still treats D1 as an “arguable cure” of the missing pre-commitment Finance approval. That is a clean anachronism. The memo correctly insists that rules apply at the act, then applies a later email to an earlier commit. Primary class stays NONCOMPLIANT because A3 is independently fatal, so the error is not outcome-dispositive — but it is a first-order process failure, and it understates P-502. On 14 July, A2 is an open breach as well as A3; there is no Finance-side overlay yet.

**2. Misreading of P-503’s authorization record and of C3.**  
The file contains `Emergency_Authorize 10:55 by PROC-3 and IC-1` before `Commit_to_Supplier 11:05`. That is contemporaneous recorded joint authorization, not “no evidence of record within 4h.” Treating C3 as unrecorded is false on the given evidence.  
Separately, C3 requires two *distinct* Incident Command members. PROC-3 and IC-1 are two people. The SoD problem is A1 (requester/committer as final business approver), not C3 distinctness. Collapsing those into “R-C3: not distinct (self-auth)” is sloppy and makes the catalogue look more decisive than the source text.

**3. Unanalyzed A3 trigger on “unapproved” vs “new” (especially P-504).**  
A3 is not only an amount threshold. It fires on **any new supplier**. P-504/P-507/P-508 are “unapproved”; only P-502 is labeled “new.” A careful opinion must say whether those predicates are identical. The answer never does. For P-504 it asserts Compliance is “not triggered” because of amount. That is either a miss of A3 or a silent interpretive choice. Either way it is undisciplined. If unapproved = new, P-504 is also a pre-commitment Compliance failure, and “low severity / amount” is too soft.

**4. False-positive rule satisfaction.**  
- P-505 lists **R-A3 as satisfied**. A3 does not apply: EUR 88k, approved supplier.  
- P-502 lists **R-B6 as “implicit.”** No emergency form appears in the evidence. That is invented compliance.  
- P-502 lists R-B3 as satisfied while the supplier is new; the real point is a C2 override, which should have been written that way.

**5. B6 and other local evidence duties are not applied uniformly.**  
B6 is checked for P-501 and used against P-504, then dropped or hand-waved for the ECD purchases. Create_PO is entirely absent from the P-505 record and is under-flagged. P-501’s 08:42/08:50 stamps fall inside ON-44; the outage cannot pull the commit past 09:00 (ERP is late, not early), but the memo never tests that. These are exactly the “evidential discipline” points the prompt asked to be visible.

**6. B2 timing is asserted more firmly than the SOP supports, then used to harden P-501.**  
The catalogue admits B2 does not say before/after commit, then treats post-commit Finance/Plant as a clear breach. For P-501 (EUR 38k) GPIP A2 is off. The solid P-501 defect is B4: Plant Manager reachable, no active delegation, SHIFT-1 had no substitution right. Finance delay is a weaker, theory-dependent count. “High” confidence overstates that file.

**7. Catalogue imprecision that later infects the cases.**  
A1 is pairwise (requester **or** committer may not be the final business approver), not a three-way identity. R-C3’s actor line imports SoD (“≠ committer/requester”) as if it were in ECD-2. That is interpretation presented as the rule.

**8. The JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION trio (P-505/P-507/P-508) is coherent only as a contested theory, and P-508 is the weakest call.**  
The memo is honest that a strict A4/A7 reading makes all three NONCOMPLIANT, and it is entitled not to let GPIP-4 automatically crush every other source. Using JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION rather than COMPLIANT is the right hedge **if** D1 is treated as a contemporaneous Finance interpretation inside the window. Even on that theory:  
- P-505 still has no Plant event and no Create_PO in the file; Medium confidence is earned, but the satisfied-rule list is too clean.  
- P-507 has **no Finance event at all** and IC members are not established budget owners. A2 is two-headed (Finance Controller **and** independent budget owner). D1 can at most speak to the Finance operational step. Residual A2 is not a footnote.  
- P-508 exceeds D1’s **EUR 150,000** cap. Reading D2’s “as well” as “IC remains sufficient, plus Legal” is one available reading, not the only one, and it is the reading most favorable to the largest, unapproved-supplier buy. Primary JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION with a messy dual-class cell is too confident a headline for that file. INDETERMINATE, or NONCOMPLIANT with a documented alternative, would have tracked the memo’s own definition of indeterminacy more closely.  
No case is classified INDETERMINATE despite the method promising that label when both readings are reasonable and missing evidence would reverse. That makes the decision method look more operational than it is.

**9. Small drafting defects that a strict grade should not ignore.**  
P-508 “SoD satisfied (BUY-8 authorizers)” is the opposite of the intended “BUY-8 ≠ authorizers.” Policy 7.3 drops “within” (“stoppage expected 6 hours”). 7.4’s “All purchases €25k” is missing the inequality. None of these are harmless in a norm-hierarchy draft.

**Classifications that survive the errors.**  
P-501, P-502, P-503, P-504, and P-506 are still correctly NONCOMPLIANT on independent grounds. P-505’s preference for the 17:59:31 acknowledgment over ERP 18:06 is the right evidential move and is consistent with ON-44. The refusal to treat ECD-2 as a silent A3/A1 waiver is the strongest part of the opinion. The problem is not the architecture; it is uneven execution against the architecture.

**Net.** This is a good professional draft with a real method, not a near-perfect assessment. It loses substantial credit for applying D1 before it exists, misstating P-503’s record, leaving the new/unapproved predicate unargued, marking rules satisfied that are either inapplicable or unevidenced, and then giving the most generous primary label to the largest post-D1 purchases. Those are the exact dimensions the prompt said would be judged.
