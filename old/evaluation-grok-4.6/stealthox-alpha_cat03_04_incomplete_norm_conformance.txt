## Material defects

**1. Section 1 is damaged at the point where the semantics are defined.**  
The open-world sentence ends “unlisted  violated.” The missing words invert or at least scramble the central claim of the paper. The surrounding paragraph is otherwise correct, so this is not a conceptual collapse — it is a clarity failure in the section that was supposed to define the classification rules. That alone bars a high score.

**2. The seven classes are declared mutually exclusive and then immediately violated.**  
The table uses hybrids throughout: “Forbidden behavior (DQ possible)”, “Missing mandatory / likely DQ”, “Abstraction mismatch or missing mandatory”, “Typical-pattern absence / insufficient evidence”. Dual status is often the *right* reading (especially E10 and E6). Declaring mutual exclusivity and then abandoning it is a logic error, not a subtlety.

**3. The PM4Py sketch misses a stated assumption and implements the wrong comparison.**  
The prompt requires `norm_ocdfg`, `norm_otg`, and `norm_etot`. Those variables are never used. Conformance is called as `conformance_*(discovered_model, ocel)`. A model checked against the log it was mined from is near-tautological and does not test the *norm*. Raw outputs are preserved, metadata is kept outside the graph, and the six function names are present — so the sketch is not empty — but presence-checking OC-DFG edges as booleans would mark N1 and N4 *conforming* because the edges exist (98/100, 198/200). The semantic layer therefore cannot see the actual mandatory shortfalls that the prose treats as central. `classify()` also never implements abstraction mismatch, likely DQ, or insufficient evidence, contradicting Section 1.

**4. E8 is softened past the norm text.**  
E8 is mandatory except for events *explicitly typed* as unapplied cash, and “none are so typed in this snapshot.” The table class “Typical-pattern absence / insufficient evidence” is too generous. Section 4 is better (“violation iff not unapplied cash”), which means the answer disagrees with itself. Finance testimony is a *hypothesis*, not a typing. The finding belongs primarily in missing-mandatory-pending-label, with DQ/governance as the alternative.

**5. Write-offs are classified two ways.**  
Rank 10: “Unexpected but unregulated.” Section 4: legitimate N5 exception channel. N5’s scope note already names write-offs. “Unexpected” is the wrong bucket.

**6. N1’s stated scope is only half-used.**  
N1 applies to “newly created *standard* Order[s]” and is eventual, not DF. The answer leans on unrecorded review / DF mismatch and never tests the “standard” qualifier. That is exactly the scope discussion the prompt required for mandatory gaps.

**7. Section 8 misgroups N1 with unspecified behavior.**

Uncertainty is evidential (DF vs reachability, missing events, standard-vs-nonstandard), not coverage.
