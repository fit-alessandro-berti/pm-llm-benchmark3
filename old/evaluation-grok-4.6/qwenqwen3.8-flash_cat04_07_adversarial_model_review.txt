**6.5**

The review of Models S and C is strong: it refuses to crown a winner by aggregate score, separates exploration from enforcement, and reasons from concrete traces. The probe matrix for S and C is largely correct under standard PM4Py process-tree semantics (`X` = XOR, `+` = AND). Pathologies, governance, validation design, and the dissenting view are serious rather than decorative.

The core deliverable is not. The formal regex uses a Kleene star (zero or more post-check episodes), but the PM4Py tree is `*(X(correction | Supplier | Expedite), tau)`. In process-tree loop semantics that is **do–redo**: the first child runs **at least once**. The backbone therefore **rejects B1 and B2** (and any valid case with only the two mandatory checks and an approval). Section 8 still marks those as `CF yes`. That is a showstopper: the coded `model_third` is not safer-and-more-general in the way the prose claims, and the audit table does not describe the code.

Further defects, stacked on that mismatch:

- Treating more than one Supplier/Expedite as a **hard** violation is not in P1–P7; it is an extra policy the checks smuggle in.
- `POLICY_CHECKS["hard"]` omits several violations the function actually raises (`register_not_exactly_once`, `mandatory_after_approval`, etc.).
- `correct_before_initial_mandatory_checks` is invented, not stated.
- Validation skeletons call APIs the prompt did not give (`align_trace`, `filter_case_ids`) and then hard-code a `0.95` holdout cutoff after saying the board must set thresholds.
- Operand order `*(tau, X(...))` (or XOR-with-tau around a `+` loop) would have matched the regex; that is a standard loop encoding, not an exotic requirement.

Intent of the hybrid (mandatory XOR-pair, optional S/E, correction-gated rechecks, post-hoc attribute uncertainty) is the right *kind* of third model. As written, the executable model and the comparative audit are inconsistent with each other and with B1/B2. That keeps the grade well below the near-flawless band.
