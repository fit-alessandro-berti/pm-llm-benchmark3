**7.0**

The write-up is complete, internally consistent, and much stronger on POWL than on the process tree. The POWL is essentially exact under the stated data abstraction: split `C→PV` / `A→DCA` edges, approval and `cs_opt` unconstrained against the trial, and a real 3-way join at `Release Material`. Assumptions, witnesses, and the semantic-comparison table are mostly precise. That is not enough for a high score under a strict reading.

The process tree is the main failure of “as faithful as possible.” Serializing the trial after approvals and supplier consultation deletes the one non-block pattern the prompt highlights: trial waits only for `DCA` and `PV`, while approvals may overlap the trial and release is the synchronizer. A same-size conservative tree, `SEQ(R, PAR(SEQ(C,PV), SEQ(A,DCA)), PAR(trial, approval, cs_opt), RM, AC)`, keeps that pattern and only extra-waits approvals/CS on the `C`/`PV` stream. Calling the chosen cut “conservative” is true; it is not a defense of *this* cut. W5 exposes the gap but does not justify it.

Smaller issues compound. D16 is not a well-formed dependency row. W2 never shows `QR∥RR` (only `QR` then `RR`). The compact tree is informal nested-function notation, not a parseable PT string. The validation sketch leans on APIs that likely do not accept a POWL (`fitness_alignments(log, powl)`, `play_out(..., max_trace_length=20)`). Self-critique #3 blames the library instead of a residual modeling defect (the tree cut already is one). None of these is fatal; together they keep the answer well short of nearly flawless.
