The submission never becomes the required deliverable. The task demands one Markdown document with exactly nine specified top-level headings and exactly one code block. What is present is an unfinished scratchpad that stops mid-correction of transition counts (`ReworkReview`). There is no `# 1`–`# 9` document, no encoding table for all 32 cases, no finished statistical plan as a plan, no operational section as such, and no Python. Under a format-constrained evaluation, that is a hard fail.

Even if the scratchpad is treated as the “final” content, it is not a finished analysis. Estimands, AJ arithmetic, transfer scenarios, and leakage comments are drafted and repeatedly revised, but they are not frozen into a consistent report. Several quantities that would have been the paper’s headline numbers (overall CIF at 72h, RMST, stratum-specific CIFs, transfer-scenario contrasts) go through arithmetic errors that the author later patches. A reader cannot tell which figure is the intended one without re-deriving the whole chain. That is not a minor presentation issue; it is an internal-consistency failure.

Specific problems that remain even after self-corrections:

- **Estimand/estimator mixing in the working notes.** Early KM, CIF, and “not resolved by 72” quantities are confused (wrong pre-event survival at several times, e.g. using \(S(16)=0.90625\) instead of \(0.875\)), then rewritten. The intended story—AJ \(F_{\mathrm{res}}(72)\approx 0.30\) vs KM-with-competing-as-censor \(\approx 0.64\) vs completed-case \(4/13\approx 0.31\)—is conceptually the right one, but it is not stably computed in a single pass.
- **Stratum and transition counts are unreliable in the text.** P2 at-risk sizes, B `Screen>Review` counts, `Review>Resolve` (5 vs 6), `Screen>Escalate` (2 vs 5), and AutoCheck token totals are wrong, then partly fixed. A process-aware analysis that cannot keep the prefix graph consistent is not trustworthy.
- **Transfer “gaming” at 68h (S08)** is interesting as a hypothesis, but it is still one case. The notes sometimes treat it as a governance signal and sometimes as mere color; that should have been labeled more tightly.
- **No executable encoding.** The task required in-memory construction of the 32-row table, two estimands, three transfer treatments, risk-set data that retain open cases, a stratified plus sensitivity comparison, and a PM4Py prefix parse **without invented timestamps**. None of that exists. Hand AJ on \(n=32\) does not substitute for the specified artifact.
- **Uncertainty is asserted, not shown.** Greenwood \(\mathrm{SE}(S(72))\approx 0.09\) and bootstrap language appear, but no interval is actually produced from a defined procedure. With \(n=32\) and cells as small as P1 \(n=8\), point estimates to three decimals are over-precise.

What is salvageable is the *direction* of the reasoning, which is why this is not a 1.0. The notes correctly:

- put estimands before estimators;
- refuse to treat withdrawal/transfer as ordinary non-informative censoring;
- distinguish right censoring, competing events, and already-observed breaches (open \(>72\)h vs open \(<72\)h);
- flag `Escalate`/`Rework` as post-baseline (immortal time / leakage);
- use concrete IDs (S03, S06, S08, S09, P1 transfers S08/S21/S32);
- note that P1 failure is stalls/transfers and P3 failure is churn, so ranking can reverse under transfer or withdrawal coding.

Those are the competence the quality bar asked for. They remain buried in an incomplete, self-contradicting draft that does not meet the output contract. Under a hypercritical rubric, format failure, missing code, unstable numbers, and an unfinished document dominate.
