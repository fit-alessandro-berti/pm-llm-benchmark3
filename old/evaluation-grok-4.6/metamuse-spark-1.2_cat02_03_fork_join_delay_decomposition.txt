The submission is substantively competent on the core fork-join task, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it loses material credit for factual slips, one inverted secondary-effect claim, a contradictory double-counting example, and repeated numeric/prose sloppiness.

**What is actually strong.** Section 1 defines the right objects: `i_queue = i_start − fork_release`, `join_ready = max_i i_end`, `fork_join_elapsed = max_i i_completion`, and the additive identity `total = upstream + fork_join + downstream + final`. That identity is the correct way to avoid double-counting elapsed time. Case-level arithmetic in §2 is almost entirely correct; the twelve-row table, the four worked cases, and the CF1/CF2 join-shift tables check out against the raw timestamps. The central inference is also right: A has the largest service (50, zero variance, zero queue) but B has the largest completion (56.7) and is last to finish in 8/12 cases, so service must not be confused with influence. Zero-benefit cases in CF1 (10/12) and CF2 (4/12) are identified. §7 is appropriately cautious about capacity, preemption, and whether `final_start − join_ready` is a queue or a policy.

**Logical / conceptual flaws.**

- §1 asserts that summing branch sync waits “would exceed elapsed time,” then gives F01 as the example: sync waits 0+5+22 = 27, which is *less* than `fork_join_elapsed = 50`. The right point is that the sum is not elapsed time because the waits overlap; the “exceeds” claim is false and the example refutes it. (`∑ completion` would exceed elapsed time; that is a different identity.)
- CF2 secondary effects invert F06. Previous F2 finishes at 10:30; CF2 moves F06’s join to 10:15. If F06 stays on F2, the 15-minute join gain is fully absorbed as extra downstream wait, not “absorbed immediately.” F1 is also busy until 10:20. The later claim that “in 4 cases resource idle gaps > saving, so no masking” is therefore untrustworthy.
- “Full elimination [of B queue] would add ~9.6 min extra” treats the remaining half-queue as if it were on the critical path in every case. After the 50% cut several cases have already switched to A, so the incremental gain is smaller.
