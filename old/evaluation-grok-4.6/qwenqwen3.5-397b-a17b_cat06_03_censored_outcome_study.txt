**5.5**

The write-up gets the central scientific point right: default is not observed, and is not even well-defined, for rejected, withdrawn, open, or most transferred applications, and those cases are not relabeled as non-default. The nine-section structure is followed, the 48,000-case accounting adds to 48,000, and the leakage table correctly drops post-decision and post-activation fields. That is not enough for a high score. Several **required** design elements are missing or internally inconsistent, and the validation scheme is statistically broken.

**Target and estimand.**  
Conditional default given activation is a defensible primary target and is labeled as such. The alternative competing-risk formulation is named, not specified (no time origin, state space, or estimand). The chosen prediction time is the last Credit Review before a decision, which is not “the first few events of every credit application.” Worse, defining the prefix as the *final* review loop requires knowing that no further document loop occurs. That is look-ahead in the sampling time itself. The 90-day clock starts at activation, which is still in the future at that point; variable review-to-activation delay is ignored.

**Cohort.**  
The flow table is clear, but the study then throws away everyone except the 21,600 matured activated accounts. Transfers are excluded wholesale despite a 60% linkage table. Partner-originated missing feeds inside the labeled set are not handled. As-of rules for still-open vs not-yet-mature activated cases are thin.

**Censoring.**  
The taxonomy (right censoring, delayed labels, activation selection, transfer, permanent missingness) is the strongest part of the memo. The *strategy* is not. Primary analysis is complete-case classification on the convenient labeled export; IPCW is mentioned in one sentence; competing risks, transfer bounds, and partner-feed missingness never become an operational plan. `label_available_at` is invoked and then not used to build as-of training sets.

**Prefixes and features.**  
The prompt required both fixed event-count and elapsed-time prefixes. Both are dismissed rather than specified. Short cases are not handled. Queue features are essentially absent. A pre/post July 2025 dummy can absorb the policy break instead of testing transport. Resource-level rolling default rates are proposed without an as-of maturity rule, which is a second leakage path.

**Leakage table.**  
Most mandated fields are covered and the keep/repair/drop calls on `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, and `collection_status` are correct. `label_available_at` is not a row, despite “label availability” being required. Resource experience is left as “Drop/Repair.” The table never flags that “last review before accept” encodes that acceptance is coming.

**Validation (decisive error).**  
Train / val / test cuts on 2025-07-01 and 2025-10-15 correctly respect the policy and migration dates, and the random-split critique is right. The test window is not. Cases *started* 15 Oct–31 Dec 2025 generally cannot have activation + 90 days + 7–35 day feed by 15 Feb 2026. Any such case that appears in the 21,600 labeled rows is selected for very fast activation. The test set is therefore a biased, thin, post-migration slice, not a clean temporal holdout. Subgroup performance (region, channel, partner origin, pre/post policy) is not designed. Survival metrics are absent even though censoring was emphasized.

**Limitations.**  
Adequate. The three deployment DGP shifts are real. The association / selection / counterfactual distinction is only partly drawn.

**Code.**  
This fails the skeleton contract. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` is never called, though it was required for fixed-length prefixes. `pm4py.get_variants` is called and unused. There is no case-level start / terminal / censoring / maturity table over all 48,000 applications. The groupby-apply that writes `prediction_timestamp` does not align a case-level Series back onto event rows, so the subsequent prefix filter is not a working pattern. The leakage `assert` is vacuous because forbidden columns were never selected. Activity renaming, delayed-label as-of logic, and a configurable cutoff are missing. Length is under the requested band.

Net: honest target semantics and a usable leakage list, with a late and slightly leaky prediction time, a convenience activated-only cohort, an unusable test window, and a skeleton that does not implement the required PM4Py prefix path. Under a strict reading of the rubric that is mid-pack, not strong.
