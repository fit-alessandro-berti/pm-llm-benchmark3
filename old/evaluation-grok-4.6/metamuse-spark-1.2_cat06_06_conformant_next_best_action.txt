The answer is a strong, well-structured response that hits the task’s main quality bar: it privileges mandatory second-officer review, ranks A/B last on deontic rather than utilitarian grounds, treats re-screening as information rather than a waiver, and internalizes P-901. It is not nearly flawless. Several process-model, quantitative, and planning errors are material.

**What it gets right.** The six required sections are present and usable. Conformance labels match the allowed set. A and B are correctly `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT` and are not rehabilitated by the €20–35k deadline figure. G is correctly `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`. F is correctly conditional. The D* plan (alternate screen in parallel with S4, then independent review) is a legitimate composite and correctly refuses to treat the vendor screen as a substitute for Rule 6. Prediction transportability, option value of remaining on an Approve/Reject/Hold branch, Rule 11, and four-eyes are handled in the right spirit. The S4 35-vs-65 sensitivity is the right robustness check.

**Process-model errors.** After Evidence Validated, Rule 5 returns the case to Analyst Review; the answer never discusses that cycle and treats Second Officer Review as the sole remaining control. More serious is the Rule 3/6 muddle. Auto Clear requires *no alert and risk below 40*, not risk below 70 with a resolved match. Section 2 claims that if risk drops below 70 and the match clears, “Auto Clear path *could* become available.” Section 6 then says the same joint clearing “would still require review.” Those two claims cannot both be right, and the first is false under the stated norm. That is a core-control error, not a wording slip.

**Quantitative and causal errors.** The P-901 analysis overclaims. An incremental breach probability of 0.12–0.28 is not a near-certain full deadline loss, and the baseline breach rate for P-901 is unknown, so “likely pushes P-901 into breach (>0.5)” is invented. Equating C’s spillover to “similar €20–35k” and calling net system value “near zero or negative” is therefore unjustified; the expected incremental loss is a fraction of whatever P-901’s (unstated) deadline value is. Relatedly, F’s “avoids deadline breach as rejection is a decision within SLA” misreads the €20–35k figure, which is the value of *executing* on time, not of closing any decision on time. Rejection realizes none of that benefit; it trades into the €15–80k false-rejection band. Adding deadline loss and customer-hold harm for E also risks double-counting overlapping non-execution costs. The table’s “P(breach) ~0.35–0.70” under D* is not derived.

**Planning logic.** The advertised C fallback is too late. C is 40–90 minutes. Starting it at t+30 yields t+70–120, which largely destroys the reason to interrupt P-901. The table (“if S4 >50m”), §5 (t+25–30 if S4 >50m and screen inconclusive), and §6 (t+30 if S4 ETA >50m *or* screen confirms elevated risk) are not the same rule. The last trigger is backwards: a screen that *confirms* a sanctions hit argues for hold/SAR and *not* stealing S2 from P-901 to rush a payment. “Comfortably within 90m” at S4=35 is also overstated: 35+35+5=75 only if review is at its floor; a mid-range review already misses. Immediate tightening of S4’s 35–65 ETA is the obvious first observation and is not treated as such.

Residual false-clear risk after a procedurally valid approval is written as “€0 regulatory,” which is only true of *unlawful-execution* exposure. Ranking E third is defensible as a conformant continuation, but the write-up does not face how close E is to a dominated stall once the 90-minute clock is abandoned.
