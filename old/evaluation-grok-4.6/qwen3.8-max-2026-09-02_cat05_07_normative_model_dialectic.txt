**7.2**

The response is a strong, complete dialectical review: it uses the required headings, argues both sides at real strength, traces evidence to cases and overlays, and proposes a layered model rather than a single POWL. It is not nearly flawless. Several semantic and consistency errors are material under a strict reading.

**What is strong.** Section 1 correctly separates stipulated facts from disputed authority/scope/logging questions and states a workable burden for noncompliance versus exceptions. The prosecution brief is not a straw man: it uses CS-9 hierarchy, expiry, ineligible Auto_Approve, SUP-703/SUP-708, SoD/COI hidden by M0, and “desirable does not amend policy.” The defense is equally concrete: M0’s missing loop and routes, PD-3/FD-7 overlays, CRM migration, rejected-lead and reactivation scope, and label/dedup artifacts. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including M0-fitting resource failures (SUP-712, SUP-715). Final labels mostly track the evidence (701 compliant; 703/708 high noncompliance; 704 justified; 706 indeterminate; 707/709/713 model/scope; 714 post-sunset noncompliance). The architecture correctly parks amount, identity, dates, and eligibility outside POWL.

**Material errors.**

1. **Emergency vs sanctions is stated backwards in the proposed constraints.** FD-7 still requires `Sanctions_Check` before activation; it only relaxes quality and manual approval. Layer 3 and `DECLARE_CONSTRAINTS` (`SANCTIONS_BEFORE_ACTIVATION` with `except_routes: ["EMERGENCY_PROVISIONAL"]`) would allow emergency activation without sanctions. That contradicts N3, the POWL emergency branch (which orders sanctions before activate), and the FD-7 control table in the same answer. This is an internal contradiction on a core control.

2. **SUP-702 is classified `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` against the answer’s own burden.** PD-3 requires automated master-data validation before activation. The trace has no such event. The brief lists that gap as missing evidence, then still grants the exception. Medium confidence does not repair the mismatch; under the stated rule the case is `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` or `MIXED`, not a clean justified exception.

3. **The RMI encoding is not a reliable executable POWL loop.** PM4Py `Operator.LOOP` is a binary (do/redo) operator. A unary `LOOP` plus `XOR(silent, loop)` is likely invalid or semantically wrong. The `getattr(..., Operator.XOR)` fallback collapses “repeatable RMI” to at most one occurrence, which cannot replay SUP-707’s two `Request_More_Info` events. The limitation is noted, but the required repeatable mechanism is not actually implemented.

**Smaller but real flaws.** `DECLARE` “absence” constraints are mis-specified (they mean precedence/response, not absence). SUP-711 as `COMPLIANT` rather than a data-quality finding is too generous even with the duplicate-key story. Policy tables are useful but not truly normalized (repeated nested dicts, informal constraint types). `LABEL_MAPPING.effective_from = 2026-07-01` is an invented effective date for a mapping approved after SUP-713. Silent transitions are used only for optional RMI, which is defensible, but the surrounding LOOP construction is not.

**Judgment quality.** The system-level conclusion (do not enforce raw M0; CS-9 still binds; some deviations are model/data, some are material noncompliance) is the right shape and does not merely split the difference. Case-level severity/confidence is generally well motivated. The repeated sanctions-exception error, the 702/burden inconsistency, and the non-working loop keep this well below a high score under strict grading.
