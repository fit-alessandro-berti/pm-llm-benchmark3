**5.5**

The write-up follows the required six-section form, correctly treats A/B as currently nonconformant, keeps four-eyes and mandatory second review as hard constraints, and proposes parallel screening with an independent second officer. Those are the main things a strong answer must get right. The rest is not close to flawless.

**Material process errors.** Action D is defined as alternate screening *then* a second-officer review by S2 or S4. The table and later sections repeatedly treat D as screening-only (“incomplete,” continuation = “screening results in 15–30m”). That is a misread of the action set, and it is why D is dumped to rank 5 under reject-now and an unenabled override. In the D counterfactual, a cleared match plus score “<70” is allowed to go to “Auto-clear or 1st Analyst only.” Auto Clear is permitted only with *no alert and risk below 40*, not below 70. After an analyst path and a still-elevated score, second-officer review drops out only if *both* the ≥70 trigger *and* the unresolved fuzzy match are gone. That is a direct error in the normative model.

**Ranking and dominance problems.** G is labeled correctly as `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`, then ranked above two live conformant options. Seeking a director override that is not currently authorized burns 20–60 minutes of a 90-minute window and may produce nothing; putting it above D (a complete conformant review plan) and E is inconsistent with the answer’s own claim that G is “ranked lower because it is currently disabled.” F at rank 3, and again as the sole fallback, overweights a path whose rejection threshold is explicitly uncertain and whose customer-harm band (€15k–€80k) can exceed the deadline-loss band. A hold/SAR continuation after review is barely developed, and Rule 11 (no disclosure of a SAR to the customer) never appears.

**Overconfident operational claims.** C’s clock is 35–75 minutes of review plus 5–15 minutes to record/execute (40–90). The upper bound *is* the deadline; C does not “secure” on-time execution. Approval after a conformant review is given as 0.55–0.75, yet the top counterfactual calls approval the “likely” branch. P-901 spillover is noted, but the 0.12–0.28 figure is tied to a 45-minute interrupt while C’s review range is 35–75, so the spillover is understated. The S4 sensitivity uses optimistic arithmetic (65 + 35 = 100) and then a rerank trigger of “S4 in under 20 minutes,” which does not match the section 5 claim that 35 minutes would already be competitive.

**Incomplete required analysis.** Section 4 maps A, B, F, and G, but does not map every action to the relevant rules (3, 6–11). Observational predictions are said not to transport under intervention; that caveat is essentially unused. The 12-minute-old risk score is unused. Benefit/harm cells are often slogans rather than ranges. Parallel C+D is a reasonable primary plan, but if screening changes the match, S2’s clock can lengthen—this is not costed.

Net: the hard compliance ranking (A/B last; no shortcut-as-utility) is sound, and the parallel second-review idea is the right family of solution. The D mischaracterization, the Auto Clear threshold error, the inflation of G and F, and the false claim that C meets the SLA keep this well below a high score under strict grading.
