**6.2**

The answer is complete, well formatted, and arithmetically careful, but it is not close to flawless. It repeatedly overstates what the panel can support, contradicts its own identification analysis in the policy design, and treats two-period averages of quantiles as if they were a credible causal estimate.

**Overclaim after correct diagnosis.** Sections 2–3 and 6 correctly flag endogenous targeting, SUTVA violations through the shared fraud desk and routing, changing intensity, aggregated quantiles, and \(G=5\). Section 5 then says automation “causally reduced” median cycle time by \([-2, -5]\) hours and gave a tail “buffer” of \([-3, -8]\) hours. Section 7 assigns 75 confidence to that median range and tells leadership it may “safely” claim a 2–5 hour reduction. Those intervals are just the DiD point estimates with a small cushion. They are not bounds, not sensitivity sets, and not robust to the threats the author listed. That is a central logical failure, not a wording slip.

**Identifiability is applied inconsistently.** E4 and E5 are marked identifiable under parallel trends; E3 and E6 are marked non-identified. Parallel trends are no more plausible for rework or escalation than for cycle time. Escalation is the *least* ambiguous descriptive jump in the file and the *worst* candidate for a clean ITT: the prompt states that automated triage sent more borderline cases to a shared desk. Calling that “identifiable” while calling the intensity contrast non-identified is backwards. Alternative 1 also labels the rollout schedule an “exogenous instrument.” It is not. Adoption was assigned to historically weak, high-volume branches and bundled with routing. First-stage \(F>10\) is not a serious diagnostic with five clusters.

**The primary design is not the design that is estimated.** The author rejects TWFE, recommends a clean-cohort event study, then reports one pooled Apr–Aug vs Jan–Mar 2×2 and one Jul–Aug vs Apr–Jun 2×2. That is not an event study. It mixes the low-intensity first month, the May complexity shock, the Alpha staffing loss, and the late volume retreat into a single post mean. Gamma’s two post months are weighted as confirmatory evidence almost equal to Alpha/Beta. The code constructs `rel_month` and then never estimates it; it fits the TWFE specification the narrative just called badly biased.

**Pre-trends and regression to the mean are oversold.** Three pre months do not establish parallel trends. Alpha is 50.4 → 46.9 → 49.4; Delta is 34.4 → 32.8 → 36.2; Gamma is already deteriorating in March–June. “Broadly parallel” is not true on inspection. Gamma does not “strongly refute” mean reversion: its July drop coincides with an organization-wide easing of arrivals, and Apr–Jun as the pre window is the peak of the May shock. That is a convenient contrast, not a refutation.

**Process interpretation is good until the recommendation.** The mechanism table is the strongest part of the memo: fast-path substitution vs tail amplification, staffing, case mix, and survey selection are handled clearly, and median is not collapsed into p90. Then section 8 claims an 8-week within-Epsilon case-level A/B “cleanly isolates” the algorithm from volume, staffing, and shocks. That contradicts the author’s own SUTVA discussion. Manual and automated cases in the same branch share FTEs, specialist review, and the fraud desk. In a queueing system that is interference, not isolation. Odd/even case-ID assignment is also a weak randomization scheme. The decision rule (\(\Delta \ge 3\) hours, “statistically significant,” month 3) is arbitrary relative to the \([-2,-5]\) range and underpowered or ill-defined for CSAT. External validity for Delta (much lighter case mix) versus Epsilon (already 38% high-complexity) is barely argued.

**Quantitative discipline is uneven.** Adopter pre/post means and the two 2×2 contrasts largely recompute. That is real work. It is still the wrong object: the mean of monthly medians is not a period median, and equal-weighting months and branches hides Alpha’s June 53.5 / 119.9 blowout. Rework is sold as a 1–2 pp improvement from the April dip; Alpha finishes at 14.5 versus 13.2 pre. The intensity slopes with `workload_per_fte` and complexity, and the “covariate-adjusted panel −2.19 to −4.17,” are not in the script. Workload per FTE is also a post-treatment variable if automation or staffing responds to load. Baseline `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% while `automation=0` is never interpreted.

**Smaller defects.** TWFE bias from already-treated controls is overstated once never-treated branches exist. Routing is treated only as extra load on adopters; if it also lightens controls, DiD is biased toward finding a benefit, the same direction as the fraud-desk spillover. Section 7’s “safe” rework claim is causal. Confidence 85 on escalation is a description of a design feature, not an outcome win. The Python sandwich estimator is acceptable but does not implement the stated primary design, and printing pivots is the bare minimum of the visualization requirement.

The memo would be a solid draft if it had stayed at “descriptive DiD is negative relative to deteriorating controls; a single X% claim is not defensible; expand only inside a design that measures queues.” It loses a large amount of credit by converting that draft into a 4–10% causal effect, a 75% confidence rating, and a case-level RCT that the rest of the answer already showed cannot identify the effect.
