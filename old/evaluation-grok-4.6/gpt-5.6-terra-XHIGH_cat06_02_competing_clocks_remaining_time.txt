**7.2**

The answer is a genuine expert forecast: it uses a join-time maximum rather than a sum, keeps queue wait separate from service, respects the weekday/weekend calendars, treats FIFO vs risk-priority as material, and refuses both false precision and an invented rejection probability. It also avoids the named failure modes (unconditional mean-adding, ignoring the weekend, independent anomaly multiplication, a single completion clock). That is why it is not a mid-range answer. It is also not nearly flawless. Several claims are internally inconsistent, slightly wrong, or clearer than the arithmetic underneath them.

**Logical inconsistency (chemistry-rerun central path).**  
The scenario table puts the rerun *central* window at Tuesday morning–Wednesday afternoon, and the tail at Thursday–Friday “if rerun evidence also triggers QA investigation.” The decomposition then says the central timing *excludes* that investigation tail and that investigation cases “merge into the QA-tail rows.” Those cannot both be true. A rerun that only adds triangular(1.0, 1.8, 3.0) laboratory hours, possibly paused at 18:00, shifts a smooth Monday/Tuesday release by hours or one overnight pause—not into a Tuesday–Wednesday *central* band. The table is silently mixing in the 0.30–0.55 investigation branch that the prose claims to have left out.

**Wrong, or at least overstated, dominant clock on the priority-chemistry smooth path.**  
After a Friday start at 16:42–17:30, remaining chemistry work is typically about 1.2–2.0 h against a triangular(1.5, 2.5, 4.5) service law. That is a Monday ~09:15–10:30 completion, not a result that is usually already waiting at the door. No-on-call microbiology is recorded ~08:30–09:30. The two clocks race; chemistry is at least as often the later one. Calling the “Monday microbiology staffing gate” the common priority-case constraint is therefore backwards or, at best, a coin-flip described as a gate. Weekend on-call is also over-weighted for the *smooth* path: incubation is not technically ready until Sunday 12:00–20:00, so Saturday QA hours are unusable and Sunday readout cannot start QA. On-call mainly matters for starting a 20–30 h confirmation clock, not for unlocking an earlier release. The answer has the Sunday-QA-closed fact and never states the sharper implication that Saturday’s QA shift is stranded before readiness.

**Quantile bands are not what they claim to be.**  
With FIFO at ~55%, a Monday-afternoon chemistry join is common; with ~3–6 cases still likely present Monday morning and no guaranteed QA pre-emption, a large share of *smooth* paths already land Tuesday. Exception paths are another 40–60%. A mixture P50-like band that starts Monday 17:30 is the centre of the *good* smooth slice, not the centre of the forecast. The earliest window (Mon 09:30) further requires an almost empty QA queue, sub-median review, and chemistry essentially done at open—stacked tail events, not a “plausible earliest” in the ordinary sense of that phrase. The forecast table’s 09:30–12:30 also disagrees with the scenario table’s 09:30–11:30. At the other end, P90 as Friday–Saturday is only comfortable if laboratory investigations sit near the top of the 3–12% band (itself a high upper bound: overall investigation × 20–40% laboratory share is closer to 2–8%). The P90 / severe-tail cut is mushy.

**Scenario probabilities are framed so as to confuse mass with distinctiveness.**  
“55–75% for routine weekend readout deferral” is just the complement of on-call authorization—the *default* calendar, not a delayed-microbiology scenario. The overlap note is welcome and follows the prompt, but the band still inflates Scenario 3 and double-counts the same Monday-staffing reality already inside the 40–60% smooth path. Laboratory investigation at 3–12% is a wide, optimistic upper envelope presented as if it were on the same footing as the better-constrained query band.

**Arithmetic is asserted more often than shown.**  
The Friday free-at times (15:54–16:24 and 16:42–17:30), the 46–54 h → Sunday 12:00–20:00 incubation window, 8.5 QA hours, and ~3 expected arrivals are correct. The FIFO Monday start “11:30–16:00” is a defensible mode-plus-spread if queued assays share B-417’s service law. Beyond that, QA waiting “1–5 h central, 0–10 h tail,” remaining-work after Friday, and the leap from stage ranges to P50/P80/P90 are qualitative compressions. For a task that asked for transparent approximate arithmetic, there is no single audited chain of the form “join 15:00 → wait 3 h → hit 20:00 close → Tuesday 08:00 + residual service + 0.5 h release.” That makes the optimistic Monday mass hard to defend and hard to falsify.

**Smaller defects that still count under a strict rubric.**  
“Microbiology available by Monday opening *or earlier*” is sloppy: earlier is Sunday and does not move the QA clock. Risk-priority is interpreted as “behind urgent, ahead of ordinary,” which is reasonable, but the sensitivity row (“if it can leapfrog the urgent sample, Friday chemistry becomes plausible”) is then used to smuggle a Friday-completion remark into the smooth-path cell even though min service 1.5 h exceeds residual Friday time whenever B-417 is behind the urgent sample. Documentation-query working hours are mapped onto the QA calendar without a base-case choice between QA hours and document-owner hours—only a sensitivity note. Dependence on the temperature flag is handled well in words and then under-used when the smooth-path 40–60% upper bound is written as if the independent product still applies. Rank 3 in the uncertainty list (micro staffing) is partly a known calendar constraint, not an uncertainty driver of the same kind as the QA path or the sensor gap.

**What remains strong, so the score does not collapse.**  
Assumption ledger, overlap discipline, refusal to invent P(critical defect), explicit \(J=\max(\cdot)\), 18:00 chemistry pause, Sunday QA closure, overdispersed future QA queue, and the temperature-flag update in §6 are all the right objects. The next-event choice is defensible (common-cause residual), and the secondary trigger (first micro readout) is the correct realizing observation. This is a high-competence answer with several real cracks, not a near-miss of a 9.
