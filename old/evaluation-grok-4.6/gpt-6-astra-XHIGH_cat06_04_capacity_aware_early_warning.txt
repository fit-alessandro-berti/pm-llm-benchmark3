**7.3**

The answer is coherent, operationally serious, and hits every required section. It correctly excludes I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05’s stale refresh from I-07’s new log, refuses to treat observational `p_now` as a causal effect, and prices queue externalities instead of ranking by breach probability. Those are the main quality bars. Under a strict reading it is not nearly flawless: a few decision-rule inconsistencies and operational gaps are large enough to move the allocation.

**Decision-theoretic cracks.** The stated gate is `D_low > 0` *and* `S` positive after spillover. Spillover is applied only to `S`. For I-02, `D_low = €950` but `D_low − €1,800 ≈ −€850`. The case therefore fails a true robust check and is still booked because a midpoint-weighted score clears an assumed €1,800 debit by about €25. That debit is not data: it is `6 × 0.02 × €15,000`, with both the exposed values and congestion invented. At `q = 0.03` or `v_bar ≳ €15,200` the same rule drops I-02. Section 3 then shows that the three-case book can be net-negative on both money and expected breaches in the adverse case, which is a strong argument against including the knife-edge third case. A consistent robust policy would book I-04 and I-09 only, keep two free slots (or one reserve plus a contingent hold), and promote I-02 only after a real queue check. Naming I-02 as selected while hanging the call on an unverified average is the central flaw.

Related nits: the score overweights the lower ARR bound (`0.75L + 0.25U`) and then does not stack that conservatism with spillover on `D_low`; full false-alarm burden is charged even when Section 1 says a prevented breach is not a false alarm; calibrated `p` is restated but never used to cap ARR or to check that upper-end effects remain feasible after Enterprise down-correction.

**Operational realism.** The clock schedule assumes both specialists start I-09 at 12:00. That collides with the rest of the policy: I-09 needs language-supported diagnostic review before dispatch; I-07 has 1.5 h left and is assigned “immediate log review”; I-04, I-06, and first-alert validations also need humans. If both seniors are in a swarm, those reviews have no named capacity. Booking I-04 (10 h slack) while leaving I-07 unheld is especially weak: a 45-minute swarm plus remaining diagnosis/resolution/validation against a 1.5 h deadline is already a last-feasible-start problem, and the answer never computes that start time. “Wait” is not abandonment in principle, but here it is not turned into a timed hold against the reserve slot.

The two-refresh rule is also applied unevenly. I-04 is a first alert with ample time for a cheap second refresh, yet it is a firm intervene (only delayed to 13:30 by sequencing). That is allowed by the specialist override, but it makes the persistence rule ornamental for the highest-value pick.

**Impact table.** Arithmetic for the midpoints is auditable and correct (`0.19×60k + 0.24×20k + 0.15×25k = €19,950`, 0.58 expected direct reductions; costs and the 18q spillover identity check out). Two presentation problems remain. Optimistic/adverse envelopes pair best effects with worst spillovers (and vice versa), so the reported ranges are not coherent scenario outcomes. Adverse `q = 0.03–0.05` is an extra assumption; the prompt’s adverse shock is 15–30 minutes of delay, which the text correctly says cannot be mapped to probability without slack, then maps anyway. Charging €3,400 false-alarm burden in the optimistic column is conservative in a way that contradicts Section 1. These do not make the table unusable, but they overstate how much stress-testing has been done.

**Fairness and monitoring.** Section 5 names the right failure modes and proposes concrete controls (named owner, last-action-window review, logging of exposure, no treated-on-time false-alert label, queue-level randomization because of interference). The “one in four” opportunity rule is then defined so that it need not bind whenever conservative marginal benefit is negative—the same condition that already excludes I-05/I-07/I-10. That is a quota that ratifies value-based starvation rather than constraining it. I-09 is booked despite sparse calibration, which is a defensible anti-exclusion choice, but the ranking still treats its 0.18–0.30 ARR band as comparable in reliability to better-supported segments.

Section 6 is the strongest part: prefix logging, offer vs receipt, `Y(treatment, exposure)`, shift-level randomization, refusal to treat post-policy labels as a clean validation set, and an explicit I-02-style marginal-harm stop. Density is not the same as deployability; frontline staff cannot run this stack without a much shorter checklist.

**Net.** This is a high-quality policy memo with real command of uncertainty, stage eligibility, and spillovers. It is not a 9–10 because the selected set depends on an assumed spillover price, the robustness gate is not applied net of that price, the 12:00 dual-specialist start is incompatible with the review work the same policy demands, and the scenario table mixes bounds while inventing an adverse `q`. Those are not formatting nits; they change who gets a slot.
