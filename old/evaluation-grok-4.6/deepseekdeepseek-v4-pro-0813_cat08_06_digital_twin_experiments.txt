**6.0**

The write-up hits the required eight-section shape and respects the hard constraints: it is not an independent-case toy, calendars and cross-case coupling are in scope, wait/service are not silently collapsed, objections stay latent, PM4Py usage is convention-consistent, and it does not claim optimality. That is a competent design memo, not a near-flawless one. Under a strict reading the reasoning gaps are large enough that it cannot sit in the high band.

**What is solid.** Scope, entities, and queues are the right grain for a DES twin. The estimation plan correctly treats the performance DFG as throughput, not service, and the code path (`pandas` load → `format_dataframe` → variants / DFG / performance DFG → sanitized `parse_process_tree`) is plausible. Assumptions are split into the requested buckets. The experiment table includes baseline, D1–D10, another combination, and four stress cases with stated trade-offs. Uncertainty treatment names CRN, separate streams, quantile bootstrap, and two-level parameter/simulation noise. Validation lists the six required modes. Decision language is interval- and Pareto-based rather than “pick the winner.”

**Logical and design flaws.**

- **Decision criteria fight the data.** Observed medians are about 12–22 / 48 / 68 / 140 days by the main variant groups. Targets of 15 / 55 / 75 / 160 days are *looser than the current process* for several types, while the same section forbids worsening median duration by more than 5%. Inequality signs are also missing (`median  15 days`), so the thresholds are both inconsistent and unreadable. SLA cutoffs (80% in 30/100/150 days) are invented rather than marked as values to elicit from statute or the municipality.

- **Warm-up is confused.** “Initialize queues using the observed distribution of end-of-warm-up backlog” mixes a simulation transient with a historical snapshot. There is no diagnostic (Welch, MSER, queue-length stability through a seasonal cycle), only a flat 12-month discard.

- **Wait/service identification is hand-waved.** Completion-only logs plus time-varying queues do not identify a unique service law. “Bayesian deconvolution / survival models” is named without a likelihood, covariates, or a fallback (e.g., only split when starts exist; otherwise simulate sojourn and validate sojourn). That is the central measurement problem in the brief and it is not solved, only labeled.

- **Control flow is underspecified where it matters.** The given tree has completeness and inspection loops only. The narrative adds planning rework, legal rework, and appeal, plus an AND-join before decision drafting. The twin never states the executable synchronization, priority, or rework-return rules. D5 (“legal pre-check after planning”) sits awkwardly against a legal activity that, in the tree, follows drafting.

- **Cross-case dependence is described, not operationalized.** Architect bursts, latent neighborhood objections, and district batching appear in scope and assumptions, but there is no concrete generator (cluster/Hawkes, spatial latent event, batch-window algorithm), no KPI for first-in-batch delay vs travel saving, and **no validation** that simulated dependence matches the log. That is a major miss given the problem statement.

- **The experiment matrix is a list, not a design.** Effectiveness knobs (30/50% loop cut, 20/40% objection cut) are free numbers, not tied to elicitation or estimation. Policies are not crossed with stress in a comparable way. D10’s interactions (D2 removes inspections that D3/D4 then “improve”) are ignored. The table has no response columns.

- **UQ is computationally unserious.** “M parameter draws × 50–200 replications” with 48-month runs is unbounded. Heavy-tailed means still get t-intervals. Multiple comparisons across D1–D10 are ignored.

- **Decision frame omits the interventions’ real costs.** D1 shifts effort to applicants; D2 skips site control; D4 is a hired FTE. None of applicant effort, missed-defect risk, or staff cost appears in the selection rule. Utilization bands (70–85%) are generic.

- **Skeleton is long, not deep.** The excerpt dump is fine; every engine method is `NotImplementedError`. There is no stub intensity, no calendar predicate, no batching state, and `main()` never touches experiments. Acceptable as a sketch, not evidence the design is implementable.

**Net.** Constraint-compliant and well structured, with real PM grounding and a usable outline. Too many internal contradictions, unidentified parameters, and untestable cross-case claims for a high score. A strong answer would have made wait/service and dependence *estimable and validatable*, aligned targets to the 18-month baselines, and specified an executable control-flow and experiment budget.
