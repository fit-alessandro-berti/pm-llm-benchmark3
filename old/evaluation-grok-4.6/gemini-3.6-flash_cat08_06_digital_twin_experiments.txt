**5.2**

The write-up is complete in outline and sometimes well structured, but it is not nearly a sound digital-twin design. Under a strict reading of the constraints and the methodological claims, the failures are material, not cosmetic.

**Constraint violations.** The runnable engine is an independent-case Monte Carlo: each case draws a lognormal lead time, D2 is a 0.65 multiplier, and `event_queue`, calendars, queues, batching, rework, and resource contention are never used. That directly contradicts “do not design a simple independent-case simulation only.” Calendars are described, then ignored in execution. The excerpt has a single timestamp per event; the code builds only `duration_from_prev` and stores it as `accumulated_service`, collapsing wait and service without an in-code limitation. Missing objections are treated as an “unbiased proxy” via notice duration > 14 days—exactly the overclaim the prompt forbids. The skeleton never computes a case arrival average. The parsed tree is a toy fragment, not the given tree. `pm4py.discover_process_tree(df)` is not the standard API (`discover_process_tree_inductive`). `discover_performance_dfg` is treated as edge → seconds; in common PM4Py versions the value is a stats mapping, so `duration / 3600.0` is not convention-faithful.

**Simulation design.** Section 1 lists useful entities and three cross-case mechanisms, but omits AND-join/token state for Planning/Fire/Environment, Decision drafting’s required synchronization, council-week legal freezes, inspector no-shows, planner specialization, and Monday portal-support dedication. Cross-case behavior is asserted, not specified as an event protocol (who holds the batch, when a spillover updates other cases, how architect bursts attach to existing WIP).

**Process-mining grounding.** NHPP, variants/DFG, and an EM story for missing starts are the right headings. Identification is hand-waved: start times missing by resource are not innocently MAR; wait/service deconvolution from completions plus reconstructed calendars is only weakly identified. No plan for travel by district, no-shows, council effects, `has_prior_violations` (required by D2, absent from the log), building-area fire triggers, or dependence parameters from a flattened log. Completeness-rework vs missing-document loop vs appeal is not mapped to the given tree.

**Assumptions.** Several are convenient rather than honest. Peak/off-peak coded as 1.7 vs 0.7 (≈2.43×), not the stated 1.7× January–February. Day-of-week multipliers are not renormalized, so the 57/day base is not the mean rate. Geometric rework decay and $S_{rework}=\alpha S_{initial}$ are ungrounded. “Stationary variance ratios” undercuts the non-stationary brief. Arrival volume independent of process speed sits poorly next to D1/D7.

**Experiments.** The D1–D10 + three-stress table is the strongest section, then it invents effect sizes (35% fewer inspections, 25% less travel, 5–10 days, 40% flatter Mondays, 30% env relief) as if already known. That is the same sin as claiming results from an uncalibrated model. No interaction design beyond D10, no D10×stress, no calendar/holiday stress, and the code’s “D10” drops D4 and runs 180/30 days against a 540/60 protocol.

**Uncertainty.** Seeded warm-up plus Welch “steady state” is the wrong theory for seasonal, terminating, calendar-driven demand. Truncating fitted tails at the 99.5th percentile while the decision problem is p90/p95 and D9 is a tail intervention is self-defeating. Student-t intervals on means do not cover medians, SLA rates, or quantiles. Forty replications are thin for Complex-variance tails. Sobol is named, not specified. CRN and input bootstrap are appropriate ideas left unconnected to the actually coded generator.

**Validation.** “100% token replay / 100% conformance” is not credible given rare variants, a simplified tree, and a flattened log. KS $p>0.05$ as a pass/fail rule is statistically illiterate at $n\approx 31$k (and failure-to-reject is not validation). Utilization ±5% against HR calendars is partly circular because those calendars were not mined from the log. No protocol for validating wait/service splits or cross-case effects that the log systematically underrepresents.

**Decision criteria.** Invented SLAs are tighter than the historical variant table (simple missing-docs median 22d vs 95% in 14d; new-residential fire+notice median 48d vs median ≤35d and 90% in 45d; complex median 140d vs ≤75d). Those thresholds reject the as-is system and most plausible interventions by construction. End-of-horizon WIP ≤ WIP(0) is meaningless on a seasonal clock. Utilization 70–85% ignores structurally idle fire/legal calendars. The section frames “optimal” selection from an unvalidated twin.

**Code quality.** Imports (`heapq`), `ResourceCalendarManager`, and the event queue are theater. Policy evaluation prints fake comparative metrics. Architect mix is uniform, not 31% in the top 20. Routing flags are hard-coded by type, not estimated. The skeleton is modular only in names; it is not a coherent separation of a PM4Py estimation layer from a dependency-aware DES.

What remains creditworthy is the section discipline, the NHPP/thinning sketch, the intent to separate applicant delay from staff service, CRN, temporal holdout, and fairness/robustness as criteria. That is a competent outline with several disqualifying scientific and constraint failures, not a near-flawless design.
