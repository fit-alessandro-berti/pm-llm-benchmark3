The answer is a strong, professionally structured conformance opinion: it uses the required headings, separates stipulated facts from authority/scope/logging disputes, argues both sides without straw men, traces claims to cases and aggregates, and proposes a layered model rather than a single fitness score. ## What is actually strong

M0’s language is read correctly. \(L(M0)=R(SQ\mid QS)(MA\mid J)NC\) matches the POWL: unordered `{Sanctions_Check, Quality_Review}` before an XOR of `(Manual_Procurement_Approval → Activate_Supplier)` versus `Reject_Supplier`, then notify, then close. The observation that M0 therefore requires quality before rejection is right, and it is the right lever for SUP-709. The claim that, on raw labels, only SUP-701, SUP-712, and SUP-715 fit M0 is also right, including the important point that sequence fit still leaves SUP-712 noncompliant on the resource rule.

The burden-of-proof section is the best part of the memo. Distinguishing retrospective proof of breach, proof required to *accept* an exception, and conservative treatment of uncertainty at live authorization is the correct way to avoid both “every deviation is misconduct” and “every overlay is a defense.” The prosecution’s best points are real: PD-3 was not written into CS-9; operator-selected “pilot” is not authority; SUP-703 is independently ineligible; expiry is a control (SUP-705, SUP-714); follow-up on a valid FD-7 entry is not the same as a new entry after expiry; 14/312 is an eligibility slice, not a compliance rate; desirable `Request_More_Info` does not amend CS-9. The defense’s best points are also real: missing loop, rejected-lead/reactivation scope, CRM missingness 2%→29%, SUP-702 vs SUP-706 evidence quality, duplicate register, QA_Clearance equivalence, and trace deviation ≠ business misconduct.

Several precision points are better than typical work on this prompt: EUR 180,000 is exposure, not an FD-7 cap breach; June 15 cannot be blamed on the 1 July CRM cutover; FD-7 states a two-business-day quality deadline and does *not* state a two-business-day manual-approval deadline; eligibility fields ≠ execution of master-data validation; “no disclosure recorded” ≠ “no disclosure occurred.”

## Material defects

**1. MIXED for SUP-702 fights the memo’s own rule.**  
The exception burden is: the proponent must show competent authority, window, eligibility, and retained controls. The memo then refuses to treat FD-7 as authority-disputed, while leaving PD-3 unresolved, and dumps SUP-702 into MIXED. That is more honest than splitting the difference, but it is still a classification failure. On the stated rule, SUP-702 is either `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` with residual master-data/authority caveats (the analogue of SUP-704’s residual spend caveat) or `NONCOMPLIANT` because the exception was not proved. MIXED is allowed by the rubric, and the surrounding prose is not empty, but it is used to hold two incompatible authority stories at once. The same evidence standard is not applied to SUP-704 (justified despite incomplete spend) and SUP-702 (mixed because authority is messy). That is the largest internal crack.

**2. SUP-715 is under-called.**  
CS-9’s four-eyes rule is not the issue here; a disclosure duty is. The file says disclosure was required and none was recorded. Indeterminacy is fair *if* other channels are unknown, but the dominant operational fact is an unevidenced required disclosure on a related-party approval, not a logging outage on the order of SUP-706. `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` is too soft. `NONCOMPLIANT` at medium confidence, or at least a split control-flow/compliance result with disclosure as the live finding, would have matched the prosecution brief the memo itself wrote.

**3. SUP-711’s primary label is the wrong layer.**  
After a provenance-supported dedupe the *business* path is a standard onboarding. The *case as logged* is a data defect. Calling it `COMPLIANT` buries the thing the reviewer is supposed to flag. `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` is wrong because the duplicate is explained; the honest primary tag is a data-quality finding with compliant underlying work, not a clean pass.

**4. Section 7 overclaims verification.**  
The code requires PM4Py (`converter`, `semantics`, `validate_partial_orders`). The memo says PM4Py could not be installed, then that 22 witnesses passed an “independent Petri-net semantics harness.” That harness is not in the artifact; `accepts()` *is* the PM4Py-dependent checker. Those statements cannot all be true. For a deliverable whose job is an executable model, an unreproducible pass/fail claim is a real integrity problem, not a footnote.

## Model and revision problems (strict)

The architecture table (POWL vs DECLARE vs pandas vs org controls) is the right shape. The POWL approximation is mostly defensible, and the explicit “this XOR is not an authorization engine” warning is necessary. Strictly, though:

- Pilot and emergency put `Quality_Review` in an unconstrained parallel with the activation block, so quality-before-sanctions is in the language. That is looser than CS-9/PD-3/FD-7, which constrain sanctions relative to activation and do not make quality a free concurrent sibling of the whole prefix.
- Emergency *forces* manual approval after activation. “May precede” is encoded as “must follow.” Standard route covers the conservative path, but the emergency profile is too tight in one direction and too loose in the other.
- `Request_More_Info` is only concurrent with the pre-decision/pre-auto block. Post-activation evidence requests on pilot/emergency paths are inexpressible. That is an admitted approximation, but it is still a gap relative to the operational pattern the memo defends.
- Mid-process withdrawal is described in prose and omitted in the net (only early `Withdraw_Request` after register).
- `Master_Data_Validation` is mandatory on the pilot profile and absent from every observed trace. That is a legitimate prospective control, but as a historical evaluator it will fail every real PD-3 case unless the overlay silently supplies the event—the very “fill” the memo says it will not do. The data proposal never specifies how to represent “control required, event type not yet logged.”
- `M1_CANDIDATE` is the union of all routes. Without a binding `CasePolicyAssignment`, it would accept SUP-705/SUP-714-shaped traces. The prose says not to use it that way; the artifact still presents it as the revised model.

None of these make the revision unusable. They do mean the PM4Py object is a route catalogue plus tests, not a closed normative semantics.

## Smaller inaccuracies and unclarities

- Treating FD-7 as “facially effective” and PD-3 as “authority unproved” is argued, but PD-3 is also a dated steering-committee decision with a monitored-exception note. The later observer caveat is not a finding that the minutes are a nullity. The memo never quite says what legal instrument *would* count if minutes plus email do not.
- Confidence is sometimes a compound (“high on applicability; medium on safeguards”) while severity sometimes tracks residual uncertainty rather than established breach. That blurs the memo’s own separation of exposure, harm, and culpability.
- The leftover `sandbox:/mnt/data/...` path and raw GitHub file citations do not support any claim in the record.
- Aggregate 4.49%/92.63%/2.88% arithmetic is fine; using it only as eligibility, not fitness, is correct. The system-level paragraph still slightly undersells the 14 ineligible and 6 August auto-approvals as *pattern* evidence of failed gates, even while correctly refusing to treat 312 as a population rate.

## Bottom line

This is a high-quality opinion on dialectics, M0 semantics, and evidence hygiene. It would be a 9-range answer if SUP-702 were classified under the memo’s own exception rule, SUP-715 were not parked as indeterminate, SUP-711 were labeled at the data layer, and the POWL section did not assert a test run that the code cannot have performed without PM4Py. Those are not cosmetic issues. They are exactly the authority, classification, and model-trust problems the prompt asked the reviewer to handle cleanly.
