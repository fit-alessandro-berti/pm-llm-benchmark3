**6.2**

The write-up is a competent methods plan, not a nearly flawless analysis. It meets the heading contract, puts estimands before estimators, refuses to treat withdraw/reject as ordinary censoring, and correctly flags escalation as post-baseline (immortal time / leakage). Those are the main reasons it is not in the 4–5 range. It is kept well below a high score by concrete errors in required examples, a degenerate sensitivity implementation, and several evidence-handling mistakes.

**Required example is wrong.** For estimand C (no terminal internal disposition / still open at 72h), S03 (resolved at 76h) is marked “Not open at 72 h.” At hour 72 that case is still in process and only resolves four hours later. That is exactly the contribution the prompt asked to spell out. The same table treats S08’s Est A coding as “assuming unresolved” without carrying the three transfer assumptions through the example.

**Counts from the supplied table are off.** Resolved completed cases are 13, not “$n \approx 14$.” Open cases already past 72h are four (S09, S11, S25, S26), not five. The “completed-case mean ≈ 47h” is an unsourced and slightly wrong figure (sum of the 13 resolved durations is 647h, mean ≈ 49.8). Section 7 then lists S10, S24, S29, and S31 under “open cases above 72h,” which they are not.

**Estimand A is sloppy and makes the promised sensitivity vacuous.** $\Pr(\text{not observed Resolve}\mid t\le 72)$ is not a well-formed survival/CIF estimand and, if read literally, would drop cases whose observation time exceeds 72h (including S09). As implemented, “not resolved by 72” is 1 for every transfer, so T1/T2/T3 cannot move Est A. The code then sets `fail_T1`, `fail_T2`, and `fail_T3` to the same vector and even comments that the comparison is a no-op. The required sensitivity comparison is therefore not demonstrated.

**Process model is incomplete relative to the prefixes.** The state diagram omits observed transitions `Screen→Reject` (S05, S23), `Screen→Withdraw` (S12), and `Escalate→Review` (S29). That is a process-mining miss on a small, fully listed log.

**Section 8 is scaffolding, not an analysis.** Event-code columns are created, but there is no Kaplan–Meier, Aalen–Johansen, risk-set process, or CIF. `lifelines` is only imported. Stratification is crude `groupby` means. Timestamps of `2000-01-01 + k hours` are fabricated despite the explicit ban; labeling them “synthetic” does not satisfy the constraint. `parse_event_log_string` is unused (equivalent construction is allowed, but the fake times were not required for `get_variants`).

**Other consistency nits.** Est C’s formula mixes Open with “Transferred-at-72h” even though the transfers occur at 26/68/96h. Cohort interpretation in §7 hedges “B lower or higher” without using the obvious counts (resolved by 72h: A 4/15, B 5/17). T1 vs T3 are described as substantively different in §2/§6 and then coded identically.

What remains strong is the conceptual spine: AJ/multi-state as primary vs KM as sensitivity, three named transfer assumptions, landmarking for alerts, and a clear warning not to put `escalated` or `rework_count` in as baseline covariates. That is enough for a mid-6, not more, under a strict bar.
