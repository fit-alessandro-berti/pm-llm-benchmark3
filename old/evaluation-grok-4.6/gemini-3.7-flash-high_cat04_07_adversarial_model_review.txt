6.8

The submission is a competent governance review with the right shape: use-case separation, no metric averaging, correct probe reasoning on S and C, a genuinely stricter third control-flow backbone, and a real dissent. It is not close to flawless. Several claims overreach the evidence, the third model smuggles in policy the dossier does not contain, and the validation program does not do what it says it does.

**What is actually strong.** Sections 1–5 are the best part. The four use cases and the fitness/precision trade-off are stated in operational terms, not slogans. The metric table treats every reported number as evidence with a comparability defect, and the refusal to collapse scores is correct. The S/C language analysis is mostly exact: S accepts B3/B4 via `X(·, tau)` inside `+`; C rejects B2/B5 because Auto Approval exists only on the `CC → TR` branch and never with Supplier/Expedite; both reject B6 and B8 for the right structural reasons. T1–T6 are real admitted traces, not paraphrases. The dissent in §11 is within the word bound and uses F8/B5 as a genuine alternative philosophy rather than a polite caveat.

**Technical overclaims.** Training fitness of 0.992 is not “trivial because checks are optional.” If the training log is dominated by complete variants, any cover of those variants scores well; optionalization mainly inflates holdout fitness and hides missing events. C’s 0.744 drop is attributed almost entirely to combinatorial omission; F4 also reports missing Supplier Review, which would punish any model that treats SR as required on some paths. Calling S a “flower-like” system is loose: it is a single AND of four skippable activities, not a redo-XOR over the alphabet. “Fail alignment” for Correct Dossier on S is sloppy; the trace is simply outside the language and would take log moves.

**Third model: better than S and C, not the semantics advertised.** The backbone correctly makes `+(Credit Check, Technical Review)` non-skippable, parks Expedite after both checks, and uses `X(Manager, Auto)` plus a `*(tau, redo)` correction loop. That is the right design family. Problems:

- P6 is marked “Fully Enforced.” The loop only offers `{CC, TR, both}`. Without `affected_checks` it cannot enforce “only the affected checks.” If the affected activity is Supplier Review, the model still forces a credit or technical recheck. That is a hard semantic gap, not a footnote.
- ADV3 / premature Supplier Review is treated as forbidden policy. P3 does not order Supplier Review after the mandatory checks; that is an interpretive extra presented as a requirement.
- After correction, optional SR/ER may repeat unboundedly. The same loop that repairs P6/P7 is a precision leak (repeated Expedite, extra Supplier Review) that is never audited.
- Duplicate labels are used for CC/TR/SR/ER and never acknowledged, after §4 treats duplication as disqualifying for C. Rework copies are more defensible than eight-way unfolding, but the review never says so.
- The text claims an “equivalent POWL model” and never builds one (`Transition` / `StrictPartialOrder` are unused).
- `declarative_constraints` invents `order_value < 50000` and `has_risk_flags`. Those rules are not in P1–P7. A governance model must not mint approval policy. The lambdas are also not DECLARE, not executable, and not attached to any checker. The P6 default (`affected_checks` missing ⇒ both checks) contradicts the tree, which allows a single recheck.

**Probes and audit table.** B1–B8 vs S and C are right. ADV2 is written without `Register Dossier` while scored as accepted; the intended full trace is obvious, but a behavior-audit table cannot drop the start event. P3 for the third model is “constrained optional,” which is honest; P6 in the same table is not.

**Validation is a prospectus, not a program.** §9.1 promises attribute-stratified splits, missing/duplicate-event perturbations, unseen-combination holds, and alignment-cost sensitivity. The skeleton stratifies only on `len(t)`, never perturbs, never changes move costs, and never holds out a feature combination. `check_soundness` is stored as a boolean in §7 and indexed as `soundness_diag[0]` in §9. Unused `ProcessTree`/`Operator` imports and a hidden `sklearn` dependency are small, but they sit next to a larger integrity problem: the rejection rule “holdout fitness drop > 0.05” contradicts §2 and F4. A normative model *should* lose fitness on missing-event holdout; that rule would favor S-style optionalization, which the rest of the review correctly condemns. Negative-suite rejection at 1.0 is the one decision rule that matches the enforcement use case. Stakeholder “protocol” is a sentence (response time and error rate) with no tasks, roles, or sample.

**Governance.** Retiring S for enforcement is justified. “Retire C permanently” and “neither for exploration” are stronger than the evidence. C is a bad asset; a refactored whitelist is exactly what the dissent later treats as live. Exploration can use a labeled, non-normative view of S if the skips are disclosed. Pre-deployment gates (schema fields, negative suite) are the right kind of condition; the 0.05 fitness gate is not.

Net: this is a strong exam answer with a real third design and accurate S/C languages. Under a strict reading it still asserts policy it was not given, overstates what the third model enforces, and specifies an evaluation it does not implement. That keeps it well below the near-flawless band.
