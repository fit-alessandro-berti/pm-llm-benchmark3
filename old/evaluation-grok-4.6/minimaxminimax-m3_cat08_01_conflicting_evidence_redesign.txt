**6.4**

The write-up is well structured, segment-aware, and clearly not a generic Lean dump. The three redesigns are materially different, the contradiction is named, and most required artifacts appear. Under a strict reading it is still not close to flawless. Several quantitative and rule-level errors are material, not cosmetic.

**What holds up.** The diagnosis correctly identifies the volume spine (`Intake → Auto → Fast approval → Dispatch`, ~2,300/5,000), the 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen split, the 72h/96h/p90-180h complaint tails, replay fitness 0.91 with only 68% fitting traces, R1/R2 alignment deviations, the 34h/p90-88h evidence loop plus 26% repeat-request rate, the 22%/140h unclear-evidence segment, the 31%→9% cheapness collapse after rework, and the resource mismatch (Fraud/risk of 2, Manual cliff at 82%, idle Quality check). A/B/C are distinct mechanisms (routing, evidence front-loading, pre-dispatch gate). Sacrifices are stated. The table does not invent a unique Pareto winner. Failure modes and the staged “is it real or shifted downstream?” tests are the strongest part of the piece.

**Material defects.**

1. **Redesign A breaks the rule it claims to fix.** R1 requires Manual *or* Fraud/risk review before Fast approval on every claim above EUR 400. “Standard fast” still sends Standard low-risk accessories above EUR 400 to Fast approval after an evidence token only. That is an R1 violation, in the redesign whose selling point is compliance. Express also sends Standard low-risk “straight to Fast approval”; R2 is only rescued by a later sentence about a dispatch token, so the path description is internally inconsistent.

2. **Segment share is multiplied by path-conditional rates as if they were volumes.** “14% × 22% ≈ 150 cases on the wrong fast path” confuses Premium high-value *share* with *fast-path complaint rate*. The 22% and 18% figures apply only to cases already on the fast path; the prompt does not give the fast/reviewed split inside each segment. The same move produces the “~340 high-value complaint cases” and the EUR 410 payback story. Those counts are not supported.

3. **The 11,200h claim misreads the portal lever.** The prompt says a portal can cut *repeated* evidence requests 30–45%. The text treats that as a 30% cut of the 34h `Request missing evidence → Manual` edge on ~1,100 “unclear” cases, then labels them “cases/year.” The 22% unclear segment is not identical to every evidence-loop case; 5,000 cases are not stated as annual volume; and 34h is one edge mean, not recoverable cycle time.

4. **Fast-approval arithmetic is sloppy.** 14.1% is applied to ~2,300 (the dispatch edge). Fast approval throughput is 2,550+240 = 2,790. Direct `Fast approval → complaint` is only 140; the 14.1% is a case-level rate that also includes later `Dispatch → complaint`. Using 2,300 × 14.1% ≈ 324 as if it were a clean count overstates precision.

5. **Stage 1 stop rule is incoherent.** Standard high-value fast-path median is already 28h. The gate adds 6–18h. “Halt if median cycle time on gated cases exceeds 24h” would halt the pilot immediately. That is an experiment-design error, not a rounding issue.

6. **Overclaim where the data only support direction.** “Eliminating” the high-value no-risk-review deviation; “pays back the gate cost multiple times over”; a 60% model-below-threshold split; a 2–4h fraud touch; Quality check inspectors absorbing *fraud/risk* work in B (different skill, EUR 38 vs EUR 52). These are presented as mechanisms with implied magnitudes, not as untested design bets. C also largely restates the prompt’s own pre-dispatch-gate lever.

**Smaller issues that still count.** “Dominated by C on quality” is not Pareto language and muddies the required column. Composite “A+C plus B” is sensible but blurs the three-alternative comparison. Escalation-board R3 risk (120 cases, some going straight to dispatch/deny) is unused. Repair rework (12.5% on high-value) is diagnosed and then dropped. NPS is imported. Precision of the discovered model is unused.

**Bottom line.** This is a competent executive memo with real process-mining texture and three non-isomorphic bets. It is not a 8–10 answer: the compliance redesign violates R1, several headline numbers are illegitimate products of incompatible artifacts, and the first pilot’s halt threshold contradicts the same performance DFG the author cites. Those are reasoning failures, not missing polish.
