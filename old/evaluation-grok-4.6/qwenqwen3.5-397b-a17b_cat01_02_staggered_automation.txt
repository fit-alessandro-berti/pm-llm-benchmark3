**5.2**

The answer has the right skeleton and the right headline instinct: a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim is not defensible, availability is not use, rollout is endogenous, and the next step should be expansion with an embedded evaluation. Format compliance is mostly good. That is not enough. Under a strict reading there are material quantitative errors, an internal identification contradiction, missing required elements, and code that does not implement the design the text proposes.

**What is done well.** Section 1 separates availability, intensity, selection, co-occurring shocks, and measurement limits, and stays mostly descriptive. The estimand list hits the required types (ITT, intensity, median, tail, quality, CSAT, dynamics). The threats table is the strongest part: selection, non-parallel trends, intensity, fraud-desk spillovers, staffing, case mix, anticipation, survey nonresponse, aggregation, small clusters, and dynamics are all present with plausible bias directions. Mechanisms are process-aware rather than generic DiD talk. The claim ladder correctly puts an absolute “reduces cycle time by X%” statement in `Not supported`. The rollout choice is the only defensible one given this design.

**Serious analytical failures.** Gamma is the cleanest staggered contrast in the file, and the answer mishandles it. Defining Gamma’s pre-period as Jan–Mar ignores Apr–Jun, when Gamma was still untreated and had already absorbed the May complexity shock (medians 45.4/45.9, p90 100/101). July–August then looks like “no change” versus 37.5 instead of a large improvement versus the relevant pre-window. That window choice reverses Gamma’s qualitative story for both median and tail. A lead analyst cannot do that in a staggered-adoption setting after defining “transparent windows.”

The DiD logic is applied inconsistently. For the median, treated branches “worsened less than controls,” so automation looks relatively helpful. For p90, the answer scores a harmful effect with confidence 70 from raw treated pre/post (+5% to +15%). Controls’ p90 rose by more (Delta roughly +24% on the same Apr–Aug window). If the median comparison is informative, the tail comparison cannot be read off treated levels alone. Rework and escalation have the same problem: both move in never-treated branches after March/May. The effect table therefore does not follow from the identification strategy.

Numbers do not agree with each other. Section 5’s optimistic case is “~18% relative benefit,” obtained by subtracting percentage changes rather than taking a level DiD (Alpha −0.4 hours vs Delta +6.0 hours). Section 7 then gives −5% to +5% absolute and −10% relative. Section 10 says 10–15% relative. Those cannot all be the same estimand. Epsilon’s Apr–Aug median mean is 44.74, not 44.9; the 11.4% figure is downstream of that slip. The intensity “analysis” never reports a slope, a binned contrast, or even a correlation.

**Identification and estimands are thinner than they look.** The prompt required interference and treatment-version discussion in the estimand section; it is absent, despite a shared fraud desk, a routing campaign that sent extra volume *to treated branches*, and a 4% automated-fast-path rate in never-treated months (a treatment-version/measurement issue). The primary strategy is labeled “staggered DiD / event study” but never specifies cohort-specific effects, not-yet-treated vs never-treated, time FE, or TWFE negative weights. Adjusting for `arrivals` is a real mistake: routing was assigned because of adoption, so arrivals are post-treatment. Synthetic control with two donors is correctly dismissed; that does not make the primary design well specified.

**Section 9 fails stated requirements.** Trends are not shown for the five outcomes; only median cycle time is plotted, with an explicit shrug about the rest. `relative_time` is built and then unused. The fitted model is branch-FE OLS with no calendar-time FE and no event-time indicators, i.e., not the primary strategy. The intensity model is estimated only on `automation==1` rows, so it cannot separate intensity from calendar time or learning. Panel validation is essentially nonexistent. Cluster warnings are present and appropriate; they do not rescue the specification.

**Smaller but real defects.** “Step-wedge cluster randomized design within Delta” is a misnomer for a within-branch 50% case-level experiment; shared specialists and the fraud desk make that design interference-heavy, which is underplayed. Expanding only Delta first also learns the least about Epsilon, whose case mix is closer to the original targets. CSAT is correctly treated as weakly identified. Confidence 70 on p90 harm is overconfident given the DiD contradiction.

**Net.** This is a complete exam answer with the correct policy posture and a good threat/mechanism frame, not a nearly flawless causal assessment. The missed Gamma contrast, the median-vs-p90 inconsistency, the floating effect sizes, the omitted interference discussion, and the non-implementing code are enough to keep the score near the middle even before the “hypercritical / nearly flawless only” bar. A high score would require internally consistent estimands, correct staggered windows, matching arithmetic, and code that actually estimates the design claimed in Section 3.
