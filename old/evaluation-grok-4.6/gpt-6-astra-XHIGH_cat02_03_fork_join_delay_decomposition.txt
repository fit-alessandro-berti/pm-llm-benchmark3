The answer is analytically strong: definitions are max-based and additive, the case table and the four worked examples match the log, double-counting of synchronization is shown rather than asserted, influence is separated from mean service, and the three required counterfactuals correctly assign zero benefit when another branch remains last. Remaining defects are real and keep it from a top score.

**What is right.** Upstream, queue, service, join, downstream, and flow-time identities hold for all 12 cases (cohort totals 235 + 732 + 176 + 240 = 1,383). Critical branches are F01/F11 = A, F04/F08 = C, and B otherwise, with no observed ties. Exclusive margins (A 15, B 120, C 12) match the A−20% join gains and the “eliminate B queue” bound of 120. B-queue −50% join savings of 92.5, including caps on F05/F10 and the 11:02:30 F09 join, are correct. Downstream masking is treated as a modeling choice (fixed final starts vs carried lag vs FCFS), not as a fact in the log.

**Defects.**

1. The F03/F1 idle argument is slightly overstated. F1 is free 09:28–10:00, but F03 is join-ready only at 09:45, so the relevant idle is 15 minutes, not the full 32-minute hole. The sentence states the ready time, but the cited window still mixes ineligible idle with eligible idle.

2. Section 7 is too strong on branch capacity. Exact staffing is not in the log, but exclusive-resource *lower bounds* are: A starts every 15 minutes with 50-minute intervals, so peak A concurrency is 4; B intervals overlap across cases as well. That bound is identifiable and unused.

3. The required PM4Py block is fragile. `pm4py.get_service_time` is invoked with `activity_key` and `case_id_key`, which several public signatures do not accept. A “reproducible” script that can fail on the mandated library call is a concrete flaw, even if the pandas path is self-contained.

4. `critical_branch` in the published case table is a single A-B-C label, against the section 1 rule that the critical set is retained. Harmless here only because there are no baseline ties; the convention and the table do not match.

5. The 171-minute FCFS figure is internally consistent under the closed-cohort, two-pooled-resource, work-conserving story, but it is easy to read as measured dispatch loss. The log does not identify the policy; that distinction is in the prose more than in the summary tables.

None of these reverse the main conclusion that B, not A, dominates join readiness and that speeding a noncritical branch need not move case completion. They are enough, under a strict bar, to deny a near-perfect grade.
