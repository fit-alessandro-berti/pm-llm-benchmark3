## What it gets right

April is treated as a schema/pipeline incident, not a business collapse: `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY` 17%, missing queue 14%, unmapped 18%, predicted risk 0.28 vs observed 0.19, AUROC 0.67 / ECE 0.110, with May’s partial rebound used as evidence that operations did not suddenly break. June is correctly *not* called concept drift: within-variant rates stay flat (V1 0.16, V2 0.09, V3 0.35) while V2 mix jumps to 52% and the headline rate falls to 0.15. August–September are treated as overlapping process, concept, and population effects (parts edge 130–148 h, V3 matured breach 0.64 / 0.61, Partner Z 24% and 58% V3). October is not treated as comparable to mature months: 55% maturity, 52-day delay, and a downward-biased 0.16 rate against a still-broken 155 h parts edge. That four-way split is the core of the rubric.

Section 3’s competing-hypothesis structure is the strongest part of the paper. Fixed-vintage maturity curves, relabel-and-recompute, variant/source stratification, performance-DFG edges, residual \(P(Y \mid X, \text{variant})\), Partner Z reweighting, and lineage/missingness are all present, and most tests state two expected outcomes. Section 6’s four failure modes match the asked set and the mitigations are operational, not slogans.

## Material analytical flaws

**June is miscoded.** The table and timeline call June “pure covariate drift.” The case is a remote-routing *policy* change that alters variant mix. That is process drift (control-flow / routing) that *induces* a \(P(X)\) shift. Covariate drift is the downstream statistical symptom, not the primary diagnosis. The rubric’s preferred reading — “routing/process mix that need not imply concept drift” — is only half-adopted. Process drift is then narrowed to Jul–Oct edge latency, so the June mix shift and the July supplier delay are forced into different boxes that do not match the prompt’s own timeline language.

**July and August are flattened.** July is 98% mature, AUROC 0.76, observed 0.19 vs predicted 0.18, parts edge 65 h, V3 0.46. That is onset. August is the break: 130 h, V3 0.64, AUROC 0.68, ECE 0.120, 0.28 vs 0.19. Treating Jul–Aug as one “concept drift” block overstates July and understates how cleanly August is the first month where \(P(Y \mid X_{\text{diagnosis}})\) is clearly broken. The unused Remote Fix edge (stable 3.8–4.6 h all year) would have sealed June as mix-not-degradation and isolated the failure to the parts edge; it is never used.

**October is put inside concept drift with High confidence.** Concept drift is a claim about \(P(Y \mid X)\). October’s \(Y\) is not observed (55% mature, long paths selectively missing). Continuing 155 h latency is *process* evidence that the world has not healed; it is not evidence that a measurable concept-drift metric deteriorated in October. All six rows are “High.” That erases the uncertainty the prompt asked the auditor to keep.

**September’s observed rates are over-literal.** 82% mature, Partner Z labels mature more slowly, and the mature-by-variant table already shows V3 at 0.61. The 0.45 vs 0.27 gap is mostly mix plus the shared supplier shock (a back-of-envelope with 58% vs 36% V3 nearly reproduces it). The write-up notes overlap, then still promotes population shift as a high-confidence primary driver and treats 0.31 / 0.45 as if they were mature facts in the narrative.

**Causal overclaim on August V3 share.** Supplier delay makes V3 *worse*; it does not by itself raise V3’s share from 22% to 35%. That mix change is unexplained and should have been labeled as such.

## Self-contradictions in the remediation plan

This is the largest logical failure.

- Phase 2 recalibrates on *mature June–July*. June is the V2 mix month; July is only the start of the parts shock. That window cannot correct August–September V3 under-prediction or Partner Z. The live problem and the calibration cohort do not match.
- The ASCII plan retrains on “Jan–Jul + mature Aug/Sep.” The prose then marks **Aug–Sep as unsafe**. August is **93%** mature and the V3 0.64 figure is from the *matured* table. Quarantining August with October (55%) is disproportionate and contradicts the author’s own Phase 4.
- Section 1’s concept-drift action is “retrain on post-July data.” Section 4 forbids those months. A decision memo that cannot agree with itself on the training window is not operationally reliable.
- A blanket +0.25 V3 offset is uncalibrated containment. No clip, no segment (Partner Z vs legacy), no statement that this is a temporary override on *scores* rather than a substitute for fixing \(X\).

Jan–Mar safe and Apr–May safe only after relabel/dedup/recompute are correct. October exclusion is correct. The rest of the cohort policy is confused.

## Section 5: architecture is thin; code misses the spec

Alert suppression when maturity \(< 85\%\) or schema error \(> 2\%\) is the right idea and is the one part of the architecture that is actually specified. “Intervention/deployment” is a box labeled suppression, not a monitor of override uptake, V3 multipliers, or shadow-vs-prod. There is no rule that a process-DFG alarm must not auto-retrain, or that a population alarm first triggers a feature audit.

The code requirement list is explicit. The sketch does **not**:

- build an **event-calendar** cohort (only case-start `groupby`); a comment is not an implementation, and the different interpretations are not explained;
- call `pm4py.filter_time_range` (the natural calendar-cohort primitive);
- call `pm4py.discover_dfg` (only `discover_performance_dfg`);
- call `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe` despite promising normalization “before feature extraction.”

Three of the five named APIs are absent. `get_variants` is used as `len(cases)` on dict values; in current PM4Py those values are often integer counts, so this is a likely runtime bug. Variant typing by substring (`Remote Fix` / `Order Part` / `Dispatch`) is heuristic and will mis-count hybrids. Performance-DFG `stats['median']` assumes a dict-of-stats API that is version-dependent. Unknown-activity share is computed *after* remapping, so the April pipeline signature the auditor spent two sections on would be partly washed out of the audit extract. This is not a small style nit; it fails the stated contract for the only required artifact.
