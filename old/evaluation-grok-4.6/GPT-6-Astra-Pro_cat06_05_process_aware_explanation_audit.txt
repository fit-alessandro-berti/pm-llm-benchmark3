## Defects that block a high score

**1. Revised local explanations still rest on the leaky explainer.**  
Section 4 keeps original SHAP signs for `initial_damage_complexity` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`, and keeps region’s +0.12 as an “admissible candidate.” The caveats (“provisional,” “signs may change”) are honest, but they do not make those directions credible. In a model dominated by `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `full_trace_variant_id`, and closure duration, remaining attributions are not identified. Listing X’s −0.03 elapsed term as one of two “credible drivers” is especially weak: that magnitude is noise-scale in a contaminated additive explanation. A strict revision should have given **unsigned, qualitative** prefix facts, or refused directions until a prefix-only refit.

**2. Congestion is preserved as a class and then dropped as a case-level signal.**  
The task’s quality bar is to preserve legitimate congestion and prefix-age signals. Queue length is the main congestion feature, “normally valid within ±5 minutes,” and is a large local driver (X: +0.14; Y: −0.05). Flagging a symmetric join as **high** and omitting it from both `credible_drivers` lists over-corrects. The right move was to keep it as a process-state driver with an explicit one-sided-join caveat, not to treat the reported values as if they were in the same bin as `final_decision`. Prefix-age is kept only for X, and only because the leaky explainer happened to emit it.

**3. Severity is flattened.**  
Every sequence pattern is `critical`, including `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened`, which is unobserved but conditionally enabled after the landmark. The Why-text distinguishes it; the severity column does not. `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` are `high` solely from missing lineage notes, while `claimant_region` is `medium` because a note exists. That is methodologically consistent and still too coarse: names plus insurance-process priors make registration-time channel/reserve much closer to region than to `last_resource_role`. “High includes unresolved provenance” is a defined rule, but it collapses confirmed future outcomes and missing paperwork into the same operational class.

**4. “Credible_drivers” for Y is an audit remainder, not a process-aware explanation.**  
After stripping leakage, Y is essentially “region R4, maybe, subject to fairness review.” That is safer than repeating `REG-COV-DOC-...-APP-DEC-CLOSE`, but it is not a reconstructed `t0` explanation. The shared prefix facts that *are* known for Y (coverage-check landmark, whatever frozen complexity/age the case actually has, congestion **design**) are left unused because they were not in the displayed SHAP list. The JSON therefore still orbits the leaky explanation rather than the prefix.

**5. Small completeness and precision nits.**  
Declarative checks are implemented as guards but never named, despite the prompt listing them. The bibliography is extraneous; one Feast URL carries `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, which does not belong in an audit artifact. PM4Py is cited via a stale docs host. Section 1’s verdict that all three **probabilities** are invalid is right; calling X/Y `recompute` rather than `invalidate` is defensible, but the JSON still implies more retained structure than the evidence supports.

## Why this is not a mid-range score

A weak answer would have accepted full-trace SHAP as an early-warning story, called appeals a cause, treated `Closed -> Assessment` and `Appeal -> Decision` as the same bug, or deleted region because it is correlated. This answer does none of that. The remaining failures are identification discipline in the revised explanations, over-withholding of the congestion signal the rubric asked to keep, and severity compression. Those are enough, under a strict reading, to keep it well below a 9.
