**4.0**

The write-up has the right skeleton and avoids the two explicit trapdoors (do not swarm I-03; do not treat I-05 like I-07). That is not enough. Under a strict decision-theoretic and operational reading it is not deployable, not auditable, and not coherent with its own rule.

**Decision rule is misspecified and not the rule they used.**  
The stated score is \((\text{adjusted }p)\times V\times\text{ARR}-(\text{FA}+450)\). Absolute risk reduction is already the intervention effect; multiplying by \(p\) double-counts probability and is not the quantity the prompt offered. They then never compute that formula. I-01 is written as if the product were €14,500; the actual product is about €1,455 before costs, ~€755 after. I-06’s “score” is just the case value. I-09’s is €30,000 against a true product near €3,300. Several other cells are the same kind of invention. A “deployable” ranking that cannot be reproduced from the stated inputs is a hard fail.

The robustness check is also false as written: \(0.05\times€100{,}000=€5{,}000\) is **not** greater than €5,450. I-08 fails their own lower-bound test; they assert the opposite.

Minus signs are missing throughout (`p_now  p_prev`, `0.66  0.085`), so the persistence rule is not even parseable as an operating procedure.

**Allocation contradicts the ranking, the reserve preference, and portfolio EV.**  
On *their* scores the top four are I-09, I-02, I-06, I-01. They drop the highest-score case and insert I-07 without writing that exception into Section 1. They then burn all four slots and override the 15:00 regulatory reserve because of “high-value cases,” while the fourth slot is a €5,000 SME ticket. That justification is self-refuting.

A correct case-level EV (\(\text{mid ARR}\times V-450-\text{FA}\)) ranks I-04 (~€9k) and I-08 (~€4.5k) well above I-01 and I-07. Waiting on I-04/I-08 can be defended as option value (8–10 h left), but that is not the reason given; they cite “low adjusted \(p\)” as if observational probability were the treatment effect. I-07 is Triage with 1.5 h left. A 45-minute swarm that is useless after Diagnosis Complete is a poor fit for that prefix; this stage×horizon constraint is ignored. I-06 is intervened despite an explicit imputation artifact that the adverse scenario was designed to punish.

Worse, the rule is myopic. The fourth slot’s base spillover is already on the order of \(6\times0.02\times€15{,}000\approx€1{,}800\), before 8–20 minutes of queue delay, against I-07’s direct EV of roughly €800. A coherent portfolio rule would stop at two or three slots and keep the reserve. They never compare marginal benefit to marginal externality.

**Section 3 is the wrong estimand, then inflated by construction.**  
Optimistic “4 breaches avoided” and €54k treats every swarm as preventing a certain breach. Mid-to-upper ARRs on the selected set sum to about **one** expected breach, not four; direct value is on the order of €12k, not €54k. Base “3 breaches, I-01 fails” is arbitrary. Adverse “only I-02” is the same binary fiction. The footnote admits the correct \(\text{ARR}\times V\) arithmetic, multiplies by \(p\) again, then says the table was “scaled up for simplicity.” That is abandoning the required auditable calculation after stating it. Confidence = High on the adverse row is also backwards: a stress case is not a high-confidence forecast.

Spillover arithmetic (\(4\times6\times\pi\times€15{,}k\)) is at least following the prompt’s six-case model, but FA burden is not carried cleanly, specialist interruption of ordinary diagnosis is folded into a lump €450, and non-monetary public-sector/regulatory loss is absent from net impact.

**Repeated-alert policy is directionally right, sloppy in the details.**  
I-05 vs I-07 is the correct qualitative distinction. I-07’s \(\Delta p\) is **−0.03**, not +0.03; new evidence *lowered* risk. “Re-evaluate” is written, “automatically swarm” is what they do. Cool-down and re-entry are usable. The prompt’s hard constraint — no second *swarm* unless a material new event — is never stated as an intervention rule, only as alert suppression.

**Fairness and monitoring are the strongest sections and still leak.**  
They name the required risks and give concrete controls (10% withhold, NNL manual gate, segment audit, delay cap). But the “reserve a slot for \(V<€20{,}k\)” safeguard describes the opposite of their actual portfolio (they starved high-EV strategic cases). Recalibration triggers (€20k base net, €10k/week spillover) are calibrated to the inflated Section 3 numbers and would fire constantly if computed correctly. Calibration patches are applied to \(p\) inside a score that should not contain \(p\); they are not linked to uncertainty in ARR, and NNL is left unadjusted “because sparse,” which is the segment that most needs a conservative interval, not a point pass-through.

**Net.**  
The answer looks like a policy memo and hits the rubric’s obvious checks. It fails the things the rubric says it will actually be judged on: a reproducible objective, honest use of effect ranges as uncertain (not known midpoints, not certain breaches), portfolio-level capacity including the unused-slot option, and arithmetic that an operations lead could audit. Those are not minor presentation issues. They are the decision.
