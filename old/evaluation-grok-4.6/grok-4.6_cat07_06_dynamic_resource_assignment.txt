**6.2**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and rhetorically strong: it hits every required heading, refuses a single optimum, separates safety rules from preferences, and gives workers a non-punitive explanation. That polish does not make the policy sound. Under a strict reading it has a material error on the most important case, rewrites a stated constraint to justify that error, and then describes a scoring system that the code does not actually implement.

**C001 is the decisive failure.** Prompt constraint 5 says high-complexity, high-handover cases stay with the owner unless the owner is skill 0/1 *and supervision is not feasible*. Celine is skill 1 on Security; Gita/Hugo supervision *is* feasible. Constraint 6 explicitly allows skill 1–2 Security under that supervision. Constraint 2 is also satisfied by a named supervisor. The compliant assignment is therefore Celine performing with Hugo or Gita attached — or a true paired/hot handover that starts immediately.

The answer instead invents hard constraint 7 with an extra “or the +3.3h weak-skill delay would miss the SLA” clause that the prompt does not contain, declares a weak-owner override, and gives the case to Hugo after a 15-minute briefing. That contradicts evidence the answer itself treats as binding: owner changes on high-complexity cases add a median **2.7h of waiting**. On a 3h enterprise SLA that leftover is not a Security Review, it is a miss. The risk column (“briefing quality vs 3h SLA”) names the problem without solving it. A 15-minute brief is not a rebuttal to a 2.7h median wait. Both pure options likely miss; the answer never scores the one option that uses the specialist’s eyes *without* paying the full handover tax.

**Other assignment choices are mostly legal, not always coherent.**

- **C003** makes David’s looping diagnosis the *third* owner and gives it to a skill-1 trainee so the 20% training quota clears. The same memo cites 1.8× rework after >2 reassignments and says loops “normally want a specialist.” Celine (skill 3, low OT) is reserved; Hugo (skill 2) is unused. Training on a known loop is how you game the KPI, not how you break the loop.
- **C010** ignores the case note. Bruno is skill 2 and *may* finalize; Amal/Gita/Jonas may prepare. The answer dumps a third Approval onto Farid and claims there is “no live trainee-approver path,” which is false for the prepare/finalize split the prompt defines.
- **OT-high is promoted from “avoid” to a hard filter.** That is a defensible stance, but it is what forces the extra handovers on C003, C006, C010, and C014. The policy then congratulates itself on continuity while having converted a soft fairness rule into a continuity-breaker.
- **Capacity is decorative.** The only hours gate is `< 0.5h` when SLA `< 8h`. Farid is given two urgents (2h and 4h SLAs) plus C010 in 6.5h; Eva two highs in 5h; Celine a 0.81-complexity 6h rework plus two review gates plus the C001 brief; Jonas four activity types in 6h. None of this is checked against `expected_service`.
- Sequential greedy assignment plus a cap of 4 makes Jonas the residual sink. That is never re-solved globally, so C014 is locked to him before the quality batch even exists.

Load protection of Bruno/David/Iris, the Gita/Hugo Security split, refusing Eva/Jonas as *performers* on C015 (skill 0, not 1–2), keeping C007/C016/C005/C002/C011 with owners, and meeting a literal 2/9 training share are the parts that are actually right.

**The algorithm is not the implementation.** Section 2 promises min-max normalization, slack additives, a skill-2 training term, a Security specialist bonus, a post-high-priority T boost, quality batch merge, a loop-break exception, owner-then-OT tie-breaks, and half-load supervision. Section 7’s `score`/`assign_batch` omit most of that, apply raw unnormalized signals (so the weight table does not mean what it claims), make the owner ineligible with a `-1` hack instead of choosing a briefed specialist, pick `free_sec_supervisors[0]`, and never batch. `filter_case_performance(..., 0, 1e12)` is a no-op with a comment admitting it. A sketch may be incomplete; it should not silently disagree with the policy that section 3 pretends was run.

**Comparison and audit are better than the roster.** First-available landing C001 on unsupervised skill-1 Celine and C002 on skill-1 Jonas is a correct reading of the baseline rule. Specialist-only concentrating Security on Hugo and Diagnosis/Rework on Celine is also right. The seven-dimension table is honest that EMSA is a frontier point. The worker memo is the best section: specific, non-ranking, and operational. Audit indicators cover every required family and connect them to PM4Py artefacts in a way that is more than name-dropping — except that several functions are only wired in comments, and the sketch would not reproduce section 3.

Net: a high-quality essay wrapped around a policy that breaks its own binding continuity rule on the only 3-hour enterprise Security case, then reports a training rate achieved by handing a diagnosis loop to a trainee. That is not near-flawless, and it is not a 8–10 under the stated grading bar.
