**7.3**

The answer is decision-theoretically serious, operationally mostly realistic, and clearly better than a probability-rank or single-threshold policy. It refuses I-03 on stage grounds, separates I-05 from I-07, does not treat \(p\) as a causal effect, keeps spillover explicit, and writes an auditable impact table. It is not nearly flawless. Several rule-to-action contradictions, an operational staffing hole, and avoidable sloppiness should keep it well below the top band.

**Section 1 is a real rule, but it is not internally consistent.** Eligibility, alert consolidation, observational-vs-causal separation, and segment intervals are the right ingredients. The acceptance test (lower-bound net positive; central net positive after a queue shadow price) is coherent on the page. Then the same section smuggles undefined objects: a “high-risk/action band,” an “urgency multiplier” with no functional form, and a 0.08 jump threshold that is never related to model noise. Those are not cosmetic. They are the clauses later used to admit or exclude live cases.

Worse, the rule fights itself on persistence. One clause lets “two consecutive refreshes in the high-risk/action band” authorize action. Another says a refresh with no new information does not authorize a swarm. I-05 sits exactly in that contradiction. A deployable rule cannot leave that unresolved and then “fix” it only in Section 4.

**Section 2 makes the right headline choices and then undermines them.** I-03 out, I-05 suppressed, I-06 gated on the imputed queue field, I-08 blocked at the conservative bound, one slot reserved: all defensible. The floor arithmetic (lower \(\delta \times\) value \(-\,450-\) burden) is correct for every case.

The allocation does not obey the rule that produced it.

- I-07’s conservative floor is about €500. After any plausible shadow price — \(6 \times (0.01\text{–}0.03) \times \bar V_U\) — the *central* net is negative at ordinary untreated values (the author’s own later \(\bar V_U = €15{,}000\) implies ~€1,800 per slot). Section 1 says the central estimate must remain positive after the shadow price, and that non-monetary reasons “must not be used to hide an otherwise negative intervention.” Section 2 still marks I-07 Tier A. Section 5 then retrofits a fairness floor. That is three different justifications, not one policy.
- I-09’s \(p\) rise is 0.07, below the stated 0.08 evidence gate, with no new event. Admission therefore depends on the undefined action band. The supplied effect range is also the one the prompt flags as unreliable; the floor of €2,350 is written as if 0.18 were a usable lower bound. Human validation at dispatch is not a substitute for haircutting \(\delta\).
- I-04 is intervened *now* with 10 hours left, still in Triage, after a Strategic downward correction to roughly 0.36–0.43. The option value of waiting — same slot, later information, possible I-06 verification, possible regulatory arrival — is not priced. “Tier A because the floor is large” ignores that the scarce object is a *today* slot with a closing regulatory window, not a permanent claim on I-04.
- Reserve logic is double-talk. The text both holds the fourth slot “through 15:00” and names verified I-06 as the first release. I-06 has four hours. A verified diagnosis-stage jump held until 15:00 is a different policy from “verify, then decide.”

**The largest operational miss is staffing and order.** Two specialists deliver each swarm. Three selected swarms cannot run in parallel. Three sequential 45-minute swarms consume 2.25 hours of both seniors before any queue delay. I-07 has 1.5 hours. If I-04 is dispatched first, I-07’s window is already gone. Sequential dispatch is mentioned only later as a stress-test comment; Section 2 never states order. For a live 12:00 policy that is a concrete implementation failure, not a nicety.

**Section 3 is the strongest section and still not tight.** Midpoint sums (0.19+0.26+0.24 = 0.69; €11,400+€1,300+€4,800 = €17,500; labor €1,350; burden €2,850; spillover \(3\times 6\times(0.01\text{–}0.03)=0.18\text{–}0.54\)) are auditable, and refusing to invent \(\bar V_U\) is the correct honesty. Then: Optimistic is specified as the *upper half* of each range, but the table uses midpoint-to-max (0.69–0.90). Adverse delay is 15–30 minutes per slot, yet spillover probabilities stay in the base 0.01–0.03 band with only a verbal nod to nonlinearity. False-alarm burden is charged in full as a sure cost — matching the suggested formula, but never examined. Confidence labels are reasonable; the table is a stress test, not a savings claim. That part is disciplined.

**Section 4 does the required I-05 / I-07 contrast, then gets sloppy.** The state machine, post-swarm cooldown, and “third unchanged refresh is not an escalation” are the right machinery. I-05 is correctly parked; I-07 is correctly treated as new evidence plus a closing window, not as another 30-minute reprint. Calling I-07 both “re-entry” and “an urgent first swarm” muddies the state machine the author just defined. The 2-hour cooldown and the 0.10 re-entry threshold (vs 0.08 to enter) are arbitrary and unlinked to diagnosis-cycle time or prediction noise.

**Section 5 names every required risk and attaches actual controls.** Dual ranked lists, a lower-value service floor, language-capable review without auto-denial or auto-priority, slope-versus-intercept calibration, treatment-aware labels, and queue-level spillover accounting by segment are all concrete. Two weak spots: I-07 is a poor exhibit of a “robustly positive” floor, and the 0.8 selection-rate ratio is an imported employment heuristic, not a justification. Public-sector process obligations (I-01) are mentioned in the abstract and then ignored in the live book.

**Section 6 is comprehensive and slightly theatrical.** Operational hard stops, segment calibration, close-score randomization with an emergency path, ITT/TOT, exposed-vs-unexposed spillover blocks, and explicit triggers (calibration error >0.05, realized spillover >0.03, delay >20 minutes) are the right monitor set. What is missing is the binding constraint: four slots a day cannot identify heterogeneous effects for NNL, SME, and Strategic on any useful horizon. “Oversample” and “minimum sample sizes” do not replace a power budget. Without that, the monitoring plan overclaims what it can learn.

**Small inaccuracies that a strict read should not ignore.** I-08’s Strategic band is written as 0.34–0.41; \(0.43-0.12\) to \(0.43-0.05\) is 0.31–0.38. Several “about” floors are exact. The urgency and evidence gates are applied when convenient and waived when not.

**Net.** This is a strong policy memo with genuine decision-theoretic structure. It is not a 9: it violates its own acceptance test, does not specify the only dispatch order that makes I-07 feasible, leaves the reserve rule ambiguous, and treats the NNL effect range as more known than the prompt allows. Under a lenient rubric it would score higher. Under the required standard those are not nits.
