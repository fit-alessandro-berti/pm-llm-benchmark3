**7.6**

This is a high-quality reconstruction that meets the format contract, treats missingness and censoring as first-class, and shows real process-mining competence. It is not nearly flawless. Several statements presented as facts or as “stable conclusions” are simply false, and a few hand-computed station metrics do not match the log.

The core method is sound. Event order is an explicit half-open convention; rounding is modeled as \(\pm 2.5\) min per stamp and \((-5,+5)\) min per duration, with a correct independent-error \(2\sigma\) sketch. Missing starts are bounded by queue entry, prior work on the same resource, and nonnegative service, then carried as min-service / central / max-service scenarios rather than a silent point fill. The central rule (previous end \(+5\) min) is justified by the observed 5-minute changeover pattern. OPEN work is elapsed time only; blanks are censored waits; completed-case means are flagged as downward-biased. Sample-path Little’s Law is used as an identity; the stationary form is applied only where they argue the window is close enough to it, and they correctly reject it for S2. Utilization includes OPEN elapsed time and is not inferred from completed cases alone. The bottleneck story (S1 as transient arrival-burst constraint, S2 as structural constraint after the feed from S1 continues past noon) is supported by rates, queues, and end-of-window WIP. The I1\(\to\)C1 / I2\(\to\)C2 lane pattern through P09, the two S2 FIFO breaks, the C2 outage’s limited effect, and the P22/I2 anomaly are all grounded in the table and labeled as evidence vs hypothesis.

Case-level arithmetic for the required cases is almost all correct, including P05/P07/P11/P15 feasible windows, exact sojourns where starts cancel, and the 14 completed sojourns (mean 146, median 145, range 60–230). Flow conservation (22 = 20 S1 completions + P21 + P22; 20 = 14 S2 completions + 2 in service + 4 waiting) holds. Mean station WIP from case-minutes (S1 \(2085/360=5.79\), S2 \(1340/360=3.72\)) is internally consistent. Resource busy minutes in the three imputation scenarios for the full window match the log.

The defects are not cosmetic.

The Section 6 “stable” claim that **S1 waits exceed S2 waits for all cases up to P14** is false: P01 and P02 are 5 vs 5; **P08 is 50 vs 55**. That is a bottleneck-relevant comparison, and it is wrong. Case sojourns are also **not** “monotonically increasing with arrival order”: P08 = 180 then P09 = 155. S1 waits are not strictly monotonic either (P12 = 85, P13 = 80).

**Fully observed S1 service is 18 cases, not 17.** Sum is 505 min, mean \(\approx 28.1\), not 28.2; with P07/P15 central the mean is \(565/20=28.25\), not 28.4. This is a basic count error in the headline service summary.

**C2 busy time in 10:00–12:00 is 95 min, not 90.** P06 still occupies C2 for 50 min after 10:00 (10:00–10:50) and P08 for 45 min (11:15–12:00). Reported \(90/105=86\%\) is therefore off; \(\approx 90\%\) of the 105 available minutes is the figure that matches their own remaining-time logic. That cell is hand-computed; the Python block never prints the two-hour busy splits, so the error is not caught by the “reproducible” code.

Starvation-adjusted C2 availability is internally inconsistent: prose says “from 08:35, 320 min,” which is neither \(14{:}00-08{:}35=325\) nor \(325-15=310\). The 320 used in the code is \(335-15\) from 08:25. Nearby numbers were mixed. The S1 Little’s Law note that the \(5.79\) vs \(5.64\) gap is “exactly the residual of P21/P22” is also wrong: residual/T is \(240/360=0.67\), not \(0.15\). The S2 discussion of why \(\lambda W\) can land close for the wrong reason is the one that is actually careful.

The Section 4 subperiod table changes cohort without saying so. Waiting times are “arrivals of this block”; S1 12:00–14:00 service (“mean 29 (6 obs.) + P21 elapsed 30”) is activity in the interval, including P21 who arrived earlier and excluding imputed P15. Same row, two definitions.

On rounding, treating every duration as \(\pm 5\) min worst case is fine; claiming that 26 identical 5-minute gaps are a physical changeover is reasonable, but the occupied-utilization story still leans on those gaps being real idle rather than a logging convention, and peak WIP under timestamp jitter is not bounded. Min-service (zero-length jobs) is a bound, not a plausible world; they mostly label it that way, then still fold it into “mean service 34.3” as if it were a data summary.

The Python block does all eight required things: in-memory table, distinct MISSING/OPEN/BLANK parsing, interval dataframe, `format_dataframe`, pandas WIP/scenarios, case-level `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` on the 11 fully observed completed traces with an explicit case-level label, and `convert_log_to_time_intervals` plus a comment that pandas is safer for censoring. The unpacking `for a, b, _, _, cs in ivals` plus `pd.Timestamp(..., unit="s")` is a reproducibility hazard: if that API does not return five-tuples of unix seconds, the script dies at the end. Most pandas results would already have printed, but “reproducible” is not demonstrated.

What survives all of that is still the important picture: S1 sojourn and total completed sojourn do not depend on missing starts; S1 wait plateaus near 95–105 min after 10:00; S2 WIP is rising and holds six cases at 14:00 against two at S1; the outage delays P08 and is not the source of the S2 backlog; completed-case sojourn 146 min understates the arrival cohort. Those conclusions are earned. The grade is held down because the write-up asserts several regularities the table contradicts and because a subset of the reported station numbers is not faithful to the same reconstruction the authors otherwise execute carefully.
