**6.2**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and hits every required heading. Objectives, hard vs soft constraints, the scoring decomposition, the worker explanation, and the audit-indicator list are all competent. It does not pretend there is a single optimum, and it generally respects overtime caps, the 4-assignment batch limit, and the ban on unsupervised high-priority trainee work.

It is not close to flawless. Section 3 contains two clear rule breaks. Security Review may be done by skill 3, or by skill 1–2 only under R7/R8; skill 0 is “not allowed.” C015 is given to R5 (Security = 0). Final Approval must be finalized by skill 2–3, with skill 1 only allowed to prepare; C010 puts R8 (Approval = 0) in the assignment pair. The queue notes that name Eva/Jonas and “trainee approval” create a tension with those rules; a careful policy would have stated the conflict and used a legal performer (R8/R7 on C015; R6 or R5 alone, or a skill-1 preparer, on C010). Instead the illegal pairs are also used to claim a 33% training share, so the 20% training constraint is not actually met with valid assignments. C012, explicitly marked as trainee-suitable, is unused.

Constraint 5 is also treated loosely: C001 is high-complexity, high-handover, owner skill 1, and supervision *is* feasible, so continuity-plus-supervision was the stated rule; the urgency exception is arguable but is not reconciled with the constraint list. Same-day capacity is thin: R6 gets two urgent cases plus a training approval in 6.5h; R5 gets two high-priority cases plus a security training load in 5.0h; R8 gets a 3h enterprise security case plus two supervisory roles. “Hours remaining today” never becomes a hard feasibility check.

The algorithm sketch includes every required term, but the eligibility loop can fall through and re-add people after the Security/Approval gates. Section 7 misuses `pm4py.filter_case_performance` (duration filter, not arbitrary case attributes), invents a `view_sna` variant path, and omits `discover_batches`. Baseline comparison stays qualitative; first-available and specialist-only are never actually run on this queue.

Strong socio-technical framing and a usable audit design, undermined by material assignment/compliance errors and several technical inaccuracies. That is a mid-tier answer under a strict bar, not a high one.
