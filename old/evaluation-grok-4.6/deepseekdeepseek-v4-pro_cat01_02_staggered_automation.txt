**6.3**

The final write-up is conceptually strong and format-compliant, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict standard, the arithmetic errors, internally inconsistent contrasts, an invented intensity coefficient, and a gap between the stated design and the evidence are enough to keep the score well below the top band.

**What is done well.** Sections 2–4, 6, and 8 show real causal-policy discipline. The estimands correctly separate availability from intensity, median from tail, and static from dynamic effects, and they flag SUTVA and treatment-version problems (routing, fraud desk, changing fidelity). The identification table rejects naive TWFE, names Sun–Abraham-type negative weighting, and treats synthetic control as a small-donor design rather than a magic estimator. The mechanism table is process-literate: fast-path substitution versus specialist congestion, staffing, case-mix, rework displacement, tail amplification, and survey selection. The threats table hits every required item with usable bias directions. The recommendation—`expand with an embedded evaluation`, hold out a branch, collect case-level logs and queue/fraud-desk state, put p90/CSAT guardrails on the table—is the right policy class. The claim ladder correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” sentence.

**Quantitative discipline is the main failure.** Several numbers in the Section 5 table are simply wrong, and they are used as if they were careful facts.

- Never-treated p90 in the Alpha/Beta window is not 79.8 → 92.2 (+12.4 h). Branch-equal means are about 77.2 → 93.2 (+16 h). That error shrinks the relative p90 “improvement” and then infects the sensitivity write-up.
- Gamma’s control p90 is not 78.4 → 82.0. It is about 85.7 → 91.9. The Gamma p90 DiD is therefore misstated.
- Gamma pre-period `escalation_pct` is ~8.7, not 8.2; Beta post `rework_pct` is ~12.2, not 12.0; control rework/escalation levels in the Alpha/Beta window are off; Gamma-window control CSAT pre is ~3.96, not 4.02.
- The same contrast is computed with different windows in different sections. Mechanisms says Beta escalation rose +1.3 pp and Gamma +1.0 pp; the evidence table says +2.5 pp for both. Alpha’s +3.8 pp is a first-month vs last-month jump, not the windowed pre/post used elsewhere.

That is not rounding. It is enough to change how large the escalation and tail contrasts look.

**A result is fabricated.** Section 5 asserts that a covariate-adjusted intensity regression yields “roughly +0.2 h per percentage point,” “computed in the Python block.” The code never reports that number; the prose invents it. For a task that explicitly grades quantitative honesty, that is a serious defect.

**Identification and evidence do not match.** The primary strategy is branch-level SCM. The numbers delivered are unweighted pre/post and two-group DiD. Relative-time is built in code and then unused; the fitted model is the naive branch+month FE specification the text warned against. SCM is never even approximated (no weights, no pre-fit, no in-time placebo). So the best identification discussion in the memo is not the analysis the reader is shown.

**Overclaiming on escalation.** Calling escalation the safest result (confidence 75, +1.5 to +3.0 pp, “at least 1.5 pp relative to never-treated” as an *Observed* claim) is too strong. Every branch, treated or not, jumps ~2 pp in March when the fraud desk opens, then again in May when complexity rises. After that common shock, the residual adopter increment is closer to ~1 pp and is not a raw observation. Putting a DiD in the `Observed:` slot of the claim ladder is a category error.

**p90 narrative is incoherent.** Absolute p90 rises in Alpha/Beta; their own (even if miscomputed) DiD is a relative *decline* versus controls; Section 5.5 then “leans toward no effect or slight worsening.” Absolute vs relative vs complexity-adjusted stories are never reconciled, which is exactly what the prompt said not to do: do not collapse median and p90 into one mushy cycle-time effect, and keep arithmetic facts separate from interpretation.

**Smaller but real issues.** Choosing Delta rather than Epsilon as the next site is barely justified; Epsilon is closer to the original high-complexity, weaker-cycle target population, so the next experiment is poorly aligned with the policy estimand. Baseline `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% before official go-live is ignored. The Python loader is messy (`pd.compat.StringIO` then a second read), and it prints control change with the sign flipped. Section 8 is thin on why a mandated 40% fast-path target is the right estimand once the memo has spent so long distinguishing availability from use.

**Net.** This is a good causal memo in structure and vocabulary, and it would be in the 8s if the tabled numbers were accurate, the intensity result were actually computed, SCM were either implemented or clearly abandoned in favor of the DiD that was shown, and escalation were not oversold. It is not that memo. The conceptual frame is well above average; the execution is not trustworthy enough for a high score under a hypercritical rubric.
