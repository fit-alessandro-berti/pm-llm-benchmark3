**8.6**

This is a strong, internally consistent process-mining write-up. The fork–join algebra is correct, the 12-case arithmetic checks out, the no-double-count argument is stated properly, and the main conclusion (B controls completion via \(Q+S\), not A via mean service) is the right one. The remaining deductions are for omissions, polar counterfactual bounds, and a few presentation/reproducibility nits—not for fabricated numbers.

**What is solid**

- Definitions are the right ones: \(U_c=r_c-a_c\), \(Q_{ci}=s_{ci}-r_c\), \(S_{ci}=e_{ci}-s_{ci}\), \(J_c=\max_i e_{ci}\), \(H_c=J_c-r_c=\max_i(Q_{ci}+S_{ci})\), \(W^{\mathrm{sync}}_{ci}=J_c-e_{ci}\), \(D_c=f^s_c-J_c\), \(P_c=f^e_c-f^s_c\), and the non-overlapping identity \(T_c=U_c+H_c+D_c+P_c\).
- Double-counting is shown, not merely asserted: \(L_{ci}+W^{\mathrm{sync}}_{ci}=H_c\) for every branch, so \(\sum_i(L_{ci}+W^{\mathrm{sync}}_{ci})=3H_c\), and \(\sum_i W^{\mathrm{sync}}_{ci}\) is branch-minutes. The F02 overlap example (A 09:15–09:25 and C 08:50–09:25) is correct.
- Every required case-level field matches an independent recalculation. Worked F02/F04/F07/F11 are complete. Critical-branch counts (A 2, B 8, C 2), lag means (50.0 / 56.7 / 30.6), sync means (11.0 / 4.3 / 30.4), mean \(T=115.25\), and the partition \(19.58+61.00+14.67+20.00\) are all exact after ordinary rounding.
- Influence ranking does not confuse long service with control. A is never third; C’s large sync wait is correctly read as earliness, not delay.
- Counterfactuals 1–2 recompute \(\max\) lag rather than subtracting raw branch time. A−20% → 15 min total (only F01, F11); B-queue −50% → 92.5 min; zero-benefit cases are right. The D-held vs fixed-slot split is the correct uncertainty treatment.
- The FCFS-2 contrast (only F11 waits 5 min) is a legitimate diagnostic that observed \(D\) is not two-server congestion.
- Section 7 hits the required identification limits. The Python block meets the stated contract: in-memory table, custom join logic, deterministic A≺B≺C labels, three counterfactuals, interval rows, `format_dataframe` + `get_service_time`, and comments on why DFG performance does not recover synchronization.

**Issues (why this is not 9.5–10)**

1. **Regular structure is under-used.** A queue is *identically* 0 and A service is *identically* 50. C queue is 5 min in 9/12 cases. Final service is *identically* 20. Those are closer to policy/offset than to stochastic queues. The text treats A’s zero queue mainly as a modeling convenience and C’s 5-minute mode only implicitly. That weakens the claim that \(Q_{ci}\) is “branch-specific queue waiting” and should have been flagged as an identification issue, not just a summary statistic.

2. **Downstream counterfactuals stay at two poles.** The analysis holds \(D_c\) fixed or sets all final starts immovable. It never re-runs the work-conserving FCFS calendar on the *shifted* join-ready vector. After B-queue cuts, several joins collapse onto A’s 50-minute rail (F02, F05, F06, F10), which can bunch F arrivals. The qualitative remark about concentrated arrivals is not quantified, so “masking” is left as 92.5 vs 0 instead of a third, internally consistent schedule.

3. **Capacity story is slightly overstated relative to evidence.** “Adding final-station capacity or otherwise reducing downstream queueing” is operationalized as \(D\to 0\) (176 min). The FCFS-3 result (5 min) is then used to reject a capacity explanation. That contrast is useful, but it depends on continuous availability from midnight, no unlogged F work, and FCFS by join-ready—none of which are in the log. The 176-minute figure is an *eligibility-to-start residual*, not a capacity gap. The write-up says this, then still leads the table with 176 as the third redesign’s savings.

4. **“Critical-stage mean [max]” is compact to the point of unclarity.** It is the mean of \(H_c\) on cases where that branch is unique-critical. A hurried reader can mix it with overall mean lag (B 56.7 vs 65.0). The 12.25-minute mean slack is correct but never shown case-wise, so the “B has more room before A binds” claim is harder to audit than it needed to be.

5. **Reproducibility nits in the required code.** `Series.str.split(..., regex=False)` is version-sensitive. `pm4py.get_service_time` on a formatted DataFrame is version-sensitive. No printed output is attached, so the hand tables cannot be checked against a run. None of these are logic errors; they are defects against a “reproducible Python” heading.

6. **Minor wording slips.** “120/182 ≈ 66%” is 65.9% occupancy over F1’s first-start to last-end, not utilization. Tail is defined as the sample max in \(n=12\); that disclaimer is present, but “fork-join tail of 75 minutes” still reads more inferentially than the sample supports. The A≺B≺C label rule is disclosed, and there are no observed ties, so this is cosmetic.

**Verdict**

Competent, careful, and almost fully aligned with the required format and quality bar. The core PM mistake the rubric cares about—equating longest mean service with the controlling branch, or summing parallel waits into \(T_c\)—is avoided. The score is held below the top band because the strongest empirical regularities are under-interpreted, the downstream redesign is not closed under a single dispatch model, and the capacity counterfactual is reported as a 176-minute saving that the same section shows is not a capacity effect.
