**7.4**

The answer is complete, well structured, and hits every required heading. It does not treat throughput as the only objective, it keeps high-priority work off unsupervised trainees, it withholds new work from high-overtime resources, and it treats continuity as a first-class concern. The baselines are compared as expected trade-offs rather than as a fake optimum. That is a strong submission. It is not nearly flawless.

**Constraint 5 is bent, then relabeled as wisdom.** C001 is high-complexity, high-handover, and owned by Celine (Security = 1). The stated rule is that continuity should be preferred unless the owner is skill 0/1 *and supervision is not feasible*. Supervision *is* feasible: Security explicitly allows skill 1–2 under Gita or Hugo, and high-priority work may go to skill 1 if supervised. The historically cited +3.3h weak-skill service time is a real SLA argument, but it is an override of the letter of the continuity rule, not a deduction from it. A supervised Celine+Hugo pair would have satisfied security, high-priority supervision, *and* constraint 5 at once. Choosing Hugo alone is defensible as a risk call; presenting it as if the policy required it is not.

**The recommended batch is not operationally feasible if “hours remaining today” means what the snapshot says.**  
- Celine has 6.0h left and is given C007 (high, 6h SLA, complexity 0.81, already 3 reassignments) plus lead work on C003 and supervision of C009. “Protect a focused work block” does not create hours.  
- Farid has 6.5h left and two urgent items (C002 in 2h, VIP C011 in 4h) plus C010. If either urgent task uses most of its remaining SLA, the other misses.  
- Eva has 5.0h left and two concurrent high-risk cases (C005 8h, C016 7h, the latter high-complexity with high handover cost).  

The policy text talks about `SLAFit` and remaining hours; the table does not actually close those inequalities. That is a logical gap, not a stylistic one.

**C004 is a cheap continuity gift to a load-balancing story that the same policy then contradicts.** Amal is the owner, Eligibility = 3, handover is low, the case is routine. Moving it to Jonas (skill 2) is justified as “preserving Amal for diagnosis development,” after which Amal is still used as C010 approval *preparer*—outside his documented training priority. Jonas then hits the hard cap of 4 with a pile of low-complexity leftovers. The scoring section says continuity and development should be visible trade-offs; this row reads like residual dumping.

**C003 is a poor training vehicle and a known quality landmine.** The case is already at 2 reassignments, is a repeated diagnosis loop, and complexity is 0.66. The write-up knows that a third reassignment sits on the 1.8× rework / 1.4× appeal evidence, then assigns a skill-1 trainee into that loop and still counts the case toward the 20% documented-training floor. If Celine is truly the lead, calling this Amal’s training *assignment* is generous accounting.

**The algorithm and the table are only loosely coupled.** Section 2 specifies eligibility, urgency, skill, continuity overlay, workload, training, resilience, batching, and a training-floor repair loop. Section 3 is a hand-reasoned snapshot. The continuity overlay is applied to C007 and C016 and waived for C001; the training floor is satisfied by two convenient diagnosis cases rather than demonstrated as the output of `best_training_improvement`. The weights look precise (they sum to 1) but are never used. That makes the policy look more mechanical, and more auditable, than the actual recommendations are.

**Smaller issues that still matter under a strict reading**

- Same-day hours and multi-day SLA clocks are never converted onto one calendar, despite the snapshot mixing “3h remaining” with “72h remaining.”  
- Specialist-only counts for Celine (5) and Farid (4) are correct under the stated tie-break; first-available is only sketched, so the comparison is stronger on specialist-only than on first-available.  
- PM4Py coverage is real and names the required functions, but `skill_matrix` is never loaded, `reconstruct_state_as_of` / `recommend` are stubs, `filter_*` signatures are asserted without version pinning, and Gini is applied to event-level `workload_snapshot` rather than a resource-level state. Acceptable as a sketch; not a working audit.  
- Counting every named supervisor/trainee against the cap of 4 is conservative and should have made Celine’s package look worse, not better.  
- The worker text is the right tone. It still underplays the live risks (Celine’s day, Farid’s two urgents, Eva’s two highs) that those workers would actually feel.

**What keeps it well above a mid-range score:** hard safety rules are respected (no skill 0, no unsupervised high-priority trainee, no high-OT pickup, Security/Approval rules held, quality batching conditions held, 2/9 documented training). C015 correctly refuses Eva/Jonas. C007 and C016 keep ownership for the right reasons. The audit indicator set is the strongest part of the answer: it includes supervision validity, reason-code completeness, case-mix caution, and a decision log, not just vanity SNA plots.

Net: a serious, usable policy design with one clear continuity-rule miss, several same-day capacity contradictions, and a scoring apparatus that does not actually produce the table. That is clearly better than competent, and clearly short of near-flawless.
