**2.8**

The submission has the required nine-section skeleton and some correctly named process ideas (P1 reservation, overflow, handover, SLA clocks). Under a strict reading it is not a competent analysis. The quantitative core is arithmetically wrong, the scenario table is unearned, bottleneck migration contradicts the skill matrix, and the Python block would not run and does not implement the mechanisms it claims.

**Section 1.** Assumptions are labeled, which is good, but several are underspecified or internally confused. Treating ranges as uniforms is acceptable; treating block means as Poisson is acceptable. The ME/ES rule mixes two different decisions (case-level parallel branches vs one cross-skilled sequential resource) and then adds a comparison to an 8-minute coordination penalty without defining whose clocks are compared. The invented preemption test (“restart penalty < time saved”) is not the given rule and is never used later. The second objective is a base-case fairness/utilization constraint, not robustness across U1–U5. Decision variables are a list of themes, not controllable quantities.

**Section 2.** This section fails an audit. Offered-load examples are dimensionally inconsistent. `7 × 0.30 × 23 min = 48.3 min ≈ 0.81 h`, not 4.81 h (division by 10 instead of 60). Electrical, Software, and Validation in the same block use three different implicit unit conversions (`/10`, a rounded 4.0 h, and `7 × 14 / 100 = 0.98 h`). ME/ES double-skill demand is omitted, so Mechanical load ignores ME and Electrical load ignores ME+ES. Mixture means ignore complex-tail mass. Multipliers are not applied to offered work. Capacity for the 08:00–10:00 block is computed from Ada’s *full* 8-hour shift minus a lunch that does not fall in that block. Deepa’s 10:00 start, Emil’s 12:00 start, and Farah’s 12:00 end are not used as time-varying capacity. Farah is called the Mechanical bottleneck because she is slow; she is the *least* efficient Mechanical resource and is only present in the morning. Validation is asserted as a bottleneck without comparing ~11 hours of daily Validation work to six qualified people. U3 is described as an Electrical/Software shock in prose and then treated as Mechanical overload in the table.

**Section 3.** The three labels match the brief, but the dispatch rules are not implementable. “Strict specialization” still gives Ada Intake+Mechanical+Validation and uses SERPT. Eligibility is “e.g.” Fixed queues are not named. Hybrid reserves “Ada, Ben, Chen” *and* “50% of Mechanical and Electrical,” which is inconsistent (Chen has no Mechanical; P1 is 5–18% of volume, so 50% reservation is idle capacity unless justified). Overflow triggers, skill-family setup, the extra 5-minute team handover, and who may work in parallel on ME/ES are not specified. Policy 1 and Policy 3 collapse toward the same reservation idea.

**Section 4.** The table is presented with false precision and no calculation path from Section 2. There are no ranges, seeds, or bounds. Hybrid wins almost every cell, including shocks, which is the opposite of an honest trade-off. Utilization >100% for Farah in U5 is left unexplained given a 30-minute overtime cap. U3 “Mechanical overload” after removing Chen (Electrical/Software/Intake/Validation) is a process error, not a rounding issue. P1 SLA is computed against a 120-minute clock while mean delays of 100–180 minutes are reported as if those two statements were jointly plausible for a 90% P1 target. Resilience is an unexplained Low/Medium/High label. Hybrid’s “main failure mode” is always “P1 preemption conflicts” even where it has the best P1 SLA. This is narrative fitting, not evidence.

**Section 5.** Recommending hybrid *because the table says so* is circular. The fallback trigger (“reserved capacity >90% for two hours”) is not defined in operational units. The claim that hybrid “minimizes handover versus full pooling” is asserted, not shown. The brief’s actual tension—specialists are faster (Ada Mechanical 1.00 vs Farah 1.40; Chen Electrical 1.00 vs Ada 1.35) while pooling buys coverage when Deepa/Emil/Farah are off-shift—is never the reason for the choice.

**Section 6.** Covers the required risk list in generic language. Acceptable as a checklist, not as analysis grounded in this roster (e.g., Farah as a slow universal spare, Emil as the only 1.00 Software after 12:00, Validation as a possible *induced* bottleneck if every diagnostic resource is also pulled to close cases).

**Section 7.** Attribute list is close to the spec. The PM4Py plan is not. `discover_process_model` and `get_resource_utilization` are not the APIs the task named. There is no account of interval vs lifecycle semantics, no queue-id vs case-id distinction, and no design for comparing policy versions or shock flags. Naming functions is not a monitoring design.

**Section 8.** This is the clearest fail. A scaffold is allowed only if omitted mechanisms are identified. Here they are hidden.

- Tables are encoded, then immediately mutated in place across U1–U5, so later scenarios inherit earlier shocks.
- `demand_df.loc['08:00-10:00', …]` is not aligned to a block index.
- U2 doubles shares and does not renormalize; type probabilities can exceed 1.
- `range(arrivals)` after a 1.7× shock is a float.
- ES is treated as including Mechanical.
- Speed multipliers, calendars, queues, waiting, preemption, handover (+7), team change (+5), setup (+5), parallel coordination (+8), overtime, and once-only preemption are not simulated. Resources are picked as “first Intake skill” (always Ada) or `np.random.choice`.
- Reservation lookup uses `r in {'Mechanical': 0.5}`, which cannot select Ada/Ben.
- Policy objects do not change assignment logic; three policies are the same random walk.
- Case ids are `case_N`; SLA filters look for `'P1'` in the name, then compare completion to `Timestamp('now')+120 min`.
- “Mean delay” is the mean of wall-clock completion timestamps, i.e., minutes since epoch.
- `min_utilization` is undefined.
- Required calls `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `pm4py.get_service_time`, and `pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network` are absent; invented handover visualize-to-pandas is not a substitute.
- No seeds, no interval log that encodes waiting, no working comparison table.

That is not a reproducible experiment. It is non-executable theater that cannot support Section 4.

**Section 9.** Correct that expected arrivals and ranges are not a full system. The pilot still “implement the hybrid policy on a shift,” which is not a P1-protected design. There is no power calculation, no frozen specialization control arm, no rule for aborting if P1 SLA degrades, and no statement of which observable would *falsify* the hybrid claim.

**Overall.** Format compliance and boilerplate process vocabulary are the only solid parts. Load arithmetic, shift-aware capacity, ME/ES semantics, shock-specific bottlenecks, and the required PM4Py/simulation artifact are all broken. A robust-policy recommendation built on invented SLAs and non-running code cannot score in the competent range.
