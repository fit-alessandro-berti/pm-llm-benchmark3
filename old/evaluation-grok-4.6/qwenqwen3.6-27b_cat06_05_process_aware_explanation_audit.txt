**7.0**

The audit is structurally complete, process-aware, and mostly well judged. It follows the required five-section format, keeps congestion and prefix-age signals, distinguishes temporal leakage from process impossibility, and generally separates predictive validity from causal or actionable claims. It is not close to flawless under a strict reading.

**What is solid.** Obvious full-trace leaks (`total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`) are correctly killed. Subtler lineage bugs are also caught: extraction-date SQL for prior claims, the 23:00 same-day document snapshot, mutable unversioned `risk_score_current`, closure-time `final_handler_team` despite 65% overlap, and same-timestamp events without a sequence index. `queue_length_at_t0` and `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are rightly retained. `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` is treated as a temporally unavailable but process-legal sequence, unlike `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed`, which is flagged as impossible plus leaked. Region and week are kept as proxies rather than deleted. Case Z is invalidated; X and Y are set to recompute. The JSON schema matches the spec.

**Material gaps.**

1. **Target leakage is under-specified.** `total_case_duration_hours` after close is not just a future covariate; it is essentially the label (or a deterministic function of the 10-day breach target). `full_trace_variant_id` values that already contain `APP-DEC-CLOSE` are similarly near-outcome encodings. Calling these generic temporal leakage is true but weaker than the actual defect.

2. **Required table is incomplete.** The spec asks for every local-only feature. `claimant_channel` and `initial_reserve` never appear in Section 1.

3. **Fraud handling is slightly wrong.** After coverage check, a fraud trigger may already be enabled and may run in parallel. Treating `Coverage Check Completed -> Fraud Investigation Opened` as simply “not yet enabled” / full-trace leakage misses that this is the one reported pattern that can be a legitimate prefix signal if it actually occurred at `t0`.

4. **Revised narratives overclaim residual risk.** After stripping Case X’s +0.26 appeal and +0.06 final decision (and Case Y’s +0.31 variant), the remaining mass is much closer to the base. The write-ups still read as confidently high-risk process stories.

5. **Causal story on appeals is thin.** Leakage is the main problem, but “appeals cause breaches” is also reverse causality: appeals mark long, contentious cases. That is noted only implicitly.

Smaller nits: footprint/prefix-alignment checks are barely used despite being invited; inventing `appeal_risk_score_at_t0` is hand-wavy; “document requests are always 0 at `t0`” is only true under the simplified normative model.

Remediation advice and the four-standard split (predictive / causal / actionable / fairness) are good enough that this stays clearly above a mediocre audit, but the misses above are enough to keep it well short of excellent.
