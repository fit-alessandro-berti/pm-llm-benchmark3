**6.2**

The final write-up is a competent, format-compliant redesign with real process logic, but it is not close to flawless. Several required elements are thin or wrong, and some claims fight the log.

**What is actually strong.** The four scenarios are the right *kinds* of shocks (mix, machines, validators/specialists, courier timing), not just “more volume.” Bottleneck migration is treated as a sequence with feedback, not a single station: mix-shift work moving Assay → technical validation → medical validation → specialist review; outage work piling into prep/batch wait and into M2-driven repeats; validator absence blocking release and pushing WIP back onto assay; courier spikes feeding the recollection–intake loop. Rejecting I4 because technical validation is already at 101% peak utilization is the correct call. Splitting a standing package from triggerable policies, naming a vulnerability *created by* I7/I8, and separating leading from lagging metrics all match the brief. The conflict hints on I1/I2/I3/I8/I10 are used, not ignored.

**Material requirement misses.** Section 3 does not do what was asked. Robustness is asserted; assumptions and failure modes are not stated intervention by intervention (courier contract flexibility, false-positive early rejects, evening-shift labor, WIP-cap measurability, I5 shoving work onto specialist review). Scenario policies have on-triggers but almost no off-triggers. Accession correction—two specialists, 14 h peak median queue, explicit unblocker of many cases—is barely in the redesign at all after I4 is discarded, so a documented scarce resource is left with no substitute.

**Clear logical errors.** The dual-lane row treats Complex-panel as “High (Prioritized)” alongside Urgent. That violates the constraint that not every case type can be first, and it ignores the data: Complex-panel is a medical/specialist-heavy, long-cycle class (peak median 111 h, p90 210 h), not an 18 h contractual class. Dual-lane “High” coverage of machine outage is also overstated; a WIP cap does not create assay minutes when a machine is down. I6’s cycle-time effects are scored “Low” for every class while rework impact is “High,” which is internally inconsistent. I5 is still a targeted add-capacity move sitting at the center of the “robust” package; that can be justified, but the write-up does not show why the rest of the package still works if the evening shift cannot be staffed.

**Process-mining evidence is thinner than it looks.** Utilization and a few multipliers (3.1× medical, 2.4× specialist) are used well. Frequency and performance DFGs are underused: Urgent batch-wait bypass falling 74% → 58%, batch-wait share rising sharply in peak, repeat-assay intensification and M2/night clustering, recollection and cancel counts, and the long performance edges (recollect 42/60 h, batch wait 9/22 h, hold → medical 18/48 h, tech → medical 8/24 h) rarely drive the design. Several numeric policies (4 h WIP, 30% of daily volume in one hour, 95% for four hours) are invented without tying remaining slack to the 18 h Urgent penalty.

**Dashboard is the weakest section.** Thresholds are not usable as *peak-period* warning bands: pre-analytical peak median is already 9.5 h, specialist peak median is already 36 h, and pre-analytical recollection is already ~8%. Those lights would be on all winter. The Urgent contractual object is penalty above 18 h (peak median 17 h, p90 33 h); targeting p90 < 18 h is the wrong SLA statistic and is nearly incoherent given the peak median. Medical-validator queue (22 h), accession queue, M2/repeat rate, and Friday specialist coverage are missing despite being the migration story.

**Net.** Good structure and better-than-average bottleneck-migration reasoning, with enough data contact to be a serious draft. Too many required pieces are incomplete, Complex-panel is mis-prioritized, accession is orphaned, and the dashboard is mis-calibrated against the same peak log the answer cites. That is a mid-6, not an 8–10.
