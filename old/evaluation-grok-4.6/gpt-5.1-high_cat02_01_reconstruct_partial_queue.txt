**6.3**

The write-up is substantively competent: missing-start windows are derived from the right resource constraints, MISSING/OPEN/blank are not collapsed, sojourn is correctly treated as known when starts are missing, open work is kept in the picture, and utilization is not computed from completed cases alone. Those are the main quality-bar items, and they are mostly met. The score is still far from high because the required format is broken, several stated numbers do not match the data, an assumption is used and then ignored, Little’s Law is promised rather than performed, and the “reproducible” code does not reproduce the queue/WIP claims.

**Format.** The specification asked for exactly seven top-level headings (`# 1.` … `# 7.`) and no others. The answer uses `##` for the main sections and then *also* uses `##` for 4.1–4.2, 5.1–5.4, and 6.1–6.4, so those subsections sit at the same heading level as the required sections. Sections 2–3 use `###`; 4–6 do not. That is a direct format failure, not a cosmetic quibble.

**Internal contradiction on FCFS.** A2 asserts FCFS. The missing-start windows do not. When I1 frees at 09:25, P07 is already waiting; FCFS would start P07 then. The same holds for P15 at 11:40, P05 at 09:45 on C1, and P11 at 12:00 on C2. The pessimistic endpoints (start = completion, service = 0) imply a free server while a case waits, which contradicts A2. The text mentions a “more realistic 5-minute minimal service” and never applies it. Observed S1 services are 20–30 min and observed S2 services 25–50 min; a 0-minute pessimistic service is not a serious bound. Under a minimum-service constraint the windows collapse sharply (P11 is almost identified). That is a process-mining error, not a rounding footnote.

**WIP and Little’s Law are wrong or missing.** Station occupancy is identified by arrivals and completions, so it does not depend on imputed starts — a point that should have been used as a robustness result. Direct occupancy arithmetic over 08:00–14:00 gives time-average S1 WIP \(2085/360 \approx 5.8\) and S2 WIP \(1340/360 \approx 3.7\), hence system mean WIP \(\approx 9.5\). The answer reports “about 5” and “around 3,” and attributes this to a 5-minute grid that never appears. Peak S2 is not 6 at 14:00: around 13:45–13:50 S2 holds P14 (in service), P15 (in service), and P16–P20 (queued) — seven cases. Peak system WIP is about 11 near 12:30 (22 arrivals, 11 S2 completions). The task asked for queue length *and* total WIP *over time*, with a simultaneous-event convention. A3 states a convention; there is no trajectory, and queue length (waiting only) is never separated from station WIP.

Little’s Law is defined in §2.4 and then not computed. The full window is the worst interval for a steady-state check (empty start, 22 in / 14 out, eight residual cases). The claim that S2 mean WIP is “consistent with Little’s Law using the completed cases” is the wrong identity: completed-only \(\lambda W \approx 14/6 \times 70/60 \approx 2.7\), versus occupancy \(3.7\). Finite-horizon Little’s Law as \(L=\sum T_i / |I|\) would have been the defensible check; it is not shown.

**Small factual slips that should not be in a careful reconstruction.** S2 waits of “65–100+ minutes for … P08, P10” are 55 and 50. “P16–P20 have S2 waits of at least 15–85 min” includes P17, whose S2 wait is observed (65 min) and whose service is open. “S1 completions accelerate” / “rising S1 throughput” overstates a 6 / 7 / 7 completion pattern; S1 output is roughly stable at ~3.3/h while S2 output climbs 1.5 → 2.5 → 3.0/h toward two-server capacity. That last pattern is the actual bottleneck diagnostic and is only half-used. Aggregated S1 waits “5 to 95, median around 50” mix early and late cases into a range too wide to be a summary. P10’s 25-minute S2 service is an outlier among 40–50 minute calibrations and is never flagged.

**Rounding and the 5-minute inter-job gap.** A1 treats logged times as points plus a qualitative \(\pm 2.5\) band. Waiting times are differences of two rounded clocks (\(\pm 5\) min) and that band is not propagated into the case table. I2 (and often I1) shows a repeated 5-minute gap between consecutive jobs. Under the stated rounding, those gaps may be continuous work, changeover, or logging delay. Closing them as rounding would raise S1 utilization well above the reported 0.75–0.85. Adjacent C1 events at 11:00 (P07 end / P09 start) can overlap once rounding is taken seriously; A4’s “never two cases at once” is asserted, not checked.

**Utilization ranges are the strongest station-level numbers, and they are only in-window.** 535–605 min busy on 720 min S1 capacity and 515–570 on 705 min S2 capacity are correct given the missing-start windows and elapsed OPEN time. Central utilizations ~0.79 / ~0.77 follow. What is not justified is treating the pessimistic zero-service end as an operational scenario, and not splitting I1/I2 or C1/C2. The C2 outage is correctly described as secondary (~15 min, a fraction of one job); the cascade through P08 → P11 is real but overstated as “the outage forces P11 start ≥ 12:00” (the binding constraint is P08’s 12:00 completion).

**Bottleneck story is directionally right, over-precise in places.** Early S2 feed-limited, then both stations loaded, S2 holding most WIP by 14:00, time-varying combination — that reading survives the imputation scenarios and is the right qualitative conclusion. It is not true that S1 “has capacity headroom” as a general early-morning claim without showing occupancy, and it is not true that mid-morning congestion is driven by accelerating S1 throughput. Theoretical rates (S1 ~4/h at 30 min jobs, S2 ~3/h at 40 min jobs, arrivals 6.5/h then 4/h) should have been on the table before the narrative.

**Section 3 table.** Required special cases are present and mostly correct (P05 \(T_2=50\), P07 \(T_1=75\), P11 \(T_2=80\), P16 \(W_2\ge 85\), P22 \(W_1\ge 110\), elapsed OPEN times). Aggregation of the “unremarkable” cases is allowed but is so coarse it hides the monotone rise in sojourn from 60 to 230 minutes. Total sojourn for completed cases is discussed in prose, not tabulated. Censored S2 waits for P18–P20 (\(\ge 50, 35, 15\)) are omitted from the explicit rows even though they are part of the 14:00 queue.

**Python (requirement 8 is only partly met).** The code builds the table, distinguishes MISSING/OPEN/BLANK as statuses, calls `format_dataframe`, runs three imputation scenarios for *starts*, calls the two specified PM4Py helpers with a case-level disclaimer, and comments why pandas is safer than `convert_log_to_time_intervals`. Failures against the spec: (i) no station queue-length/WIP path and no utilization calculation, despite the report citing a grid simulation; (ii) OPEN elapsed service is zeroed to `None` rather than computed; (iii) P22’s censored S1 wait is not computed (only S2 censoring); (iv) A10’s OPEN optimistic/pessimistic rules are not implemented; (v) central midpoints are not rounded to 5 minutes as in the text (P07 central start 09:42:30 in code vs 09:45 in the report); (vi) `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are applied to fully observed S1/S2 intervals only, so external `arrival_s1` is absent, P05/P07/P11/P15/P16–P22 are incomplete or missing, and the helpers’ case-level waiting/arrival semantics are not valid on that projection — the task required a projection on which they *are* valid (completed cases with both intervals, plus arrival). Printing `.head()` of that projection does not inspect the cases that matter.

**What remains stable, and why this is not a 4.** Counts by subperiod, completed sojourns, missing-start *resource* windows, in-window busy-time brackets, 14:00 residual state (8 cases: 2 at S1, 6 at S2), and the qualitative O/C/P stability discussion are right. The answer does not confuse service with sojourn and does not drop censored cases in the narrative. Those are necessary for a passing reconstruction; they are not sufficient for a high score under a strict bar, because the quantitative WIP/Little picture is off, FCFS is inconsistent, rounding/gaps are under-analyzed, the heading contract is broken, and the code does not back the claims that depend on it.
