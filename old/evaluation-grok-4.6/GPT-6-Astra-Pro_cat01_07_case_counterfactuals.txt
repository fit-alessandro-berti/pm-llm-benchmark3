**7.4**

This is a strong, format-compliant answer with unusually careful arithmetic and the right conceptual distinctions (readiness vs completion, reservation vs service, policy change vs time saving, local gain vs non-additive scale-up). It is not nearly flawless. Several “base” schedules smuggle in favorable slots, a few causal stories are incomplete, and uncertainty is better advertised than enforced.

## What is actually good

The feasibility model is the right shape: a partial order plus declarative constraints, not one fake global sequence. Rework, originals, high-risk compliance, four-eyes, and K-411’s missing `I ≺ D` edge are all treated as distinct. Intervention classification is correct in substance: K-219 is routing, K-337 is decision policy, K-411 is an implementation serialization, K-104 is timing plus extra preparatory demand.

The derivations do real propagation instead of subtracting service times. K-337’s base case is the clearest example: compliance eligibility moves 96 h earlier, but a longer Friday-to-Monday signing wait absorbs much of the gain, leaving −48 h rather than −96 h. K-411 correctly notes that an earlier verification join can *increase* assessment wait and still yield only −1.5 h. The D1 collision between K-219 (Tue 09:30–13:30) and K-337 (Tue 08:30–14:30) is a real inconsistency in the traces, and treating the four histories as jointly infeasible is the right move. Cycle-time arithmetic for the factual cases (58.5 h, 48 h, 214 h 10 min, 29.5 h) is correct.

K-337 is not treated as a free speed-up. The path keeps final compliance and four-eyes, and the decision becomes conditional approval or rejection. That is the central normative point, and it is handled well. Capacity analysis at 100 cases is also directionally right: C1 is one person, reservations do not create capacity, D1→D2 substitution can raise total hours, parallel checks migrate congestion, and 100 local ΔT values are not a system effect.

## Material flaws

**1. “Base” cases repeatedly assume scarce downstream slots that the traces do not support.**  
All four factual `Decision` intervals are on Wednesday. K-219’s base −16.5 h depends on an invented Tuesday 16:00–16:20 M1 interval. If M1 remains at the factual Wednesday 08:30 slot, an earlier D2/A2 completion can produce **ΔT ≈ 0** even with no rework. The write-up admits this, then still labels −16.5 h as base. That is a calibration failure, not a minor caveat. K-104’s base likewise starts C1 Wednesday 08:00 despite a Wednesday 08:00 snapshot of **C1: 8 h**. The −6 h is therefore largely queue-jumping from the reservation, not the advertised 2 h of reusable reading. A non-priority counterfactual with a 2.5 h review starting near the factual 13:10 slot would likely finish in time to keep Wednesday afternoon signing and save little or nothing.

**2. K-104’s preliminary C1 hour is incompatible with the Tuesday snapshot.**  
Tuesday 08:00 shows C1 with 16 h of work. The base still inserts C1 on Tuesday 10:30–11:30. No dispatch story explains how the only certified reviewer is free then unless another urgent case is displaced. The interference section discusses this in the abstract; the local derivation does not. The same problem hits the best case (C1 Tuesday 19:30 while the morning backlog is 16 h).

**3. The K-104 worst case is not a failed intervention; it is an unexplained regression.**  
If a provisional booking is lost, the natural fallback is the factual Wednesday review, i.e. ΔT ≈ 0. Jumping to Thursday 08:00–12:30 and ΔT = +19 h requires a specific mechanism (lost place in queue, override, wasted slot that cannot be backfilled). That mechanism is named later as a risk, not derived in the worst-case path. As written, the +19 h looks like an envelope endpoint chosen for width.

**4. Constructed scenarios are used as if they were ranges.**  
Section 1 correctly says the numbers are not confidence intervals. Sections 2–3 still report “expected outcome range” as −9 to +19, −21.5 to +48, −113 to 0, −6 to 0. The prose then admits worse delays (longer C1 review, longer supplier waits—K-337’s own second wait was already 70.5 h vs the 8–36 h guide). K-219’s sign-change threshold at 25.6% is a two-point mixture, not a sensitivity analysis. Under the evaluation rubric, that is weak uncertainty calibration.

**5. Queue snapshots are used opportunistically.**  
When a snapshot helps (K-411 D2 starting ~12:00 after a 4 h Tuesday queue), it is treated as informative. When it blocks a neat schedule (C1 8–16 h, A2 6 h on Tuesday), it becomes “only a forecast.” That double standard is the main temporal-consistency defect.

## Smaller but real issues

The partial order lists `A_j ≺ Q_j` as if an information request were a normal successor rather than an optional exception. `O_(j+1) ≺ D_(j+1)` is similarly stated too generally. These are recoverable from comments, but the relation set is not actually the constraint set.

K-219’s base D2 interval 22:30–04:30 is overnight. Overnight document work exists for D1 in K-104, not for D2. A Tuesday-morning D2 start is at least as plausible and would shrink the claimed no-rework gain.

Snapshot color is underused: Monday’s “two urgent compliance cases” is the obvious threat to K-104 reservations; Tuesday’s “assessor in training” bears on A2 slots used in K-219 and K-411. Neither is tied to the local schedules.

The table path `{Identity ∥ Document Check}` for K-104 suggests concurrency that the unchanged factual timestamps do not produce. Harmless if read as a partial order; slightly misleading as a counterfactual path.

Ranking K-411 first is defensible on feasibility, but the robustness table underweights the stated integrity risk of removing the lock. If the lock is a consistency control, K-411 is not a timing-only change; it is a feasibility-breaking workaround. That caveat is present, then ignored by the rank.

## Rubric view

- **Minimal change:** generally good (K-337 prefix preserved; K-411 service hours unchanged). Weakened by extra M1/C1 opportunities in base cases.  
- **Temporal consistency:** arithmetic is clean; slot feasibility is not.  
- **Process feasibility:** compliance, four-eyes, originals, and rework rules are respected. No hard violation found.  
- **Uncertainty:** advertised well, executed as wide handmade envelopes.  
- **Interference:** conceptually strong; local bases still spend scarce C1/M1 capacity as if free.

The answer is well above a generic process-mining write-up and below a trustworthy operational counterfactual. The decisive deductions are the optimistic base slots, the unreconciled C1 backlog, and ranges that are not actually worst/best bounds. Those are not cosmetic.
