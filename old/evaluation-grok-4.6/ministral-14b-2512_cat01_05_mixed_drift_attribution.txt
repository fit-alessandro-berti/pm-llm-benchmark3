**5.3**

The macro story is the right one: March is mostly semantic/provenance, later movement is real, and the plausible engines are supplier rework, staffing queues, priority redistribution, and autumn case mix. The required ten-section skeleton is present. That is not enough. Under a strict reading the answer is repeatedly sloppy in the places the rubric cares about—process-mining rigor, causal restraint, and quantitative reasoning.

**Format and restraint.** Headings are not the required sentence case. Section 1 never uses `seasonal` as an onset type even though the prompt asked for sudden / gradual / recurring / seasonal / mixed. Evidence scores of 80–95 are incompatible with “deliberately non-unique” evidence. September 10 (Z recovery: rework 27→20, Z 38→24, cycle 45.0→41.5) is a first-class interval and is missing.

**Section 1 has real mechanism errors, not nits.** March is labeled control-flow; after mapping, `Register→Eligibility` is still 100 and `Eligibility→CollectDocs` is still 98. June treats `missing_resource_pct = 6.2` as a staffing signature; the brief says missingness is concentrated in migrated activities. July ties the priority rule to `Assess→SeniorAssessment = 9`; the documented rule is queue order, and the Senior Assessment jump is October (22). The brief also says that distinction was historically recorded inconsistently—the strongest competing account of the October DFG—and it is almost unused.

**Section 2 fails its own logic.** The persistent raw–mapped *gap* (~0.37 from March onward) is the label/duplicate problem. Real control-flow drift is the *mapped* decline (0.92→0.86). The answer cites the gap as evidence of behavioral drift. Missing resources are said to “distort conformance checks by introducing spurious paths”; they do not. Duplicate `Open Case` events do. “Proving … not behavioral” is then immediately walked back. Word count is barely inside the band and the four required points are thin.

**Section 3 is quantitatively broken.** Case-mix 10–15h + rework 5–10h + service 0.6h + queueing 15–20h = 31–46h against a January→October *median* rise of 16.3h. Shares that cannot add to the target are not a decomposition. Distance is described as frequency-weighted Euclidean on per-100 rates, reported on a 0–1 scale, and coded as unweighted Euclidean on the 100-point vectors—three different procedures. March’s *normalized* distance is blamed on `Eligibility Screen` / `Open Case`, which that fragment has already removed. May is invented as a logging change point (“incomplete docs misclassified as Validate Eligibility”) with no support; `distinct_activity_labels` is flat at 11 after March. `fast_variant_pct` (58→35) and p90 (69→121) are sitting in the table and barely used.

**Sections 4–6 mix good structure with mislabels.** Eight hypotheses and the required mechanisms are there, but ranking treats pure logging as the weakest *global* competitor instead of the best account of one artifact. Staffing is again “supported” by missing resources. Section 5 calls priority-induced delay redistribution a *population* pattern. Primary monitoring population = low-complexity non-Z cases is a control stratum, not the operational population; it conditions away the cells where drift occurred.

**Section 7 is complete and only moderately useful.** Eleven monitors cover the required families and thresholds are not identical. Then the event-order monitor allows a 105% rate on a per-case first edge; there is no raw-vs-mapped *gap* monitor (the actual logging detector); rolling 3-month medians as the seasonal reference will absorb the October ramp; Bonferroni-plus-handwaving is not a multiple-testing design.

**Section 8 would not run as analysis.** Part A drops most series. `df_monthly["baseline_relative"] = df_monthly.iloc[0]` is not a baseline comparison. The change-point predicate

`(raw_fitness_change < -0.10) | (raw_fitness_change.shift(1) > -0.10)`

flags February and then essentially every later month—persistence of a March drop, not a change point. Distance in code disagrees with the table. Part B never imports `pm4py`, never offers skeletons/DECLARE, uses `filter_time_range` with `*-31` for every month, passes a nonexistent `variant_threshold` into `get_variants`, calls `discover_temporal_profile(log, activity)` as if it were a single-activity API, and runs `conformance_token_based_replay` on a dataframe with no net. `.map(mapping_dict)` NaNs unmapped labels. That is not “consistent with the PM4Py manual.”

**Sections 9–10 repeat the same causal mistakes.** Attribution is readable and the mediation paragraph is the best part of the essay, but +12.7h is a January→July total hung on June 15, and May’s entire +5h is hung on Z. Rule 3 again fires staffing off missing resources. Nothing in the seven rules separates a recurring seasonal signature from a new shock—the explicit monitoring requirement.

Directionally competent, locally unreliable. The mistakes that matter (non-additive decomposition, gap vs. mapped fitness, missingness as capacity, broken change-point logic, wrong PM4Py, ignored Senior Assessment semantics) are exactly the ones another judge was told to score.
