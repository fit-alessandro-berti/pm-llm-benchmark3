**4.7**

The answer shows working familiarity with event-time reconstruction and gets most of the easy case outcomes right, but it is not close to the required standard. Formalization is broken, the mandatory checker is a stub, and at least one required alternative reading is factually wrong. Under a strict reading those are not cosmetic defects.

**Temporal semantics are directionally correct but underspecified.** Forward-fill, blank-as-no-update, no backward leakage, and `event_index` as a same-timestamp tie-break are the right defaults. Distinguishing event, effective, record, and evidence time is also the right vocabulary. The material-change rule is too thin: it is reduced to “compare the value at an event with the immediately preceding review,” which collapses R1’s “latest material data change,” R4’s >20% amount test, and non-amount updates (jurisdiction, risk, customer type) into one vague comparison. That later shows up as missed triggers.

**The rule table and pseudo-logic fail as formalization.** Comparison operators are simply missing: R1 is written as `jurisdiction DE and amount 100000`, R5 as `effective_from trigger`, and the Datalog as `amount(C,t,A)100000` / `jurisdiction(C,t,J)"DE"`. As written the rules are not evaluable. R2 only requires some earlier `Enhanced_Review` and some later `Compliance_Approve`; it does not require the review to be *relevant* to the HIGH/PEP state. R3 does not bind the list update to the *current* jurisdiction, and the negated screen atom is malformed. R4 is treated as a standalone “must re-do Enhanced_Review” rule rather than an invalidation rule for amount-dependent obligations. R6/R7 are meta-constraints stuffed into the same table without a trigger. This is not a usable formalization.

**Case reasoning is the strongest part and still has real errors.** TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-305, TX-307, TX-308, TX-309, and TX-311 are classified correctly on the main path. That is not enough.

- TX-302 is internally inconsistent. There *is* a timely `Enhanced_Review` and no later material change, so R1 is satisfied on the primary path. `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` is the exemption path, not the facts. The exemption is a valid backup, not the reason the case passes.
- TX-306 omits R1 entirely. Release state is SG / 230000; R1 is live. The sub-20% increase is why R1 is still satisfied, not why it is untriggered.
- TX-301 should at least record R4 as the reason the `Standard_Review` is worthless for the amount-dependent duty.
- TX-310 as `NONCOMPLIANT` / medium confidence is defensible under R5 (backdated display, cert after release, requester-witness), but the brief presents this as a designed evidential conflict. Treating it as a clean fail, with only a short hedge, is too decisive.

**Section 4 contains a clear factual mistake on a required case.** Reversing TX-311’s same-timestamp `event_index` does **not** leave jurisdiction `US` at release. Both orders apply `Update_Jurisdiction → DE` before `Release_Funds`. The order changes *what the Enhanced_Review saw*, not the release snapshot. The alternative that would actually flip the case is a jurisdiction update after release, or evaluating R1 on review-time state instead of release-time state. That error is especially damaging because same-timestamp ordering is one of the skills the task is testing.

**The PM4Py/pandas section does not do the assigned job.** The block reconstructs the CSV, parses timestamps, calls `format_dataframe`, sorts, and `ffill`s. That is items 1–3 and a fragment of 4. It does not track latest `Standard_Review` / `Enhanced_Review` / `Compliance_Approve` / `Sanctions_Screen` / `Sanctions_List_Update` / material change; does not evaluate coarse R1–R4 flags; does not join the exemption register; does not emit a case-level diagnostics frame with evidence timestamps and unknown-value flags; and does not show even a coarse DECLARE/trace-order check with an explanation of why control-flow conformance cannot carry these duties. “Simplified excerpt” plus `print(...head())` is not an executable checker. On a rubric that explicitly scores usefulness of the code, this is a major miss.

**Section 6 names the six required failure modes instead of explaining them.** Final-state leakage, post-hoc exemptions, post-review material changes, conditional activation, missing data, and same-timestamp order are listed in one sentence. There is no account of *how* a control-flow model would misfire on these traces (e.g., TX-305 looking like a 150k foreign release if the settlement amount is leaked backward; TX-310 looking conforming if `Record_Exemption` is just another event; TX-309 looking conforming because an `Enhanced_Review` exists somewhere in the trace). Listing is not analysis.

**Section 7 is adequate and is the most complete of the required write-ups.** Immutable release payloads, split signature/effective/record times, rule version, exemption scope, change provenance, and a monotonic source sequence are the right controls. They are still only a paragraph of headlines, with no concrete event schema.

**Net:** a competent sketch of the cases, not a finished compliance formalization. The broken operators, hollow checker, TX-302 misclassification, missed TX-306 R1 trigger, and wrong TX-311 counterfactual keep it well below any “nearly flawless” band.
