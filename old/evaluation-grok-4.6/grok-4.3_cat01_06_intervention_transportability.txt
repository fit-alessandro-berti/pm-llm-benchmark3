**5.6**

The answer gets the headline right—do not copy Site A wholesale; segment; treat the before/after as confounded—but it is not close to flawless. Structural, quantitative, and modeling errors are frequent enough that several required deliverables fail on their own terms.

**What is actually solid.** Site A’s 9-hour median drop is not treated as an identified treatment effect. Co-interventions (API, hiring, mix shift, huddles, timestamp inflation) are recognized. The statutory originals gate is kept. A scenario exists in which the median improves and the tail worsens. `conditional go` is the defensible call. The transport graph uses the required `S_` selection nodes. The pilot at least tries to separate “overlap never happened” from “overlap happened and nothing moved.”

**Section 1.** Twelve required features appear, but several cells are imprecise. “Cannot create Document Check until originals received” is written as a universal Site B fact; it is a 62% legal subset. Site B case mix is written as “48% high-complexity + 40% legal,” which treats overlapping attributes as additive. “Parallelization cannot touch Legal Review” misstates the issue: Legal Review is already downstream of Assessment; the risk is congestion and supplement re-entry, not accidental reordering of Legal Review. Lost arrows make the prerequisite column hard to read.

**Section 2.** The explanation is ~130 words against a required 180–280. That is a hard miss. The graph is incomplete as a transport diagram: `waiting_before_assessment -> cycle_time` is the main structural pathway and is not stated as a forward edge; `originals_required` does not block `true_overlap`; `document_capacity` and `identity_capacity` are mostly sinks. `digital_documents -> originals_required` is the wrong polarity. `API_latency -> identity_capacity` confuses latency with staffing. Shared mechanisms that the stem actually gives (independent external data, digital-at-registration, waiting-before-assessment as the putative mediator) are under-specified. The claim that a similar control-flow diagram is insufficient is correct but thin.

**Section 3.** The prompt asked for all major before/after metrics. Missing: completed volume, p90, **waiting before Assessment** (the mediator), both service times, and appeals. The table is also garbled (“4132 h”, “871 %”, “4840 %”, “1820”, “11.812.4 %”), which is not a cosmetic issue—it makes the evidence unreadable. Co-occurring changes are only partly covered. Confidences are directionally conservative, which is the one virtue of the table.

**Section 4.** Coverage of the ten required modifiers is there; several mechanisms are wrong. Role specialization is treated only as an obstacle to concurrency. At Site B it is the opposite and the constraint at once: two pools can execute in true parallel, but the 4/7/3/2 split makes imbalance and downstream saturation likely. Digital claims with “harmful signature: none” is complacent (incomplete packets, uncoordinated checkers, rework). Legal-review mitigation (“stage after Assessment”) is the current process, not a mitigation. Copying Site A’s WIP of six onto a four-person identity pool is cargo-cult operations. Complexity and the legal-review flag are collapsed as if they were the same stratum.

**Section 5.** The 38% “digital” share is reused for designs that also exclude legal review. Those restrictions do not multiply to 38%. If the flags were independent the eligible share would be about \(0.38\times0.60\approx 23\%\); if they are positively correlated it is smaller. That error is reused in §§5–7 and 9. No mapping from Site A’s *confounded* 6-hour wait drop / ~3.5-hour serial identity time to the stated ranges is shown, despite an explicit request for that reasoning. Optimistic 6–9 hours largely recycles Site A’s contaminated 9-hour headline. The bottleneck-shift and IT-lock rows are the best quantitative work in the answer.

**Section 6.** Eligibility is the right idea; the process spec then breaks. Assessment is said to wait for Legal Review. Site B’s rules and the DFG both put Legal Review *after* Assessment (`Assessment -> Legal Review` 1,360; `Assessment -> Approve/Reject` 1,940). Originals Receipt is treated as a task the document pool “owns”; it is an exogenous arrival delay. The textual POWL forces originals and legal onto the candidate path even though eligibility excluded them, and it does not cleanly encode Assessment → Approve/Reject without legal. The DFG is almost unused (identity already starts after Register in 2,050 cases; identity and originals already interleave).

**Section 7.** Skeleton is competent; arithmetic is not. Site B finishes ~3,400 cases in six months (~131/week). Eight weeks is ~1,050 cases. The eligible slice cannot supply 600 per arm. Case-level randomization on shared pools of 4 / 7 / 3 / 2 is heavy interference; “separate team queues” is not a credible fix with two legal reviewers. Stop rules and §9 expansion rules do not match. The mechanism-vs-implementation paragraph is the strongest short prose in the answer.

**Section 8 (largest technical failure).** Site A trees are fine. The Site B POWL is not a valid POWL model of the candidate process. `StrictPartialOrder` cannot contain a cycle; `supplement -> document` plus `document -> assessment -> legal -> supplement` is a cycle. Choice and loops require `OperatorPOWL` (XOR / LOOP). `SilentTransition` and `OperatorPOWL` are imported and unused, so optional legal review and the supplement loop—the two structures the prompt required—are not encoded. Partial-order edges to both `Approve` and `Reject` mean both must occur. Originals and legal are forced, not optional. `pm4py.check_is_workflow_net` is not a reliable top-level API as written. The comment about what Petri nets cannot encode is correct and does not salvage an invalid net.

**Section 9.** Below the 250-word floor (~200). The 38% figure is repeated for a digital ∩ non-legal stratum. “Must not be copied” is the wrong verb for Site B’s lock and statutory gates: those are B-constraints to retain or relax, not A-features to refuse. Expansion thresholds lost their inequality symbols, so the decision rule is not parseable. The transferable object—overlap of *already unblocked*, informationally independent checks—is never stated as cleanly as the non-transferable list.

**Section 10.** Eight rows in the required shape. Several are good (lock, timestamps, mix, API). Two fight the design: supplement independence matters little if legal cases are excluded; WIP=6 is assumed rather than justified. The important unstated assumption is that identity and document work are informationally independent *at B* (stated for A, never evidenced for B), and that the legal flag is known at registration.

**Net.** This is a structured, mostly well-aimed transport memo with a correct recommendation, undermined by an invalid process model, a propagating eligibility-share error, an inverted Assessment/Legal dependency, missing/garbled evidence rows, skipped quantitative reasoning, impossible pilot numbers, and two word-count violations. Under a near-flawless bar, that is mid-pack, not high.
