**6.1**

The write-up has the right skeleton and mostly the right process-mining instincts: regimes are not forced into one cut, outage/recovery is not treated as healthy throughput, median service is not used as mean flow time, Little’s Law is refused where boundaries or throughput proxies collapse, and assumptions are labeled. That is why this is not a low score. Under a strict reading it is also not close to excellent.

**What holds up.** The primary split (baseline weekday / baseline weekend / outage-recovery / high-complex / intervention) is defensible and operationally useful. The alternative (mix-break first) is actually different, not a relabeling. Section 6’s verdicts for R1 (`approximate only`), R3 (`not defensible`), and the full pool (`not defensible`) are the correct calls. Carry-over, 07:00 backlog, and the arrivals-vs-completions gap are handled as first-class threats rather than footnotes. The 04-16 staff spike is correctly kept separate from the documented 04-29 intervention.

**Calculation and consistency failures (these are not rounding noise).** The series is four weeks, **28 days**, which the embedded table itself contains. The prose repeatedly uses **30 days** and therefore reports the wrong pooled levels: arrivals ≈84 vs ≈90, completed ≈80 vs ≈86, WIP ≈27.9 vs ≈29.9, staff ≈5.7 vs ≈5.14. The headline “pooled ratio ≈14.7 arrivals/FTE, a balanced middle that does not exist” is built on those bad denominators. Even internally it contradicts the author’s own weekend figure (≈17). R4 weekday Little’s Law with completions is **≈308 min, not 256**; the stated 16-minute proxy gap is really ≈36 minutes, which weakens the “approximate only” case they were trying to make. R4 `complex_pct` mean is ≈43.8, not ≈44.9. The 04-07 hourly vector sums to **101, not 93**, so the quoted Fano ≈3.6 is off; the bursty 04-21 Fano is ≈10, not “6.2+”. The 7.6 FTE / “+40% load” staffing claim does not follow from the R4 weekday numbers (arrivals and service rise together).

**Code does not meet the spec as a runnable artifact.** The R5 window line is a syntax error (`isin(...)` never closes the outer `(`). The same window is named `R5_Weekdays_Only` but includes Sat/Sun, so it does not implement the matrix row it is supposed to support. Prose says n=30; the dataframe has 28 rows. Hourly Fano in code would not match the numbers advertised in Section 5.

**Missed evidence.** The “representative” hourly profiles are treated as that calendar day’s actual clock hours. Their two-hour 08:00–09:59 shares do not match `peak_08_10_pct` (04-21 is the obvious break: vector ≈10% vs table 47). A data audit that lists five threats should have flagged that inconsistency and stopped treating those bins as a full stochastic hourly census.

**Lesser but real issues.** Lag-1 WIP ≈0.70 is asserted, not computed. The Simpson’s-paradox language is mostly aggregation bias. Load compounding in R4 is understated. None of these would matter if the quantitative core were tight; together with a non-running code block they keep the piece in the “competent structure, sloppy execution” band.

A high score here required coherent arithmetic, a working reproduction block, and no 28-vs-30 audit miss. This answer has the framework of a strong paper and the numerical discipline of a draft.
