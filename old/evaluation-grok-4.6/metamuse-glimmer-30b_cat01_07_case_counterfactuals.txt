**6.2**

The answer is conceptually competent on the points the rubric cares about most—K-337 is treated as a **decision-policy** change rather than a time cut; earlier readiness is distinguished from earlier completion; four-eyes and final-compliance-after-assessment are preserved; queues are treated as forecasts; interference is named. It is not close to flawless. Under a hypercritical reading the defects are frequent enough to keep it well out of the 8–10 band.

**Format and completeness.** The eight required headings exist and the tables have the right columns, but two hard length constraints are missed. The 100-case rollout is ~180 words against a stated 250–400; the final synthesis is ~200 against 220–320. Both passages therefore stay at slogan level on topics the prompt explicitly required to be developed (C1 reservation arithmetic, D1/D2 substitution under volume, weekend external-response batching, timestamp vs true concurrency). The case table also omits a markdown header rule, and the first feasibility fence mixes unconditional partial order with conditionals that were supposed to live in the second block.

**Temporal consistency.** This is the clearest factual miss. For K-411 the derivation states Document Check “can start at Triage completion Tue 09:35.” The Tue 08:00 snapshot gives D2 a 4 h queue; a 09:00 arrival cannot seize D2 at 09:35. Realistic concurrent start is ~Tue 12:00, overlapping I2, not a skip of the document queue. That error feeds an inflated best-case of −12 h. K-104’s −24 h best case is also poorly anchored: Assessment still ends Tue 19:10, so even an immediate reserved C1 review of ~3 h plus Decision/Notify cannot beat roughly −18 to −20 h versus Wed 18:40. K-219’s −18 to −24 h best case similarly underweights Tue assessment congestion (A2 ~6 h at 08:00). Elsewhere the same author *does* invoke queue absorption, so the failure is inconsistency, not ignorance.

**Process feasibility.** Control-flow is mostly right: no-originals concurrency for K-104/K-411; Originals Receipt before Document Check for K-219/K-337; Compliance after the latest Assessment for high-risk cases; A1 ≠ C1. Gaps: Identity-before-Originals is never stated as allowed; K-104 never resolves whether a “provisional C1 slot” is a calendar hold or actual C1 service during a 20 h Monday backlog (those are different interference mechanisms); Friday M1 availability after a pulled-forward K-337 compliance review is ignored.

**Uncertainty and interference.** Ranges and reverse-evidence paragraphs are present, which is a real strength. Calibration is still loose (point-ish best cases, “positive short term” system-wide sign for K-337 in §4 while §5/§7 treat it as the most dangerous policy). The 100-case discussion lists the right nouns—C1 scarcity, D1↔D2 substitution, extra/avoided rework, assessment bottleneck migration, weekends, false concurrency—but does not show the nonlinearities. One certified reviewer cannot absorb 100 reservations; Monday C1 is already at 20 h; prelim reads on incomplete packets can *consume* C1 without shortening finals; shifting 100 handwritten files onto D2 can create a rework amplifier, not a shorter D1 queue. Those are the calculations a strict grader expects.

**Ranking and pilots.**  K-411 > K-104 > K-219 > K-337 is defensible if robustness and reversibility dominate cycle time, and the pilot rows cover every required element. Calling K-411 highest-information-value “medium” while ranking it first is slightly in tension with the “ability to learn” criterion, but not incoherent.

**Net.** Strong skeleton, correct high-level causal story, several required boxes only half-filled, and at least one queue-ignoring start time that the rubric is designed to punish. That is mid-pack work, not near-flawless work.
