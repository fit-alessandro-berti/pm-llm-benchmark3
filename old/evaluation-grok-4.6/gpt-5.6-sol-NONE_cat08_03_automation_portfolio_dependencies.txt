**7.4**

The answer is strong process-mining work: it refuses frequency-first ranking, uses the DFG and resource network, keeps humans accountable for KYC, and blocks A11 until controls are real. It is not near-flawless. Under a strict rubric the planning contradictions, a soft reading of the compliance constraint, and several ranking/portfolio mismatches are enough to keep it well below the top of the scale.

**Internal contradiction on the two-integration constraint.** Section 2’s “recommended allocation” of deep core integrations is **A6 + A9**. Section 6, for the portfolio the author actually picks, uses **A6 + A4** and demotes A9 to a year-two shadow test. That is not a nuance; it is two incompatible plans for the one hard IT constraint the prompt required the answer to respect. Section 2 also says A4 can start as a workbench on KYC APIs and read-only case data, then Phase 1 “establishes” A4 as deep integration 2. A reader cannot tell which design is intended, whether A4 actually needs core-banking depth, or what happens to the second integration slot if A9 is out of scope.

**The matrix and the recommendation do not agree.** Individual ranks 1–4 are A5, A6, A4, A9 (A9: high process-level benefit, high first-year suitability). The chosen four are A4, A5, A6, A7, with A7 sitting at rank 6 below A9, A3, and A12. Complementarity can justify a swap, but the text never reconciles why A9 is more suitable as a standalone than A7 and then is dropped from the year-1 set. That makes the rank column look like a separate essay from the portfolio.

**A4 is a weak substitute for the mandatory control module.** Compliance asked for at least one first-wave module that *strengthens controls*, not only labor. A12 and A9 are built for that (remediation governance; contract-vs-decision checks; 8.5% / 14% renewal loops; 650→510 remediation DFG). A4 is a low-volume specialist assistant whose main cited prize is a 31-hour wait. Calling it the control module only “if implemented conservatively” is a hedge, not a design. A first-year set of A4/A5/A6/A7 can be sold as bottleneck work; it is much harder to sell as satisfying the compliance gate. Phase 0 “interim manual hard gates” for KYC-before-signing and four-eyes implicitly admits the portfolio does not encode those controls.

**A5 as rank 1 overstates what the module can do.** Request-missing-documents wait is 8 hours and “customer-response delay dominates.” A checklist/nudge does not own that wait. The 3,300 / 2,800 loop and 52% repeat rate are the real target, and those are important, but they are not clearly larger than A4’s 31-hour critical-path wait (82% of 1,750 cases) or the 58-hour external valuation wait (2,350 cases; 71% of equipment finance). Standalone downstream risk for A5 is scored “low-positive,” which is too kind: cleaner files move into normalization (75 min, 20-hour wait) and credit analysis (110 min, 28-hour wait) on a 9-FTE pool the prompt already says will overload if inflow is cleaned without A6. That dependency belongs in the matrix score, not only later prose.

**A7’s end-to-end claim is inflated.** Feasibility of the wait activity is “low internal.” The 58 hours sit with external valuers. Triage, earlier request, and parallelization are process redesign around a partner the bank does not control. The DFG still has Credit analysis → Collateral request (2,400), so early launch is valuable—but only if policy allows valuation before credit is done, which is assumed rather than evidenced. Quality-dispute and partner-SLA risk is acknowledged, then the narrative still treats A7 as if it “targets the largest listed wait” in the same way A4 targets a bank-owned queue.

**A2 is missing from every portfolio.** Classification has 8% rework and “errors misroute cases.” Wrong product/exposure can drop collateral (71% vs 18%), skip committee, or break four-eyes. That is a control-path defect, not a convenience defect. A compliance-first set of A3/A4/A9/A12 that never touches routing is incomplete. The matrix correctly flags A2’s control risk as high; the portfolios then ignore it.

**Variant design is thinner than the artifacts allow.** The log is sliced by Micro / SME / High-exposure and by product. Four-eyes (5.8% of high-exposure, month-end), weekly committee, 14% renewal contract defects, and collateral incidence differ by slice. Portfolios are global. There is no explicit “A7 only earns a slot if equipment-finance share is material,” no high-exposure-first rule for A4/A9, no renewal-first rule for A9. Phase 1’s pilot on SME before high-exposure is the only real segmentation.

**Other precision problems.**

- “Low-positive” is not a defined rating; several cells mix impact direction with severity.
- Alternative portfolios never say which two modules consume the six-month integration budget. Only the preferred rollout does.
- A9’s “faster contracts increase signing queues” is weakly supported: signing wait is 44 hours and customer-dominated.
- Quick-win still leads with A1 after the same essay has shown back office is the *visible* queue, not the binding one (12 FTE, &lt;65% after 15:00; 1.5-hour wait; 4% rework; position 2 in 98% of cases). Including A1 as a “political” module is honest; treating it as a serious year-1 use of one of four slots undercuts the process-level thesis.
- Compliance-first pairs A3 + A4 onto 3 FTE / two seniors. The overload warning is present; a capacity model or a “not both in wave 1” rule is not.
- Case leakage (12,000 → 7,100 activations) and committee being only 3,900 of 8,900 credit-analysis cases are barely used.

**What still holds up.** Dependency direction is right: do not run A8/A9/A10/A11 on dirty KYC, statements, or decisions; keep suggestion/override/version audit; do not let A3/A4 auto-clear; hard-gate signing on KYC (6.5% provisional signing is used correctly); A6 before accelerating intake; A8 is gated by four artifacts, so fixing prerequisites beats auto-assembly, especially with a weekly committee cadence. Resource-shift analysis (analysts central, specialists concentrated, legal month-end, RMs causing document loops) is grounded. The A1-last argument in section 7 is the correct management answer.

**Net.** This is a coherent, artifact-aware memo with one real planning inconsistency, a compliance-requirement dodge, and a rank/portfolio mismatch. Those are structural flaws, not nits. Strictly, that is high-7 work, not 9–10.
