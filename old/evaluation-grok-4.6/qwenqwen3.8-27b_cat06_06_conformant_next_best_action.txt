**6.4**

The answer is structurally complete and substantively strong: it uses the six required sections, ranks A–G with the mandated conformance labels, keeps A/B as currently nonconformant, refuses to let operational forecasts legalize shortcuts, treats rescreening and SAR as non-substitutes for independent second review, and accounts for P-901, API quota, and S4 timing. Those are the main things the task rewards. It is not close to flawless.

The most serious problem is the **primary plan’s decision logic**. Rank 1 waits for S4 and interrupts P-901 only if S4 is “not confirmed by t30.” S4’s stated window is 35–65 minutes, so at t30 S4 is almost never already available. As written, the interrupt rule either fires almost always (collapsing the composite into a **delayed C**) or is too vague to operationalize. The same plan then quotes earliest continuation as t80–85, which is already at the 90-minute deadline under the most optimistic review/record/execute draws. Ranking that composite strictly above immediate C is only coherent as a P-901-protection choice; the writeup never makes the on-time probability of the S4 path explicit enough to justify that ranking.

There is a **clear timing error**. Action C’s 5–15 minutes is “to record approval/rejection and, if approved, execute.” The table repeatedly adds another 10–15 minutes of execution time from Action A, inflating earliest times (C as t50 rather than ~t40; the composite as t80–85). That error propagates through the counterfactuals and the 35-vs-65 spillover discussion.

**Process-cycle fidelity is incomplete.** The prefix ends at Evidence Validated; the normative cycle then requires Analyst Review before second-officer review. This is mentioned as a caveat in §4, not as a sequenced step in the ranked plan or traces. After Analyst Review has already occurred, Auto Clear (no alert and risk < 40) is also not a live path; the “screening clears ⇒ may proceed faster / second review may drop away” branch is too loose.

Smaller defects: benefit cells mix **mutually exclusive** worlds (on-time approval *and* regulatory savings from rejecting a true match) without stating they are alternative branches; the €100k regulatory floor is first treated as if it dominates with certainty, then only later qualified by probability; F at rank 3 sits awkwardly next to a 0.55–0.75 post-review approval probability; and “confirmed by t30–35” is used inconsistently across the table, §5, and §6.

The conformance mapping, F/G evidence requirements, 35-vs-65 reranking, and the final primary/fallback/trigger package are good. The answer is a solid expert draft with several real operational and timing flaws, not a near-flawless one.
