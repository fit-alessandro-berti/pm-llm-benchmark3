**6.3**

The final write-up matches the required eight-section structure and is strong on digital-twin framing: case/resource/bundle entities, non-stationary arrivals, calendars as first-class objects, rework loops, and explicit treatment of objections as unobserved. It does not claim optimality, and sections 4–7 are usable experiment, uncertainty, validation, and decision design. It is not close to flawless.

**Process-mining grounding is weaker than it looks.** The 18-month mix and variant table are barely used as calibration targets. The engine’s missing-document rates (e.g. 10% for Simple renovation) conflict with “Simple with missing docs = 19% of all cases” given a 45% Simple share. Commercial fire/environment/legal routing is similarly under-calibrated. Estimation is described as if it will be learned from a full log, but the executable path only sees a two-case excerpt and then overwrites key rates with invented numbers.

**PM4Py usage is only partly faithful.** Creating a pandas frame and calling `format_dataframe` / `convert_to_event_log` / `discover_dfg` / inductive discovery is appropriate, and avoiding a fake `read_csv` is correct. The process-tree handling is not. `pm4py.parse_process_tree` is specified in the prompt and, in current PM4Py, accepts the `->`, `*`, `X`, `+`, `tau` string form used here. Claiming it “expects PTML XML” and skipping the parse is an avoidable error on a stated requirement. Activity names in the frame (`Request missing docs`) also diverge from the tree and the excerpt.

**The simulation does not implement the model it describes.** Resource calendars, capacities, council weeks, travel, and queues are defined, then `_start_activity` adds a sampled duration to wall-clock time and never requests a server. That collapses waiting into elapsed service and violates the calendar/queue constraint in the actual dynamics. Architect bursts are a TODO; `trigger_objection` is never called; D3 never reduces travel; D2’s `prior_violations` is always false; D6/D7/D8/D9 are essentially absent; `demand_multiplier` is unused. Cross-case dependence is designed in prose and almost idle in the engine.

**Several quantitative pieces are wrong or oversold.** NHPP thinning does not find a true λ_max and does not normalize the Monday factor against Wednesday. Applicant delay uses a confused Pareto transform that will not match a 5-day median. Public notice is simultaneously a 14-day legal minimum and a lognormal “service” with p90 = 60d. CRN is a single `seed=rep` stream, which desynchronizes as soon as an intervention changes the event count. D5 can finalize a case while drafting is still running. SLA cutoffs and many branching probabilities are presented as decision inputs without being marked as ungiven.

**What still holds.** Assumptions are well partitioned and mostly realistic; missing starts and missing objections are not treated as observed; service vs wait is explained even if the engine ignores the split; the experiment matrix (baseline, D1–D10, extra combinations, five stresses) and the Welch / replication / CI / tail / sensitivity plan are the best parts of the answer. Those do not offset the PM4Py mistake, the uncalibrated routing, or a skeleton whose core DES still behaves like independent cases with no resources.
