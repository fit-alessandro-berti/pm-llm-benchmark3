**6.8**

The case conclusions are mostly right, but the answer is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has executable-code failures, formalization gaps, and several places where the reasoning is sloppy or overconfident.

**What is actually strong.** The 11 classifications are the defensible ones: TX-301/303/304/307/309/310 noncompliant, TX-302/306/308/311 compliant, TX-305 indeterminate. Event-time reconstruction is handled correctly in the narrative: blanks are not nulls, final state is not copied backward, TX-311 is decided by `event_index`, TX-305’s next-day settlement is not treated as the release amount, TX-308’s decrease does not kill the review, and TX-307’s standard packet does not satisfy R2. Sections 6 and 7 cover the required failure modes and control improvements.

**Code is not executable, which is a major miss.** The diagnostics print uses `di` rather than `diag`. The “executable” block then imports `pm4py.algo.conformance.decl`, which is not a reliable public path and will abort before the trace-order demo. The task asked for one runnable checker, not a conceptual sketch that crashes.

**The checker also gets the semantics wrong in places.** After `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `event_index` is glued back on by row position. If pm4py reorders same-timestamp events, TX-311’s review/jurisdiction order flips and the whole point of R7 is lost. R2 missing-data handling defaults the wrong way: unknown `customer_type` forces `r2_triggered = False`, and the later `REVIEW_REQUIRED` guard only catches unknown risk. That is a silent low-risk default, i.e. an R6 violation. Material-change detection compares timestamps only, appends both a time and `'UNCLASSIFIABLE'` into one list, and never uses `event_index` as a tie-break. Exemption handling is a quality flag, not R5.

**Formalization is incomplete and internally inconsistent.** The table is the right shape, but R1/R3/R4 often use raw timestamps while `before()` is defined on `(timestamp, event_index)`. `latest_material_change_time` does not match `material_change_between`. R2’s indeterminate path lives only in R6. “Material data change” is treated as amount-only; the policy text is broader than R4, and that choice is never defended.

**Section 4 undercuts itself.** The TX-311 alternative first says a review-time reading would be noncompliant, then immediately says it would still be compliant. TX-310 is labeled NONCOMPLIANT with **high** confidence even though the prompt says contradictory evidence has no single canonical answer (requester witness vs. post-release certificate). NONCOMPLIANT is reasonable; high confidence is not.

**Smaller nits.** Severity is invented with no scale. Section 6’s “final-state leakage” paragraph confuses control-flow blindness with forward-fill. Several temporal constraints use `≤` in one place and strict `<` in another without stating the consequence. None of these would matter in a generous grade; they matter here.

The legal read of the log is good. The formalization and especially the code are not. That is why this is a high-6, not an 8–10.
