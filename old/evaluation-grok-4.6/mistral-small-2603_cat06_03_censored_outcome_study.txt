**4.0**

The write-up has the right section scaffold and a few sound instincts (do not treat rejects as non-defaults; activation is a selection mechanism; prefer a temporal split; drop collections/outcome fields). Under a strict reading it still fails the assignment’s own quality bar: it trains on the convenient 21,600-row export, mishandles the 3,600 immature activated accounts, inverts prefix semantics, and never actually uses PM4Py.

## 1. Target definition

Choosing time-to-default after activation is defensible. The alternative competing-risk target is also a real, different question. That is the high point.

The rest is loose. The business ask is an **early** warning from the **first few events of every application**. Prediction “after Credit Review decision” is late—often after Accept/Reject—so the model is closer to post-underwriting account monitoring than to application-time warning. The estimand \(P(T>t\mid X,\text{activated})\) conditions on a post-prediction event unless prediction is already at Accept; that two-time-scale / selection issue is never stated. “Regardless of final outcome” is meaningless for an activated-only population. The competing-risk alternative treats **still-open** as a terminal class; it is an extraction-date administrative status, not a process outcome. Claiming the primary target “directly answers” the lender’s request oversells a restricted, selected-population estimand.

## 2. Cohort construction

The 48,000 arithmetic is correct. Almost everything else is not.

A survival primary target **must include** the 3,600 activated-but-immature cases as right-censored. Parking them outside the “primary analysis cohort” and fitting only the 21,600 mature labels is exactly the biased complete-case export the prompt warned against. Later activations and slower feeds are systematically dropped.

“Censored at 90 days” is the wrong time: immature cases should be censored at last follow-up / extract (or `label_available_at` logic), which is **before** 90 days. The 18,900 non-defaults are the ones observed through the horizon.

Still-open is again misclassified as a competing outcome. The ~24k/~24k policy split is invented; the calendar is 18 months pre-change and 6 months post-change. Label maturity as `data_extract_time - label_available_at` “should be 90 days” is the wrong formula. Transfers, 60% linkage, and partner missing feeds are not given operational inclusion rules. Prediction time is specified as the first event **after** the credit decision, which already conflicts with “first few events.”

## 3. Censoring and missing-label strategy

The 3,600 are described as “still active after 90 days.” That is backwards: they are cases whose 90-day outcome **or delayed feed has not matured**. Ordinary right-censoring, delayed labels, and incomplete follow-up are therefore collapsed into one wrong story.

Excluding partner accounts with missing feeds from the primary analysis is an unstated MNAR deletion. Sensitivity 1 mixes a valid composite/ITT endpoint (\(Y=1\) only if activated and defaulted) with a counterfactual “only if they would have activated,” which is not ITT. Best/worst-case bounds on transfers are fine.

Assumption labels are inconsistent: rejected-as-non-default is called both partially testable and unverifiable; “policy change only affects selection, not \(X\to T\)” is called unverifiable even though Section 7 is built to detect that shift. Delayed-feed dependent censoring is not discussed.

## 4. Prefix extraction

This section is process-mining-wrong.

Standard prefixes are events **from case start up to** the prediction instant. The design (and the code) takes events **at or after** “prediction time,” i.e. suffixes. Prediction time is the first event after Credit Review; the code uses the **last** Credit Review (`max` timestamp), so document loops have already finished and the “early” process is excluded.

“First 3 events after prediction time … to capture early process behavior” does not capture early behavior. After the last review the next events are Accept/Reject, Contract Signed, Activation—the very future the leakage section says to drop. Short-case and rename-mapping remarks are thin; invented activity names (`Credit Review (Old)`) are not in the schema. Multiple \(k\)-prefixes from the start and elapsed time from **application start** are never specified.

## 5–6. Features and leakage

Historical reference windows “prior to prediction time” are correct. So are dropping `collection_status`, future events, `label_available_at`, and `data_extract_time`, and repairing same-day `documents_total` / later `risk_score`.

`final_limit` is **not** “only available after activation”; the schema ties it to the **final credit decision**. Keep/drop therefore depends on a prediction time the authors never stabilize, and Section 5 contradicts Section 6. `decision_reason` is “keep” only because prediction was moved after the decision—circular. `default_90d` is marked **Keep** in a feature-or-data table; it is repeated on events when known and must be stripped from features. Resource experience is listed as a valid prefix feature in Section 5 and dropped in Section 6. Queue length is not in the file and is not derived.

## 7. Validation

The case against a random split is the strongest paragraph in the answer.

The scheme then breaks. The event window ends **31 December 2025**; a test set of applications **started** 1 Jan–15 Feb 2026 is empty. Starts from mid-Oct to Dec 2025 almost cannot have a mature 90-day **post-activation** label by 15 Feb 2026 (process time + 90 days + 7–35 day feed). The design talks about maturity and then ignores it. The code’s split does not even match Section 7 (no 2026 test slice; everything after 14 Oct 2025 is `val2`). AUC after coding censored cases as 0 is biased; no embargo for overlapping case lifetimes; no “evaluate only on labels that were mature as of the as-of date.”

## 8. Limitations

Best section: association ≠ selection ≠ counterfactual; three deployment feedback loops. Still overclaims that the model “accounts for” the policy change (training stops on 30 Jun 2025; that **detects** shift, it does not adjust for it). Transfer linkage, partner MNAR feeds, and delayed labels are missing from the claim boundary.

## 9. Code

Required PM4Py surface is absent: no `format_dataframe`, no `get_prefixes_from_log`, no DFG/variants/temporal helpers. `import pm4py` is dead. `sklearn.model_selection.train_test_split` is imported and unused—the opposite of the requested temporal-vs-`split_train_test` contrast.

Worse than missing APIs: `get_target` maps `censored_activated` → **0**, i.e. immature cases are labeled non-default. That is the dishonest target semantics the study was supposed to avoid. Outcomes are inferred from activity names, not `default_90d` / `label_available_at`. Terminal type uses unsorted `iloc[0]`. Prefix extraction is a post-decision suffix, then truncated before activation. Leakage “audit” only checks whether `final_limit` survived a feature builder that never included it. No column firewall for `collection_status`, `default_90d`, `final_limit`, `decision_reason`. Survival time and event indicator are comments only. The block is also well over the 80–160 line spec, with fragile `iloc[0]` feature expansion and a `tz_localize(None)` that throws on naive timestamps.

## Overall

The answer knows the vocabulary (censoring, competing risks, as-of, temporal shift) and then implements the weak design the rubric named: **fit the 21,600 mature activated rows, treat incomplete follow-up as non-default in code, predict from the wrong end of the trace, and skip the PM4Py pipeline.** Internal contradictions (prediction time, `final_limit`, ITT vs counterfactual, Section 7 vs code, 3,600 definition) keep it from scoring as a merely incomplete but statistically honest plan.
