**8.0**

This is a strong, internally coherent hybrid design. It uses each formalism for what it can actually express, puts the R5/R6 conflict in the open, and the T1–T8 matrix is mostly right. It is not nearly flawless. Several encodings over-claim, one published dictionary disagrees with the T6 “guarded” row, and a few authoritative constraints are stricter than the stated policy.

## What is actually good

The layering is the right one: Petri-net lifecycle and enabledness, DECLARE for case-wide obligations, Log Skeleton for counts, temporal profile as warnings only, and a data layer for R6/R9/R11 occurrence matching. Resolution A vs B is real, and choosing strict R5 because the log cannot prove an emergency is the auditable choice. A1–A14 are mostly load-bearing, not decoration. The net’s AND-join, XOR after `Classify Risk`, independent clarification monitor, and “no activation from `san_pending`” are the correct structural reading of the backbone plus R3/R5/R7. Unary vs binary DECLARE keys, `support`/`confidence`, Log Skeleton’s six keys, pair orientation, and hour→second conversions are correct. T1–T5, T7, and T8 are judged accurately if you accept the stated policy choices.

## Material problems

**1. The printed DECLARE model is not the model used in the T6 guarded row.**  
`nonsuccession[("Activate Vendor", "Obtain Legal Opinion")]` is unconditional. On T6 it fails as soon as `Obtain Legal Opinion@23` follows `Activate Vendor@13`, emergency evidence or not. Marking DECLARE `policy-dependent` under guarded R6 is only true of a *different* dictionary that was never written. The honest cells are: strict → `fail`; guarded → still `fail` for this `declare_model`, unless the nonsuccession rule is removed. That is a real inconsistency between §4 and §8.

**2. R5 is not encoded in DECLARE; a stronger rule is.**  
Response(escalation, opinion) + nonsuccession(activate, opinion) also rejects “activate, then an unsolicited opinion” when there was never an escalation. That is not R5. It is disclosed as conservative, but it is still a false-positive generator and should not be sold as an R5 encoding.

**3. The authoritative procedural layer rewrites R11.**  
R11 is eventual suspend after a post-activation bank change, and a *new* verify before any later first payment. The net hard-wires `Change → Suspend → Verify → (loop | pay)`. A legal trace `Change → Verify → Suspend → Pay` fails the net and can fail the dataframe check’s “reverify after suspend” test. A9 admits the strengthening; it should not have been the *authoritative* control-flow. The compact algebra also repeats `Reject Vendor` in the XOR and again in the rejected suffix, which is sloppy relative to the net.

**4. Extra DECLARE rules change the policy.**  
`succession(EDD, Compliance Approval)` forbids starting enhanced due diligence and then rejecting. `response(Manager Approval, Activate Vendor)` and the compliance analogue forbid approve-then-abort. Those are not in R1–R12. Vacuity is discussed in §9; this over-constraint is not treated with the same care.

**5. `directly_follows: set()` is undefended against PM4Py’s usual reading.**  
Discovery-style skeletons treat this set as *allowed* pairs. An empty set can make every non-singleton trace fail. The write-up treats it as “no mandatory adjacency,” which is a different semantics. That ambiguity needed an explicit conformance assumption.

**6. Small Log Skeleton holes.**  
There is no `never_together` for EDD/Reject (DECLARE only catches it via the extra EDD–compliance succession). `always_after(Change Bank Details, Verify Bank Account)` is stronger than R11 when there is no later payment. `activ_freq` omits several labels; that is safe only if PM4Py ignores missing keys.

**7. Verdict nits.**  
T5 temporal `pass` is defensible (only observed pairs are scored) but should have been qualified the same way T3/T8 are `not evaluated`. T8 resource `pass` is generous: after contradictory terminals there is no stable “applicable approval.” T6 resource `fail` under strict is fine; under guarded, “policy-dependent” is right, but only in the data layer.

**8. Net details left implicit.**  
Activation/rejection consume the sanctions token, so post-outcome escalation is impossible. Unsolicited opinions are excluded. Clarification after activation is excluded because `clar_ready` is consumed. None of these is required by the text; only some appear in §9.

## Why not higher

The task asked for defended choices, not a unique partition. Defended over-approximation is allowed. Silent disagreement between the shipped `declare_model` and the T6 guarded row is not. Neither is putting a strengthened R11 into the canonical accepting net. Those two, plus the extra DECLARE successions/responses and the `directly_follows` semantics gap, are enough to keep this out of the 9s.

What remains is still a serious answer: executable-looking PM4Py, honest R5/R6 risk, working happy-path net, and a deployment order that does not majority-vote layers. That is why it stays at 8.0 rather than the mid-6s.
