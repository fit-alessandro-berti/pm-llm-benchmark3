**6.5**

The write-up is format-compliant, methodologically in the right family (decision sets, labeled assumptions, competing-rule predictions, explicit uncertainty), and mostly faithful to the log. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, several moderate analytical errors and a few sharper misses keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is actually strong.** Sections 2–4 are the core of the task, and they are often done correctly. QA 08:20 / 08:32 / 09:05 / 09:20, QB 09:16 / 09:31 / 09:48 / 10:00 / 10:28 / 10:36, and QC 10:10 / 10:18 / 10:32 / 10:54 / 11:40 are reconstructed as choice problems, not as start-order folklore. The write-up does not treat every FIFO violation as priority, which is the main failure mode this benchmark is designed to catch. B05 waiting at 09:48, C02 beating both C01 and C03 at 10:10, and D08 waiting behind D04 are used properly. Scan-delay semantics are handled in the right direction (physical presence can be earlier than the stamp; the stamp does not make a job appear later). Counterfactuals in §4 are concrete and tied to observables that would actually split the hypotheses. Confidence numbers are not rhetorical.

**QA is mis-labeled relative to the same evidence the tables produce.** Calling the primary discipline “appointment-window scheduling with FIFO gap-filling” overweights one honored appointment (A03) against a long FIFO spine. The 09:05 row already falsifies appointment-aware look-ahead: A05’s estimate is 15, so a start at 09:05 is a *predictable* miss of A06 at 09:15. Worse, A07 (est 9) would have finished at 09:14 and released the resource on time. That is the sharp test, and it is missing. Using 08:58 A04 as support “because it can finish before A06’s appointment” is a clear identifiability error: FIFO, priority, and SPT all pick A04, so the episode cannot support an appointment story. The identifiability column later admits the overlap; the supporting-episode cell ignores it. That is internal inconsistency, not extra nuance.

**QD’s eligible sets are incomplete, which is a serious defect given that QD is declared most identifiable.** D-X is mechanical *and* electrical. At 11:20–11:30 the write-up lists only D06 and D07. Skill-match also includes D03 (electrical, still waiting until 11:21), D04, D08, and D09. In particular D08 (priority 1, est 8) would have finished about 11:28, before the 11:30 appointment. Idling D-X through that window is either appointment reservation *or* an unstated dedicated-pool rule (D-M owns pure mechanical work). The analysis treats the restricted list as if it were the skill-eligible set, then infers skill-based routing from that restriction. That is circular. The unique-resource fact for D07 (only D-X has mechanical+electrical) is the best skill-based reason to start D07 ahead of D10, and it is underplayed. The D-F 11:36 idle is flagged, correctly, but the write-up is clearer that D05 may not fit before the meeting than that D10 (est 12) *would* fit; the D10 skip is the real anomaly if D-F was available.

**QC’s hybrid label is fair; the discrimination is not finished.** C04 over C06 at 10:32 and C01 over C07 at 11:40 do break live SPT, priority, and EDD. Confidence 35 is earned. A committed/static SPT order of the 10:10 backlog (C02–C03–C04, then later arrivals) is a real competing explanation for C04 over C06 and is not tested. “Possible readiness hold on C06” is allowed as a hypothesis, but it is doing a lot of work for a diagnosis that is then still sold as SPT-with-escalation.

**Smaller but real problems.** QB’s 10:14 resumption (B01 over shorter B04 and higher-priority B06) is one of the better FIFO/resume tests and is missing from the decision table. Remaining time on B01 is exact (45−14=31), not “roughly.” A1’s wording (“earliest provably dispatch-visible”) is slightly muddled against the later, better B05 discussion. §7’s claim that QD is the most identifiable queue sits poorly next to the D-F hole and the incomplete D-X choice set; QA’s FIFO-plus-one-appointment pattern is cleaner on the evidence actually reconstructed.

**Not a collapse.** The piece is still a competent process-mining write-up: assumptions are labeled, invented facts are mostly avoided, appointment tolerance vs exact time is treated as unknown rather than faked, and the collection plan in §6 matches the actual ambiguities. Those virtues are why this is not in the 4–5 range. They are not enough for a high score under the stated rubric. The main quality bar—reconstruct *full* eligible sets, and do not use observationally equivalent episodes as unique support—is missed in the two places that matter most for the headline diagnoses (QA primary, QD eligibility).
