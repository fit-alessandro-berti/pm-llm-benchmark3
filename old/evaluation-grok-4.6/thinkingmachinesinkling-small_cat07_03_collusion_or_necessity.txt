**6.8**

The write-up is a competent, cautious audit memo: it does not accuse anyone of fraud, does not treat high co-working as suspicious by itself, keeps emergency handling and data quality in play, and correctly splits resource pairs instead of dumping them into one risk bucket. The ranking of concern (Lena/Kim/V-77x first; same-person retrospective justifications second; Carla/Gita and Dan/Hugo as explainable-but-verify; Aline/Elena and Boris/Felix as monitor-only) matches the evidence. That is the core of the task, and it is done well.

It is not close to flawless under a strict reading of the spec.

**Format and constraints.** Section 6 is about 335–340 words against a hard 200–300 window. Headings match, but that is an explicit miss. Several tables drop the intended arrows (`Buyer_Carla Approver_Gita`), which is sloppy rather than fatal.

**Section 4 is the weakest part and contains real inaccuracies.** The prompt required code for `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`. The answer never calls it. It substitutes a custom `groupby`/`unstack` helper that can `KeyError` when a case lacks one of the two activities, and it never takes the complement of the official filter (the public API keeps *compliant* cases, not violations). `pm4py.filter_between(..., min_duration=..., max_duration=...)` is not that function’s interface. `discover_subcontracting_network(df, n=2)` is not a valid signature; `n` is not a public kwarg. `pm4py.algo.analysis.extended_conditional_profile` is not a standard PM4Py entry point and looks invented. The right tool for unusual timing is closer to `discover_temporal_profile` / `conformance_temporal_profile`, which is unused.

The retrospective-justification check is a stub (`pass`). That is not a minor omission: the scenario’s stated exception is “same-day override allowed, justification within 3 working days by a *different* human.” Neither a Declare constraint set (Response / Succession, plus a same-resource exclusion) nor a log-skeleton conformance call is actually built. `conformance_log_skeleton` is named in the prompt and never used.

**Smaller evidential nits.** High-priority cluster H1 blends 9 true four-eyes breaches with 44 co-cases; reviewing the vendor family is reasonable, but the write-up slightly over-aggregates. `legacy_erp` / `Bot_PO` mapping is flagged as high-severity in the indicator table and then not given its own review cluster. “Email/messaging logs” as escalating evidence is thinner and more speculative than the log fields the prompt actually supplies (`entered_by`, `approved_by`, source-system mapping). Section 4 lists handover / working-together / subcontracting discoveries but does not say how those graphs would be *interpreted* against the benign specialization story.

**What still holds.** Benign alternatives are specific and testable. Prioritization and the report paragraph distinguish control exceptions from misconduct and name the right subgroup. No unsupported accusation. The grade is held down by the word-count breach, the skipped required API, several wrong PM4Py signatures, and an unimplemented 3-day justification rule—not by the audit judgment itself.
