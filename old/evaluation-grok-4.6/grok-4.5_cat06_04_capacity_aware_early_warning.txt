**6.2**

The memo is well-structured and makes the three calls the rubric treats as necessary: I-03 is stage-ineligible, I-05 is suppressed as a refresh, and untreated-queue spillover is treated as a real cost. It is not a near-flawless decision memo. The scoring rule does not generate the stated portfolio, the impact arithmetic does not audit, and several “deployable” formulas are incomplete or unused.

## The rule and the allocation disagree

Section 1 defines a numeric rule: rank by `RENB_adj` built from **ARR lower bound**, urgency, and segment confidence, and take the top cases with `RENB_adj > 0` under the adverse band. Section 2 never computes a single RENB. It only prints tiers.

Approximate lower-bound RENBs (ARR_low × value − €450 − false-alarm burden) are:

| Case | RENB (€) | Urgency as written | Implied rank |
|---|---:|---|---|
| I-04 | ~3,550 | 0.4 (10 h triage) | top after adjustment, still high |
| I-09 | ~2,350 before the extra 0.6–0.7 effect haircut | 1.0 (2.5 h diagnosis) | top 2–3 |
| I-02 | ~950 | 1.0 | mid-high |
| I-01 | ~920 | 0.4 | above I-07 |
| I-07 | ~500 | 0.8 | **not top 3** |

I-07 is labeled Tier 1 and given a slot. Under the authors’ own formula it loses to I-01 and usually to I-09. Urgency bands do not close the gap; “ties broken by SLA clock” does not apply because these are not ties. Selecting I-07 can be defended as a hard clock / new-evidence override, but that override is not in the rule. A scoring rule that is not the rule used is a first-order coherence failure, not a presentation nicety.

Related specification holes:

- Diagnosis Started at 4 h (I-06) is not in the urgency schedule.
- Operators are missing in both RENB equations (`RENB  (ARR_low × Value)  swarm cost…`).
- `spillover_share` is defined with an unexplained `× 0.5` and an undefined “median untreated diagnosis-case value,” then never calculated.
- The authors’ own post-selection spillover re-check is not performed.
- Segment offsets are applied to `p_now`, then `p_now` is not used in ranking. Calibration work is real for suppression/escalation and ornamental for prioritization.
- Conservatism is stacked (ARR_low + segment_confidence + NNLS 0.6–0.7 effect multiplier + I-06 30% haircut). For I-09 it is unclear whether the 0.6 factor is applied once or twice.

## I-04 now is the weakest live decision

I-04 is 10 h out, still in Triage, Strategic (model overpredicts 0.05–0.12), first alert, and carries a €2,000 false-alarm burden. The same memo later recomputes the book at 14:00 and holds a reserve for a possible 15:00 regulatory arrival. Using a scarce slot at 12:00 on a long-clock triage case fights that sequential design. “EV gap vs alternatives is large” is asserted, not shown, and after the 0.4 urgency weight the gap is much smaller than the raw €60k headline. A tighter policy would swarm I-02 now, treat I-07 as eligible-and-urgent rather than automatically funded, and leave I-04 on a 14:00 trigger.

I-02 is more defensible (diagnosis stage, 3 h, second alert) but the ARR band is only 0.08–0.22 after a segment that overpredicts. Calling it Tier 1 with the same certainty as a cleaned-up high-ARR case overstates the evidence.

I-09 is the opposite error: lower-bound EV is among the best in the book, the case is already in Diagnosis Started with 2.5 h left, and it is parked as “wait / reserve candidate” almost entirely on a small validation sample. Sparse calibration justifies a human gate, not implicit deprioritization below a €5k SME case.

## Section 3 does not add up

Stated mid ARRs are 0.15 + 0.19 + 0.26 = **0.60** and mid direct benefit is  
`0.15×25k + 0.19×60k + 0.26×5k = €16.45k`.  
The base row reports **~0.45–0.55** breaches and **~€11.5k** benefit. Those figures are not a rounding of the stated mids; they are a different, unexplained model (possibly silent confidence haircuts). The suggested formula was supposed to make the table auditable. It does not.

Other impact issues:

- Optimistic “upper half” sum `0.18+0.24+0.29` is roughly fine; the base row is not.
- Full false-alarm burden is subtracted in every scenario, which matches the prompt’s sketch but is never distinguished from `(1 − p) × burden` or from disruption that occurs even when the swarm is “right.”
- Spillover monetization assumes an €8–15k “untreated diagnosis exposure” for the six unnamed queue cases. That is a guess, and it is not labeled as one with enough humility given how much net-impact sign depends on it.
- Adverse net “near zero to negative” is plausible, which undercuts treating three slots as the default rather than two plus reserve.
- Non-monetary obligations (public-sector duty, regulatory option value) are named and then barely priced. I-01 is a well-calibrated public case left waiting so I-04 can take a 10 h-out slot.

## What is actually good

Hard gates are right. I-03 is not swarmed because `p_now = 0.82`. I-08 is rejected on low ARR and €5k burden, not worshipped for €100k value. I-06 is held for imputation. One slot is reserved as a preference with a release rule, not a superstition. I-05 vs I-07 in §4 is the correct distinction (refresh vs new diagnostic log; the 0.75 → 0.72 dip is not treated as all-clear). Cool-down, suppression, escalation, and 50% ARR shrink on re-swarm are operationally usable.

Section 5 hits every required failure mode with mechanisms rather than slogans: a non-Strategic daily floor, NNLS human confirm and `n ≥ 30` before global ARR updates, monthly segment offsets, intervention logged as a feature, IPW/DR or a small uplift set, shadow “policy-off” predictions, spillover incidence by segment, and a simultaneous-swarm cap. Section 6 is the strongest part: process (swarm before Diagnosis Complete > 95%), calibration drift triggers, matched DiD plus queue interrupted time series, congestion stop-loss (delay > 25 min/slot or untreated uplift > 0.03 → cut to 3 slots), fairness floors, and a shadowed week before rule cutover.

Even here the causal plan is too tidy. Matching swarmed vs eligible-unswarmed on stage, clock, segment, and calibrated `p` does not remove selection on RENB; the memo mentions propensity adjustment but still talks as if monthly DiD will identify ARR. Randomized leftover slots are wishful at four slots and a preferred reserve.

## Net

This is a competent policy essay that would pass a “did you notice I-03, I-05/I-07, and spillovers?” check and fail an audit of “show the rule, apply the rule, add the numbers.” The highest-leverage flaw is not tone or missing themes; it is that §1 and §2 are different policies, and §3 cannot be reproduced from the figures it cites. Under a strict reading that is well below excellent.
