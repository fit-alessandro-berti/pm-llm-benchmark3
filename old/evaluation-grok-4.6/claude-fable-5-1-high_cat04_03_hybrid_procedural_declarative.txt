**7.8**

This is a strong, well-structured hybrid design that does the things the prompt actually rewards: it splits control-flow from resource/data/time, it refuses to paper over R5/R6, the dictionaries are internally coherent, and the T1–T8 matrix is mostly right. It is not nearly flawless. Several encodings are stricter than the requirements, one skeleton constraint is the converse of the rule it claims to encode, and a few layer claims do not line up with the code.

## What is actually good

Policy handling is the best part of the answer. R5 and R6 are treated as incompatible, three resolutions are stated, Resolution A is implemented as fail-closed, and T6 is scored under both A and B. That is the correct methodological move. Mapping is also mostly right: XOR/disjunction in the tree, pairwise co-occurrence in DECLARE/skeleton, alternation in the alternate templates, cardinalities in `activ_freq`, durations in the temporal profile, SoD and the unobservable emergency flag outside activity-only models. The DECLARE commentary correctly distinguishes response vs precedence, altresponse vs chainresponse, and noncoexistence vs nonsuccession. The log-skeleton pair orientation `(later, earlier)` for `always_before` matches PM4Py’s actual dict convention, which many answers reverse. T4 (resource-only fail), T5 (temporal vacuity), T6 (skeleton blind to conditional order), T7 (warning, not fail), and T8 (R1 as earliest break) are the right layered story.

## Material modeling errors

**1. `always_before(Suspend Vendor, Change Bank Details)` is the converse of R11.**  
R11 is “if bank details change after activation, then suspend eventually.” It does not say a vendor may be suspended only after a bank change. Putting `(Suspend Vendor, Change Bank Details)` in `always_before` (and selling it in §2 as an R11 encoding) forbids every other suspension reason. The correct skeleton piece is `always_after(CBD, SV)` plus the data-layer between-check. This is a false constraint, not a disclosed approximation.

**2. R5 is encoded as “opinion before Compliance Approval,” not “before Activate Vendor.”**  
The requirement is OLO ≺ AV after an escalation. The tree places `->(SE, OLO)` inside the AND that *joins before* CA, so a legal trace `… CA … OLO … AV` fails the tree and passes DECLARE `nonsuccession(AV, OLO)`. That layer disagreement is never mentioned, unlike the RFP/tree vs skeleton disagreement they do discuss.

**3. Extra assumptions are compiled in as if they were the spec.**  
A3 (escalation only on the high-risk branch), A4 (no opinion without escalation), A5 (CBD is by definition post-activation), A9 (SE/OLO at most once), A10 (no EDD→reject) are all optional. Some are defended; several then leak into DECLARE/skeleton as confidence-1.0 rules (`precedence(SE, OLO)`, `always_before(OLO, SE)`, `activ_freq` caps, EDD↔CA equivalence). A4 in particular will reject an opinion that is not preceded by a logged escalation, which no stakeholder required.

**4. DECLARE does not actually force “answered before the final outcome.”**  
`altresponse(RQC, RCC)` plus `nonsuccession(AV, RQC)` still accepts `RQC … AV … RCC`. They know this and push the real check into `check_clarifications`, but §2 still calls the DECLARE pair an encoding of R3 rather than a partial approximation. The tree only covers pre-classification cycles, so the authoritative R3 check is the data layer, not DECLARE.

**5. Resolution B’s tree is slack in the opposite direction.**  
`X(tau, OLO)` in parallel with the whole high-risk sequence allows OLO before SE, after RFP, and skipped even when SE occurred. R4 is then only in DECLARE. That is an over-approximation, and the 24h bound is also jammed into a two-sided N(12h, 6h) profile whose job was already defined as non-rejecting. The hard SLA lives in the data layer; the temporal hack is the wrong tool and is easy to misread as statistics.

## Smaller but real issues

- `activ_freq` caps `{0,1,2,3}` for clarifications/VBA/CBD are format artifacts placed in the live model. §9 admits they are not business rules; they will still fail conformance.
- `response(MA, AV)` / `response(CA, AV)` and skeleton `always_after` of the same pairs forbid approval-then-reject. That matches the backbone, not a stated prohibition on a manager refusing after MA.
- SoD uses set intersection over *all* occurrences of VBA vs *all* approvals. After R11 re-verification that is stronger than “the applicable approval differs from the check it relies on.”
- Alignment diagnostics are hand-waved (“model move on RCC”, “log move OLO”). Fitness&lt;1 is enough for a fail; the claimed earliest move is not the only optimal alignment.
- §2 vs §10 disagree on ownership: R2’s disjunctive half is a tree property in §2 and is swept into “R1–R5 owned by DECLARE” in §10.
- `TREE_STR_OPS` is unused by `procedural_model`; fine as a variant, but n-ary `X`/`+` and duplicate `Verify Bank Account` labels are assumed to parse and align without being run.
- `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` polarity is left as “check your version.” That function is not a drop-in SoD oracle.
- All support/confidence values are 1.0, which the task allows, but setting them and then warning that checkers ignore them is slightly double-talk unless the reporting layer actually suppresses those fields.

## Test matrix

T1–T5, T7, T8 verdicts are credible. T6 under A/B is the right split (`fail` vs `policy-dependent`). Temporal-never-fail is consistent with A11 and R12. I would still not treat the matrix as executed evidence: no EventLog is built, timestamps are hours while the profile is seconds, and T4 is not “identical” to T1 (RFP at 70 vs 80). Those do not change the cells, but they are sloppy.

## Net

This is a serious answer: right architecture, right conflict treatment, right T4/T5/T6/T7 lessons, usable PM4Py-shaped artifacts. It loses a high score because it over-encodes converses and assumptions, mis-states a couple of “exact” encodings, and never validates the models against the traces it scores. Under a strict reading that is well short of flawless.
