6.7

The answer is structured, operationally literate, and hits the required contrasts (I-03 is gated out; I-05 is suppressed as refresh noise; I-07 re-enters on new evidence and is then declined on economics plus window fit; spillover is priced). It is not nearly flawless. Several of its own rules are violated in the allocation, and a few decision-theoretic and operational gaps are large enough to matter.

**Self-contradictions in a supposedly deployable rule.** Step 3 makes an alert actionable only after two refreshes above an undefined “watch level,” unless waiting would miss the swarm window. I-04 is a first alert with 10 hours left. The rule requires waiting; the table spends slot 1 immediately. I-01 and I-08, also first alerts with long buffers, are told to wait. The difference is the score, but that exception is not in the rule. “Watch level” is never given a number, so the persistence clause is not implementable as written.

Step 1 says marginal go/no-go uses the conservative end of the adjusted interval. I-02 is the definition of marginal. At Δ = 0.08, 0.08 × €25,000 = €2,000, which does not clear swarm + expected false-alarm + ~€2,400 spillover. The table still uses the unshaded midpoint 0.15 to manufacture ≈ +€650 and commits a slot. The preamble claims SE/NNLS effects are “shaded toward the lower half,” then I-02 is scored at the raw midpoint. That is not robustification; it is midpoint shopping.

Step 5 requires the Section 5 fairness floor. Today every committed slot goes to Strategic Enterprise or NNLS; every SME/Public Sector case is declined. The equity budget is deferred to “weekly/daily” and is not applied to the live book the rule claims to govern.

**Weak portfolio choice under the author’s own criteria.** Intervene only if base S > 0 *and* adverse is “not catastrophic.” Two of three selections are barely positive after spillover; I-09 is explicitly assumed to get ~no effect in the adverse row. The 3-slot adverse net is about −€4k to −€10k, driven by queue harm. There is no comparison of that book to the obvious robust alternative: I-04 only (still clearly positive at the low end of Δ), hold more reserve, decide I-06 after the 15-minute check. “Catastrophic” is undefined, then a negative adverse portfolio is accepted anyway. Option value of an unused slot is stated well; it is not used to size the book.

**Operational sequencing is missing.** Two specialists deliver a 45-minute swarm. If swarms cannot run fully in parallel, order by score (I-04, then I-02, then I-09) is the wrong order. I-04 has 10 hours; I-09 has 2.5 hours and is already in Diagnosis Started. A third swarm starting after two serial 45-minute blocks can eat I-09’s remaining window. Specialist load (3 × 45 minutes for both seniors, plus ordinary diagnosis) is not scheduled. This is a concrete feasibility issue, not a polish issue.

**Arithmetic is mostly auditable and not always tight.** Spillover is €900–3,600 in the rule, then 6 × 0.03 × €25k = €4,500 in the same model; the impact table’s upper base spillover (€8,100) does not match 3 × 6 × 0.02 × €25k (€9,000). Adverse charges the full €3,400 false-alarm burden while still claiming 0.15–0.2 breaches avoided. €15–25k as the untreated-case breach value is an invented average. None of this is fatal, but the piece presents more precision than it earned.

**Required themes that are thin.** Non-monetary obligations are almost only the management reserve for a possible regulatory arrival. Public-sector duty, contractual/reputational loss not in the euro column, and whether I-01/I-06 should be scored purely in S are not really argued. I-09 is “flagged for effect review” after the slot is already committed; under the stated NNLS uncertainty that review had to be a precondition, not a footnote. The 14:30 reserve release is slightly early relative to a “before 15:00” arrival and a 45-minute intervention.

**What still works.** Stage eligibility is absolute and I-03 is refused despite p = 0.82, with non-swarm escalation. Calibration offsets by segment are the right sign and are applied to p before scoring. I-06 is gated on the imputed queue field. I-05 vs I-07 is the correct policy distinction, and alert disposition is separated from slot spend. Spillover is internalized rather than treated as color commentary. Section 5 names the actual mechanisms (value starvation, NNLS exclusion, SME vs SE calibration stacking, treatment-contaminated labels, harm landing on the untreated diagnosis queue) and attaches tripwires, treatment flags, and a compensating uplift. Section 6’s tie-break randomization at the policy margin is the right causal design; policy-epoch versioning is right. Those parts are why this is not a mid-pack answer.

**Net.** Coherent enough to run a conversation with operations; not coherent enough to deploy without rewriting the rule so that today’s table is an instance of it. The high-value mistakes are inconsistency and missing robustness/sequencing, not ignorance of the case. Under a strict reading that is a clear step below excellent.
