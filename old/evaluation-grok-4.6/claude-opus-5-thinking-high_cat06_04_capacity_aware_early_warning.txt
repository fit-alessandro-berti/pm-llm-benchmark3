6.4

The answer is operationally ambitious and hits the main quality bars (I-03 refused on mechanism, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers can flip the sign, observational \(p\) is not a causal effect). Under strict grading it is not close to excellent. The decision rule and the actual noon allocation contradict each other in several places that matter, and several numbers that the text invites the reader to audit do not survive that audit.

**Rule vs. action.** Step 3 authorises a swarm only if `Net > 0` at the unfavourable end of the bands, and Step 1 makes the runway and effect tests hard vetoes. I-02 is then given slot 2 despite a stated net of \(+€0.5\mathrm{k}/-€0.3\mathrm{k}\) and despite a 12:55 serial start that leaves ~1 h 20 m after the swarm against the rule’s own 1.5 h post-swarm assumption. I-08 is marked “Eligible now?” even though the shrunk midpoint \(0.10\times0.8\times0.85=0.068\) fails the “shrunk midpoint \(\ge 0.10\)” effect gate. I-09 and I-02 are second alerts with no recorded new event and \(\Delta p < 0.10\), yet the evidence gate is applied rigorously only to I-05. The public-sector “obligation floor” is written as an alternative authorisation path, then used to support I-06 and quietly ignored for I-01. Exceptions for closing windows and learning quotas may be defensible; they are not written into the rule that supposedly governs them.

**Arithmetic that does not add up.** I-04 is claimed to remain “\(+€2.5\mathrm{k}\) at its effect lower bound” in the same breath as a post-congestion net. With the author’s own shrinks and the €1,800 congestion price that figure is about \(+€0.6\mathrm{k}\) (or \(+€2.5\mathrm{k}\) only if congestion is dropped). Optimistic net is given as \(+€7.4\mathrm{k}\) to \(+€12.0\mathrm{k}\); pairing the stated bounds on benefit and spillover produces a floor near \(+€5.4\mathrm{k}\). Optimistic ranges are not constructed the same way as Base/Adverse ranges. Shrinkage is applied in the scoring table, dropped in the Optimistic row, and reapplied unevenly to I-02’s Base effect (\(0.09\)–\(0.12\)). The I-04 lower-bound effect is \(0.068\) in §3 and treated as if it were \(0.10\) when convenient in §2. Euro-level nets (\(-€320\), \(+€500\)) are presented as ranking inputs after the text has said the causal inputs are priors.

**Portfolio priced ≠ portfolio decided.** The impact table’s headline book is I-09 + I-02 + I-06. I-06 is explicitly unverified; I-02 is conditional on mitigations that restore a runway the serial schedule has already consumed. The only unconditional commitment is I-09. Pricing an unverified three-slot book overstates both benefit and confidence. The “Adverse + guardrails” row is the more honest object and should have been the primary book.

**I-09 as slot 1 is not robust under the author’s own uncertainty doctrine.** Sparse calibration, no segment offset (correct), a cosmetic shrink \(0.24\to0.22\), a possible interpreter bottleneck, and a runway that is already on the invented 2.5 h knife-edge. The same essay argues for cheap-lane first, sign-stability, and not spending scarce capacity on weakly identified effects — then burns the first serial window on the least-identified case, with the exploration quota offered after the fact. A 10-minute bottleneck check, which the escalation ladder itself requires before a swarm, is skipped.

**Invented stacked modifiers do too much work.** The 2.5 h runway gate, triage \(\times0.85\), short-runway \(\times0.75\), SE \(\times0.8\), and a congestion price built on an assumed €15k untreated case are all extra structure. Together they zero out almost every SME/public triage case, after which a 30% below-median weekly quota and a cheap lane are introduced to undo the exclusion. That quota does not bind today (I-01/I-05/I-07/I-10 all unserved). “Log-spaced” tiers at \(\le€10\mathrm{k}/€10\)–\(35\mathrm{k}/>€35\mathrm{k}\) are not log-spaced. The €1,800 price is a scenario, not a measurement; treating it as the thing a case “must beat” makes the ranking look more identified than it is.

**Smaller defects.** False-alarm reweighting by \((1-p_{\mathrm{adj}})\) is reasonable but the promised side-by-side with the template formula never appears. The pooled-mean prior of \(\approx0.17\) is not the midpoint average of the table. Serial-vs-parallel staffing is asserted, not justified against the reading that two specialists could run two swarms. Reserve logic (“preemptible-by-deferral”) is hard to execute at 14:30 if slot 3 is already running. Several phrases overclaim auditability that the arithmetic does not support.

**What remains genuinely strong.** I-03 is refused despite \(p=0.82\) and €40k. I-05’s unchanged refresh is suppressed as an alert and kept as a case; I-07’s new log is accepted as evidence and still denied a slot on feasibility. I-08’s €100k does not buy capacity. I-06 is held for a cheap verification. Spillovers are treated as first-order and possibly programme-signing. Calibration is interval-valued. Label destruction, regressive congestion, and grey-band randomisation are real safeguards rather than slogans. The closing caveat — that the programme is a guarded experiment, not a known-positive ranking machine — is the right epistemic stance.

Those strengths make this a serious policy memo. The self-contradictions on gates, the I-04 figure, and the impact table’s counterfactual portfolio keep it well below a high score when every inconsistency is charged.
