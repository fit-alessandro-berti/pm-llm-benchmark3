**6.4**

The answer is substantively strong and clearly above a generic process-redesign memo, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the required format, numeric discipline, and internal logic, several defects are material.

**What is actually good.** The diagnosis is the best part. It uses the frequency DFG, performance DFG (mean/median/p90), token replay (fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces), alignment move-on-log deviations, model precision, R1–R3, rework rates, segment outcomes, and resource constraints. It states the core contradiction correctly: `Auto → Fast approval → Dispatch` is the modal cheap/fast path, then collapses after complaints, reshipments, and credits (31% cheaper before rework, 9% after). It does not treat the slow path as uniformly superior; the 3–4pp complaint gap on low-risk accessories vs the 12–17pp gap and EUR 410 error on high-value devices is the right cut. Redesigns A/B/C are materially different (upstream triage, downstream fulfillment/gate, exception decoupling), each with a real sacrifice. The experiment section correctly refuses average cycle time and asks for end-to-end tails so gains are not just shifted.

**Required table is broken.** Section 5 was supposed to use qualitative signs. In the delivered table the directional markers are missing from both the cells and the legend (`*Signs:  = decrease...`). Rows A–C therefore read as fragments: “median for unclear”, “upfront”, “repeat evidence”, “strongly”, “for unclear”. That is not a usable Pareto comparison, and it is a failure of a mandatory section, not a cosmetic glitch.

**Numeric and DFG discipline is sloppy.** Complaint inflow edges sum to 510 (`140+260+80+30`), while 9.2% of 5,000 is 460 cases. Calling this “460 total … minus overlap” does not explain the gap. Outgoing from complaint is only 430. More important for process-mining literacy: listed END edges total 3,615 (`3015+220+390`), so about 1,385 of 5,000 cases never reach a listed end. A diagnosis that leans so hard on DFG frequencies should have flagged incomplete coverage instead of treating 3,015 closes as if the log were closed. Smaller errors compound this: “13–17pp” should be 12–17pp for standard high-value (18% vs 6%); mixing unique-case rates with edge counts (1,430 evidence inflows, “complaint 460 + rework 45”) is repeated.

**Pilot design contradicts its own rationale.** The first pilot is “A-light on one low-risk accessory subsegment + one high-value *control* group,” then immediately says the point is whether blocking `Auto → Fast` for >EUR 400 / high-value cuts 18–22% complaints. If high-value is the control, that hypothesis is not being tested. “Control group” is either misused or the design is wrong. That is a logic error in the section that is supposed to show the author can learn rather than shift work downstream.

**A few causal overreaches.** `Fast approval → Close` (350) is interpreted as a “close-discipline problem feeding complaint after close.” The alignment diagnostic supports *complaint after close* as a deviation; it does not by itself prove that the 350 edge causes it. Redesign B also quietly repurposes two *repair* quality inspectors as a *pre-dispatch part-selection* gate without saying the work is different, so the “use underused inspectors” mechanism is weaker than claimed. Redesign C is a real alternative, but it is mostly an organizational exception lane; it leaves R1/R2 and wrong-part selection intact, so it only “balances” the five objectives if recovery quality is treated as a substitute for first-time-right—which the failure-modes section itself admits can become a hidden rework factory.

**What keeps the score from collapsing.** This is not a Lean/Six Sigma laundry list, it does not optimize only mean cycle time, it does not claim exact ROI or strict Pareto dominance, and the three designs are grounded in the given artifacts rather than slogans. Those are the main rubric axes. They are executed well enough that a lenient grader could land in the mid-8s. Strictly: a mandatory comparison table that cannot be read, an experiment that does not test the claim it states, and several DFG-accounting misses are enough to pull it well below “nearly flawless.”
