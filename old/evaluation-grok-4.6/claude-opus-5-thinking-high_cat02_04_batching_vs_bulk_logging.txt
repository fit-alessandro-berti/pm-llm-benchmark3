6.8

The write-up is unusually strong on process-mining substance: it separates execution from recording, refuses to treat PM4Py category names as physical proof, and gives a coherent account of how each cluster would bias waiting, service, utilisation, and bottleneck calls. Format compliance is essentially complete. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading the defects below are enough to keep it well out of the top band.

**Internal contradiction on the central contrast (most serious).**  
Section 1/7 correctly separate G1 (fixture-level simultaneity at 1 s resolution) from G7 (15 min coarsening). The independent `verdict()` in §4 does the opposite of what the prose claims. `grid_mismatch` is `exec_grid > max(rec_grid, declared_prec)`. The author even states that G1’s gcd is 180 s and that this comparison is “why G1 is not classified as coarsened.” That predicate is true for G1 (`180 > max(1, 1)`) and false for G7 (`900 > max(60, 900)`). Control flow then labels G1 “timestamp coarsening” and G7 “suspect: simultaneity exceeds capacity.” That inverts the one distinction the quality bar exists to protect. A rule that used declared precision as an *explanation* of the grid (`declared >= exec_grid` and `exec_grid > rec_grid`) would have matched the narrative; the implemented inequality does not. Because §4 is a required deliverable and is offered as the methodological check on PM4Py, this is a logical failure, not a nit.

**Numerical and attribution slips.**  
- G3: “Flow time for E01 is overstated by up to 22 min” is wrong. Recorded flow is 50 min; serial reconstruction overstates by 20 min; the bound if completion can sit anywhere in `(start, 11:30]` is almost 28 min. 22 min is E01’s *waiting* time, reused in the wrong place.  
- G8: the safety interval is 15:00–15:35 (35 min), not 30. The 30 min figure is `first start − last arrival` (15:38−15:08). Treating that idle gap as “30 minutes of unavailability” mixes congestion-after-arrival with resource downtime and understates the overlap for the early cases.  
- `max_concurrency` processes ends before starts at a tied instant, so G6’s seven zero-length events at 17:00 yield concurrency 0. The flag `zero_duration_rows` saves the diagnosis, but the concurrency statistic is then misleading for the cluster that most needs a non-zero simultaneous count.

**Weaker consistency elsewhere.**  
If workstation IDs may be shared, excluding T-D from that caveat while applying it to T-H (same `scanner` / `T-*` pattern) is arbitrary; utilisation is then unidentified for G4 as well. G2’s claim that true simultaneity is “implausible for manual visual inspection” is an unlabeled operational assumption. “Spurious parallelism” for G1 conformance is confused: if the fixture claim is accepted, the parallelism is real. Calling G4/G8’s 240 s / 420 s start-to-start spacing an “inter-item gap” is inexact (idle gap is 0). G8’s posterior of 80 sits poorly next to the author’s own statement that different clocks make the 15:35→15:38 story unproven.

**What remains genuinely good.**  
The signature matrix, the G6 mechanism (one substituted timestamp inflating wait and collapsing service at once), the G2 sign-error on the queue trend, interval-censoring of G7 including the arrival-after-start impossibility, the derived-interval hypothesis for the scanners, and the redesign/adjudication sections are all at a high professional standard. Those strengths are why the score is not lower. They do not cancel a self-contradictory classifier on G1 vs G7 or the concrete arithmetic/attribution errors.
