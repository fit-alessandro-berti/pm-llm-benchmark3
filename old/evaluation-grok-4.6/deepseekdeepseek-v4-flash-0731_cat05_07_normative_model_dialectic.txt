**7.2**

The review is dialectically strong, well structured, and mostly faithful to the materials. It still falls well short of “nearly flawless” because the required executable model is semantically wrong in a core place, one revised-route design cannot replay the best-evidenced pilot timeline, and a few case calls overreach the proof the brief itself says is missing.

**What is solid.** Headings match the spec. Section 1 separates stipulated facts from authority/scope/logging disputes and states a real burden for noncompliance versus exception versus indeterminacy. Both briefs are argued at strength and include the required concessions: prosecution admits loop, CRM, label, duplicate-register, and scope defects; defense admits 703/708/712/714/715. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including control-flow-fit / non-flow violations (712, 715). System judgment does not split the difference: M0 is rejected as a sole norm, and “high deviation = obsolescence” is rejected for the unauthorized high-risk and post-expiry cases. Architecture layering (POWL vs DECLARE vs pandas vs org controls) and the data proposal (stable `notification_id`, rule/version IDs, eligibility snapshots, aliases, delegation, overlay IDs, immutable effective/record timestamps) match the prompt.

**Model/code defects (largest deduction).** PM4Py `Operator.LOOP` is binary (`do`, `redo`). The code builds a three-child loop and describes a literature-style `(do, redo, exit)` triple. That is not a documented PM4Py POWL constructor; typical versions assert exactly two children. The “reorder the children” caveat does not fix arity. The intended 0..n `Request_More_Info` pattern is `*(τ, Request_More_Info)` or `X(τ, *(Request_More_Info, τ))`. As written, the required artifact is not a defensible executable approximation.

Second, the auto-approval branch forces `Activate → Quality →` then common `Notify → Close`. N4/SUP-702 give a stable CRM notification at +2 hours and quality the next business day, i.e. notify *between* activate and quality. The revised model cannot accept that order. The thinking considered a post-decision `quality_or_skip` and then abandoned it; the shipped model still hides the mismatch. “Notify follows the decision” is also closer to notify after `Auto_Approve`/`Activate`, not after delayed quality.

Smaller model nits: `Request_More_Info` exists only on the standard branch though N5 describes it as a general pre-decision practice; `Master_Data_Validation` as a silent node is reasonable, but then PD-3’s visible validation requirement lives only in prose; auto-route quality is mandatory *after* activate even though PD-3 only *permits* delay.

**Judgment/proof issues.** SUP-715 as NONCOMPLIANT is too strong relative to the brief’s own gap: no explicit ban, and it is unproved that this relationship is a reportable conflict rather than a missing form. Medium confidence does not convert an unproved trigger into a primary noncompliance finding; DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE or a clearly scoped MIXED (flow COMPLIANT / COI unproved) would fit the burden section better. SUP-711 as COMPLIANT is acceptable after same-`source transaction ID` dedup, but the logged trace is still nonconforming; the primary label should have carried that data-quality overlay more explicitly. SUP-713 as COMPLIANT is slightly generous: mapping was approved *after* the case. SUP-702 as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION with medium confidence is a coherent call, not a mushy split, given a documented windowed decision plus the observer’s “monitored exception” language.

**Net.** Excellent process-aware legal analysis and a usable layered redesign; the POWL deliverable and a few proof-to-verdict steps are not tight enough for a high band under a strict rubric.
