**7.6**

The audit is substantively strong: it catches both blatant complete-trace leakage and the subtler lineage defects (extraction-date SQL, 23:00 same-day snapshot, missing sequence index, mutable `risk_score_current`, closure-time `final_handler_team`), keeps queue/complexity/elapsed as legitimate prefix signals, treats `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` as future-but-process-legal rather than impossible, and generally separates predictive use from causal/actionable/fairness claims. That is the right skeleton. It is not nearly flawless.

**Material gaps and errors**

The most important miss is process-horizon emptiness, not just leakage. After `Coverage Check Completed`, `num_document_requests_before_t0` is typically zero; the team’s “document rework is an early warning” story is process-inconsistent at this `t0` even if the count is repaired. The same applies to prefix-izing `full_trace_variant_id` and to `Documents Requested → Documents Received`. Section 1’s remediation (“replace with `num_document_requests_before_t0` / `prefix_variant_id_at_t0`”) treats these as salvageable early-warning features. They are not, at this cutoff. That is a real process-mining error, not a wording quibble.

Section 3 then fails to classify several items it already flagged: repaired document-request counts, prefix variants, `handler_team_at_t0`, and post-repair `same_day_document_count`. Putting `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` only under “defensible predictive signal” also blurs the required process-state/slack category—the feature’s actual meaning is consumed SLA buffer, which the narrative later gets right and the taxonomy does not.

Severity and labels are sloppy in places. `same_day_document_count_snapshot` is intra-day lookahead into the feature vector; Medium is too soft. `final_decision` is outcome leakage, not “target” leakage (the target is 10-day breach, not Approve/Partial/Deny). `queue_length_at_t0` does not “accurately” capture congestion given a ±5 minute join. `Coverage Check Completed → Fraud Investigation Opened` is almost always after `t0` (prediction is *immediately after* coverage check); calling it a borderline “if logged prior to cutoff” state transition understates that.

**Weaker process and explanation work**

Section 2 has the right checklist (prefix cutoff, sequence-index disambiguation, enablement vs impossibility vs future-possible, appeal-justified second decisions, DFG ≠ normative rule). Two imprecisions matter. Alignment does not “isolate true process delay from data anomalies”; it measures fit to a reference model. And “a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` without an intervening Appeal is invalid” is stricter than the given semantics (corrections/reopens are not ruled out). The Appeal vs same-timestamp distinction is present but split: the table barely states that `Appeal → Settlement Decision Recorded` is *semantically legitimate later*, which was a stated quality criterion.

Team causal stories (“appeals cause breaches,” “the final team determines delay”) are implied-wrong rather than audited. Predictive importance ≠ cause was the brief; a row-by-row takedown was warranted.

In the JSON, `initial_reserve` is treated as a credible `t0` driver with no lineage note that it existed by then—section 1 says “verify,” section 4 uses it anyway. Case Y correctly drops the leaky prior-claim SQL, but does not say the count of 3 may survive a correct `< t0` filter. Case Z’s note is good on impossibilities and mutable fields, but omits that `calendar_week = 52` is the one remaining, explicitly flagged holiday/policy proxy and should be handled as drift, not discarded with the junk. Status `"recompute"` for X/Y is right; the narratives stay clean of future outcomes.

Section 5 names every required heading and is still thin where it matters most: explanation stability (recomputed SHAP after leakage removal will reallocate mass; old attributions must not be reused), calibration protocol beyond a passing AUC/Brier mention, and operational rules for when a proxy may stay in the scorer but must not appear in handler-facing text.

**Verdict**

This is a competent, well-structured audit that would pass a real review with revisions. Under a strict reading it is not excellent: it repairs some features into emptiness, under-rates a clear lookahead, leaves the interpretation taxonomy incomplete, and is looser on process enablement and explanation-stability than the prompt demanded. High-7 is the correct band; 9+ would require the empty-at-`t0` insight, tighter severities, and a fully populated section 3.
