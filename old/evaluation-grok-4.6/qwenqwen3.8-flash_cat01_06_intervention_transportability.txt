**6.2**

The final write-up (from the required headings onward) is a strong transportability memo on process structure, confounding, and pilot design, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, invented model API, a few causal-graph errors, unused DFG counts, and several quantitative handwaves keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What it gets right.** The answer correctly refuses a wholesale copy of Site A. It treats the nine-hour median drop as a confounded before/after contrast, not an identified treatment effect, and names the co-interventions (API, added reviewers, mix shift, huddles, timestamp quality). The structural comparison is the right one: Site A’s `+` operator is only legal at Site B when originals are not required; otherwise Identity Check may overlap Originals Receipt, but Document Check stays gated. The bottleneck-shift scenario (median down, p90 up) is present and well motivated by 3 assessors and 2 legal FTE. The candidate intervention, first subgroup (digital, non-legal, no originals), and ITT vs per-protocol pilot are aligned with that logic. Format constraints (headings, table columns, required graph nodes, eight assumptions, recommendation choice) are largely met, and the Section 2/7/9 word bands look in range.

**Material defects.**

1. **Section 8 fails model fidelity.** Required PM4Py POWL types are `Transition` / `SilentTransition` / `StrictPartialOrder` / `OperatorPOWL`, not `Activity`, `Tau`, `ExclusiveChoice`, `Concurrent`, or a standalone `Loop`. The import block as written should raise `ImportError`. The rest of the script is a nest of guessed constructors, duplicate kwargs attempts, and unused helpers. That is not “constructors consistently with the supplied PM4Py manual.” The legal fallback also installs a **cycle** (`supp → … → legal_rep → supp`) inside something labeled a strict partial order, which is invalid. Repeat-labeled activities (`Document_Check_Repeat`) are a further mismatch with a true supplement loop on the same tasks. Playout/soundness wrappers are speculative public-API guesses (`get_initial_marking`, keyword `max_traces`). A required executable artifact that would not construct the claimed net is a large miss on “model fidelity.”

2. **Transport graph errors.** `S_siteB -> S_co_intervention_API_latency_improved` is false: the API upgrade is a Site A co-intervention. `appeal -> cycle_time` runs the wrong way for the index spell. `API_latency -> identity_capacity` conflates latency with headcount. Extra selection nodes are fine; wrong edges are not.

3. **DFG underuse.** The 3,400-case relation counts (e.g. Register→Identity 2,050, Identity→Originals 1,020, Originals→Document 2,108, Legal→Supplement 360) are almost unused. Eligible-share and “save ~3.4 h” arithmetic treat sequencing as if identity were fully serial with originals, despite baseline overlap of 6% and mixed DFG order. That weakens the quantitative-transport criterion.

4. **Smaller analytic slips.** Optimistic eligibility of 80–100% sits uneasily with a 62% statutory originals gate. Digital-claim-only (30–40%) is not the same as the recommended first wave (digital ∩ non-legal). Power (“300 cases, 90% to detect 3 h”) has no SD, test, or clustering assumption. WIP caps (5/4/3) are invented. Identity service-time change is attributed too cleanly to the API. `queue_huddles` is drawn from both sites as if it were a shared measured node.

**Partial credit.** Tables 1, 3, and 4 are complete and mostly careful. Scenarios include ranges and a tail-worsening case. The POWL *text* in Section 6 preserves the statutory edges even though the Python does not. `conditional go` is the right call, and the expansion/abandonment gates match the failure modes.

The analogical core is well above mediocre; the code, graph, and DFG-to-number pipeline are not. A nearly flawless answer needed a runnable POWL net with acyclic order edges, a graph that does not assign Site A co-interventions to Site B, and scenario math tied to the given frequencies.
