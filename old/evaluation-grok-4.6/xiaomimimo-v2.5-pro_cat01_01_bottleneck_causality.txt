**7.3**

The final report is a strong, process-aware causal memo: it correctly detects the crude-versus-stratified reversal, refuses to treat stratification as identification, separates queue wait from review service, treats resource group as a bundled routing/shift/skill treatment, and recommends a safer learning design than a 50% reassignment. Arithmetic on the supplied tables is accurate. Format compliance is high.

It is not nearly flawless.

**Causal slips.** Section 1 never explicitly names total versus direct versus mediated Orion effects, though later sections imply them. Calling `compliance_required` “upstream of `resource_group`” is wrong: both are downstream of complexity/policy. That error then infects H4, which still proposes a front-door/mediation analysis through a variable the same answer says the group does not cause. The compliance-required near-tie (26.69 vs 27.00) is over-read as “process path controlled”; those cells remain mixed on complexity, shift, and load.

**Table logic.** H2’s “predicted observation” is the result that would *weaken* H2 (Orion still faster), not the observation H2 itself predicts. Discriminating tests are otherwise specific enough.

**Code.** The script matches the required diagnostics and does not use dummy timestamps for performance analysis, but `pd.StringIO` will fail on current Pandas (`io.StringIO` is required). That is a reproducibility defect in a required section.

**Overclaim.** “Negligible” Nova experience with hard/night work overstates two high-complexity and four night Nova cases. Confidence 60 is poorly anchored: the crude association is more clearly confounded than that number suggests; any residual Orion effect is less identified than it suggests.

These are not cosmetic. Under a strict bar they keep an otherwise high-quality answer well below the top of the scale.
