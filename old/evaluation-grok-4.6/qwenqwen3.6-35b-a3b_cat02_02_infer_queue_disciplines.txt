**4.5**

The write-up looks like a complete process-mining memo and gets the *labels* for QA and QD in the right neighborhood, but it fails the actual assignment: reconstruct decision sets from the event log. Several of those reconstructions are factually wrong, one invented data defect contaminates later sections, and the QA table contradicts the QA prose.

**Fatal evidence errors**

1. **QA at 09:41.** A05 finished 09:20, A06 09:32, A07 09:41. The only remaining job is A08. Listing A05–A08 as eligible and “observing” A08 versus FIFO’s A05 is not a close call; it is the wrong decision time. The analysis then says this “aligns with FIFO,” which is the opposite of what that table implies (observed A08, FIFO A05, priority A08). That is an internal contradiction at the core of §2.

2. **B01 intervals.** `09:31-09:48; 10:14-10:28` means B01 *stopped* at 09:48. There is no overlap with B05 `10:00-10:14`. B03 ran 09:48–10:00; B05 is a normal start after B03, not a preemption of B01. The answer treats 10:00 as a preemption, then builds an “implicit preemption / logging gap” story that the log does not contain. That error reappears in §1 (identifiability), §2, §3, and §5 exception #4.

3. **QC at 10:32.** C06 arrived 10:25 (`est_min` 6) and was waiting when C04 (`est_min` 22) started. Omitting C06 makes C04 look like an SPT success; including it is an SPT failure. The claim that “C02–C03–C04–C06 exactly matches `est_min`” is therefore false as a decision-sequence argument (and C05 sits between C04 and C06).

4. **QD at 11:25.** D08 (P1, mechanical, `est_min` 8, arrived 11:16) and D09 (arrived 11:20) were eligible for D-M. The table lists only D04. The narrative elsewhere notices D08 waiting, so the reconstruction and the commentary are not even aligned.

**Missed discriminating episodes (the task’s main demand)**

The strongest anti-priority / anti-SPT episode on QA is **08:30/08:32: A02 over A04** (P1, shorter). It is absent. Also missing: **09:05 A05 over waiting P1 A08**; **QB 09:31 resume B01 over P2 B03**; **QB 09:48 B03 over already-arrived P1 B05** (scan delay is a possible excuse, not used); **D-X idle 11:20–11:30** holding for D06’s appointment. Those are the observations that separate appointment, resume-preempted, manual reassignment, and FIFO from the named textbook rules.

**Diagnosis quality**

- **QA (appointment + FIFO, 75)** is directionally right, but A08-after-P2/P3 is *support* for FIFO, not the strongest contradiction of that hybrid. The real identifiability issue at 08:20 is appointment **vs SPT** (both pick A03), not “priority delayed by calibration.”
- **QB (preemptive priority + FIFO, 85)** is overconfident and partly false. B02 preempting B01 is solid. B05 “preempting” B01 is not. Resume-B01-over-B03 and B03-over-B05 break automatic preemptive priority. The second stop is labeled “dispatcher reassignment,” i.e. a plausible manual exception, not a clean rule.
- **QC (SPT + deadline override, 70)** is a fair hypothesis, undercut by C04≻C06 and by starting C05 *after* deadline 10:50. “Deadline override” that fires four minutes late is weaker than claimed.
- **QD (skill + FIFO, 90)** is the best row, but appointment hold on D-X is a real secondary component, and the decision tables do not compare cross-resource eligibility (D-M vs D-X for mechanical work).

**Other strict deductions**

§2 promised FIFO / priority / SPT / appointment / skill at each time; QC swaps in an ad hoc deadline column; QD barely tests skill alternatives. The “±15 minute” appointment buffer is invented (A06 is five minutes late; B07 is ignored). §4’s QC 10:10 FIFO-vs-SPT test is the only clean counterfactual; the QB 10:28 test is valid but weaker than episodes already in the log. §5 ranks a non-existent logging gap. §6 and §7 are the strongest parts (fields are relevant; QD most identifiable and “don’t infer priority on QA/QC” are defensible).

**Net.** Format-compliant, some correct high-level labels, and not the naive “every FIFO violation is priority” failure. That is why this is not a 2–3. It is not close to flawless: the required decision-set work is repeatedly wrong, and those errors are used as if they were evidence. Under a hypercritical reading that weights reconstructions and consistency first, **4.5**.
