## Central error: the 36% working-together calculation

The answer states that if co-case rates use all ~4,100 claims, Mira–Carla at 8% is ~328 claims, or ~36% of Mira’s 905 cases. That denominator is inconsistent with the same table. Alice–Carla is reported at 27%. 27% of 4,100 is ~1,107 cases, which exceeds Alice’s 665 distinct cases. The percentages therefore cannot all be shares of the full log.

A careful reading had to notice this before using 328 / 36% as a fact. Plausible alternatives:

- Jaccard / union normalization. Mira 905 and Carla 870 at 8% implies roughly 130 shared cases, about 14% of Mira’s caseload, not 36%.
- Normalization by one resource’s own case set. Then 8% of Mira is ~72 cases; 8% of Carla is ~70.
- A filtered human-only or multi-resource subset, or a scenario-reporting inconsistency.

The *direction* of the argument still holds—a globally small pair can be common inside Mira’s cohort—but the specific number used to resolve the main contradiction is not supported. That is a real process-mining validity failure, not a cosmetic nit, because the question’s core demand is to reconcile high handover/subcontracting centrality with low working-together centrality.
