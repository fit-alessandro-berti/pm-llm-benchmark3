**6.3**

The memo is complete and lands in a defensible decision family (E, not A or B). It hits every required section, does not claim one intervention dominates, and uses most of the process-mining artifacts. It is not close to flawless.

**What works.** Alternative E is the right shape: keep the simple-house gains, refuse national scale, refuse a blanket rollback, and gate expansion. The five metric-conflict explanations are mostly mechanistically correct (fitness vs precision, median vs tail, handovers vs resilience, visible vs downstream cost, segment split). A–E are all scored with advantages, risks, and conditions. M1–M8 are ranked with an explicit “what would change the decision” clause. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are at least *attempted* as separate columns.

**Analytical errors.** The rollout gate that apartment/old-building p90/p95 must return to 31/45 days treats *overall* baseline tails as *segment* baselines. Those numbers are not in the evidence. “FTR ≥ 80%” is looser than baseline (82%), while complaint ≤ 6.1% is a full restoration—inconsistent strictness. “Skip-rule accuracy ≥ 90%” ignores the relevant error (false skips on addresses that needed a survey). The exec memo cites DFG edge shares (manual activation 8%→19%, second visit 10%→17%, complaints 3%→8%) as if they were the case-level metrics (7%, 11%, 6.1%). Utilization is labeled “social-network data”; it is not.

**Strategic gaps.** Customer-segment and building-risk attributes are incomplete before week 3, yet E is treated as immediately operational (“identified by available attributes”). That is the main reason C is more robust as a near-term control: it does not require a reliable risk classifier. The memo underrates C and treats D as mere delay. Scaling coordinator ownership while ranking M5 (absence/resilience) 7th of 8 is a VOI–decision mismatch. M7 is cheap, already motivated by alignments, and addresses the compliance constraint, but is ranked below an RCT. Business, rural, and SLA (13%→16%) barely affect the gates.

**Presentation and separation.** Change figures are systematically unreadable (`34%12%`, `8%19%`) across the memo, table, and analysis. The three-column table aligns unrelated facts, interpretations, and assumptions on the same row, which *re-merges* what the format was supposed to keep apart. Assumption 4 (“sparse start timestamps mean duration decomposition is accurate”) is backwards as written.

A strong memo would have chosen E *conditional on* attribute completeness (default-high-risk otherwise), put M7/M4/M5 next to the actions they actually unlock, and used only segment-valid thresholds. This draft is competent strategy with several real mistakes, not a near-flawless decision product.
