That is a solid process-mining essay. It is not an accurate quantitative analysis of the table that was supplied.

## Material numerical errors

These are not rounding differences. They sit in the tables a reader would actually use.

**Pooled volume is invented.** Across 28 days the table has **2,519 arrivals and 2,409 completions** (means **90.0 and 86.0 per day**, **7.50 and 7.17 per open hour**). The answer states **2,730 and 2,712** (**97.5 and 96.9 per day**). That is +211 arrivals and +303 completions. The claimed near-balance of 18 cases is false; the real net is **+110**. The “month looks stable, so average staffing is fine” argument still works directionally, but the quantitative premise is wrong and the imbalance is *larger* than advertised.

**Primary-regime intensities are wrong in three of five rows.**

| Regime | Claimed arrivals / completions per day | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline 6–14 Apr | 85.4 / 84.6 | **86.8 / 86.2** (781 / 776) |
| Recovery 16–19 Apr | 76.5 / 82.0 | **74.5 / 82.0** (298 / 328) |
| High-mix 20–28 Apr | 88.0 / 76.2 | **99.1 / 87.3** (892 / 786) |

The high-mix miss is about 12%. The table therefore understates later demand and makes the mix-shift period look almost like the post-intervention overall rate (88.6). Weekday high-mix arrivals are also off (112.8 vs **114.0**). The “roughly +96” deficit on 20–28 Apr is **+106**.

**Little’s Law numbers inherit the same mistakes.** Baseline \(W\) uses \(\lambda=7.11\) instead of **7.23**, so 2.63 h should be about **2.58 h**. High-mix \(L=38.1\) should be **39.2**; with the wrong \(\lambda\) the 5.20 / 6.00 pair is not the calculation implied by the data (about **4.75 / 5.39**). The *verdicts* remain reasonable; the displayed arithmetic does not.

**Mean WIP is slightly wrong** (30.5 vs **29.9**). Small, but it is the pooled \(L\) they lean on.

The code embeds the correct daily rows. If executed it would print different regime means than Section 2 and different pooled totals than Section 4. A “reproducible” appendix that contradicts the narrative is a serious internal-consistency failure, not a cosmetic one.

## Factual and conceptual slips

**Threat 12 is false.** Every representative hourly vector sums to the daily `arrivals` and `completed` counts (101/99, 105/58, 115/101, 51/43). Presenting 4 Apr 7 completions as a mismatch, and then waving the other days away as “not a complete record,” is careless reading of the only event-level evidence in the prompt.

**\(A-C\) is mischaracterized.** Given only arrivals and completions as flows, \(A_d-C_d\) *is* the open-hours inventory change. Unobserved opening WIP affects the *level*, not the increment. Overnight carry-over matters for interpreting WIP and flow time; it does not make \(\Delta Q \approx A-C\) inexact in the way claimed.

**Pooled service “low-30s / upper-60s” is an unweighted mean of daily quantiles**, not a case-level summary. In an answer that otherwise lectures about Simpson-like aggregation, that should have been labeled.

**`peak_08_10_pct` is almost unused.** It jumps with the mix break (weekday peaks ~26–34% before 20 Apr vs ~40–50% after) and is independent corroboration of the burstiness / non-stationarity story. Ignoring a supplied column is a missed check, not a fatal flaw.

Sections 5–6 discuss Fano, CV, lag-1 autocorrelation, and a change-point heuristic, but the narrative never reports the numbers the code is supposed to produce. Definitions without results are thinner than the task asked for.

## What still holds

Most qualitative conclusions survive the arithmetic: weekdays and weekends are different operating modes; 15 Apr is a capacity shock; 16–17 Apr is backlog clearing, not a new productivity regime; from ~20 Apr the joint movement in complexity, service tails, WIP, peaks, and daily deficits is a different pressure state; 29 Apr onward is a short, contaminated recovery window; a single \(M/M/c\)-style daily model is misleading; Little’s Law is only even approximately usable on the early weekday stretch. Uncertainty language (coding change vs real mix, unknown go-live time, serial dependence) is appropriately cautious.

That is why this is not a 4. The analysis understands the process. It does not reliably count the process.

## Why not higher

A high score required correct definitions *and* coherent calculations. Inventing ~8–12% extra volume, publishing a high-mix arrival rate of 88 instead of 99, and contradicting one’s own code are not minor. Combined with the false hourly-reconciliation claim and the \(\Delta Q\) slip, a strict reading stops in the mid-6s. Fix the arithmetic so that Sections 2, 4, and 6 match the Python output, drop the bogus hourly caveat, and this would sit closer to 8.5.
