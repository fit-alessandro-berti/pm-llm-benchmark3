**6.6**

The final write-up is a strong causal-policy memo with the right overall stance: no single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim, availability ≠ use, median ≠ tail, endogenous staggered rollout, and a learning-oriented next step. It is not close to flawless.

**What holds up.** Sections 2, 4, 6, 7, 8, and 10 are the core of the score. Estimands are well posed (ITT vs intensity, median vs p90, rework/escalation, CSAT, dynamic/heterogeneous effects), with interference (shared fraud desk) and treatment-version issues named. Mechanisms are process-aware rather than generic DiD talk. Threats cover the required list and map to bias direction and decision consequences. The effect table is appropriately unidentified/low-confidence. The rollout choice (`expand with an embedded evaluation`), Delta-then-Epsilon holdout, queue/fraud-desk data, guardrails, and a pre-registered scale-up rule actually improve both operations and identifiability. The claim ladder is correctly ordered and calibrated.

**Quantitative discipline is uneven.** Pre/post and Mar–Aug arithmetic is mostly right and windows are transparent. Two real defects remain. First, the control-trend sensitivity drops signs: the implied DiD figures are reductions of about 7.8 / 3.1 / 5.3 hours, but the text reports “7.8 h … 3.1 h … 5.3 h” and then “**8 h to +3 h**,” while §7 is only readable as a reduction-to-increase interval if the leading minus is inferred. That is sloppy on a criterion the rubric weights. Second, Jan–Mar averages for escalation mix the March fraud-desk jump into the “pre” period; the later Mar–Aug table partly fixes this, but the headline adopter table does not.

**Identification and code do not match.** The primary design (cohort-specific event study / CITS with Delta–Epsilon as not-yet-treated controls) is the right family and correctly rejects naive pooled TWFE. The Python event-study then fits relative-time dummies **only on the three adopters**, with branch FE and **no calendar-time FE and no never-treated units**. That is within-treated time variation, not the design in §3; relative-time coefficients absorb common shocks. Dummy support is also unbalanced (early leads are Gamma-only; long lags are Alpha/Beta-only), so the single regression is not the “cohort-specific” estimator advertised. The intensity TWFE is labeled descriptive and clustered, which is fine; the event-study implementation is not.

**Format and completeness nits (each would already block a top score).** §3’s justification is ~130 words against a 150–250 requirement. §1 omits Beta’s April FTE gain, Delta’s June FTE gain, and Gamma’s ~5% post-July routing, and slightly misstates Gamma as “received a pilot.” §5’s intensity analysis is narrative only (no coefficient). Strategies are one combined table rather than a table per strategy.

**Net.** Conceptually well above a generic “run TWFE and announce X%.” Hypercritically, sign errors, the code–design contradiction, and the missed word-count/co-event details keep it well short of near-flawless.
