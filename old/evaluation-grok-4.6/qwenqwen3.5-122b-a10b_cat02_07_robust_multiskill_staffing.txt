3.2

The write-up has the required nine headings and some of the right *ideas* (two objectives, three named policies, specialization-vs-pooling tension, Chen/software and morning-coverage risks). It is not close to a competent, auditable analysis.

**Load and mechanics are wrong.** Offered load is treated as \(42 \times 50\) minutes and ~84% utilization. That ignores that ME/ES cases consume *two* diagnostic branches, complex-tail mix, speed multipliers that *lengthen* work, handovers/setup/coordination, and time-varying qualified capacity. There is no skill-by-block offered-load table, which was the core capacity task. Speed is also inverted: the prompt defines 1.25 as 25% *slower*; the narrative and code treat larger multipliers as faster. Breaks, overtime, and team-change delay are stated then dropped from the math.

**The hybrid policy is operationally incoherent.** “Core” is Ada/Ben/Deepa, none of whom can do Software. Routing P1 to Core first therefore fails for S/ES P1 work—the scarcest skill family. That error alone makes the robustness claim unreliable.

**Section 4 is not the required comparison.** It omits `p90_or_p95_delay`, `P2_SLA`, `utilization_fairness`, `resilience`, and `main_failure_mode` as first-class fields. The numbers (3.5h, 92% P1 SLA, etc.) are not derived from the stated method or the code; they are invented and then used to “prove” Policy C. That is a serious consistency failure.

**The Python does not meet the spec.** It is not a working DES or a transparent capacity model. Diagnostic activities are logged as `"Diag"` and then looked up in `SERVICE_TIMES` / the skill matrix (would error or silently misuse speeds). Complex-tail uses only Intake’s probability. U1’s 1.7× multiplier is applied to *all* blocks. U2 is a random M→E flip, not double E/ES then renormalize. Parallel/sequential ME-ES, 7-minute handover, 8-minute coordination, preemption+10% restart, and breaks are not implemented. Only Base/U1 Hybrid are run. Required PM4Py calls are stubbed or commented (`insert_case_service_waiting_time` is `pass`; handover network is commented). No policy×scenario metric table, no seed/uncertainty summary, no correctly labeled case-level wait vs service.

**Smaller but material issues:** document is pasted twice; `pm4py.get_bottleneck` is not a real standard API; observations, estimates, and hypotheses are not kept distinct; parallelism trade-off is asserted, not bounded; recommendation “dominates” on fabricated metrics.

Structurally complete scaffolding with several correct qualitative instincts, but incorrect definitions, non-reproducible numbers, a broken simulator, and a policy that cannot serve P1 software. Far from the quality bar.
