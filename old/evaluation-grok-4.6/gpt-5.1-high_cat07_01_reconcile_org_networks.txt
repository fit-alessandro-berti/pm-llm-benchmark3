**Format and coverage.** All six required top-level headings are present. Each network type gets an explicit “does / does not imply” treatment. Four hypotheses are offered, including the three mandated ones. The six named risks appear. PM4Py proposals cover the six required families. The executive summary is within the word bound and readable. These are table-stakes, not distinction.

**Core contradiction.** The handover-versus-working-together puzzle is handled correctly in qualitative terms: Mira is a short-duration routing node, so directed sequential centrality is high while undirected co-case rates stay low. That is the right mechanism. It is still under-specified. The answer never uses the working-together weighted degrees (Mira 0.16 vs Alice/Carla ~0.46–0.49) or the many-weak-ties versus few-strong-ties contrast, which is the actual graph-theoretic statement of the contradiction.

**Serious interpretive error.** The bottleneck hypothesis’s *limiting* evidence claims that exception cases “can still be processed (albeit slowly) in her absence, so there are alternative, if less efficient, paths.” The given facts do not support this. Exception cases *accumulated in queues*, and the 90th percentile rose from 8.4 to 13.9 days (+5.5), against a 6-day absence. That is much closer to work waiting for her return than to a degraded substitute path. Inventing a limiter that the natural experiment contradicts is a calibration failure, not a nit.

**Hypotheses are not competing.** Formal exception desk, bottleneck, boundary spanner, and quality gatekeeper can all be true of the same person at once. The prompt asked for distinct, testable alternatives and explicitly cued data artifact, automation monitor, informal team lead, and specialist scheduler. None of those is developed.
