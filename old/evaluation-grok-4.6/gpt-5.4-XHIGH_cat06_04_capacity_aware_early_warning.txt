**6.8**

The answer is structurally complete and passes the main “strong answer” gates: I-03 is refused on stage grounds, I-05 is suppressed as refresh churn while I-07 is treated as a legitimate re-entry, and spillovers actually bind capacity. It is not nearly flawless. Several rules contradict the cases they are applied to, the ranking is not auditable, and a few quantitative objects are internally inconsistent.

**Decision rule.** The gated structure is the right shape: stage eligibility, material-change re-entry, imputed-feature provisionalization, segment calibration bands, and a conservative action value that prices labor, false-alarm burden, and a queue externality. Two design flaws weaken it. First, the €1.5k spillover charge is asserted, not derived; the later impact table implies about €2.1k at base (6 × 0.02 × €15–20k). The rule and the evaluation model therefore do not use the same price of a slot. Second, the NNL “use the lower half of the effect range” instruction is almost empty where it matters. Conservative value uses the lower bound; the lower half of 0.18–0.30 is still 0.18 at the floor, so I-09’s binding score is identical to using the raw range. Caution is claimed, not implemented. The 0.08 risk-move cutoff and the €2.5k / 20-minute stop rule are also poorly anchored: 8–20 minutes is already the stated normal delay, so the stop fires at the top of business-as-usual.

**Allocation.** I-04 and I-09 are defensible on the stated conservative-value rule, and holding a regulatory reserve is consistent with the prompt. The table still has clear errors of application.

- I-07 and I-10 have positive midpoint value after cost and should be **B** under the author’s own tiering (“midpoint positive but conservative case not yet clear”). They are labeled **C**. I-10 is then given a “Wait” action that the rule reserved for B-tier. The mapping from score to tier to action is not stable.
- I-07 is the sharpest inconsistency. The same memo says: break cool-down below 2 hours; new customer evidence re-opens review; protect very-high-risk cases from pure-euro starvation; SME risk is underpredicted by 0.04–0.09. I-07 is 1.5 h from SLA, has a new log, and is an underpredicted SME. The *decision* not to swarm can be justified on value (€5k cannot pay for slot plus spillover). The *tier*, the unused protected-lane language, and the failure to treat the 1.5 h clock as almost equivalent to “do not intervene” are sloppy. Waiting and refusing are not distinct actions at 1.5 h.
- I-02 is the main operational risk. It is already in Diagnosis Started with 3.0 h left. The intervention dies at Diagnosis Complete. Calling this “Wait” and parking it as a promotion candidate under-weights window closure. The conservative score is negative, so the wait is rule-consistent, but then the rule is too crude for stage-and-clock constraints the author listed as secondary sort keys and never showed numerically.
- No case-level conservative/mid arithmetic is shown. A1 vs B1 vs C1 cannot be audited from the memo. That is a failure of the required “deployable / auditable” standard, not a presentation nit.
- Using two slots, reserving one, *and* leaving one uncommitted is a coherent robust stance, but specialist sequencing is missing. Two seniors deliver the swarm; two slots now occupy both of them for ~45 minutes. That is the actual reason to keep afternoon inventory, and it is never stated.

**Expected-impact table.** Optimistic and base breach totals and direct-benefit bands for I-04/I-09 are reproducible (upper-half effects 0.19–0.28 and 0.24–0.30; mids 0.19 and 0.24). Adverse is not. €3.2–4.2k is consistent with zeroing I-09 and keeping I-04 near its floor, possibly still charging I-09’s €800 burden, but that path is not written down. Spillover uses an unexplained €15–20k untreated-case value. Unlisted diagnosis-queue cases were not alerted and are likely lower value or lower risk; using alerted-portfolio values inflates harm and then is used to justify leaving capacity idle. Base “~€13.4k” is a point, not a range. Confidence labels are reasonable; the arithmetic standard the prompt asked for is only met on two of three rows.

**Repeated-alert policy.** Cool-down, no-repeat-swarm, and I-05 vs I-07 are the strongest page in the memo. Residual gaps: it is not explicit that cool-down expiry *without* a material event does not restore eligibility (the eligibility gate implies this, the temporal section does not). I-07 is “re-opened then refused,” which matches value logic but collides with the <2 h escalation clause if a reader expects escalation to mean action rather than review.

**Fairness.** All five required risks appear, and several safeguards are concrete (value-decile audit, intervention indicators, harm attributed to untreated segments, concurrent-swarm cap). Two problems. The protected public/high-risk lane is not applied to the live book: I-01 (public, good effect, complete data) waits; I-07 (very high adjusted risk, 1.5 h) is refused. Selecting I-09 so as not to “hard-code a fairness problem” is the right instinct, but it sits beside treating I-09’s least-identified effect range as if the lower bound were a known causal floor. That is exactly what the prompt forbids.

**Monitoring.** Breadth is good (ops, calibration, causal, capacity, fairness, drift, numeric triggers). The headline trigger is broken: `ECE > 0.05` for two review periods is *already* true for SME and Strategic Enterprise in the setup. The rule would fire on day one and does not distinguish known accepted miscalibration from new drift. Monthly doubly robust estimates from a handful of slots per day will be weak; randomized near-threshold perturbations are the right idea, but sample-size and governance (who changes the rule, what the fallback is) are absent.

**Bottom line.** Decision-theoretic posture is real—conservative value, scenario stress, reserved optionality, refusal to rank on `p_now`. Execution is not tight enough for a high score: the author does not follow their own tiers, prices spillover two different ways, leaves the ranking and the adverse row under-specified, and ships a recalibration trigger that the memo’s own calibration section already violates. Those are logic and audit failures, not polish issues.
