6.2

The write-up is well structured and often technically competent, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it contains several factual errors, overclaims, and incomplete treatments of quantities the prompt explicitly asked for.

**What is actually strong.** Assumptions A1–A9 are labeled and mostly operational. Waiting, service, and sojourn are defined separately; OPEN work is treated as elapsed time, not finished time; missing starts are bounded from resource release, queue entry, and completion rather than filled with one silent value. The invariance of station sojourn and WIP to start imputation is correct and well used. Case-level arithmetic for the 22 traces is largely accurate (P01–P22 waits/services, P05/P07/P11/P15 feasible windows, 2085 and 1340 case-minutes, mean WIP 5.79 / 3.72 / 9.51, peak WIP 8 and 7, central busy times 595 / 570). Censoring and survivor bias are discussed. The bottleneck story — early S1 congestion from a front-loaded arrival burst, then structural S2 after the handoff, with the C2 outage as an amplifier rather than the root cause — is the right qualitative picture. The PM4Py caveats (case-level `@@waiting_time` is the S1→S2 gap, not S1 queue wait; pandas is safer for station WIP) are exactly the distinction the task wanted.

**Factual errors.** S1 completed-wait median is reported as 67.5 min; the 20 completed waits sort to a median of 72.5. Mean completed-system sojourn is given as 147.5 min / 2.46 h; the 14 completed totals sum to 2050 min, mean 146.4 min / 2.44 h. Section 5.3 claims S1 WIP is identically 8 for 75 minutes from 10:15–11:30 and that flow is perfectly balanced there. On a `[start, end)` clock the occupancy oscillates 7–8 whenever a completion precedes the next arrival (10:20–10:25, 10:35–10:45, 10:55–11:00, 11:05–11:15). Time-average S1 WIP on that interval is about 7.1, not 8.0. The same document asserts an S2 plateau of 5 from 11:30–13:00, which is also false (it drops to 4 at 12:10 and 12:25). Little’s Law Test 1 (`B̄ = λ_dep E[S]` plus open elapsed fractions) is an accounting identity, then advertised as “100.0% precision.” That is not a consistency check.

**Requirement gaps.** Queue length and total WIP *over time* are requested, including a convention for simultaneous rounded events. A5 states a convention, but there is no trajectory (even a 5-minute grid or a table of regime breakpoints). Section 4 gives only the full 08:00–14:00 window; subperiod arrival/departure rates appear as scattered narrative figures, not as a station-level breakdown. P15 and P17 have fully observed S2 waits (75 and 65) that are excluded from the S2 wait mean and dumped into the censored list; only their services are right-censored. A6 asserts FIFO, yet P09 enters S2 later than P08 and starts earlier on C1 while C2 is in/after maintenance — the one place FIFO vs. resource-locking actually matters, and it is not analyzed. A9 (work conservation) is contradicted in-sample by I2 idle 13:45–14:00 with P22 waiting; that fact is noted as a local hypothesis and never fed back into the “earliest feasible” imputations for P07/P15.

**Smaller inconsistencies.** S2 capacity is 3.00/h from a 40 min service in §5 but 38.2 min in §4. Post-starvation C2 time is 315 min rather than 320 (08:40–14:00) or 305 after maintenance. Primary ranges in §4 are imputation-only; rounding (±2.5 min per stamp, ±5 min per duration) is discussed later and not folded into those intervals. Central starts for P07/P15 insert a 5 min idle on I1 while a case is waiting, which sits in tension with A9.

**Python.** The script does build the table, distinguish MISSING / OPEN / blank, call `format_dataframe`, run three imputation scenarios, and label PM4Py outputs as case-level. It does not reconstruct a WIP path for the reader, does not implement the Little’s Law intervals argued in the text, does not print the case-level reconstruction table, and only reports `len(pm4py_intervals)`. Passing a DataFrame into `convert_log_to_time_intervals` / the insert helpers may or may not match the installed PM4Py API; that is unverified in the answer. Scenario D from the narrative is absent from the code.

The document reads like a strong draft that was not fully checked. The core reconstruction is usable; the errors in summary statistics, the invented plateaus, the tautological “validation,” and the missing WIP trajectory are enough that it cannot be scored as nearly correct.
