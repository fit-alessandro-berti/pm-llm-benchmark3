7.2

This is a strong, well-structured process-mining analysis that meets the required format and most of the quality bar: it separates demand, mix, capacity, and backlog; it refuses to treat outage recovery as a normal high-throughput regime; and it is appropriately cautious about Little’s Law. It is not nearly flawless. Several numerical, definitional, and segmentation errors are material under a strict reading.

**What is done well.** Estimands are defined correctly. Opening-hours convention, the distinction between active service and flow time, unpaired same-day arrivals/completions, unobserved overnight backlog, and the unknown 04-29 implementation time are all handled with the right epistemic status. The primary split isolates outage, recovery flush, the 04-20 mix/peak shift, and the intervention transition rather than pooling them. The Simpson-style comparison (pooled post-intervention completions 85.0 vs pre 87.3, while both weekday and weekend strata improve) is computed correctly and is the right kind of pooling critique. The hourly-vector vs `peak_08_10_pct` contradiction is a sharp, evidence-based caveat. The Little’s Law matrix is the best section: windows with unstable boundaries are marked not defensible, implied \(W\) is labeled open-hours-equivalent, and median service is never substituted for \(W\).

**Material errors.**

1. **Backlog identity is wrong.** Cumulative \(C-A\) from 04-06 through 04-28 is \(-128\), not \(-115\). The \(-115\) figure is weekday-only nets (weekends dropped). That is not a backlog. Weekend nets through 04-28 sum to \(-13\) and must count. Section 5 then presents \(-115\) as “relative backlog,” which is internally inconsistent with the inventory story the rest of the memo relies on. The nearby “about \(-51\)” / “about \(-21\)” figures are only accidentally close (true values \(-52\) and \(-22\)) because early weekend nets are small; the error becomes large after 04-25/26.

2. **The alternative segmentation contaminates its own causal contrast.** The date list double-counts 04-25/26 (already inside 04-20–04-28) and, more importantly, the reported unassisted averages (\(A \approx 90.0\), \(C \approx 80.4\), \(\mathrm{WIP} \approx 35.8\), net \(-106\)) only reproduce if 05-02 and 05-03 are folded into “unassisted high-complex.” Those days are after the intervention, have 3.0 FTE (not 2.5), and are balanced. The code’s `staff_fte >= 6.5` rule is what causes this. That undercuts the claim that the alternative is the cleaner causal story of “unassisted vs assisted.”

3. **Weekday Fano / lag-1 are over-interpreted.** Pooled daily arrival Fano \(\approx 10\) vs weekday \(0.66\) / weekend \(0.73\) is a good mixture point, but “all weekdays” still mixes ordinary, outage, recovery, high-complex, and post-intervention days. Within-regime weekday Fano would be the relevant comparator and is not shown. Lag-1 on the weekday subsequence deletes weekends, so it is not calendar serial dependence. Treating \(0.54\) / \(0.79\) as weekday persistence without that caveat is a competence slip.

**Smaller but real issues.** Average intensity \(8.35/\mathrm{h}\) is written into the primary table as if it were a usable stationary rate, while the same memo shows strong intra-day concentration; the table should have said “12-hour average, not a constant rate.” The \(n=2\) post-intervention weekend Little’s Law window is too short and too exposed to unknown 07:00 stock to be more than a numerical illustration; “approximate only” is generous. 04-06–04-14 is called a stable baseline even though 04-13/14 already drift up in mix, peak share, and WIP. Confidence labels have no stated rule. Unweighted means of `complex_pct` and `peak_08_10_pct` are used as if they were volume-weighted process parameters.

**Python block.** It satisfies the mechanical requirements (in-memory tables, regime summaries, two dispersion measures plus serial dependence, an explicitly heuristic change-point score, Little’s Law only on chosen windows, PM4Py-as-comment, compact prints). It also encodes the alternative-segmentation bug above, so reproducibility here means reproducing a flawed state definition.

The memo would be in the high 8s if the inventory accounting were correct and the alternative states did not mix post-intervention weekends into the unassisted bucket. Those are not polish nits; they affect the backlog narrative and the intervention contrast. Under the requested strict standard, that caps the score well below “nearly flawless.”
