**6.2**

The write-up is complete, on-format, and directionally right: it refuses a frequency-only ranking, keeps A11 off the real path until controls are reliable, treats A8 as GIGO, and uses resource counts (3 / 9 / 4 FTE) plus the compliance mandate. That is competent portfolio work, not a 3–4. It is not close to flawless.

**Material reasoning errors**

- The 22.0h wait *before* contract preparation is attributed to preventing the 8.5% remediation loop. Those loops are `Contract preparation → Remediate control issue` (650) *after* prep has started. Cutting them reduces rework and cycle time, not the inbound queue.
- Four-eyes is defined as credit analysis and committee packaging not being the same person on High-exposure cases (5.8%, month-end). Treating A9/A12 as the fix, and a Phase-2 gate of “zero four-eyes violations in A9-generated contracts,” mis-locates the control. A downstream identity check can at best *detect* an upstream breach; it does not automate four-eyes.
- A6’s matrix cell “downstream bottleneck risk: Medium” contradicts the rest of the paper, where A6 is the *relief* for analyst overload.

**The matrix does not rank**

The spec asks for a table *ranking* all twelve candidates. The table is A1–A12 in catalogue order. A7’s process-level benefit is also wrong: 58.0h wait, 19% late, and a hard sync into committee packaging is not “Medium” next to A4’s 31.0h “High.”

**Explicit process-mining instructions were skipped**

Concurrency is stated and then ignored: KYC and financial-document review run in parallel; the log is sequential. No portfolio effect discusses the long pole on the parallel branch. The DFG split `Classify → KYC` (7250) vs `Classify → Financial review` (3940), the join `KYC → Credit` (4200) / `Normalize → Credit` (5000), `Resolve → KYC` (1160) vs `Resolve → Contract` (380), and `Wait valuation → Packaging` (1980) vs `Credit → Packaging` (2700) are almost unused. Register-in-position-2 for 98% of cases, committee weekly cadence for high exposure, BO utilization falling after 15:00, and the handover network concentrating KYC work on two of three specialists never inform the ranking.

**The recommended four miss the problem’s own thesis**

The stem is that low-frequency sync, rework, and risk dominate frequency. A4 (1750 cases, 55 min, 31.0h wait, 38% rework, on the critical path in 82% of those cases, 3 FTE) and A7 (58.0h external wait, required in 71% of equipment finance) are the exhibits. The recommended set is A2, A6, A9, A12 — data quality, analyst time, and contract controls. That is a coherent *compliance-and-intake-quality* bet, but A4 is dismissed in one sacrifice sentence (medium feasibility / limited coverage is never argued), and A7 is left in the unused bottleneck archetype. A mixed four (e.g. A4 + A6 + A9 + A12) is the obvious synthesis and is not considered. Archetype portfolios are tidy; they are not the best use of a four-slot budget.

**First-wave control constraint is only half-met**

Year-1 includes A9 and A12, so the annual portfolio satisfies “at least one module strengthen controls.” Phase 1 is only A2 + A6 — routing and extraction. If “first wave” means the first deployment, compliance is deferred six months. A12 needs no core-banking slot and could have gone in H1. The two-deep-integration cap is respected, but H1 uses only one deep slot and the six-month wording around A9 is muddled.

**What still works**

Dependencies are mostly right (A2/A6 before A8/A9; A10/A11 after KYC; A1 floods specialists and analysts). A11 is correctly first-year Low. Exception handling, audit trail, drift, and PDF fallback are present. The A1 political win is rejected with a process-level argument rather than a slogan. That keeps the score in the mid-6s rather than the 4–5 range. The unused artifacts, the wait-time/four-eyes errors, the unranked matrix, and the failure to put the 31h/58h sync points into the actual four-slot mix are enough to bar anything that looks “strong.”
