**7.4**

This is a strong, well-structured response that meets the required format and is clearly grounded in the given artifacts. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several inaccuracies and logical gaps are material.

**What works.** The diagnosis correctly frames the core contradiction: the modal `Auto eligibility screen → Fast approval → Dispatch replacement` path (~2,300 of 5,000 cases) is fast and 31% cheaper before rework, then collapses to a 9% net advantage after complaints, reships, and credits. Artifact use is dense and mostly accurate: frequency DFG volumes, performance DFG means/medians/p90s, R1–R3, alignment move-on-log deviations, fitness 0.91 vs 68% fitting traces, low precision, 14.1% vs 3.3% reopen rates, segment splits (22%/18% vs 5%/6% on high-value), the 26% repeat-evidence statistic, the 140-hour unclear-defect segment, underused quality check, and the EUR 95 / EUR 410 replacement-error costs. The three redesigns are genuinely different mechanisms (front-end policy gating, evidence-loop redesign, fulfillment/exception control), not parameter tweaks. Sacrifices are stated. The table avoids fake precision. The experiment section correctly insists on full-loop, post-close measurement rather than first-pass cycle time.

**Factual error.** The claim that `Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review` (34h mean) is “the single largest delay jump in the prompt” is false. Even if complaint edges are treated as outcome lags rather than handoffs, `Customer complaint/reopen → Escalation board` is 54h mean and `Customer complaint/reopen → Manual eligibility review` is 40h. Among outcome tails, `Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen` is 96h / 180h p90. This is sloppy evidence use on a criterion the answer is being judged on.

**Redesign C is over-bundled and internally inconsistent on compliance.** C combines a pre-dispatch quality gate, an event-driven exception cell, pre-priced parts, and reopen rerouting. That is three or four interventions sold as one redesign. More importantly, C explicitly “does not fix upstream overuse of fast approval,” yet the table rates compliance “Medium-low.” R1 requires Manual or Fraud/risk review *before Fast approval* for claims above EUR 400; the alignment diagnostics specifically flag dispatch without risk review for high-value devices. A part-selection gate after a noncompliant fast approval does not satisfy R1. The compliance rating is therefore optimistic.

**Redesign A’s volume and SLA logic is under-argued.** A hard-blocks all high-value devices, all claims above EUR 400, and all unclear-defect cases from fast approval. Premium + Standard high-value is already 35% of volume; many of those cases currently sit on the fast path (Premium HV median 20h). The failure-mode section then treats inflow risk as the prompt’s 15–35% rules-update range, which is the expected effect of a *rules refresh*, not necessarily of this harder gate. That is a substitution of a nearby number for an unestimated effect.

**Pilot sequencing clashes with the segment data.** The plan starts with A on high-value / >EUR 400 cases, including Premium HV, while protecting only Premium *low-risk* accessories. The same prompt says Premium HV reviewed/repair median is 96 hours and Premium service-credit exposure is high above 24 hours. The go-criteria ask for a one-third to one-half complaint drop *and* Premium SLAs not breaking. For Premium HV under A, those conditions are likely incompatible. The stop rule watches Premium *low-risk* drift above 24 hours—the segment the pilot deliberately leaves on the fast path. The hardest trade-off in the data is therefore the one the experiment is least set up to detect.

**Smaller defects.** “Dominated? Conditional” for C is unexplained. Standard customers’ 72-hour tolerance *if status visibility is good* is an unused CX lever. `Fast approval → Quality/compliance close` (350) and `Repair quote → Customer complaint/reopen` (80) are ignored. The 1,430 figure is a valid edge count, but it is not unique cases. C’s title centers the exception cell while the mechanism write-up leads with the gate.

The piece is defensible, non-generic, and better than a typical process-redesign essay. It is not tight enough on evidence precision, compliance logic, or experimental identification to score in the high 8s or 9s.
