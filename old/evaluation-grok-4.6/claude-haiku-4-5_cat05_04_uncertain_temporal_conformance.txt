3.2

This is an unfinished draft that gestures at the right evaluation framework and then repeatedly breaks it. A strict reading cannot treat volume, headings 1–4, or a few correct headline calls as compensating for missing required sections, a systematic profile-band error, and several SLA misclassifications that change the legal finding.

**Missing required deliverables.** The prompt demanded exactly seven top-level headings. Sections 6 (Evidential Sufficiency) and 7 (Governance Recommendation) are absent. Section 5 is truncated in the `try: import pm4py` block: there is no `pm4py.format_dataframe`, no `pm4py.conformance_temporal_profile` at ζ = 1, 2, 3, and no comparison of midpoint diagnostics with interval conclusions. That is not a style issue. It fails the stated contract.

**Wrong temporal-profile geometry.** The acceptance region is `mean ± ζ · std`. The write-up uses `mean ± (ζ+1) · std` throughout:

| Pair | Correct ζ=1 | Used as ζ=1 |
|---|---|---|
| Alert→Start | [420, 780] | [240, 960] |
| Start→Complete | [1500, 2700] | [900, 3300] |
| Alert→Complete | [1980, 3420] | [1260, 4140] |
| Complete→Close | [64800, 108000] | [43200, 129600] |

ζ=2 and ζ=3 are then shifted the same way ([60, 1140] is actually ζ=3 for Alert→Start). Every profile label and the entire ζ-sensitivity matrix is therefore computed against the wrong bands. T-408 Start→Complete at ~3300 s is a **CERTAIN_VIOLATION** at true ζ=1, not a knife-edge possible. T-401 Alert→Start is wholly above [420, 780], not a partial overlap with [240, 960].

**Interval construction is inconsistent with the stated semantics.** The write-up correctly says resolution first, then `true = recorded − offset`. It does not apply that rule uniformly.

- **HUB:** tables use `[t−0.5 s, t+0.5 s]` and drop the [−1, +1] s clock bound. The composed interval is `[t−1.5 s, t+1.5 s]`. The code later uses the wider interval; the classifications use the narrower one.
- **OPS-BER:** floor `[HH:MM:00, HH:MM:59.999]` minus [−90, +120] is `[start−120 s, end+90 s]`. T-401 Start should be about `[08:12:00, 08:16:30]` UTC, not `[08:13:30, 08:16:39.999]`. VENDOR-NY and LEGACY-BER are done correctly with the same floor rule, so the method is not even stable across sources.
- **SENSOR:** “round to nearest 10 seconds” is a ±5 s quantum, then ±5 s clock, not recorded ±10 s then ±5 s.

**Arithmetic errors that flip legal calls.**

- **T-403 S1.** Latest alert is 08:03:59.999, earliest start is 08:15:59.5. That difference is **719.5 s**, not 959.5 s. The 960 figure is `08:16 − 08:00`, i.e. the vendor floor window was ignored. Feasible duration is roughly **[720, 1141] s**. Against a 900 s cap that is **POSSIBLE_VIOLATION**, not CERTAIN_VIOLATION. The same paragraph also says the violation “depends on clock offset,” which contradicts the CERTAIN label.
- **T-404 S2.** S2 is Start→**Containment_Complete** (10:55), not Start→Investigation_Complete (10:35). Using [1080, 1501] s is the work-record duration, not the SLA pair. Correct Start→Containment is about **[2280, 2700+]** s and sits on the 45-minute knife-edge. The UTC table also prints Containment as `[10:34:59.5, 10:35:00.5]`, which is the wrong event.
- **T-401 Start→Containment.** `08:50:55 − 08:16:40` is 2055 s, not 2415 s. The “inverted interval” is an arithmetic artifact, not a causality problem. The pass label happens to survive; the duration does not.

**Sensitivity is wrong even on the author’s own intervals.**

- T-401 S1 `[810, 1000.5]` vs +2 min (1020 s) is a **CERTAIN_PASS**. The matrix says “no change.”
- T-403 S1 `[960, 1141]` vs +5 min (1200 s) is a **CERTAIN_PASS**. The matrix keeps a certain violation.
- T-404 S1 `[600, 1021]` vs +5 min is a **CERTAIN_PASS**. Marked still possible.
- T-407 S1 `[0, 1021]` vs +5 min is a **CERTAIN_PASS**. Marked still possible.
- Observation text then says T-402 “remains a SLA S1 violation regardless of tolerance” after the table correctly moved it to possible at +2 min.

**Weak-evidence cases are over-decided.** T-407 has no `Alert_Received` and the receipt-before-start rule is explicitly unlogged; that is the textbook INDETERMINATE (or a branched POSSIBLE under a stated causal assumption). The write-up collapses it to a single inferred interval and a POSSIBLE_VIOLATION. T-409 replaces the missing NTP bound with a “weak engineering estimate” and treats it as a hard interval; INDETERMINATE is the honest class unless that estimate is ring-fenced. T-406 never stably chooses whether the 5-minute entry delay moves occurrence time or only record time. Intervals wander from `[12:08:30, 12:20:30]` to `[12:09:00, 12:19:30]` to `[12:08:30, 12:14:30]`. The last choice is what produces CERTAIN_PASS on S1; the clock-only interval does not. The prompt forbade silently collapsing that choice.

**What is actually good, and why it does not save the score.** Clock-offset direction is stated correctly. DST fold logic for T-405 is the strongest part: ingestion order uniquely forces Alert in the first 02:50 (00:50 UTC) and Start/Containment in the second 02:xx (01:05 / 01:42 UTC), and those LEGACY intervals are then built correctly. T-402 S1 as a certain 15-minute breach and T-408 S2/S3 as certain breaches are the right headline legal findings. Midpoints are not used as the primary clock. That is competent fragment work, not a finished interval analysis.

**Code, even if it had not been cut off, would not have rescued this.** It depends on `pytz` rather than stdlib `zoneinfo`, still applies official OPS-BER offsets to T-409, ignores mobile entry delay, invents an Alert_Sent→Start profile, and sequences pairs by sorting `utc_lower` instead of the specified activity pairs. It also never implements S1–S3.

The piece reads as an unreconciled scratchpad (“let me recalculate,” “wait,” contradictory bounds in adjacent tables). Under a hypercritical rubric, missing two mandated sections, a systematic ζ error, and multiple wrong certain/possible calls preclude anything in the middle of the scale. Remaining credit is only for the interval vocabulary, the T-405 fold argument, and the T-402/T-408 SLA calls that survived the arithmetic.
