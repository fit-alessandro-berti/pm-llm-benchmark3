**2.5**

The write-up is well organized and matches the required heading list, but the **operator semantics are inverted**, and that error invalidates almost every verdict, trace, and structural claim.

In standard process-tree / PM4Py notation — and as the prompt itself states — `+` is **parallel** and `X` is **XOR**. Model B is described as “one check runs concurrently with the entire revision loop.” The answer treats `+` as XOR and `X` as AND, then builds the whole comparison on that reading. That is not a defensible alternative; it contradicts both the notation and the problem statement.

**What the models actually do**

- **A:** each loop body is `Analyze ‖ Check`, then optional `Approve`, then either exit or `Rework` and repeat. Every iteration has **exactly one Analyze and one Check**. Min Check = 1; max Check = number of iterations.
- **B:** `Check` is **mandatory and concurrent with the entire loop**. The loop is only Analyze + optional Approve, separated by Rework. Exactly **one** Check, arbitrarily interleaved with the whole revision cycle.

So both models always produce at least one Analyze and one Check. The answer’s claim that either model can omit Check (or that B can take Check *instead of* the loop) is false.

**Verdicts**

| View | Correct verdict | This answer |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | not equivalent (B does not give each analysis its own check before the next Rework/Close) | not equivalent, but for the wrong reason |
| P2 | equivalent under the stated dashboard abstraction (Check collapsed to a Boolean; interleaving hidden; Approve/`tau` hidden; Analyze count and Rework flag remain) | **wrong**: “not equivalent” because “B cannot produce both” |
| P3 | not equivalent (different bounded visible languages) | hedged and unclear |

Getting P2 backwards is especially costly: the prompt’s whole point is that equivalence depends on the observation boundary, and P2 is the view where A and B *can* coincide.

**Counterexamples are invalid**

Under the real semantics, none of T1–T4 are legitimate distinguishing traces:

- T1 `Open, Analyze, Rework, Check, Close` — after Rework the loop body must run again; A also requires Check in *every* iteration. Invalid in both.
- T2 `Open, Check, Rework, Analyze, Close` — Rework cannot precede the first Analyze. Invalid in both.
- T3 `Open, Analyze, Rework, Analyze, Close` — both models **require** Check. Invalid in both.
- T4 `Open, Check, Close` — both models require at least one Analyze. Invalid in both.

Required A-not-B witnesses (two checks; two analysis attempts) would look like  
`Open, Analyze, Check, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close`.  
Required B-not-A witnesses would look like  
`Open, Analyze, Rework, Analyze, Check, Close` or  
`Open, Analyze, Check, Rework, Analyze, Close`.  
Those are never given.
