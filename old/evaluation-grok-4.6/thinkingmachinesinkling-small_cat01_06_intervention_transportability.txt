**6.1**

The write-up is a competent transportability memo with the right high-level decision, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the rubric—process structure, identified (not confounded) effects, quantitative coherence, model fidelity, and internally consistent pilot design—several required pieces are wrong or only gestural.

**What holds up.** The answer correctly refuses a blanket copy of Site A. It treats the nine-hour median move as an unidentified before/after bundle (API, two extra reviewers, mix shift, huddles, possible timestamp cleanup), not as a treatment effect. The comparison table covers the mandated features and ties each one to design. Statutory order is respected: document check is not started before originals when originals are required. Bottleneck shift, IT-lock false concurrency, role specialization, and legal/supplement loops are named as effect modifiers. The recommendation (`conditional go`), first stratum (digital / no originals / no legal), and expansion/abandon triggers are directionally right.

**Transport graph and evidence tables are only partly causal.** The adjacency list includes the required nodes and useful `S_` selection nodes, and the 180–280-word note correctly says a similar control-flow diagram is not enough. But `identity_capacity` and `document_capacity` are dead ends; `case_mix` is introduced and never points at an outcome; `treatment_parallelization` is not routed through capacity. That undercuts the claim that the graph encodes the operating mechanism. Site B’s DFG counts (e.g. Register→Identity on only 2,050/3,400 cases, Identity→Document 1,160, Legal→Supplement 360) are almost unused; path shares are taken from the narrative 62%/40% figures instead of being reconciled with the observed relations. “Digital ~38%” is treated as interchangeable with “originals not required,” which the data do not establish.

**Quantitative scenarios are internally inconsistent.** Four-plus labeled scenarios and one “median down, p90 up” case are present, as required. The magnitudes do not survive mixture arithmetic. A 23–30% eligible share cannot move the **overall** median by 4–8 hours unless the treated subgroup is cut by something like 15–25 hours, or spillover is assumed without a queueing argument. Optimistic vs digital-only vs bottleneck-shift are not calibrated to the same staffing and eligibility assumptions. Ranges are asserted more than derived from Site A’s confounded deltas and Site B’s constraints.

**Pilot design contradicts its own power claim.** Stratification, a control arm, a huddle co-intervention arm, mechanism-vs-implementation diagnostics, and stop rules are the right ideas. The power target (200 cases per arm per stratum) cannot be met by sequential 4-week phases. Site B finishes ~3,400 cases in six months (~130/week). Four weeks is ~520 cases; a ~25% S1 slice is ~130 cases, not 400. Case-level randomization with shared specialist pools also leaves interference only weakly controlled. The 220-word mechanism/implementation note is one of the stronger paragraphs; the design that is supposed to implement it does not add up.

**Section 8 is a fail on model fidelity, not a style issue.** This is the largest defect. The code invents `POWL.add_node` / `add_order_edge` and even labels them conceptual. That is not the `pm4py.objects.powl.obj` construction the prompt asked for (`Transition`, `StrictPartialOrder` + `order.add_edge`, `OperatorPOWL` with `XOR`/`LOOP`). A strict partial order cannot contain the supplement cycle the text draws (`RequestSupplement → DocumentCheck → Assessment → LegalReview → RequestSupplement`). Optional legal review is not an XOR; `Decide` appears in §6 and not in the net; non-legal cases have **no** path `Assessment → Approve/Reject`. `check_is_workflow_net` / `play_out` calls are bolted onto an object that would not convert. Comments about what POWL cannot encode (data guards, capacity, locks, durations) are fine and do not rescue a non-executable, unsound control-flow model. The same cycle-as-partial-order error is already in the §6 relation set.

**Smaller cuts.** Word-count claims are sloppy. Section 6’s “one open verification task per stage” sits awkwardly next to concurrent identity and document work. Confidence numbers are reasonable in direction but not argued. None of these would be fatal alone; stacked on the POWL failure, the median-effect overclaim, and the unworkable pilot N, they keep the score well below the 8–10 band.

**Net.** Strong on “do not copy the tree; segment; A is confounded; B’s binding constraints are originals, lock, specialization, and legal loops.” Weak on the pieces the rubric weights equally: a faithful partial-order model, numbers that respect eligibility shares, and a pilot that can actually identify the mechanism it describes. That is a mid-6, not a high score.
