The answer is complete against the required eight-section scaffold: typed graph and motifs, five-plus timed calculations with counterfactuals, a full ranking table, flattening critique, multi-object narratives, spillover-aware interventions, a PM4Py-shaped script, and falsifiers. The most serious analytical miss is failure to confront contradictions already in the prompt. OVEN-1 is said to be idle 12:55–14:50 (115 min) while the machine flattening’s longest event gap is 73 min; TEST-1 is idle 16:35–17:25 (50 min) against a 39 min longest gap. A strict reading should have treated that as evidence that flattening and the object-centric narrative are not measuring the same thing—or that the idle claims are about productive state, not event absence.

A second gap is weak reconciliation with the job DFG. Stage Component → Machine Process has median 1.1 h, Pack → Dispatch median 1.1 h, and no job is >1.8 SD above mean total duration, yet the same write-up assigns J1 235 min of batch wait and 355 min of packed wait. That leaves the central puzzle—normal flattenings, abnormal waiting—only half explained.

CNC-2 is under-analyzed in the same spirit. Idle is only 10:05–10:47; 10:47–11:45 is *not* idle, and J4 still does not start until 11:45 (FIX-A frees 11:40). Ranking fixture contention as a clean local mechanism overstates what the timestamps uniquely support.

Conceptually, shipment consolidation is ranked #2 without distinguishing finished-goods/delivery wait from in-process blocking. Net: rubric-complete, numerically careful, epistemically cautious, and still missing the hardest consistencies in the evidence. That caps the score well below the near-flawless band.
