**8.1**

This is a strong, format-complete answer with verified arithmetic, real process semantics, and unusually good separation of estimands. It is not near-flawless. Under a strict rubric the defects below are enough to keep it well short of 9–10.

**What it gets right.** The crude-versus-stratified reversal is detected and not over-identified: Orion is slower overall (+6.71 h) and faster in every complexity stratum, with an auditable pooled-weight standardization (17.140 vs 19.648) and a Kitagawa split (+9.437 composition, −2.724 within-stratum). Queueing is not treated as service; waiting is correctly described as mixed with compliance delay; rework is not double-counted into service. Resource group is treated as a routing-policy bundle, not a simple treatment. Total, controlled-direct, mediated, and 50% policy effects are distinguished, including interference/SUTVA and the pre-assignment prefix of cycle time. Completed-case selection, positivity failure, and n=2 cells are named as binding. The four required hypothesis types are present, tests have predicted observations, and the intervention plan refuses the 50% move in favor of clocks → capacity → low-intensity randomization → intensity/spillovers.

**Section 1 — overconfident verdict.** `mainly confounded` at **80** is the largest judgment error. The composition term explains why the overall mean is a bad policy sufficient statistic; it does not establish that the remaining contrast is non-causal. The same section says genuine group effects, residual risk, and selected completion can coexist with the reversal, and that every shared complexity–shift cell has n<3. That evidence supports a lower confidence or `not identifiable from the supplied evidence` with a directional lean. The 80 is rhetorically hedged (“not that Orion has zero causal effect”) rather than calibrated to the positivity failure the author documents.

**Section 2 — DAG vs prose.** Several arrows are fine (routing engine; required→performed; selected skill as mediator; completion as collider of risk and time; suppressed Review–Request Info–Review and backlog feedback). Problems: (1) `resource_group → cycle_time` plus wait/service/rework makes “direct” a residual dumping ground, which the later text warns against but the graph still invites; (2) the table says assignment may affect compliance timing, yet there is no `resource_group → compliance_performed` (or timing); (3) shift is a parent of routing, while the note correctly says actual work shift may be a descendant—both cannot be the working graph; (4) `quality_outcome → []` / `case_completed → []` is not an adjacency list. Supported vs assumed is stated, but too briefly relative to the number of speculative edges (`compliance_required → routing_rule`, `arrival_load → routing_rule`, `cycle_time → quality_outcome`).

**Section 3 — estimands are good; a few roles blur.** Confounder, mediator, collider, selection, and proxy are all explicit. Weaker spots: `queue_wait` as collider is asserted without a concrete opened path; `unobserved_reviewer_skill` as both selected mediator and roster confounder is discussed, but the DAG only encodes the first; `trace` is correctly barred as a baseline covariate, then still used as if it were a clean mechanism proxy.

**Section 4 — numbers check; some “stratified” cells are not.** Recalculated totals, stratum means, wait/service split (ΔW −2.145, ΔS −0.269, ΔR −0.094), variant counts 4/10, 6/3, 1/2, 3/1, shared joint cells = 4 covering 20/30, and “all 17 nights are High load” all match the CSV. Faults: **Day Orion 7.80 vs Nova 14.49** is one Low case versus a mixed Nova day book and should not sit in “adjusted or stratified pattern” without n in-cell. Night 23.05 vs 17.50 is the same Simpson problem they elsewhere catch. The not-required crude (Orion 13.44 vs Nova 11.33) is a second reversal and is dumped in the table without the composition explanation they give everywhere else. Calling the wait share “~85.5% of the arithmetic difference” is correct only because they immediately deny mediation identification; the residual is still slightly over-interpreted as a “physical” remainder.

**Section 5 — one recycled contradiction.** H1–H2–H5–H6 are well posed. H3’s “contradicting” point—that Nova’s crude High-load mean is not higher than Normal—is itself composition-confounded (Normal includes C30 at 36 h). That is a weak falsifier for a load/shift mechanism. H4 leans on compliance-path and rework frequency gaps that are largely the same case-mix story as H1; the within-complexity rework comparison (equal or lower for Orion) is the actual relevant fact and mostly undercuts H4. Ranking is presented as if it were evidential order rather than a convenience sort of coexisting stories.

**Section 6 — operationally sound, slightly over-specified.** Capacity-before-label and a dual-eligible 1:1 pilot with ITT, open-case RMST, and spillover intensity blocks are the right family of interventions. The 15% p90 queue-age trigger and 10% enrollment cap are invented governance numbers presented with more precision than the data can support. That is still better than a 50% reassignment.

**Section 7 — diagnostic intent is right; the PM4Py contract is not clean.** Pandas checks (30 unique rows, flag vs `Compliance Check`, `Request Info` vs `rework_count`, residual, common weights, full complexity×shift grid, n<3 warnings) are excellent. Failures: the prompt’s `parse_event_log_string` path is ignored; `pm4py.get_variants_as_tuples` is not a stable high-level API; PM4Py is not run; the sandbox download link and raw GitHub citation are dead artifacts. Frequency DFGs without timestamps are defensible, but then the code cannot “compare directly-follows relations” beyond counts already available from string splits—so the PM4Py block is partly theater.

**Section 8 — meets the spec.** Five uncertainties, each with one data/design fix, all tied to residual risk, reviewer identity, wait decomposition, inception follow-up, and policy scale. No padding.

**Net.** Causal discipline and arithmetic are well above typical. The score is held down by an overconfident label, a few stratified contrasts that are not comparable, one circular hypothesis test, DAG/prose mismatches, and a process-mining appendix that does not fully do what it claims.
