**6.2**

The answer is unusually strong on process structure, intervention hygiene, and timestamp arithmetic, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has factual errors, overclaims, and internal contradictions that materially affect the causal story.

**What is actually good.** The opening distinction among “set flag := 0,” P1, and component-wise bundle changes is the right object of analysis. The estimand table is well specified: interventional/policy contrasts rather than natural direct/indirect effects, with positivity, exposure-induced confounding, and interference given as reasons. Stage handling is correct in principle (absent stages are not zeros). The Section 3 medians I recomputed from the CSV match the table exactly, including awkward cells such as flag=0 residual 1.00 h, flag=1 cycle 16.45 h, flagged-high-load 18.10 h, and flagged-escalated 21.90 h. The code is complete, does not fabricate missing stages, and does not pretend to identify natural effects.

**Factual errors.** In Section 5 the High-complexity comparison is wrong and cherry-picked. Flagged High cycle times are 16.4, 19.7, 24.1, 25.2, 26.6 h (median **24.1 h**, not “≈21 h”). Unflagged High are R09 18.4, R17 **8.6**, R20 19.2 (median 18.4 h). Citing only R09/R20 to say the gap “largely closes” drops the unflagged High case that did not escalate and understates the flagged High median. The residual gap is about 5–6 h, not “largely” gone; the sensitivity conclusion that unobserved risk “almost” explains the flag contrast is therefore oversold. Separately, “M2 handles all High-complexity reviews” is false: R17 is High and reviewed by M1.

**Graph and mechanism slips.** The intro treats flag as a deterministic function of `risk_score`, but the adjacency list also has `complexity -> flag`. In these data that extra edge is unsupported. More importantly, `risk_score` has no arrows into human review, escalation, or service, even though P1 leaves the score visible. That makes P1 vs P0 poorly represented in the graph. The “adjacency list” is also not an adjacency list; it is annotated prose with grouped tails.

**Logical tension.** Sections 2 and 5 argue that case-level contrasts are ill-defined under shared queues, then Section 6 randomizes **cases** 50/50 onto mandatory vs discretionary review. Blocking by time and a queue-length kill-switch do not stop the two arms from contaminating the same review/senior queues. That is the interference problem the answer itself diagnosed. The same sections disagree on magnitude: Section 5 says the flag’s contribution beyond confounding is small; Section 6 still forecasts large P1/P2 cycle-time gains for flagged cases.

**Overclaim and thin support.** “Unflagged triage waits longest exactly where flagged density is high” is not identified as interference; it is collinear with `arrival_load` and night hours. The local-threshold band is five cases (three unflagged, two flagged). Calling that an RD-style design without a hard support warning is too confident. Overnight calendar time is reserved for reversal condition 4, but several of the headline “queue” medians (R13’s 10 h review wait, several 7–10 h senior waits) are mostly off-shift clock time; Section 3’s causal caveat should have led with that. “Service times differ little” in Section 9 understates a systematic triage-service gap (0.60 vs 0.95 h).

**Format and estimand nits.** An extra H1 and preamble violate “use exactly the following top-level headings.” E3 reverses contrast direction relative to the other rows. E-values are invoked for a median difference in hours. P3 is labeled as isolating wait mediation; added capacity is a policy analogue, not a wait-distribution intervention, and the answer only half-marks that.

The skeleton of a careful process-aware answer is here. The grade is held down by incorrect High-complexity arithmetic, a false resource claim, a graph that contradicts the flag-generating process, and a pilot that ignores the interference theory used to justify the estimands.
