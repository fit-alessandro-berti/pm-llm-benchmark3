**7.6**

The final write-up is a strong compliance analysis: event-time reconstruction is stated correctly, the eleven cases are mostly right, and Sections 4–7 show real grasp of late evidence, moving materiality, and why control-flow conformance fails. It is **not** nearly flawless. Formalization quality is damaged, the checking code misses a stated requirement, and a few rule readings are tighter than the text supports.

### What is solid
State reconstruction is the right model: blanks are no-ops, last write wins under `(timestamp, event_index)`, and later values are not copied backward. The four-clock distinction (event / effective / record / evidence) is the correct way to separate EX-77 from EX-88. Case outcomes that are actually forced by the log are right: TX-301, TX-303, TX-304, TX-307, TX-309 as NONCOMPLIANT; TX-305 as INDETERMINATE; TX-306, TX-308, TX-311 as COMPLIANT; TX-302 as COMPLIANT because the Enhanced_Review independently satisfies R1. Flip conditions in Section 4 are specific enough to be useful. Section 6 actually names distinct failure modes rather than restating “data matters.”

### Formalization is weaker than it claims
Section 2 is a required scoring surface, and the pseudo-logic is partly unreadable. Comparison and quantifier symbols are dropped, so the formulas collapse into strings such as `val(c,jur,rel(c))DE`, `errel(c)`, and `eff(x)t(rel(c))`. A reader cannot check polarity or temporal direction from the formulas alone. The table in prose mostly saves the section, but “formalization quality” cannot be scored highly when the formal object is corrupted.

R1’s “latest material data change” is also narrowed without saying so in the formal system. The write-up treats only `>20%` amount increases (plus jur/type/risk writes) as material. That is a reasonable reconciliation with R4 and with TX-306’s note, but it is an interpretation, not what R1 literally says. A strict reading in which *any* post-review amount write resets the R1 clock would flip TX-306. Section 4 mentions that alternative; the formal rules do not.

### TX-310 is argued well and still over-decided
R1 is triggered and there is no Enhanced_Review, so the case turns entirely on EX-88. Certificate creation at 10:30 after release, a backdated display, and an interested witness are enough to **refuse** the exemption. They are not enough to make NONCOMPLIANT the only honest label. The prompt itself says there is no canonical answer under contradictory evidence. INDETERMINATE (or a primary label of REVIEW_REQUIRED with NONCOMPLIANT as the audit-burden reading) would have matched that instruction more closely. Medium confidence and a clear flip condition reduce the error; they do not erase it.

### Code misses a required tracker and is only coarsely faithful
The block does the important things: `StringIO`, timestamp parse, `event_index` order, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, forward-fill, release-time snapshot, R1–R4 flags, exemption join, unknown-amount flag, no case→label lookup. That is better than a narrative-only answer.

It does **not** track the most recent `Sanctions_List_Update` as a first-class event the way it tracks the other five named activities. That was an explicit requirement. R3 is computed from SLU rows, but the diagnostics object never exposes `last_sanctions_list_update`. Other nits: `np.nanmax(... + [np.nan])` is sloppy; the DECLARE-style check evaluates conditions on **final** case state, which demonstrates leakage but is not a fair control-flow baseline; pm4py is optionalized behind `try/except`, so the required call is not guaranteed.

### Smaller unclarities
- TX-308 is correct that 100,000 meets “at least,” but the table does not say what would happen if the decrease had crossed below the threshold.
- Severity/confidence are asserted, not defined.
- JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION is never used as a primary label. That is defensible for TX-302; it still leaves the allowed four-way scheme under-exercised.

Net: excellent temporal case work with a broken formal object, one over-committed contested case, and a checking script that is close to the spec but not on it. That is clearly above competent and clearly below near-flawless.
