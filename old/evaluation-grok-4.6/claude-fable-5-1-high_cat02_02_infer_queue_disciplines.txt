7.8

The answer is a genuine decision-set reconstruction, not a start-order narrative, and it treats observational equivalence, idle time, and data artefacts as first-class evidence. That is the right method. It is not nearly flawless. Several local claims overreach or contradict the same document’s own tables.

**What is actually strong.** Eligible sets are rebuilt at starts, preemptions, and idles; delayed scans are handled as a one-sided error (late, never early); remaining work is defined from `est_min`, not realized time; and competing rules are scored only where they disagree. QA’s 08:20 / 09:05 / 09:32 triple is the cleanest identification in the log (appointment at the exact minute, then FIFO, with p1 and SPT both losing). QB’s two-class reading is earned by 09:31 and 10:14 (resume p3 over p2/SPT) plus 10:00 (p1 over resume and over shorter B04), not by the 09:16 preemption alone. The cross-queue p1 scorecard (bypasses vs. the three cases where priority and FIFO split) is the right way to kill a “documented priority” story. Realized duration = `est_min` on all 32 jobs is an important data-quality finding and is used to cap SPT/SEPT claims. Exceptions in §5 are ranked, and each one says how the labels would move.

**Factual and counting errors.** QA is said to have six discriminating decisions and FIFO 5/6; the QA table has five splits (08:20, 08:32, 09:05, 09:20, 09:32). 08:02 and 08:58 do not discriminate. That off-by-one is repeated in the confidence paragraph. QB’s identifiability cell says p2 vs p3 “cannot” be ranked and cites “the one clean test” at 10:28; §2 already has 09:31 and 10:14 as additional p2 losses. That is an internal contradiction, not residual ambiguity. “Priorities and deadlines are perfectly correlated in QC” is false as stated: class order tracks EDD, but C03 (p2, 12:30) vs C07 (p2, 12:00) is an EDD distinction priority cannot make. “Two of two endangered-but-feasible deadlines that required a non-SPT choice were lost” is not supported: C07 is the one clear feasible-and-sacrificed case; C05 is infeasible from 10:11+40 against a 10:50 deadline (the write-up says so elsewhere).

**Weaker inference than the prose implies.** QD’s primary label “skill-based routing” names a hard eligibility filter that is true by construction of the assignments; the tested sequencing rule is FIFO inside that filter (11:25 D04 over D08, 11:48 D07 over D10). The hybrid column is more accurate than the primary cell. QA confidence 80 sits badly next to the same row’s caveat that unreadiness of A04/A08 would collapse several FIFO wins into ties. The §1 claim that early appointment tolerance is unobservable is later contradicted by 09:05 (A06 not started 10 min early; A07 not packed before 09:15). Test 1’s SLA-slack alternative is not a coherent full-policy rival: a p1 15-minute wait limit does not predict B02’s preemption at +2 min, and adding “p1 always preempts” recreates the 09:48 failure the test is meant to explain. The 95–100 min age cap for C01 is a one-point fit (C06 still wins at 94 min of C01 wait) and should not be presented in §4 as a stable predictor.

**Omissions that matter for this log.** Preemptive priority in QA is already falsified by non-preemption (A04 arrives 08:13 during A01; A08 arrives 08:40 during A02); that is not used. The APPT column silently assumes a 10-minute lookahead. The pandas script does not reproduce the idle decisions it relies on (disclosed, but then described as reproducing “every row”). None of these sink the diagnoses, but they are exactly the places a strict reading should have been tighter.

**Format and uncertainty handling.** Headings match the spec; confidence tracks discriminating, confounded tests rather than rhetoric; fitted thresholds are usually flagged. QC at 35 is the honest number. The dangerous-inference conclusion (priority, then EDD-from-the-C05-note) follows from the evidence.

Net: methodologically the right paper, with enough local overclaim, self-contradiction, and unforced counting error that it is not a 9. The remaining 7.8 is for decision-set competence and for not converting FIFO violations into priority by default.
