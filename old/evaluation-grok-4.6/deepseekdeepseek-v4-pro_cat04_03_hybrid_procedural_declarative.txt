**5.5**

The write-up is complete and conceptually stronger than it is correct. It hits the required ten-section shape, treats R5/R6 as a real conflict, and is right that activity-only models cannot recover SoD or off-log emergency authorization. Under a strict reading, that is not enough. The procedural model is not executable as written, is over-constrained relative to the stated policy, and is inconsistent with the author’s own test matrix.

**What is actually good.** Two R5/R6 resolutions are stated, one is chosen, and the compliance risk is named. R3 as alternate response (not chain response) is the right DECLARE template. T4/T5/T8 are judged in the right layer. Temporal means are converted correctly and correctly treated as warnings (`zeta = 2.0`). Section 9’s points on vacuity, missing attributes, and fake `support`/`confidence` are on target.

**Fatal internal inconsistency.** The high-risk branch is

`EDD → Compliance Approval → Obtain Legal Opinion → Activate Vendor`.

Legal is therefore mandatory on *every* high-risk case, and it is forced *after* compliance. That is not R4/R5. R4/R5 bind legal opinion to *Sanctions Escalation*, not to the high-risk route. Consequences:

- **T2** (canonical high-risk accept, no escalation, no legal) **cannot replay** on the tree, but is scored procedural `pass`.
- **T7** has `EDD → Legal → Compliance → Activate`, which also **cannot replay** on that sequence, but is scored procedural `pass`.

A model that rejects its own happy path, then a verdict table that does not match the model, is a first-order error.

**The PM4Py artifacts would not run.** `Operator.AND` is not a PM4Py operator (`PARALLEL` is). `Operator.LOOP` is not a unary node; PM4Py loops are `*(do, redo, exit)`. `alt_response` is not the listed template name (`altresponse`). `absence` with `n: 2` is not in the allowed DECLARE API (`absence` means never; “at most once” belongs in `activ_freq` or is simply not a DECLARE primitive here). Section 3’s “compact formal description” is also garbled (````` ` fragments instead of operators).

**Log skeleton is sloppy in ways the author should have caught.** `activ_freq["Verify Bank Account"] = {1}` (and `{0,1}` for Change/Suspend) contradicts both R11 and the author’s own post-activation loop. Several `never_together` pairs are extra-legal (`Reject`+`Obtain Legal Opinion`, `Sanctions Escalation`+`Reject`, `Manager Approval`+`Obtain Legal Opinion`). The single `always_after` pair `("Close Onboarding", "Receive Onboarding Request")` is almost certainly the wrong orientation for PM4Py. R11’s “Suspend after Change” is not even backed by a DECLARE `response`, so it only exists inside a loop that cannot see post-payment changes.

**Smaller spec misses that still count.** Heading 1 is not the required title. T6’s overall cell is `pass+warn`, which is not in the allowed vocabulary (`policy-dependent` was the obvious choice). `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` is never used. Process-tree approximations (escalation only after classify and only on the high-risk branch; no clarification after the XOR; bank-change only before first payment) are under-disclosed.

The architecture essay is a 7–8. The executable models and the T1–T8 matrix are not. Strictly, that is a mid-5.
