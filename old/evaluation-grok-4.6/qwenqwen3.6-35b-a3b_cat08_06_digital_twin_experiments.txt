**5.0**

The write-up has the required eight-section shape and touches the right themes (non-stationary arrivals, calendars, latent objections, cross-case batching, no optimality claim). Under a strict reading it is not close to a reliable digital-twin design: several hard constraints are missed, and a few choices are simulation-methodologically wrong.

**What is actually sound.** Boundary and entities are named. Objections are treated as unobserved and proxied, not as logged events. Applicant delay is separated from municipal service time in the prose. D1–D10 plus three stresses appear. Uncertainty language (replications, bootstrap tails, Morris/Sobol) is in the right neighborhood. The skeleton is modular and avoids the nonexistent `pm4py.read_csv`.

**Process-mining / PM4Py (major).** The code is not faithful to PM4Py conventions. `pm4py.read_pandas`, `pm4py.variants`, `pm4py.directed_graph`, and `pm4py.performance_analysis` are not the public APIs; the usual calls are a formatted DataFrame or `convert_to_event_log`, `get_variants`, `discover_dfg`, `discover_performance_dfg`, and `get_case_arrival_average`. Section 2 also invents `pm4py.loop_mining` and a vague `performance_analysis`. The required service/waiting columns are never computed, even as inter-event proxies with an explicit start-timestamp caveat. `parse_process_tree` is declined in favor of a custom parser that cannot match `*(`, does not split `+(` / `X(` children, and will not recover the given tree. Arrival rate in cases/second is the wrong unit and assumes `log` is still a DataFrame after a fictional conversion.

**Simulation design (major).** A 30-day (or 10%) warm-up is far too short when complex-variance p90 is ~260 days and public notice is a multi-week timer; initialization bias will dominate the tails the decision criteria care about. Empty-system start vs. a loaded snapshot is not discussed. Means still get t-intervals despite acknowledged heavy tails. There are no common random numbers for intervention contrasts. Resource weekday constraints that drive the process (fire Tue–Thu, inspectors Tue–Fri, legal council weeks, Monday clerk reservation) are stated in text and then dropped from `ResourceCalendar`. Cross-case effects that the prompt insisted on—architect same-week bursts, geographically bundled tours, objections that hit several related cases—remain comments and a per-case latent flag, not shared state.

**Experiments, validation, criteria.** The matrix is a list of one-factor stories, not a design with held-fixed factors, interaction structure, or how D10 is identified versus components. D2 “quality risk” is not an outcome the model can produce. A last-3-month holdout is confounded by the same seasonality the twin is supposed to represent. Face/replay/utilization checks are named, but utilization is to be checked against the same HR calendars used as inputs (circular), and variant-share ±5% is an unrealistic replay target. SLA cutoffs are inconsistent with the given empirical medians/p90s (several types would fail baseline by construction) without saying so.

**Assumptions / estimation.** Flags are treated as if they fully determine fire/environment/notice routing, while the brief says area and environmental sensitivity are incomplete. “HR calendars are accurate” ignores that they were reconstructed, not mined. Flattened-log bias on dependencies is noted once and not turned into an estimation or identification strategy.

In short: a competent outline with the right headings, undermined by invented PM4Py, a missing required timing split, an indefensible warm-up, and cross-case/calendar mechanisms that are described but not actually specified as a simulable system. That is mid-pack work, not a high score under a strict rubric.
