**7.4**

This is a strong, process-aware design that correctly refuses the convenient 21,600-row complete-case story and does not recode rejects, withdrawals, or open files as non-default. It is not nearly flawless. Internal contradictions, a broken validation detail, and a skeleton that fails its own hardest safeguards are enough to keep it well below the top of the scale.

## What is actually good

The primary estimand is the right kind of object: activation-conditional 90-day default, with \(Y\) undefined when the file is never booked. Horizon is correctly anchored at `Account Activated`, not application start. Alternatives A and B are real questions, not decorative variants, and the informal “call rejects non-default” target is treated as a biased negative control. The cohort table closes on 48,000 and separates competing terminals, immature activations, linked vs unlinked transfers, and the matured export. The five missingness mechanisms in §3 are not collapsed into one “censoring” bucket. The leakage table covers every required field and several extras (`label_available_at` as a feature, extraction-time tenure, full-log DFG/variants, random `split_train_test`). Random case splits are rejected for the right reasons (policy era, migration, maturity selection). The decision-use boundary in §8 is honest: association on the selected booked set is not counterfactual default risk.

Those are the reasons this is not a 4–6.

## Design flaws that are more than nits

**The §1 estimand is not the §3 analysis.** Section 1 writes
\(\pi(x_\tau)=\Pr(Y=1\mid X_\tau,\text{activated},\text{label identifiable})\).
That is a maturity-and-feed-selected functional. Section 3 then says the primary fit is the full 25,200 activated cases with a censored/survival likelihood, precisely so one does *not* condition on identifiability. Those are different targets. Partner MNAR feeds and delayed labels are the reason the distinction matters; the write-up both emphasizes that point and then blurs it in the official estimand.

**The temporal split cannot support the model they say they will fit.** Train is starts through 31 Mar 2025; validation is Apr–Jun 2025. Both are pre–1 July. They then say to “fit with a start-era indicator and interactions (risk score × post-July, channel × post-July).” In the primary split that dummy is identically zero in train and validation, so the interactions are not identified. Walk-forward is mentioned but not specified as a retrain-after-July protocol. Test-A is a clean *shift* check; it is not a place where those interactions can be learned.

**Label-maturity arithmetic is sloppy.** The 31 Aug 2025 train cutoff is described as a “125-day buffer after a late-March *activation* window.” Activation is not start. Document loops can push \(T_{\text{act}}\) months after start; those files are either unlabeled in train (the conservative reading) or illegally labeled from later feeds (the code’s reading). Test-A runs through 14 Oct 2025; \(14\text{ Oct}+90+35\) is after the 15 Feb 2026 extract, so the last weeks of Test-A cannot meet the paper’s own 125-day rule even under instantaneous activation. Test-B is warned; Test-A is not.

**§3.1 still conflates two things the rest of the memo keeps apart.** “Ordinary right censoring” is defined to include still-open, never-activated files, then immediately said to be a setting where default is *undefined*. Undefined is not censored. The later text knows this; the taxonomy at the top of §3 does not.

**Minor conceptual looseness.** “5.6% of all starts” appears next to the default count and is only a descriptive ratio if non-activated cases are treated as non-events in the denominator — the move the rest of the answer forbids. Combining event-count and elapsed-time clocks is asserted (“used in parallel”) without saying whether that is six models, a joint likelihood, or a deployment rule.

## Skeleton: right APIs, wrong safeguards

The code shape is appropriate: `read_csv` + timestamp parse, `pm4py.format_dataframe`, case-level status as of a cutoff, start-time split, `get_prefixes_from_log` on a terminal-stripped log, DFG/variants on *prefixes*, no fabricated survival API, `split_train_test` only as a commented anti-pattern. That meets the letter of §9.

It does not meet the memo’s own standard.

1. **`assert not set(LEAKY_COLS) & set(pref.columns) or True` is not an audit.** `or True` makes the assertion a no-op. After `format_dataframe`, leaky columns are still present on prefix rows; the honest fix is to drop them and *then* assert. Silencing the check is the opposite of a leakage audit.

2. **Maturity is computed only at the global extract.** Section 7 says training labels must be identifiable by the *training* cutoff, not by 15 Feb 2026. `case_status(..., cutoff=EXTRACT)` uses the extract for every fold. That is exactly the future-feed leakage the prose forbids.

3. **`primary` is the 21,600 complete cases.** The comment says immature activations remain censored; the next line keeps `mature` only. Survival is deferred to a comment pointing at lifelines/sksurv. Fine as a stub *if* the design had not already named the 25,200 censored analysis as primary.

4. **Maturity logic can accept \(Y=0\) too early.** If `label_available_at <= cutoff`, the case is mature even when \(T_{\text{act}}+90\text{d}\) has not passed. That is valid for an early default; it is not valid for a premature non-default. The conservative 125-day rule from the text is only the second disjunct, and that disjunct also requires `len(lab)>0`, so a permanently missing partner feed is not recoded as 0 — good — but the first disjunct can still leak an early warehouse 0.

5. **Last-event `risk_score` is not last-as-of-\(\tau\) `risk_score`.** Section 4/5 specify the last score on any retained event. The skeleton takes `tail(1)` of the prefix, so a missing score on event \(k\) drops a valid earlier update.

6. **Elapsed-time prefixes, fold-specific cutoffs, and transfer linkage are comments, not code.** Allowed to be incomplete; less acceptable when those are the mechanisms that distinguish this design from a naive export dump. `{k: len(v) for k, v in variants.items() if False}` is dead code.

7. **Terminal classification is order-sensitive and coarse.** `next(... if a in acts)` on a set will, for a transferred-then-linked activation, depend on list order, not time. Accepted-but-not-activated files fall into `OPEN`, which may or may not match the 5,100 figure.

## Strict scoring

A 9–10 would need a single consistent estimand, a split in which every advertised coefficient is actually identified, maturity rules that match the 90+35-day clock on both the page and in code, and a skeleton that implements as-of labeling and a real leakage assert. This answer is the right *kind* of study and is still full of places where the careful prose and the operational plan disagree. That is why it stops in the mid-7s rather than the 8s.
