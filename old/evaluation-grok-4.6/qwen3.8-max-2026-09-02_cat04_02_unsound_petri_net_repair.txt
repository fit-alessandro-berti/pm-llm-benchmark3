It is not near-flawless. Several issues keep it well below a top score.

**R2 is a weaker alternative than claimed.** Adding `p_analysis → t_waive_review` does not make analysis optional on the waiver path. It makes analysis and waiver mutually exclusive: waiver consumes the analysis token, and if `t_analyze` fires first, waiver is impossible. That is a larger policy change than “optional analysis under waiver,” and it is a poor fallback if mandatory analysis is non-negotiable. A second intent-preserving repair (rewire the waiver outcome into the existing AND-join, or give `t_join_waived` a proper two-input preset while keeping analysis mandatory) was available and would have been a better contrast. The “Preserved intent” cell for R2 is also wrong: “the net becomes sound” is not preserved intent.

**Terminology is loose in a few places.** Terminal markings `{sink:1, p_analysis:1}` and `{sink:1, p_analysis_done:1}` are improper completion (easy soundness), not a classical deadlock before the sink. Section 6 then says the repaired reachability graph “should not contain reachable proper-completion states with leftover analysis tokens,” which is self-contradictory. In Section 2 the “Potential symptom” column is used for non-symptoms (“Correctly creates parallel branches”).

**Smaller nits.** The edit-cost model is explicit and the cost-1 tie is justified, but behavioral distance is not separated from syntactic cost, so R1 and R2 look equally “minimal” only because the metric ignores policy damage. Safeness and transition liveness are not discussed; they happen not to be the defect, but a formal soundness write-up should say so. The diagnostic helper reimplements firing by hand instead of using the library semantics object; that is allowed, but it is extra surface for error.
