**3.2**

This answer looks structurally complete and casually fluent, but it fails the core causal and empirical tests of the task. The decisive pattern in the data is a crude-versus-stratified reversal: Orion is slower overall (21.96 vs 15.24) and faster in every complexity stratum (Low 7.50 vs 9.64, Medium 17.17 vs 19.38, High 31.57 vs 35.00). A competent analysis must lock that inequality in place and then refuse to treat stratification as identification. This write-up repeatedly loses the inequality, invents counts that contradict both the case file and the supplied aggregates, and then builds policy on the inverted reading.

**Section 1 is disqualifying.** The “strongest evidence for” the verdict states that Orion’s within-stratum means *exceed* Nova’s, then immediately cites High 31.6 vs 35 — the opposite comparison. That is not a wording slip. It is a failure to read the table that the prompt already computed. Complexity is then called a *mediator* of a resource-group effect. In the process as specified, complexity is assigned at Classify and then used by the routing engine; it is a confounder (and likely an effect modifier), not a mediator of `resource_group → cycle_time`. The 50% reassignment discussion therefore runs the wrong way: if Orion is faster after holding complexity fixed, moving Orion’s hard cases to Nova is the scenario most likely to *raise* cycle time, especially at night where Nova has almost no support. Confidence 65 is also too high once positivity, selection on completed cases, and unobserved risk are admitted.

**Section 2 is a thin, incomplete DAG.** Several arrows are reasonable (`complexity → routing_rule → resource_group`, `compliance_required → compliance_performed`). The graph still omits the arrows that actually explain the reversal: `complexity → review_service`, `complexity → queue_wait` / `rework`, `shift → queue_wait`, `compliance_performed → queue_wait` (the prompt says wait includes compliance waiting), `unobserved_case_risk → quality_outcome`, and `case_completed` as a selection node. `unobserved_reviewer_skill` is in the allowed set and then left out of the adjacency list. Calling `resource_group → review_service` a “supported fact” overstates the evidence; that arrow is a hypothesis. The one-sentence feedback remark is fine; it does not rescue the missing confounding paths.

**Section 3 mis-labels roles.** Complexity as mediator is the same structural error. “Complexity may be unobserved in some cases” is false; it is fully observed. Quality outcome is not the selection variable; `case_completed` is. Shift-as-collider is asserted without a coherent collider triple. The prompt required an explicit confounder, mediator, collider/selection variable, *and* proxy. The table scatters those words around but does not show that the author can keep them distinct relative to a defined estimand (total vs direct vs mediated effect of assignment to Orion).

**Section 4 contains fabricated statistics.** This is the most serious integrity failure after the reversal error.

- Orion day is *one* case (C02, 7.8h), not N=3 with mean 22.9h. Orion night is 13 cases with mean ≈23.05h, not N=11 / 20.2h.
- Orion compliance-required mean is ≈26.7h, not 19.5h; non-compliance ≈13.4h, not 12.5h. High Orion cycles are 29.5–33.5, not an “18.2–31.6h spread.”
- Rework is not “0 for 14/14 Orion.” The prompt already states Orion rework 28.6% (C06, C10, C12, C14). Nova is 3/16, not 6/16.
- Appeals are 1/14 vs 1/16 (C14, C24), not “5/14 vs 3/16.”
- Low-Nova wait/service figures are sloppy relative to the actual means (5.06 and ~3.64).

Section 4’s stratified cycle times are in the right direction, which only makes Section 1 and Section 5 look more careless. Day-versus-night is not actually compared across groups; Orion-internal day/night is invented. “Causal interpretation allowed” repeatedly treats descriptive hints as if they license mechanism claims.

**Section 5 does not meet the hypothesis contract cleanly.** There is no ranked, coherent “Orion is genuinely harmful” hypothesis. H1 is case-mix confounding, then immediately contradicts itself by calling 31.6 “elevated” relative to 35.0 and by claiming Orion is faster only in Low and slower in Medium/High — false in both medium and high. H2’s “Orion rework = 0” and “fewer high-complexity appeals” are false (high Nova appeals 0/2; high Orion 1/6). Nova night is four cases, not one. Methods are named, but several predicted observations inherit the inverted contrast, so they would not discriminate what the author thinks they discriminate. Matching on complexity should *reproduce* Orion’s within-stratum advantage if the already-computed strata are right; the write-up expects the gap to vanish as if the residual Orion effect were harmful.

**Section 6 has the right skeleton and the wrong first cut.** A small routing pilot plus a compliance-capacity phase satisfies the formal checklist. Phase 1 still proposes moving medium/high Orion work onto Nova, which is the low-overlap, high-risk direction. Phase 2 (“no night Orion high-complexity cases remain”) is not a careful quasi-experiment; it is a large operational rewrite with almost no historical Nova night-high support (one high-Nova night case). The recommendation therefore “learns” by first testing the intervention most likely to backfire. A safer design would randomize only in the overlap region (especially Low/Medium where both groups exist), or add night/compliance *capacity* before changing labels.

**Section 7 fails explicit requirements.** There is no complexity-standardized contrast and no stated target weighting distribution. Overlap code is broken (`unstack` on a 3-key groupby; `counts[col, col]`). Trace-variant and DFG comparisons are not implemented. PM4Py is commented out and the sketched API (`csv_importer`, `dataframe_visualizer`) is not the specified `parse_event_log_string` / `discover_dfg` path. The embedded CSV silently recodes C15 from Night/High to Day/Normal, so even a correct script would not reproduce the prompt. Printing “Process mining trace inspection complete” after doing nothing is cosmetic.

**Section 8 is the least damaged part** — five real uncertainties, each paired with a data need — but it is generic and does not name the highest-value missing fields first (reviewer id, event timestamps, open-case status, the actual routing-rule log, queue length at assignment).

**Net.** Required headings are present, and the author knows the vocabulary of confounding, pilots, and selection. That is not enough. The answer misstates the central contrast, fabricates rework/appeal/shift numbers that the prompt already refutes, treats a pre-treatment case-mix variable as a mediator, omits the required standardized estimand and PM4Py diagnostics, and recommends moving hard Orion work to Nova. Under a strict rubric this is well below a competent analyst memo.
