**7.0**

This is a strong, well-structured answer that hits the intended evaluation targets: crude-versus-stratified reversal, refusal to treat stratification as identification, queueing versus service, group as a routing/skill/shift bundle, tests with predicted observations, and a safer learn-then-scale intervention. It is not close to flawless under strict causal and process-mining standards.

**Verdict and identification.** `mainly confounded` at 70/100 is too confident and slightly misaligned with the rest of the memo. The crude Orion penalty is almost certainly mix-driven, but remaining identification is poor (empty/tiny cells, shift–group entanglement, completed-case selection). That combination is closer to `not identifiable from the supplied evidence`, or `partly causal through mechanisms` with low confidence. Section 1 also never cleanly separates a **total** effect of assignment, a **direct** effect holding mediators fixed, and effects **mediated** by queue, compliance wait, service, and rework, even though that distinction was required.

**Bias direction is mishandled.** If Orion is preferentially given harder, night, high-load, compliance-heavy work, omitted severity and operating conditions should make Orion look *worse*. Orion is still faster in every complexity stratum, with lower wait and slightly lower service. That pattern is more consistent with a beneficial (or at least non-harmful) group/skill effect than with “mainly confounding and the stratified gap could easily flip.” The memo treats unobserved risk as generic uncertainty that “could overturn” the strata, without stating this directional implication.

**A causal slip in the hypothesis table.** Rank 3 cites Orion-night 23.05h versus Nova-night 17.50h as support for a harmful Orion night effect. That contrast is the same case-mix problem the rest of the answer exists to reject (Orion night is mostly medium/high; Nova night includes lows). Using it as supporting evidence is inconsistent with the paper’s own discipline. Rank 1’s predicted result (“near 0 or negative”) is also too elastic to falsify.

**Positivity and mix are under-specified in the narrative.** The text flags High-Day Orion = 0 and Orion-Day n = 1, but not Medium-Day Orion = 0, High-Day Nova n = 1, or High-Night Nova n = 1. Arrival load is in the DAG and hypotheses but never decomposed in Section 4, despite being listed as a required substantive factor.

**Process mining is coded, not interpreted.** Both groups share the same four trace variants; DFG differences are frequency/mix, not control-flow. That is the main process-mining finding and it never appears in Sections 1–6. PM4Py is also not used “consistently”: a homemade DFG is primary, PM4Py is a `try/except` add-on.

**DAG and residual nits.** Treating `shift` only as a parent of routing (not also as a possible *consequence* of assigning night-heavy Orion) is an incomplete treatment of the intervention. There is no `shift → review_service` path despite the fatigue hypothesis. Section 4’s Medium wait 11.13 versus the supplied 11.12 is harmless; unused `itertools` is harmless. The 50% reassignment discussion, phased plan, and residual-data list are the best parts of the memo and are not the reason for the score.
