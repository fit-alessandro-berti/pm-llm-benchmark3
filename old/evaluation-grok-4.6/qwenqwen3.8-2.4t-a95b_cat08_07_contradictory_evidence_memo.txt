**7.8**

The final memo is a strong, format-compliant strategic synthesis. It chooses Alternative E without pretending any option wins on all metrics, refuses national scale while tail/rework/activation issues remain open, and refuses a full rollback because simple-house gains are real. Facts, interpretations, and assumptions are separated cleanly. The five metric conflicts are explained with the actual process-mining artifacts (fitness/precision/generalization, alignments, DFG shifts, temporal profiles, resource/social networks, and segment tables). The recommendation is implementable: pause the two high-harm mechanisms, keep the two high-benefit ones in a narrow segment, and gate expansion.

It is not near-flawless.

The value-of-information ranking is the main analytical flaw. M7 is treated as the highest-value next measurement, but once E is chosen and billing-before-activation plus permit checks are already suspended, M7 is mostly a control dashboard, not the information that would most change the remaining strategic bets (can skip be repaired, is full cost actually worse, is single-coordinator ownership resilient). Those are M6/M1, M4, and M5. Ranking a no-regret monitor first inflates operational hygiene into decision value.

There is also a mild inconsistency in the action plan. The memo flags 93% utilization, absence stalls, and centralized working-together networks as material risks, then immediately scales coordinator ownership for simple houses while putting M5 fifth. Backup and an invented “about 85%” utilization cap are offered as substitutes for evidence. That 85% cutoff, like “stalls are rare,” is stipulated rather than derived.

Smaller defects: DFG arrows are missing in F8; a few fact rows compress several observations; “accountability improved only where the coordinator was available” is slightly stronger than the log supports; some Section 6 gates are precise (0% billing-before-activation) while others are vague (“improves materially,” “rare”). None of these sink the memo. They keep it clearly below a 9.
