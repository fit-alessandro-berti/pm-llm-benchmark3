6.4

The write-up is a real decision-set analysis rather than a label-only pass, and that is its main strength. QA and much of QB are reconstructed from eligible waiting sets, competing rules are compared instead of being collapsed into “not FIFO therefore priority,” and the later sections on discrimination tests, exceptions, and data collection are in the right shape. Under a strict reading, though, several factual and logical mistakes are large enough to move the diagnosis, not just the commentary.

**QD skill eligibility is internally inconsistent, and the QD diagnosis leans on that error.** D05 is correctly treated as a conjunction (`firmware+electrical` → only D-F). The same reading of `+` makes D07 (`mechanical+electrical`) eligible only for D-X, who is the only resource with both skills. The answer nevertheless puts D07 in D-M’s eligible set at 11:25 and 11:35, then treats D08-before-D07 as a FIFO violation and as QD’s strongest contradicting episode. If D07 is ineligible for D-M, D-M’s sequence D01→D04→D08→D09 is ordinary per-resource FIFO: D08 is the oldest remaining mechanical job at 11:35, and priority/SPT are observationally equivalent rather than decisive. That also inflates the claimed “soft intra-skill priority” component. The same section never treats the better contradiction: at 11:36 D-F is free until the 12:00 meeting with a 24-minute window, D10 is a 12-minute P1 electrical job, and D-F idles instead of taking it.

**QC is over-diagnosed as SEPT, including in a way that contradicts the author’s own table.** At 10:32 the table correctly says SEPT would start C06 (`est_min` 6) and the observed choice is C04 (22). That is a live, non-urgent backlog decision, i.e. exactly the regime called “SEPT on non-urgent backlog.” The narrative then says early decisions from 10:10–10:32 “align strongly with SEPT.” They do not. A more careful primary would be a frozen/short-job ranking of the opening C01–C04 set (no reinsertion when C06 arrives), then an SLA grab of C05, then mixed leftovers. Confidence 70 is too high relative to QB (65), whose hybrid is at least named in the interruption fields. C01-before-C07 is a real contradiction, but it is not the first one, and calling the opening batch “exactly as SEPT would predict” oversells a pattern that already breaks at the first new arrival.

**QA is mostly right, but the diagnosis table is sloppy.** Non-preemptive near-FIFO plus a due appointment (A03) is the best reading; A02-before-A04 is the right smoking gun against priority and SPT. Two problems remain. First, A03 is listed as the strongest *contradicting* episode even though the proposed primary is already FIFO-with-appointment-overlay; A03 supports the hybrid rather than challenging it, and there is no strong residual violation once that overlay is allowed. Second, the supporting cell bundles 08:30 and 09:05 into one job list that includes A07 and A08 as if they were waiting when A02 was chosen; A07 arrives 08:35 and A08 arrives 08:40. A06 is also weakly used as appointment evidence even though FIFO alone selects it at 09:20.

**Scan delay, estimates, and missed discriminating starts.** The 2-minute scan lag is stated as enlarging waiting sets, then applied unevenly (C05 is considered at 10:10; A05 is not considered at 08:20). More importantly, every `est_min` in the log equals realized service time. QC’s identifiability problem is still real (deadlines, priority, and size can co-move), but “estimate noise” is not an observation in these data; SEPT and shortest *realized* time are indistinguishable here. Several high-value starts are skipped in favor of weak ones: QB at 10:28 (B04 vs P2 B06 vs short/appointment B07) is a cleaner FIFO test than several included rows; QD at 11:48 (D07 vs P1/short D10) is a cleaner intra-resource FIFO-vs-priority test than 11:01 or 12:16.

**QB is generally careful, with one overclaim.** B02’s preemption and B07’s ignored appointment are used well, and 09:48 is correctly flagged as matching neither priority nor SPT. “P1 jobs consistently override FIFO” is too strong: at 09:48 B05 is already in the recorded arrival set and B03 is started instead. The compressed “09:31 and 10:14 … (B03, B06)” cell in the summary table also invites the false reading that B03 is still waiting at 10:14.

**Section 7 mis-ranks identifiability.** QA is the most identifiable queueing discipline: repeated decision sets reject priority and SPT, non-preemption is clear, and the only clean exception is the due appointment A03. QD’s skill mapping is partly a hard eligibility constraint, not a recovered discretionary rule, and it is muddied by idle gaps and the D07 error. QC, not QB, is the least stable object: the author’s own competing predictions change winner across adjacent decisions. The warning not to infer a *formal* priority/deadline policy is still the right danger call.

**What remains good.** Competing-rule tables for multiple timestamps, refusal to treat every FIFO break as priority, explicit preemptive vs non-preemptive branching, appointment-as-overlay rather than appointment-as-primary in QA, the D-X idle as reservation vs hidden work, and a collection plan that is actually tied to those ambiguities. Those are why this is not a low score. The grade is held down because the QD contradiction, the QC primary label, and several “strongest episode” cells would not survive a close audit of eligibility and of the author’s own counter-predictions.
