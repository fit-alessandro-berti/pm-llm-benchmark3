6.7

The final write-up follows the required seven-section structure, respects the main constraints (not every deviation is bad; throughput without controls is not resilience; the T4 audit tail is not real-time compliance), and is directionally right on A–L. It is not close to flawless.

**What is solid.** Section 1 uses the T0–T4 metrics and conformance drop (0.96 → 0.71 → 0.82 → 0.90) to mark a real before/during/after shift. The A–L table mostly maps intent, segment, and keep/modify/retire in a way that matches the case: C and G as the dangerous shortcuts, B/F as control-preserving, I/J as capacity tools that need explicit rules, K/L as recovery work rather than operational success. Section 3 correctly separates mission-preserving adaptation from undeclared control loss and refuses to equate Invoice close → Audit remediation with in-process compliance. Section 7 is decision-usable.

**Material analytical gaps.** The DFGs are cited more as labels than as evidence. T2 volumes that should drive the argument are barely used: skip-authorization 1,600; dispatch-without-scan 1,800 + 1,450; phone PoD 2,600; spreadsheet invoice hold 2,200; temporary release only 1,650 of 3,600 packs. That last point is a real tension with F: the narrative says controlled orders were checked, yet dual-control missing rose from 0.2% to 2.9% and most packs never hit temporary release. The answer does not reconcile those facts. Stabilization likewise leaves 2,300 audit-remediation cases and a higher compliance-hold rate uninterpreted. Hospital fill “remained 91%” is treated as proof of A without a baseline fill rate.

**Logical and column errors.** “Keep/modify” is not a legal value in a keep / modify / retire column; C and G are labeled “retire except…” which is modify. Section 3 then treats a *redesigned* fast lane (with waiver rules) as if it were the observed pattern A, which sometimes skipped authorization. That blurs diagnosis and prescription.

**Redesign weaknesses.** Section 4 invents 15-minute / 30-minute / 5% / 24–72h–7d thresholds with unearned precision and never marks them as policy choices. T3 (intake up, inventory/scan unreliable) is not a designed operating mode. Spreadsheet invoice hold is never replaced with an explicit “no high-risk invoice without evidence” rule. Phone/ePoD remains acceptable for hospital-critical ambient. Exit criteria for returning to the normative path are missing. Section 5’s “outside resilient operation” line is internally inconsistent: dual-control >0% vs cold-chain >0.4% (the baseline), so the firm is always on the boundary for temperature docs. Fairness indicators never set a pharmacy service floor despite the 61% fill collapse.

**What a stricter answer needed.** Quantify risky vs resilient T2 paths from the DFG; separate observed A/F/H from the controls that would make them legitimate; put hard stops on controlled / cold-chain / recall; give T3 its own sync-and-scan degraded procedure; and treat the 2,300-case audit queue as a first-class recovery process with owners and clocks, not a dashboard afterthought.

Competent, well-shaped, and usable as a draft. Too many unforced inconsistencies and unused frequencies for a high score under a strict reading.
