## What is actually sound

The seven required headings are present. Offset is defined in the correct direction, resolution is applied before clock correction, HUB uncertainty of ±1.5 s is right, June offsets (CEST/BST/EDT) are conceptually right, midpoints are not used as the primary evidence, and Section 7 hits the requested control themes (UTC storage, raw retention, offset telemetry, resolution metadata, lifecycle flags, DST fold, causal IDs, interval-overlap policy). A few S1 calls are directionally correct even with sloppy bounds: T-402 certain violation, T-403 possible, T-406 possible, T-408 pass, T-409 possible.

That is not enough. The task is an interval-reasoning exam. The arithmetic and completeness are the grade.

## Section 1 — assumptions are incomplete and, in two places, false

“True time = recorded time  offset” drops the minus sign; “EDT = UTC4” drops the minus as well. Those are not cosmetic in a timezone problem.

T-404 is defined incorrectly: Investigation_Start is inferred from **Containment_Complete** minus 18–25 minutes. The signed record is investigation duration, and **Investigation_Complete** is sitting on the same case at 10:35. The legal inference is Start ∈ Complete − [18, 25] min, i.e. roughly 10:10–10:17 UTC, not 10:30–10:37. The author’s interval is also internally impossible: a start at 10:30–10:37 and a complete at 10:35 cannot be an 18–25 minute investigation.

T-406 entry delay is applied as `[+0, +300 s]` added to recorded time, without a clear occurrence-vs-record-time commitment. “Entered up to five minutes late” more naturally puts occurrence *before* the stamp (`recorded − [0, 300]`), or leaves occurrence at the stamp and treats delay as provenance only. Adding delay to the future is the least defensible of the three and is not justified.

T-407 states that `Alert_Sent` is not `Alert_Received`, then Section 3 treats the pair as a near-identity (1020–1170 s). The assumption and the finding do not cohere.

## Section 2 — interval construction is systematically wrong

Resolution and clock offset are not combined as specified.

- **OPS-BER.** Floor `[mm:00, mm:60)` minus offset `[-90, +120]` is `[mm:00−120, mm:60+90]`, about 4.5 minutes wide (`…:12:00` to `…:16:30` after the CEST→UTC shift). The table uses a neat 4-minute window (`08:12–08:16`, `09:16–09:20`). The minute remainder and the +90 s bound are dropped.
- **SENSOR.** Nearest 10 s is ±5 s *first*; offset `[-5, +5]` is *then* applied. Feasible time is about `08:51:00–08:51:20`, not `08:51:05–08:51:15`. The notes claim “rounding + offset” while the interval is rounding only.
- **VENDOR-NY.** Floor plus `±180 s` expands *both* ends. After EDT→UTC the interval is about `07:57:00–08:04:00`, not `08:00:00–08:03:00`. The early side of a ±3 minute bound is simply missing.
- **LEGACY-BER.** Same error: floor is kept, `±60 s` is not. The T-405 Alert cell is literally broken (`2025-10-, two intervals: [00:50:00–00:51:00] or [01:50:00–01:51:00]`). Investigation_Start and Containment_Complete are not given as UTC intervals at all (“two intervals after Alert”).
- **T-404 / T-407.** The missing start is inferred from the wrong anchor. Alert_Received is never materialized as a latency-bounded UTC interval.

Evidence-strength labels are plausible; the timestamps they describe are not.

## Section 3 — self-contradiction, missing rows, and several wrong classes

The instruction was one row per *testable* case–rule pair, including S2, S3, and every supplied profile pair. The table is almost only S1, plus S2 once and one profile row. That omits the easiest certain violations in the set.

**T-401 S1 is internally impossible.** The author’s own duration is 720–960 s against a 900 s cap, labeled CERTAIN_PASS, “fully inside SLA.” 960 > 900. Under their intervals this is POSSIBLE_VIOLATION; under correct OPS-BER bounds it is still POSSIBLE_VIOLATION (~719–992 s). High confidence on a comparison that fails grade-school inequality is disqualifying.

**T-401 profile band is wrong.** ζ = 1 on (600, 180) is `[420, 780]`, not `[180, 1020]`. 720–960 is not a certain pass of `[420, 780]`. They appear to have used `mean ± (mean − sd)` or an equivalent invention.

**T-404 S1** is CERTAIN_VIOLATION only because Start was inferred from Containment. Correct inference from Investigation_Complete gives ~10–17 minutes after the 10:00 alert → POSSIBLE_VIOLATION of S1, and S2 sits on the 45-minute knife-edge (~2267–2703 s vs 2700 s). Both are unreported.

**T-405** is waved off as INDETERMINATE because “DST fold prevents test.” Ingestion order is not decorative. Displayed 02:50 then 02:05 then 02:42 cannot live in one fold pass. The surviving reading is Alert on the first pass (CEST / UTC+2) and Start/Containment on the second (CET / UTC+1), still as intervals, not points. That yields a testable S1 window on the order of 12–18 minutes → POSSIBLE_VIOLATION, not an untestable union.

**T-407 S1 as CERTAIN_VIOLATION** overclaims. There is no Alert_Received. Latency `[0, 20]` min plus a *plausible, unlogged* “receipt ≤ start” constraint produces a duration from ~0 up to ~17 minutes, i.e. POSSIBLE_VIOLATION if you accept the causal story, otherwise INDETERMINATE. 1020–1170 s is the “Sent ≈ Received” answer the author promised not to give.

**T-408 is the clean HUB case and is only half-graded.** S1 pass is correct. S2 is 55 minutes (`16:05 − 15:10`) against a 45-minute cap: CERTAIN_VIOLATION. S3 is Close `2026-06-03 06:30` vs 36 hours after `16:05` on 1 June (deadline `04:05` on 3 June): CERTAIN_VIOLATION by ~2 h 25 min. Missing both certain violations on the only fully precise four-event case is a severe completeness failure.

**T-409** possible S1 is fair; the negative lower bound is left uninterpreted (causal impossibility vs weak offset). S2 and profiles are absent.

Unassessed but testable: T-401 S3 and the other three profile pairs; T-402 S2 (possible: containment ~41–46 min after start) and all three profiles; T-403 S2 and profiles; T-406 S2/profiles; T-407 S2.
