The month-by-month narrative is the strongest section. It refuses to treat April, June, August–September, and October as one homogeneous failure, which is the central quality bar.

## Section 1 is less clean than it looks

Several cells are wrong or mislabeled.

The “contradicting” cell for label delay (“October’s outcome API backlog disproportionately affects longer resolution paths”) is supporting evidence, not contradicting evidence. That is a category error in the one place the rubric asked for explicit falsifiers.

Covariate drift is scoped only to April–May cached features. That is residual pipeline corruption, not the main covariate story. The real P(X) shifts are June’s variant mix and September’s Partner Z rural/device-age profile. Those features “were not present in the original model,” which is covariate shift via missing attributes, and they are dumped into population/concept instead.

Concept drift is smeared onto Partner Z. Partner Z is first a population shift; any concept change must be shown *conditional on path and features*. The table does not make that cut.

Process drift’s contradicting evidence (“AUROC recovers after May despite process mix change”) only describes June. It does not apply to August–October, where AUROC is 0.68/0.66/0.64. Confidence labels are roughly sensible; the supporting/contradicting columns are not reliable enough to trust as an audit artifact.

June is not a clean “model generalizes.” Observed breach falls to 0.15 while mean predicted risk stays at 0.20 and ECE is 0.060. That is mix-driven miscalibration, not concept drift, but it is not “no issue.”

July is not “no immediate model degradation.” V3 breach is already 0.46, AUROC is 0.76, Brier is 0.151, and the parts edge is 65 h. The break begins in July; August is intensification.

September’s 0.31 observed rate is treated as if it were almost as trustworthy as August. It is not: 82% maturity, Partner Z at 24% with slower labels, and V3 at 42% all bias the observed rate. The answer mentions Partner Z immaturity and then proceeds as if the month were still a clean concept/population reading.

October is handled well. The rest of the timeline is good enough that these are not fatal, but they are not minor either.

## Section 3 is the largest intellectual failure

The test list is complete on paper and then repeatedly assigns the wrong expected result.

Label-maturity curves do not diagnose pipeline failure. April and May are 100% mature; a schema rename does not change when breach labels arrive. “April–May curves flatten early” under a pipeline hypothesis is false. Concept drift also does not predict uniformly steepening vintage curves; it predicts different *asymptotic* rates after labels mature.

Fixed-vintage backtesting is specified as “retrain on Jan–Mar; predict April–October,” i.e., *without* feature repair. Under that protocol, pipeline failure still produces a bad April (AUROC ~0.67), not a recovered 0.81. The concept-drift column then predicts April AUROC ~0.67, which attributes the pipeline month to concept drift. That inverts the whole point of the test.

Population reweighting is misunderstood. Reweighting cannot make Partner Z’s own breach rate “drop to ~0.27.” It changes the *aggregate* by changing source/mix weights. If within-stratum rates stay high after mix is held fixed, that is concept or unmeasured confounding, not a change in Partner Z’s rate. Both expected-result cells for this test are conceptually wrong.

The October DFG cell under label delay (“Order Part edge stable”) is factually false. The parts edge is 155 h. Process-performance statistics do not require outcome maturity.

Stratification by source is named and then not really specified. The useful October check is also missing: if the problem is censoring, short V2 cases should look much more mature and better calibrated than V3. “All variants poor because immature” is the opposite of that discriminator.

Feature-missingness expected results talk about rural distance and device age under a *pipeline* hypothesis. Those fields were never in the model. The actual lineage audit is cached “events before diagnosis,” queue-feature backfill, UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY, and post-20 April duplicates. That audit is largely absent.

A diagnostic-test section that cannot state what would be observed under competing hypotheses cannot separate the drifts the table claims to have separated.

## Section 4 proposes the wrong short-term fix

Immediate containment freezes April–May and under-weights the live problem (V3 under-prediction from July onward, and October reporting). May is already 100% mature with AUROC 0.78; it needs recompute, not a freeze. October needs the freeze/caveat.

Short-term recalibration “on Jan–Mar with corrected features” cannot fix the regime that matters. Jan–Mar V3 breach is ~0.31–0.32; August V3 is 0.64. An old-data calibrator will still understate current parts-dependent risk. Recalibration is proportionate for June-style mix/calibration drift while AUROC is ~0.79. It is not sufficient when AUROC is 0.66–0.68. The answer never makes that discrimination-versus-calibration cut.

Excluding September from training *because Partner Z exists* is the wrong default if Partner Z is the new production population. The unsafe reason is immature and differentially delayed labels, not the mere presence of a new partner. “Reweight to 2026Q1–Q2” in Section 1 has the same problem: it trains for a world that no longer exists.

“Add a supplier delay feature” is not available at the stated scoring point (immediately after diagnosis). The delay is downstream. At prefix time you need exogenous lead-time/backlog features, later-prefix models, or policy thresholds — not a feature that has not occurred yet.

A variant-aware model is defensible. A three-head ensemble is heavier than the evidence requires; interactions plus stratified calibration would be the proportionate next step. This is a smaller issue than the training-window error.

## Section 5: monitors are fine; the code is not a sketch of the asked API

The monitor set and the key dependency (label-delay alarm must not auto-trigger retraining) meet the prompt. A few extra gates are missing — predictive alerts should also be blocked by data-quality failure, and a process-DFG alarm should not be treated as a retrain signal by itself — but the architecture is serviceable.

The code does not. It was asked to use `pm4py.format_dataframe`, `pm4py.filter_time_range`, and `pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe`. It uses none of them. It converts a dataframe `TO_DATA_FRAME`, imports non-existent or unused discovery/filtering paths, never calls `filter_time_range`, and invents a `perf_dfg.get((src, tgt), {}).get("median")` interface. The field is named `median_order_part_received_days` while every given statistic is in hours. Duplicate rate is computed after global deduplication, so it is structurally ~0. Event-calendar versus case-start cohorts are created, then only one is used for DFG, and their different interpretations are not stated. UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY is left unmapped after the rename normalization.

A sketch may be incomplete. It may not invent the wrong library and skip three named functions.
