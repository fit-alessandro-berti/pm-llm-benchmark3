**6.5**

The answer is process-aware, schema-complete, and clearly better than a DFG-copying baseline, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the required distinctions (enabled action vs next log event, obligations vs frequencies, feed quality as a *constraint* on skip probability), several ranking and calibration choices are hard to defend.

**What is actually strong.** Latent-state calls are mostly right: C6-A/B/E as S3 after `Evidence Received`, C6-C as S4 waiting on Regulatory Assessment, C6-D as a concurrent investigation fork with a corrupted observation trace. Obligations are applied as rules, not as vibes: Fast Close is correctly blocked for A; Technical Investigation + Regulatory Assessment are mandatory for A and D; Supplier Analysis is triggered by lot relatedness in A and especially E, and correctly *not* forced in C (same failure mode, different lots, no supplier object). The A-vs-B paragraph is the best part of the writeup: same prefix, opposite risk objects, opposite closure vs investigation futures. C6-D does not treat missing evidence events as proof of non-occurrence, and it uses the R-R2 worklist snapshot as a start signal rather than a completion. C6-C is the only case that marks `process_enabled: false` in a principled way (Disposition / Close before Regulatory Assessment under the country rule). Intervals are wide on purpose, alternatives are retained, and the DFG is treated as a weak prior.

**Material process-reasoning failures.**

1. *Skip-ahead is overweighted whenever the feed is normal.* For A, B, and E the visible prefix is parked on `Evidence Received`. With a normal feed and a review station that is actually staffed (4 waiting, 2 reviewers, 1–5 h after queue entry), the immediately enabled next *logged* completion is `Evidence Review Completed`. Rank-1 bands of 0.35–0.55 / 0.42–0.62 / 0.32–0.52 are underconfident. The complementary mass is dumped onto later activities that are only next in the log if review already finished unlogged or if two completions collapse into one observed step. That is allowed by the observation model, but it is not *disciplined* given `feed quality: normal`. C6-A is the clearest miss: ranks 2–3 (`Technical Investigation Completed` 0.18–0.35, `Regulatory Assessment Completed` 0.12–0.28) jointly assign roughly a third to a half of the probability to events that sit behind review *and* behind long, degraded queues (7 technical items, one engineer out; 12 regulatory items, 2–14 h). The writeup’s own contradicting-evidence bullets say this, then the ranking ignores it.

2. *`process_enabled` is not an operationalized field.* The prompt requires a distinction between a process-enabled action and the event most likely to appear next. Only C6-C uses `false`. Closures and investigation completions that are not enabled on the visible prefix are `true` in A/B/E. In D, delayed/backfilled evidence events are also `true`, even though the caveat correctly says Regulatory Assessment is the work in progress and an evidence line may merely *appear*. The distinction is verbalized and then discarded in the schema that was supposed to encode it.

3. *C6-E rank 2 is poorly calibrated.* `Supplier Analysis Completed` at 0.16–0.34 as the *next* log event, 40 minutes after the second `Evidence Received`, against a stated 6–36 h cycle and 3 waiting items, is not supported unless analysis has been running since the previous day *and* is about to finish before review of the new package. The obligation is real; the next-event probability is not. Rank 3 (`Customer Evidence Requested`) has the same skip problem: the historical loop is after `Evidence Review Completed`, which has not been logged.

4. *C6-D rank-1 is slightly mis-centered.* Regulatory Assessment opened at 12:40 into a 12-deep, risk-priority queue with 2–14 h post-assignment time; the clock is 14:00. Completion can still be the next *event* if nothing else is pending, but this is the migration-affected case whose distinctive evidence is a source-system attachment with no analytical evidence cycle. Putting RA completion at 0.34–0.56 and the two evidence backfill candidates at 0.12–0.30 / 0.10–0.28 underuses the one fact the scenario went out of its way to supply. Rank 2 vs 3 (`Customer Evidence Requested` vs `Evidence Received`) is also under-argued: an unsolicited attachment more naturally supports a delayed *receipt* (or a skipped request) than a fresh request.

**Smaller defects that still count under a harsh rubric.**

- C6-B rank 3 `Fast Close` after a completed evidence cycle is substantively eligible and historically awkward. The text admits this, then still ranks Fast Close above any investigation path while listing R-T4 (reportability-associated routing) as contradicting evidence and never converting that signal into a prediction. The alternative state (S1) is a straw man.
- C6-C rank 3 `Close — No Safety Issue` at up to 0.20 is a double skip (Regulatory Assessment and Disposition) on a normal feed. A thin tail is fine; 0.20 is not thin.
- Midpoints of the top three already sum to ~0.85–0.91 in every case, which is a quiet claim that almost nothing else can appear. That sits poorly next to long unknown-lists and branching obligations.
- Cosmetic sloppiness: `dfg_limitations` ends with a dangling `Source: `.
- Queue and batch information is used unevenly. Review/tech/regulatory/supplier waiting times appear, but the 16:00 notification batch is unused (acceptable, because no case is notification-ready) and elapsed-since-last-event is only loosely tied to the stated service times (A: 1 h 50 min since receipt; E: 40 min; D: 80 min since RA open).

**Net.** This is a competent, structured, obligation-sensitive answer that would look strong in a lenient mark scheme. It is not nearly flawless: the central task was to keep next-event forecasts attached to what is actually enabled and to what the observation process can show, and on that criterion A, D, and E are each wrong in a different, avoidable way. Strictly, that is mid-6s work.
