**6.1**

The write-up is complete, follows the required headings, and is strong on structure, timing discipline, and most case outcomes. It is not close to flawless. Two primary classifications rest on a weakly justified premise that the rest of the analysis simultaneously undercuts, several material facts are unused, and genuine indeterminacy is never allowed to produce `INDETERMINATE`.

**What is solid**

Process method is explicit: issue-specific hierarchy, act-time application, A6 non-cure, contemporaneous evidence over ERP stamps, and a stated burden of proof. P-501 (pre-window, undelegated Shift Lead, Plant Manager reachable), P-502 (new-supplier Compliance after commit), P-503 (backup line, three-day stock, missing A3/A2 plus SOD), P-504 (outside ECD-2 scope, unapproved supplier), P-506 (post-expiry, unproven oral extension), and P-508 (above the D1 cap, Legal present, Finance absent) are the right primary calls with usable justifications. P-505’s 17:59:31 acknowledgment versus the 18:06 ERP stamp, and P-507’s signed PDF at 12:40 versus ERP at 14:30, are handled correctly as evidence questions. Alternatives exist for more than four cases. The policy clause in §7 is clearer than the case law the author actually applied.

**Material analytical failures**

The central error is treating CFO email D1 as enough to make P-505 and P-507 `COMPLIANT`. GPIP-4 A2 is a board rule requiring an independent budget owner *and* a Finance Controller *before* commitment. A4 keeps those controls in place unless A7 CRO exception exists. D is an uncontrolled email; D3 does not authorize a GPIP waiver; D4 says essential integrity controls are not removed; Incident Command’s charter does not make members budget owners. The author states in §1 that ECD-2/D cannot displace GPIP integrity controls, then lets D1 displace A2 (and, more weakly, Plant_Approve, which is not a finance-interpretation question at all). That is an internal contradiction, not a sophisticated issue-specific hierarchy. Under the author’s own “possible / defeasible” framing, the honest primary labels are `INDETERMINATE` or `NONCOMPLIANT`, with `COMPLIANT` as the alternative. Choosing `COMPLIANT` hides the ambiguity the rubric tells the analyst not to hide.

Related misses: A2’s dual approval is almost never analyzed; “IC members are not budget owners” is an available organizational fact that should have blocked D1-as-A2; Plant_Approve is swept into “operational approvals” as if the CFO owned the plant control; `INDETERMINATE` is never used despite C6, D1’s status, and a 29-second timing margin on P-505; D4 is underused as a defeater of a broad D1 reading.

**Smaller but real defects**

The rule table repeatedly puts source IDs in the actor-constraint column. R8 hard-codes B2 as pre-commitment, then P-501 treats that as uncertain. P-503’s “satisfied rules: none convincingly” is false (approved supplier, if nothing else). Prior practice that signed incident directives have been treated as overlays—except for new-supplier Compliance—is unused. §7’s recommended rule (written CFO authorization; committer cannot be one of the two authorizers) is stricter than the case holdings and the tension is not acknowledged.

**Net**

Six of eight cases are defensible. The hierarchy apparatus is real. Two compliant findings, the unused budget-owner fact, and the GPIP-versus-email contradiction are enough to keep this well out of the high band under a strict reading.
