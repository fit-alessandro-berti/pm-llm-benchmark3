**3.4**

The submission has the required nine headings and a defensible *qualitative* preference for a P1-protecting hybrid, but it fails the core analytical, tabular, and reproducibility requirements. Several mistakes are definitional, not merely imprecise.

**Format vs. substance.** Headings match the spec. Almost every required *analysis* under those headings is missing, inverted, or asserted without auditably shown work.

**Section 1.** Two objectives (tail/SLA and fairness/robustness) are present, which is correct. Service-time modeling is internally inconsistent: ranges are called Uniform, then complex-tail is described as “increasing the upper bound,” then illustrated with the wrong interval (`8–14` / `U(8,14)` vs. the table’s Intake `5–8` / `10–14`). Parallel-vs-sequential is a vague contention heuristic, not a decision rule. Speed multipliers, overtime, the 90% utilization fragility rule, team-switch +5 min, skill-family setup +5 min, and “preempt at most once” are not turned into operational assumptions.

**Section 2 is the largest competence failure.** Available time is a single daily total (2520 min) with no 2-hour block × skill capacity, no shift overlap, and no effective-capacity adjustment for multipliers (Farah at 1.40 is 40% *slower*, not extra capacity). Offered load is counted as M / E / S *cases only*. ME work is Mechanical *and* Electrical; ES is Electrical *and* Software. That omission understates electrical load especially and makes every bottleneck claim unauditable. There is no minutes-of-work calculation (cases × service draw × tail mix × multiplier). U1–U5 are one-line qualitative risks with no renormalization for U2, no tail-mean shift for U4, and no Chen-shaped software hole for U3. The “43 minute” critical path ignores dual-branch ME/ES, handovers, coordination, waiting, and multipliers. Late-day Validation coverage (Farah off at 12:00; Ada/Ben/Chen off at 16:00) is not analyzed.

**Section 3.** Labels exist; implementable dispatch does not. Policy A “drops” cases after five in queue—an invented, operationally indefensible rule. Policy B is named SEPT then specified as least-loaded assignment. Policy C inverts the speed matrix: “Speed Multiplier > 1.0 preferred” treats 1.25–1.40 as *faster*; the prompt defines the opposite. Eligibility, priority scores, reservation, preemption protocol, parallelization trigger, handover minimization, and overflow are not all specified for any policy.

**Section 4 fails a hard output requirement.** The table is policy-only, not policy-by-scenario. Base/U1–U5 never appear. Cells are unlabeled adjectives (`High`/`Best`) with no scale, units, or uncertainty. `P2_SLA` is identical across policies. Claims cannot be checked against Section 2 or Section 8 because neither produces those numbers.

**Section 5.** Hybrid-for-robustness is a reasonable *type* of recommendation and is not justified solely by base-case mean delay—that is the one real conceptual plus. It is not supported by scenario evidence. Mode-switch rules contradict each other (5% P1 breach over 2 hours vs. P1 SLA < 90% for 240 minutes). The 5% breach trigger is tighter than the 90% SLA, so the system would “fall back” while still meeting the stated target. No serious discussion of when specialization or full pooling wins (e.g., U3 software collapse, U4 tail-driven preemption thrash).

**Sections 6–7.** Secondary effects are the strongest prose: gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, preemption, coordination, Validation pile-up. Still generic. Monitoring omits several mandated attributes (queue-entry, assignment decision, eligible-resource set, skill used, planned vs actual service, pause/resume). `pm4py.find_bottlenecks` is not a standard API to lean on.

**Section 8 does not meet the eight code rules.** It encodes some tables, then simulates a single heuristic, not three policies. `current_time` never advances, so every case starts at 08:00. ME/ES execute only `activities[1]` (second branch dropped). Speed multipliers, calendars, breaks, overtime, handover, setup, coordination, preemption, U3 absence, U5 outage, and U4 tail doubling are stubs (`pass`, “simplified: 0”). P1 boost is not renormalized, so choice probabilities need not sum to 1. Complex-tail probability is a flat 0.12. Only `scenario='Base'` is generated; the U1–U5 loop is empty. `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`, and `discover_handover_of_work_network` are not actually executed. A scaffold is allowed only if omissions are identified; here they are hidden. A leftover `strptime(...).split` line would throw. Printed “metrics” are not policy- or scenario-comparable.

**Section 9.** Limits are partly right (Poisson vs means, omitted mechanisms) but then treat Policy A as “current standard” with no evidence. The pilot does not say how policies would be distinguished while protecting P1.

**Net.** Correct document skeleton and a plausible hybrid instinct; wrong load object, inverted speeds, missing scenario table, and non-compliant code. That is well below the quality bar, which required specialization–pooling tension *with* multipliers, time-varying shifts, and robustness across U1–U5—not adjectives.
