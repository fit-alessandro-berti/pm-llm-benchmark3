**3.4**

The answer is well-scaffolded and looks like a compliance memorandum, but it fails the evaluation axes that matter: temporal application, internal consistency, and evidential fidelity. Structure is not the same as analysis.

**Decisive failures**

The hierarchy and the case table do not implement each other. Section 2 says ECD-2 probably cannot override A1, A2, or A3. Section 1 says incident directives do not implicitly waive GPIP-4 integrity controls. A4 and A6 are then treated as ornamental. Five of eight cases become `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` on a theory of “deferral” that no source states and that A6 expressly rejects. That is not a close interpretive call. It is a contradiction.

P-501 is the cleanest demonstration. Commitment is 08:50; ECD-2 starts 09:00. The case table itself flags this. The answer still applies ECD-2, waives the approved-supplier rule for an already-approved supplier, and cites A2/R-002 on a €38k purchase that is below the €50k trigger. Plant Manager was reachable and never delegated. B4/B5 therefore cut against the result, not for it. Classifying this `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` at high confidence violates the instruction to apply rules at the time of the act.

**Case-level fact errors**

These are not rounding issues. They change the rule set.

- P-502: Compliance at 18:30 cannot “satisfy by deferral.” A3 is pre-commitment. The CFO email is 15 July and cannot govern a 14 July act. No Plant_Approve is recorded. High-confidence exception is indefensible under the author’s own catalogue.
- P-503: Supplier is approved; listing R-006 as a deviation is false. The stronger independent defects — €140k with no Compliance (A3), no Finance (A2), and backup-line parts with three-day stock (C6) — are underplayed relative to SoD. The SoD call itself is reasonable.
- P-505: Compliance occurred at 17:40 and A3 did not require it. Calling R-003 missing is a misread. “ECD-2 covers post-18:00 actions if critical” is invented. ON-44 is a 14 July outage; it is not a license to rewrite the 18:00 expiry.
- P-506: Finance_Approve is *before* commitment. “None” satisfied is false. The supplier is approved; the R-006 hedge is false.
- P-507: Compliance is 08:50, commitment 09:00. Describing Compliance as deferred or retroactive is false. The signed PDF at 12:40 is correctly preferred to the 14:30 ERP stamp; that one evidential point is sound and then attached to the wrong legal conclusion.
- P-508: D1’s operational-approval substitution stops at €150k. This purchase is €155k. Legal clearance satisfies D2, not A2. Missing Finance is therefore not cured even on the most generous reading of the CFO email.

P-504 is the only classification that is cleanly right for the right reason (scope). P-503 and P-506 are directionally right and still factually sloppy.

**Formalization is broken**

The rule table drops the comparison operators (`Purchase EUR 50k`, `Purchase EUR 100k`). R-001’s actor line is unintelligible. R-004 collapses B4 and ECD-2 into one rule despite different clocks, actors, and predicates. The pseudo-logic is not executable even as defeasible sketch: missing connectives, `normally`/`unless` packed with conjunctions that do not match the prose, and no real `overrides_for` conditions for Finance, SoD, or the €100k Compliance trigger. This section does not earn credit as a formalization.

**Uncertainty is performed, not used**

`INDETERMINATE` is never used. Confidence is “High” on cases that turn on unresolved premises: whether C4’s “supplier-onboarding” swallows A3, whether IC members are budget owners, whether ECD-2 is an A7 exception, whether backup-line contingency is in scope. Section 5 is the best part of the memo — P-502 and P-508 alternatives are the classifications the main table should have taken seriously — but several alternatives attack the wrong premise (P-501 Finance waiver; P-507 late ERP for Compliance that was timely).

**What is actually good**

Facts and assumptions are at least labeled. The CRO/COO split, the informality of the CFO email, and the non-normative status of ON-44 are correctly seen. P-504’s scope exclusion is right. P-507’s contemporaneous PDF versus late ERP is the correct evidential move. Section 7 is the strongest section: explicit waiver checklist, SoD treated as non-waivable, defined “critical,” contemporaneous record rule, and a 72-hour post-incident audit as an anti-normalization brake.

Those merits do not offset applying a directive before it exists, contradicting the stated hierarchy in the operative table, and misstating who was an approved supplier and when Compliance occurred. Under a strict reading of consistency, time, scope, and evidence, this is a formatted argument that repeatedly fails its own method.
