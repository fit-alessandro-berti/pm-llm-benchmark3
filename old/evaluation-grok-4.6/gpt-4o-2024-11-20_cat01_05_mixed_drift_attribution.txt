**4.4**

The answer has the right *skeleton* (March is mostly semantic, later movement is real, multiple mechanism-specific breaks, supplier/staffing/priority/season all in play) and it mostly obeys the heading/table contract. Under a strict reading that is not enough. The quantitative core is sloppy, several required deliverables are simply missing, and a few claims contradict the prompt.

**Hard requirement misses.** Section 3 asks for at least six metrics; the change table has five. It also asks for *at least two* candidate change points *each* for logging, behavioral, and performance drift; the answer gives one logging point (March), one behavioral point (July), and two performance points. Part A must *embed* the monthly summary and the normalized DFG-rate table; the code reads phantom CSVs. The monitor list never includes a dedicated **service-time** monitor, despite the prompt forcing the service-vs-queue split. Section 9 is asked to assign major changes with confidence levels in 250–400 words; it is barely at the floor, uses only “strongest / plausible,” and never attributes the residual mapped-fitness decline, the October `Senior Assessment` spike, or p90-vs-median divergence.

**Quantitative errors (not rounding).** Feb→Mar median cycle is \(31.2-30.8=+0.4\)h, not \(+1.3\). May high-complexity vs Feb is \(+5\) pp, not \(+7\). Oct fast-variant vs Feb is \(-22\) pp, not \(-25\). Dec high-complexity vs Oct is \(-7\) pp, not \(-11\). The table also silently mixes Jan and Feb as the baseline. The Jaccard figures (0.18 / 0.25 / 0.40) are not reproducible from the supplied rates. After mapping, Jan vs Mar is essentially \(\{100,98,95,12,11,4,83\}\) vs \(\{100,98,94,12,11,5,82\}\). Any honest fragment distance is near zero; that near-zero is the *main* evidence that March is logging rather than control-flow. Reporting 0.18 erases the finding the rest of the memo depends on. `scipy.spatial.distance.jaccard` on two scalars is also not a defined DFG distance.

**Mis-typed mechanisms.** May is labeled population/case-mix. The identifying shock is incomplete Z bundles → `Assess→Request Info` (12→24 per 100 cases) and rework-variant 13→24 *before* the June staffing cut. Z share 18→43 is a mix shift, but it is not the primary dimension. July is labeled control-flow/behavioral; a queue-priority rule changes waiting-time allocation, not the normative path. The prompt already says the monthly aggregate does **not** show high-complexity vs standard waiting; Section 4 still lists “Queue gap widened post-July” as supporting evidence. That is invented confirmation. Cycle-time shares (~30% mix, ~40% rework, 35–45% queue, plus service) add to more than 100% and treat overlapping channels as additive. Given explicit text that Assess service only moved \(4.8\to5.4\)h while reconstructed queueing moved much more, a 35–45% queue share is also too small and too precise for the aggregates.

**Causal and PM discipline.** Mapped fitness recovering \(0.55\to0.90\) in March is handled correctly at the slogan level, but Section 2 never says mapped fitness *keeps falling* to ~0.86–0.87, which is the residual real-drift series. Duplicates and missing resources are described generically; there is no account of variant inflation, illegal `Open Case→Register`, or non-random missingness breaking resource/social analyses. Senior Assessment is historically inconsistently logged; October’s 4→22 jump is treated as pure seasonality with evidence 95, with almost no logging/semantic alternative. June–August is one staffing interval that overlaps a separate July row, so onset types collide. September recovery (Z 38→24, rework 27→20, median 45→41.5) is omitted, which weakens any later claim that December is intervention-not-load.

**Monitoring and code.** The primary monitoring population is “high-complexity Supplier Z cases.” That is the most selected, most confounded cell (Z is already non-random and more complex; priority already reorders that class). It will hide standard-case delay from the July rule and is exactly the collider the segmentation table warns about. The primary series should be all cases after label normalization, with Z and complexity as *strata*, not the intersection as the frame. Monitors use ad-hoc thresholds and a one-month rolling window that will absorb the March break. Seasonality and multiplicity are waved at, not specified (same-month prior year, STL residual, holdout, hierarchical error control). Part B is a set of one-line wrappers: no copy-safe label mapping, no `timest_format`, no skeleton/DECLARE, no windowed comparison contract. The change-point rule applies \(0.1\) to both fitness and raw case counts, so it is not an explainable detector.

**What is salvageable.** Format compliance is mostly there. The high-level story (March semantic collapse ≠ later operational drift; supplier drives rework; staffing and priority drive queues; October is seasonal and amplifying; December is mixed) is the story a competent analyst should tell. Hypotheses 1–8 cover the required set. Discrimination and segmentation tables are directionally the right artifacts. That is why this is not a 2–3. It is still far from a 7+: too many arithmetic mistakes, too much unsourced precision, several explicit checklist failures, and at least one claim the prompt forbids. A nearly flawless memo would have shown March DFG distance ≈ 0 after mapping, kept May in the rework/supplier channel, refused to “see” a priority gap in the monthly totals, embedded the data, and monitored service, queue, mix, and schema on separate clocks. This one does not.
