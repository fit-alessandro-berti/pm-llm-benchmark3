**5.2**

The write-up looks complete and methodologically literate, but it fails the one check that is fully determined by the CSV: the stage table and every number derived from it.

**Empirical core is wrong.** There are 10 flagged and 10 unflagged cases, not 9 and 11. The author’s own case list in §3 already contains ten unflagged IDs, then the table says `n=11`. Flagged cycle times are about 16.5h mean / 16.4h median, unflagged about 8.2h / 5.7h — not 19.60 vs 16.56. The headline “3-hour paradox” is an artifact. Downstream counts are also false: review is 10/10 vs 4/10, not 9/9 vs 5/11; flagged escalation is 6/10, not 5/9; complexity is Low 4 / High 8, not 5 and 7; flagged high-load is 6, not 4. Approval/reject splits used in the quality pathway are wrong. These errors propagate through §§3–6 and 9, so the quantitative policy ranges are not estimates — they are miscalculations.

**Internal contradictions.** The load subgroup table exists, yet §4 claims no flagged case arrives in a normal-load window. E5 says approve/reject and escalation are “not recorded as distinct”; both are in the file. The mechanism diagram embeds invented waits (“~3 hours for flagged”).

**Causal framework is uneven.** Mixing CDE, natural effects, and interventional analogues is defensible, but E3 is incoherent (NDE of flag on *review occurrence*, “net of escalation,” which is downstream). Natural effects are repeatedly invoked after a correct SUTVA/interference discussion that makes those cross-world objects ill-defined. E2 is marked well-defined while the contrast (P0 vs P1) does not isolate priority routing. “Set the flag to zero” vs “change post-flag policy” is clearer in the P0–P4 labels than in the estimands.

**Other requirement misses.** `discover_performance_dfg` is not run on flagged vs unflagged logs. The event log omits Register/arrival, so DFGs cannot show the triage-wait edge the narrative relies on. §8 has six reversals but none on timestamp semantics (clock time, overnight gaps, start/complete meaning). Pilot eligibility `risk_score ≥ 0.70` drops real flagged cases at 0.64–0.67; P2 and P3 are entangled so the trial does not identify either policy cleanly.

**What still works.** Occurrence vs wait vs escalation mediation is conceptually separated; interference, exposure-induced confounding, and treatment–confounder feedback are named and used. §5 designs (threshold, queue state, encouragement, E-value, peer spillover, negative-control stage) are actual designs, not name-drops. The code does not fabricate absent stages and warns against causal DFGs. The pilot has guardrails and stopping rules. Format compliance is high.

That is a competent methods essay sitting on a broken decomposition. Under a strict reading of “decompose queue and service intervals correctly,” it cannot score much above the midpoint.
