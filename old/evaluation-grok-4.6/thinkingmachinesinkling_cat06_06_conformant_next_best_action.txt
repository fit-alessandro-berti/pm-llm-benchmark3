**6.5**

The final write-up is complete, on-format, and substantively aligned with the hard constraints: A/B are last, G is an unenabled exception, F is conditional, screening is not treated as a substitute for second review, and P-901 is actually priced into the ranking. That is well above a weak “optimize P-884’s SLA” answer. It is not close to flawless.

**Material ranking error.** Rank 1’s S4 path (“review by ~50 min, execution by ~60–75 min”) is unsupported. The only review duration given is C’s 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 to record. If S4’s review is in that band, wait 35–65 plus review plus record is roughly 75–155 minutes and usually misses the 90-minute deadline. The same optimistic ~15–25 minute S4 review is repeated in §5 (“S4 at 35 min… review by ~50 min, ~40 min buffer”). That is not a slip; it is the justification for putting an S4-first composite above immediate C. A correct timeline would either (i) put C first, with screening in parallel, or (ii) keep S4-first only as a spillover-avoidance choice that *accepts* a much higher P-884 breach chance. The 35-vs-65 sensitivity therefore sits on a false premise.

**Inconsistent and invented monetization.** Rank 2 uses €11k–€26k (deadline band × 0.55–0.75). Rank 1 uses €18k–€32k “if executed,” which is neither the raw €20k–€35k nor the same weighting. Approval probability is 0.6–0.75 in one branch and 0.55–0.75 elsewhere. Harm rows mix euros, probabilities, and qualitative tails without a common comparison rule.

**Overclaims and loose language.** “E is dominated by D” is too strong: vendor rescreen and customer evidence are not the same information set. Regulatory harm is called “unbounded” though the prompt gives a wide but finite band plus non-monetary tail. Mapping alternate screening to rule 5 is a stretch (that cycle is Evidence Requested → Received → Validated).

**Smaller unclarities.** Fallback invents an “S2 unavailable” state the resource model does not give (S2 is available by interrupting P-901). Table timelines disagree (80–100 vs 80–95 vs “near 90”). The Confidence column silently changes meaning for A/B (“high that it is invalid”). The §6 line about reassessing whether second review still applies if the score updates is slightly softer than the prompt’s “does not substitute while current conditions remain,” even though the same section later says to complete the review anyway.

**What remains strong.** Exact six-section scaffold; correct conformance labels; A/B cannot be rescued by a favorable forecast; four-eyes and rule 10 handled correctly; SAR non-disclosure; observational predictions flagged as non-transportable; parallel screening plus an explicit cutoff/fallback; P-901 spillover kept visible. Those are the pieces the rubric cares about most, which is why this is still a mid/high-6 rather than a 4–5. The S4-duration error and the messy benefit arithmetic are enough, under a strict reading, to keep it well short of 8+.
