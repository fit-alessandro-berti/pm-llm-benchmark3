6.5

The delay-decomposition core is competent: formulas are max-based, the additive identity is correct, synchronization is treated as slack rather than an elapsed-time term, and the F07 double-counting example is exact. Case-level arithmetic is almost entirely right. That is not enough for a high score under a strict bar.

The most serious failure is in the counterfactual that the rest of the argument depends on. After cutting B’s queue by 50%, B remains last to finish in F03, F07, F09, and F12 (fork-join 55–60). F02 and F06 are ties, not A wins. Calling the new regime “A-dominant,” saying further B improvement is “nearly worthless,” and treating A’s 50-minute service as the binding tail constraint are all false. The tail cases F07 and F12 are still B-critical. Redesign 2 therefore points the next investment at the wrong branch.

A second clear error is in the same section: F10 is listed with F04/F08/F11 as gaining flow time “without any change to their own `join_ready`.” F10’s join moves by 5 minutes. The 8-minute flow saving is a mix of own-join and cascade, not a pure spillover.

Smaller defects compound this. “Policy fix (171)” is ranked as if it were a verified intervention; the residual gap is an unidentified leftover, which §7 later admits could be mandatory work. §7’s “constant upstream (20 min)” and “15-minute spacing” erase F01 (upstream 15; 10-minute gap to F02). Mean A sync of 11 minutes is used as runner-up evidence even though that mean includes the two zeros where A is critical. The specific claim that the performance DFG yields “A → B ≈ −43 min” is not derived from the supplied timestamps and reads as an unearned observation.

Definitions, worked cases F02/F04/F07/F11, the no-double-count argument, downstream masking on F11/CF1, and the limits section are strong. The grade is held down because the influence ranking after CF2 is wrong in a way that changes the recommended next step.
