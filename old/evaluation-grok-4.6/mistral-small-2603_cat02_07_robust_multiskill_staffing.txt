**3.4**

The submission follows the nine required headings and gestures at the right objects (offered load, three policies, U1–U5, fairness, PM instrumentation). That is almost the only thing it does well. Under a strict reading it is internally inconsistent, arithmetically wrong on the central capacity question, and the Python block fails the specification.

**Section 1.** Two objectives are named, but they are not cleanly operationalized. “Minimize P(any P1 > 120)” is not the same as “minimize P1 p95,” and the fairness objective mixes a min-max utilization criterion with a hard P1 p90 constraint without saying how conflicts are resolved. Staffing is listed as a decision variable even though the roster is given; the real decisions are eligibility, reservation, preemption, and parallelization. Overtime (30 minutes), end-of-shift unfinished work, and whether complex-tail draws are independent per activity or per case are not pinned down. The uniform-within-range assumption is acceptable and labeled.

**Section 2 is a material failure of process-mining / offered-load competence.** Diagnostic demand is not “M cases need Mechanical.” ME work consumes *both* Mechanical and Electrical; ES consumes Electrical and Software.

Weighted expected case counts from the block table are approximately M 9.8, E 11.1, S 7.9, ME 8.3, ES 5.0. Mechanical starts are therefore ~18, not 10.1; electrical starts are ~24, not 16; software starts are ~13, not 8.4. Those omissions cut diagnostic load by roughly a third to a half and make every later bottleneck claim unreliable.

Service-time expectations are also mishandled. Midpoints of ordinary and complex ranges are averaged *without* the complex-tail probability (Intake 8.75 vs a probability-weighted ~6.9 minutes; similar bias on other activities). U4’s “+20–40%” is overstated; doubling diagnostic tail probabilities raises means by roughly 12–16%.

Capacity is not resource-and-shift specific. All six people can do Intake, yet Intake is treated as Ada/Ben only. Ben’s Mechanical multiplier is 1.00 and is dropped from Mechanical capacity. Farah (everything, 08:00–12:00) and Ada’s slow Electrical (1.35) are essentially ignored. Breaks, Deepa’s 10:00 start, Emil’s 12:00 start, Farah’s noon exit, and the 16:00 collapse to Deepa+Emil then Emil alone are not turned into a time-block × skill matrix—the analysis the prompt asked for. “8 × 1.25 = 10 hours” and “6 × 8 × 1.2 = 57.6” are not valid availability calculations.

U2’s “173% electrical utilization” is an offered-load ratio computed from the already-wrong 8.9 hours, not a utilization. U3’s claim that software is “Emil only” is false (Farah is qualified). U1’s “~23% P1” is the 10:00–12:00 rate, not the 08:00–12:00 mix (~21%). Parallel vs sequential load, handover/setup as a fraction of capacity, and preemption waste are not quantified.

**Section 3.** The three policy sketches are directionally right (specialization / pool / reserved P1) but not implementable as written. Policy A assigns M only to Ada/Deepa and never says who does Intake or Validation; Ben and Farah vanish. Policy B’s SERPT score multiplies remaining time by 1.5 for P1, which *deprioritizes* P1 under a shortest-first rule—the opposite of the stated intent. Policy C’s “80% of Ada/Chen reserved” is not an operator rule (how is 80% enforced intra-shift?), and Chen cannot be 80% reserved for both P1-E and P1-S. Handover minimization is not a dispatch feature; it is a side effect. Overflow, parallelization, and preemption barely differ across policies, so the comparison is thinner than it looks.

**Section 4 vs Section 5 is a direct contradiction.** The table has Policy B better than C on mean delay and P1 SLA in *every* scenario, including U2 (B 60–75% vs C 55–70%) and U5 (B 75–85% vs C 70–82%). The recommendation then asserts that C keeps P1 SLA “above 80% even in U2/U5” and that “B dips below 75% in U2.” Both sentences are false on the author’s own numbers. Validation utilization in U4 (88–92%) also contradicts Section 2’s ~25% validation load. Utilization >100%, handover “120–220” without units, and fairness 0.85–0.88 have no definition. Later scenario blocks drop required columns (P2 SLA, fairness, handover). The figures are scenario-colored guesses, not outputs of the code, and they do not share a scale with the capacity section.

Because the robustness claim is inconsistent with the comparison table, the Section 5 recommendation is not a reasoned trade-off; it is an unearned preference for the hybrid label. The fallback “if reserved lane >90% for 30 minutes, switch to B” implicitly admits B is the stress policy, which undermines choosing C as primary.

**Section 6** is the strongest part: gaming of P1 labels, P3 starvation, skill erosion, preemption cascades, coordination, validation as a possible downstream bottleneck, and interpretability are all on-topic. Even here, several mitigations are generic and one (preempt only if <30 minutes remain) is unmotivated relative to the 10%-of-remaining penalty.

**Section 7** lists many of the required attributes but not a clean event schema (eligible set, planned vs actual, handover/setup as events, policy version, shock flag). Proposed APIs are wrong or non-existent (`pm4py.discover_conformance` for service-time drift, `pm4py.get_bottleneck`). The required trio—`format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`—is named only in passing, not designed around.

**Section 8 fails the contract.** The code encodes tables, then simulates only Intake. Arrivals are dumped per block (and U1 is multiplied twice). Cases with no free resource are popped and discarded. `case_data` is never filled but is read. Completion timestamps do `timedelta(minutes=datetime-datetime)`. Chen absence, U4 tails, U5’s 09:30–10:00 Intake outage, overtime, breaks-during-service, diagnostics, Validation, ME/ES fork-join, preemption, handover, and setup are not executed (helpers are dead). SERPT resamples service instead of using an expectation. There are no repeated seeds. The log is malformed (start = complete on the start row; only Intake). Required calls are absent: no `pm4py.format_dataframe`, no `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, no `get_service_time`, no `discover_handover_of_work_network`. Utilization is `n_events × ordinary_min × speed / 8`. `pivot(..., columns=None)` is invalid. This would not run and cannot support Section 4.

**Section 9** correctly says expectations-plus-ranges cannot identify preemption or handover effects, but the pilot (one week, staggered A/B across teams) does not protect P1 under U2/U5 and does not specify sample size, stop rules, or how to identify a shock day.

**Net.** Format compliance and a readable policy story cannot offset: (i) forgetting multi-activity case types in the load model; (ii) a recommendation that negates the comparison table; (iii) a non-executable scaffold that omits the process. Those are not nits. A nearly flawless answer is not in view.
