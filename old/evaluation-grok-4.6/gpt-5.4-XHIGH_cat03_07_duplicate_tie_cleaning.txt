**7.0**

A complete, well-structured pipeline that hits every required section and the hard constraints (do not drop `e05`/`e14`, do not treat activity+timestamp as identity, do not make ingestion primary, do not erase original timestamps, do not sell a total order as physical truth). The object-centric reasoning on `e03`/`e05`, `e08`/`e09`, and especially `e13`/`e14` versus `have_common_object=True` is the strongest part. It is not nearly flawless. Several implementation and consistency faults are real, not cosmetic, and a strict reading has to mark them down.

**What is actually good.** The decision tree matches the six required terminal classes and keeps merge authority behind hard evidence rather than collapsing the whole log. The event table covers every mandated pair and correctly splits `e06`/`e07` into conservative retain-and-qualify versus optional transition merge. Timestamp policy separates semantic rules from deterministic fallbacks and states the microseconds hazard in the right terms: a synthetic total order becomes a fake sub-second story if it is written before the partial order is fixed. Conservative retention of 15 events and ~39 E2O relations is arithmetically right (17−2 events; 44−3−2 relations after unioning `e03`/`e04` and `e11`/`e16`). The naive-library collapse to ~10 events / ~28 E2O relations is also counted correctly, and the explanation that `e13` and `e14` share `O1`/`V1` while differing on `M1`/`M2` is exactly the point the prompt is testing. Sensitivity names more than six concrete pattern shifts, and the failure-mode list includes all eight required cases with usable safeguards.

**Decision tree and pair table.** These are the cleanest sections, but they already leak. The tree treats “same physical event” as an automatic merge. That is defensible for `e03`/`e04` (reconciliation report) and arguable for `e11`/`e16` (same invoice issuance), yet fact 6 stresses that ERP and billing are authoritative for *different* attributes. A strictly conservative tree should allow “retain both with a shared business-key link” as a first-class terminal, not jump to merge. The table then promises both payload hashes for `e03`/`e04` and an invoice-authority map for `e11`/`e16`; the code keeps a single `payload_hash` and sets authority to the already-canonical source, so the merge policy on the page is not the merge policy that runs. `e06`/`e07` is handled honestly as medium-confidence ambiguity. `e08`/`e09` as a harmonization collision is correct and important.

**Ordering.** The partial order `{e03, e05} < e06 < e07`, `e08 < e09 < e10`, and `e12 < e13` with `e15` after payment messages is the right semantic skeleton. Two problems remain. First, `e06 < e07` is treated as semantic in the intended 10:00 order, then immediately encoded with index-based microseconds, which is the same class of false narrative the section warns against if an analyst later reads pack-to-summary latency as a business duration. Second, the implemented `ordering_key` injects lexicographic `source` (and then a poorly parsed `ingested_at`) *before* the stable `eid` fallback. That silently picks `e05` before `e03` because `"SCANNER" < "WMS"`, and `"ERP" < "PSP"` for invoice versus authorize, after the prose said those pairs are only deterministically ordered. The principle is understood; the key reintroduces an arbitrary story. `ingested_at` values are time-of-day strings; `to_datetime(..., utc=True)` is brittle and, on failure, dumps ties to `Timestamp.max`. After merging `e03`/`e04` into the WMS row, the SCANNER `source_seq` 551 that would have ordered the I1 scan against `e05` (552) is discarded and never discussed.

**Conservative versus aggressive design.** Scenario split is the right architecture. Evidence-based merge of only `e06`/`e07` → 14 events, with damage framed as lost ERP checkpoint / latency observability, is a fair trade-off. Mechanically aggressive merge is correctly treated as a sensitivity instrument, not a cleaning step. The write-up’s “naive raw log → ~10 events” path is not the path in the code, which calls `refine_labels` first. After refinement, `e06`/`e07`, `e08`/`e09`, and `e15` no longer share labels, so the library merge would more likely yield ~13 events and a different damage profile (still collapsing `e03`/`e05` and `e13`/`e14`). That is a material inconsistency between the claimed sensitivity case and the executable case.

**PM4Py implementation.** The block satisfies the checklist on paper: deep copies, original columns, candidate-group pre-check, `ocel_drop_duplicates`, warned `ocel_merge_duplicates`, sort-by-column, timedelta only after an order, custom verified-pair helper, referential checks, `ocel_raw` left intact. Several of those uses are wrong or unsafe.

- `ocel_drop_duplicates` is justified as “remove duplicate E2O rows after verified merges.” That is not what the function does. It drops *events* that match on activity/timestamp/(typically) object set. The helper already `drop_duplicates` on `(eid, oid)`. The library call is therefore either a no-op or, if the implementation keys only on activity+timestamp, a constraint violation that can delete `e05` or `e14`. A PM4Py-aware answer must state the match key and why `e03`/`e05` and `e13`/`e14` survive.
- `semantic_merge = deepcopy(conservative)` copies an OCEL that has already received `ocel_add_index_based_timedelta`, then applies timedelta again. Conservative and evidence-based performance numbers are therefore not comparable, which undercuts section 7.
- `merge_verified_pair` never stores the dropped event snapshot; `source_eids` plus a conflict JSON cannot reconstruct scanner-only or billing-only attributes. `events.loc[keep_mask, keep_row.index] = keep_row.values` is alignment-fragile. O2O integrity is checked on a single object-id column.
- Candidates are printed and then ignored. Quarantine exists in the tree and nowhere in the pipeline.
- Hard-coded eids and a closed activity-rank map are acceptable for this fixture, but the rank map is itself a hidden total-order policy competing with the partial-order write-up.

**Sensitivity and audit.** Directionally right, locally thin. Order/item/payment flattenings and the eight named edges are the useful core. ET–OT and OTG stay qualitative (“fall slightly,” “stay faithful”) with no worked weights. There is no remark that refined labels change the activity vocabulary, so raw versus conservative DFGs are not the same graph. Provenance schema on the page is complete; reversibility is overclaimed. Keeping `ocel_raw` is the actual undo. Exploding `source_event_ids` without full pre-merge rows is not mechanical reconstruction.

**Net.** This is a strong exam answer with the right conservative posture and the right object-discriminating instincts. Strictly, it is a 7: the design is sound enough to implement, but table/code contradictions, a mis-specified library dedup step, a double timedelta, and deterministic keys that re-encode the false story the author warned about keep it well short of nearly flawless.
