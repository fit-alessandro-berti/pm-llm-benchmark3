**6.1**

The write-up is formally complete and methodologically above average: it reconstructs eligibility sets, compares competing rules at decision epochs, labels assumptions, treats hybrids and observational equivalence as first-class issues, and follows the required seven-section format. Those strengths are real. They are not enough. Under a strict reading the analysis contains several material errors, internal contradictions, and overfitted reconciliations. Those defects hit the core diagnostic claims, not just the prose.

## What is actually strong

Decision-set reasoning is the right unit of analysis, and the author mostly uses it. QA T1 (A03 over A02 and A04 at 08:20) is a clean appointment identification: FIFO, non-preemptive priority, and SPT all fail, and only the due appointment explains the choice. Later QA starts (A02 before A04; A05 before A08/A07; A07 before A08) correctly kill pure priority and pure SPT. QD’s D05 wait is correctly treated as a hard skill partition rather than a priority/SPT mystery. QB is correctly refused as a single rule. Section 4’s extra-observable tests are the right kind of test. Section 6 is well prioritized. The synthesis that FIFO violations are not automatically priority is the sentence the task was written to elicit.

Confidence numbers are at least attempting to track ambiguity rather than rhetoric. That is better than most answers of this type.

## Material errors

**1. QC 10:32 is a false SPT confirmation.**  
C06 arrives at 10:25 with `est_min = 6`. C04 starts at 10:32 with `est_min = 22`. Scan delay is at most two minutes, so C06 is eligible. Dynamic SPT predicts C06, not C04. The diagnosis table omits C06 from the 10:32 waiting set and calls C02–C04 “ordered by `est_min` exactly.” The decision table then lists C06 as waiting and still writes SPT = C04. That is both a factual error and an internal contradiction. It is the load-bearing episode for “QC is SPT,” and it does not support that claim.

A more careful reading is that the first three jobs look like SPT on the *opening* backlog (or a next-job commitment made at 10:18, before C06 existed), after which C05 is an SLA insert and C01 vs C07 is aging/FIFO. The author never considers pre-commitment or static-at-gate SPT, then invents two later exceptions to save a rule that already failed at 10:32. Section 3 therefore lists the wrong challenges. Confidence 50 is still too high for the evidence they actually have.

**2. QB’s “strongest supporting episode” is overclaimed and self-contradicted.**  
Section 1 says only preemptive priority predicts the 09:16 interruption. Section 2 says remaining-time SPT would also preempt (15 < ~31). Both cannot stand. The interruption reason (“urgent arrival”) is the actual priority-supporting fact; uniqueness versus SRPT/SEPT is not established. The second stop is worse for the primary label: B01 is pulled at 09:48 for B03 (priority 2), not for waiting B05 (priority 1). That is not “preemptive priority for p1.” Resume-to-head is also violated by the same interrupt. The hybrid story is directionally plausible; the primary label and the uniqueness claim are not.

**3. QD’s D-F idle is mis-explained.**  
At 11:36 D-F is free. D05 (30) does not fit before the 12:00 meeting; D10 (12) does (11:36–11:48). Non-preemptive meeting look-ahead predicts *start D10*, not idle. Observed idle therefore requires a different mechanism: hold D-F for uniquely qualified D05, assign D10 to D-X, or some other reservation. The author treats “won’t start a job that overruns the meeting” and “reservation for D05” as one exception. They are not. The contradicting episode is well chosen; the rescue is wrong.

The D-X 11:20–11:30 idle is the complementary miss. If D06 is held until 11:30, work-conserving FIFO among the rest predicts D07/D08/D09 at 11:20. The resource waits. That *discriminates* appointment reservation from FIFO. Section 1 still says the two are “indistinguishable” because at 11:30 D06 is also oldest eligible. Instant-of-start equivalence is not path equivalence.

**4. QA’s 08:30–08:32 idle is given an impossible data-quality explanation.**  
A02 has been present since 08:04. Arrival-scan delay cannot explain a two-minute gap after A03. Calibration is 08:52–08:58 and cannot create that gap either. Section 1 nonetheless ties the gap to calibration “confounded with 2-min scan delay,” and assumption A1 repeats the same reconciliation. A03 “holding until 08:20” is also over-read: A1 is busy with A01 until 08:20, so early insertion was not feasible. The identifying fact is the 08:20 *choice*, not the wait.

## Smaller but non-trivial issues

- “FIFO order correlates with reverse-priority” in QA is sloppy. A04 is served in an order consistent with both FIFO and priority; A08 is the actual priority violation.
- B07: section 3 says the missed 10:30 appointment is “actually no violation” because the resource was busy, then the 10:28 row shows B04 chosen while B07 was available. Those sentences conflict.
- Assumption A2 (estimates treated as noise-free) is empirically true in *all four* queues, not just QC; section 5 still entertains strategic noise more than the realized=estimate identity warrants.
- Skill-vs-FIFO “almost perfectly confounded” in QD sits next to multiple within-partition choices that *do* separate FIFO from priority/SPT (D04 over D08; D07 over D10). The identifiability paragraph argues against itself.
- The Python fragment does not reconstruct any decision set. Harmless, but it does not earn the “self-contained analysis” gesture it makes.

## Scoring rationale

A nearly flawless answer would keep the same skeleton and get the eligibility arithmetic right: C06 at 10:32, remaining-time SPT at 09:16, D10 fitting in the D-F window, D-X’s pre-appointment idle, and A02 already being queued at 08:30. This answer’s architecture is that answer; its reconstructions are not. Four queues, four defects in the episodes used to name the policy. Format compliance and genuine PM competence keep it well above a failing reconstruction. The QC SPT claim and the QD idle rescue are enough to bar anything in the 7–8 range under the stated strictness.

The grade is 6.1, not lower, because the author still does the thing the rubric values: compare rules on reconstructed waiting sets, refuse fake certainty on QB/QC, and ask for snapshots that would actually break the remaining equivalences. It is 6.1, not higher, because several of those comparisons are computed incorrectly.
