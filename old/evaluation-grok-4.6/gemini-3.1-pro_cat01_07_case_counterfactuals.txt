**5.4**

The answer follows the required skeleton and gets the central conceptual points right: K-337 is a decision-policy change, not a pure time cut; four-eyes and final-compliance-after-final-assessment are mostly respected; queue forecasts are sometimes treated as uncertain; K-411’s earlier readiness need not mean earlier completion; and the 100-case discussion of C1 reservation, D1/D2 substitution, and false concurrency is the strongest part of the write-up. Under a strict reading that is not enough. The temporal mechanics are repeatedly sloppy, several causal claims in the capacity table are wrong, and ranges are internally inconsistent.

**Feasibility model.** The first fence mixes a partial order with conditionals (`Assessment < Compliance Review (if …)`), which the prompt asked to separate. The order omits `Originals Receipt`, `Escalation Advice`, `Request More Info`, rework `Document Check`/`Assessment`, and the distinction between preliminary and final compliance. Rule 3 concurrency is never stated as a relation. The second block is closer, but it still drops that Identity may precede originals, that a preliminary screen does not replace the final review, and that Compliance as well as Assessment may issue `Request More Info`. The timing/routing/policy split is directionally correct; calling K-411 “timing only” and “no resource change” is acceptable, though the workaround is also a logging/control change.

**K-104.** This is the weakest case derivation. Monday 08:00 C1 wait is 20 h and Tuesday 08:00 is still 16 h. The main narrative nonetheless has S1 and C1 start preliminary work on Monday and has final compliance start “immediately” after Assessment on Tuesday evening. A 1–3 h reduction in *service* time does not move a *start* time out of a 16–20 h backlog. The reserved slot is also double-counted: the same scarce C1 capacity cannot be spent on an incomplete Monday packet and still be free at Tuesday 19:10 unless other work is displaced. Best case “C1 finishes preliminary work on Monday, final review 2 h Tuesday night, ~20 h saved” is incompatible with the snapshot the author later cites as reversing evidence. The table range (6–24 h saved, decision unchanged) excludes the author’s own worst case of zero saving plus wasted C1 time. The path also drops post-Assessment escalation as if the Monday packet substitutes for it.

**K-219.** Originals at 17:30 plus a 5 h D2 forecast implies a start near 22:30, not a vague “Monday evening,” and D2’s *standard* band is 4–8 h. Using 8–14 h silently reclassifies handwritten standard work as complex corporate work; the stated complication is clarification/rework risk, not a different service-time family. Table worst case is +10 h; the derivation is +10–24 h. That is not calibrated uncertainty, it is an inconsistent estimate.

**K-337.** Policy recognition is good, and dropping the second loop is the right minimal path. The Friday C1 start is asserted, not derived: Thursday C1 wait is 5 h, Friday is unobserved, and review of *incomplete* evidence is not the 3–6 h “complete evidence” band. The text never asks whether C1, who may also issue `Request More Info`, is bound by the same prohibition. “Immediately … Friday afternoon … 5 full days” is a point story dressed as a range.

**K-411.** Rule 3 is applied correctly, and the Assessment-bottleneck caveat is the right idea. The clocks are not. Tuesday 08:00 D2 wait is 4 h and I2 wait is 2 h; D2 does not start at 11:30 “alongside I2.” Finishing “around 16:00 instead of 19:30” inherits that false start. Table savings of 2–4 h contradict the derivation’s best case of ~18 h. Reverse evidence that I2 and D2 might be the same person is idle: they are defined as separate pools.

**Capacity table and rollout.** Two cells are simply false. Faster final C1 cannot shrink the Assessment queue, because Assessment precedes Compliance. Parallelizing K-411 does not shrink the Document queue; it materializes D2 work earlier and is more likely to fatten that queue and then Assessment. K-337’s “queue likely to grow: None” ignores Friday bunching onto the single compliance reviewer. The 100-case prose is better, but it inherits these mistakes and overclaims that K-337 is the only net-capacity intervention.

**Ranking, pilots, synthesis, assumptions.** Ranking K-337 above K-104 after stating that rank must include quality/compliance is inconsistent: an irreversible high-risk decision-policy change is treated as more robust than a scarce-resource reservation that a small pilot could test. Calling a K-104 pilot low information value is wrong; a limited early-packet trial is exactly how one would learn whether prelim work saves 1–3 h without gridlocking C1. Assumption 2 (“strictly reduces final compliance time”) overstates the service-time guidance. The synthesis answers the four required questions in roughly the right length, but it repeats the same overconfident K-411 robustness claim.

The piece looks well-structured and conceptually informed. It is not a careful counterfactual. Queue snapshots are used as decoration, several downstream times are invented rather than propagated, and the capacity table misstates what moves. That is why it cannot sit in the high band.
