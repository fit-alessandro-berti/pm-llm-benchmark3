**7.4**

The final write-up is strong on the dimensions that matter most: it treats the fitness drop as overdetermined, uses the staged 32→58→81→89 recovery correctly, refuses to equate log moves with misconduct, keeps missing `Compliance Check` events live as an unresolved residue, and flags that an optional sign-off tree can buy fitness by overgeneralizing. Format compliance is mostly exact. It is not nearly flawless.

**Causal graph errors.** `UI_release -> manual_override_semantics` is chronologically false: override logging is the 12 August patch, not the 31 May label change. `policy_change -> normative_model` states the wrong mechanism; the policy changed the real process, and *failure to update* left the tree obsolete. `missing_compliance_events -> possible_compliance_bypass` collapses two competing explanations into one directed cause. `true_process -> recorded_order` is too direct given the ETL story already in the graph.

**Hypothesis table.** The ranking is defensible, but the required distinction between “cause of the measured drop” and “real operational concern” is only implied, not operationalized in the table. Label change at 92 and technical events at 88 are too sharply separated given similar fitness gains (26 vs 23 points). The 30 June–July traces that still use `Classify` plus `Queue Routed` are underused as transition/logging evidence.

**Code.** `pm4py.conformance_alignments` and `pm4py.apply_alignments` are not the usual high-level names (`conformance_diagnostics_alignments`). The fallback uses `EventTrace`, which is the wrong PM4Py class (`Trace`). Perfect-alignment counting can double-count when both `cost==0` and `fitness==1` are present. The generated `<log>` has no XES namespace. Source-sequence repair is caveated but never even stubbed, so the code cannot reproduce the most important ordering result in the alignment table.

**Smaller defects.** Ledger row 1 does not name a hypothesis. Several “supports/weakens” cells are commentary rather than hypothesis IDs. Phase 4 of the diagnostic program is thinner on actual PM4Py artifacts than phases 1–3. The 180–280 and 250–400 word constraints appear to be met, and the six governance rules match the required topics.

Substance is clearly above a mid-range answer; the causal slips, API/executability issues, and incomplete “drop vs concern” discrimination are enough, under a strict bar, to keep it well short of 9–10.
