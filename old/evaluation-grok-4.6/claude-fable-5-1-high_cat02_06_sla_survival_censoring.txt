**6.6**

This is a strong, estimand-first survival analysis that meets the required structure and is clearly more competent than a typical completed-case write-up. It is not nearly flawless. Under strict grading, two classes of error keep it well below the top band: an apples-to-oranges competing-risk comparison that is central to the task, and a repeated miscount of cohort-B / P3 cases that omits **S17**.

**What is actually good.** Time origin, horizon, and the distinction among right censoring, competing exits, and already-observed 72h breaches are defined before any estimator. E1–E4 (plus landmark and RMST) are mapped to decisions. The four illustrative cases are encoded correctly under those estimands. Transfers are given three real assumptions, not a single default. Open cases are not dropped; open-below-72 vs open-at/above-72 is the right split. Immortal time for `Escalate`, leakage from `last_event_gap_h` (identically 0 on closed rows), and post-treatment `rework_count` are handled as process-aware issues rather than as baseline covariates. The AJ numbers that can be recomputed from the table are right: CIF_resolve(72) ≈ 0.304 so E1 ≈ 0.696; E3 ≈ 0.380; cause-specific masses 0.140 / 0.101 / 0.075; E1 bounds [0.562, 0.719]; transfer-as-success bounds [0.500, 0.656]; P3 E1 = 0.80 with no unknown cases; landmark crude bounds 0.60–0.76 at 24h and 0.75–0.875 at 48h. The code path matches the spec (in-memory table, two estimands, three transfer encodings, open cases retained, PM4Py variants/prefixes, no duration timestamps invented).

**1. Central competing-risks error (Section 3).** Overall, `1 − KM_net(72) = 0.361` vs `F_res(72) = 0.304` is the correct comparison (net resolution vs CIF). The P1 sentence immediately after is not: `1 − KM_net(72) = 0.27` vs `E1 = 0.75`. Those are different estimands. Net KM on P1 gives P(resolve by 72) ≈ 0.27, hence P(not resolve) ≈ 0.73, which is close to competing-risk E1 = 0.75. The “extreme distortion” is almost entirely the transfer coding (failure vs success vs censor), which Section 6 later gets right (P1 0.75 vs 0.375–0.50). Section 3 therefore overclaims the KM-vs-AJ effect by comparing a resolution probability to a breach probability. That is a serious slip in a competing-risks task.

**2. Systematic omission of S17.** S17 is B, P3, `Submit>Screen>AutoCheck>Review>Resolve` at 32h (on-time resolve, AutoCheck present but not first). The answer repeatedly writes as if this case does not exist:
- “13/17 B cases” AutoCheck-first, then S9 uses n = 12 vs 5.
- “All 4 non-compliant B cases” / list `S20, S23, S26, S30`, dropping S17.
- “S02 is the only P3 resolved within 72 h” — false; S17 also is.
Section 3 then uses that truncated list to say non-compliant B “look bad,” which is selection of the failures. S17 is exactly the case that breaks “no AutoCheck-first ⇒ bad P3.” Using AutoCheck-anywhere (13) in one place and AutoCheck-first (12) in another is not a typo; it is two exposure definitions. P3 E1 = 0.80 remains numerically correct (2/10 on-time resolves), so the rate was computed with S17 while the prose was not.

**3. Identification overclaim on the tail.** E2 is defined as `F_res(∞) − F_res(72)` and reported as 0.301 / eventual incidence 0.605. With nine censored cases that quantity is not identified without a tail assumption. Section 6 shows this (0.46–0.47 if long gaps are stalls), but the main results still present 0.605 as if it were on the same footing as the 72h CIF. Related: the SAP promises case-level bootstrap intervals; the code never produces them and leans on Manski-style bounds instead. The bounds are the better object here; the plan and the artifact disagree.

**4. Landmark oversold.** The 24h/48h figures are finite-population bounds on a binary 72h outcome among `{t > L}`, not a landmark survival or state-occupation probability. That is defensible given missing intermediate times, but Section 7 calls them “landmark conditional probabilities” and “the natural alert thresholds.” State-at-L is discussed and then unused in the numbers, which the authors elsewhere admit cannot be reconstructed.

**5. Smaller inconsistencies.** S4’s “E1 = 0.639 (= 1 − 0.361)” is easy to misread against Section 3’s `1 − KM_net`. Stratified RMST “47–52 h” is asserted without a displayed calculation. The scenario’s escalation-policy change is mostly collapsed into AutoCheck-first; escalation is treated well as a time-varying state, not as the second part of the rollout. `F_res(∞)` and “assumption-free” are slightly looser than the actual identifying assumptions (young open cases resolve immediately vs never by 72; stalled gaps never resolve).

**Net.** The design is the right design: AJ/CIF primary, KM only for any-disposition, transfers as a modelled cause, process features time-ordered, naive 4/13 rejected with case IDs. The grade is held down because the two mistakes a judge was explicitly told to punish — muddling censoring/competing-risk estimands, and not using the supplied rows faithfully — both occur, and S17 is missed more than once. A nearly flawless answer would have compared 0.27 to CIF_res (or 0.73 to E1) on P1, used one compliance definition, and not claimed a unique on-time P3 resolve that the table contradicts.
