**5.8**

The final write-up is complete, on-format, and operationally literate. It is not close to flawless. Several featured calculations are wrong, the three policies are specified much more carefully than they are implemented, and the comparison table is not produced by the model that is supposed to support it.

**What holds up.** Assumptions A1–A10 are labelled, ranges are mapped to uniforms/midpoints, and ME/ES parallel vs sequential is treated as a real trade-off (elapsed-time saving vs two-resource occupancy, 8-minute coordination, 7+5 handover/team-move). Two objectives exist: an SLA/tail loss and a min-max robustness/fairness loss. The policy write-ups are implementable on paper (eligibility, dispatch score, reservation, preemption, parallelization, overflow). Bottleneck migration across U1–U5 is directionally right (morning surge, E/S spike, Chen/software hole, tail inflation, combined intake outage). The recommendation is Hybrid with a Pooling crisis mode, not “whatever wins on base-case mean delay.” Sections 6–7 and 9 are strong: gaming, P3 starvation, skill erosion, Validation as a second bottleneck, event attributes, PM4Py use with a correct parallel-branch caveat, shadow/pilot/stopping rules.

**Material calculation errors.** The block-pressure table is the audit core of §2 and it is wrong in more than rounding.

- Deepa starts at 10:00. 10:00–12:00 capacity is Ada+Ben+Chen+Deepa+Farah = **600**, not 480. That is a 25% capacity miss in a peak block.
- 10:00–12:00 electrical demand is E+ME+ES = 60% of 9 cases, not 45%. Load is about **675** resource-minutes, not 620. Correct pressure is ~1.13, not 1.29.
- 12:00–14:00 and 14:00–16:00 loads are also low (~524 vs 500, ~751 vs 710).

Daily midpoint load (~3138 vs ~2490 contracted, ~2670 with OT) is internally consistent and is the best part of the capacity work. That makes the Deepa/10–12 error worse: the featured time-block story and the daily story were not reconciled.

**Internal inconsistency.** Midpoint load already exceeds capacity, including overtime. Under that view, base P1 SLA in the 80–97% band for Pooling/Hybrid is optimistic, and U5 ranges so wide they are almost unfalsifiable (P90 480–1400). §4 is not filled from §8; it is a separate guessed table. The code would print different numbers. The prompt allows ranges, not two disconnected metric systems.

**Policy vs code gap.** §3 defines distinct operating rules. §8 is one Monte Carlo engine with coefficient knobs.

- Hybrid **reservation** (hold Chen/Emil for P1) is not in the code.
- Pooling **dispatch score** is not in the code; assignment is weighted random sampling by speed/availability.
- Specialization is skill-restricted sampling, not fixed per-resource queues.
- Waiting is `wait_from_util` on arrival-block load, not a queue. There is no busy-until, no calendar-constrained processing, no overnight wait from 16:00/18:00/20:00 close until the next open shift — which matters for P2 (8h) and P3 (24h).
- U5’s 09:30–10:00 Intake outage is modelled as **−30 minutes of total block capacity**, not lost Intake capacity across all on-shift Intake resources (~4×30).
- Preemption is a probability times `0.1 × 0.5 × diagnostic load`, not an interrupt of an active diagnostic with a 10% remaining-time restart.

A scaffold is allowed if omissions are named. The code comments the remaining-time hack and PM4Py overlap; it does not clearly say that Hybrid/Pooling as specified were not implemented, or that delay is a congestion formula rather than a schedule.

**Smaller issues.** U2 “proportions renormalized” is more naturally a mix shift at **fixed** block volume; doubling E/ES counts and raising arrivals is flagged but still used. Objective weights are symbolic. Fairness `1 − std/mean` is a CV transform, not a utilization-cap/fairness constraint. Setup is scaled by 0.4/0.7/1.0 instead of a 0-or-5-minute event. SPEC/POOL/HYBRID therefore differ less than the prose claims.

**Net.** This is a competent operations/process-mining essay with the right heading contract, the right robust-policy instinct, and a runnable-looking PM4Py scaffold. It fails a strict audit on the capacity table, on fidelity between policy and code, and on grounding the scenario table in the model. That is well below “nearly flawless.”
