5.3

The answer has the right skeleton and the right institutional stance (pilot first, do not trust averages, East ≠ North/South), but it is not a reliable forecast. Under a strict reading it is a mid-tier structured essay with broken quantitative language, systematic baseline errors, and a PM4Py section that would not run.

**Presentation and recoverability.** Directional claims are repeatedly stripped of verbs and operators (`** from 4.0h to 2.5h**`, `Shared queue handovers ****`, `( local specialist handover loops)`). A reader cannot tell increase from decrease without reconstructing missing arrows. That is not cosmetic. In a forecasting memo the sign of each effect *is* the claim.

**Mechanism map.** The four actions are the right ones, and East vs North/South is named. What is missing is an actual causal map: action → operating mechanism → queueing/organizational consequence → *observable PM measure*. “Nominal handover reduction” is listed as if it were an outcome rather than a *designed* change that can be offset by central queueing. There is no utilization–delay nonlinearity (Legal/Risk already at 91–93%), no Little’s-law WIP story, and no distinction between fewer *nominal* handovers and fewer *effective* waits. Resilience is treated as a slogan, not as the pooling-vs-single-bottleneck tradeoff that the redesign actually creates.

**Scenario arithmetic and logic.** Several numbers are internally inconsistent or attached to the wrong baseline.

- Management’s *post-change estimates* (4.0h TSS wait, 10.5h legal/risk wait, 3.0h shared approval) are repeatedly used as if they were the current baseline. Current embedded-tech wait is 3.2h; legal/risk is 9.6h. Optimistic “improvement from 4.0h to 2.5h” therefore describes beating a *proposal*, not beating *today*.
- East’s **90th** percentile (22.8d) is repeatedly labeled **99th**. Tail claims are therefore unanchored.
- Optimistic legal/risk wait *falls* “due to broader detection.” Broader detection raises arrivals into a queue already near saturation. That rationale is backwards.
- Adverse “cross-team **20%** (from 6 to 7.2)” cannot be a 20% reduction and a rise to 7.2 at once.
- After merger, “E_Tech utilization 82% → 88%” is not a well-defined object. The role is pooled. Adverse East harm needed a different mechanism (shared-queue age, priority inversion, lost local triage).
- Optimistic still has local-expertise rework and CSAT worsening. Residual harm is intellectually honest, but then the scenario is not “optimistic” so much as “average improves, the thing we should care about does not.”

The three-scenario frame is correct. The numbers inside it are not a disciplined sensitivity analysis; they are loosely invented deltas.

**Indicators.** The leading/lagging split is the strongest table in the memo, and Gini-on-cycle-time is a real fairness idea. The PM4Py *bindings* are often wrong. `get_service_time` is processing time, not handover wait. `discover_working_together_network` is not queue depth. `discover_organizational_roles` is not utilization. `filter_event_attribute_values` on `priority` is not SLA miss rate. `filter_variants` / `get_variants` are not local-expertise filters. `discover_subcontracting_network` is not “ambiguous handover paths.” Several leading indicators that the log actually supports are absent: shared-queue WIP and age, referral-rate drift, disappearance of the local caseworker↔tech loop, resource-similarity collapse, variant entropy, `reassignment_count` tails, `handover_reason` mix.

**Hidden risks.** The seven bullets cover the required themes and correctly refuse average cycle time as a sufficient statistic. Quality is uneven: tail “doubling to 45d” is unearned; local-knowledge and learning are near-duplicates; customer-segment / language fairness and absence-shock resilience are thinner than the prompt demanded; `discover_performance_dfg(..., percentile=99)` is not a real API.

**Pilot.** Pilot-first is the only defensible recommendation, and North+East vs South is a recognizable difference-in-differences idea. Identification is sloppy. Once N_Tech and E_Tech are pooled you cannot keep a clean *case-level* “local specialists only” control in those teams. South is a reasonable team-level control for North, not for East. Twelve weeks is short for 90th-percentile cycle times of 16–23 days and almost useless for skill atrophy. Guardrails are underspecified (“cross-team >30%” of what?) and disagree with Section 6 (10% vs 12% rework). South CSAT is listed as a pause trigger even though South is the control. Supervisor removal and the already-saturated legal/risk queue are not piloted as first-class risks.

**Recommendation.** Directionally correct: do not full-rollout; do not infer success from early averages; do not let East’s relief hide North/South knowledge loss. The hybrid fallback is the one operationally serious idea in the memo. Triggers are copied rather than derived from power or from the current distributions.

**PM4Py sketch.** This section should have been decisive and is the largest technical failure. Required surfaces that never appear as real calls: `format_dataframe`, `discover_dfg` / `get_variants` / `filter_variants`, `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity`, `discover_organizational_roles`, `get_rework_cases_per_activity`, SLA-miss construction, filters on `required_specialty` and `customer_segment`. Invented functions (`get_handover_frequency`, `get_handover_wait_times`, `get_resource_utilization`, `insert_case_service_time`) and invented kwargs (`attributes=[...]`) are used as if they existed. SNA outputs are NetworkX graphs, not DataFrames you boolean-index. `working_together` is used as utilization. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is assumed to create a `service` column containing the string `"shared"`. The cutoff date is an arbitrary 2024 timestamp. The sketch would not compare before/after on the quantities the rest of the memo claims to monitor.

**Net.** Right outline, right refusal to rubber-stamp the redesign, some genuine organizational insight (East vs North/South, hybrid retain-local-for-complex, do not read averages). Wrong baselines, broken signs, weak queueing logic, unusable measurement code. That is a 5, not a 7.
