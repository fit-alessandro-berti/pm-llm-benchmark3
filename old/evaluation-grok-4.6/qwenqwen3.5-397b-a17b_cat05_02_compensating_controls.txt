**6.2**

The answer is structurally complete and does the most important intellectual work: it does not treat four-eyes form as the objective, and the AP-203 / AP-204 / AP-205 / AP-208 / AP-209 calls are directionally right. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of the spec, several analytical inconsistencies and a clearly non-compliant code section keep the score well below the top band.

**What is actually good**

Case differentiation is the strongest part. AP-203 is correctly treated as a real ED-1 success (token before prepare, amount under the cap, clean AV-2, independent pre-release MG-4, same-day audit). AP-204 is correctly failed despite distinct users, because a fabricated receipt plus a repeated pair/shared workstation attacks the independence objective. AP-205 is correctly `LOSS_PREVENTED_BEFORE_COMPLETION`. AP-202 and AP-208 are correctly failed for independence, timing, coverage, and recoverability reasons rather than for amount alone. AP-209 as `INDETERMINATE` is the right refusal to equate “no defect found” with “objective met.” The hybrid reading of post-release / pre-settlement manager review is conceptually sound. The “same expected loss is not enough” paragraph is one of the few places that actually answers the judge’s scoring rubric.

**Material analytical flaws**

AP-207 is internally contradictory. The rationale says “ED-1 valid” and the facts satisfy all seven ED-1 conditions, including MG-4 *before settlement*, which is what ED-1 actually requires. The row still says `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` because Section 2 quietly rewrote “fully equivalent” as “manager before `Release_Payment`.” That is a stricter private standard than the stated rule, and it is not applied consistently (AP-203 is MET for a stronger timing profile; AP-207 is demoted even though the governing permission is met).

Section 1 does not do what it claims. The five threat rows are reasonable, but the control mapping is sloppy: AV-2 is over-credited as preventing fraudulent initiation; RA-7 is almost never treated as partly corrective inside the recall window; ED-1 is labeled “mitigate / neutral / fail / risk” instead of prevent–detect–correct–does-not-address. Shared-account / non-human actor risk from FEP-1 is missing.

Section 2 lists four equivalence buckets but does not systematically test the required dimensions. Evidence quality and collusion resistance are not first-class criteria. Overrideability and recoverability appear only as asides. The test therefore cannot actually decide borderline cases; the later table has to improvise.

Section 5 repeatedly conflates a *possible* loss mechanism with what the case file says happened. AP-201 is described as “user error (wrong amount/vendor)” even though the facts say the vendor and amount were correct. AP-208 is framed as fraud/error exposure even though the payment was legitimate; the real residual issue is unverifiable bank ownership at release time plus missed recall. AP-206 is reduced to “emergency abuse (non-urgent)” despite an outage documented at 12:40; the live issue is token sequencing, which the prompt itself flags as possibly a logging delay.

Corrective capacity is too thin to support the classifications. Several rows say “High” or “None” without tying that to the 24-hour recall window, the presence or absence of a `Bank_Settlement` event, or whether RA-7 actually still had a stop/recall lever. AP-204 and AP-202 have no settlement event in the log; the write-up does not notice.

**Code section fails the spec**

This is the largest single deduction. The prompt asked for executable evidence-generation code, not a sketch.

- After renaming columns only inside the `HAS_PM4PY` branch, the fallback path still indexes `case_id` / `activity` / `resource`. If `pm4py` is absent, the script crashes.
- `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` is not actually used as specified. The result is stuffed into `isin(...)` as if it were a list of case IDs; in real pm4py it returns a log or DataFrame. Polarity (keep violations vs keep compliant cases) is a known version split and is not explained.
- Required evidence flags are incomplete. There is no emitted AV-coverage flag and no bank-change flag. “Emergency eligibility” is just `TOKEN_ACTIVE` / `NO_TOKEN`, which would mark AP-208 eligible even though amount, bank-change, and AV-2 all fail ED-1.
- Repeated pairs are hardcoded to `['AP-204', 'AP-209']` instead of being computed from the log.
- The case-facts frame invents fields the source table does not give, including a settlement lag for AP-204 (no settlement event) and AP-202.

Those are not polish issues. They mean Section 7 does not do the job it was asked to do.

**Net**

A competent control-objective review with the right instincts on the hard cases, undermined by an inconsistent ED-1 standard, an incomplete sufficiency test, some fact-inverting counterfactuals, and a code block that misses mandatory flags and would not run cleanly. That is a mid-six, not a high score.
