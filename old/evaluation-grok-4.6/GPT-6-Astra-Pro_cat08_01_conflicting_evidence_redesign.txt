**8.4**

This is a strong, evidence-grounded answer with real process-mining literacy. It is not nearly flawless. Several design, targeting, and presentation flaws keep it well short of a top score.

**What it gets right.** The diagnosis treats the frequent fast path as genuinely faster and still ~9% cheaper after stated rework, while refusing to treat that as a full risk-adjusted result. It uses the frequency DFG (3600 auto-screen, 2550 auto→fast approval), performance DFG (34h/88h evidence return, 30h/80h quote acceptance, 40h complaint→review), replay (fitness 0.91 ≠ 68% fitting traces), alignment semantics, temporal-profile deviations, rework rates, and segment tables. R1–R3 are read precisely: EUR 400 is not “high-value device,” R2 constrains dispatch not approval, R3 requires a *post-reopen* manual review. The DFG audit (terminals 3625 vs 5000; dispatch in 2365 vs out 2580) correctly blocks fake simulation/ROI. The three redesigns are different mechanisms, not parameter tweaks. Uncertainty and non-additivity of percentiles are handled honestly.

**Material design flaw: Redesign A is aimed at the weaker economic target.** The pre-dispatch gate is justified by wrong-replacement cost and the dispatch→complaint tail. Those losses are largest on high-value devices (18–22% fast-path reopen, EUR 410 expected error, R1/R2 exposure). A instead names complete-evidence **low-risk accessories** as primary/best-fit (EUR 95 error, 6–8% vs 3–4% reopen). Staging accessories first to protect the 24-hour Premium credit window is a reasonable *pilot-safety* choice; presenting that cohort as the redesign’s home is not. High-value replacement that is not repaired then sits in a gap between A (accessory-led replacement) and C (repair cells).

**Incomplete process flows.** A is written as a single replacement spine: intake → screen → fast approval → compatibility check → dispatch → close. It drops Fast approval → Quality/compliance close (350 cases), denials, evidence loops, fraud branching, and repair fallback. That is a happy-path sketch, not a redesigned process. B’s “named case owner” is the core mechanism but is an uncosted new role the prompt does not resource; disclosure does not make the operating model specified. C’s “cells” and WIP limits are better grounded (quote delay is part-price wait; inspectors are underused except in campaigns) but still lean on generic work-organization language.

**Table and domination.** Required columns are present and qualitative, which is correct. Marking every row `No*` ducks the allowed `conditional` value even though the footnote argues conditionality (B vs A by evidence state; C vs replacement by repairability). The current process is called **inadmissible** where R1–R3 fail, then still scored as not dominated. That distinction is conceptually right and tabularly messy.

**Underused artifacts in the redesigns.** Fraud/risk (2 specialists, Monday/campaign peaks, 11–18h mean into the activity) is diagnosed and then mostly reduced to “real risk triggers.” Quote-stage abandonment (70) and customer-approval wait (mean 30h, p90 80h) remain largely untouched in C. Segment shares (18/25/14/21/22%) are never used to weight which failures dominate the 5,000-case portfolio. Mid-risk devices are correctly flagged as missing outcomes, then left out of routing logic.

**Experiment section is careful but slightly incoherent with A.** Stage 0 (stop randomizing into known noncompliance; split first fulfillment from post-reopen resolution; do not multiply elapsed hours by labor rates) is excellent. Stage 1 then tests “A” as *compliant accessory replacement ± gate*, i.e. one component of A, not policy-safe routing as a whole. Stop/go bands (15–25% relative wrong-selection improvement, 75–80% reviewer warning, +1–2pp abandonment, 30/60-day complaint windows) are invented. Labeling them as design choices avoids a data lie; it does not make them less arbitrary. Sequencing A/B/C on different segments reduces confounding, but reviewer/inspector queues are shared, so interference is asserted more than designed against.

**Minor accuracy/clarity nits.** Reviewer math (7×36=252h; 82%≈206.6h; 15–35% inflow needing baseline utilization ≲71%/61%) is correct under constant effort, then treated as if utilization were the only congestion mechanism. “206.6” is false precision. Alignment move-on-log for approval-before-evidence is rightly not equated with an R2 breach, but the answer slightly over-lawyers a diagnostic the rest of the text still treats as a real control failure. Fast-path cost “still favors” approval is an **aggregate** residual 9%; it may already reverse for high-value once EUR 410 errors and 18–22% reopens are isolated—the text only gestures at that.

**Verdict.** Defensible, non-generic, and unusually disciplined about what the log cannot support. It fails a high bar because A’s targeting does not follow the strongest quality/compliance/cost-of-error evidence, the “process flows” are incomplete, B depends on an unresourced role, domination is fudged, and several stated bottlenecks never enter the redesigns.
