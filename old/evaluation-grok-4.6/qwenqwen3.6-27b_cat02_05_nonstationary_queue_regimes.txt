**5.6**

The write-up has the right skeleton: all eight required headings, a defensible primary split, a genuinely different alternative, an explicit refusal to treat 15–17 April as a normal high-throughput regime, and Little’s Law verdicts that are directionally correct. That is not enough to survive a strict reading. The quantitative layer is repeatedly wrong in ways that change the operational story, and several required subtleties are either skipped or mishandled.

**What is actually solid.** Opening-hours, carry-over, median≠mean flow time, and non-independence of daily rows are named. The primary cut (baseline weekday / outage-recovery / high-mix weekday / intervention weekday / weekend) matches the visible breaks in `complex_pct`, service tails, WIP, and FTE. Recovery is not sold as a capacity success story. The applicability matrix correctly marks the outage day, the drain days, and the four-week pool as `not defensible`, and does not swap median service in for \(W\). The alternative (mix × capacity rather than calendar) is a real competing narrative, not a relabeling. Section 7’s “staff to the pooled 5.3 FTE and 31-minute median” policy is a fair Simpson-style caution.

**Fatal arithmetic, not rounding.** Pooled completions are stated as 94.6/day versus arrivals 92.4. The table sums to **2,409 completions and 2,619 arrivals** (means **86.0 and 93.5**). Net inventory rose by about 210 cases. The text therefore claims a roughly balanced, slightly draining system when the data show sustained accumulation. That error is not cosmetic: it underpins the pooled-versus-segmented contrast, the backlog story, and the pooled Little’s Law row (\(L/\lambda\) using 28.1/94.6 instead of \(\approx 29.9/86.0\)). Related slips: FTE mean 5.32 vs \(\approx 5.14\); outage-day net backlog “+14” vs \(105-58=+47\); intervention drain “−10 to −17” vs about −5 to −7; weekday demand “~105” vs \(\approx 113\).

**Little’s Law units are incomplete.** \(W = L/\lambda_{\text{day}}\) is then multiplied by **24**. Snapshots and service occur in a **12-hour** window; overnight the queue is frozen. The prompt asked for window, units, and boundary conditions. Calendar-hour \(W\) (baseline 4.8 h, implied wait ~4.4 h) is not the same object as open-hour sojourn \(L/(\lambda/12)\approx 2.4\) h. The matrix still gives useful *verdicts*, but the only numbers it publishes rest on an undefended clock.

**Burstiness section does not match the vectors it cites.** Apr 7 arrivals sum to 101 (mean 8.42, not 8.25); Apr 21 sums to 115 (mean 9.58, not 8.5). Reported Fano factors (~4.2 and ~13.9) are therefore not properties of the given series. “08–09 is 24% of Apr 7” is also wrong (~20%; the 08–10 share is the relevant peak statistic). Overdispersion and the Apr 20 mix break are qualitatively right; the indices are not.

**Internal inconsistency and incomplete code.** Section 4’s pooled figures are hand-waved and would not be reproduced by the script, which never prints a pooled row at all. Hourly data are only two arrival vectors, not the four representative arrival *and* completion series required. `np.var` Fanos, a rolling-`complex_pct>35` flag, lag-1 WIP autocorrelation, and three explicit \(L/\lambda\) windows are present, and the PM4Py comment correctly says these rows are not an event log. That meets the spirit of items 3–6 and fails a strict reading of items 1, 2, and 7.

**Smaller but real analytic gaps.** Weekends are pooled despite the same mix/service/WIP break the weekday split is built on (the text notices this and still does it). “04-20 to 04-28 (Mon-Tue)” is a mislabel. The alternative double-books late weekends as both high-mix and low-volume. Several “estimates” (6.8 FTE to hold WIP \(<30\), E[S] 30–40% above the median) are not derived. Assumption labeling is thin beyond one regime-boundary note.

Conceptually this is a competent process-mining memo. Under a hypercritical standard it is a B-structure carrying several inverted or invented numbers, an unresolved 12-vs-24-hour Little’s Law conversion, and a Python block that would not regenerate the prose. That is a mid-5s paper, not an 8.
