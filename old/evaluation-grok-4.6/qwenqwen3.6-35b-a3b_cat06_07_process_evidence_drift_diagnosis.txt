**6.3**

The final six-section write-up has the right skeleton and the right headline story — April is a pipeline/schema break, June is a mix change, October is label-maturity confounding — but it is not close to flawless. Several errors sit in the exact places the rubric said it would be judged: concept vs process, maturity vs observed labels, and whether August–September are treated as one overlapping regime rather than a single driver.

**What is solid.** April is correctly treated as a source-schema and feature-lineage incident (rename, `UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY`, missing queue feature, wrong prefixes, incomplete May backfill), not as a sudden jump in true breach risk. June is correctly *not* treated as concept drift: V2 share rises, V2’s own breach rate stays low, and aggregate observed breach falls. October is not compared naively to mature months; the 0.16 observed rate is flagged as censored, and the API backlog / long-path selection effect is recognized. The table format, the month-by-month refusal to collapse the year into one failure, the test list (maturity curves, vintage backtests, stratified ECE/AUROC, DFG edges, conditional-on-path checks, Partner Z reweighting, lineage), the monitor-dependency idea (maturity and pipeline alarms should block retraining), and the failure-mode section are all directionally what was asked.

**Concept drift is the main diagnostic miss.** After diagnosis, realized part delay is not in \(X\). When `Order Part → Part Received` goes from ~40h to 130–155h, \(P(Y\mid X)\) at the prediction point changes for V3. That *is* concept drift, with process drift as the mechanism. Calling concept drift “apparent,” giving it low confidence, and listing the V3 spike as *contradicting* concept drift is circular. The quality bar explicitly wanted August–September as overlapping process, concept, and population shift. Treating “add part delay and the concept disappears” as a refutation is also wrong for this prediction horizon: case-level realized delay is future information. A supplier-level or expected-wait feature would be legitimate; using the observed edge duration as a training feature is leakage. The “do not retrain for concept drift” action is therefore too strong.

**There are clear factual slips.** August is headed “process drift peak + population shift” in the same paragraph that says Partner Z is not yet onboarded. June is labeled a population shift; it is a routing/process-mix change. September’s 0.45 / 0.27 Partner Z contrast is called a *matured* rate; the brief says it is the *currently observed* rate and that Partner Z labels mature more slowly — a mistake on the exact distinction the task rewards. Covariate drift is dumped onto April–May, which is mostly pipeline-corrupted features, not a clean \(P(X)\) shift. The retraining section contradicts itself: Apr/May are quarantined, then the strategy says “retrain on Jan–Jun” and also “do not include Apr/May.”

**Tests and code are uneven.** The tests exist and usually give two hypotheses, but several of them test \(P(Y\mid\text{path}, \text{realized delay})\) rather than \(P(Y\mid X_{\text{diagnosis}})\), so they cannot actually separate process mechanism from prediction-time concept drift. The Python sketch reads pandas, normalizes the diagnosis label, and avoids a fake PM4Py drift detector, but it fails an explicit requirement: it never builds *both* event-calendar and case-start cohorts or states their different interpretations. Deduping on `(case_id, activity)` is too coarse. `get_variants` is not a Series with `.value_counts()`. The event log has no `variant` column to filter V3. `discover_dfg` / `discover_performance_dfg` return 3-tuples of `(dfg, starts, ends)`, not `(dfg, edges)` or a scalar duration. For a required “concise but auditable” extract, that is more than cosmetic.

**Net.** This would still be a usable first-pass audit memo, which is why it is not in the 4–5 range. It is not a 8–10: the concept-drift call is wrong relative to the stated standard, maturity is mislabeled for Partner Z, August is miscategorized, training advice is inconsistent and leaky, and the mandated process-evidence code does not do what was asked. Under a hypercritical reading, those are enough to keep the score in the low 6s.
