The answer is analytically ambitious and mostly well-aimed, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading it has a load-bearing contradiction, several quantitative slips, two hard format breaches, and a few conceptual imprecisions that a grader should not wave away.

**The central flaw.** The variant tables are a complete census (600 / 650 / 620). From those tables, raw June–July perfect-fit is 0%, label mapping alone yields 90/650 = 13.8%, dropping `Queue Routed` yields 82.3%, and repairing `Notify`/`Decision` yields 93.1%. The answer itself records this in §6 and, in the ledger, flags that the official 93% / 32% figures cannot be reconciled with the same tables. It then builds the entire causal decomposition — “largest single share,” ~26 pp / ~23 pp / ~8 pp, ranks 1–2, the §3 staircase, and the §7 primary explanation — on the unreproducible official ladder (32 → 58 → 81 → 89). That is not a cosmetic inconsistency. If the variants are trusted, technical-event inflation dominates the fitting-trace collapse and the rename is a much smaller perfect-fit effect. If the official ladder is trusted, the variant census is not a census. The answer asserts both and, in §7, even tells the reader not to quote the percentages it has just treated as the measured cause. That is a serious abductive failure: it detected a provenance problem and then used the worse source for the headline attribution.

**Format.** §3 is ~300 words against a 180–280 limit. §7 is ~415–420 against 250–400. These are explicit constraints, not style nits.

**Smaller factual and conceptual errors.**
