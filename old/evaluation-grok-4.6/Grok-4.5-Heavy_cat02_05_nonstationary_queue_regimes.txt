A competent, well-structured answer that hits the required format and most of the queueing discipline, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading there are factual slips, overclaims, and several places where the prose and the code disagree.

**What is actually strong.** Estimands and units are defined clearly. Median/p90 service is never treated as flow time. Outage and recovery are isolated rather than folded into a “high throughput” regime. Little’s Law is refused where net flow and WIP are trending, and the baseline calculation (`L≈20.3`, `λ≈8.3/h`, `W≈2.43 h`) is arithmetically correct and properly caveated as open-hours time. Most headline regime means check out (baseline WD ≈100/100, high-mix pre ≈114/101 and net ≈−13/day, post ≈115/121 and net ≈+6/day, pooled arrivals ≈90, net ≈−4/day, completion ratio ≈0.96, cum-net −128 on 28 April). The alternative three-era cut is a real alternative, not a relabeling. The Python block is self-contained and does the six required jobs.

**Material errors and inconsistencies**

1. **False description of the change-point heuristic.** The text says a rule `|Δcomplex|>10` or `outage>0` or `|Δstaff|>0.5` “recovers these three dates.” That rule, which is what the code implements, also fires on every weekend staff step (Apr 11, 13, 18, 20, 25, 27, May 2, …) and on the Apr 16 recovery staffing increase. The narrative overstates a noisy calendar heuristic as a clean recovery of three events.

2. **“Nine weekdays” of backlog growth.** High-mix pre-intervention weekdays are Apr 20–24 and 27–28: **seven** days, net −94. “More than 90 cases in nine weekdays” is wrong. The nearby claim that cum-net “reaches −128” *through* the high-mix window is also easy to misread: −128 is the running total from Apr 6, not the high-mix increment (about −94 weekdays / −106 including those weekends).

3. **Primary table and code do not implement the same segmentation.** Section 2 lumps `04-25/26` with `05-02/03` as one “Weekend high-mix” cell, while the code splits `HighMix_WE` vs `HighMix_Post_WE` (staff 2.5 vs 3.0). A document that asks the reader to trust “reproducible” regime summaries should not publish a different grouping than it computes.

4. **Apr 29 is treated as a clean post-intervention day with “High” confidence.** The prompt states the intervention *began during* 2026-04-29 and that the time was not logged. Three weekdays is already a thin window; calling it high-confidence and fully post-treatment is overprecise.

5. **Prose vs code on Fano.** Section 5’s weekday arrival Fano `≈0.66` is the *sample* variance ratio. The supplied `np.var` uses `ddof=0` and would print `≈0.63`. Same analysis, two estimators, unacknowledged.

**Analytical gaps that a strict judge should penalize**

- Hourly Fano/CV is computed on raw hour-of-day counts. That confounds the deterministic morning peak with stochastic burstiness. On a process-mining task that explicitly supplies `peak_08_10_pct`, this is a real competence miss, not a nit.
- The representative hourly vectors do **not** reconcile with `peak_08_10_pct` (Apr 7: 08:00–09:59 is 5+12=17% vs 31%; Apr 21: 3+8=11% vs 47%). The answer uses both sources as if they were consistent.
- Lag-1 autocorrelation of `complex_pct≈0.93` is largely the Apr 20 step, not within-regime persistence. Reporting it as evidence of strong serial dependence without that caveat inflates a change-point into an AR story.
- Section 4’s “Simpson-type” point is aggregation masking, not a reversal of association. Acceptable given the prompt’s wording, but the example (“6 FTE looks fine”) is the same staffing story repeated, not three distinct quantitative concealments of equal sharpness.
- Means of medians and unweighted means of `complex_pct` are used throughout, including in cells with n=2 or n=3, despite the instruction to prefer ranges or robust summaries when means are unsupported. Recovery service “31 / 66” simply averages two unlike days.
- Implied flow times appear in the matrix for windows the verdict itself calls `not defensible` (“4.7–5.3 h are invalid”). That undercuts the paper’s own rule that L/λ is computed only when defensible.
- Weekend W≈4 h is asserted in Section 6 but is not one of the “explicitly chosen windows” in the code.

**What keeps the score from collapsing.** The answer still does the hard things right: it will not sell a pooled M/M/c model; it will not treat recovery completions as capacity; it will not substitute 25 minutes of handling for 146 minutes of sojourn; assumptions A1–A2 are labeled; and the operational “6 FTE is enough because the completion ratio is 0.96” policy is a fair pooled-vs-segmented contrast.

It is a good exam paper with several avoidable accuracy and consistency failures. That is well above a weak answer and well short of near-flawless.
