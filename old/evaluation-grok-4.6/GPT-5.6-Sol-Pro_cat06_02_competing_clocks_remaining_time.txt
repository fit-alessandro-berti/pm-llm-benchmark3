**7.4**

The answer is structurally complete and conceptually aligned with the task: it uses \(\max(C,M)\) rather than a sum, keeps wall-clock and working time distinct, respects the Friday chemistry close and weekend microbiology staffing, treats queue discipline as a mixture, and refuses to invent a rejection probability. Those are the hard parts, and they are mostly right. Under a hypercritical reading, however, several numerical claims are too optimistic, the QA-queue model is thinner than the problem demands, and a few important mechanisms are named but not carried through.

**What is solid.** The assumption ledger is the strongest section. Interpreting risk-priority as “urgent, then B-417, then ordinary,” treating the 1–3 h on-call window as inclusive of readout, starting confirmation only after the primary readout, and refusing to multiply rerun and contamination as independent events are all defensible and explicitly stress-tested. Date arithmetic from Friday 18 Sep 15:30 is internally consistent. The scenario table correctly refuses to partition to 100%, and the derived investigation bands (\(\approx 10\)–\(30\%\), then \(6\)–\(24\%\) documentation-only / \(2\)–\(12\%\) laboratory) are at least transparent about being planning ranges. The P90 discussion — laboratory-investigation probability straddling the 10% threshold, so the percentile is structurally unidentified — is the best piece of uncertainty communication in the write-up. Choosing the primary microbiology readout as the next update, with the temperature flag as secondary, is the right pair.

**Optimistic and internally inconsistent earliest times.** Several “earliest plausible” cells do not survive their own stage logic. A contamination-confirmation path cannot terminate Monday 10:00–13:00: even an early Sunday on-call readout plus a 20 h confirmation plus a staffed readout puts the join at \(\approx\) 09:30–10:30, after which QA wait, review, and release still remain. A documentation-only investigation cannot finish Monday evening: the process returns to QA Review after 8–20 working hours, so an investigation opened Monday midday yields a Tuesday-morning terminal at best. Section 2’s smooth earliest (10:00–13:00) also disagrees with Section 4 (10:00–13:30). These are small on the page and large for a forecast whose first required number is the left tail.

**QA queue at the future join is asserted, not computed.** The problem explicitly requires treatment of join-time queue uncertainty. The answer gives 8.5 pre-Monday reviewer-hours and \(\approx 3\) expected arrivals, then jumps to “2–6 working hours waiting” and an “8–14 h + 4–6 h” congestion tail. There is no remaining-work calculation, no use of the median / p80 service pair to bound how many of the five current batches clear Friday–Saturday, and no Monday arrival term inside the wait. The qualitative remark that an early-Monday join faces a longer queue than a late-Monday join is correct — and then underused. That offset *compresses* the effect of chemistry discipline on the terminal clock: risk-priority finishes chemistry when QA is most congested; FIFO finishes chemistry after two reviewers have been draining. The smooth-path spread “Monday 16:00 – Tuesday 12:00” therefore over-attributes intra-week variation to the laboratory and under-attributes it to the 20:00 QA close and right-skewed review time.

**Percentiles are not derived from the scenario table.** Smooth \(40\)–\(60\%\) is a plausible product of complements, but the P50-like band (73–97 h) and especially the P80-like band (100–140 h, into Thursday morning) are impressionistic envelopes, not a mixture of the rows above. A 15–30% query plus a 6–24% documentation investigation can support a P80 in the query/documentation region; they do not, without stacking or an extra congestion story, justify the Thursday 11:30 end of that band. The laboratory path is already segregated into P90. The mapping from scenarios to percentiles needed one explicit weighting paragraph and does not have one.

**A few modeling shortcuts are sloppy rather than stated.** FIFO chemistry “possibly starts a third sample Friday” is only true if one of the first two is short enough to finish before 18:00; with two servers and modal 2.5 h jobs it usually does not. The rerun is written as “1–3 h,” dropping the triangular mode 1.8 h. The 25–50% “anomaly or excursion trigger” sits a bit low once a 20–40% excursion is already on the table and the quoted rerun/contamination rates are treated as current marginals; the union should be argued, not stipulated. Monday-morning microbiologist congestion is unmentioned: the 08:30–09:30 readout assumes a free reader the moment incubation is ready. Driver 3 conflates a path event (contamination) with a calendar event (weekend authorization) and then labels the bundle “calendar uncertainty.”

**What keeps the score from collapsing.** The answer never adds branch means, never ignores the weekend, never emits a single false-precision timestamp, and never treats the temperature flag as an independent coin flip. Those are the failure modes the rubric names. The remaining problems are bound-quality, derivation gaps, and unused mechanisms — enough to move it well off “nearly flawless,” not enough to call the forecast indefensible.
