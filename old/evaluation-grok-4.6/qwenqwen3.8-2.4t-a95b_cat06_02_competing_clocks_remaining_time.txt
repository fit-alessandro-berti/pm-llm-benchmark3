**7.0**

The write-up follows the required six-section format, uses `max` rather than summed branch times, keeps weekend calendars in view, separates queue wait from service, and treats the temperature-excursion flag as a dependence device rather than an independent coin-flip. Date arithmetic from Friday 15:30 is mostly careful. That is a competent forecast envelope, not a mean-adding collapse. It is not nearly flawless.

**Scenario table is internally inconsistent.** The “delayed microbiology” row is the largest structural error. Its probability band (35–70%) is driven by the *modal* case of no weekend authorization. Its central terminal window (Tue 08:00–Wed 12:00) is the *contamination* subset (~7–20%). Those are not the same path. Routine Monday readout is also already inside the smooth path, so the row is not a clean, materially distinct scenario. The prompt allowed overlapping bands; it did not allow a high-probability label attached to a low-probability clock.

**Quantile ranges include impossible clock times.** P50’s upper edge (Tue ~21:30) and P80’s lower edge (110 h ≈ Wed 05:30) sit outside QA opening hours. Electronic release is continuous *after* acceptance; acceptance still has to occur while QA is open (Mon–Fri to 20:00, Sat 09:00–13:00). The prose later walks this back to “practical working windows,” which means the headline ranges were not calendar-constrained. For this task that is a real fault, not a rounding quibble.

**P50 is a bit late relative to the mixture the author themselves wrote.** Smooth is 35–55% and is said to finish Mon 18:00–Tue 13:00; no-auth micro without contamination still joins Monday. A P50 band stretching to Tue 21:30 needs more delayed mass than the smooth-plus-minor-rework story supports. The bands are honest about width and then slightly mis-centered.

**Dependence is named more precisely than it is quantified.** The ledger correctly refuses independence, but “probabilities increase” is not an operational conditional. Without even a rough pair of rates (base vs excursion-on), the correlated tail is asserted rather than computed. Rejection at 2–10% is likewise invented; the prompt gives no critical-defect probability. Flagging the invention in the ledger does not make the number defensible.

**Chemistry-queue math is thinner than the chemistry emphasis requires.** The answer leans on Monday chemistry as a frequent binding clock, which can be justified (four jobs ahead, ~2–3 analyst-hours left Friday, triangular service 1.5–4.5 h). It never actually closes that capacity argument in the delivered tables. Friday finish is correctly called rare; residual Monday work after a Friday partial start is only sketched. Given how much weight chemistry carries in the smooth/central story, that gap matters.

**Smaller cuts.** Overnight dead zones are left inside numeric ranges even when the narrative knows better. The fifth “excursion/rejection” row mostly restates the QA-tail and contamination tails. Documentation-completeness 0.82 is unused except as atmosphere. QA overdispersion is acknowledged and then not used beyond “0–4 remaining, maybe more.”

What keeps the score at 7 rather than the mid-6s: Monday is the right earliest floor; Sunday QA closure is respected; Saturday QA is used only to drain backlog; on-call authorization is correctly treated as often *not* terminal-binding if chemistry or QA still wait; rerun/pause-at-18:00 is handled; query and investigation hours are converted on the QA calendar; lab investigations are treated as calendar days; the next-event section (primary micro readout, with excursion flag as secondary) is the right Bayesian update. Those are the things the rubric said to reward. The defects above are why it is not an 8.
