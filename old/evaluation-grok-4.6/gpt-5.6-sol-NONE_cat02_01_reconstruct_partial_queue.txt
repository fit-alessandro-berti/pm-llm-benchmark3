**6.0**

The write-up is well organized and often methodologically sophisticated, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict standard, the numerical, definitional, and reproducibility defects are too material to support a high score.

**What is actually strong.** Assumptions A1–A12 are mostly the right ones: rounded-event order, missing starts as feasible intervals rather than point fills, elapsed versus final service for OPEN work, C2’s 15-minute outage in the S2 denominator, and Little’s Law only as a finite-window accounting check. The resource-feasibility bounds for P05/P07/P11/P15 are correct. Individual completed sojourns are correct. The bottleneck story is the right one: S1 absorbs the front-loaded arrival burst, congestion then moves downstream, window utilization is a poor bottleneck identifier, and the 14:00 state is direct evidence that S2 is the operational constraint. The maintenance counterfactual is appropriately modest. Format compliance and the eight Python requirements are met.

**Case-level errors that should not have survived a self-check.** P12–P14 are aggregated as S1 wait 85–95, but P13 is 80 minutes (11:10−09:50). The listed P01–P14 sojourns sum to **2050**, not 2055; the Little’s-Law block therefore inherits a 5-minute addition error. The median of those 14 values is **145** (average of 135 and 155), not 155. These are not rounding ambiguities. They are arithmetic and aggregation mistakes in quantities the author already tabulated.

**Station-level WIP and utilization are internally inconsistent.** Completed S1 service among the 18 fully observed intervals is 505 minutes. Adding the two missing-start ranges (0–35 each) and P21’s 30 elapsed minutes gives window busy time **535–605** (central **570**), hence utilization **74–84%** on 720 resource-minutes and mean number in service **1.5–1.7**. The reported 510–580 / 71–81% and “mean active roughly 0.7–0.8” cannot be reconciled with those figures. The 0.7–0.8 band is S1 utilization mislabeled as time-average active WIP. That is a basic queueing identity failure: \(L_{\text{service}}=\text{busy minutes}/360\), not busy/720. S2 is handled differently (queue ~2.2 is close to the wait-area integral; active 1.1–1.2 is still low relative to 515–570 busy minutes). So the two stations are not even on the same accounting convention. A10 says open elapsed work counts toward utilization; the published busy times do not fully do that.

**Peak WIP and snapshots are wrong relative to the author’s own rules.** At 10:10, S1 has six waiting and two active (P08, P09) after P15’s arrival, so station WIP is **8**, not 7. The same peak of 8 reappears around 11:50 (seven waiting plus P14). The 12:00 S2 WIP of 6 only works if P08 is still in service at its reported completion instant, which contradicts A2’s “completion first” rule (under A2, S2 WIP is 5). The headline mean S1 WIP of 4.3 looks like an average of four clock-time snapshots, not the time-weighted integral defined in §2.

**The two-hour rate table is not a well-defined partition.** 10:00 events are placed in 08:00–10:00, but the 12:00 S2 completion is placed in 12:00–14:00, while the 12:00 S1 completion is placed in 10:00–12:00. That is ad hoc boundary handling, not a stated half-open convention. The accompanying code uses `[lo, hi)` and would print **13 / 6 / 3**, **8 / 7 / 5**, **1 / 7 / 6**, not the 14 / 7 / 3, 7 / 7 / 5, 1 / 6 / 6 table in §4. A “reproducible” section that contradicts the narrative on the quantities it is supposed to generate is a serious defect.

**Python vs. prose.** The script would include OPEN elapsed busy time and therefore cannot reproduce the §4/§6 utilization table. Mean-wait figures in the prose (~70–73 and ~39–42) are also high relative to the same endpoint arithmetic the case table already implies (~65–69 for 21 S1 starts; ~37–40 for 16 S2 starts). Demonstrating `insert_case_service_waiting_time` on a single synthetic arrival-to-completion interval is formally “case-level,” but on that projection PM4Py service is sojourn and waiting is zero, so the call does not validate the station reconstruction.

**What remains credible after the errors.** Front-loaded arrivals, rising S1 waits, a leftover S2 backlog of six at 14:00, C2’s outage sitting cleanly between P06 and P08, completed-case mean sojourn near 146 minutes, and the non-stationarity caveat are stable. Exact utilization ranking, peak WIP, mean active/WIP, and the two-hour counts are not. The analysis reads as a strong first draft that was not checked against its own identities.
