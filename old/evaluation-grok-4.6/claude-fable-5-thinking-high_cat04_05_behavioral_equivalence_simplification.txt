7.4

The core formal work is strong: the observation boundaries are explicit, the loop-scope analysis is right, the inclusion/disjointness argument is valid, T1–T4 are genuine complete-trace witnesses, the P3 cardinalities 76 / 85 / 4 are exactly the right counts, and the proposed sequential simplification is a coherent P1 artifact. Under a hypercritical reading that is not enough for a high score. Several definitions are slightly misframed, one numerical claim is false, and a few “executed” statements are sloppier than the surrounding rigor.

**Equivalence notions.** Visible-trace and P3 equivalence are defined cleanly, and the “sequence of labels, no leaf identity” convention is the right one. P1 is not. After introducing a genuine compliance predicate `comply(t)`, the text immediately collapses P1-equivalence into `L(A)=L(B)`. That erases the point of a business abstraction: two different languages can both be fully compliant (A vs. the §8 model). The later preservation table treats P1 as a *property* to keep, while §1 treats it as full language equality. Those are not the same verdict rule. The P2 map `α` is the right idea, but writing `{(n, true, n≥2): n≥1}` is not a well-formed set of triples; the intended set is `{(1, ⊤, ⊥)} ∪ {(n, ⊤, ⊤) | n≥2}`.

**Structural comparison.** Loop scope, check multiplicity, approval placement, concurrency window, and the AND-join before Close are all correct. The parenthetical on `Check`/`Rework` directly-follows versus true concurrency is right but poorly phrased (“never overlap … though both occur as directly-follows”). Tree size and depth (12/12, 4 vs. 5 with root = 0) check out.

**Verdicts.** The four verdicts are the right labels. Unbounded non-equivalence really is certain by the `#Check=#Analyze` vs. `#Check=1` invariant, and the n=1 residual gap (`Analyze, Approve, Check`) is correctly identified. Calling P2 only “High” after a structural invariant is an inconsistent evidence standard: if the invariant holds, confidence should be the same as for the counting argument. The P1 caveat about `Check` before `Analyze` is useful, but it is an extra reading; the stated P1 rule only requires a check in each Rework-delimited segment, which A already guarantees.

**Counterexamples.** T1–T4 are valid and well chosen (two multi-revision traces, both inclusion directions). The n=1 ⊆ / n≥2 disjointness lemma is the right organizer. The tau/approval paragraph then overclaims: “approvals can never be used to *force* a distinction” is false in the same section that uses T3 as an approval-order distinction. What is true is only that approvals are *unnecessary* for non-equivalence.

**Bounded code.** The BFS design is the right one (final-marking equality, silent steps, `(marking, visible trace)` visited set, explicit P3 cuts, soundness ⇒ boundedness). The 76/85/4 numbers match an independent enumeration, including the easy-to-miss cut that A’s n=3, k=3 traces have length 13 and fall outside the bound. Weak spots: `marking_key` hashes `p.name`, which is not a sound place identity; `check_soundness(...)[0]` is version-fragile; the public API asked for was `pm4py.get_enabled_transitions`; and printing five shortest uniques does not “print traces unique to each model.” The sentence “the shortest uniques reproduce T1–T4” is false: T2 is not among the shortest A-only words.

**Footprints / EMD.** The methodological warnings are correct: pairwise footprints cannot see multiplicity, and EMD cannot decide language equality. The reported sequence pair `Check→Approve` only in A is right. The parallel difference they print is defensible *if* the engine is DF-based (loop-induced `Check⇄Rework` then looks like parallel in both models). They never say which footprint semantics they invoked, and `discover_footprints(parse_process_tree(...))` is not obviously a legal call. “~half of each sampled language lies outside the other” is an undeclared, probably wrong mass estimate.

**Complexity.** Hand-checkable tree metrics are correct. Net sizes and `simplicity_petri_net` values are plausible but not derived in the text; the causal story that B is “less simple because the AND-join concentrates arcs” is speculative. Exact net figures should have been shown as raw API output.

**Simplification.** Choosing P1 and forcing `Analyze→Check` is a legitimate, documented strengthening; 11 nodes, the 13-trace P3 language, R1–R3, and N1–N2 are all correct; `L(simplified) ⊂ L(A)` holds. “Exact (strengthened)” is an oxymoron: the core check-per-revision guarantee is preserved, the interleaving language is not. That should have been two rows, not one.

**Recommendation.** The P1/P2/P3 assignment is right, as is the warning that a negative bounded result is a proof and a positive one would not be. The closing arithmetic is wrong: `|L≤(A)|+|L≤(B)|=161` double-counts the four shared traces; the union has 157 words, and **153** of those (not “157 of 161”) are unique to one model.

The answer would sit near the top of the band if those slips were fixed. As written, the formal skeleton is reliable and the local wording, P1 definition, footprint/API claims, and final count are not.
