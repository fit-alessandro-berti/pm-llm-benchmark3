**7.3**

This is a structurally complete, methodologically serious forecast: it uses the parallel maximum, keeps queue wait separate from service, respects calendars, treats FIFO vs risk-priority as a mixture, refuses independent anomaly multiplication, and does not treat today’s five-batch QA queue as the Monday join queue. The headline shape — Monday morning floor, Monday–Tuesday centre, working-day tail into the following week, Electronic Release as the default terminal — is defensible. It is not nearly flawless. Several calendar-arithmetic mistakes, hour/clock mismatches, and mildly optimistic central cases are enough, under a strict reading, to keep it well out of the 8.5–10 band.

### Material errors

**1. Confirmation windows are a day too long.**  
From a Monday ~09:00 indication, 20–30 h of continuous incubation is Tuesday 05:00–Tuesday 15:00, not “Tuesday 05:00 – Wednesday 15:00.” The extra “Wednesday 15:30–16:30” readout is an artefact of that slip. From a Sunday ~16:00 on-call indication, +30 h is Monday 22:00, not Tuesday 22:00; the staffed readout is Tuesday 08:00, not Wednesday 08:00. The same off-by-one appears twice, which looks like “next calendar day, then add 20–30 h” rather than elapsed-time addition. For a remaining-time memo whose job is temporal decomposition, that is a real fault, not a rounding issue.

**2. Scenario C’s earliest time contradicts its own Sunday path.**  
The table says earliest terminal Tuesday 10:00–12:00 after “a Sunday or Monday indication.” A Sunday indication plus a 20 h confirm is ready Monday midday; with chemistry already done, join is Monday afternoon and a thin QA path can finish Monday evening or Tuesday morning. Tuesday 10:00–12:00 is the Monday-indication earliest, not the Sunday one. The required “delayed microbiology” row therefore misstates its own best case.

**3. Remaining hours and clock stamps do not agree in several places.**

| Stated pair | Implied clock from Fri 18 Sep 15:30 | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Earliest 66–70 h and Mon 10:15–12:30 | 66 h = Mon 09:30; 10:15 ≈ 66.75 h | Band opens ~45 min too early relative to the clock window they actually argue |
| Scenario B “Tuesday” and 88–105 h | 88 h = Tue 07:30; 105 h = Wed 00:30 | Not a Tuesday window |
| P80 100–150 h and “Fri 25 Sep morning” | Fri 25 08:00 ≈ 160.5 h | Clock sits outside the hour band |
| Scenario A body 70–95 h vs summary 72–96 h | — | Unreconciled |

These are the sort of inconsistencies a careful pass would have caught. They make the quantitative layer look less checked than the prose.

### Smaller but genuine problems

**Optimistic chemistry centre.** “Typically Monday 10:00–15:30” leans toward the 45% risk-priority slice. The 55% FIFO slice is centrally Monday mid-afternoon, with a real Tuesday-morning spill when long queued jobs plus a long test hit 18:00. The mixture median is later than that “typical” window.

**Optimistic QA drain.** Friday 4.5 reviewer-hours plus Saturday 4.0, against five waiting batches plus ~3 expected arrivals, only “works down” the queue if service is near the 1.5 h median. At a ~2–2.5 h mean implied by median 1.5 and P80 3.5, leftover Monday work is closer to four batches than two. The 0–8 envelope is honest; the 2–4 “central leftover” is the hopeful side of that envelope.

**Smooth-path tail is overstated.** Scenario A’s tail to Wednesday afternoon, attributed only to “QA congestion or long review,” is not a clean-path outcome. A 3.5 h review from a Monday join is Monday evening or Tuesday morning. Wednesday on a no-query, no-investigation path needs an extreme arrival draw that the row does not earn.

**Weekend confirmation staffing is mostly inapplicable here.** Primary indication is Sunday or Monday; +20–30 h lands Monday–Wednesday, not Saturday night. The remark that a Saturday-night confirmation waits until Monday is generic queueing lore, not a clock that this prefix generates. On-call is correctly second-order on the smooth path; it is then reused as if it were first-order on the confirmation path without showing a weekend-ready confirmation.

**“Plausible earliest” is a conservative mid-best-case, not an earliest.** With priority start Friday, residual ~0.2–1.0 h, Monday join ~08:30, near-empty QA, and a below-median review plus 0.25 h release, a terminal around 09:30–10:30 is more natural than 10:15–12:30. The brief asks for a range, not a point, but opening that range at 10:15 already bakes in median-like QA service.

**Top of the 35–55% smooth band is high.** Even with disclosed common-cause lifting, stacking no-rerun, no-indication, no-query, and no-investigation at the low ends of every band is what gets you near 55%. The lower half of that band is more credible than the upper half.

**Scenario C’s probability label does not match its title.** 8–18% is a contamination-confirmation band (baseline 5–12% plus flag lift). Routine delayed readout — wait until Monday 08:00 — is the *majority* micro path (authorization 0.25–0.45 implies no on-call 0.55–0.75) and is correctly treated in the shared prefix. Naming the 8–18% row “delayed readout or confirmation” blurs a required scenario with a rarer path.

**A few invented quantities are fine only because they are labelled.** Elevated post-flag rates 0.25–0.45 / 0.15–0.35, residual review 0.5–2 h after a query, QA mean “informally ~2 h,” and P(investigation) ≈ 0.20–0.28 are not in the brief. The ledger flags most of them; they should not be read as identified.

**Risk-priority vs “waiting ahead.”** Putting the three ordinary samples *behind* B-417 is a defensible reorder, and the sensitivity cell is good. The stem’s “waiting ahead of B-417” can also be read as four jobs already in front, with high priority only beating ordinary *if* that policy is interpreted that way. The chosen reading is the more optimistic of the two, and it feeds the early Monday chemistry mass.

### What remains strong (so the score is not lower)

The assumption table is the right artefact: no-overtime, join = max(branches), flag as common cause rather than a third token, QA calendar for working-hour delays, lab investigations as calendar days, overdispersed arrivals, electronic release ungated. Parallelism is never summed. Weekend on-call is not allowed to “save” a join that chemistry has already pushed to Monday. Future QA congestion is modelled as a random leftover, not as five frozen batches. Overlaps among B–E are stated instead of being forced to 100%. Uncertainty ranking and the next-event section are the best parts: the temperature flag as a joint shifter of rerun, indication, and investigation probability is the correct Bayesian hook; the Monday micro readout is the right secondary trigger; the Friday 18:00 chemistry snapshot is correctly demoted to a centre-shifting, not path-shifting, observation.

Those strengths are why this is still a solid 7, not a 5. The confirmation arithmetic, the broken hour/clock pairs, and a systematically early/smooth central tendency are why it is not an 8 or 9. A nearly flawless answer would have added 20–30 h without slipping a day, made every remaining-hour band match its clock window, and kept “earliest,” “typical,” and “tail” from borrowing each other’s mass.
