**6.3**

The answer is methodologically strong and formally complete, but it is built on a basic, consequential arithmetic error in the primary remaining-time clock. Under a strict standard that is not a high score.

**Critical error: the microbiology readiness window is wrong by a day.** Friday 14:00 + 46 h is Sunday 12:00; Friday 14:00 + 54 h is **Sunday 20:00**, not Monday 20:00. That is an 8-hour technical-readiness window entirely on Sunday, not a 32-hour window running through Monday evening. The ledger presents the wrong endpoint as a direct translation of the spec, then reuses it as if it were fact.

That error is not local. It produces a fictitious “primary result ready after Monday 18:00 → Tuesday readout” path, inflates “delayed microbiology readout” into a 30–55% scenario, and lets late primary micro compete with chemistry into Tuesday on the smooth path. Correctly: without on-call, every uncomplicated primary result is read from Monday 08:00 (usable ~08:30–09:30). On-call can move readout to Sunday afternoon/evening, but chemistry is closed and QA is closed Sunday, so the join still cannot occur before Monday. After Monday morning, **chemistry queue/calendar and QA**—not primary incubation—drive residual smooth-path variation.

The headline “microbiology dominates the smooth path” is therefore only half right. Micro + the weekend correctly dominate the **lower bound** (nothing before Monday). They do not dominate Monday–Tuesday variation. The quality criteria invite that narrative; the answer followed it instead of checking the clock.

**What is actually good.** Parallelism is handled as a join maximum, not a sum. Friday 18:00 / Monday 08:00 chemistry pause and Sunday QA closure are used. On-call is correctly judged nearly useless for an uncomplicated primary result and mainly relevant if confirmation incubation is needed. Temperature excursion is treated as a common cause, not an independent coin-flip. The future QA queue is not frozen at “five batches.” Scenario bands are allowed to overlap. Rejection is not given a fake base rate. Ranges are used instead of a fake point forecast. The six-section contract is met.

**Other defects, smaller but real.**

- **Earliest ~09:30 Monday is too optimistic.** Even with chemistry resuming at 08:00 and micro usable ~08:30, Friday+Saturday QA has ~8.5 reviewer-hours against five queued batches plus overdispersed arrivals (~0.35/h). An empty Monday-morning queue plus sub-median QA plus instant release is a thin sliver, not the natural left edge. ~10:30–13:30 would be more honest.
- **Friday chemistry capacity is described, not computed.** Two analysts free at 15:54–16:24 and 16:42–17:30; remaining Friday time is ~2–3.5 analyst-hours against four waiting samples plus B-417 and a 1.5 h minimum service. Under FIFO, B-417 almost certainly does not start Friday. Under risk-priority it can start after the urgent sample but still cannot finish (min 1.5 h vs ≤1.3 h to close). That calculation would have pinned the Monday restart state; the answer leaves it qualitative.
- **“Delayed readout” is the default, not a 30–55% branch.** With a Sunday-only readiness window, Monday-morning staffing delay is nearly certain absent on-call. The scenario table therefore double-counts the smooth path and mislabels calendar structure as path uncertainty.
- **P50/P80 attribution is partly spurious.** Mon 16:00–Tue 12:00 as a central band is still a plausible envelope—chemistry spillover and QA congestion can create it—but the answer credits late primary micro that cannot occur. Right-ish numbers, wrong mechanism.
- **Next-event section overweights readout timing.** Primary readout *content* (contamination yes/no) is high value; a “late readout timestamp” is not, once the window is corrected. A nearer update—Friday 18:00 chemistry status, or the temperature flag—is underplayed.
- **QA rework is flattened.** A query is “4–12 working hours then resume,” with little sequencing of the return review, Saturday’s 4-hour window, or a second wait. Acceptable as a shortcut if labeled; it is only partly labeled.
- **P90 upper bound to ~1 Oct / 310 h** is a severe-tail ceiling, not a P90. Laboratory investigation is on the order of 5–10% once investigation rates and the 20–40% lab share are combined. Calling that “P90-like or severe-tail” papers over the mix.

**Net.** This is a competent scenario-envelope forecast with the right architecture: max-join, calendars, overlapping paths, dependence, queue-at-join uncertainty. It is not near-flawless. A 24-hour mistake on the main incubation clock, propagated through the scenario table, stage decomposition, uncertainty ranking, and update section, is a serious forecasting failure. The central Monday–Tuesday band survives mostly because chemistry and QA would have produced similar dates anyway—that is luck, not precision.
