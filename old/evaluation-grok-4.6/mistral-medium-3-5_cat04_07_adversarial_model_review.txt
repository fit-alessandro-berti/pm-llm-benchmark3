**4.3**

The submission looks complete and is directionally right (S is too loose for enforcement, C is a memorized variant catalog, a hybrid is needed). Under a strict reading it fails the parts that actually decide the review: language membership, probe evaluation, and an executable third model.

## Critical errors (these dominate the grade)

**The behavior-probe matrix is wrong in the cells that matter most.**

Reasoning from the given expressions:

- **Model S** is `Register → AND of four XOR(activity, τ) → XOR(Manager, Auto) → Release`. Each of CC/TR/SR/ER occurs **at most once**. There is **no** `Correct Dossier`, **no** loop, and **exactly one** approval. It cannot emit B6 or B8.
- **Model C** is an XOR of **eight concrete sequences**. B2 (`…, TR, CC, Auto, …`) is not among them (the only Auto path is `CC → TR → Auto`). B5 (TR-first + SR + Expedite + Auto) is not among them. B6 (correction then **only** Technical Review) is not path 7 (path 7 repeats **both** checks).

The matrix nevertheless marks S as accepting B6 and B8, and C as accepting B2, B5, and B6. That is five incorrect memberships. Those probes exist to show C missing legitimate combinations and S not being an unrestricted bag of activities. Getting them wrong collapses sections 3–5 and 8.

**Section 3 invents behaviors S cannot produce.**  
`X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` forbids both approvals. The four XOR(activity, τ) branches forbid a second Credit Check. Listing “Auto + Manager” and “repeated checks without correction” as admitted traces is a misparse of the tree, not a subtle semantic point. The real S pathologies (skip both mandatory checks, Expedite-first, SR without checks, CC without TR, no representation of P6 at all) are only partly named.

**Section 4 overclaims C’s coverage.**  
Calling C “explicitly encode valid variants (e.g., B1, B2, B5, B7)” and “includes B6” is false for B2, B5, and B6. The strongest case against C — that it **enumerates a cherry-picked subset** (Auto only on one path; no TR-first+Auto; no SR+Auto; one rigid correction path) — is therefore under-argued, even though overfitting and duplicate labels are noted.

## Section-by-section

**§1** Distinguishes exploration vs enforcement only in slogans. It does not state a use-case-conditional decision rule (e.g., enforcement must reject the negative suite even if holdout fitness drops; exploration may tolerate precision ~0.58 if the language is disclosed as a hypothesis space). Simulation is mentioned, then unused.

**§2** The table is the strongest part: no illegal scalarization, F5 is used correctly, undocumented duplicate-label definition is flagged. Still thin on *comparability*: no seeds, no alignment costs, unknown holdout cases. Training-fitness “comparability concern” is just “holdout differs,” which is a validity point, not a measurement-comparability point. Token-based generalization’s known bias toward oversized models is only implied.

**§5** Besides the wrong Yes/No marks, the “uncertainty” note is confused. `τ` does not “bridge” Correct Dossier into S. Uncertainty should have been reserved for C’s exact-sequence vs alignment semantics, or for B6’s policy dependence — not used to paper over a missing label.

**§6** The pathology *list* is adequate. The *ranking* conflates leakage with split sensitivity; nothing in the dossier shows train/holdout contamination, only that C’s 0.744 holdout fitness is consistent with variant memorization. Alignment-cost sensitivity and silent-transition effects are named, not analyzed (e.g., standard unit costs make optionalizing mandatory work cheap; that is exactly how S buys 0.99 fitness).

**§7 is not a deployable third model.**

1. `ProcessTree("Register Dossier")` passes a string as `operator`, not `label`. `ProcessTree(None)` is not a silent transition. Loop is given **one** child; process-tree `↺` requires a do/redo pair. This will not parse or convert cleanly.
2. Control flow is `checks → SR? → ER? → approval → LOOP(Correct + recheck) → Release`. Corrections run **after** approval and need not be re-approved. That violates the intended lifecycle and rejects B6 (correction **before** approval).
3. ER is forced after SR, so `…, Expedite, Supplier, …` (allowed by P4) is excluded.
4. The DECLARE block is invented (`pm4py.objects.declare.model.DeclareModel`, `SimpleCondition`, etc. are not the PM4Py surface). Constraints shown only cover CC ≺ Manager and TR ≺ Expedite — not Auto, not “exactly one approval,” not P7. “Add more constraints” is unfinished.
5. Defaulting missing `external` to “allow SR” is the opposite of conservative and skirts the instruction not to paper over missing attributes by optionalizing.

The *strategy class* (backbone + guards + explicit correction) is the right family. The artifact is not.

**§8** Acceptance marks did not survive as readable symbols, so the required comparison table is largely unusable. The three “adversarial” traces use abbreviated labels and incorrectly imply S accepts `Register, Supplier Review, Release` (S still requires exactly one approval). They do not surgically separate Third from S and C.

**§9** Right experiment *menu* (seeds, negative suite, perturbations, cost sensitivity, soundness). The skeleton is not reproducible as written: `train_test_split` on a log plus `stratify` on an attribute the dossier says is **absent**; mutation of `log` after the split; fabricated `cost_function` parameter; generalization computed on the test set; decision rule `fitness < 0.8 AND precision < 0.8` would not reject a high-fitness, low-precision explorer on metrics alone (negative-suite clause saves it only if probes are evaluated correctly — they were not). No fabricated numbers: good.

**§10** Sensible at headline level (S only for exploration, C not for a living compliance model, Third only after gates). Thresholds (fitness ≥ 0.90, precision ≥ 0.85, 0.1s) are arbitrary and unearned. “Retire C for exploration / retire S for enforcement” is muddy.

**§11** The rebuttal is real but ~120 words (required 150–250) and leans on “C encodes all observed variants,” which this C does not even do for B2. The “what would change my mind” clause is the only fully adequate part of the section.
