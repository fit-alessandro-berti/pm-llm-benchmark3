**7.2**

The write-up is a strong, structured dialectic: it follows the required headings, steelmans both briefs in the main text, separates stipulated facts from authority/scope/logging disputes, states a usable burden of proof, and mostly refuses a lazy split. Case calls are generally well chosen (701 compliant; 702/704 justified in-window overlays; 703/708 genuinely severe; 705/714 unauthorized after expiry; 707/709/710 treated as model/scope; 706/711 as data). Prosecution concedes real M0/data defects; defense concedes 703, 708, and resource-control failures. The layered L1–L5 architecture and the overlay/data tables are the right *kind* of revision.

It is not near-flawless, and the required model-revision / POWL-semantics piece is materially weaker than the legal analysis.

**POWL / model semantics (largest deduction).** The revised model forces `Register_Supplier → Request_More_Info` on every path. That is not an optional or repeatable approximation; it makes the clean happy path (SUP-701) non-conforming and is worse than the obvious XOR-skip (`SilentTransition`) or LOOP encoding. `SilentTransition` is imported and unused. The same `Transition` objects (`sanctions`, `quality`, `notify`, `close_case`, `activate`) are reused across XOR children, which is invalid or at best semantically unsafe POWL (shared node identity). Emergency approval is renamed `Retrospective_Approval` instead of reusing `Manual_Procurement_Approval`, so logs will not align. Reactivation is discussed in L5 but never given a control-flow handle. These are not cosmetic; they undermine the artifact the prompt weighted.

**Classification and matrix nits.** SUP-713 is over-called as `DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE` after audit equivalence is already stipulated; substance is known. SUP-715 is labeled noncompliant for a disclosure gap even though CS-9 SoD (distinct humans) is satisfied—the distinction is mentioned, then flattened. Several matrix “prosecution” cells concede the defense mid-row (especially 702), so the table is less dialectically fair than the briefs. SUP-711 is almost certainly a duplicate log of a compliant flow, not a true indeterminate.

**Authority handling is good, not perfect.** Expiry vs informal continuation (705/714) is handled correctly and is the right place to refuse the defense. “Authority is binary” in the prosecution brief is fine as advocacy; the judgment is more careful. The analysis still slightly underplays that PD-3’s own legitimacy is internally contested (observer later denied approving a CS-9 weakening)—702 is justified *if* PD-3 binds, which the piece assumes more than it litigates in the final call.

**Net.** Excellent process-aware legal reasoning and evidence coverage; clearly above a competent pass. Under a strict bar, the mandatory RFI edge, shared-transition XOR, and log-mismatched emergency label keep it well short of 8–10.
