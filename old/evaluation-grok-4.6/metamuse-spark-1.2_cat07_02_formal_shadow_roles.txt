**6.4**

The answer has the right architecture and mostly the right instincts: it refuses frequency-only clustering, keeps complexity and authority in view, treats substitution as coverage rather than competence, and names shadow/bridging roles instead of forcing a clean partition. The six required headings are present and on-task. That is not enough. Under a strict reading, the write-up is repeatedly sloppy with evidence, internally inconsistent, and imprecise about both the log and the PM4Py APIs it claims to use.

**Factual and quantitative errors.** Nora does not originate “>60% of all A1/A2.” A1 is 760/1668 ≈ 46%; A2 is 610/2251 ≈ 27%; combined ≈ 35%. That is a central claim in the automation-boundary argument and it is wrong. Giorgio’s 72 A7 events are ~59% of Fatima’s 122, which §4 states correctly and §2 states as “~37% of Fatima’s 10-week volume.” Those cannot both be true. B1’s A2/A4/A12 share is not “>75%” for Amira (≈70%). “3874min” appears three times as if it were a measured duration; the source evidence is 38 → 74 minutes. Even if arrows were stripped in transmission, leaving a 64-hour pricing-exception duration in the main role table and again in the staffing advice is a serious failure of quantitative hygiene. A process owner would stop trusting the table.

**Invented or overclaimed evidence.** Kai–Lina similarity to B1 is given as “~0.9”; that pair is not in the similarity table. Amira’s 18 pricing events are narrated as “drafts note before Fatima/Giorgio” with no support. Human A1 is explained as “fallback when Nora incompleteness”; the counts (Amira 220, Ben 210, temps ~175, Giorgio 90) are also consistent with a parallel intake channel, and the log is not shown. Handover/subcontracting “reveals” Mateo as central subcontractor and “likely highest betweenness” is asserted as if computed; this is a summarized matrix, not a mined graph. R-D calls Giorgio an “authorized shadow” while the scenario and later text say informal substitute—authority is exactly what is in dispute.

**Broken key sentences.** Comparison operators are missing in the places that carry the argument: “Similar profiles  similar risk authorization,” “Substitution  qualification,” “Frequency  Authority or Competence.” Sequence fragments collapse into “Norahuman,” “CloseReopenrework,” “DiagnoseErik SecurityMateo.” The intended “≠ / →” reading is recoverable from context, but as written several sentences can be read as asserting the opposite of the paper’s thesis. For a deliverable whose point is that similarity is not authority, that is not a cosmetic defect.

**Role structure is mostly sound, not tight.** Splitting B1/B2, isolating Nora, keeping Erik as a rare-critical role, and refusing to merge Hana with Mateo are the right moves. Chen/Dina and Amira/Ben are correctly flagged as same-profile, different-risk. Giorgio in both R-D and R-E matches the “don’t force a clean cluster” brief, but the table then double-counts him and blurs whether commercial authority is a role, a person, or a temporary coverage pattern. The core/peripheral rule (“>15% of their events AND high share within activity AND cert/team AND complexity”) is never applied uniformly: Mateo’s “core” activities fail the frequency test by design, Giorgio’s commercial work is core in one row and peripheral in another, and Amira’s A1 is core in the table and “fallback” in §2.

**§5 is illustrative in the allowed sense and still wrong in ways that matter.** `discover_organizational_roles` does not return `List[List[resource]]`; PM4Py roles are activity sets with originator weights. Treating the naïve baseline as a resource partition is the exact confusion the rest of the essay warns against. Handover/working-together/subcontracting objects are NetworkX graphs, not DataFrames to feed `from_pandas_edgelist`. `timest_key` is not a `format_dataframe` argument (`timest_format` is). `from pm4py.statistics.service_time import get` and `get_service_time(..., agg_by_activity=True)` are not reliable public signatures. Filtering Nora out after the fact is good advice; claiming the snippet would recover the 0.94/0.71 figures and Mateo’s betweenness is theater.

**§4 and §6 are the strongest parts and still overreach.** The Giorgio / Chen–Dina / Amira–Ben / Mateo / temp decision criteria are the right ones (`temporary_substitution` timing, complexity *within* activity, recheck rate, subcontracting direction). The two cautions match the constraints. Then the text treats week-5 13/21 rechecks as a general “62% recheck” competence statistic, recommends “cross-train Chen for Level-1 security” as if Chen’s 24 A6 events were known to be lower-complexity security work (only his *overall* case mix is given), and writes “3874min gap” into the training plan. Shadow-role benefits and risks are discussed as required; “authorized” vs informal is not resolved.

**What keeps it well above a failing score:** it does not assign roles by top activity count; it does not ignore complexity; it does not treat Fatima’s absence or Hana’s workshop as proof of permanent competence; it does not equate 0.94/0.71/0.54 with equal authority; it does not moralize shadow work. The required three shadow/bridging discussions are present and substantively correct. That is a coherent organizational reading of the matrix.

**What blocks anything near the top of the scale:** wrong percentages on the two highest-volume facts (Nora’s intake share, Giorgio vs Fatima A7), a repeated impossible duration, an unsupported similarity, missing operators on the thesis sentences, and PM4Py code that misstates the naïve method’s output type. Nearly flawless this is not.
