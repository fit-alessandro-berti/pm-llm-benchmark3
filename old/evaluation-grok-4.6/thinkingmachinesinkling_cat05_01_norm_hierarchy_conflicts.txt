**6.4**

The write-up is structured, time-aware, and usually careful about authority versus chronology. It is not close to flawless. Several defects are structural, not cosmetic.

**Classification collapse.** Every case is `NONCOMPLIANT`. That directly violates the instruction not to treat every deviation as noncompliant and wastes `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` and `INDETERMINATE`. P-504 and P-506 are clear breaches. P-508 is not in the same class: Compliance and Legal precede commitment, two IC members authorize, requester/committer is not an authorizer, and the goods are the only available restoring pump. A defensible primary label there is `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` (or a dual label), not the same stamp as a cafeteria freezer with no IC authorization. P-507 is the same pattern minus Legal. P-503’s scope fight and P-505’s expiry/clock edge were natural `INDETERMINATE` or split-label cases; calling them `NONCOMPLIANT` with high confidence flattens the uncertainty the prompt asked to keep visible. Secondary classifications were allowed and almost unused.

**Broken formalization.** The Section 3 pseudo-logic inverts the triggers it is supposed to encode. `unless(P, amount >= 50000): requires(...finance...)` means finance is required when the amount is *below* 50,000. The ECD-2 line is the same polarity error: `unless(P, within_ecd2_window) and (P, critical_line_scope): allows(...)` does not mean “if in window and in scope.” For a required defeasible-logic catalogue, that is a serious defect, not a notation quibble. Several rule-table triggers also drop the ≥ (rendered as a blank), so R2/R4 are ill-formed on the page.

**Hierarchy table is inconsistent with its own columns.** “What it can override” is filled, for GPIP-4 A1–A7, with what can override *them* (“Nothing except a valid A7 exception”). The ECD-2 row uses the column correctly. That is not a formatting slip; it scrambles the hierarchy the section exists to state.

**Finance / Source D is argued more tightly than it is applied.** The piece correctly says the CFO may interpret Finance controls and may not waive A1 or A3. It then treats every missing `Finance_Approve` event as a full A2 failure, including inside the ECD-2 window after D1. That is a possible reading, not the only defensible one, and it is why the scorecard is monochrome. A2 still needs an *independent budget owner*, and IC members are not chartered as such—that point is good—but D1’s work on the Finance-Controller half is not given any effect in the primary labels. P-501 (EUR 38k) is also over-read: A2 is off, and B2 does not clearly require Plant/Finance *before* commitment the way A2 does. Late local approvals are a problem; they are not the same problem as a missing pre-commitment GPIP-4 gate.

**Smaller accuracy and completeness issues.** Unapproved register status is silently equated with A3’s “new supplier.” P-507’s eight-hour need fails B4’s six-hour test; the text uses the eight hours only as ECD-2-scope color. `Receive_Goods` is listed as a relevant activity and never used. The P-505 alternative in Section 5 first strengthens noncompliance and only then describes the premise that would actually flip the label. None of these would sink a strong paper; together with the three structural faults they keep it well below a high score.

What remains good should be said plainly. Acts are judged at commitment time, not at file close. A6 is applied and not waved away. ECD-2 is not treated as an A7 instrument. P-501 is correctly held outside the 09:00 window. P-505 prefers the 17:59:31 supplier acknowledgment over ERP 18:06. P-507’s 12:40 PDF is treated as timely C3 evidence and a logging defect, not a fake authorization. P-504 is scoped out without drama. Section 7 is a usable clause: CRO exclusivity, 24-hour scope, retained A2/A3, three-role segregation, no verbal extension, and an anti-normalization tripwire.

The legal narrative is better than the apparatus. The apparatus was required. Under a strict reading, this is a solid mid-tier opinion that repeatedly chooses the same conclusion and then mis-writes the logic that was supposed to justify the distinctions.
