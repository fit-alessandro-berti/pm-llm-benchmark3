**6.8**

The answer is format-complete, arithmetically careful, and correctly refuses a single “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. Availability vs. use, endogenous targeting, staggered timing, intensity ramp, median-versus-tail divergence, fraud-desk interference, and substitution vs. capacity creation are all recognized. That is a strong skeleton. It is not nearly flawless.

**What holds up.** Section 1 stays descriptive and hits the required distinctions. The estimand table covers the mandated contrasts and is appropriately pessimistic about identification. Pre/post windows are explicit; Alpha/Beta/Gamma contrasts and the never-treated comparisons check out (e.g., Alpha/Beta median +0.7h vs. controls +5.2h; p90 +9.7h vs. +16.0h; Gamma vs. controls about −3.8h / −7.1h). Intensity bins are computed on the right months. Threats, effect ranges, and the claim ladder are conservative in the right places. “Expand with an embedded evaluation” is the only defensible policy given two remaining untreated branches.

**Identification is the main scientific miss.** Augmented synthetic control is a weak primary design here: Alpha/Beta have three pre months and two never-treated donors, one of which (Gamma) is only usable through June. Cluster-robust SEs being unreliable is not a reason to prefer SC; SC does not repair small-N inference, and it does not “make interference visible.” A group-time ATT / event study with never-treated controls is the more natural primary design and is relegated to an alternative. The code then estimates the TWFE-style models the writeup disavows, and never implements the stated primary strategy or prints event-study coefficients.

**Quantitative discipline has gaps, not arithmetic errors.** The primary Gamma window (Apr–Jun vs. Jul–Aug) is the most favorable pre period and includes the May complexity shock as “pre”; the Jan–Jun supplement is shown but not treated as co-primary. There is no April-only DiD, which is the cleanest month before the product change and Alpha’s FTE loss. Bounded intervals (−5 to +1h, −8 to +2h) are judgmental and sit uneasily next to “not identified.” Equal-weighted branch averages ignore volume. `automated_fast_path_pct` of 4–6% under `automation=0` is never interpreted.

**Rollout logic is only partly thought through.** Holding one branch out is correct. Pairing Epsilon (high complexity, weak CSAT) with Delta as control is a poor match and is not treated as a first-order identification problem. Within-branch 50% randomization is useful but does not solve centralized fraud-desk SUTVA, which the same memo treats as a first-order threat. Absolute p90 deterioration in Alpha/Beta after adoption is underweighted relative to the relative DiD.

**Other nits.** Estimands E3–E7 are mostly outcome slices of E1. Goodman-Bacon / heterogeneous timing is named, not used. Section 3’s preference paragraph overclaims what SC can do. Confidence 55 on escalation is high given the March fraud-desk common shock.

Net: a competent, well-structured policy memo with real quantitative care and the right bottom-line humility, undermined by a mismatched primary design, a few convenient windows, and an evaluation plan that does not fully respect the interference and comparability problems the memo itself raises. Under a strict bar that is a mid-to-high 6, not an 8+.
