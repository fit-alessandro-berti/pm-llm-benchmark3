**5.5**

The write-up has the right skeleton and gets the hardest case-level reconstructions mostly right, but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it loses a lot of points for wrong station-level numbers, missing required analyses, and Python that would not reproduce the prose.

**What is actually solid.** The seven required headings are present and nothing extra is added at that level. Missing starts are bounded from resource calendars rather than silently filled: P05’s S2 start in [09:45, 10:15], P07’s S1 start in [09:25, 10:00], P11’s S2 start in [12:00, 12:25], P15’s S1 start in [11:40, 12:15]. Those intervals match the data. OPEN vs WAITING vs MISSING are distinguished in the narrative; elapsed service is not treated as final service; completed-case totals for P05/P07/P11 are correctly invariant to the missing start. The bottleneck story is directionally right: S1 waits grow from 5 minutes to ~100, S2 is initially starved by S1, and C2’s outage is a transient second-order shock.

**Section 4 has clear factual errors, including in the one interval the author chose to highlight.** During 10:55–11:10 the table says 0 S2 completions and C1 busy 10/15 minutes. P07 finishes at 11:00, so there is one completion, and C1 is busy the entire 15 minutes (P07 until 11:00, P09 from 11:00). S2 arrivals in that window are not “1 new (P11) + P08 queued”: P10 enters S2 at 10:55 and P09 is already queued. S2 WIP in the outage is about 4–5, not 2–3. Post-outage C1 busy time is written as 130/170; the residual P09 slice alone is 30 minutes (11:10–11:40), which makes ~150/170. Those mistakes sit in the main metrics table, so they are not cosmetic.

**Required quantities are incomplete.** Case-level total sojourn for the 14 completed cases is never summarized (only P05’s 110 appears in a note). Arrival and departure *rates* by subperiod are not reported; S1 is given only as a full-window count. Queue length / WIP “over time” is reduced to an end count of 8 and a peak, with no trajectory. Little’s Law is defined as an accounting identity and then not actually checked with numbers in the delivered document. S1 peak WIP of 5–6 is too low: around 11:00–12:10, S1 waiting plus in-service is about 7–8.

**Smaller but real slips.** P18–P20 never appear in the case table (not aggregated, not listed). “S1 waits increase monotonically” is false (P13 wait 80 < P12 wait 85). A 0-minute service lower bound is used for utilization without saying it is only a mathematical floor. Rounding is discussed and then never applied to any reported range. Central values such as 57.5 minutes are finer than the 5-minute grid really supports.

**The Python section fails its own brief.** `prev_end` keeps ends with `e <= end_dt` and therefore includes the *current* case’s own end, collapsing every missing-start interval to zero service. Utilization figures are hardcoded (`245.0`, `315.0`, …), not computed. WIP at 14:00 uses a magic `14`. The three imputation scenarios are not implemented. `log.to_dataframe()` after `format_dataframe` is version-fragile, and the script never prints the columns that `insert_case_service_waiting_time` / `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` are supposed to demonstrate.

So: competent reconstruction of the named cases and a defensible qualitative bottleneck reading, but not internally consistent at station level, not complete against the task list, and not reproducible. That is a mid-pack answer, not a high one.
