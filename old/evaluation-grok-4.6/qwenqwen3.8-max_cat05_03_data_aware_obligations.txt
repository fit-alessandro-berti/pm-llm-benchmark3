**7.4**

The write-up is complete, well structured, and mostly right on the hard parts: event-time reconstruction, blanks as non-updates, `event_index` ties, late record vs signature time, R4’s 20% / decrease rules, R6 on TX-305, and why control-flow conformance cannot carry these obligations. TX-301–309 and TX-311 are classified correctly with the right triggers. The pandas/PM4Py script meets the mechanical requirements and would emit the right coarse flags on this log.

It is not close to flawless.

The required formalization is damaged. Temporal operators in the pseudo-logic and parts of the rule table are blank (`e1  e2 iff ord(e1)  ord(e2)`), so the direction of several relations cannot be read from the formalism itself. That is a direct hit on a scored criterion, even though the surrounding prose is clearer.

TX-310 as `INDETERMINATE` is a defensible hedge given contradictory evidence, but it slightly misuses R6. The R1 trigger is established (AE, EUR 200k, no Enhanced_Review). What fails is proof of the exemption. On a burden-of-proof reading that is `NONCOMPLIANT`; R6 is about unresolvable triggers, not unproven exceptions. The ambiguity section almost says this, then does not let it constrain the table.

The code has a real logic gap: if a claimed exemption is `INVALID_COARSE`, R1 is marked `VIOLATION` without checking whether an Enhanced_Review already satisfies the rule. That does not bite these eleven cases, but it is not a faithful encoding of “unless a matching exemption was already valid.” Baking `STRONG`/`WEAK` into the register also smuggles the EX-77/EX-88 verdict in as data rather than deriving it only from timestamps and evidence fields.

Smaller faults: R2 is quietly strengthened with a post-trigger-change freshness condition the policy does not state; TX-302 is labeled `JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION` even though Enhanced_Review independently satisfies R1 (acknowledged later, still a table-level slip); R3’s obligation formula is redundant; R1 “material change” is broadened to every risk/jurisdiction/customer update without defending that against R4’s narrower amount rule.

Strong casework and a usable checker, but unreadable formal operators, a rule-encoding bug, and a soft TX-310 call keep this well short of a high score under a strict rubric.
