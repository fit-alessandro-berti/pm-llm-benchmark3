**7.4**

This is a sophisticated, format-compliant process-mining write-up with correct arithmetic, a real distinction between logged geometry and physical semantics, and a PM4Py-plus-pandas design that matches the spec. It is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several load-bearing moves are overconfident, under-labeled, or conceptually blurred.

**Evidence handling.** The prompt states that extra operational notes are “not guaranteed to be causal.” The table still uses the four-slot fixture, the “begin group” button, and the folder-submission anecdote as *strongest evidence* for G1–G3. Those notes are useful priors, not observations. Treating them as decisive, while giving 94–97 confidence, overfits commentary that the task told the analyst not to treat as fact. Only assumption A1 is formally labeled. Other load-bearing assumptions—within-source clock identity, group-button causality, copied G6 starts, safety check actually removing T-H capacity—are written as if they were data.

**Ranking problems.** G1’s first and second rows are the same physical story (one fixture cycle) in two data models, not competing explanations. G4 and G6 park two ranked hypotheses in the top cell, which avoids the required ranking. G5 at 99% as a “shared-account artifact” is incomplete: the login is necessary but cannot explain four-way overlap against a stated three-analyst cap; the top label should have been a conjunction. G6’s preferred “synthetic start and complete” is more speculative than the source name `manual_bulk_form` alone supports; the second row is about as likely. G8’s capacity-loss story is ranked first despite the prompt’s explicit different-clocks warning; 82 is better than the 97s, but the ranking still outruns the evidence.

**Overprecision.** Confidences of 97, 98, 99, 99 on n=4–7 fragments are not defensible once context is non-causal and several clusters remain compatible with two or three mechanisms (section 5 even shows this). G1’s own counterevidence (minute-aligned PLC times, sequential inserts) is enough to knock that 97 down. Calling G1 “no timestamp correction” while flagging minute alignment is a small internal tension.

**Section 4.** The script is the strongest part: in-memory data, interval columns, `format_dataframe`, a 2×2 `merge_distance`/`min_batch_size` sweep, independent overlap/equality/lag/granularity/queue diagnostics, and retention of semantically bad rows. Expected PM4Py labels are plausible. They are still *expected*, not observed, and the write-up does not qualify the `merge_distance=0` edge case for touching sequential intervals (G4/G8) or zero-duration simultaneous events (G6). The trailing PM4Py URLs sit outside the required heading set and lean on material not in the prompt.

**What remains strong.** Signature matrix, bias mechanisms, and the G6 dual (inflated wait + zero service from copying 17:00 onto both endpoints) are exact and well argued. Utilization overcounts (400%, 259%, 239%, 233%, 500%) are computed correctly and tied to capacity, not treated as proof of multitasking. G7 negative waits and interval-censoring are handled properly. Section 7 correctly refuses to treat a PM4Py “Simultaneous” label as a physical batch.

The piece fails a near-flawless bar because it repeatedly converts optional context into near-certain diagnosis and then assigns 90+ confidence to those rankings. That is a methodological error, not a nit.
