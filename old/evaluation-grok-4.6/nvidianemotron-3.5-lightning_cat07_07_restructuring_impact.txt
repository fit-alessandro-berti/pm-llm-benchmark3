**5.4**

The seven required headings are present and the answer is complete on paper: mechanisms, three scenarios, indicators, hidden risks, pilot, conditional recommendation, and a PM4Py sketch. Coverage of East vs North/South, fairness, learning, and “do not trust the mean” is real, not decorative. That is the ceiling. Under a strict reading the answer is not close to flawless.

**Factual and logical errors**

Legal/Risk is already a scarce shared pool (9/100 cases, 9.6h wait, 91–93% utilization). The central scenario treats those 9 cases as “previously handled locally” and now newly queued. That misreads the baseline table. Management’s +15% utilization target is also accepted uncritically: Legal 93% × 1.15 exceeds capacity; Risk is the same. The only slack is in local tech (68–82%), i.e. load-balancing East onto North/South, not a uniform specialist-utilization lift. The answer never states this, so the main economic mechanism of the redesign is blurry.

Optimistic waits are internally inconsistent: they are called “stable or slightly below current” while listing 4.0h vs today’s 3.2h caseworker→tech hop. Adverse Legal/Risk volume “doubles” while wait “stays at 10.5h” despite 91–93% utilization — impossible under elementary queueing. Resilience is one-sided: today’s worst edge is already cross-team cover (6/100, 8.9h, 18% rework). A shared pool could replace that failure mode; the write-up almost only models central fragility.

**Pilot design does not test the proposed change**

An East-only “shared service” with North/South frozen is not a merger of N_Tech, S_Tech, and E_Tech. You cannot observe the central bottleneck, North/South expertise loss, or cross-team fairness that the rest of the memo treats as first-order. Four-week baseline / eight-week steady state is also too short relative to 16–23 day 90th-percentile cycle times, so the tail, rework-after-implementation, and learning effects the answer correctly flags will be right-censored. Guardrails and rollback triggers are otherwise specific.

**PM4Py sketch is not a working comparison design**

`log_before` is filtered to `"after"`; `log_after` is never bound. `format_dataframe(..., organising_key=...)` is not a PM4Py API. `discover_handover_of_work_network` is treated as a dict with a `"handovers"` key; it is a graph. `get_service_time` is not a per-edge median wait. `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is only commented. `format_dataframe` is applied to a summary dict at the end. Working-together and subcontracting are called and then unused. Calling this “pseudocode” does not excuse a swapped cohort and invented return types when the prompt asked for a before/after analysis sketch.

**Presentation and precision**

Repeated “Hander pattern,” plus glued forms (`processmining`, `taillatency`, `cycletime`, `localspecialist`) make the memo look unedited. Several PM mappings are hand-wavy (roles from `discover_organizational_roles` as if they were RACI; utilization from `get_service_time`). `priority`, `reassignment_count`, and `local_expertise_flag` are barely used despite being in the log.

**What still works**

Sections 1, 3, 4, and 6 are the strongest: mechanism table, leading vs lagging split, six-plus mean-hidden risks, and a pilot-first recommendation that tells management what not to infer from early averages. Those parts are why this is not a low score. The misread baseline, impossible utilization arithmetic, non-representative pilot, and broken comparison code are why it cannot sit in the high band.
