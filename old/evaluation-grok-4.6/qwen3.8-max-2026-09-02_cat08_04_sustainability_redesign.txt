**7.2**

The final write-up (sections 1–7) is a strong, on-format answer: it stays inside the process evidence, treats service/quality/resilience as hard constraints, and packages interventions instead of listing green slogans. It is not nearly flawless.

**What it gets right.** The boundary is explicit and appropriately operational rather than fake full-LCA. Indicators are tied to log attributes and DFG paths. All twelve candidates are analyzed with the required four elements, and air/dedicated-van use is segmented by slack and true downtime rather than banned. The effects table uses prompt-supported ranges (air 4–6×, consolidation 35–55%, CNC_A 2.8→1.3 kWh/item, CNC_B vs CNC_A rework, heat-treat 1.7× late risk, 6% obsolescence). Rebound coverage includes batching, transport, packaging, rework, obsolescence, and customer overrides. The recommended package has guardrails (SP1 probability, CNC_B overflow, dedicated-van fallback, Q1) and a usable validation plan.

**Material defects.** The required effects table is damaged: the direction legend is blank, so many cells read as fragments (“where air…”, “or slightly”). That is a failure on a mandatory artifact. Lifecycle accounting is inconsistent: `energy_kwh` is defined as production energy, then S5/S8/S11 attribute trip counts and empty running to energy rather than `transport_kgco2e`. S11’s energy claim is weakly grounded (partial shipments are mostly stock, where energy is already ~0.8–0.9 kWh). The inspection-queue rebound named in the prompt (rushed inspection → later rework) is only a side note in S7, not a first-class risk in section 5. Notation is sloppy (`kgCOe`). The 12,000-order mix table is not used to rank hotspots, even though it supports that ranking. Governance cadences are invented.

**Bottom line.** Multi-criteria process reasoning is clearly present and mostly disciplined. Under a strict bar, presentation failure on the effects table plus energy/transport conflation keep it well below the top band.
