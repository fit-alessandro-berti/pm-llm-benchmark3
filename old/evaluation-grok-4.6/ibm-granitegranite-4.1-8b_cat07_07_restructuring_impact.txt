## Critical numerical and logical errors

The mechanism map states a **nominal handover reduction** of local-to-specialist loops as **36 → 41 per 100 cases**. That is an increase. Management’s own table also raises tech return legs (31 → 36), approval traffic (42 → 49), and legal/risk traffic (9 → 12). Combined specialist-side handovers rise, they do not fall. Calling this “reduction” inverts the central claim being evaluated.

The same section says median wait **“shortens slightly (2.4h → 3.0h)”**. That is a lengthening. Management already forecasts *longer* waits on the new hubs (3.2 → 4.0 tech, 2.4 → 3.0 approval, 9.6 → 10.5 legal/risk). An adequate forecast would treat that table as evidence of added queueing, not as a story of streamlining.

The **adverse** median cycle time is **7.2d**. A rough volume-weighted baseline is already about **7.5d** (820×6.8 + 760×7.4 + 520×8.6). Even the worst case is drawn as a net improvement on the mean. That empties the adverse scenario. Optimistic 6.2d / central 6.5d are also asserted without a queueing argument, and without splitting East (overloaded E_Tech at 82%, worst tails) from North/South (stable local loops). Heterogeneity is named, not modeled.

Legal and Risk are already at **93% and 91%** utilization. Routing more work into the same shared queue is the most important capacity fact in the prompt. It is barely used.

Resilience logic is backwards: removing N_Tech/S_Tech/E_Tech does not make local teams “need less intra-team backup.” It removes the backup and creates a single congested node. Fairness “worse for South because of weaker relationships” is invented; the log does not support that South-specific story. Customer-segment fairness (regional conventions, language) is required and then mostly dropped.

Optimistic role clarity “improves because queues log who approved” assumes away the stated accountability risk. Early-warning signs under the optimistic heading are failure symptoms (latency spikes, complaints), not signatures of the optimistic path. Learning appears as a slogan, not as a mechanism (caseworkers stop seeing hard work; activity profiles polarize; `local_expertise_flag` decays).

## Indicators and hidden risks

Lead/lag separation is the right idea; the mappings are often wrong.

- `discover_activity_based_resource_similarity` is **not** utilization.
- `discover_subcontracting_network` is the A–B–A interruption pattern, **not** cross-team handover.
- `get_service_time` is processing time, **not** queue wait; `insert_case_service_waiting_time` is the listed function and is unused.
- `extract_temporal_features_dataframe` is timestamp feature engineering, **not** case duration.
- `filter_event_attribute_values` filters a log; it does not measure a rate.
- Outcome `values=["miss"]` is not given in the schema.

Section 4 lists six risks and folds high-complexity into tail latency, so the required set is not cleanly covered. The *ideas* (tails, knowledge loss, fairness, learning, accountability, exceptions) are the right ideas. The observability column recycles the same API mistakes. Section 6 then contradicts Section 3: average cycle time is classified as **lagging**, then management is told to treat it as a **leading** indicator only.

## Pilot design

This is a structural fail. Scope is **North, South, and East**—the entire known organization—plus a fictional **West** control. That is a full cutover with an imaginary comparator. A real design would keep at least one intact local team (likely North or South) as control, or phase only East (already bottlenecked) or only high-complexity / legal-risk work.

Other design errors: 4 weeks pre / 8 weeks post is short relative to 16–23d tails and a 9/100 legal-risk base rate; the 1.2 team duration ratio already fails on current East/North (~1.26); `len(set(required_specialty))==4` is not a learning measure; “rollback if any guardrail fails” is unusable given guardrails that fail at baseline; multi-attribute `filter_event_attribute_values` and `get_service_time(..., quantile=0.95)` are not the APIs given.

## PM4Py sketch

This section should have been the empirical spine. It is mostly invented client code.

Never calls `format_dataframe`. Uses non-functions (`visualize_network`, `plot_histogram`, `export_dfg` to a nonsense path). Invents `params={"variant": "optimal"}` throughout. Applies `filter_event_attribute_values` to a **performance DFG**. Passes several attributes at once. Treats `get_service_time(log, "Technical Shared Service")` as a named-queue wait API. Computes SLA miss as `len(filtered_log)/len(log)` (events, not cases). Comments refer to “trainees,” who do not exist. `customer_segment`, `handover_reason`, `reassignment_count`, `priority`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `discover_dfg`, and `filter_variants` are essentially unused despite being in the brief.

A correct sketch would format the frame; split pre/post; build handover / working-together / subcontracting networks on **resources** and then aggregate by `team`; compare performance DFGs and variants by `case_complexity`, `required_specialty`, `customer_segment`; insert service/waiting times and look at tails after the new hub activities; use role discovery and resource similarity to see caseworker profiles lose technical work; count rework and reassignments on the filtered slices.
