6.6

The answer is substantively on the right side of the hard design choices: it refuses to recode reject/withdraw/open as non-default, treats activation as a selection mechanism, keeps an as-of discipline, and writes a leakage table that actually matches the known data risks. Under a strict reading it is still not close to flawless. Several claims that look careful do not survive contact with the snapshot dates, the process semantics, or the code.

**Target.** The primary estimand, \(P(\text{default}_{90}\mid\text{activated}, X_{t^*})\), paired with a separate activation model and an explicit ban on reject-as-zero, is defensible and correctly distinguished from a multi-state application outcome and from reject inference. Two problems remain. First, \(t^*\) is defined as the earlier of the \(k\)-th event and the first decision event, which places \(t^*\) *on* the decision timestamp, while Section 4 and the code both require a strictly pre-decision prefix (`timestamp < t_dec`). Elapsed-time features therefore do not match the prose. Second, Alternative 1 asks for terminal-state probabilities “within a fixed calendar horizon from case start” while default is defined 90 days *after activation*; no horizon length is given, so the competing-risk target is not actually identified.

**Cohort.** The 48,000-row flow arithmetic is correct and the strata are named honestly. The table then contradicts itself on transfers: ~1,740 linked cases are said to have recoverable outcomes, yet they remain entirely in the transferred row and are never moved into the labeled or censored-activated counts. Partner missing feeds are invoked as a separate MNAR stratum with no residual headcount (the omitted buckets already partition the 48,000). Inclusion requires a prefix of length \(\ge 3\), then short cases are “not excluded”; those cannot both be true without a precise rule. Conflicting terminals after the October 2025 migration are ignored.

**Censoring.** Mechanism separation is the right idea and is then applied inconsistently. Transfer in this process leaves Credit Review *before* activation. Putting unlinkable transfers into a time-from-activation survival model as ordinary censoring is a category error; they are not in the activated risk set. Still-open cases are likewise called ordinary right-censoring of default even though default is undefined without activation. The 3,600 immature accounts are late activators (or delayed feeds), not a random administrative sample; training only on label-mature complete cases therefore selects on speed-to-activation, which is likely associated with risk. Section 1 says survival is the estimation machinery; Section 3’s primary analysis is complete-case binary default among mature activated accounts, with IPW only for missing feeds. Those two procedures are not the same and the selection induced by the 125-day maturity gate is not discussed. The reject-inference bound is also wrong: using the activated default rate as the lower bound for rejected applicants has no justification (rejected risk can be lower than originated risk). The honest interval is nearer \([0,1]\) or a bound justified by a stated monotone-selection assumption.

**Prefixes and features.** Pre-decision truncation, last-`risk_score`-within-prefix, lagged `documents_total`, and fold-wise historical DFG/variant tables are sound. Queue load “at \(t^*\)” is only leakage-safe if openness is determined solely from events \(\le t^*\); that caveat is implied, not specified. `policy_period` is listed as a feature while the primary split makes it a constant in train (all pre-July 2025) and a different constant in test, so it cannot be estimated and will not exist as a contrast at deployment.

**Validation.** The critique of a random split is excellent. The proposed second split is not feasible. A 90+35-day label requires activation by about 13 October 2025. Cases *started* on or after 1 October 2025 almost never have a mature 90-day outcome by 15 February 2026, and essentially none of the post-15 October 2025 migration cohort does. A split advertised to “isolate the migration effect” therefore has an empty (or pathologically selected) labeled test set. That is the exact maturity mistake the study claims to prevent. The primary July–onward test set is usable only for July–early-October starters who activate quickly; that extra selection is not quantified.

**Code.** The skeleton is the right shape (format_dataframe, temporal split by start time, prefix truncation, banned-column asserts, train-only DFG/variants, explicit non-use of a fake PM4Py survival API). It would not implement the design as written. `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log(log, length)` does not take `case_id_key` and, on a DataFrame, is version-sensitive; as called, it is likely to throw. Linkage recovery, elapsed-time prefixes, origin-specific maturity, and IPW are absent despite being load-bearing in Sections 2–3 and 7. `y` is joined into the feature table with no target/feature partition. Cohort asserts check internal completeness, not the known 18,900/2,700/48,000 margins. `np.where` on a mapped Series is a fragile way to attach labels.

**Net.** This is a strong outline with the correct scientific posture, not a nearly correct study design. The transfer/censoring mix-up, the impossible post-migration backtest, the unjustified reject-inference bound, the unresolved complete-case vs survival estimand, and a skeleton that cannot execute the stated API or the stated cohort rules are each independently disqualifying for a top score.
