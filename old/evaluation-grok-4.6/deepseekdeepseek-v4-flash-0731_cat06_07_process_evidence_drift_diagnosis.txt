**6.0**

The final six-section writeup gets the *core audit thesis* right: April is a schema/pipeline break, June is a routing/mix change with stable V2 rates, August–September are overlapping process, concept-like, and population effects, and October’s 0.16 observed breach rate is not comparable to mature months. That is the main thing the rubric rewards, and it is present.

It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading, execution errors are frequent enough to cap the score well below the top band.

**What holds up.** The diagnosis table (when the columns are readable) separates drift types instead of slapping one label on the whole year. Matured V3 rates (0.35 → 0.46 → 0.64 → 0.61) are correctly used as within-variant evidence, and the answer flags that this may be missing lead-time features rather than a pure change in \(P(Y \mid X)\). June is not over-called as concept drift. October is treated as censored, including the point that the outcome API hits long paths first. Tests mostly include two competing expected results. Remediation is staged (contain → repair pipeline → recalibrate → retrain), Jan–Mar/April/October training safety is mostly right, and the monitor design correctly says a label-maturity alarm must not auto-trigger retraining. The five failure modes are the right ones.

**Material defects.**

1. **Broken required table.** The label-delay and data-pipeline rows have shifted columns: months leak into evidence, “Very high”/“High” land in the action column, contradicting evidence is empty or displaced, and “Training presentation not a single cause…” is not English. For a deliverable defined as that table, this is not a cosmetic miss.

2. **Clear factual error.** Section 2 says the parts edge “jumps from 62 h in Jun to 65 h in Jul.” June is **42 h**, July is **65 h**. That understates the first real process break and treats a 55% increase as a rounding change. July is also under-used as the cleanest mature window where V3 risk has already moved (0.46) before Partner Z exists.

3. **Internal contradiction.** The answer forbids training on immature September labels, then proposes a logistic recalibrator on “corrected May–Sep scores.” September is 82% mature; that is the exact mistake Section 6 warns about.

4. **Code would not produce an audit.** After `format_dataframe` the case id is `case:concept:name`, but the case-start cohort uses `case:id:concept`, so that cohort is empty or throws. `discover_performance_dfg` returns a `(dfg, start, end)` tuple, not a dict you can `.get()`. The loop’s `end_month <= 10` guard **drops October**, the month that most needs a maturity audit. Summaries never emit unknown-activity share, missingness, or label delay—the actual DQ columns in the stem. Duplicate handling keeps UNKNOWN even when a canonical event exists at the same case/timestamp. Calling the part-edge value a “median” is unjustified.

5. **Sloppy or invented phrasing** that a strict grader cannot ignore: “parts order fibre,” “planning drift,” “copper-level,” “process volume to V2,” “model starts undetectable,” “blank classes,” “orthotic”-level noise in adjacent wording. These are not polish nits; they make operational recommendations harder to trust.

**Net.** Conceptually this is a competent multi-cause audit. Strictly, it is a correct skeleton wearing a misaligned table, one wrong process KPI, a self-contradictory training rule, and non-runnable monitoring code. That is a mid-pack answer, not a high one.
