**7.2**

This is a strong, largely coherent reconstruction with real process-mining competence, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several inaccuracies, overclaims, and methodological gaps keep it well below the top of the scale.

## What is actually good

The skeleton is right. Assumptions are labeled, waiting is not confused with service, open work is kept as elapsed or lower bounds, and missing starts are bounded rather than silently filled. The feasibility windows for P05, P07, P11, and P15 are correct on the nominal resource calendars: P07 cannot start on I1 before P05 ends at 09:25; P15 cannot start before P13 ends at 11:40; P05 on C1 cannot start before 09:45; P11 on C2 cannot start before P08 ends at 12:00. The three-scenario design is defined clearly, and the observation that total station WIP is invariant to missing-start placement is the right robustness point.

A large share of the arithmetic checks out. Completed sojourns, the 146/145 mean/median, S1/S2 arrival and completion counts by subperiod, the 705-minute S2 denominator, busy-time ranges 535–605 and 515–570, utilizations, mean WIP 5.79 / 3.72, end-of-window states (S1: 1+1, S2: 4+2), and the 10:25–12:20 queue-area figure 5.87 are all consistent with the table. The bottleneck narrative — early S1 overload from a 6.5/h vs 3.0/h mismatch, later S2 dominance from longer calibration plus a residual 3.18 vs 2.47 gap after 11:10 — is the interpretation the data support. The code also does the required PM4Py/pandas split instead of pretending case-level PM4Py helpers are station queue metrics.

## Defects that block a high score

**1. A false empirical claim in §3.** Completed sojourns are not monotone. They rise overall from 60 to 230, but P08 = 180 then P09 = 155, and P08 is a local spike aligned with the C2 outage. Calling this “monotone growth” and “not isolated outliers” is simply wrong. That is a small sentence, but it is an accuracy failure in the case-level section.

**2. Little’s Law is only a loose, post-hoc check.** The 10:25–12:20 window is chosen after seeing the starts, the queue is not particularly flat (it moves between 5 and 7), and the check multiplies the start rate by the *full* waits of the seven leavers, including time before the interval. The 5.22 vs 5.87 comparison is therefore not a clean test of \(L=\lambda W\). The caveats are present, but they do not make the construction defensible enough for the weight it is given.

**3. The PM4Py “valid projection” overclaims.** Restricting `insert_case_service_waiting_time` to fully observed completed cases is reasonable for sojourn/service/wait. Applying `insert_case_arrival_finish_rate` to that same gapped set is not. Dropping P05, P07, and P11 makes non-adjacent cases look consecutive, so the inter-arrival/inter-finish numbers are not valid process arrival statistics. The writeup still presents that projection as semantically valid for both helpers.

**4. Uncertainty is only partly propagated.** Duration rounding of about \(\pm 5\) minutes is stated, then mostly left beside the tables. Feasibility windows are not widened by the \(\pm 2.5\) minute timestamp error; I2 utilization is given as a point 81%; whole-window utilization ranges reflect imputation only. Adjacent endpoints (P07/P09 on C1 at 11:00, several 5-minute resource gaps) are the obvious places where rounding could create or hide a conflict, and they are not examined.

**5. A2 and the code do not match.** A2 specifies completions, then arrivals, then starts. The sweep sorts all \(-\)1 events before \(+\)1 events, i.e. starts/departures before arrivals. After *all* events at a timestamp the occupancy is the same, so reported \(L(t)\) is mostly unharmed, but the stated convention is not the implemented one. Peak logic also updates after each intra-timestamp event; they avoid spikes only because departures are processed first.

**6. Some station summaries mix incompatible quantities.** “Mean started-case station sojourn ≈ 94 / 72” folds completed sojourns together with elapsed open sojourns (P21, P15, P17). That is not an unbiased sojourn mean. The extra column `total_case_sojourn_completed_only` also stores right-censored lower bounds, which contradicts its name.

**7. Reproducibility is thinner than §7 implies.** The code rebuilds station scenario totals and resource utilization, but it does not emit the case-level reconstruction that §3 asks the reader to trust. `numpy` is unused. The PM4Py case-level print keeps both S1 and S2 rows, so case-constant fields are duplicated and easy to misread as activity-level.

**8. Smaller unclarities.** Snapshots are written as “about” even when the nominal post-event counts are exact. The 10:55 outage slice includes P10’s 10:55 S1 completion without discussing the tie. Pessimistic zero-minute services are flagged as implausible, yet they still enter the utilization lower bound with little discussion of a more realistic positive-service floor. Theoretical capacities (roughly 4.4/h at S1 vs 2.8/h at S2) are described qualitatively but never computed.

## Bottom line

The answer does the hard things well: it keeps MISSING / OPEN / blank distinct, bounds rather than invents starts, includes censored busy time in utilization, and gives a time-varying bottleneck account that survives the three imputations. Under ordinary grading this would sit in the high 8s. Under the requested standard — any inaccuracy or loose step is costly, and only near-flawless work gets a very high mark — the monotone error, the overstated Little’s Law and PM4Py-rate claims, incomplete rounding treatment, and the A2/code mismatch are enough to land it at **7.2**.
