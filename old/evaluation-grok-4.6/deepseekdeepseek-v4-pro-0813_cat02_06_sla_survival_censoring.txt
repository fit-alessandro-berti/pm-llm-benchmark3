**7.5**

The final write-up is a strong, process-aware survival analysis: estimands are defined before estimators, withdrawal/transfer are not waved through as ordinary censoring, open cases are kept in the risk set, and post-start activities (`Escalate`, rework, full variants) are treated as time-varying or leakage-prone rather than baseline covariates. Case IDs are used accurately, the three transfer assumptions are distinct, and the Python block meets the stated requirements without inventing timestamps.

It is not near-flawless. The SAP never makes uncertainty quantification a first-class output of the primary Aalen–Johansen analysis (no Greenwood/Aalen variance, bootstrap plan, or small-sample warning on P1/cohort cells of size ~5–15). Estimand A is written as \(\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(\text{max follow-up})-\mathrm{CIF}_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(72)\), which is only the identified late-resolution mass under independent censoring, not an unrestricted \(P(\text{resolve after }72)\); that identification caveat is thinner than the rest of the piece. Sensitivity 3 mixes two different interventions (transfer-as-failure and open-beyond-72-as-failure). The state diagram omits the observed `Rework → Resolve` arc (S27) and never names immortal time, though the concept is present. Fairness is generic relative to a process that *intends* unequal priority treatment. Those are enough real gaps that a hypercritical score cannot sit in the 9–10 band.
