The write-up meets the required structure and the stated quality bar: it does not chase the largest aggregate score, separates exploration from enforcement, reasons from concrete traces, proposes a real third model, and includes a non-token dissent. The probe matrix is mostly right, including the easy-to-miss fact that Model C rejects B2 (`Technical Review` then `Credit Check` then `Auto Approval`) because Auto appears only on the `Credit Check → Technical Review` branch.

It is not nearly flawless.

The operator account is sloppy at a critical point. PM4Py `+` is parallel AND, not inclusive OR (`O`). Calling it “parallel / inclusive-style” is wrong. The behavioral reading of Model S still works, because `X(activity, tau)` under AND is the usual optional-concurrent pattern, and the third model’s `+('Credit Check', 'Technical Review')` is the right encoding of “both, either order.” A reader who took “inclusive-style” literally could make one mandatory check skippable and recreate Model S’s main failure.

Model C’s training fitness of 0.973 is under-read. That is not “slightly more forgiving” than 0.992; it means the eight-branch “frequent variant” model already fails some training behavior. That is direct evidence of incomplete enumeration even before holdout, unseen combinations, or missing Supplier events, and it is not pressed hard enough.

The third model is directionally better (mandatory AND, Expedite after both checks, exactly-one approval, correction then one-or-both rechecks, Supplier optional with an external guard). Gaps remain. Duplicate labels are still used for rechecks, which reopens F3 and alignment ambiguity: a cheap model-move of `Correct Dossier` can make B8 look better than exact language suggests. The checker is weaker than the tree (`Correct Dossier` anywhere in the prefix, not a closed correction episode). Supplier-before-mandatory is excluded by assumption, which P3/P4 do not fully settle. Loop/`tau` semantics are hedged rather than pinned. Validation code is appropriately non-fabricated but includes placeholder costs and shaky top-level APIs.

The dissent is in range and actually argues the other side. The remaining misses are why this is a strong review, not a near-perfect one.
