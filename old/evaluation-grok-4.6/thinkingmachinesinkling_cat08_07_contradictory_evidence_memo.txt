**7.4**

The answer is a strong strategic synthesis with the right decision (hybrid E / “Safe R”), clean fact–interpretation–assumption separation in the table, and a defensible VoI ranking. It is not nearly flawless.

**What works.** The recommendation does not scale R nationally and does not roll it back just because some metrics worsened. Survey-skip and post-billing activation are correctly identified as the high-risk levers; simple-house gains are preserved. Fitness/precision, alignments, DFG shifts, temporal profiles, resource/social networks, and segment splits are all used, not mentioned in passing. Alternatives A–E have advantages, risks, and conditions. M1–M8 are ranked with a stated uncertainty and a decision-flip. The evidence table meets the eight-item bar and mostly keeps the three columns distinct.

**Hard constraint miss.** Section 1 is required to be ≤700 words. It is roughly 1,200–1,300 words: it restates facts, then re-explains every metric conflict, then previews the full measurement plan and gates. That is not an executive memo; it is a condensed version of sections 3–6. The later “Final recommendation” then repeats the same actions and thresholds. For a decision-memo task this is a first-order communication failure, not a cosmetic one.

**Overclaim and false precision.** Rollout gates (FTR ≥80% in every segment, p95 ≤45, full cost ≤198, complaints ≤6.5%, SLA ≤13%, violations <1%, false-skip <10%, utilization <85%) are presented as if they were implied by the evidence. They are invented cutoffs. Option A is dismissed with “only if all metrics improved,” which is too absolute: a net-value case could exist with mixed metrics. M7 is ranked as value-of-information; it is mostly a control/guardrail, not an experiment that resolves an open causal question.

**Smaller slips.** Segment effects are treated as reliable enough to route live work, while the table correctly notes incomplete risk attributes before week 3 and a non-comparable region mix—the operational plan underweights that measurement hole. Coordinator ownership is scaled into a process already at 93% utilization; the 85% cap is a patch, not evidence that the organizational model is safe. A few “facts” in the memo’s narrative (happy-path vs exception-loop causality) are interpretations, even though the table labels them correctly.

Substance is in the high-7 to low-8 range. Instruction-following, brevity, and epistemic discipline keep it from going higher.
