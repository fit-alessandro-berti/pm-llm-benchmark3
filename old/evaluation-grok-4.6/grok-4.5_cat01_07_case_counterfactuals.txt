**7.3**

The answer is substantively strong on the dimensions that matter most—policy vs. timing, readiness vs. completion, four-eyes/compliance preservation, queue-forecast uncertainty, and bottleneck migration—but it is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading it has damaged formal notation, a few temporal inconsistencies, light shift-calendar analysis, and small spec violations.

**Format and formal apparatus.** Headings, table schemas, derivation labels, eight typed assumptions, and the two-intervention pilot table all match the spec. That is a real compliance success. The required partial order, however, is not a usable relation set. Precedence, concurrency, disjunction, and inequality symbols are stripped or collapsed, producing unreadable constraints such as “Identity Check may run  with waiting for Originals Receipt,” “high_risk  high_value,” “A1  C1,” and “slight  early C1 hold,  late cold CR minutes.” A reader cannot tell whether high-risk/high-value is AND or OR (the norm is OR). The K-219 line “Identity  wait(Originals)” is also the wrong constraint: identity must not wait on originals. The 100-case writeup is slightly over the 400-word cap; the synthesis is slightly over 320. These are not cosmetic if the section is supposed to be the feasibility model.

**Temporal consistency.** Ranges are used, which is correct, but several bounds do not survive the factual timestamps. K-104’s “0.5–1.5 d faster” / “Tue evening–Wed midday” overstates the upper end: documents finish Tue 05:40, and Assessment plus a still-mandatory final CR plus Decision cannot reach a 1.5-day pull-in from Wed 18:40 (that would be ~Tue 06:40). Best case is about one day, not 1.5. K-219’s table worst case (+0.5–1.5 d) disagrees with the derivation (+1–2 d). External RMI of 8–36 h plus a new DC/Assessment can exceed both if it crosses a weekend. K-337’s “Fri–Mon” vs “3–5 d” is a smaller mismatch (Mon vs next Wed is ~2 d).

**Shift and queue mechanics.** The answer treats snapshots as forecasts and distinguishes earlier readiness from earlier completion, especially on K-411. It is weaker on operating hours. K-219’s speedup is not robust if D2 is a day-shift resource: originals at Mon 17:30 into a ~5 h D2 backlog more naturally implies a Tue late-morning start and a *later*, slower finish than D1’s factual Tue 09:30–13:30. The prompt hints that D2 “could start much earlier,” so the evening-start story is licensed, but a strict answer would separate (i) after-hours start, (ii) overnight FIFO only, and (iii) next-morning start, and would not let the clean-path “0.5–1.0 d faster” sit as the headline. K-104 similarly needs a clearer statement that a C1 hold cannot be consumed before Assessment ends.

**Process feasibility and minimal change.** This is the best part of the answer. K-337 is correctly treated as a decision-policy change, not a duration tweak; silent ignore is forbidden; final CR after Assessment 2 is retained; four-eyes (A1 ≠ C1) is kept. K-104 does not waive final CR. K-411 does not invent illegal concurrency across one resource. Paths are explicit enough to audit. Residual weakness: Escalation Advice’s status after an early packet is hand-waved, and M1 evening/Friday availability is assumed rather than bounded.

**Uncertainty, interference, ranking.** Calibration is generally honest: reservation cancelation, packet double-read, RMI tails, Assessment absorption, weekend calendar nonlinearity. The capacity table and 100-case discussion hit every required theme (C1 hoarding, D1/D2 substitution, rework, Assessment migration, weekends, false concurrency). Ranking K-411 > K-104 > K-219 > K-337 is defensible if robustness, not raw speed, is the criterion; putting the lock-workaround first is still slightly generous given the stated audit/false-concurrency hazard. The pilot table is better than typical, but randomizing C1 reservations is a SUTVA problem: treated cases contaminate control waits on a singleton. Measuring spillover does not fix the design.

**Net.** Expert-level counterfactual reasoning with real interference literacy, undermined by a broken feasibility block, a few infeasible or inconsistent time ranges, and under-specified calendars. That is clearly above competent and clearly below near-flawless.
