**7.0**

The final write-up (sections 1–7) is complete, data-grounded, and mostly aligned with the required format. It does not rank by frequency, defers activation, keeps compliance responsibility with humans, and uses the DFG, variants, resource network, and control findings. It is not nearly flawless.

**What is strong**

The matrix covers all twelve candidates with the required columns and qualitative ratings. Register is correctly treated as high-frequency but low process-level value. A11 is ranked last and excluded from year one. Dependencies are mapped with real artifacts: missing-document loops (3300/2800), entity-mismatch critical path (82%), concurrent KYC/financial review, committee packaging’s four-way wait, and the two deep-integration cap. Resource shifts are explicit: 3 compliance specialists, 9 analysts at risk of clean-inflow overload, legal month-end, back office idle after 15:00. The three portfolios have distinct objective functions and honest sacrifices. The anti-A1 argument in the final recommendation is the right process-level claim.

**Material flaws**

1. The “bottleneck/synchronization” portfolio omits the activity the table itself labels a synchronization point. `Wait for external valuation` is 58h median wait, 19% late, 2,350 cases, required in 71% of equipment finance. A7 is ranked 5th and deferred. That is the largest waiting-time driver in the log. Partner dependency and low labor saving make exclusion *arguable*, but a portfolio sold as synchronization that leaves the 58h wait untouched is a process-level hole. The later “swap A7 for A5” hedge weakens the recommendation instead of resolving it.

2. Compliance required a control-strengthening module in the **first wave**. Phase 1 deploys only A5 (portal nudging). A4 and A9 arrive later. That fails a stated constraint if “first wave” means first deployment, not the full-year bundle.

3. A4 is over-sold. The candidate note is limited coverage with large waiting-time impact. The write-up repeatedly treats A4 as if it will relieve the two-senior-specialist bottleneck. An assistant with limited coverage may not do that; the handover network risk is underweighted.

4. The two deep **core-banking** slots are assigned to A4 and A6. A4 is primarily KYC/entity/compliance case management. Using a scarce core-banking slot on A4 is a stretch and may crowd out a true core integration (A9) for a system that may not need that slot.

**Smaller issues that still cost**

- A6 is ranked #1 while the same matrix warns it can overload credit analysts. Ranking should not put a known downstream-bottleneck creator above the 31h/82% critical-path activity without a tighter justification.
- The quick-win set includes A9 and A10, i.e. downstream contract and signing automation, while the brief forbids downstream bots when upstream controls are unreliable. Flagging the risk does not make the portfolio well-specified.
- Four-eyes (5.8% of high-exposure, month-end) is never operationalized in the recommended four. “Workflow must still enforce” is a leftover, not a design.
- Gate criteria are often vacuous (“pre-agreed tolerance,” “unacceptable friction”).
- Credit-line renewal’s 14% contract-remediation rate and committee weekly cadence are cited and then barely used in the recommended effects.

The answer is a solid process-mining portfolio brief with one wrong emphasis (A7), one constraint miss (first-wave control), and several overclaims. That is well above a generic frequency ranking and well short of a tight, internally consistent recommendation.
