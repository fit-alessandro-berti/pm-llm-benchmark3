**5.4**

The write-up looks like a board-ready review: the use-case split, metric table, pathology ranking, validation agenda, and dissent are the strongest parts. Under a strict reading of this brief, those strengths do not offset repeated language-membership errors and a third model that is then audited as if it implemented requirements it does not.

**Behavioral reasoning is the core failure.** Model S is `->(Register, +(X(CC,τ), X(TR,τ), X(SR,τ), X(ER,τ)), X(Manager, Auto), Release)`. That XOR **does** enforce exactly one approval, and each parallel child fires at most once. Claiming S admits dual approval, B8 (`…, Credit Check, Technical Review, Credit Check, …`), or a post-release `Correct Dossier` is false. `Correct Dossier` is not even in S’s alphabet, so B6 is not a clean accept; the brief required uncertainty to be stated, not guessed. The §8 row that marks P5 “Not enforced” for S is the same mistake written into the comparison table.

**The third model is only partly better, and the audit of it is inconsistent.** The backbone correctly locks P1, mandatory CC/TR (either order), XOR approval, and a redo loop around correction. It then **fixes SR before ER**, so it does not implement P4’s “before or after Supplier Review.” After `Correct Dossier`, the do-part still requires **both** checks, so B6 and adversarial T2 are **not** in the language. The paper still scores those traces “Accepts (with flag).” That is not a small slip: the alternative was supposed to be distinguished from S and C on exactly those traces.

**Other deducts.** “C still admits some forbidden traces” is asserted without a single C-language witness. Loop syntax is written as `O(...)` while PM4Py’s process-tree parser is `*(do, redo)`; the promised `model_third` snippet is therefore not reliably executable. The validation skeleton invents APIs (`split_log`, `calculate_fitness`, `check_liveness`) and treats `1 − fitness(negative log)` as precision, which is the wrong quantity. None of this is fabricated metrics, but it is not a usable experiment design as coded.

What remains genuinely good is the refusal to crown a winner by averaging the table, the ranking of positive-log precision and optionalized-mandatory fitness as the high-impact pathologies, the exploration-vs-enforcement split, and a dissent that actually argues C’s low holdout fitness as a regulatory feature. That is why this is not a collapse into the 3s. It is also why it cannot sit in the high 7–10 band: the task asked for probe-level fidelity and a third semantics that is then evaluated honestly. This answer fails both.
