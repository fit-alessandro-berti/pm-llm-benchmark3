7.8

The submission is a strong, complete dialectic that meets the required heading contract and is factually careful on dates, overlays, aggregates, and the distinction between trace deviation and misconduct. It is not nearly flawless. Several classification choices sit in tension with the brief’s own burden of proof, and the revised POWL has real control-flow errors that the text does not disclose.

**What is strong.** Section 1 cleanly separates stipulated M0 semantics (including both checks before the XOR, hence before rejection), controlled CS-9 rules, time-bounded overlays, and unproved claims. The burden-of-proof design is the right one: missing log events are not non-performance when N4 exists; exceptions need authority, window, eligibility, compensating controls, and an invocation record. Both briefs argue at strength and concede what they must. The matrix covers all 15 cases, including M0-fitting resource/COI failures. The system judgment is conjunctive rather than a split: material operational noncompliance *and* M0 is invalid as a sole yardstick. Layering POWL / DECLARE / pandas / organizational controls is the correct architecture, and the overlay table (exclusive ends `2026-08-01` / `2026-07-13`, surviving post-activation FD-7 duties, fail-closed eligibility) is precise.

**Classification and consistency flaws.** MIXED is an allowed label and is not used as a substitute for analysis, but it still fights Section 1. On the brief’s own rule, an unproved master-data control plus genuinely disputed derogation means the exception is not established; SUP-702 should fail closed as NONCOMPLIANT (medium confidence) or be a qualified JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION if PD-3 is treated as a real monitored exception. MIXED records the dispute instead of applying the stated standard. SUP-711 is the clearer miss: a same-`source_transaction_id` duplicate is a high-confidence repair to an otherwise fitting CS-9 trace, i.e. COMPLIANT after canonicalization, not DATA_QUALITY_INDETERMINATE. Indeterminate is for cases where the fact of performance cannot be recovered (SUP-706). SUP-715 as NONCOMPLIANT is defensible only if COI disclosure is in the onboarding control set; the relationship itself is not prohibited, so the case is a policy-layer failure adjacent to CS-9, not a peer of SUP-703/708. SUP-710 as MODEL_OR_SCOPE_DEFECT is historically fair, but Section 6 then says future reactivations default to the standard route—so the historical default is left softer than the proposed one without saying so.

**POWL / model-semantics flaws (the largest deduction).** The code is executable and hits the required families, and the LOOP(`tau`, `Request_More_Info`) approximation plus its documented limit is correct. Three encodings are wrong or unsafe, and the prompt required those limits to be stated rather than hidden.

1. Emergency route adds `Activate → Quality` and `Activate → Manual`. FD-7 says provisional activation *may* precede those steps. The model makes post-activation quality/approval mandatory. A lawful mixed order (quality before activate, manual after) fits neither `standard_route` nor `emergency_route`.
2. In `auto_route`, `Auto_Approve` is unordered with `Sanctions_Check` and `Automated_Master_Data_Validation`. Only activation is a join. Auto-approval before sanctions is admitted. `Quality_Review` has *no* edges, so it may also precede sanctions.
3. Route choice is a top-level XOR of entire families. That forces an a-priori route rather than M0’s checks-then-XOR decision. Rejection after a full intended-approve check set is only recovered because optional quality exists on `rejection_route`; there is still no reject-after-manual path, and the design is not justified as a disclosed approximation.

Smaller model nits: invented pre-activation `Start_Daily_Transaction_Monitoring` is reasonable but not labeled as a log-alphabet extension; `standard_route` leaves the info-loop unordered with checks, so all requests may occur before any diagnostic; reactivation is omitted from M1 after the text says the default *is* the standard route (which still demands `Register_Supplier`).

**Residual unclarities.** “M0 admits no … label aliases or duplicate events” conflates language and log hygiene. Severity on SUP-702 (“medium”) is unexplained next to low operational and medium governance risk in the matrix. High confidence on SUP-711 is incompatible with an indeterminate label.

**Net.** Dialectical fairness, authority/scope handling, and evidence discipline are well above typical. The judgment does not mush the two sides together. The score is held down because the two criteria the prompt weights hardest after fairness—model semantics and the quality of the revision—are exactly where the answer is loosest, and because a few case labels do not follow the burden of proof the author set.
