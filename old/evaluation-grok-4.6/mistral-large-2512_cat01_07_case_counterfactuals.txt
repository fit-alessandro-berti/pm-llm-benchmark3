**5.2**

The answer is well-structured and captures several intended qualitative points (K-337 is a decision-policy change; four-eyes and final-compliance-after-assessment are preserved; C1 reservations do not scale; D2 trades queue time for quality; timestamps can fake concurrency). It is not close to a high score. The quantitative skeleton is unreliable, queue snapshots are largely ignored, and several capacity claims are backwards. Those are core failures for this task, not presentation nits.

**1. Cycle-time arithmetic is systematically wrong and internally contradictory.**  
K-104 runs Mon 08:10–Wed 18:40 (~2.4 calendar days, ~58.5 h). The table’s “4.5 factual” is off by ~2 days. K-411 runs Tue 09:00–Wed 14:30 (~1.23 days). “2.2 factual” is invented. K-219 is exactly 48 h; “2.5 factual” is sloppy. Only K-337’s ~8.5–9.0 day baseline is roughly right.

Worse, K-104’s own derivation ends Decision on Wednesday afternoon in every scenario, including the “worst” full 6 h review from Wed 08:00. That cannot produce a 5.5-day cycle. The table (3.5–5.5 vs 4.5) and the event list do not describe the same counterfactual. Once baselines are inflated, every “saved 0.4–1.0 days” claim, the ranking, and the pilot power calculations are untrustworthy.

**2. Queue snapshots are treated as optional color, not constraints.**  
This is the central methodological miss.

- Mon 16:00: C1 = 18 h; Tue 08:00: C1 = 16 h; Wed 08:00: C1 = 8 h. Starting C1 preliminary work Tue 08:00–12:00 *and* parking a Wed 08:00–14:00 reserved slot requires jumping a large backlog and the “two urgent compliance cases” added Monday. That is not a minimal-change holding of other behavior fixed; it is preemption. The write-up still treats Wed 08:00 as the expected start.
- Mon 16:00: D2 = 5 h. D2 cannot start K-219 at Mon 17:30. A queue-respecting start is closer to late Monday evening, and only if D2 works nights — never argued.
- Tue 08:00: A2 = 6 h. K-219’s best-case Assessment at Tue 09:30 is infeasible. K-411’s base-case Assessment at Tue 16:00–18:00 is optimistic in the same way: earlier *readiness* is not earlier *service*.

The exercise explicitly says queue estimates are forecasts, not guarantees. Forecasts still bind a *minimal* counterfactual. Ignoring them turns “reserve a slot / start immediately” into magic capacity.

**3. Partial order and constraints are sloppy, and K-219’s control flow is wrong.**  
A “partial-order relation set” is written as space-separated traces plus comments. Four-eyes is written `Assessment.resource  Compliance Review.resource` with the inequality missing everywhere. K-219 asserts `Identity Check || Document Check` only after originals; Rule 4 says Identity may proceed *before* originals, which is exactly what the factual trace does (Identity ends Mon 16:20; originals arrive 17:30). The feasibility model also bakes the K-337 intervention into the allowed language, mixing the normative model with one policy change.

**4. Incomplete early packets are named, then not priced.**  
K-104’s S1 review Mon 16:00–18:00 occurs before any document check (D1 finishes Tue 05:40). A triage-time sanctions flag is not a document file. Crediting 2–3 h off final C1 from that Monday packet is unjustified. Tuesday-morning C1 pre-reading is more plausible *if C1 were free*, which the snapshot says it is not. The stated complication (“early packets may be incomplete”) is treated as a footnote rather than a reason to shrink or defer the claimed saving.

**5. Capacity / interference mechanics are incorrect in multiple cells.**  
- K-104: reserving C1 should *grow* the compliance wait for non-target high-risk work, not shrink the C1 queue. Identity/Document/Assessment demand does not increase; listing those queues as “likely to grow” is unexplained.  
- K-411: parallelizing I2 and D2 does **not** add 1.5–3.5 h + 4–8 h of demand. The work already exists. Local demand change is ~0 plus workaround overhead. “Assessment queues grow by ~200–500 hours” in a 100-case rollout confuses *workload* with *wait*. Same assessment hours, earlier arrivals, higher peak congestion — that distinction is the whole point and is fumbled.  
- K-219: adversely affected cases are D2’s other work, not “cases needing D1.” D1 becoming freer is a benefit to complex-document cases.  
- K-337: bringing compliance forward to Friday can displace other C1 work; “queue likely to grow: None” is too clean. External 800–3600 h is elapsed waiting, not resource hours.

The 100-case C1 arithmetic (200–400 h vs ~40 h/week) is the one interference calculation that actually lands. D2 substitution at 800–1400 h is directionally right but presented as if D2 could absorb it. Required discussion of timestamp behavior vs true concurrency is almost absent from the rollout section.

**6. Uncertainty is performed, not calibrated.**  
Ranges exist, but several numbers are fake precision: D2 rework “20–40% vs 5%,” K-337 “60% conditions / 40% reject,” night completions to the half-hour. Service-time guidance is misapplied (D1 standard is 3–6 h, not 4–8 h; 8–14 h is D2 *complex*, while K-219 is “standard but handwritten”). Worst cases sometimes lengthen cycle time without a mechanism that fits the same timeline. Evidence-that-would-reverse for K-219 (“if D1 is idle at Mon 17:30”) contradicts the D1 = 11 h snapshot.

**7. Ranking and pilots inherit the bad baselines.**  
K-411 as rank 1 is defensible on *feasibility and reversibility*, not on “benefits insensitive to external factors” — the authors’ own assessment-queue caveat contradicts that sentence. K-337 last is reasonable (policy change, residual discrepancy, appeal/loss). K-104 reversibility is overstated as “low”: a reservation can be released; an issued K-337 decision cannot. Mixing case-level and policy-level reversibility makes the column incoherent.

Pilots have the required rows, then stumble: K-219 stop-if-“D1 queue shrinks >50%” treats a benefit as a hazard; 100 handwritten-original cases in two weeks is an implausible support; primary outcome is cycle time only on an intervention whose main risk is rework/quality.

**What is actually good.**  
Format compliance is high. K-337 keeps the first loop and forbids silent acceptance. Final compliance stays after the last assessment; A1 ≠ C1. Single-case vs scale is attempted. Incomplete packets, C1 displacement, D2 handwriting risk, and false concurrency are all *mentioned*. Assumption labels are legal and the list is the required length.

Those are the ingredients of a 7–8 if the clocks, queues, and conservation of work were right. They are not. A process-mining counterfactual that misstates factual cycle times by a day or more and starts scarce resources through 8–16 h published backlogs is not a small deduction. The prose looks finished; the mechanism is not.
