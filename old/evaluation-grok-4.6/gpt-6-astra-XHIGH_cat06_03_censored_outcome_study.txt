**7.3**

The answer is conceptually strong on the points this prompt is designed to catch: activation is treated as selection, omitted applications are not relabeled as non-default, clocks are separated, leakage fields are dropped, and a random split is rejected. It is not nearly flawless. Several design choices and the executable skeleton contain real statistical and implementation flaws.

**Target and estimand.** The six-state first-disposition formulation is a legitimate primary estimand and is clearly distinguished from conditional default, survival after activation, and counterfactual approval. The factorization through \(F_A\) and \(q_{90}(X,u)\) is coherent and does not feed eventual activation time into \(X\). Two problems remain. First, the 180-day disposition window is an unmotivated study constant, not derived from process times, and it is poorly matched to this snapshot: a full 180+90+35-day path is only supported for starts through about 16 April 2025, entirely before the 1 July 2025 policy change. That undercuts the claim that the primary target is operationally defensible on these data. Second, the business request is default from the first few events; the primary model is \(k=1\) at Application Started, where most process features are empty. Later landmarks are allowed to redefine both the at-risk population and a fresh 180-day clock, so they are not the same estimand.

**Cohort.** The 48,000-row ledger is complete and the snapshot-versus-horizon distinction is stated. The table still blurs snapshot accounting with target roles, and the 3,600 unlabeled activations are correctly left unpartitioned in prose but are not reconstructed as the required labeled/censored/competing/selectively-unobserved/not-yet-mature flow with dates. Linked transfers (≈1,740 / 1,160) are handled as a split-dependence issue rather than extra rows, which is right.

**Censoring.** The five mechanisms are separated, administrative censoring at coverage (not last event) is correct, rejects are not inverse-probability reweighted into fake non-defaults, and testable vs untestable assumptions are mostly well classified. Making stabilized IPW the primary attack on missing feeds is inconsistent with the same section’s claim that partner missingness makes independent censoring implausible; pattern-mixture/bounds should have been co-primary. Cause-specific versus Fine-Gray/Aalen–Johansen for the four disposition hazards is unspecified. The `observed90` rule `available_at >= activation+90` is a real ascertainment error: a valid early default whose feed arrives before day 90 is treated as unobserved and can bias the default component downward. The 35-day lag gate described in prose is not the same rule.

**Prefixes and features.** No padding, no “will die before \(k\)” flag, vintage-limited aliasing, and refusal to treat PM4Py service/waiting times as measured processing time are all correct. Quarantining every same-timestamp pair is too destructive for ordinary logs (start and KYC often share a timestamp) and can gut the cohort. Section 5 promises origin channel, region, and requested amount; the skeleton never attaches them. Queue/DFG features are described as training-cutoff reconstructible and then omitted.

**Leakage table.** This is one of the better sections: `final_limit`, `decision_reason`, `collection_status`, future events, `documents_total`, overwritten `risk_score`, extraction-time tenure, and label fields are all handled with keep/repair/drop logic. Static attributes and `source_system` are rightly treated as versioned, not timeless.

**Validation.** A start-time split and the explanation of why a random split fails are right. Follow-up arithmetic (17 Nov / 13 Oct / 4 Jul / 16 Apr) is accurate. The schedule is not. A 1 May 2024 fit has almost no lag-mature 90-day labels (earliest activation plus 90 plus 35 is about 5 May 2024), so step 1 cannot support the A-D/A-N component. The “test” period after 2 April 2025 mostly cannot be scored on the primary six-way outcome without selecting on fast resolution. `policy_era` / `migration_era` are constant on every honestly trainable cohort in this plan. Metrics for competing risks, refusal to score unknowns as O or A-N, and subgroup/calibration requirements are appropriate.

**Limitations.** Predictive association, selection, and counterfactual default are separated, and the warning that a low A-D probability can be a high rejection probability is exactly the decision-use boundary needed. Five deployment feedback loops are listed. This section is close to the required standard.

**Skeleton (largest gap between prose and code).** `case_table(cutoff)`, the event allowlist, no padding, and the survival comments are in the right spirit. The runnable path is not an as-of pipeline: prefixes and targets are built from `case_table(EXTRACT)` and then sliced by start date, so training rows can carry labels and later-landmark events that were unknown at `TRAIN_FIT`. The certified replay branch never builds prefixes or targets. `snapshot_group` does not reproduce the seven-row 48,000 table (no default/non-default split, no 3,600 accounting). Forbidden-column asserts do not implement the point-in-time repairs promised for `risk_score` or documents. Linked-group purging is a comment. `X.index.equals(Y.index)` is brittle. Line count sits at the top of the requested band.

The piece would sit near the top of a normal scale because it refuses the usual biased cohort. Under the requested standard, the infeasible 180-day evaluation window, the `available_at >= horizon_end` rule, the empty first fit, and a skeleton that does not actually enforce the as-of discipline it describes are enough to keep it well below a high score.
