**6.3**

The answer is complete, well-structured, and makes the right high-level call (reject a blanket copy; segment; treat Site A as confounded). It is not close to flawless. Several process and quantitative errors are material, and a strict reading of the rubric should penalize them heavily.

**What is actually strong.** Format compliance is high: all ten headings, the required tables, the required node list, a non-sequential Site B POWL, a median-up/tail-down scenario, and a pilot that tries to separate fidelity from mechanism. Site A’s −9 h median is correctly refused as an identified treatment effect. The answer does not authorize `Document Check` before legally required originals. Co-interventions (API, +2 FTE, mix shift, huddles, timestamp inflation) are named. The assumption register and the mechanism-versus-implementation discussion are the best parts of the writeup.

**Process-structure errors.** Site B’s rules already allow `Identity Check` and `Originals Receipt` after `Register`. The DFG confirms that path is common (`Register → Identity Check` 2,050; `Register → Originals Receipt` 1,860; `Identity Check → Originals Receipt` 1,020). Selling “run identity during the 18 h mail wait” as the core physical-case design is mostly restating the status quo. The 6% “overlapping verification intervals” statistic is identity∩document, not identity∩mail wait. Identity can already finish during postal delay without appearing in that 6%. Physical-case cycle-time savings are therefore overstated, and the optimistic all-case range (−4 h to −7 h median) is not supported by Site B’s existing partial order.

**False equivalences.** “Originals legally required for 62%” is treated as “38% digital.” Those are not the same. The 23% / 15% strata further assume independence of digital channel and legal-review status; nothing in the evidence justifies that. Both numbers are then used as if they were measured.

**Internal quantitative contradiction.** Section 3 attributes only about 3–4 h of Site A’s 9 h drop to concurrency at a ~90% digital site. Section 5 then gives Site B’s digital cohort 6–9 h. That cannot be reconciled with the author’s own confounding analysis. Utilization 0.82→0.95, Kingman tail claims, the 3–4 h / 5–6 h split, and “>85% power” for *N* = 1,200 are invented precision. Eight weeks at Site B’s throughput is ~1,040 completions, not 1,200, and is too short for 90-day appeals. `appeal → cycle_time` in the DAG is the wrong causal direction: 90-day appeal is a post-completion quality endpoint.

**Smaller defects that still count.** `S_treatment` does not match the required `S_` naming pattern. API upgrade, added FTE, and the absent negative control are folded or omitted rather than given full evidence-transfer rows. Copying Site A’s WIP = 6 is not adapted to 3.4 h vs 8.1 h specialized tasks. The PM4Py sketch is in the right family, but `check_soundness(...)[0]` is version-fragile if the API returns a boolean, and duplicate same-label transitions are uncommented. Queue-split SUTVA controls are barely feasible with pools of 4 / 7 / 3 / 2.

The recommendation `conditional go` is defensible, but it rests on an inflated physical-case mechanism and inconsistent effect sizes. That is enough to keep the score well below the top band.
