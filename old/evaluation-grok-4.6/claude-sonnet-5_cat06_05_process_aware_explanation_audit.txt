**7.2**

The audit is structurally complete and substantively strong: it catches obvious full-trace leaks, subtler lineage bugs, preserves congestion and prefix-age, treats `Appeal -> Settlement Decision Recorded` as process-legal but post-`t0`, and keeps predictive / causal / fairness standards apart. It is not close to flawless. Several errors and instruction misses are material under a strict reading.

**Factual error.** Case X’s revised narrative says the two dominant drivers were both leakage (appeal and `final_decision`). They were not. The local attributions are appeal +0.26, **queue 18 +0.14**, complexity +0.11, channel +0.07, **final decision +0.06**. Queue is the second-largest driver and is one of the features the audit correctly wants to keep. That is a clear misread of the evidence being audited.

**Direct instruction miss.** The prompt forbids reproducing leaked future outcomes in revised narratives. Case X still tells the reader the case has an appeal and a recorded settlement decision. Case Y states that the variant “encodes the eventual appeal and closure sequence,” which discloses this case’s actual path (`…DEC-APP-DEC-CLOSE`). Those facts belong only in a removal-reason written generically (“complete-trace path ID”), not in the corrected story of the case.

**Process-awareness gap.** Replacing `full_trace_variant_id` with `prefix_variant_id` is formally right and operationally empty at this `t0`: the admissible prefix is almost always `Claim Registered -> Coverage Check Completed`, so the repaired feature is nearly constant. The same holds for a repaired `num_document_requests_before_t0`. The audit notices the latter is “structurally 0” under the normative model, then overstates that as an empirical guarantee. Logs can deviate; a prefix replay could be non-zero, and that would be a conformance finding, not proof the column is vacuous.

**Actionability overclaim.** Section 3 calls `queue_length_at_t0` “a legitimate, actionable operational lever.” The brief explicitly says predictive importance is not an operationally actionable reason. Congestion is the best *candidate* for an intervention, but SHAP-plus-association does not license staffing/triage claims. Section 4 later walks this back for Case X; the two sections contradict each other.

**Contaminated attributions treated as still valid.** After arguing the model is soaked in duration, appeal, variant, and outcome features, the audit still presents remaining directions (high queue, high complexity, broker, reserve) as “credible drivers.” Those SHAP signs come from a leakage-trained model and need retraining before they are explanatory. “Recompute / don’t trust 0.82” is the right status; treating leftover local attributions as if they survive is not.

**Inconsistent case status and channel handling.** Case X is `recompute` and Case Y is `invalidate` despite similar leaked mass (appeal +0.26 and `final_decision` +0.06 vs variant +0.31 and a broken prior-count +0.05). The variant is uglier, but the split is under-argued. `claimant_channel` is a “defensible predictive signal” in §3 and a fairness-review proxy in §4.

**Smaller defects that still count.** Severity is not restricted to the required four-level enum (`Medium/High`, `Low/Medium`). `same_day_document_count_snapshot` is only analyzed as post-`t0` inclusion; a 23:00 batch can also be *stale* if `t0` is after 23:00. The prior-count SQL (`<= extraction_date`) can include the current claim, not only later claims. Repairable fields are parked in “unusable” with no post-repair class (prior count → history signal; same-day docs → prefix volume; assigned team → proxy). Week 52 is left as a generic calendar proxy rather than tied to the stated holiday/policy confound. “Legitimate signal” is an odd finding type in a leakage table for items whose only live issue is a ±5 minute join window.

**What remains genuinely good.** Critical leaks (`total_case_duration_hours`, appeal flags, full-trace variant, `final_decision`, `last_resource_role`) are correctly killed. `final_handler_team` is handled as a closure-time field that only coincidentally matches `t0` 65% of the time. `prior_claim_count` and nightly document snapshots are caught as lineage, not as conceptual nonsense. Region and week are kept with proxy/drift caveats rather than purged. The validation suite (prefix bounds, footprint vs DFG, alignments, tied timestamps as *unknown* order, enabled vs occurred vs impossible, Declare-style second decision iff appeal after Partial/Deny) is the right design and states DFG limitations clearly. Case Z’s uncertainty note is the correct posture. Section 5’s priority stack matches the ask.

A top score required no invented “dominant” pair, no future-path spoilers in revised narratives, an explicit “clean-feature SHAP is still untrustworthy until retrain,” and the observation that several proposed repairs are constant at this exact prediction point. This is a strong auditor’s draft, not a finished one.
