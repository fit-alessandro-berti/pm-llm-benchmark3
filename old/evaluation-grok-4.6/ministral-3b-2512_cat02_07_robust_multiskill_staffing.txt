This submission has the right skeleton and almost none of the required substance. It would fail a process-mining / operations review on evidence use, arithmetic, internal consistency, the comparison design, and the Python contract. The structure is not a substitute for a working analysis.

## What little works

The nine required headings are present. Three policies are named. Two objective formulas are written down. A few secondary risks (P3 starvation, Validation coordination, interpretability) are mentioned. That is the entire credit.

## Section 1 — assumptions and objectives are thin and partly wrong

- Treating block means as Poisson is fine; rounding 2.1 expected ME cases to 2 is not. Offered-load work should keep fractional expectations.
- Uniform draws on the given ranges are acceptable, but there is no bound/midpoint/pessimistic-tail discussion and no resource-multiplier treatment in the assumption layer.
- ME/ES logic is inverted and factually false: “defaults to sequential unless a cross-skilled resource exists (e.g., Emil handles ME).” Emil has **no Mechanical skill**. Cross-skilled ME resources are Ada and Deepa (and Farah, slowly). Emil is ES-capable, not ME-capable.
- Objective 1 is weighted tardiness on “delay − SLA,” not tail/SLA risk. The stated SLAs are **elapsed-time attainment rates** (e.g., 90% of P1 inside 120 minutes). A mean-excess-delay sum does not encode p90/p95 or attainment.
- Objective 2 mixes max utilization with “queue length variance” and then talks about “90% capacity,” which is not the 90% **planned utilization** fairness rule in the prompt. Robustness across U1–U5 is not in the objective at all.

## Section 2 — the capacity analysis is not auditable because the numbers are wrong

This is the decisive analytical failure.

- 08:00–12:00 demand is \(7+9=16\), not \(7+9+7=23\). The extra 7 is the 12:00–14:00 block.
- “Ada/Ben can handle ~100 min/day (8h × 120 min)” is incoherent. An 8-hour shift is 480 minutes before breaks, not 100, and \(8\times120=960\) is also not 100.
- Farah is **08:00–12:00** (4 hours, no break), not an 8-hour shift and not “12:00–12:00 … 20 min/day.”
- “20 ME cases” is invented. Day expected arrivals are 42; ME share implies about **8.3** ME cases, not 20.
- Intake is treated as Ada/Ben (plus a misunderstood Farah). **Every resource can do Intake.**
- Chen is described as a 12:00–16:00 resource. Chen is **08:00–16:00**.
- U3 (“Farah’s ME/E workload doubles”) misses the actual hole: Chen is the only morning/midday person who is fast at Electrical **and** can do Software. Farah is 1.4× on diagnostics and gone at noon. Deepa/Emil/Ada matter here; Farah does not absorb a doubled ME/E day.
- U4 is essentially dropped from the load table.
- There is no skill × block offered-load using mix, both ME/ES branches, speed multipliers, breaks, Deepa’s 10:00 start, Emil’s 12:00 start, Farah’s noon exit, or the 16:00–20:00 Software/Validation cliff (only Emil). Validation is never sized. Handover/setup/preemption minutes are never added to load. Parallel vs sequential critical path is never computed.

A capacity section that misreads the skill matrix, the shifts, and the arrival table cannot identify bottlenecks or bottleneck migration.

## Section 3 — policies are not implementable as written

- Specialization routes Mechanical to Farah. Farah is the **slowest** Mechanical resource (1.40) and works only four morning hours. Ada/Ben are 1.00 on Mechanical. That is not a serious specialist design.
- Overflow = **reject P1** after 10–15 minutes of busy time. Incident intake does not reject P1 work. That rule is operationally illegitimate and makes later SLA numbers meaningless.
- Preemption thresholds (>10% complete vs <50% complete) contradict the prompt (P1 may preempt; restart penalty = 10% of **remaining** time; at most one preemption) and are not justified.
- Handover (7), cross-team (5), skill-family setup (5), 30-minute overtime, and 90% utilization fairness are not built into dispatch.
- Hybrid reserves “Farah for ME, Chen for E.” That reserves a short, slow resource for P1 ME and makes U3 (Chen absent) destroy the reservation by construction.

## Section 4 — required comparison table is missing

The prompt asked for a **policy-by-scenario** table with  
`mean_delay`, `p90_or_p95_delay`, `P1_SLA`, `P2_SLA`, `max_resource_utilization`, `utilization_fairness`, `handover_or_setup_burden`, `resilience`, `main_failure_mode`.

What appears is a **scenario-only** table: no policy dimension, no `P2_SLA`, no U4, and numbers with no derivation. You cannot tell which policy produced 70–85% P1 SLA. Utilizations of 105–115% ignore the 30-minute overtime cap. Without a policy × shock grid, Sections 5’s “hybrid is robust” claim is unsupported.

## Section 5 — recommendation is circular and contradictory

Hybrid is picked using the unaudited base ranges and the claim that reservation “prevents U3/U5 bottlenecks.” U3 removes Chen, the reserved Electrical resource. The fallback (full pooling when Chen is absent) admits the primary policy fails the shock it was said to survive.

Mode switches are backwards: move to **full pooling** after 98% utilization for two hours, and to **strict specialization** if P1 SLA < 60%. Specialization is the policy that usually **worsens** SLA under surge/absence. Robustness is never defined as a worst-case or regret metric over U1–U5.

## Section 6 — several risk stories are inverted

Skill erosion: pooling maintains unused skills; **reservation/specialization** rusts them. The text says the opposite.  
Gaming: “delay P1 to avoid preemption penalties” is not the natural incentive; inflating remaining-time estimates, avoiding P3, or hoarding reserved slots is.  
Fairness: hybrid is said both to keep every resource under 95% and to “penalize overflow P1,” while full pooling “ensures no resource is overloaded.” Those cannot all be true given the Section 4 saturations.

## Section 7 — incomplete relative to the spec

Missing required attributes: eligible-resource set, assignment decision, pause/resume, policy version, scenario/shock flag, SLA clock.  
PM4Py discussion does not use the mandated APIs (`format_dataframe`, `insert_case_service_waiting_time`, `get_service_time`). `max_queue_length` is not a PM4Py analysis. There is no design for drift in service-time distributions, stage-specific waiting vs case-level waiting, or handover-of-work vs setup-time separation.

## Section 9 — validation does not protect a live P1 process

“Simulate 1000 cases” is not a pilot. There is no shadow dispatch, no replay against a historical log, no P2/P3-only field test, and no holdout that keeps P1 on a known-safe rule.
