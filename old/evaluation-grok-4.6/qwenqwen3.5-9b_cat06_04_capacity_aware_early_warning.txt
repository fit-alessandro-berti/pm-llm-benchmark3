**5.1**

The write-up looks complete and hits the three headline traps (I-03 ineligible, I-05 ≠ I-07, spillovers exist), but it is not decision-theoretically coherent. Several of its own rules contradict the allocation, the impact table is not auditable, and the live portfolio choice mishandles time-to-deadline.

**Decision rule.** Eligibility by stage is correct. Almost everything else is loose or inconsistent. Calibration notes concern *observational* `p`, not the supplied ARR ranges; shifting ARR by the SME/Strategic bias is the wrong object. The `p_now > p_prev + 0.10` gate would exclude I-02 (Δ = 0.08), which is later selected. The NNLS rule (intervene only if ARR lower bound > 0.15) would *admit* I-09 (0.18–0.30), which is later waited. The RPS subtracts a constant portfolio spillover from every case, which cannot rank cases and double-counts a cost that depends on *how many* slots are used. Effect ranges are then treated as known midpoints, against the brief.

**Allocation.** Excluding I-03 and suppressing I-05 is right; reserving a slot is defensible. The chosen set {I-01, I-02, I-04} is not. I-04 has 10 hours left, Strategic over-prediction, and a €2,000 false-alarm burden — the option value of waiting is ignored. I-07 has 1.5 hours left, a genuine new log, SME under-prediction, cheap burden (€50), and ARR 0.20–0.32; it is dropped solely because €5k < €9k. That is the wrong comparison once the swarm is 45 minutes and the clock is 90 minutes. I-08’s midpoint direct EV is about `0.10 × 100000 − 450 − 5000 = +4550`, competitive with I-02, but it is dismissed as “low ARR” with no arithmetic. I-06 is “suspended” in the rule and “Eligible = Yes” in the table. Tiers do not map to actions.

**Impact table.** “Direct breaches avoided” is written as 3 / 2+1 / 1 case counts. It should be the *sum of ARRs* (roughly 0.7 / 0.6 / 0.3). Intervention cost in the table is €1,350 (3 × €450) and omits listed false-alarm burden (€250+€600+€2,000). The footnotes then use ~€4,600. Those two presentations cannot both be the official account. Spillover uses an unexplained €15k average value for six unnamed diagnosis-stage cases and does not note that intervening on I-02 removes one diagnosis-stage patient from the untreated set. Adverse is described as I-01/I-02 “failing to breach”; the brief’s adverse case is *ineffectual intervention* (wrong reason for the alert), not a false-negative-of-breach. Precision such as €17,580 is unwarranted.

**Temporal policy.** The I-05 vs I-07 distinction is the strongest part of the memo. The 2-hour cool-down is arbitrary relative to the 30-minute refresh, would be dangerous if applied near a 1.5-hour deadline, and is not aligned with I-02 (second alert, no prior swarm, still selected). Escalation vs re-entry after `Diagnosis Complete` is confused, because the swarm is already powerless then.

**Fairness.** Required topics are named, but the concrete safeguard is immediately violated: “no more than 40% of slots to Strategic Enterprise,” then 2 of 3 selected cases are Strategic. I-01 is mislabeled “Public/SME.” The feedback-loop discussion mixes “false positive intervention” labels with the actual problem (missing untreated counterfactuals). Inverse-probability weighting is named, not specified. SME under-prediction plus value ranking is a *compound* bias against I-05/I-07/I-10 and is not treated as such.

**Monitoring.** The five buckets are present, which is structurally fine. Identification via propensity-score matching after value-based selection will not recover ARR. There is no holdout/randomized unused slot, no explicit intervention log for label surgery, no directional check that SME remains under and Strategic over, and a fallback “wait until ARR > 0.20” that reintroduces a single threshold. “Reserve used >1 time” is ill-defined for a single reserved slot. No rule releases the reserve after 15:00 if the regulatory case never arrives (the natural place to put I-07).

**Net.** Format-compliant and not a naive `p_now` ranking, but too many self-contradictions and one serious timing error to score higher. A strong policy would intervene on the time-critical, evidence-changing case, wait on long-horizon high-value triage, put costs and expected breaches in the same units in the scenario table, and not break its own fairness cap.
