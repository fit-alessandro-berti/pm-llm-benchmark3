The answer has the right high-level shape—A/B last, G not enabled, a conformant second-review primary path, some parallel information-gathering—but it is analytically sloppy, internally contradictory, and wrong on several process and timing facts. Under a strict reading it is not close to an expert solution.

**Ranking errors.** Ranking E third is a serious mistake. Additional customer evidence takes 6–24 hours and almost certainly burns the 90-minute deadline; it cannot compete with D (15–30 minutes plus a still-mandatory second review) or with a time-boxed S2/S4 choice. Standalone D is essentially the sequential core of the author’s own rank-1 plan, yet it is placed below both E and F. F is also too high: approval after a conformant review is given as 0.55–0.75, evidence already moved the score from 78 to 72, and the fuzzy match is only 0.46. Immediate rejection of an established-supplier payment is not a near-substitutable alternative to completing the required review. The table therefore does not implement a coherent preference: it is a formatted list, not a dominance ranking.

**Broken timelines.** The S2 vs S4 comparison treats S2’s 35–75 minute review as comparable to S4’s 35–65 minute *wait*, omitting S4’s subsequent review. C itself is 40–90 minutes against a 90-minute deadline, so the claimed €20k–€35k deadline benefit is an upside, not an expectation. The fallback is temporally impossible: “use C if S4 is delayed beyond 65 minutes” would interrupt P-901 when that case has about five minutes left on a 70-minute deadline. Any S2 decision must be time-boxed early, not after waiting out S4. The rerank trigger “if S4 becomes available in <35 minutes, switch to the composite plan” is vacuous—the composite *is* the primary recommendation.

**Process-model gaps and contradictions.** After Evidence Validated the prescribed cycle returns to Analyst Review; the answer jumps to second-officer review and never names that step. Auto-clear is only available below 40 with no alert; the write-up repeatedly uses “risk <70” as if that removed second review. Rule 6 requires second-officer review for *either* risk ≥70 *or* an unresolved fuzzy match. Section 6 then says: if screening “resolves” the match, abort S4 and do a “single review” if risk <70. That is inconsistent with the same section’s execution steps (always S4) and is easy to read as dropping four-eyes while a match might still be unresolved. Reject, hold, and SAR are conflated; SAR does not authorize execution and must not be disclosed to the customer (relevant to E). The stale 12-minute risk score and the warning that observational predictions may not transport under intervention are ignored.

**Falsified quantities.** P-901’s incremental breach risk is attached to P-884’s €20k–€35k figure. P-901 is only “another high-value payment”; that monetization is invented. Harm columns are equally loose: C is charged the full €15k–€80k customer-harm band even though C is a review that may approve. Rank 1 claims both the full deadline save and “high (false clear)” harm. Regulatory 10–50× is overstated relative to the given ranges (closer to ~3–25× on money alone).

**What is actually sound.** Conformance labels for A, B, F, and G are correct. A and B are rightly treated as unacceptable regardless of a favorable forecast. Alternate screening is not sold as a substitute for second review in the table. Parallel D while waiting for S4, and reluctance to cannibalize P-901, are the right *kind* of plan. The closing remark that the process is not a utility maximizer is the one genuinely sharp insight.

**Net.** Format compliance and the demotion of unlawful shortcuts keep this out of the failure band. The E/D/F ordering, the impossible C fallback, the <70 / single-review contradiction, the omitted post-evidence analyst step, and the fabricated P-901 loss keep it far from a high score. Confidence “High” is not earned.
