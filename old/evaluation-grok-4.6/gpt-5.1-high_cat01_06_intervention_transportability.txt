**6.1**

The write-up is a competent transportability memo with a structurally invalid process model, several causal-graph mistakes, and internal quantitative contradictions. Under a strict rubric that treats model fidelity as a first-class criterion, that is well below “nearly flawless.”

**What is actually strong.** Sections 1, 3, 4, 7, and 9 do the main analogical job. Site A’s 9-hour median drop is not treated as an identified effect. Co-interventions (API, +2 FTEs, mix shift, huddles, timestamp inflation, no negative control) are named and carried into the transfer table. The answer refuses a blanket copy, keeps originals-gated `Document Check` from starting early, and includes a median-up / p90-down scenario. The pilot’s V1 vs V2 split is the right instinct: unbundle control-flow from huddles/WIP. The recommendation (`conditional go`, digital / no-originals / non-legal first) is directionally correct.

**Section 2 is not a clean transport DAG.** `digital_documents -> originals_required` is the wrong direction and the wrong kind of arrow; those are competing case attributes, not a causal path. `case_complexity -> legal_capacity` confuses demand with capacity. Site A’s API upgrade and huddles are co-interventions, not “shared mechanisms.” The omitted edge `originals_required -> true_overlap` is the central modifier of the treatment. Direct `treatment_parallelization -> waiting_before_assessment` bypasses the mediator the prose claims to care about. Capacity nodes never point at `true_overlap`, even though specialization and thin pools are why overlap may not occur.

**Section 5 reimports the unidentified 9 hours.** After correctly refusing to identify Site A’s −9 h, the “optimistic” cell still posts −6 to −10 h for the “similar” stratum. That is transferring the confounded composite, not a mechanism. Eligible shares are not pinned to the DFG. The log already identifies the legal share exactly (`Assessment -> Legal Review` = 1,360 / 3,400 = 40%) and a supplement rate of 360/1,360 ≈ 26.5%. Neither is used to bound tail or rework scenarios. Identity can already start after `Register` at B (2,050 `Register -> Identity Check`); the incremental treatment is early document work where lawful, plus overlap with originals receipt. That bound is never computed from service times (3.4 h identity vs 18 h originals delay vs 8.1 h document).

**Section 6 over-blocks and then contradicts itself.** Legal review sits *after* assessment. Parallel identity/document work is not legally impossible for the 40% legal-review cases; the risk is downstream congestion. Excluding them from eligibility conflates a bottleneck modifier with a statutory start constraint. The same section then writes a partial order for the “digital/no-legal segment” that still contains `Originals Receipt`, `Legal Review`, and `Request Supplement`, and it places `Assessment < Approve` *and* `Assessment < Reject` *and* `Legal Review < Approve`. A strict partial order over a single activity set cannot encode XOR or an optional loop; it says every activity occurs, including both approve and reject.

**Section 8 fails the model-fidelity bar.** The required Site B object is a POWL with a strict partial order *and* an optional legal branch *and* a supplement loop, with `Assessment` waiting on both checks. The code dumps every activity into one `StrictPartialOrder`, so the intended net is: always originals before document, always legal, always supplement, always approve *and* reject. That is not Site B and is not the candidate intervention. Standard PM4Py POWL construction uses `Transition` / `SilentTransition` / `OperatorPOWL` (`XOR`, `LOOP`) and `StrictPartialOrder(nodes=...).order.add_edge(...)`. `Event`, `POWL(spo)`, and `StrictPartialOrder(activities=..., order=set_of_tuples)` are not that API. Site A trees and the “what the net cannot encode” comments are fine; they do not rescue an unsound control-flow object. A sequential-looking failure mode the prompt warned against was avoided only in the weak sense that some edges are concurrent; choice and iteration were not modeled at all.

**Pilot arithmetic and unit of assignment do not work.** 3,400 cases / 6 months, 25–30% eligible ⇒ ~140–170 eligible cases per month. A 3–4 month, two-arm study cannot deliver 600–800 per arm. Median cycle time is 44 h and p90 is 110 h, so shift-cluster assignment cuts across in-flight cases and does not block interference. Stepped-wedge on top of cluster randomization is unspecified. These are not polish issues; they undermine the claim that the pilot can separate mechanism failure from implementation failure at the sample sizes advertised.

**Smaller cuts that still matter under a harsh bar.** Rework is duplicated in the feature table. Copying “six open verification tasks” onto specialized pools of 4 / 7 / 3 / 2 is not a mapped WIP rule. Timestamp reliability is discussed, but DFG frequencies are almost unused. Section 10 drops the required `assumption — why needed — how to test — consequence if false` template. None of these would sink a near-flawless answer; stacked on the POWL error and the sample-size contradiction, they keep the score near 6, not 8.

The decision recommendation would still be usable. The artifact as a whole is not, because the process model and the quantitative scaffolding do not implement the structure the prose describes.
