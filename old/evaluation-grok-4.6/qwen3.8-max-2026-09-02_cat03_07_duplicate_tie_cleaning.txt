**7.4**

The final nine-section write-up is complete, conservative where it should be, and aligned with the hard constraints (keep e05/e14, do not order by ingestion, keep original timestamps, do not treat a total order as physical truth). The decision tree, pair table, ordering policy, provenance schema, and failure modes are the strongest parts. It is not nearly flawless.

**What is right.** The tree separates technical clones, multi-system observations, summaries, harmonization collisions, concurrent same-label events, and unresolved cases, with explicit terminals (drop / merge / refine / retain / quarantine). The pair table covers every required pair and treats e03/e04 and e11/e16 as evidenced merges, e03/e05 and e13/e14 as distinct lifecycles, e08/e09 as a label collision, and e06/e07 plus e15 as abstraction-level records. Ordering rules distinguish semantic vs deterministic cues and correctly warn that synthetic microseconds, if applied before a semantic order exists, bake a false story into durations and DFGs. Conservative retention of 15 events (drop only e04 and e16) is arithmetically and conceptually sound. The `have_common_object=True` discussion for e13/e14 is the right objection. Sensitivity counts (17/44, 15/39, 14/37, 13/34) check out, and the named edges (`Pick Item → Pick Item`, `Pack Package → Pack Package`, `Dispatch Package → Dispatch Package`, capture self-loops, etc.) are the ones that actually change meaning.

**Material gaps.**

1. **“Mechanically aggressive PM4Py merging” is not mechanical.** After `ocel_merge_duplicates`, the count of 13 is produced by the same hand-picked `merge_verified_pair` calls, still shielding e05/e14. A library merge on activity+timestamp (or activity+timestamp+any shared object) would also collapse the three 10:00 picks and the three 12:00 captures, i.e. a much lower count and the exact damage the prompt asked to quantify. The warning is present; the damaged baseline is not.

2. **PM4Py-awareness is thinner than claimed.** Real OCELs keep E2O in `ocel.relations`. The helper only mutates an events `related_objects` cell. After merges, a relations table would be stale; integrity checks never look at it. Top-level `pm4py.ocel_*` signatures are invented and then wrapped in `try/except` / `is not None`, so the block is a sketch, not an executable pipeline. `candidate_precheck` is computed and then ignored; library calls are not gated on it.

3. **Canonical identity is left open.** e11 vs e16 is “e11, or e16 if billing is system of record.” That is not a deterministic merge policy. e03-as-canonical is reasonable (richer WMS payload) but never justified against the scanner as the original observation.

4. **Implementation fragility.** Nested lists assigned with `.loc`, `pd.isna` on list-valued cells, `semantic_order` fillna via DataFrame index, and `fillna` on a mapped activity series are all easy ways for the merge/order steps to corrupt rows. Provenance columns are attached to the canonical row, but dropped source rows are not written to an audit table in code, so section 8 is not actually implemented.

5. **Small unclarities.** Section 3’s PSP order is written as spaced ids rather than a stated precedence. Evidence-based merge of e06/e07 is still only medium-confidence derivation, yet it is treated as the default “evidence” scenario. Prose is at or past the 2,900-word cap.

The analysis of *what not to collapse* is strong. The aggressive/library contrast and the PM4Py artifact are not. That is why this is a solid design note, not a near-flawless one.
