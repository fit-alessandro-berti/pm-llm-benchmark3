**6.2**

The final write-up follows the required seven-section format, ranks G1–G8 in mostly the right order, and is strongest where it matters most: it does not treat equal timestamps as proof of simultaneous work, it separates operational batches from logging batches, and the G6 “inflated wait / zero service” mechanism is correctly explained.

It is not close to flawless.

**Factual errors.** G3 queue-to-start waits are about 18–22 min, not “22–50 min”; queue-to-complete is 25–50 min, not “38–50 min.” That is a plain misread of the table in the performance section. G1’s “400% utilization” wording confuses item-minutes with resource occupancy. G5’s four-way overlap (~13:12–13:20) is incompatible with a hard “up to three analysts” cap, but the diagnosis table never treats that as counterevidence.

**Over-certainty and thin seconds.** G2 `physical_concurrency_plausible = No` is too strong (staggered completes only rule out simultaneous finishing). G4 has no second explanation. Assumptions are used throughout but almost never labeled as such. Throughput and conformance are not carried systematically across clusters.

**Section 4 does not meet its own spec.** The parser never uses the format strings; `start_timestamp` is reattached by row position after `format_dataframe` (order-breaking); lifecycle events are sorted so `complete` precedes `start`; `convert_to_interval_dataframe` / `merge_distance_threshold` look like guessed APIs. If those calls fail, the required PM4Py-vs-pandas comparison is empty. That is a material miss, not a nit.

The redesign and final include/exclude table are competent. The errors above are enough that a strict reading cannot go much higher.
