**7.0**

The write-up is a serious governance review: it follows the required 11-section skeleton, refuses to crown a winner by averaging scores, separates exploration from enforcement, reasons from concrete traces, and proposes a third design that is actually safer than S and less enumerative than C. Under a hypercritical reading, that is not enough for a high score. The core deliverable is internally inconsistent, several technical claims are wrong, and a few required details are only half-met.

**What is strong.** Use-case framing in §1 is the right lens: fitness/precision mean different things for exploration vs enforcement. The metric table in §2 covers every reported number and, correctly, treats precision-on-positives and seedless 80/20 splits as non-comparable evidence. The probe matrix in §5 is mostly exact: C rejects B2 because the only Auto Approval branch is `CC→TR→AA`; C rejects B6 because variant 7 redoes *both* checks; S accepts B3/B4 via `X(_, τ)` and `+`; S rejects B8 because each `X` child of `+` fires at most once. Those are easy places to bluff. The third model’s *intended* language—mandatory concurrent checks, optional SR/ER after checks, XOR approval, correction only through `CD` then a non-empty recheck—is a genuine alternative, not a blend of the two scores. The dissent names real weaknesses (unbounded correction, phasic lock-in, inert DC1/DC2) instead of a token caveat.

**Formalism and code do not describe the same model.** The boxed POWL uses `LOOP(concurrent(CC, TR), CD → recheck)`, i.e. standard `A(BA)*`: after every correction the *full* check pair must run again. The “trace language,” the Python (`LOOP(τ, correction)` after one check pair), and the B6/A2–A4 claims all describe `A B*` with a *partial* recheck and no forced second full pair. Under the formal loop, B6 is **not** in the language; under the code it is. That is not a wording slip. It is the central design (P6 / F6) specified two incompatible ways. “`LOOP(A,B)`: execute A, then exit or execute B and repeat” never says what repeats.

**The “self-contained PM4Py” block would not run as written.** `OperatorPOWL.XOR` / `OperatorPOWL.LOOP` are not the public constants; PM4Py wants `Operator.XOR` / `Operator.LOOP` from `pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj`, which is never imported. `play_out(..., noTraces=5000)` is also not the stable public signature. A skeleton may omit details; this one presents itself as assignable code.

**Metric and semantics errors.** Token-based generalization (`generalization_tbr`) is a replay-frequency / “how crowded are transitions” estimator, not a score for “accepting unseen token sequences.” A fully connected net does not automatically score 1.0. That column and pathology #6 argue from the wrong quantity. Thresholds wander: enforcement is “holdout ≥ 0.85” in §1 and “≥ 0.80” in §9–10. Deployment gate “all eight probes correct” treats B6 as settled even though the prompt marks it policy-dependent.

**Spec nits that matter under a strict rubric.** §3 is asked for five *admitted* bad or ambiguous traces. Items 1–4 qualify; item 5 is an *omission* (no `Correct Dossier`), not a word the model generates. Easy extra admits exist (ER between the two checks; SR with both checks skipped). DC1/DC2 are honest about missing attributes, but they are inactive comments—the running model still optionalizes Supplier Review for every case, which is the same control-flow hole as S for P3, only better labeled. Reviews are hard-sequenced after the entire correction loop; P3/P4 do not require that, and the dissent admits the model would reject overlapping SR/correction. That limitation belongs in §7, not only in the rebuttal. The dissent is a bit over the 150–250 word cap.

**Net.** This is competent skeptical PM review with a real third strategy and a correct probe table. It is not nearly flawless: the third model’s formal meaning disagrees with its code and with the audit tables, the PM4Py fragment is not executable, and generalization is misread. Those are not cosmetic.
