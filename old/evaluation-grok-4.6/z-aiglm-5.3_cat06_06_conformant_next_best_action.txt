**7.5**

The final six-section write-up is a strong conformance-first ranking with the right qualitative conclusion, but it is not nearly flawless. Several quantitative identifications are wrong or internally inconsistent, and those numbers are then used as if they were tight enough to support a “near-wash” portfolio claim.

**What is done well.** All six required sections appear with the specified headers and table columns. The four conformance labels are used exactly and in the right places: C/D/E and the two review composites as `CONFORMANT NOW`, F as `CONFORMANT IF STATED CONDITION HOLDS`, G as `EXCEPTION PATH NOT YET ENABLED`, A/B as `CURRENTLY NONCONFORMANT`. Unlawful shortcuts are ranked last and are not excused by a favorable operational forecast. Parallel screening plus an independent second review is the primary plan, with sequence, concurrency, and an S4 fallback made explicit. Rule 6 (risk ≥ 70 **or** unresolved match), four-eyes, SAR-does-not-authorize-execution, SAR non-disclosure, and the two-part override test are mapped correctly. A and B are excluded on deontic grounds, not just expected-value grounds. F and G each have a clear enablement condition. P-901, the API quota, and the S4-at-35 vs S4-at-65 reranking are all treated. Observational vs interventional forecasts are distinguished.

**Material analytical flaws.**

1. **C is identified with “continue normal processing.”** The table states C’s in-deadline probability as `0.36–0.52` because that “matches [the] stated ‘normal processing’ band” (breach 0.48–0.64). C is an *expedited interruption* of S2, with stated duration 35–75 minutes plus 5–15 minutes against a 90-minute deadline. That is not the baseline “continue normal processing” forecast. Using the baseline breach band for C understates C/P1’s speed advantage and inflates their residual-breach harm. The later claim that S2-now vs wait-for-S4 is a “near-wash” rests on this mis-identification.

2. **Deadline value is not filtered by approval.** “Estimated value of executing within the deadline” is only realized if the case is approved and executed. Ultimate approval after a conformant review is given as 0.55–0.75. The table’s €7–18k for P1/C is essentially `P(finish in time) × €20–35k` and omits `P(approve)`. A consistent figure is closer to €4–14k (or lower if completion and approval are positively dependent). Benefit is therefore overstated for every review plan that can still reject.

3. **The same lottery is entered twice.** Rank 1 lists “€7–18k expected deadline value” as benefit and “residual breach loss €10–22k” as harm. Those are complements of one incomplete-information draw, not separate effects. A reader who nets the two columns gets a meaningless residual. Spillover is also duplicated: P-901’s €2–10k appears in both Expected harm and Resource spillover, and that euro range depends on an unstated assumption that P-901’s deadline stakes equal P-884’s €20–35k.

4. **Internal probability drift.** P1 is given in-deadline `p0.35–0.5` while C is `0.36–0.52`, even though screening is described as off the critical path. Section 6’s confidence is “Medium (60–70%)” while the table says “Medium-High” for the same plan. These are small, but they show the numbers were assembled, not checked.

**Smaller issues.** “Dominate” is used loosely for E vs F (E preserves optionality but adds large hold harm; that is a trade, not dominance). Constructed ranges are written with the same typographic authority as prompt-given ranges. Rank 4’s speculative “both triggers lapse ⇒ R17 may approve without SOR” is flagged as policy-dependent, which is appropriate, but it still sits in the continuation as if it were a live branch of D rather than a residual uncertainty.

**Bottom line.** The recommendation—conformant second review now, screening in parallel, S4 as relief, A/B excluded, G not enabled—is the recommendation the rubric is looking for, and the constraint reasoning is better than the monetization. The grade is held down because the supporting expected-value story, which the answer leans on for S2 vs S4 and for the table’s benefit/harm columns, is not internally valid.
