7.2

The answer is process-aware, structurally complete, and clearly stronger than a DFG-copying baseline. It still has several non-trivial reasoning defects. Under a strict reading those defects keep it well short of near-flawless.

**What it gets right.** Latent-state calls are mostly sound: C6-A/B/E as S3 after `Evidence Received`, C6-C/D as S4 with Regulatory Assessment still open. It uses severity, injury, lot/object links, country reportability, queue load, the 16:00 batch, and the migration flag instead of raw successor counts. It repeatedly separates a hidden start from a missing completion, and it states the right DFG caveat: historical `Technical Investigation Completed -> Disposition Recorded` does not license disposition when Regulatory Assessment is mandatory. C6-A vs C6-B is explained on obligations, not prefix identity. Intervals are wide, overlapping, and not fake-precise; midpoints roughly rank a top-three without being forced to sum to 1.

**C6-A: ranks 2–3 are poorly justified.** After a *normal* feed and an explicit evidence path (`Initial Triage -> Customer Evidence Requested`, not the historical triage-to-investigation shortcut), the next logged event is overwhelmingly `Evidence Review Completed`. The model itself says Evidence Review is what opens investigations or another evidence cycle. Predicting `Regulatory Assessment Completed` then `Technical Investigation Completed` as the next *visible* events requires an unlogged parallel investigation that skipped the review decision. That is possible, but 0.10–0.20 and 0.08–0.18 are high for a normal-quality completion log with no supporting DFG edge from `Evidence Received`. The more process-native runner-up is another evidence cycle (completeness 0.86; historical `Evidence Review Completed -> Customer Evidence Requested` = 101). Ranking RA completion above TI completion is thinner still: after review the log goes to TI, RA usually follows or precedes TI, and RA has a longer, risk-prioritized queue. The alternative S4 is fair as a *state* hedge; using it to promote investigation *completions* into the next-event forecast over-corrects the “missing ≠ did not happen” rule.

**C6-B: `process_enabled` is inconsistent with the answer’s own standard.** Rank 2 `Close — No Safety Issue` and rank 3 `Fast Close` are marked enabled in S3, while C6-C/D correctly mark `Disposition Recorded` disabled until the blocking assessment exists. If the required distinction is “enabled in the current state” vs “may appear in the log,” Close is not enabled before review completion. Fast Close is eligibility-true (Low, no injury, one device, no lot cluster) but historically only follows Initial Triage; after an evidence cycle it is a coding fiction, not a live branch. Putting it in the top three at 0.04–0.10 is a small probability but a real control-flow error. `alternative: null` is also too clean: evidence was requested instead of fast-closed, and R-T4 is the reportable-case resource. That is enough to keep a low-weight “review escalates” alternative.

**C6-C: the alternative is not an alternative state.** Primary and alternative are both S4; the second sentence only restates that assignment is unobserved. The pattern used for C6-D (S5: completed but delayed) would have been the coherent alternative, especially 2h15m after TI with a 2–14 h RA cycle. Rank 2 `Customer Evidence Requested` after TI is a legal loop given an unconfirmed root cause, but it is not the natural second forecast: the case already left the evidence stage, and the real competitor is a non-compliant or delayed-RA `Disposition Recorded` (historically the more common TI successor). That item is demoted to a 0.07–0.15, `process_enabled: false` third, which is directionally right and then underweighted relative to a weakly enabled evidence reopen.

**C6-D: observation-model thinking is good; the second prediction is arbitrary.** Primary S4 plus a migration S5 alternative is the right structure. R-R2 at 12:40 *before* TI at 13:05 is used correctly as concurrent-start evidence. Rank 2 `Evidence Received` / `process_enabled: false` shows the required log-vs-work distinction, but choosing Received rather than `Customer Evidence Requested` or a delayed `Evidence Review Completed` is under-argued. An attachment in the source system does not preferentially predict that *receipt* will be the next analytical event. Rank 1’s 0.40–0.62 is acceptable only if the quantity is “next event whenever it occurs,” not “completes in the next hour”; 80 minutes into a 2–14 h RA cycle makes immediate completion unlikely. The write-up notes the timing and then still posts a high next-event range without saying which quantity is intended.

**C6-E: mostly strong, slightly generous on Supplier Analysis.** S3 primary with S4-as-concurrent-SA is the right fork. Six lot-linked complaints correctly trigger Rule 5 *if* they are “apparently related,” and 0.72 completeness plus a prior loop makes a third `Customer Evidence Requested` a real third. `Supplier Analysis Completed` at 0.15–0.30 as the *next* log event is optimistic: typical SA is 6–36 h, three items are waiting, and the second package has only been in the 1–5 h review area for ~40 minutes. That interval is defensible only under the unobserved “SA opened after the first review” story, which is not established. TI/RA are correctly treated as not yet mandatory for Low / no injury, with the lot pattern left as an unknown.

**Cross-case text is better than the case-level forecasts.** Ambiguity sources, DFG limits, and the calibration note are accurate. They do not repair the local defects: over-use of hidden concurrency on a normal feed (C6-A), inconsistent `process_enabled` (C6-B vs C6-C/D), a non-alternative alternative (C6-C), and a historically illicit Fast Close (C6-B).

**Net.** This is a strong, literate PPM answer that would beat most submissions. It is not tight enough on enablement, next-event vs eventual-obligation, and same-state “alternatives” to sit in the high band.
