The answer is a strong, well-structured control-objective review. It follows the required headings, refuses mechanical four-eyes mapping, and generally reasons from timing, independence, coverage, recoverability, and case risk. It is not near-flawless. Several classification, factual, and code-logic defects remain.

**What is done well.** Section 1 decomposes FEP-1 into six real threat scenarios and maps AV-2 / MG-4 / RA-7 / ED-1 to prevent / detect / correct / fail on each, including collusion and goods-receipt gaps that AV-2 cannot cover. Section 2’s sufficiency test is multi-dimensional and correctly rejects “same expected loss” as a sole standard. Case work is mostly non-mechanical: AP-204 fails despite formal four-eyes; AP-205 is prevented before completion; AP-206 is left indeterminate on the token-timing ambiguity; AP-208 fails despite a legitimate outcome; AP-202 is a clear package collapse. Section 4’s hybrid semantics for post-release, pre-settlement MG-4 are right. Section 6 correctly treats repeated pairs, overrides, and shared workstations as alerts, not proof. The script embeds the log, calls `format_dataframe` and `filter_four_eyes_principle`, isolates AP-205, joins case facts, and labels output as evidence rather than a verdict.

**Material defects.**

1. **AP-207 is mis-tiered against the written ED-1 rule.** Condition 6 requires independent MG-4 *before bank settlement*, not before release. AP-207 has token-before-prepare, amount/vendor/bank/AV all inside the envelope, m15 independent, ratification at 14:40, settlement at 16:00, substantive review, and audit within one business day. Facts call this a plausible ED-1 use. `OBJECTIVE_MET` is the classification that follows from the rule as written. Downgrading to `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` because MG was post-release invents a stricter standard than ED-1 and is inconsistent with AP-203 (MET for pre-release MG). Conservatism is not a free pass when the permission text is explicit.

2. **Code emits a false manager-timing flag.** When `Bank_Settlement` is absent from the log, `manager_before_settlement` is forced to `True` if any manager event exists. That is not “facts-based knowledge”; settlement times from the case table were never put in `case_facts`. AP-202 (manager two days later, after the recall window) would be flagged as timing-OK. `ed1_mg_timing_ok` inherits the error. For a section whose only job is reliable evidence flags, this is a real logic bug, not a style issue.

3. **AP-208 vendor tenure is handled sloppily.** Eight months is above the 180-day ED-1 floor. Treating “vendor <1 y” in parallel with amount, AV outage, late MG, and late audit as if it were an ED-1 breach is inaccurate. Vendor youth is residual-risk color, not a failed precondition.

**Lesser defects.**

- `keep_violations=True` is presented as documented 2.7.x behavior. Public `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle(log, activity1, activity2)` typically returns *compliant* cases and has no such argument. The try/except plus pandas fallback saves executability, but the API claim is likely invented.
- AP-201’s corrective-capacity cell talks out of both sides: settlement is “irrevocable for internal-control purposes” and also “partial” because of a borderline 24-hour recall. Pick a semantics and apply it.
- `manager_independent=True` for m21 is not in the facts (only m04, m02, m11, m15 are attested). The narrative does not rely on it, but the joined facts table overclaims.
- AP-201 still has a mild generosity problem: same-user convenience, no token, so ED-1 is unavailable. `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is defensible given low amount, AV pass, independent pre-settlement MG, and the instruction not to auto-fail same-user cases — but the missing review-quality evidence sits uneasily next to Medium-High confidence.
- AP-209 as `OBJECTIVE_PARTIALLY_MET` is a defensible caution; `INDETERMINATE` would have matched the missing org-design documents more cleanly. Neighboring-category, not a howler.

**Net.** Control reasoning, case differentiation, and monitoring design are well above a competent baseline. The grade is held down by one rule-inconsistent classification, one evidence-code bug that would mis-flag a high-risk case, and several smaller factual/API inaccuracies. That is clearly short of near-flawless.
