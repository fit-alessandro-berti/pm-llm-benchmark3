The write-up is complete, constraint-aware, and mostly well reasoned, but it is not nearly flawless. Under a strict reading, several concrete defects keep it well below the top of the scale.

**What is strong.** The seven required headings are present and used as specified. Hard safety/compliance rules are separated from preferences, and the policy does not collapse into throughput-only optimization. The dispatcher has the required pieces (eligibility, urgency, skill, continuity, fairness/overtime, training, resilience, deterministic tie-breaks, exception codes). The C001–C016 table is coherent: high-priority work stays at skill ≥ 2; Security stays at level 3; Approval is finalized only at 2–3; overtime-flagged staff get no new work; the cap of 4 is respected. The C015 note is handled correctly: Eva and Jonas have Security = 0, so they cannot execute even under supervision. The 20% training miss is reported instead of being quietly patched. The worker text is non-punitive and explainable. Audit indicators cover all requested families.

**Material issues.**

1. **Internal inconsistency.** C016 is justified with “H5 continuity,” but in Section 1 H5 is the batch-size cap; continuity is P1 / scenario rule 5. That is a real labeling error in the assignment table, not a slip in the scratchpad.

2. **The 20% training rule is not operationalized.** It is a scored bonus of weight 0.05 plus a post-hoc ledger. Nothing in the algorithm prefers remaining normal/low work toward the quota when the running share is short. The shortfall is therefore partly by design of the procedure, not only by roster collision.

3. **Capacity is asserted, not checked.** Farid takes two urgents (2h and 4h) with 6.5h left; Eva takes C005, C016, and C010 with 5h left; Celine must finish a 6h-SLA rework the same day she also receives C003. No effort estimates are applied even though the policy’s own feasibility gate depends on them.

4. **Baselines are not instantiated on this queue.** Section 4 is a sensible qualitative matrix, but first-available vs specialist-only are never run on C001–C016. The given evidence already implies sharp differences (e.g. first-available can send urgent Security to Celine at skill 1). Those contrasts are left generic.

5. **Section 7 is a sketch with actual bugs, not just omissions.** Sorting `case_priority` as strings (`ascending=[False,True]`) processes `high` after `normal` and `low`. `hard_gates.reason_codes` does not exist. `mismatch_rate` is a broken placeholder (`{"ConceptName->map": ...}.get("k","")`). The handover-band tie-break is a case attribute, so it does not distinguish candidates. `get_service_time` / `insert_case_service_waiting_time` are invoked on a log that only has `time:timestamp`, with no start/complete pairing and no discussion of that limitation. `filter_event_attribute_values(..., level="event")` mixes APIs.

6. **Smaller policy gaps.** Development and resilience at 0.05 cannot carry the constraints they are asked to represent. Hugo still concentrates two of three Security cases. C009 keeps a Diagnosis-1 owner despite the stated 3.3h weak-skill penalty, with only a vague “Celine/David” review.

The assignment recommendations and the socio-technical framing are good enough that this is clearly above a mediocre answer. The labeling error, unenforced training quota, missing instance-level baseline comparison, unchecked hours, and defective code are enough, under the required strictness, to cap the score at **7.0**.
