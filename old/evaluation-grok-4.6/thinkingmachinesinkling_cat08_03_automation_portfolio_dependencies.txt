**6.4**

The final write-up hits the required seven-section shape, does not rank by frequency, keeps A11 off the first wave, and is unusually well grounded in the given frequencies, waits, DFG loops, FTE pools, and control rates. That is the floor, not a reason to score it as near-flawless. Under a strict reading there are several material process errors, not just nits.

The worst process-mining mistake is in the rollout rationale: it linearizes concurrent work as `A5 → A6 → A3/A4`. The brief is explicit that KYC and financial review often run in parallel and that the system record is misleading. Treating entity/KYC clearance as downstream of document completeness is the opposite of process-level thinking. The same section then deploys A4 *before* A5, so the stated dependency story and the actual sequence contradict each other.

Calling A3+A4+A5+A6 the “bottleneck/synchronization” portfolio while leaving out the longest wait in the table (external valuation, 58h, 19% late, a named synchronization point on 71% of equipment cases) is a second structural miss. The write-up admits the sacrifice but never really justifies why a synchronization portfolio can ignore the dominant external wait, especially when A7 is listed as medium–high feasibility and the brief says the wait can be triaged or parallelized. Committee packaging is said to improve because “collateral context” arrives more reliably, which this portfolio does not make true.

A3/A4 are over-credited as *ensuring* KYC-before-signing. They can shorten and clean clearance; they do not install the legal gate. The 6.5%/1.1% provisional-signing finding is a control-path problem, not automatically solved by a faster specialist assistant. That overclaim shows up in both the effects section and the final recommendation.

Other issues that should not survive a hard review:
- A1 is described as freeing the entire 12-FTE back-office pool and as “an 8-minute, 3-minute task,” mixing Receive and Register and overstating labor impact. Back office also owns classify and missing-doc work.
- A2 is ranked as high process-level value (misroutes, wrong control path, collateral trigger) and then dropped from the recommended set with almost no consequence analysis. That undercuts the matrix.
- A4’s “low–medium direct benefit” follows the candidate blurb but ignores that 1,750 × 55 min is on the same labor-hour scale as registration. The matrix therefore treats frequency as if it were effort.
- Variant effects (Micro / SME / High-exposure; working-capital vs equipment vs renewal) are mostly named, not used. The 380 mismatch→contract DFG arc, weekly committee cadence, and legal-ops idle-then-month-end pattern are barely integrated into portfolio choice.
- Gate numbers (25%, 30%, 40%, 90%, 80%) are invented precision. That is the same vice the prompt forbids on ROI.
- First-year suitability ratings fight the ranking (A1 “High”, A3 “Medium” while A3 is a Year-1 deep integration). Readable if you squint; unclear as a decision tool.
- Phase 4 audits A9/A12-style work that is not being built. That is control theater, not a gate.

What remains is a competent, complete memo with real portfolio contrast and a defensible “do not automate registration first” argument. It is not tight enough on concurrency, the largest wait, or the signing control to sit in the high band.
