**6.2**

The write-up is well structured, follows the required headings, and gets most of the easy SoD and identity-normalization calls right. It is not close to flawless. Under a strict reading of event-time authority and delegation **scope**, several material findings are wrong or incomplete, and the PM4Py block is not a reliable executable check.

**What is actually solid.** Alias normalization (`b.koch` → Ben) before RR1 is correct and correctly produces the CHG-508 violation. Service accounts are not treated as humans. Eva is judged from the 9 August verifier role, not from the later QE master. Asha on leave is not stripped of standing QM authority. D-IC-04 is correctly rejected as prohibited, email-only subdelegation. CHG-502, CHG-507, CHG-508, and CHG-510 are rightly unauthorized. CHG-509 is rightly left indeterminate on the “principal must be absent” weekend gap. RR6 is respected: repeated Clara–Ben pairing is treated as an indicator, with benign and adverse explanations. The later-QMS-record-versus-signed-PDF doctrine is stated clearly.

**Delegation scope is the largest analytical failure.** D-QM-01 authorizes *QM approval for production-equipment change controls*, not every QM act and not every scope. The answer still treats Ben’s `Retrospective_QM_Review` on **CHG-505** and **CHG-510** (both `cooling_system`) as covered (“QM del valid but performer identity weak”). That is not what the instrument says. RR4 requires a QM or a *valid* QM delegate; validity requires that “its scope covers the change.” Those retrospectives are out of scope even before sick-leave identity is considered. The matrix records the narrow scope, then the case table ignores it. That is exactly the kind of event-time scope error the rubric is built to catch.

**CHG-505 is misclassified.** Farid’s approval can be a valid IC substitution *if* the case is CI-44. The late Friday retrospective and the sick-leave account use are separate failures. Calling the whole case primary `UNAUTHORIZED` with only a parenthetical IC substitution collapses a valid emergency approval path into a follow-up timing/identity defect. The control-objective split the prompt asked for is not maintained.

**CI-44 is assumed, not shown.** D-IC-03 is “Cooling Incident CI-44 only.” The log only says `cooling_system`. Two emergency cooling cases exist on the same day. The answer asserts interval/scope validity for CHG-505 without binding evidence that CHG-505 *is* CI-44. CHG-510 is rejected only for subdelegation, which implies the same unverified CI-44 mapping. That should have been a stated assumption or an `INDETERMINATE` scope caveat.

**Principal-absence is applied too mechanically.** D-QM-01 requires Asha to be absent. Leave covers 10–14 August, but Asha had an authenticated VPN session on 13 August 09:15–09:40 and claims she personally handled an urgent case. The answer never asks whether that same-day availability breaks the absence condition for Ben’s **CHG-506** approval at 13:00. Leave records and live presence are not the same fact.

**RR4 is only half-operationalized.** Business-day counting (Tue 11 Aug implement → Thu 13 Aug EOD; Fri 14 Aug late) is reasonable and internally consistent. The answer does not test the other RR4 predicate: that the emergency flag *and a credible safety rationale* exist. No rationale is in the log. That missing evidence is never listed.

**Identity/data calls are slightly soft in a GMP setting.** Treating the 9 August signed PDF as enough to authorize CHG-501, when the controlled QMS row appears only on 12 August “during migration,” is defensible as a legal-instrument argument and weak as a pharma data-integrity argument. CHG-503’s HIGH-risk verification by an unattributed shared-terminal service account is only medium severity; the control objective (independent verification) is not shown to have been met.

**Section 5 does not meet the executable-check bar.** The CSV reconstruction, `format_dataframe`, alias replace, and pandas interval flags are the good parts. The rest is fragile:

- `from pm4py.algo.filtering.pandas.four_eyes_principle import filter_four_eyes_principle` is not the documented public path; the prompt asked for `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle`.
- The polarity claim (filter returns *violators*) is asserted, not verified. If the installed API keeps *conforming* cases, every printed SoD result inverts.
- Required handover / working-together calls are wrapped in `try/except` and reduced to a placeholder. That is not network interpretation.
- `filter_activity_done_different_resources` is named and then replaced by a blunt `nunique` that does not do the same thing.
- Authority logic never evaluates `Retrospective_QM_Review` (the exact activity where scope and sick-leave identity matter).
- An unused `temporal_profile` import is noise.

So the code documents intent; it does not demonstrate working PM4Py checks.

**Smaller defects.** Section 4’s alternatives are fine but generic; they are not grounded in any computed handover or working-together metric. CHG-501’s dual label `AUTHORIZED (VALID_SUBSTITUTION)` blurs standing authority and delegation. Emergency IC scope, weekend absence, and service-account attribution are handled more carefully in prose than in the case table, so the table overstates confidence (especially CHG-505 “QM del valid”).

The executive conclusion is directionally right — mix of genuine authority failure, identity/data weakness, and pattern risk — and the three investigations are sensible. They are incomplete without (a) re-opening Ben’s cooling-system retrospectives for **scope**, (b) binding CI-44 to named cases, and (c) testing Asha’s 13 August presence against D-QM-01.

Net: a competent audit memo with the right skeleton and several correct hard cases, undermined by a real delegation-scope error, a smeared CHG-505 classification, and PM4Py that would not survive a strict execution review. That is a mid-6, not an 8+.
