3.4

The submission shows a real grasp of the core problem—Site A’s before/after shift is not an identified treatment effect, originals are a hard start constraint, and a blind copy of “create both checks after Register for every case” is the wrong intervention. That is not enough. Against the required contract and a strict reading of process structure, the answer is incomplete, locally inaccurate, and in several places conceptually wrong.

**Missing required sections.** Sections 8, 9, and 10 are absent. There is no PM4Py construction, no `go` / `conditional go` / `no-go` recommendation, and no eight-item assumption register. Those were not optional appendices; they were first-class deliverables. Section 7 then collapses: stop rules and transportability analysis are missing, several rows are truncated into near-gibberish (“Validate null dilution introduction response”, “MQA: multi-context trail correlation”), and the mandatory ≤220-word note on separating mechanism failure from implementation failure is replaced by the sentence “Pilot isolates distinct variables.” An answer that omits three full headings and guts a fourth cannot be close to competent on this rubric.

**Section 1 is the right table with the wrong facts.** Huddles are listed as a Site A *before* feature; they were a co-intervention. Case mix is described as already “simplified after marketing intervention” in the before column; the mix shift is contemporaneous with treatment (high-complexity 48% → 40%). Tail “after” is just “Persisted.” Site B concurrency is waved at as “limited,” which buries the actual structure: identity and originals receipt already may start after register; document check is blocked only when originals are required. The table therefore mis-times the co-interventions it later needs for transport and never states the partial order that actually differs.

**Section 2 fails the node list and the word gate.** `assessment_capacity` never appears. `identity_capacity` and `document_capacity` are sinks with no path to waiting or cycle time, so the graph cannot express a bottleneck shift. `originals_required` points at waiting rather than at `true_overlap`, which is the relevant mechanism. Several required site-difference nodes lack `S_` parents. The prose is well below the 180–280-word band and treats API latency as a shared mechanism when it is a Site A co-intervention.

**Section 3 violates an explicit instruction.** The first row is “9-hour median cycle reduction / Overlap reduces waiting.” That is exactly the identified-treatment reading the prompt forbids. Completed volume, complexity mix, wait-before-assessment, both service times, appeals, the API upgrade, +2 document FTE, marketing mix shift, huddles, and timestamp inflation are not given their own rows, even though those are the confounders that make the 9 hours non-transportable. Five thin rows cannot carry the evidence-transfer task.

**Section 4 is structurally complete and locally false.** “Legal-review requirement → clear legal-consistency gains” is invented. Case-lock “beneficial signature: clear fields where possible under lock” does not mean anything. Timestamp back-entry is a measurement failure mode, not a source of “accurate fine-structuring.” Role specialization does not “avoid errors” as a benefit of parallelization. The required ten modifiers are named; several interaction cells do not describe an interaction.

**Section 5 is numerically careless and missing the required derivation.** Optimistic transfer uses “60% (digital-only cases)” while Site B requires originals on 62% of cases, so the digital share is about 38%. The digital-only row then uses 38%, so the table contradicts itself. There is no mapping from Site A’s confounded −9h / −18h p90, mix shift, extra FTE, and API change onto the stated ranges. The legal/rework row has median −2 to +2, which is not a clean “mean improves, tail worsens” case; the required mixed-sign scenario is therefore not actually delivered. An IT-lock failure scenario is omitted despite being one of Site B’s binding constraints.

**Section 6 makes a process-structure error.** Legal review sits *after* assessment. It does not forbid creating identity and document tasks after register. Excluding all legal/high-value cases from the intervention (as opposed to from a first pilot stratum) throws away 40% of volume for a dependency that does not exist. The POWL then drops `Originals Receipt` and `Notify`, writes `Register → (Identity || Document)` as if that were the Site B model rather than an eligible-subset model, and never encodes `Originals Receipt → Document Check` or the supplement → document → assessment loop as a loop. WIP is not quantified against the 4/7/3/2 pools or Site A’s six-task cap. The IT lock is not an operational control, only a logging hope.

**What remains.** The answer is right to refuse a universal copy, right that originals and the lock dominate transport, and right that digital-only is the first eligible set. Those are the ideas a passable memo would start from. They are not developed into a sound transport graph, a non-identified evidence table, a legally accurate partial order, a measurable pilot, a model, or a decision. Under a hypercritical standard that already penalizes minor slips, the omissions and the legal-review mistake dominate.
