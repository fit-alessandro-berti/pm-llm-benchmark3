### Material case-analysis errors

**Delegation scope is under-analyzed, which is one of the judge’s explicit criteria.** D-QM-01 authorizes *QM approval for production-equipment change controls*. CHG-505 and CHG-510 are `cooling_system`. The retrospective reviews by `u_bk` are therefore out of scope even before timing or identity is considered. The answer never applies that constraint. For CHG-505 it hangs the whole finding on a late clock; for CHG-510 it stops at the bad approval. That is a core miss, not a quibble.

**CHG-505 is incompletely reasoned at event time.** Ben’s certified sick leave runs 2026-08-13 12:00 through 2026-08-14 23:59. The retrospective review is 2026-08-14 16:00, i.e. inside that interval. The answer uses sick-leave identity conflict for CHG-506 and then ignores the same conflict on the 505 review. Combined with the scope mismatch, “UNAUTHORIZED (late review)” is an oversimplified and partly wrong story. “3 bd” is also sloppy: from a Tuesday implementation the Friday review is one business day *after* a two-business-day deadline, not a well-defined “3 business days” count.

**CHG-503 SoD is misstated.** RR5 says a service account is not a human identity unless session evidence maps the act to an authorized person. The WS-DM-04 evidence is weak (shared terminal, Diego badge-in, no re-auth). You cannot say “RR2 formally ok, RR5 doubtful.” RR2 is *not* formally satisfied; it is unevaluable, and the only hinted human is the implementer. `DATA_OR_IDENTITY_PROBLEM` can stand; “formally ok” cannot.

**Classification is internally inconsistent.** Ben’s in-scope delegated QM approval on CHG-501 is `AUTHORIZED`; Eva’s delegated QM approval on CHG-509 is `VALID_SUBSTITUTION`. Same legal structure, different labels. CHG-504 states Asha was “present (remote)” as if that were established; the file only says she *claims* she handled one urgent case and security cannot exclude credential sharing. That is `INDETERMINATE` on identity with a possible authorized overlay, not a fact of presence.

**The matrix is not an event-time matrix.** It is a person roster. Eva’s role flips at 2026-08-12 00:00; Diego is Maintenance Lead and later IC; Ben’s delegated authority and availability change across the week. A single row per human hides the thing the prompt required. Farid’s row (“Diego at incident site (absence justified)”) is confused: Diego implements CHG-505 ten minutes after Farid’s approval, so the principal is available, and D-IC-03 does not clearly impose an absence predicate the way D-QM-01/02 do.

**Section 7 cannot count.** “Five of ten cases (CHG-501, 504, 509 plus part of 503)” is four items sold as five. The “four clear authority failures” omit CHG-508, which the same answer classified as `UNAUTHORIZED`. The systemic split is therefore not trustworthy.

**Technical conformance vs control objective is mostly asserted, not used.** Eva verifies after she is no longer Independent Verifier; Clara–Ben concentration can be SoD-compliant case-by-case and still hollow out independence; CHG-509 maps `svc_qms` to the same human who approved. ### PM4Py / pandas section is a fail against its own spec

The prompt required **one executable** block that reconstructs the full CSV, formats it, normalizes aliases, runs four-eyes, interval-checks authority, and emits handover / working-together evidence without auto-calling collusion.

1. The CSV is replaced by an ellipsis. That is not executable and violates “do not read external files.”
2. `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` **keeps cases that satisfy** four-eyes (different resources). The code prints those case IDs as “RR1/RR2 violations.” The operational result is inverted: 502/508/510 would be dropped, and the compliant cases would be flagged. The high-level API also takes two activity *strings*, not lists plus `attr_key`.
3. `discover_handover_of_work_network` returns an SNA object (typically `.connections`), not `hand_net['matrix']`. `discover_working_together_network` is assigned and never used.
4. `filter_activity_done_different_resources` keeps cases where an activity is done by *different* resources; the printout claims the opposite, and no case has two `Approve_Change` events anyway.
5. The pandas “delegation” check tests only timestamp ∈ interval. It ignores scope, principal-absence, QM vs IC path, emergency vs ordinary HIGH, prohibited subdelegation, and `Retrospective_QM_Review`. Farid would be treated as a valid HIGH-risk approver of anything. Ben would be treated as valid for cooling-system QM acts. That is not an evaluation of RR3/RR4.
6. Service-account attribution is a comment, not a represented field (candidate human, evidence grade, SoD-under-hypothesis). Weekend/business-day logic is discussed in prose and absent from code.

A judge scoring “quality of the PM4Py/pandas checks” should treat this as largely non-conforming, not as a minor polish issue.
