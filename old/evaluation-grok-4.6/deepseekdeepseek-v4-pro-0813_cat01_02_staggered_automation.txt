**7.0**

The final write-up is a competent causal-policy memo that hits the required structure and refuses the indefensible “automation reduces cycle time by X%” claim. It is not close to flawless. Quantitative labeling errors, a gap between the stated design and the estimated models, and several thin justifications keep it well below a high score.

**What holds up.** Sections 1–2 correctly separate availability from `automated_fast_path_pct`, endogenous targeting of weak high-volume branches, and the March fraud desk, April/July routing, May complexity shock, and FTE moves. The estimand table covers ITT availability, intensity, median, tail, rework, escalation, CSAT, plus dynamic and heterogeneous contrasts, and it flags SUTVA/fraud-desk interference and treatment-version heterogeneity. Mechanisms in section 4 are the strongest part: fast-path substitution versus capacity creation, specialist congestion, learning, mix/routing, staffing, rework displacement, tail amplification, and survey selection are all given testable signatures. Section 5’s pre/post windows are transparent, most arithmetic checks out, median and p90 are not collapsed, and control comparisons show adopters deteriorating less than Delta/Epsilon rather than posting clean absolute gains. Confidence scores in section 7 are appropriately low. The claim ladder is well ordered. `expand with an embedded evaluation`, Delta first / Epsilon held back, queue-state collection, spillover monitoring, and a pre-registered scale-up rule is the right operational-and-learning choice. The Python block meets the spec: embedded CSV, panel checks, separate (not subplot) trend displays, adopter windows, relative-time dummies, clustered OLS, and explicit non-causal comments.

**Material flaws.** p90 effects are labeled “−3 to −10 pp vs controls” in §§5.5 and 7 after the contrasts were computed in **hours**. That unit slip is then mixed with “−15% to +5% absolute,” so the headline ranges are not internally consistent. The primary design is sold as a staggered event study / group-time contrast with calendar controls; the fitted models are static branch-FE regressions on `automation` or intensity **without month FE and without using the relative-time indicators just constructed**. That is close to the naïve pooled coefficient the prompt told the author not to lean on. Gamma as a not-yet-treated control in Apr–Jun is advertised in the design and then unused. Section 3’s justification after the table is under the required 150 words. Section 1 omits Beta’s FTE gain, Gamma’s loss, and Delta’s gain. Choosing Delta next is not justified against Epsilon, which is the closer analogue on complexity and baseline performance. Intensity “low vs high” is mostly early vs late calendar time; the text admits confounding but still presents the split as intensity evidence. Conservative ranges are asserted more than bounded.

**Smaller issues.** Several estimand cells are just “Same.” Escalation confidence of 60 is high for five clusters once the March common escalation jump is acknowledged. CSAT claims are properly weak, but the safe-claim language still edges toward “no harm” without response counts. The code’s `automation` coefficient will absorb the May product shock; the warning comment does not fix the specification.

Net: disciplined on the policy question and process interpretation, sloppy in places where quantitative discipline and identification implementation were the grading criteria.
