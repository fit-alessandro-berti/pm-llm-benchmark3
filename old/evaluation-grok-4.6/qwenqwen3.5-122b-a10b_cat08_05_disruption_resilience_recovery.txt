The answer follows the required seven-section structure and is substantively stronger than a generic process-mining writeup: it uses the phase metrics, DFG volumes, fitness scores, and A–L patterns; it does not treat every deviation as failure; and it correctly refuses to equate T4 operational recovery or after-the-fact audit remediation with real-time compliance.

It is not close to flawless.

**Factual and temporal errors.** Pattern K is placed in T3 (“T3 cycle time inflation”), but `Reconcile manual reservation` appears only in the stabilization DFG. The table invents a T2 backorder comparison (“Backorder wait 900 … vs higher in T2”); T2 has no `Backorder wait` node. Aggregate T2 rates are pinned on single adaptations (12.5% rework on E; 7.8% cancellation on I) without support. T4’s 2.2% traceability rate is repeatedly treated as residual post-remediation liability; that is the in-period exception rate, and 2,300 cases already sit on `Invoice close → Audit remediation`.

**Conceptual slips on resilience vs. noncompliance.** A is listed as “true resilience” and as an authorization bypass; those cannot both be unqualified. D (stale snapshot) is operational risk, not concealed noncompliance. F’s mixed profile—CS dual-control held, cold-chain documentation missed—is under-analyzed. Section 7 misstates the actual trade-off: hospital median only moved 8h→10h, while pharmacy/wholesale absorbed the delay and fill-rate collapse.

**Recovery redesign gaps.** The model has the requested headings but omits the named T2 move-on-model hole—missing Quality/release check—and barely treats T3’s unreliable sync/scan hybrid, recall-sensitive product, or spreadsheet invoice hold. Arbitrary thresholds (15/30 minutes, 4-hour snapshot, 5% audit cap, 3:1 fairness, 20% hidden-workload hours) are ungrounded. Calling a numbered list a “process tree” is empty notation.

**Indicators and recommendations.** Backlog age is required and never defined. Control completeness is collapsed into one checklist rate instead of separate temp-doc and dual-control measures. Recommendations are plausible but miss dual-control fallback, T3 reconciliation design, and pharmacy fairness/comms—the failure modes the log actually shows.

Useful advisory draft; too many invented facts, mis-timed mechanisms, and missing mandated controls for a high score under a strict rubric.
