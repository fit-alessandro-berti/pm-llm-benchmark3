**3.2**

The write-up has the required skeleton and a few sound cautions, but the core process-mining claims are internally contradictory, numerically wrong, or forbidden by the prompt. That is well below a competent segmented analysis.

**What is acceptable.** Section 1 names the right objects (arrivals vs completions, service quantiles ≠ flow time, opening-hours counts, snapshot WIP, unknown 07:00 backlog). Isolating 2026-04-15 as a broken day, refusing Little’s Law on the pooled series, and asking for 07:00 backlog / finer timestamps are the right instincts. The heading list matches the spec.

**Primary segmentation is not a near-miss; it is incoherent.**  
- 2026-04-06–04-14 is labeled “Baseline Weekdays” but includes Sat 04-11 and Sun 04-12.  
- 2026-04-16–04-28 is labeled “PostIntervention / Normalized Weekdays” even though the prompt places the intervention on 2026-04-29. Extra FTE on 04-16/04-17 is outage catch-up (completions 129 and 112 vs arrivals 112 and 99, WIP 52 then 35), which the quality bar says must not be treated as a normal high-throughput regime. The same window also contains two weekends.  
- The same row claims “After 20260429, `staff_fte` rises to 7FTE” while the dates stop on 04-28.  
- 2026-04-29–05-01 (Wed–Fri) is labeled “Weekend / LowComplex” and is said to include 04-25, 04-26, 05-02, 05-03. Those dates are not in the stated range; complex share is 43–45%, not low.  
- The largest clean break after the outage—complex% and `peak_08_10_pct` jumping on 2026-04-20, with WIP 37–50 on 6 FTE—is never a regime. Recovery is explicitly declined as a separate state.

An alternative that only cuts on 04-29 is admissible as a contrast, but it does not repair the primary story and is not quantified.

**Pooled numbers do not match the table.** All-days means are about 90 arrivals/day, 86 completions/day, 33% complex, WIP ≈ 30—not 99, 101, and 40%. Completions are systematically below arrivals over the month; a pooled throughput of 101 is the opposite of the net backlog build. Several “segment” means are also unreconcilable with the date ranges in §2. That undercuts every composition claim in §4.

**Little’s Law is applied incorrectly, then marked applicable.**  
Opening hours are **12** hours (07:00–19:00), not the stated 14, and WIP is an open-hours snapshot mean. Converting \(L/\lambda\) with \(24\times 60\), or with a bare \(\times 60\), is the wrong clock. \(L\approx 20\), \(\lambda\approx 102\) per open day implies on the order of **2+ hours** in system, not 20–23 min. The 20–23 min figure is service-time scale and is openly “corrected” toward median/p90 service—the one substitution the task forbids. Baseline and weekend verdicts of `applicable` are too strong given unknown overnight stock, completions ≠ same-day arrivals, and non-stationary mix. The Python then takes `mean_wip.iloc[0]` (one day, not the window mean), which is a third, independent error.

**Burstiness/stationarity is partly invented.** Hourly arrivals on 04-07, \([3,5,12,20,\ldots,4]\), are strongly overdispersed (Fano on the order of 3, not 0.7). Pooling all four representative days into one Fano mixes the regimes under discussion. Lag-1 “typical” correlations (~0.35) are not computed from these series; weekly weekday/weekend oscillation is the dominant serial structure and is ignored. The median-service jump heuristic is more likely to hit mid-April (outage + mix) than 04-29, when median service actually eases.

**§8 does not meet the contract.** The markdown parser is not robust (header regex, types left as strings). The script does not compute candidate-regime summaries or compact comparison tables. Only one dispersion measure is actually coded (Fano; “index of dispersion” is the same ratio). The change-point print is a row index, not a date. PM4Py notes omit the requested arrival-rate helpers in any operational way. Comments advertise flow times of ~20 min that the coded \(24\times 60\) formula would not produce. The block is not a reproducible argument; it is scaffolding plus conjectured output.

**Smaller but material defects.** Carry-over is misstated (“arrivals after 18:45 are not captured” is not in the prompt). Capacity pressure is never turned into a clear offered-load comparison (e.g., arrivals × service vs FTE × 720 open minutes). Simpson-style reversals are named more than shown (higher FTE / worse WIP after the mix shift is the obvious one). Symbols and date punctuation are sloppy enough that §2 cannot be executed as written.

A structured, cautious draft that got weekday/weekend, outage, recovery, mix shift, and intervention on the right dates, used a 12-hour open clock, and refused \(L/\lambda\) except as a bounded approximation would be in a different band. This answer repeatedly contradicts its own table and the data.
