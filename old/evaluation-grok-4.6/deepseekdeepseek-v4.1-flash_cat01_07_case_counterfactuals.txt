**6.4**

The final write-up is conceptually strong on the points the task actually cares about: K-337 is treated as a decision-policy change, not a time subtraction; K-411 correctly separates earlier verification readiness from earlier completion; four-eyes and mandatory compliance placement are preserved; queues are treated as forecasts; and local gains are shown to migrate congestion. Format is mostly exact (headings, tables, labeled derivations, eight typed assumptions, ranking, pilots). That is why this is not a low score.

It is not close to flawless.

**Temporal / feasibility errors.** The K-219 table path starts Assessment at Tue 11:30 even though the Tue 08:00 snapshot gives A2 a 6 h queue. If Document Check finishes ~Tue 05:30, A2 should start near 14:00, not 11:30. The derivation text admits absorption by A2; the explicit path does not. K-104’s primary path is worse: C1 has a 16 h queue at Tue 08:00, yet the table both inserts a Tue 08:00–10:00 prelim review and a Tue 19:30–22:30 final review, then has M1 decide at 22:45. That is not minimal-change under “other system behavior held approximately fixed,” and a 22:45 manager sign-off is operationally implausible. The best/base/worst text is more careful than the table it is supposed to support.

**Thin, off-spec system analysis.** The 100-case rollout is required at 250–400 words and must actually analyze reservation of C1, D1/D2 substitution, rework, assessment bottlenecks, weekends/external response, and timestamps vs true concurrency. The delivered paragraph is ~180 words. Required topics are named, not analyzed: there is no capacity arithmetic (one C1 reviewer cannot absorb 100 reservations; D2 cannot absorb 100 handwriting cases), little on weekend/external delay distributions, and almost no discussion of bottleneck migration magnitudes. That is both a format miss and a content miss on interference.

**Sloppy outcome wording.** Several “expected outcome range” cells say “cycle time 12 to 24 h” / “10 to 20 h” / “48 to 110 h versus factual” without stating reduction vs remaining time. Intent is recoverable from the derivations, but the required table is the artifact being graded.

**Lesser issues.** K-104’s listed path runs prelim C1 after documents, not concurrently with identity/document checks as the intervention allows. Cycle-time ranges are wide and sometimes internally inconsistent with the optimistic table times. Ranking K-411 first is defensible on robustness, but the write-up overclaims “small to moderate” benefit for a change whose own analysis says the gain may be fully absorbed.

The skeleton of a good counterfactual memo is here. Under strict grading, the queue-inconsistent paths, the implausible C1/M1 timing, the short 100-case section, and the ambiguous metrics keep it well below high scores.
