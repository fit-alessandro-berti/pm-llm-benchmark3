**7.4**

The write-up hits the required structure and gets most of the hard compliance reasoning right: event-time roles rather than the current master, alias normalization before SoD, service-account attribution as a precondition rather than a person, D-IC-04 as prohibited subdelegation, D-QM-01 limited to production equipment, and a later migration timestamp as non-proof of an earlier delegation. Those are the load-bearing judgments, and they are largely sound.

It is not close to flawless.

The CHG-509 primary label is too soft. D-QM-02 requires the principal to be absent; Asha’s recorded leave ends 14 August; 15 August is only a weekend, and the site calendar has no holiday. Weekend is not formal absence. Eva is a Quality Engineer that day, not QM. Once the absence condition fails, HIGH-risk approval is an RR3 failure. `INDETERMINATE` with a parenthetical secondary unauthorized finding understates a condition that the record simply does not meet.

The code and the narrative disagree on CI-44. The case text treats Farid’s CHG-505 approval as valid only if the case is CI-44, then the script hard-codes `incident_link = {"CHG-505": "CI-44"}` and will mark that approval valid. That is an evidential leap the log does not support. The RR4 syllogism is also left half-finished: a failed or untimely/out-of-scope retrospective means the emergency exception does not hold, so RR3 applies and an IC delegate is not a lawful HIGH-risk approver. The table leans on the retrospective defect without always closing that loop.

Event-time role handling slips in the script. Diego is Maintenance Lead *and* IC from 1 July; the code ends Maintenance Lead on 30 June. Harmless for these approvals, but it is a direct error in the role-interval logic the task asked to be careful about. Asha’s 13 August VPN session is treated as a CHG-504 identity caveat and is not carried through to whether she was still “absent” that same day for D-QM-01 (CHG-506 at 13:00). That is exactly the kind of same-timestamp availability conflict the prompt flagged.

The PM4Py block satisfies the checklist on paper and then undermines itself. `format_dataframe`, four-eyes, handover, working-together, and interval checks are present, aliases are normalized first, and collusion is not auto-declared. The implementation is still a stack of try/except fallbacks around APIs that are unlikely to match (`convert(..., variant="eventlog")`, `log.get_case_ids()`, discover-network calls stuffed with `activity_key` / `case_id` / `timestamp_key`). A “working” run can therefore print empty networks and empty four-eyes sets while the pandas tables do the real work. Network *interpretation* is also thin: section 4 restates the qualitative pair evidence; it does not read the handover or working-together output the code is supposed to produce.

Smaller nits: CHG-504 as `AUTHORIZED` is optimistic given an explicit note that credential sharing cannot be excluded (`INDETERMINATE` would fit the identity rule better); CHG-510’s retrospective is timely but that fact is almost invisible in the row; Grace’s verification is correctly not invented into an extra formal rule, but the independence concern is only a clause. None of these sink the analysis. Together they keep it well short of a high score under a strict reading.
