**7.6**

The final memo is strategically sound and format-complete: it recommends hybrid **E**, refuses national scale and blanket rollback, separates facts from interpretations, explains the required metric conflicts, scores A–E, and ranks M1–M8 with decision impact. That is the right call on incomplete, conflicting process-mining evidence.

It is not near-flawless.

**What holds up.** Segment-gated scale matches the data: simple low-risk houses improve on median, p90, first-time-right, and complaints; apartments and old buildings drive the tail, rework, and escalations. Precision 0.51, alignments (billing before activation, skipped permits, repeat visits without survey), DFG shifts, utilization (coordinators 93%, second-visit 92%), and the centralized working-together network are used, not just listed. Visible-cost vs incomplete downstream-cost is handled correctly. Thresholds for later scale exist.

**What fails a strict read.**

- **Causal overclaim.** The executive memo treats aggregate p90/p95 worsening and the EUR 207 figure as established effects of R. Pilot regions were heavier in apartments and older buildings; cost and complaint data are truncated after week 4; attributes are missing before week 3. Composition and missingness can move aggregates without R being uniformly worse. Segment tables still support E, but the memo does not keep that distinction as cleanly as the evidence table does.
- **Core metrics dropped.** SLA (13% → 16%, better for simple houses) and NPS (+21 → +17) are in the prompt and barely used. The SLA split is independent support for E and should have been in the evidence table and the rollout gate.
- **VOI is ranked, not valued.** M1 then M4 is defensible, but M7 (cheap conformance on billing-before-activation / missing permits) and M5 (coordinator absence) are under-ranked relative to the memo’s own risk story. There is no cost-of-information or “what would flip E → C / keep hybrid / reopen skip” logic. M6 as a faster observational screen than M1 is noted only weakly.
- **Soft gates.** “Agreed tolerance,” “non-inferior,” and “agreed threshold” are not operational. A decision memo should state numbers or a decision rule (e.g., non-simple p90 ≤ baseline, 30-day complaints ≤ 6.1%, 60-day fully loaded cost ≤ EUR 198, zero unverified billing-before-activation).
- **Alternative D is undersold.** Incomplete segment/risk attributes and truncated cost data are the main reasons the evidence conflicts. A controlled, better-instrumented experiment is not only a delay tactic; it is how mix bias and downstream cost get resolved. E can still be first, with D as the learning vehicle—this link is thin.
- **Polish.** “There were strong evidence”; broken DFG arrow (“Address validation Combined validate/schedule”); §1 and §6 repeat the same actions.

The recommendation is defensible and the structure matches the spec. The remaining gaps are mix confounding, unused SLA/NPS, vague thresholds, and a VOI ranking that does not fully track decision-flip value. Those keep it well short of 9–10 under a strict judge.
