The submission has the right outline and a recognizable business story, but it is not a reliable OCEL construction or analysis. Under a strict reading of the spec, that is a low-to-mid score: the pipeline would not run as written, the tables contradict the narrative, and several required artifacts are missing or wrong.

**Code is not executable.** `ocel:amount` is 18 values for 17 events (`[None] * 12 + [100, None, None, 150, None, None]`), so `events` never builds. Even if the length were fixed, the 100/150 amounts land on Confirm Delivery / Close Order, not the two Record Payment events. The first E1/E2 relation rows are then rebuilt in the loop, so those relations are duplicated and the leftover “for brevity” comment is not a complete construction.

**Referential integrity fails on a core fact.** O2O starts with `("O100", "Item", "contains")` instead of `I1`. That drops “O100 contains I1”, inserts a non-object id, and would trip the orphan-object assertion. Payment links use undifferentiated `settles` rather than `partially_settles` / `completes_settlement`. Every E2O qualifier is a single generic `associated_with`, which is consistent but not meaningful.

**Mandatory pipeline steps are missing or invented.** Validation never checks activity/timestamp consistency or duplicate relation rows. Frequency / object-type prints go through a non-existent `ocel.get_event_log().get_activity_occurrences()` (and similar) instead of `pm4py.ocel_get_object_types` and `pm4py.ocel_object_type_activities`. Commented OC-DFG / OC Petri net visualization calls are absent. Section 2 is a pointer to a block placed above the required heading, not the specified one-block pipeline.

**Analysis disagrees with the author’s own E2O graph and with flattening semantics.** Order traces omit Pack P2, Dispatch P2, Issue Invoice, and Confirm D2, all of which are related to O100 in `event_to_objects`. Package traces claim Add Item events, but P1/P2 are only attached to pack/dispatch. Flattening is described as putting E7 twice *inside* the O100 trace; a shared event is copied across cases (O100 and O200), not duplicated inside one case. Delivery and Invoice flattening are barely treated, so the required four-view comparison is incomplete.

**What still has value.** Object inventory, 17-event timeline, timezone-aware event timestamps, P1 as a cross-order hub, V1 billing both orders, payments as event attributes, and the three E2O policies plus most named failure modes are in the right neighborhood. Those points are why this is not a 1–2. They do not offset broken construction, hallucinated APIs, and flattened-view errors that a reviewer could not trust.
