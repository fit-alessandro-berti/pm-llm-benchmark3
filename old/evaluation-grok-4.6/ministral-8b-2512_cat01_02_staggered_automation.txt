**3.8**

The answer has the right skeleton and a few sound qualitative instincts (availability ≠ use, endogenous targeting of weak branches, no single “X%” claim). Under a strict scientific standard it is not close to reliable. It repeatedly misreads the panel, inverts signs, fabricates precision, and then recommends expanding a branch that already adopted.

**Format and compliance.** Top-level headings are not exact (`## **1. What is Observed**`, extra title). Alternatives in §3 are not in the required strategy table. §3’s rationale is well under 150 words. Several required phrases and column names are paraphrased. These are not cosmetic when the spec is explicit.

**§1 is the strongest section** and still sloppy. It correctly separates the binary flag from `automated_fast_path_pct`, names selection, routing, FTE, case-mix, and the fraud desk, and avoids a causal claim. It then misstates intensity: Alpha does not “jump from 4% to 46% in July”; use ramps 25→32→39→46. Comparing that July figure to Gamma’s second treated month (32%) as weaker fidelity is apples-to-oranges.

**Estimands are named, not identified.** The table misses the required column title, and the identifiability cells are placeholders (`(pre/post)`, `(staggered)`). ATT vs ITT vs “Median Cycle Time” are overlapping and poorly contrast-coded. The “ATE (Actual Use)” is defined at the case level, which the file cannot support. “Dynamic Learning” on Gamma July–August is two points, not an estimand. Interference and treatment-version get a note, not a workable definition (SUTVA failure via the shared fraud desk; multiple versions of “automation=1”).

**Identification is TWFE in event-study clothing.** Relative time is described as `t=0` for Alpha/Beta and `t=3` for Gamma — calendar time, not event time. There is no discussion of forbidden comparisons, already-treated controls, or heterogeneous adoption-cohort effects, which the prompt explicitly required if anything TWFE-like was used. “Staggered design reduces selection bias” is false: Alpha/Beta were chosen *because* they were worse. Synthetic control with two donors and two post periods for Gamma is named, not designed. The preference paragraph does not earn the primary strategy.

**Mechanisms are directionless and sometimes backwards.** Beneficial/harmful signatures omit ↑/↓, so the table does not encode a testable implication. Specialist-queue congestion lists a rising `p90` as a *beneficial* signature. Survey-selection signs are scrambled. Evidence cells cherry-pick (Alpha Jan 50.4 vs Aug 43.3, skipping June 53.5) or contradict the mechanism (Beta p90 called “stable at ~105h” as tail-amplification evidence; it rose from the low 90s after May).

**§5 is the collapse.** The stated window is “pre = 3 months, post = 4 months.” The numbers do not implement that window and do not even stay in the correct outcome.

- Alpha post median is reported as 47.9h (July), not the Apr–Jul mean (~49.8h).
- Alpha post p90 is May’s 112.6, not the post-window mean (~110h).
- The rework column is populated with escalation-like figures (Alpha 11.6, Beta 10.8, Gamma 10.7). Pre-adoption rework is 13.2 / 12.8 / 12.3, not what is tabulated. This is a column swap, not rounding.
- Gamma’s pre median 38.1 is Jan–Apr, not the three months before July (Apr–Jun ≈ 43.7). Post p90 is July only, then described as an “improvement” of +5.7h. An increase in p90 is a deterioration.
- Never-treated contrasts use March vs August, which hides the common May–June spike and the partial recovery that *all* branches share.
- The intensity result “−1.5h per 10% (p<0.05)” and Gamma’s “~2.0h” are not recoverable from a careful reading of the five series and are not credible with five clusters. This is manufactured precision.
- Pre-trend text says Gamma’s pre-period is stable at 36.4–36.5 and that a move to 38.4 is “unlikely RTM.” That ignores the untreated May–June jump to ~45–46h — the most important pre-trend in the file.
- The May p90 jump is treated as an automation effect. It is a common shock: Gamma, Delta, and Epsilon (all still untreated) move about as much as Alpha/Beta when `high_complexity_pct` rises. Collapsing that into “automation harms tails by +5 to +20h” is the central causal error.

Arithmetic facts and causal interpretation are not kept apart.

**Threats are listed, bias signs are not.** Coverage of the required threats is fine. Several rows say both “positive” and “underestimate,” or “negative” and “overestimate.” Intensity-as-instrument is not a diagnostic; `automated_fast_path_pct` is endogenous to learning, routing, and mix. This section reads like a checklist, not an identification argument.

**Effect assessment is overconfident and downstream of §5.** Confidence 85 that p90 is harmful is indefensible once the common May shock is recognized. Leadership is told it may *safely* claim a 5–20h tail increase; that is not a safe claim. Median “up to 3 hours in high-fidelity implementations” and CSAT confidence 50 with unknown branch-month response counts are also oversold. Escalation is called unchanged while Alpha’s +3pp is used as counterevidence — internally inconsistent, and still unadjusted for the March fraud-desk level shift that hits every branch.

**Rollout chooses the right label and the wrong branch.** `expand with an embedded evaluation` is the only defensible family. The plan then “expands to Gamma first” and “deploys automation to Gamma in September.” Gamma already adopted in July. That is a process-mining failure, not a wording slip. Delta-as-next is under-motivated (most unlike the original high-volume, high-cycle targets, so a poor external-validity test for Epsilon). One-branch-per-month phase-in does not create a control that stays clean, does not break the fraud-desk spillover, and does not fix case-level missingness. Guardrails (p90 +10%, CSAT −0.2) are arbitrary relative to month-to-month noise already in the table.

**The Python block would not do what §3 and the spec require, and would not run as written.** `pd.compat.StringIO` is not a portable loader. Trends are not plotted or printed month-by-month; matplotlib is imported and unused. `escalation_pct` is omitted. `adoption_month = groupby(branch).month.min()` is January for every branch, so “relative time” is calendar time. The “ITT” OLS has neither branch FE nor month FE nor event-time dummies — it is not the staggered event study advertised. Clustering on five branches is warned about, which is the one honest line. Identifying assumptions are not stated as comments in any serious way. A “reproducible analysis” that cannot reproduce §5’s starred −1.5h / p<0.05 result is a tell that those numbers were not computed.

**Claim ladder ends by contradicting itself.** The required four prefixes are present, but the last bullet says a blanket “X%” claim *is defensible without* branch-specific or intensity-adjusted analysis. The intended word is “not.” In the one place leadership might quote, the polarity is wrong.

**Net.** This is a template-aware memo with the correct refusal of a single headline elasticity, sitting on a broken quantitative core. A lead scientist who swaps rework for escalation, calls a p90 increase an improvement, attributes a system-wide complexity shock to automation, publishes a p-value they did not identify, and schedules a go-live for a live site would not pass internal review. The remaining credit is for structure, confounder inventory, and the high-level decision class — not for the analysis.
