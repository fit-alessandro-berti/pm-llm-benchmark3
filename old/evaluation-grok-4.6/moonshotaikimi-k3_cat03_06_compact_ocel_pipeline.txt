**8.4**

This is a strong, internally consistent solution that hits the required structure and almost all of the stated technical constraints. It is not nearly flawless.

**What is solid.** The four DataFrames cover all 17 events and 12 objects; E2O rows copy activity and timestamp from the event table; O2O covers contains / packs / carries / bills / settlement; amounts stay on payment events; timestamps are UTC-aware; IDs are unique. Validation is better than the minimum: orphans, type alignment, O2O direction, duplicate rows, and payment-attribute placement are all checked. Flattening, DFG, log skeleton, OC-DFG, OTG, ET–OT, OCPN, and interactions are stored as specified. The expected Order / Package / Delivery / Invoice traces match the chosen E2O policy, including the I2 path with no reserve and the shared-event copies of E06, E07, and E11. The OCPN is correctly treated as non-unique. Failure modes and reviewer checks are concrete.

**What keeps it off a high score.**

1. **Policy drift on E08.** Event 7 is explicitly multi-order. Event 8 is only “Pack P2 with I2.” Linking E08 to O100 is inferred from containment, which is the same style of propagation the write-up claims to keep out of canonical `relations` for dispatch, confirm, and payment. The alternative is discussed, but the implemented “source-scoped” rule is not applied evenly.

2. **M2 as `completes_settlement`.** The narrative calls both M1 and M2 partial payments and gives no invoice total. The assumption is disclosed, but the label is still stronger than the facts.

3. **Version surface is wider than admitted.** Only the constructor is isolated. Unpacking `discover_dfg` as a 3-tuple and iterating flattening output as an EventLog are also version-sensitive and would break the pipeline if those contracts differ.

4. **A few analysis soft spots.** OTG is hedged into two incompatible stories instead of committing to what `discover_otg` actually uses. Log skeletons on two-trace logs are statistically weak; that is not said. Payment flattening is argued but not run, so the “especially weak” claim is not demonstrated in the pipeline.

None of these break the business story, but they are real inconsistencies and executability risks. Under a strict reading, that is well above competent and short of near-flawless.
