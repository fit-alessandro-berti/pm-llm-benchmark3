**5.4**

The final write-up is structurally complete and gets the main policy call right (`conditional go`, do not copy “create both checks after Register for every case,” keep statutory order, start with the no-originals subset). It also correctly refuses to treat Site A’s 9-hour median drop as an identified treatment effect and names the main co-interventions (mix shift, +2 FTE, API, huddles, timestamp artifact). That is not enough for a high score under a strict rubric. There are material factual, quantitative, and model-fidelity failures.

**Format and required content.** Section 1 has 11 rows, not the required twelve-plus. The mandated feature **loops** is missing as a row. Two rows are mis-filled: “tail behavior” puts transport commentary in the Site B column; “rework” describes a hypothetical future harm instead of Site B’s baseline 17%. Section 9’s heading is not the required exact heading. The transport “adjacency list” is not a clean directed graph: it invents nodes (`identity_check_service`, `document_check_prerequisite`), smuggles a comment into an edge, and then adds tautological `S_X -> X` arrows for almost every variable, including outcomes. `S_digital_documents` — the most important site difference — is absent. Co-intervention arrows from huddles, API latency, staffing, and mix into `cycle_time` are missing.

**Causal / analogical substance.** The qualitative story (originals on the critical path, role specialization, case lock, legal supplement loop, assessment bottleneck) is directionally right and is the strongest part of the memo. The quantitative analogical work is weak. Site B’s DFG is almost unused: 2,108 `Originals Receipt -> Document Check` matches the 62% legal constraint; 1,160 `Identity Check -> Document Check` is the natural no-originals path; 360 supplement loops on 1,360 legal reviews is a usable rework rate; identity already starts from Register in 2,050 cases. None of this is turned into a bound. A tight structural bound is available and unused: for originals-required cases, Document Check cannot start at Register, so copying Site A cannot remove the 18h originals delay; the only free overlap is Identity ∥ Originals Receipt (already partly present). Maximum service-time saving from overlapping 3.4h and 8.1h is `min(3.4, 8.1)`, not a 15–25% cycle-time cut. Resource counts (4 / 7 / 3 / 2) are never converted into load. The 38% “digital” share is treated as the complement of 62% originals-required; Site B never reports a digital-channel share, and legal originals ≠ channel. That conflation is then reused in Sections 4–7, 9, and 10.

**Scenarios.** Ranges are asserted, not mapped from Site A’s confounded deltas plus Site B’s constraints. The “optimistic” row assumes legal prerequisites are bypassed and still sets eligible share to 38% — internally inconsistent. The required “mean improves, tail worsens” case is not actually delivered: bottleneck-shift gives median **−5% to +2%** and p90 **−2% to +5%**, i.e. both intervals straddle zero. That is not a scenario in which average rises and the tail falls.

**Pilot.** Required fields are named, and the mechanism-versus-implementation paragraph is the best short piece in the memo. The design itself is incoherent: team-level randomization, a six-week calendar phase-in by case type, and three treatment versions are specified at once without a factorial structure. “200 cases / 80% power / 10% median reduction” is unsupported given p90 = 110h. Daily shared queue metrics “not acted on” conflicts with the WIP/huddle controls the same memo recommends.

**Section 8 is a fail on model fidelity.** The code will not run. Invented or misspelled APIs include `pm4py.objects.powl.factory`, `create_powl_from_edge_list`, `PetrinNet`, `pm4py.conversion.petri_net.PetriNetConverter`, `pm4py.formatadores`, `add_ enabling` (syntax error), `pm4py.play_out(net, 10)`, and `get_traces_as_list`. Activities are empty `POWL()` objects rather than `Transition`s. `parse_process_tree` is called on unquoted labels. Worse conceptually: a **strict partial order is given a cycle** (`Legal Review → Request Supplement → Document Check → Assessment → Legal Review`). A partial order cannot encode a loop; the prompt required an operator loop plus an optional legal branch. Legal review is also wired as always-on, not XOR-optional. The limitation comments are fine; they do not rescue a fabricated, unsound construction.

**Recommendation and assumptions.** `conditional go`, first no-originals stratum, keep statutory gates, add WIP and atomic logging — reasonable. Expected 15–25% median gains are overconfident relative to the author’s own confounding caveats. The eight assumptions follow the required template; several tests are operational checklists rather than attempts to falsify the claim, and assumption 1 inherits the digital/originals conflation.

Net: a competent policy memo with the right high-level transport verdict, undermined by a missing required row, an invalid transport graph, unused DFG/capacity evidence, internally broken scenarios, a self-contradictory pilot, and non-executable PM4Py that violates the partial-order/loop distinction the question was written to test. Not near-flawless.
