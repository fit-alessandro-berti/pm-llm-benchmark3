**5.2**

The write-up has the right skeleton for this task—parallel max rather than a sum, weekend pauses, FIFO vs risk-priority, path bands, and a real attempt at a future QA queue—but it is not close to flawless, and the numbers that matter most do not hold together.

The decisive failure is in §4. Remaining hours and clock times contradict each other. Friday 15:30 → Monday 16:30 is **73 h**, not “~49 h (2.0 days).” Friday → Tuesday morning is about **88–95 h**, not 55–80 h. The 80 / 90 bands are similarly shifted. The model’s own earlier timeline (Monday 08:00 as t = 64.5 h) is abandoned in the headline forecast. For a remaining-time problem, that is a core error, not a rounding slip.

A second structural contradiction: §3 and §6 correctly imply that Sunday on-call readout usually **does not move the join**, because chemistry cannot finish Friday and QA is closed Sunday. §5 then ranks weekend microbiology staffing as the **#1** uncertainty driver. That ranking is incompatible with the join analysis.

Calendar handling is uneven. Confirmation incubation ending Tuesday ~05:00 is treated as a 05:30 result; readout still needs a microbiologist in 08:00–18:00 hours, so the early edge is too early. QA wait is argued two ways at once (≈6 h behind ~4.8 jobs vs a 2–3 job queue by 10:30). Rejection is asserted at “<2%” with almost no path math.

Smaller issues compound under a strict read: other chemistry jobs are given B-417’s full triangular law without a tighter bound; QA capacity uses a σ≈1 lognormal mean as if it were a stable rate; the “smooth” band quietly folds in “no on-call” and “no QA tail,” so the table is neither a partition nor a clean set of overlapping mechanisms; temperature-excursion dependence is named well in §5–6 but never shown as a joint tail in the scenario table.

What remains creditworthy is real: required six-section form, max-vs-sum stated, chemistry Friday start/pause reasoned, FIFO vs priority distinguished, contamination and investigation tails separated from the smooth Monday join, and the next-event discussion (flag vs next chemistry pull) is directionally right. That is a competent qualitative forecast sitting on broken arithmetic and one self-contradictory ranking. Under the requested standard, that is mid-pack, not high.
