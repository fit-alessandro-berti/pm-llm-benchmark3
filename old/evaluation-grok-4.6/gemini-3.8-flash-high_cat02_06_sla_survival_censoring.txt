**6.2**

The submission is a competent, well-structured process-mining survival analysis: it defines estimands before estimators, keeps open cases in the risk set, treats transfer/withdrawal as competing or informative rather than casual KM censoring, and flags immortal time and leakage on `Escalate`. Those are the right instincts. Under a strict bar it is not nearly flawless. Several numbers and process claims are invented or internally inconsistent, one priority conclusion misclassifies a still-at-risk case, the process model omits an observed arc, and the code does not fully implement the analysis the prose reports.

**What is actually strong.** Time origin at `Submit` and horizon \(\tau=72\) are correct. \(\psi_1=1-F_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(72)\) and \(\psi_2=S_{\mathrm{active}}(72)\) are distinct, operationally mapped, and the four worked examples mostly show the right contribution of late resolve, already-breached open, early withdraw, and pre-horizon transfer. The completed-case critique is concrete (S03/S13/S20/S28 vs discarded S09/S11/S25/S26). The \(1-\mathrm{KM}\) competing-risk fallacy is stated correctly. The three transfer stories (absorbing competing state, informative censoring concentrated in escalated P1, contractual failure) match the data pattern. Landmarking as a guard against immortal time is the right primary design for these prefixes. The AJ active-survival figures \(S(72)\approx 38.0\%\) (transfer as event) and \(43.5\%\) (transfer as censor) are consistent with a hand reconstruction of the 32-row risk set. P1 CIF \(25\%\) and P3 CIF \(20\%\) also check out. Section 9 hits every requested missing-data item.

**Estimand problems.** Estimand 4 is not a fourth target; it is \(F_{\mathrm{Resolve}}(72)=1-\psi_1\), mislabeled as “resolution velocity.” \(\psi_3=F(\infty)-F(72)\) is not identifiable from this freeze (nine opens, three transfers with unknown external outcomes); that is never said. For S06, section 1’s formal \(\psi_1\) counts non-resolution as breach, then the case walkthrough reintroduces an undefined “customer-exemption” rule. For S03 the text asserts the case was “in the second `Review` step” at hour 72. Intermediate timestamps are not in the extract; that is a fabricated observation.

**Encoding and naive-analysis section.** Status counts and ranges are correct. The transfer discussion is the best part of the paper. Two slips: the mapping table only lists two transfer alternatives while the prose promises three; open-case sensitivity “impute breach if gap \(>24\)h” is harmless here only because every such open is already past 72h, which is not stated as a data accident. In section 3, “erases 4 confirmed breaches” ignores that a resolved-only completed-case analysis also drops S32 (transferred at 96h, already past SLA). Cohort B “has had less follow-up” is a calendar-time hypothesis and should have been labeled as such.

**Analysis plan.** The method table is relevant to these data, not a generic laundry list. KM-for-\(\psi_2\) vs AJ-for-CIFs is the right split. Overreach: Fine–Gray and a semi-Markov model are recommended without a hard stop that \(N=32\), no dwell times, and no interval timestamps make both infeasible as fitted models. Landmark logistic models at 24h/48h on a handful of still-open cases are not something that “respects the small sample size.” No variance, CI, or bootstrap is planned despite later reporting tenths of a percent.

**Process features.** Immortal time and full-trace leakage are explained well, with S18/S16/S05 as counterexamples to baseline `escalated`. Failures: the Mermaid graph includes `AutoCheck→Resolve` labeled “Instant Auto-Resolution,” which overreads S01 (`Screen` then `AutoCheck` then `Resolve`), and **omits observed `Screen→AutoCheck`** (S01, S15, S17)—exactly the compliance path the cohort discussion needs. `last_event_gap_h` is listed as a general time-varying covariate; in this table it is \(0\) for every terminal row, so it is nearly an open-vs-closed indicator and is leaky if used to predict duration on completed cases. That circularity is not flagged.

**Sensitivity: real calculations mixed with a material error.** Transfer scenarios 1–2 are coherent. Scenario 3’s “Breach \(=47.4\%\)” is never defined in section 1; it appears to be \(S_{\mathrm{active}}(72)+F_{\mathrm{Transfer}}(72)\), a fifth estimand smuggled into a table. Point estimates to one decimal with \(N=32\) are presented as if they could reverse policy. The P1 “reversal” is directionally fair (transfers and stalled opens wreck a completed-resolve story) but **\(75\%\) failure counts S29 (open at 51h, gap 6h) as a failure**. S29 is still inside the SLA window; the known P1 non-resolution mass at 72h is 5/8, with 1/8 still at risk. That is not a minor wording issue. The Cohort B “as-treated reversal” is also oversold: AutoCheck-first CIF \(33.3\%\) vs screen-first \(28.6\%\) does not reverse a benefit, and “severe tail degradation” among S20/S23/S26/S30 is not a subgroup analysis, just four mixed outcomes.

**Operations.** Prognostic-vs-causal language on escalation is correct and should be kept. The alert tree’s \(15\%/55\%/30\%/80\%\) and the “exceeds \(70\%\)” claim at the 48h landmark are not computed from the 32 cases; they violate the “do not invent observations” rule. The subsection titled calibration only discusses fairness audits. No calibration design (horizon-specific CIF vs observed, stratified reliability by priority/cohort) is given.

**Code vs specification.** The in-memory table, two printed estimands, retained opens, cohort/priority stratification, prefix parsing, `get_variants`, and a lifelines fallback sketch meet most of the checklist. Gaps that matter: `s3_codes` is never passed into the estimator, so section 6’s third transfer scenario is not produced by the code that supposedly generates the report; competing events are collapsed to a single code, so cause-specific transfer CIF cannot be recovered; \(\psi_3\) is undefined in code; control-flow features used in analysis (`repeated_review`, `autocheck_initial`) are string splits, not PM4Py objects; `parse_event_log_string` typically injects synthetic timestamps and may return an event log rather than a frame, so the `case:concept:name` remap is brittle. No CIs are printed.

**Net.** This is an upper-tier exam answer on concepts and structure, not a clean analysis. The P1 75% figure, invented 72h stage for S03, invented alert probabilities, missing `Screen→AutoCheck`, unidentifiable \(\psi_3\), unused scenario-3 encoding, and precision without uncertainty are enough, under the instructed strictness, to keep it well below the high band.
