The response covers all seven requested sections, classifies patterns A–L, and uses the timeline, DFG deviations, conformance findings, and segment/product risks to distinguish resilience from concealed noncompliance. It also proposes concrete recovery controls and indicators, including the lingering audit-remediation workload.

The grade is limited by several unsupported prescriptions. It introduces numerical triggers and targets—such as WMS outage thresholds, remediation deadlines, and “zero tolerance” indicators—that are not grounded in the supplied evidence or justified as proposed policy choices. Its simplified risk score and triage rules also risk obscuring conflicts between customer criticality and product controls. Finally, some adaptations are classified too broadly: for example, “sample release” for Pharmacy-standard ambient orders is not established by the prompt, and the response does not clearly distinguish the observed temporary release control from its proposed future design.
