The response follows the required nine-section structure and makes the key distinction between observed default among activated accounts and a broader, policy-dependent question about all applications. It appropriately rejects treating rejected, withdrawn, or open applications as non-default; discusses censoring, delayed labels, transfer, missing feeds, leakage, and temporal validation; and provides a concrete PM4Py-oriented skeleton.

The main weakness is that the cohort-flow treatment is not fully auditable: it needs to reconcile the listed counts without double-counting, especially the 3,600 activated accounts with immature outcomes, and its code’s terminal-state and maturity logic does not clearly distinguish those accounts from mature activated cases with a missing feed. The code also appears to derive some cohort states and features from the full event file before enforcing the prediction-time cutoff, which makes its no-future-information guarantee less convincing than the prose. These gaps limit confidence in the implementation and the claimed accounting, despite a strong overall design.
