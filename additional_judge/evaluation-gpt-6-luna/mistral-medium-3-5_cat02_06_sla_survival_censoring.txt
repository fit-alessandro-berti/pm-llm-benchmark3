The largest error is treating late resolution as the primary event in a standard time-to-event analysis. “Not resolved by 72 hours” is a horizon status: a case resolved after 72 hours is already a breach at hour 72, not an event occurring after that horizon. The response’s KM setup instead labels every resolution—including those before 72 hours—as the event, then interprets survival at 72 as breach probability. That does not estimate the stated probability. Its comments about truncating competing events and open cases also do not fix this mismatch.

The response correctly identifies that open cases beyond 72 hours have already breached, but its encoding does not consistently preserve that fact across the proposed analyses. It also fails to state clearly how each required example contributes under each estimand. Its transfer scenarios are useful in principle, but the scenario that treats a transfer as a resolution conflates external transfer with internal `Resolve`, and the Aalen–Johansen event-code remapping is not reliable as written.

The statistical plan is broad and relevant, and it correctly cautions against causal claims about cohort B. Yet it proposes synthetic timestamps from activity order for PM4Py, which cannot support valid stage-time or transition-hazard analysis. The response notes that timestamps are missing, but should have kept process mining descriptive or explicitly limited the pseudocode to features derivable from sequences. Overall, it has good coverage and several appropriate caveats, but the flawed estimand implementation and event coding materially weaken the analysis.
