The selected portfolio is not well supported by the stated rule. It applies an invented 40% Strategic Enterprise cap and a three-slot limit despite management’s reserve preference being nonbinding. The impact table is not auditable as written. It counts “3” avoided breaches optimistically and “2 … 1” in the base case without explaining how fractional risk reductions translate into plausibly avoided breaches. The base arithmetic uses midpoint ARR for I-01, although its Public Sector calibration is described as broadly acceptable; it also subtracts an unexplained average false-alarm burden and gives a different total from the stated per-case calculation. Spillover harm assumes an average queue value of €15,000 that the prompt does not supply. The adverse case’s €6,000–€8,000 direct benefit is not reconciled with its claimed one effective case.

The response does well to suppress I-05 absent new evidence, distinguish I-07’s new log, exclude I-03 after Diagnosis Complete, and identify calibration, selective-label, and spillover risks. But the temporal policy has an unsupported fixed two-hour cooldown, and re-entry after Diagnosis Complete is confusing because the swarm is then ineffective. Monitoring proposals are concrete in places, though propensity matching alone does not establish causal effects, and “false alarm” is treated too simply for an intervention that can prevent breach.

Overall, this is a structured and relevant answer, but unsupported thresholds and caps, inconsistent calculations, and insufficiently justified case selection keep it from being a reliable deployable policy.
