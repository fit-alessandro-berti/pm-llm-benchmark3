6.5/10 — The answer handles the central issue well: it defines a conditional target for activated accounts, does not label rejected or withdrawn applications as non-default, recognizes activation selection, and covers major leakage and temporal-shift risks.

Several problems materially weaken the design:

- The survival setup is internally inconsistent. It calls the target a 90-day binary outcome, yet proposes survival analysis without a default-event timestamp and assigns observed non-defaults a horizon-end time. That can support a fixed-horizon outcome analysis, but not the stated event-time analysis as written. The censoring-time expression is also ambiguous, and the code uses `EXTRACT - BUFFER` as a common censor date rather than censoring each account based on activation time and observed follow-up.
- The cohort table treats all 3,600 immature activated accounts as right-censored, although some may have completed the 90-day outcome window and only be awaiting the delayed feed. Delayed ascertainment and outcome censoring need separate handling. It also calls about 1,160 unlinked transfers “permanently missing” without evidence that all are permanently unlinked.
- The proposed all-application alternative is underspecified: treating rejection, withdrawal, and transfer as competing outcomes does not by itself identify the probability of default, which occurs only after activation. The answer acknowledges selection but overstates that a multistate model “reduces selection bias.”
- Prefix extraction does not actually demonstrate `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log`, as required. The claimed survival and competing-risk metric recommendations also need more careful alignment with the fixed 90-day outcome and the available event-time data.
- The code’s terminal-state logic is wrong for the described process: it searches for “Accepted” as terminal even though activation follows acceptance, and takes the first terminal event without ensuring ordering. The audit assertion checks feature names against a leakage set but does not verify that prefix rows are correctly time-truncated or that `risk_score` values were observed by the prediction time.
- The proposed test period ends on 30 September 2025, so it does not test the 15 October migration, despite describing the final test as post-migration. The walk-forward phases also conflict with the stated train/validation/test periods.

The answer is broad and mostly directionally sound, but these errors affect the validity of the censoring strategy and the promised implementation, preventing a higher grade.
