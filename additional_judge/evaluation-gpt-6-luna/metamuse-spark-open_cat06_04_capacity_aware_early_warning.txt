1. Decision rule

A case is eligible only before Diagnosis Complete, with enough time to start the swarm before its benefit window closes, and with a material new alert or event. Prioritize by robust expected value: calibrated breach risk × plausible risk reduction × value avoided, less the €450 swarm cost, false-alarm burden, and congestion-dependent harm to untreated cases. Treat effect ranges as uncertain estimates, not causal facts; show optimistic, base, and adverse results. A case whose estimated net benefit is negative even under reasonable assumptions waits or is declined.

Adjust risk as an interval: add 0.04–0.09 for SMEs, subtract 0.05–0.12 for Strategic Enterprise, and leave Public Sector broadly as predicted while reflecting moderate sample size. For Non-Native-Language Support, retain a wide uncertainty interval and require human review. Do not let a probability adjustment alone decide allocation. Missing or imputed decision-critical features require validation before using the alert. Repeated alerts persist only when a new event or material evidence change warrants review. Reserve one of four slots for the possible regulatory incident where feasible; release it if the incident does not arrive and a suitable case remains.

4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Suppress refresh-only alerts for a case during a 60-minute cool-down. Reopen review sooner only for a material event, a validated feature change, a stage transition that affects eligibility, or a substantial risk change—for example, at least 0.10 absolute after accounting for calibration uncertainty. After a swarm, prohibit another for the same case unless a materially new event occurs and a reviewer documents why another intervention could help. Escalate unresolved high-priority cases to a human reviewer; do not equate repeated alerts with repeated evidence.

I-05 has no new event, so suppress the repeated alerts and do not swarm. Its SME calibration issue warrants portfolio-level correction, not repeated action on a stale alert. I-07 has a new customer log, so review the log and rescore; if it materially changes the diagnosis or expected benefit, it can re-enter prioritization despite the lower risk estimate. The new event justifies review, not automatic re-intervention.

5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Value-based allocation can starve lower-value cases. Set a minimum share of review capacity for lower-value or underserved groups, and audit breach outcomes as well as swarm rates. Do not let a value floor override stage eligibility or a negative expected-benefit assessment, but use it to break close allocation ties.

Non-Native-Language Support has sparse calibration and intervention evidence. Use manual review, record uncertainty explicitly, offer appropriate language support during intervention, and avoid interpreting a low observed intervention rate as evidence of low need.

Calibration differs by segment: SME risk is underpredicted, Strategic Enterprise risk is overpredicted, and Public Sector evidence is moderate. Use segment-specific uncertainty intervals, publish adjusted and raw risks, and review calibration and allocation by segment. Do not treat the adjustments as exact corrections.

Selective swarming changes outcomes and therefore future training labels. Log alert, eligibility, intervention timing, stage, and treatment assignment. Evaluate outcomes with causal methods and, where feasible, randomized tie-breaks among similarly ranked eligible cases. Do not retrain as if untreated outcomes were observed for swarmed cases.

Spillovers may fall disproportionately on untreated groups, especially if those cases share the diagnosis queue. Track queue delay and outcomes by the exposed cases’ segment and value. Include spillover costs in allocation decisions, rotate specialist interruption fairly, and stop or limit swarms when congestion makes external harm excessive.

6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

Track daily eligibility, alerts suppressed, swarm starts, slot use, specialist interruption, queue length, and added diagnosis delay. Compare observed delays and breach outcomes for exposed untreated cases on swarm days with comparable periods, stratified by segment.

Review calibration weekly by segment, including feature missingness and imputed values. Use the stated adjustment ranges until post-redesign data support more reliable estimates; investigate material drift and recalibrate after workflow changes. For Non-Native-Language Support, report wide intervals and manually review decisions while sample sizes remain small.

Estimate intervention effects using randomized tie-breaks among near-equal eligible cases when operationally feasible, with outcomes recorded by assignment and actual treatment. Supplement with carefully adjusted observational estimates and report confidence intervals by stage and segment. Monitor whether effects differ by congestion, and update the supplied ranges only as evidence accumulates.

Review capacity utilization, reserve use, queue externalities, direct benefit, and estimated net portfolio impact weekly. Monitor intervention and breach rates by segment, value band, and language-support status; investigate persistent disparities and any concentration of spillover harm on untreated groups. Keep a decision log with raw and adjusted risk, evidence changes, chosen action, and rationale so policy effects can be separated from model drift.
