## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw-fitness collapse (0.93 to 0.55) alongside mapped fitness of 0.90, near-baseline mapped adjacency rates, and newly appearing activity labels strongly implicates the migration’s label and event-provenance changes in the raw metric discontinuity. The gap indicates that the old model and raw labels are poorly aligned; it does not establish that execution was unchanged or that mapped fitness measures every business rule correctly.

Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` restores a known semantic equivalence for conformance checking. That is useful only to the extent the mapping is valid and complete. It can recover conformance while missing changes in routing, timing, case mix, or unmodeled behavior. A mapping is not a replay against a fully specified normative model.

Duplicated `Open Case` events can create spurious DFG edges, starts, and apparent variant changes; removing them improves those analyses. Missing resource identifiers impair resource attribution, handover and workload analysis, and team-level comparisons. Because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities and varies by month or team, resource-complete-only analysis risks selection bias. Neither issue alone explains worsening cycle time or the post-May increase in normalized information-request and return relations.

After May, mapped fitness declines, information-request rates increase, and both median and p90 cycle time rise. These patterns persist in normalized series that account for the stated label substitution and duplicate events. They cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone, though supplier exposure, complexity, staffing, seasonality, and incomplete timing semantics prevent a unique causal decomposition.

### Cycle-time deterioration decomposition

Relative to the January–February median of 31.15 hours, the November median is 49 hours, a net increase of about 17.9 hours. The p90 rises about 53 hours (68→121). The aggregates do not identify additive causal shares, and the following components can overlap:

- **Queueing and capacity:** Likely the largest proximate contributor to sustained delay. Reconstructed waiting before `Assess` rises much more than recorded Assess service time. Four assessors left in June, and volume rises toward the autumn peak. This is consistent with congestion and backlog, but capacity and backlog are not quantified.
- **Service time:** Recorded median Assess service time rises about 0.6 hours (4.8→5.4 by July–August), small compared with the total median-cycle increase. It is one activity’s recorded median, not total labor time or all service.
- **Control-flow/rework:** Information requests rise from 12 to 29 per 100 cases by July; rework variants rise from 11–12% to 29%. Extra document collection and reassessment plausibly add labor and external waiting. The amount of elapsed-time contribution is unknown.
- **Case mix/load:** High-complexity share rises from about 20.5% to 45% by November, and monthly volume from roughly 613 to 790. These shifts plausibly increase both work content and queue pressure. Without within-stratum times, their separate contribution cannot be estimated.
- **Priority redistribution:** The July rule may reduce high-complexity waiting while increasing standard-case waiting. Aggregate medians and tails cannot identify this distributional effect.

These are explanatory channels, not separable percentages: for example, supplier-driven rework can increase queueing, and seasonal complexity can increase both service demand and waiting.

### Candidate change points and global-change assessment

- **Logging drift:** March 1 is the documented candidate onset; March is the first monthly observation showing new labels, duplicate opens, and increased missingness. April is a persistence check, not an independent onset.
- **Behavioral drift:** May 5 is a candidate onset for supplier-linked return-loop behavior; June 15 is a separate candidate for staffing/queue consequences; July 1 is a dated priority-rule change. Monthly data cannot resolve exact within-month effects.
- **Performance drift:** May is the first monthly cycle-time rise; June–August is a candidate sustained queueing regime after the staffing loss; October is a further seasonal/load-associated increase.

The evidence favors multiple mechanism-specific candidate change points over one global change point. March’s raw-fitness break is largely accounted for by measurement changes, while performance and normalized loop changes emerge from May onward; staffing and priority events follow, and seasonal composition rises in October. Formal change-point detection on monthly aggregates would have low temporal resolution and could mistake concurrent shifts for one event.

## 7. Process-mining analysis plan

1. **Validate and prepare the event log.** Confirm case and event identifiers, timestamp semantics and timezone, event ordering, lifecycle fields, and source-system versions. Preserve the input and original event labels. Record the eligibility alias mapping and duplicate-open rule as versioned transformations with counts and provenance. Do not drop cases with missing resources.

2. **Construct comparable arrival cohorts.** Assign cases by intake time, use a common observation cutoff and explicit maturity horizon, and report open/censored cases. Compare the same case populations in raw and normalized views. Separate cohort outcomes from events merely occurring in a calendar month.

3. **Produce canonical and diagnostic views.** Build per-case variants and full DFGs under raw and validated canonical labels; retain event counts, case frequencies, starts, ends, and loop intensity. Assess conformance only after approving a normative model that encodes permitted loops and senior-assessment semantics. Track raw and mapped fitness separately.

4. **Decompose performance.** Derive intake-to-ready, ready-to-start, activity service, inter-activity waits, post-decision, and total cycle time. Report medians and upper quantiles with censoring rules; relate waiting and backlog to staffed capacity. Distinguish repeated visit workload from external document wait where timestamps permit.

5. **Stratify before attributing.** Compare supplier and non-supplier cases by intake complexity, region, customer type, renewal period, and cohort maturity. Compare high- and standard-complexity waiting around the July priority change. Report overlap, cell sizes, and standardized as well as unadjusted outcomes.

6. **Assess missing resources explicitly.** Profile missingness by activity, month, team, and case type; preserve a missing category in resource analyses. Compare full-log process/performance results with resource-complete subsets as a sensitivity analysis, not as the primary population.

7. **Test candidate change points and interactions.** Use daily/weekly series where available, assess March logging, May supplier, June staffing, July priority, October renewal, and December interventions separately, and quantify uncertainty. Test plausible interactions—supplier exposure with capacity, and seasonal complexity with priority—without treating observational associations as causal effects.

8. **Validate findings.** Review sampled traces with process owners and reconcile migration mappings, supplier defect records, staffing changes, and priority dispatch logs. Report which conclusions are descriptive, which are mechanism-consistent, and which remain unidentified.

### Implementation sketch

The following Python illustrates a reproducible comparison workflow using event-level data. It is deliberately not executable on the supplied aggregates alone; the exact input schema, timezone, duplicate key, maturity horizon, and approved normative model must be configured. The log is prepared once, and arrival windows are taken from that common prepared log. Missing resources remain in the log. The snippet is an analysis template, not an assertion that any results were computed here.

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from collections.abc import Mapping
from typing import Any

import pandas as pd


CASE = "case_id"
ACTIVITY = "activity"
TIME = "timestamp"
RESOURCE = "resource"


def require(condition: bool, message: str) -> None:
    if not condition:
        raise ValueError(message)


def pm_module():
    try:
        import pm4py
    except ImportError as exc:
        raise RuntimeError("Install pm4py in the analysis environment") from exc
    return pm4py


def prepare_events(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    *,
    source_timezone: str | None = None,
    sequence_key: str | None = None,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    required = {CASE, ACTIVITY, TIME}
    missing = required - set(df.columns)
    require(not missing, f"Missing required columns: {sorted(missing)}")

    out = df.copy(deep=True)
    out[TIME] = pd.to_datetime(out[TIME], errors="raise")

    if out[TIME].dt.tz is None:
        require(source_timezone is not None, "Naive timestamps require source_timezone")
        out[TIME] = out[TIME].dt.tz_localize(
            source_timezone, ambiguous="raise", nonexistent="raise"
        )

    if sequence_key is not None:
        require(sequence_key in out.columns, f"Missing sequence key: {sequence_key}")
        require(
            not out.duplicated([CASE, sequence_key]).any(),
            f"{CASE}/{sequence_key} must identify an event",
        )
        order = [CASE, TIME, sequence_key]
    else:
        order = [CASE, TIME]

    out = out.sort_values(order, kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)
    return out


def normalized_copy(
    raw: pd.DataFrame,
    *,
    duplicate_flag: str | None = None,
) -> pd.DataFrame:
    """Return a copy with only explicitly approved event-level normalizations."""
    out = raw.copy(deep=True)
    out[ACTIVITY] = out[ACTIVITY].replace(
        {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}
    )

    if duplicate_flag is not None:
        require(duplicate_flag in out.columns, f"Missing duplicate flag: {duplicate_flag}")
        out = out.loc[~out[duplicate_flag].fillna(False).astype(bool)].copy()

    return out.sort_values([CASE, TIME], kind="stable").reset_index(drop=True)


def arrival_window(
    log: pd.DataFrame,
    start: pd.Timestamp,
    end: pd.Timestamp,
    *,
    as_of: pd.Timestamp,
    horizon_days: float,
) -> tuple[pd.DataFrame, dict[str, Any]]:
    """Select cases by intake time and classify maturity at a fixed horizon."""
    first = log.groupby(CASE, sort=False)[TIME].min().rename("arrival")
    eligible_ids = first.index[(first >= start) & (first < end)]
    cohort = log.loc[log[CASE].isin(eligible_ids)].copy()

    age_days = (as_of - first.loc[eligible_ids]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    mature_ids = age_days.index[age_days >= horizon_days]
    mature = cohort.loc[cohort[CASE].isin(mature_ids)].copy()

    metadata = {
        "case_ids": sorted(map(str, eligible_ids)),
        "n_arrivals": int(len(eligible_ids)),
        "n_mature": int(len(mature_ids)),
        "n_censored": int(len(eligible_ids) - len(mature_ids)),
        "horizon_days": horizon_days,
        "as_of": str(as_of),
    }
    return mature, metadata


def discover_artifacts(log: pd.DataFrame, *, skeleton: bool = False) -> dict[str, Any]:
    pm = pm_module()
    kwargs = {"case_id_key": CASE, "activity_key": ACTIVITY, "timestamp_key": TIME}
    dfg, starts, ends = pm.discover_dfg(log, **kwargs)
    n = log[CASE].nunique()
    out = {
        "n_cases": n,
        "dfg": dfg,
        "starts": starts,
        "ends": ends,
        "dfg_per_100_cases": {
            edge: 100 * count / n for edge, count in dfg.items()
        },
        "variants": pm.get_variants(log, **kwargs),
        "temporal_profile": pm.discover_temporal_profile(log, **kwargs),
    }
    if skeleton:
        out["log_skeleton"] = pm.discover_log_skeleton(
            log, noise_threshold=0.05, **kwargs
        )
    return out


def compare_monthly_views(
    df: pd.DataFrame,
    months: list[str],
    *,
    as_of: pd.Timestamp,
    calendar_timezone: str,
    horizon_days: float,
    source_timezone: str | None = None,
    sequence_key: str | None = None,
    duplicate_flag: str | None = None,
    skeleton: bool = False,
) -> dict[str, Any]:
    raw = prepare_events(
        df, source_timezone=source_timezone, sequence_key=sequence_key
    )
    normalized = normalized_copy(raw, duplicate_flag=duplicate_flag)

    result: dict[str, Any] = {"months": {}}
    for month in months:
        start = pd.Timestamp(f"{month}-01").tz_localize(calendar_timezone)
        end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
        pair = {}

        for name, log in (("raw", raw), ("normalized", normalized)):
            window, metadata = arrival_window(
                log,
                start,
                end,
                as_of=as_of,
                horizon_days=horizon_days,
            )
            pair[name] = {
                "cohort": metadata,
                "artifacts": discover_artifacts(window, skeleton=skeleton),
            }

        require(
            pair["raw"]["cohort"]["case_ids"]
            == pair["normalized"]["cohort"]["case_ids"],
            "Raw and normalized populations differ",
        )
        result["months"][month] = pair

    # Input df is untouched. Missing resources remain in the analysis.
    return result
```

The future pipeline also needs an agreed normative model before calculating event-level replay fitness. The model must represent the permitted information-request loop and clarify senior-assessment semantics; discovered skeleton regularities must not silently replace those business rules.

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest attribution is the March raw-fitness collapse to migration-related semantic and provenance changes: confidence is very high. Label replacement, technical duplication, increased missingness, substantial fitness recovery, and near-stable normalized adjacency agree. This does not establish that every business rule remained unchanged; it identifies the dominant explanation for this particular measurement discontinuity.

Supplier disruption is a high-confidence contributor to the May–summer deterioration, but confidence is only moderate that it increased rework within otherwise comparable Supplier Z cases. The disruption is documented, yet supplier exposure and complexity also change. Increased document-return traffic plausibly adds both external waiting and assessment demand. The aggregate series cannot divide those channels or distinguish them cleanly from population replacement.

Staffing loss is a moderately high-confidence amplifier of June–August waiting. Assessment service time changes modestly while reconstructed waiting increases much more, and deterioration persists while supplier share starts declining. Nevertheless, May worsening predates the departures, staffing capacity is not quantified, and inherited backlog can explain delayed responses. Priority-driven redistribution is plausible, not demonstrated: no class-specific waiting trajectories or dispatch decisions are supplied. October–November strongly support seasonal population/load drift, with moderate confidence that altered senior-routing or recording contributes beyond coarse complexity composition. December provides clear evidence of aggregate improvement, not an identified overtime or campaign effect: lower load and changing mix occur simultaneously.

Persistent behavioral adaptation remains a lower-confidence explanation for elevated December rework and senior assessment. Backlog, incomplete supplier recovery, finer case mix, and recording changes remain viable alternatives. Supplier-generated extra visits, reduced staffing, and seasonal demand can all mediate their effects through the same queue; assigning each the full waiting increase would double-count.

I would revise this account if normalized, intake-standardized mature cohorts showed stable within-stratum rework; corrected ready/start timestamps eliminated the queue increase; audited senior-assessment records showed unchanged execution but improved labeling; or season-matched historical cohorts reproduced the autumn pattern without excess residual delay.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** raw fitness falls while mapped fitness, canonical business relations, and validated timing remain stable, **THEN** investigate schema, label mapping, and technical-event provenance before redesigning the business process, **BECAUSE** the observed discontinuity is primarily consistent with measurement semantics rather than changed execution.

2. **IF** information-request incidence or loop intensity rises within comparable supplier and intake-complexity strata, **THEN** audit document defects and introduce targeted supplier-quality remediation while monitoring added assessment demand, **BECAUSE** within-stratum rework provides stronger evidence of changed document handling than an aggregate supplier-share shift alone.

3. **IF** validated ready-to-start waiting and backlog age rise while conditional service durations remain approximately stable, **THEN** assess staffed capacity, repeated-visit workload, and temporary capacity options, **BECAUSE** the dominant proximate problem is queue congestion rather than slower execution of each assessment.

4. **IF** standard-case waiting or maximum queue age deteriorates after the priority change while high-complexity waiting improves or deteriorates less, **THEN** audit eligible-case dispatch decisions and consider an approved aging or waiting-time safeguard, **BECAUSE** prioritization can redistribute delay and create starvation without materially changing within-case activity sequences.

5. **IF** aggregate DFGs and cycle times change but intake-standardized, season-matched patterns remain stable, **THEN** classify the signal primarily as population/load drift and adjust workload planning rather than infer a new routing rule, **BECAUSE** a different mixture of existing case types can change portfolio behavior and performance.

6. **IF** organizational conclusions vary materially between resource-complete records and analyses retaining missing-resource cases, **THEN** suspend individual productivity judgments and repair or reconcile resource lineage, **BECAUSE** non-random missingness can manufacture apparent staffing, handover, and workload differences.

7. **IF** canonical within-stratum rework, senior-routing, or excess waiting remains elevated after supplier quality, backlog, and seasonal load have demonstrably normalized—or a verified mandatory-control violation appears at any time—**THEN** investigate persistent operational change and review the process model and controls, **BECAUSE** persistence supports more than a temporary mixture effect, while a genuine mandatory violation warrants action regardless of aggregate recovery.
