6/10 — The response covers many of the requested dimensions: stage eligibility, scarce capacity, repeat-alert handling, uncertainty scenarios, spillovers, calibration differences, fairness, and monitoring. Its main weakness is that the proposed allocation and calculations are not consistently grounded in the case data. It recommends four interventions despite management’s preference to reserve one slot, and its segment caps and SME examples are internally inconsistent with the listed segments. Several monitoring proposals are also unsound: reducing the spillover assumption when observed spillover rises would move in the wrong direction, “alert persistence” is not a credible instrument, and pinball loss does not measure probability calibration. The response also gives conflicting spillover estimates and uses vague or malformed thresholds. A stronger evaluation would show a coherent portfolio comparison across the three scenarios, account for delays to untreated cases, and explain how calibration and intervention effects would be estimated without conflating treatment outcomes with baseline risk.
