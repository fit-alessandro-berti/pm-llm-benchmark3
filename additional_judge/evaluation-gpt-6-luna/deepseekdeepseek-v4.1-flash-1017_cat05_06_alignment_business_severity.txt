1. Materiality Framework

Severity reflects the business consequence of the behavior, not the alignment score. I use a 1–5 scale: 1 = harmless technical noise; 2 = limited process or evidence issue; 3 = material control or evidence gap needing timely review; 4 = serious control failure with meaningful exposure; 5 = critical failure with substantial or potentially irreversible harm. I consider the control objective, amount at risk, whether payment can be stopped or recovered, timing, customer or regulatory impact, recurrence, evidence reliability, and whether the case is in the model’s scope.

Confidence is how strongly the evidence supports the interpretation. High confidence means the logs and context clearly establish what happened; medium confidence reflects unresolved linkage, labeling, or scope; low confidence means key evidence is missing. A severe possible failure can have low confidence, and a high-confidence finding can still be low severity.

## 3. Repeated Deviation Patterns

- **Data-aware Auto Approve policy failures — 4 cases.** CL-609 and CL-610 are shown individually; the additional pattern evidence says four cases in the month had synchronous Auto Approve paths despite violating P1 or P2. Their zero alignment costs do not establish compliance. Check every such case against amount, risk, and recent bank-change data; correct routing rules and add data-aware controls to monitoring.

- **Notification retry duplicates — 74 cases.** CL-603 illustrates an extra notification event with the same message ID. All 74 are associated with one retrying connector. This points to a connector or event-ingestion issue, not 74 established cases of duplicate claimant contact. Check whether retries cause repeated delivery, preserve delivery receipts, and deduplicate events by message ID.

- **External-adjuster assessment evidence — 9 high-value cases.** CL-606 illustrates a missing core Assess Loss event despite an external report. Two of the nine lack a stable document-to-case identifier. Establish reliable linkage and verify the report’s timing and scope before treating these as missing assessments.

- **Denied and withdrawn cases outside the payable-claims model — represented by CL-607 and CL-612; most alignments with cost at least 4 are in these categories.** Large costs arise because the model expects payable-case steps after denial or withdrawal. Agree scope with process and model owners, then add explicit termination paths if these cases are in scope. Do not count expected non-payment as a control failure.

- **Notification evidence and linkage — CL-608.** The CRM/portal message may satisfy P4 within three hours, but a mutable text link does not provide strong retained evidence. Preserve the message and use a stable claim identifier. Do not infer a missed notification solely from the missing core event.

- **Label mapping — CL-611.** The discrepancy between `Assess Damage` and `Assess Loss` appears to be a naming mismatch supported by the procedure and UI. Confirm the equivalence and repair mapping history so future traces do not create false missing-activity findings.

- **Early payment and missing approval evidence — CL-604; missing fraud-screen evidence — CL-602.** These are distinct cases, but both involve high-impact control evidence. CL-604 establishes payment before recorded approval and has no recorded fraud screen. CL-602 has manual approval and payment but lacks fraud-screen evidence. Investigate each separately and preserve the original event sequence.

- **UI telemetry — CL-601.** Workbench and autosave events are explicitly identified as telemetry. Excluding or classifying them separately should prevent harmless technical events from inflating deviation counts.

## 4. Prioritized Remediation Plan

1. **Address the highest-risk control failures first.** Investigate CL-604’s €90,000 payment made before recorded approval, including whether funds are recoverable and whether Fraud Screen occurred. Investigate CL-602’s missing fraud-screen evidence for the €480,000 high-risk claim and establish whether the verbal check had any documented basis. Keep the findings distinct: one is confirmed early payment; the other is an absent control record with uncertainty about undocumented work.

2. **Close data-aware approval gaps.** Review the four synchronous Auto Approve cases that violate P1 or P2. For CL-610, the recent bank change triggers both P1 and P2 controls. For CL-609, the amount exceeds P1’s Auto Approve limit. Enforce the required routes using amount, risk, and bank-change data rather than relying on the control-flow model alone.

3. **Strengthen evidence and event integrity.** Establish stable claim-to-document and claim-to-notification identifiers. Verify the nine external-adjuster assessments, paying particular attention to the two without stable linkage. Preserve portal delivery evidence for CL-608, and confirm whether the 74 retry-pattern cases represent duplicate events or duplicate claimant messages.

4. **Correct model and mapping gaps.** Decide whether denied and withdrawn claims are in scope and represent their terminal paths accordingly. Confirm the `Assess Damage`/`Assess Loss` equivalence and maintain effective-dated mappings. Classify UI telemetry and connector retries so they do not masquerade as business activities.

5. **Monitor outcomes separately.** Track policy failures, event-label mismatches, missing evidence, model-scope mismatches, and harmless technical noise as separate measures. Do not use raw alignment cost as a proxy for financial exposure, customer harm, or confidence.
