**6/10**

The response addresses most of the requested policy dimensions: stage eligibility, scarce capacity, repeated alerts, uncertain effects, calibration differences, spillovers, fairness, and ongoing monitoring. It makes concrete decisions for individual cases and distinguishes optimistic, base, and adverse scenarios, so it is more operational than a simple probability threshold.

The main weakness is the decision arithmetic. The base-case example discounts estimated direct benefits without a clear method, assumes a €20,000 value for each spillover-affected case, and then applies an additional breach-conversion reduction. That makes the net-benefit result hard to audit and may double-count uncertainty. It also appears to recommend three active slots while reserving one, but does not clearly compare that portfolio against using fewer slots or identify the actual untreated diagnosis-stage cases bearing spillover.

Some policy details are too rigid for the evidence given: a four-hour cooldown, segment quota, and numerical monitoring triggers are proposed without showing why those values fit the case or portfolio. The counterfactual-label suggestion is also unsound as stated: the pre-intervention prediction is not a counterfactual outcome. A stronger answer would separate predictive calibration from causal effect estimation, state assumptions and ranges transparently, and show how the selection changes across scenarios.
