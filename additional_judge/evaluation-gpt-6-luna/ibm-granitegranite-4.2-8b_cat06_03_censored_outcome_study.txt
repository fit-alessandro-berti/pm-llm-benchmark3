The answer recognizes the central problem: activation selects who has an observed default outcome, and rejected, withdrawn, and open applications must not be labeled non-default. It also proposes relevant alternatives, temporal validation, and a substantial leakage audit.

However, the primary target and prediction time are inconsistent. It describes predicting for activated accounts using a prefix through Account Activated, while claiming this answers a request for predictions from the first few events of every application. That target is only useful at activation and cannot support early application screening. It also treats non-activated cases at points as competing outcomes that prevent activation, without clearly defining the estimand or separating that observed-policy outcome from counterfactual default risk.

The cohort accounting is not reliable: it fails to give a sound flow for all 48,000 cases and conflates immature outcomes, delayed feeds, and permanently missing feeds. The label-maturity logic is incorrect in the code: a known non-default may have `default_90d` missing, so requiring a nonmissing value misclassifies it; taking the minimum availability time and substituting extraction-plus-one for missing timestamps also does not establish label status. The event and case-level merges and feature construction contain indexing and alignment errors, and the code does not actually provide a valid, coherent implementation of the promised pipeline. Its PM4Py prefix demonstration is not clearly tied to a defined prediction landmark or a usable case-level feature table.

The validation and censoring discussion includes further errors, including suggesting that censored cases could be treated as non-default or assigned estimated outcomes for comparison. These defects undermine the requested statistical design despite several sound cautions.
