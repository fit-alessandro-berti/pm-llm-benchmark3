## 1. Consistency rules and evidence model

**Hard invariants**

- No new events or objects may be invented.
- Every proposed E2O endpoint must reference an existing event and object; every O2O endpoint must reference existing objects.
- Observed relations are retained unless directly contradicted; none are contradicted here.
- O2O qualifiers are directional. `DEL-X carries PK-7` does not imply `PK-7 carries DEL-X`.
- Do not add duplicate E2O or O2O tuples.

**Strong defaults**

- `Create Order` normally relates to one Order; `Add Item` to one Order and one Item.
- `Pack Item` normally relates to one Item and one Package, though an item-only row may reflect a scanner outage.
- `Dispatch Package` normally relates to one Package and one Delivery, though bulk dispatch is possible.
- `Confirm Delivery` may relate to one Delivery, several Deliveries, or only a customer or Order if the carrier identifier is lost.
- `Issue Invoice` relates to one Invoice and one or more Orders.
- `Record Payment` relates to one Payment and normally one Invoice, but unapplied cash is possible.
- Lifecycle order is usually Create Order, Add Item, Pack Item, Dispatch Package, Confirm Delivery. Invoice and Payment usually follow order creation.
- An exact or near-exact identifier match is strong evidence. Existing O2O structure supports an event’s coherence, but it is not independent proof if inferred from the same event.

**Weak heuristics**

- Wildcard or masked identifiers, such as `N-42*`, are suggestive but inconclusive.
- A shared route (`NORTH`), customer (`C17`), or nearby timestamp is supportive but weak.
- Amount equality or complementarity is corroborating, not proof: fees, discounts, taxes, partial payments, and unapplied cash are possible.
- Gross weight is not useful here because item weights are absent.

**Evidence against a repair**

- `ev08` is item-only. `PK8-2` is suggestive, but scanner outages can produce such rows.
- `ev10` has `external_ref=N-421?`; its match to `DEL-Y` is strong but imperfect.
- `ev11` has `N-42*` and is observed only with `ORD-A`. Assigning it to `DEL-Y` may imply delivery of `ORD-B` content without direct evidence.
- `INV-44` is observed only with `ORD-A`. Its total of 230 equals the two declared values, but that does not prove `ORD-B` is billed.
- `PAY-2` is observed only with itself. Its amount complements `PAY-1`, but it could be unapplied cash.

A candidate relation should pass the hard invariants and have a strong identifier, lifecycle, or cardinality signal before being added. Candidates supported only by weak heuristics remain unresolved. Inferred O2O edges are not independent confirmation of the E2O relations that motivated them.

## 3. Alternative repair bundles

**Bundle A: conservative/minimum-change repair**

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Leave unresolved. Do not add `ev08 → PK-8` or `PK-8 packs IT-B1`. The item-only row may reflect a scanner outage; adding the relations would imply cross-order packing without direct O2O evidence.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Add `ev10 → DEL-Y` provisionally and `DEL-Y carries PK-8` provisionally. Reject `ev10 → DEL-X`: `N-421?` is a much closer match to DEL-Y’s `N-421` than to DEL-X’s `N-420`.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** Leave unresolved. Do not assign it to DEL-X, DEL-Y, or both. The wildcard and existing ORD-A relation do not settle whether one or several deliveries are involved.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Leave unresolved. Do not add `ev12 → ORD-B` or `INV-44 bills ORD-B`. The amount match supports this possibility but does not establish it.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Leave unresolved. Do not add `ev14 → INV-44` or `PAY-2 settles INV-44`; unapplied cash remains plausible.

This bundle gives DEL-Y a plausible dispatch lifecycle while preserving incomplete states elsewhere. ORD-A has order, item, packing, dispatch, invoice, and payment evidence, but no resolved delivery confirmation. ORD-B has creation and item-addition evidence, while its packing, billing, and payment connections remain incomplete or uncertain.

**Bundle B: plausible complete-customer interpretation**

- **ev08 and PK-8:** Add `ev08 → PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`, treating PK-8 as consolidated packaging for IT-A2 and IT-B1.
- **ev10 and DEL-X/DEL-Y:** Add `ev10 → DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`.
- **ev11 and the delivery objects:** Add `ev11 → DEL-X` and `ev11 → DEL-Y`. This treats the event as a multi-delivery confirmation, consistent with the wildcard and the rule allowing joint acknowledgments. It does not uniquely assign ev11.
- **INV-44 and ORD-B:** Add `ev12 → ORD-B` and `INV-44 bills ORD-B`, treating the invoice as covering both C17 orders.
- **PAY-2 and INV-44:** Add `ev14 → INV-44` and `PAY-2 settles INV-44`, treating the two payments as settling the invoice.

The resulting lifecycle is coherent: both orders are created, their items are packed, packages are dispatched and jointly confirmed, one invoice covers both orders, and two payments settle it. Its weaker points are the cross-order package and the financial links, which rely mainly on identifier or amount consistency rather than explicit references.

**Bundle C: diagnostic single-delivery interpretation**

- Add `ev08 → PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1`; add `ev10 → DEL-Y` and `DEL-Y carries PK-8`.
- Assign `ev11` only to DEL-X, since it is associated with ORD-A, whose item is carried by DEL-X. This is a diagnostic single-delivery choice, not a strong conclusion: the wildcard leaves DEL-Y and a two-delivery acknowledgment plausible.
- Leave the invoice-to-ORD-B and PAY-2-to-INV-44 candidates unresolved.

This gives ORD-A a confirmed-delivery path while leaving ORD-B’s delivery status unresolved. It is less complete than Bundle B, but it avoids inferring that ev11 confirms delivery of ORD-B content.

## 5. Lifecycle and cardinality audit

**Order:** ORD-A has a plausible but incomplete lifecycle in Bundle A: creation, item additions, packing through its items, dispatch through PK-7, and invoicing. Delivery confirmation remains unresolved, and PAY-1 is its only observed invoice payment. ORD-B has creation and item-addition evidence but no resolved packing or billing connection. There is no suspicious one-to-many relation in Bundle A. The observed `contains` edges are supported by corresponding item-addition E2O relations; `INV-44 bills ORD-A` aligns with ev12’s ORD-A relation.

**Item:** IT-A1 and IT-A2 have add, pack, and dispatch paths through their packages. IT-B1 has an add event and an item-only pack event, so its pack lifecycle is incomplete rather than orphaned. The observed package membership for IT-A1 and IT-A2 aligns with their pack events. In Bundle B, the added PK-8-to-IT-B1 O2O edge and ev08-to-PK-8 E2O edge support the same hypothesis, not two independent pieces of evidence.

**Package:** PK-7 has a plausible pack-to-dispatch lifecycle. PK-8 has one observed pack event and a dispatch event under Bundle A. Bundle B gives it a second item and creates a cross-order package, which is possible but requires business context. DEL-X’s observed `carries PK-7` relation aligns with ev09. DEL-Y’s added `carries PK-8` edge follows the proposed ev10 relation and is not independent confirmation.

**Delivery:** DEL-X has a dispatch event; DEL-Y gains one in Bundle A. Neither is confirmed in the selected bundle. There are no orphan Delivery objects. The added DEL-Y link is plausible given the carrier reference, but its O2O and E2O support share the same dispatch hypothesis.

**Invoice:** INV-44 has an issue event, an observed bill-to-ORD-A edge, and a payment relation to PAY-1. This is a plausible partial lifecycle. Bundle B’s additional ORD-B billing edge is allowed by the multi-order invoice default, but amount matching and the proposed event relation are not independent confirmation.

**Payment:** PAY-1 has a record event and an observed settlement edge to INV-44. PAY-2 has a record event but remains unapplied in Bundle A, which is allowed. Bundle B’s E2O and O2O additions for PAY-2 would both stem from the same amount-complement hypothesis.

No event is truly orphaned in Bundle A: ev08, ev11, and ev14 each already have at least one observed object relation. They are incomplete relative to typical event cardinality, not disconnected. No object is orphaned either. The key circularity risk is treating an inferred edge as corroboration: for example, assuming ev10 relates to DEL-Y, adding `DEL-Y carries PK-8`, and then counting that new O2O edge as independent support for ev10’s DEL-Y relation.

## 6. Downstream process-mining effects

**Unrepaired log:** Direct object-linked fragments include ORD-A’s Create Order, Add Item, Confirm Delivery, and Issue Invoice events; ORD-B’s Create Order and Add Item events; and the observed pack, dispatch, invoice, and payment events on their directly related objects. DEL-X has Dispatch Package only; DEL-Y has no event. The ET–OT graph lacks a Dispatch Package–Delivery link for ev10 and any Confirm Delivery–Delivery link. The OTG has a Delivery–Package edge only for DEL-X and PK-7. A Delivery flattening based on direct E2O relations has DEL-X but not DEL-Y.

**Bundle A:** Adding ev10-to-DEL-Y gives DEL-Y a Dispatch Package event; it does not add a new event or a direct-follow edge on DEL-Y. The ET–OT graph gains Dispatch Package–Delivery, and the OTG gains DEL-Y–PK-8. The Delivery flattening gains DEL-Y with its dispatch event. Direct Order, Package, Invoice, and Payment flattenings gain no new event associations from these additions. There are still 14 events and 12 objects; the E2O link count rises from 22 to 23, and the O2O count from 8 to 9. Event-based activity counts do not change, while the number of Delivery objects associated with Dispatch Package rises from one to two.

**Bundle B:** In addition to Bundle A, ev08-to-PK-8 gives IT-B1 a Pack Item event and gives PK-8 a second Pack Item event before dispatch. The OC-DFG may therefore show Add Item → Pack Item for IT-B1 and Pack Item → Pack Item → Dispatch Package for PK-8. Assigning ev11 to both deliveries gives each a Dispatch Package → Confirm Delivery fragment. Adding ev12-to-ORD-B gives ORD-B an Issue Invoice event; adding ev14-to-INV-44 gives INV-44 a second Record Payment event. The ET–OT graph gains Pack Item–Package, Confirm Delivery–Delivery, and Record Payment–Invoice associations. The OTG gains PK-8–IT-B1, INV-44–ORD-B, and PAY-2–INV-44 edges.

In direct-object flattenings, ORD-B gains Issue Invoice, PK-8 gains a second Pack Item, DEL-X and DEL-Y gain Confirm Delivery, and INV-44 gains a second Record Payment. Event count remains 14, while total E2O and O2O links rise to 28 and 12. Unique objects associated with Confirm Delivery rise from zero to two; unique items associated with a Pack Item event on PK-8 rise from one to two. The total object count remains 12. Bundle B changes object-based activity frequencies and may make the two Orders appear more similar in equivalent-execution clustering, especially if O2O paths are considered. Direct E2O-only clustering still shows differences because ORD-B has no directly related delivery event.

Counts depend on whether a metric counts events, unique objects per activity, or the total object population. These repairs do not change event counts or total objects; they change which objects and object types are associated with activities. Flattening and clustering results also depend on whether the implementation follows only direct E2O links or incorporates O2O paths.

## 7. PM4Py-oriented validation outline

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

# Preserve the original tables.
events_orig = events.copy()
objects_orig = objects.copy()
relations_orig = relations.copy()
o2o_orig = o2o.copy()

# Bundle A additions.
e2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {
        "ocel:eid": "ev10",
        "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",
        "ocel:qualifier": "related_to",
    }
])
o2o_add = pd.DataFrame([
    {
        "ocel:oid": "DEL-Y",
        "ocel:oid_2": "PK-8",
        "ocel:qualifier": "carries",
    }
])

relations = pd.concat([relations_orig, e2o_add], ignore_index=True)
o2o = pd.concat([o2o_orig, o2o_add], ignore_index=True)

# Referential integrity.
if (~relations["ocel:eid"].isin(events["ocel:eid"])).any():
    raise ValueError("E2O references unknown event IDs")
if (~relations["ocel:oid"].isin(objects["ocel:oid"])).any():
    raise ValueError("E2O references unknown object IDs")
if (~o2o["ocel:oid"].isin(objects["ocel:oid"])).any():
    raise ValueError("O2O references unknown source object IDs")
if (~o2o["ocel:oid_2"].isin(objects["ocel:oid"])).any():
    raise ValueError("O2O references unknown target object IDs")

# Report duplicates before removing accidental duplicate rows.
dup_e2o = relations.duplicated(
    subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"], keep=False
)
dup_o2o = o2o.duplicated(
    subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"], keep=False
)
if dup_e2o.any():
    print("Duplicate E2O rows:\n", relations[dup_e2o])
if dup_o2o.any():
    print("Duplicate O2O rows:\n", o2o[dup_o2o])

relations = relations.drop_duplicates(
    subset=["ocel:eid", "ocel:oid", "ocel:qualifier"]
)
o2o = o2o.drop_duplicates(
    subset=["ocel:oid", "ocel:oid_2", "ocel:qualifier"]
)

# VERSION-DEPENDENT: the OCEL constructor and accepted column schemas
# vary by PM4Py version; adapt this line to the installed version.
ocel = pm4py.OCEL(
    events=events,
    objects=objects,
    relations=relations,
    o2o=o2o,
)

ocdfg = pm4py.discover_ocdfg(ocel)
otg = pm4py.discover_otg(ocel)
etot = pm4py.discover_etot(ocel)

order_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Order")
delivery_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Delivery")

# Optional additional views.
package_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Package")
invoice_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Invoice")
payment_flat = pm4py.ocel_flattening(ocel, object_type="Payment")

# O2O enrichment may help explore a view, but it does not establish
# the business meaning of any inferred relation.
```

## 8. Over-repair warnings

1. Adding `ev08 → PK-8` and `PK-8 packs IT-B1` may create a false cross-order package and imply consolidated shipping.
2. Assigning ev11 uniquely to DEL-X or DEL-Y may create a false confirmation and hide a plausible multi-delivery acknowledgment.
3. Adding `INV-44 bills ORD-B` solely from the 230 total may create a false invoice scope and alter ORD-B’s billing lifecycle.
4. Adding `PAY-2 settles INV-44` may mask legitimate unapplied cash and make the invoice appear fully settled.
5. Linking ev10 to DEL-X or making DEL-Y carry PK-7 may duplicate or misattribute dispatch behavior.
6. Adding `ev11 → ORD-B` directly may create a Confirm Delivery event on ORD-B without direct evidence.
