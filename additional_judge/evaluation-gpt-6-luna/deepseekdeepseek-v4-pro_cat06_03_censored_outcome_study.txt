5/10 — The response follows the requested nine-section structure and recognizes the central estimand problem: default is defined only after activation, so rejected and other nonactivated applications cannot simply be labeled non-default. It also discusses censoring, delayed labels, transfer, migration, feature leakage, temporal validation, and deployment limits.

The cohort accounting is materially inconsistent. The listed snapshot categories sum to 48,000, but the response’s flow table treats 3,600 immature activated accounts as additional cases, yielding 51,600. It also assigns transfer and open cases categories without consistently separating competing outcomes from censoring or selective observation.

The implementation skeleton has more serious issues. It appears to set one cutoff for all cases instead of constructing prediction-time prefixes per case, and it uses case-level labels and maturity information in ways that risk outcome leakage. Its PM4Py prefix example operates on already-truncated data but does not clearly enforce the requested elapsed-time prefix semantics or reliably connect extracted prefixes to their case-level targets. The code also makes unsupported assumptions about event ordering and case identifiers despite transfers potentially reappearing under new IDs.

Overall, the response has a sound conceptual direction but does not deliver a coherent, auditable cohort flow or a sufficiently reliable implementation for the stated data risks.
