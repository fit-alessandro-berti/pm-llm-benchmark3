7/10 — The response gives a clear, evidence-based distinction between useful adaptations and compliance risks, and it proposes concrete recovery controls. It correctly treats hospital prioritization, trained manual authorization, temporary release checks, and backlog reconciliation as potentially resilient when governed and documented, while flagging skipped authorization, stale inventory snapshots, unscanned dispatch, and phone-based delivery proof as risks.

It also uses the phase metrics to show that operational recovery lagged behind system recovery: cycle times and exception rates improved by stabilization but remained above baseline, and the 2,300-case audit-remediation queue signals unresolved control debt.

The main weakness is that some recommendations overstate what the data supports. It proposes fixed cancellation thresholds and targets such as a 95% hospital fill rate or “<1% hidden compliance gaps” without grounding them in the supplied evidence or explaining trade-offs. It also mislabels pharmacy fill rate as 100% at baseline and 95% at stabilization, though those figures are not provided. The answer would be stronger if it tied control requirements to product risk, distinguished documented exceptions from actual control failures, and defined auditable reconciliation exit criteria.
