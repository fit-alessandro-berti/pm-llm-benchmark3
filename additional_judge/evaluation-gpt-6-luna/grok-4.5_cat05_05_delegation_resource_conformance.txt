The answer follows the required seven headings, covers all ten cases in the requested matrix, and gives a substantial implementation sketch with alias normalization, service-account attribution, event-time role checks, delegation intervals, and network indicators. It also handles the central uncertainty well: repeated collaboration is not proof of collusion, and leave or account use alone does not establish who acted.

Several material errors and inconsistencies lower the score:

- The answer’s RR4 deadline rule conflicts with its CHG-505 finding. For an Aug 11 emergency approval, two business days after the approval date are Aug 12 and Aug 13; a review on Aug 14 is late. Yet the code’s `business_deadline` calculation uses `start_ts.normalize() + 1 day`, which reaches Aug 14 as the second business day. The code therefore misclassifies the deadline.
- D-QM-01 requires Asha’s absence. The answer treats recorded annual leave as sufficient for CHG-501, but does not carefully distinguish “on leave” from being absent or formally unavailable under the delegation’s condition. It should state the assumption or explain what evidence confirms that condition.
- CHG-510 is not simply a case of Clara relying on an invalid IC subdelegation: Clara also drafted and approved the same case, a direct RR1 breach. The conclusion mentions both, but the case analysis should make the independent SoD failure explicit and separate from the authority failure.
- The code does not actually use `pm4py.filter_four_eyes_principle` for both required pairs. It calls it only for draft/approve; implement/verify is checked with custom pandas logic. Also, PM4Py’s filtered result is not reliably interpreted as the violating cases, despite a comment acknowledging version-dependent semantics.
- The code’s service-account logic is internally inconsistent: CHG-509 is attributed to Eva in `attr_human`, but later authority evaluation reads `org:resource` (`svc_qms`), so it cannot recognize Eva’s role for that verification. CHG-503’s weak attribution is represented as no human, which is defensible, but the distinction needs to be handled consistently.
- The matrix and findings sometimes overstate certainty, such as treating CHG-501’s DB lag as merely documentary noise and assigning high confidence without resolving the absence condition.

These are substantive because the requested evaluation centers on precise event-time authority, emergency review timing, and executable PM4Py/pandas checks. The answer is broad and often careful, but the contradictions and implementation gaps prevent a higher grade.
