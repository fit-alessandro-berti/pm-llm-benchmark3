The response follows the required six-section structure, gives a case-by-case allocation, and addresses stage eligibility, repeated alerts, calibration uncertainty, queue spillovers, fairness, and monitoring. Its strongest feature is treating the decision as a portfolio problem, including effects on untreated cases and a plan to estimate both direct effects and spillovers.

The main weakness is that the selected portfolio’s impact table appears to give insufficient weight to spillover harm. It selects three cases and estimates 18–54 additional breaches among untreated cases, yet the adverse interpretation still describes net harm as possible rather than clearly showing that the estimated spillover can overwhelm direct benefit. The impact arithmetic would be more auditable if it tied those breach counts to an explicit value or stated why a monetary estimate is not supportable. The decision rule and priority tiers could also state more clearly how urgency and non-monetary obligations alter the ranking when value-based estimates conflict.

Overall, it is operationally thoughtful and appropriately cautious about causal claims, but its quantitative portfolio comparison does not fully resolve the tradeoff its own assumptions reveal.
