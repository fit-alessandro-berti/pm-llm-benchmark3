The answer recognizes the central selection problem: default is observed only after activation, so rejected and withdrawn applications must not be labeled non-default. It also flags major leakage risks and proposes temporal validation around the policy change and migration.

However, the design has serious inconsistencies. It calls the target conditional on activation but predicts at a point before activation, and the cohort flow does not reconcile the stated 21,600 labeled cases with the stricter 125-day maturity rule. Its proposed survival sensitivity is not actually specified, while treating transfers as excluded misses the distinction between competing outcomes and missing follow-up.

The implementation is not reliable enough to support the design: prediction timestamps are assigned with a misaligned groupby operation, the prefix filter compares event timestamps to a per-row field that is not correctly constructed, and it does not demonstrate the required `pm4py.get_prefixes_from_log` API. The maturity check also substitutes a fixed 35-day delay for observed label availability, and the leakage audit assertion checks columns that remain present in the data pipeline. These gaps substantially weaken the otherwise sound high-level cautions.
