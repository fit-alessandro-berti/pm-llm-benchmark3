The last group records event-object relations. The event links are direct observations as stated; connections from those events to a job’s waiting or delay are interpretations, not explicit wait records.

Three higher-order motifs show how the jobs can be coupled:

- **Batch–machine–tool–jobs–components:** B1’s single cure involves OVEN-1, RACK-7, three jobs, and their components. A synchronization rule can make J1 and J2 wait for J3.
- **Lot–components–jobs–batch:** L7 links C2/J2 and C5/J5. Its quarantine/release events explicitly touch both jobs, while J2’s override complicates whether the lot gate was actually enforced.
- **Shipment–jobs–batch–machine:** S1 links J1 and J4. J1 is packed much earlier, but its departure is tied to J4’s later completion.
- **Tool–machine–jobs:** J3’s FIX-A hold overlaps J4’s arrival at CNC-2; the later J4 process event also relates to FIX-A. A wait by J4 for the tool is plausible but not directly recorded.

The remaining 15 minutes are after J4 is packed.

If CAL-3 was required, unavailable during recalibration, and J6 could have started at 16:05 absent J5’s test and calibration, the 45-minute overlap is plausibly tool-associated; the other 35 minutes are not explained by this overlap alone.
- **B2 and RACK-7:** OVEN-1 is idle from 12:55 to 14:50 = **1 h 55 min**, while RACK-7 is marked cleaning/quarantine until 14:35. If the rack was necessary and the state accurate, up to **1 h 40 min** of the idle interval coincides with the recorded rack restriction. ## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job:** Shows each job’s activity path and elapsed times. It can hide shared causes, omit events not directly linked to the job (as with L7), or duplicate a shared batch cure across each member’s trace. A normal-looking per-job duration distribution can miss long waits concentrated at shared joins. Its DFG medians do not identify which partner or object delayed a job.
- **Machine:** Shows machine events, utilization, and gaps. It can hide tool holds, batch eligibility, shipment policy, and whether idle time is planned. A gap on OVEN-1 or TEST-1 is not evidence that machine capacity caused a delay.
- **Batch:** Shows the batch membership and common cure, making synchronization visible. It can hide member-specific ready times, tool states, and later shipment effects; a single batch event may be duplicated when flattened by member. A low count of batch–machine interactions says little about the slack imposed on members.
- **Tool:** Shows acquire/release or maintenance intervals and shared claims. It may lack explicit waiting requests, making inferred contention ambiguous; flattening by tool can duplicate a shared hold across jobs. A tool’s busy duration alone cannot establish that it was the critical blocker.
- **ComponentLot:** Shows quarantine and release across components and jobs. It can obscure which job actually obeyed the gate, especially with J2’s override, and inconsistent links can make the lot appear disconnected from jobs. A job DFG that omits lot events can falsely suggest uninterrupted progress.
- **Shipment:** Shows shipment-level creation, loading, and dispatch. It can hide the time an already packed member waits for later shipment partners. The Create→Load→Dispatch median is not a measure of a job’s packed-to-dispatch wait.

Low machine utilization can coexist with long job waiting because a job may wait for a batch partner, a shared tool, a lot release, or a shipment partner while the machine is free. Resource feasibility at the machine does not guarantee that policy prerequisites or other objects are ready.

## 5. Object-lifecycle evidence

**B1 morning batch**

- Temporal precedence: J1 and J2 are marked ready at 08:10 and 08:25; J3 at 11:55; the shared cure starts at 12:05.
- Resource feasibility: The cure event names OVEN-1 and RACK-7, but the data do not establish their availability throughout the morning.
- Policy constraint: The timing is consistent with a batch synchronization barrier. The supplied evidence does not state whether all members were formally required before start.
- Causal speculation: If all-member readiness controlled start, J1 and J2 waited for the batch; this does not prove J3 caused the wait. The jobs’ components and batch share the cure, while S1/S2 later link these jobs into shipment groups.

**FIX-A and J4**

- Temporal precedence: J3 acquires FIX-A at 09:00; J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; J3 releases the tool at 11:40; J4 starts at 11:45.
- Resource feasibility: CNC-2 is idle for part of this period. J4’s process event relates to FIX-A, suggesting a tool requirement, but no reservation or wait event establishes when J4 requested it.
- Policy constraint: No explicit tool-queue or allocation rule is given.
- Causal speculation: FIX-A may have prevented J4 from using idle CNC-2. J4’s batch membership and S1 consolidation provide potential downstream propagation paths, but the data do not show that the tool delay changed B2’s or S1’s schedule.

**L7 quarantine and J5**

- Temporal precedence: L7 is quarantined at 10:20 and released at 13:50; J5 proceeds only after release. J2 has already entered B1 by supervisor override.
- Resource feasibility: The lot events name L7, C2, C5, J2, and J5, not a machine.
- Policy constraint: The quarantine appears to be a material gate for J5; J2’s override indicates at least one exception.
- Causal speculation: A release gate plausibly delayed J5, which is in B2 and S3. This could affect B2 or S3 timing, but the evidence does not establish that it did. The events’ omission from the job extract is an integration artifact that can distort attribution.

**CAL-3 and J6**

- Temporal precedence: J5 tests 16:10–16:35; CAL-3 recalibrates 16:35–17:20; J6 tests 17:25–17:50 after being ready at 16:05.
- Resource feasibility: TEST-1 is otherwise idle from 16:35 to 17:25; every final test also requires CAL-3.
- Policy constraint: Calibration makes the tool unavailable during the recorded interval, but no queue or calibration trigger is supplied.
- Causal speculation: CAL-3 plausibly contributes to J6’s wait even while TEST-1 is idle. J5 and J6 share S3, creating a possible downstream shipment coupling not quantified here.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

The batch-synchronization hypothesis would weaken if OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was unavailable until near 12:05; if readiness only meant staging and another prerequisite completed around then; if J3’s readiness timestamp was backfilled; or if the batch could start earlier and the timing followed a shift, operator, or other schedule. It would also weaken if similar batches generally start soon after the first member is ready.

The tool hypothesis would weaken if J4 did not request FIX-A until shortly before 11:45, or if another prerequisite determined its start. The CAL-3 hypothesis would weaken if J6 was not actually eligible at 16:05 or the recalibration was not blocking access to the tool. Shipment consolidation would weaken if S1’s dispatch time followed a fixed route cutoff independent of J4. The lot hypothesis would weaken if J5 had another blocking prerequisite or if the lot events were incorrectly related to its components.

Useful additional data include explicit wait and reservation events with blocker object IDs; consistent lot-event links to jobs and components; override actor, time, and waived rule; machine and tool state intervals; readiness checklists; batch partial-load rules and recipe constraints; calibration reason and schedule; shift/operator calendars; and shipment cutoff, route, customer, and hold-for-partner attributes. These would help distinguish observed temporal overlap from resource feasibility, policy constraints, and causal effect.
