## 3. Causal diagnosis and interaction analysis

The June–July conformance collapse is best explained by several interacting data and semantic changes. The label replacement explains an initial portion of the alignment drop; removing `Queue Routed` and restoring source-sequence order each produce further gains. These are not independent causes: a changed label, added event, and changed timestamp source can all affect how the same trace aligns. The reported stepwise gains support their importance but do not establish that every preprocessing choice is valid.

The evidence is weaker for a broad real-process deterioration. Stable approval, appeal, and cycle-time summaries weigh against a large operational shift, but these aggregates cannot rule out control violations, and incident detection is incomplete. The no-check enhanced-review variants require a case-level audit before classifying them as bypasses or missing events.

August–September adds a separate governance mismatch: a new sign-off policy is absent from the normative model. The candidate model’s improved fit supports that explanation, but an optional step cannot enforce a threshold-based requirement. High-value status must be joined to the event log to assess compliance. `Manual Override` remains ambiguous: it may record a legitimate correction, a changed process action, or both.

## 5. Process-mining interpretation and metric critique

The alignment summaries are diagnostic evidence, not direct estimates of compliance. A fitting trace percentage and mean alignment cost depend on activity mapping, event inclusion, ordering, model structure, and any cost function used. The sharp increases after label mapping, event removal, and sequence ordering show that the raw June–July comparison conflates process behavior with representation and extraction changes. The supplied percentages do not include uncertainty or enough method detail to independently reproduce the results.

A directly-follows graph summarizes adjacent observed events but cannot establish causality, requiredness, or policy compliance. For example, `Notify -> Decision` is a useful ordering signal, not proof that notification truly preceded the decision; source evidence suggests some inversions are artifacts. Likewise, `Compliance Check -> Supervisor Sign-off` does not show whether sign-off was required for those cases.

The normative tree is control-flow only. It cannot express the EUR 50,000 condition or resource authorization. Missing resources and omitted high-value attributes limit conclusions about who acted and whether the policy applied. Comparisons should preserve event-time and ingestion-time provenance, source sequence, model version, case attributes, and transformation history, and report how filtering or relabeling changes the metrics.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

Version the normative model with its effective dates and tie each conformance run to its model, event schema, mappings, timestamp source, and filtering rules. Preserve raw event labels, event time, ingestion time, source sequence, and transformation history.

Require release validation when UI, gateway, ETL, or policy changes occur. Validate label mappings and technical-event treatment against sampled source cases before using them in conformance reporting. For attribute-dependent requirements such as the EUR 50,000 sign-off rule, retain case attributes and encode the condition explicitly; report control-flow fit separately from policy and resource-authorization checks. Track unresolved or missing events as data-quality findings until independent evidence establishes whether they represent behavior or capture failures.
