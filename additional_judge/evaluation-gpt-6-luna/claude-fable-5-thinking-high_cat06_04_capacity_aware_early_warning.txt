## 1. Decision rule

Use a two-stage policy: first determine whether a case is actionable, then rank actionable cases by conservative net benefit.

A case is eligible only before `Diagnosis Complete`, with enough time to complete the 45-minute swarm and act on its findings. Require either a first alert supported by valid data or a material event or stage change that justifies renewed review. Do not escalate repeated refresh alerts on their own.

Correct probabilities before ranking: add 0.04–0.09 for SME cases and subtract 0.05–0.12 for Strategic Enterprise cases, while carrying the full interval into uncertainty estimates. For Public Sector, use the unadjusted estimate with a moderate-sample uncertainty flag. For Non-Native-Language Support, widen uncertainty and require human review. If a key live feature is imputed, verify it before acting on the resulting increase.

Rank cases by conservative estimated absolute risk reduction × adjusted breach probability × breach value, less swarm cost, false-alarm burden, and expected spillover harm. Use the lower end of the effect range for the conservative ranking; also examine midpoint and upper-end results. Reserve one of four slots for the possible regulatory incident, subject to a same-day review if no such case arrives.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Apply a two-hour cool-down after a decision. Log subsequent alerts, but suppress those caused only by refreshes when there is no new event, stage transition, or material probability change. Keep cases on a watchlist.

Re-enter a case for review after a material new event, a verified probability increase of at least 0.10, a stage transition, or entry into the last realistic intervention window. Do not swarm the same case again unless a material new event occurred and a senior reviewer approves.

**I-05** has repeated alerts without a new event and only a 0.02 probability change, so suppress it. **I-07** has a new customer log, so review it despite the slightly lower probability; on the supplied value and time window, choose an expedited log review rather than a swarm.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

Value-weighted allocation can starve lower-value cases, including SME and Public Sector cases. Set aside a small share of capacity for risk-based selection independent of euro value, and review intervention rates, breaches, and resolution times by segment. Avoid hard segment quotas that could misallocate scarce capacity; investigate sustained disparities and adjust the policy based on evidence.

Sparse Non-Native-Language Support data could lead to systematic under-service. Require human review, report uncertainty explicitly, and include these cases in effect estimation without assuming their effects are zero.

Apply and periodically re-estimate segment-specific calibration adjustments. Report calibration and alert outcomes by segment, stage, and data-quality status so that offsets do not mask drift or feature failures.

Intervention changes observed outcomes, so treated cases cannot be used as if untreated labels. Preserve treatment and timing records, use pre-intervention prefixes for prediction training, and estimate effects with designs that account for treatment assignment.

Queue spillovers may fall disproportionately on untreated lower-value groups. Track delays and breach outcomes among untreated cases sharing the queue, broken down by segment; include this harm in the allocation decision and lower the slot cap if it becomes material.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

Track slot use, swarm duration, reserve decisions, alert volume, suppression, overrides, and queue delays daily. Monitor predictive calibration and feature availability by segment and stage, with particular checks for the SME and Strategic Enterprise adjustments and imputed queue features.

Estimate intervention effects using randomized assignment among similarly ranked eligible cases when capacity permits, or another credible causal design that accounts for why cases were selected. Stratify estimates by stage and segment, including Non-Native-Language Support, and update effect ranges only when evidence supports doing so. Compare untreated diagnosis-stage cases on swarm-heavy and swarm-light days to estimate spillovers.

Review intervention rates, breach rates, resolution times, and spillover exposure by segment on a regular schedule. Keep treatment flags in training data and evaluate treated and untreated outcomes separately. Revisit thresholds and capacity limits when effects, calibration, spillovers, or fairness measures materially depart from assumptions.
