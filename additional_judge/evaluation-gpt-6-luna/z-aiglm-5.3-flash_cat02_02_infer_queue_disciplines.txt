# 1. Queue-by-queue diagnosis

**Working assumptions**

- **A1 — Appointment semantics:** The appointment field gives a promised start time; its tolerance is unknown. An exact start and a start within a small window are both possible.
- **A2 — Estimates:** `est_min` is the estimate visible to dispatch at arrival, as stated. In these records, each job’s service-segment durations sum exactly to `est_min`. That supports comparisons using the published estimates, but does not establish that future estimates will be accurate.
- **A3 — Scan delay:** A recorded arrival may be up to two minutes late. For two jobs with a recorded gap \(g\), their actual arrival gap could be from \(g-2\) to \(g+2\) minutes. This can affect close pairs, but does not change the decisive comparisons below.
- **A4 — Starts after resource release:** A short start lag may be dispatch latency. I treat the 24-minute D-F idle period as meaningful; the records do not establish why it occurred.
- **A5 — Eligibility:** Skills and resource availability are as listed. Hidden readiness constraints remain possible.
- **A6 — Interruption reasons:** Recorded reasons are useful evidence, but may not capture the full operational context.

# 3. Supporting and contradicting cases

- **QA:** A03 supports appointment insertion: it starts at 08:20 ahead of older A02 and shorter, higher-priority A04. A02 starting next supports FIFO. A05 starting at 09:05 ahead of priority-1 A08 challenges priority scheduling; A08 then waits until 09:41. This is consistent with FIFO and needs no special exception under that diagnosis. A06’s appointment is missed by five minutes, so A03 alone does not establish a hard reservation rule. A04’s 08:58 start is non-discriminating because FIFO, priority, and SPT all predict it. A one-off accommodation for A03 remains plausible; the later exact-time start of D06 in QD provides some cross-queue support for appointment handling, but not proof of a common policy.

- **QB:** B02 supports preemptive priority: B01 is stopped for a new priority-1 job, and the recorded reason says “urgent arrival.” B01’s resumptions at 09:31 and 10:14 support a separate resume-first rule. B05’s 10:00 start supports priority over older B04 and interrupted B01. However, B03 runs before B05 at 09:48, and B04 beats priority-2 B06 at 10:28. The first needs an exception to strict priority—possible late readiness, a late priority flag, or a secondary SPT rule. The second is a direct contradiction to uniform priority and supports FIFO among fresh jobs. B07’s appointment is substantially late; this fits weak or advisory appointment semantics.

- **QC:** C02, C03, C04, and C06 support SPT. At 10:18, C03 is chosen over priority-1 C05 despite C05’s deadline; at 10:32, C04 is again the shortest choice. C05’s 10:54 selection could be an escalation override: priority and deadline rules both point to C05, although the deadline was already past. At 11:40, C01 beats C07, whose deadline is two minutes away and could still have been met by starting it then. That choice contradicts SPT, priority, and earliest-deadline rules; FIFO or an age-based override explains it, but no such policy is documented.

- **QD:** D02’s assignment to D-F and D05’s assignment to D-F support skill-based routing. D07’s assignment to D-X while D10 waits is consistent with D07’s restricted eligibility and FIFO among the relevant jobs; it does not establish that skill routing overrides priority generally. D06’s exact 11:30 start supports an appointment reservation, but resource start-up or other unrecorded constraints are alternatives. D04 beating priority-1 D08 supports FIFO over priority. D10’s wait until 12:16 is explainable by D-F’s meeting and D07 occupying D-X. D-F’s 24-minute idle period while D05 waits is not explained by the listed constraints: it requires an undocumented readiness, calendar, or operational exception.

# 4. Counterfactual discrimination tests

- **QC, 10:54—priority-1 mandate versus deadline escalation.** Both explanations predict C05 at 10:54, so that selection alone cannot distinguish them. At 11:34, a priority-1-only override followed by SPT predicts C06, as observed; an ongoing earliest-deadline rule predicts C07. A timestamped SLA alert or escalation record would reveal whether C05’s start followed a deadline trigger and whether the trigger cleared on completion.

- **QD, 11:36—calendar-aware deferral versus hidden unreadiness.** A rule avoiding work that cannot finish before a known resource block predicts that D-F remains idle until the meeting. A parts or readiness constraint could produce the same observed idle period. Compare similar episodes: calendar-aware deferral predicts idling when the shortest eligible job exceeds time until the block; readiness constraints predict idling tied to readiness events regardless of that time gap. Capture readiness changes and the dispatcher’s decision-time eligibility set.

- **QB, 09:48—late priority visibility versus SPT among newcomers.** If B05’s priority flag was not yet visible, B03 could be selected and B05 could run at the next decision point. If the dispatcher uses SPT among newcomers, B03 (12 minutes) beats B05 (14 minutes). The priority-effective timestamp and the dispatcher’s queue-view snapshot at 09:48 would discriminate directly; repeated choices alone may not.

# 5. Operational exceptions and alternative explanations

Ranked by how well they explain the observed ambiguities:

1. **Unrecorded readiness or staging gates.** This is the leading explanation for D-F idling while D05 waits, and could also explain B05 being passed over at 09:48. It would shrink the true eligible set and weaken inferences drawn from apparent priority or work-conserving violations.

2. **Unrecorded SLA escalation or manual override.** This could explain C05 starting at 10:54 despite SPT. The observed choice fits both priority and deadline ordering, but the late start and already-passed deadline do not reveal the trigger. Evidence of alerts or a manual override could turn the apparent policy switch into a documented exception.

3. **Appointment tolerance or per-queue appointment settings.** A03 and D06 start at their appointment times, while A06 is five minutes late and B07 is much later. Different tolerances could explain these outcomes without a shared appointment rule. This would reduce confidence in appointment insertion for QA and reservation behavior for QD.

4. **Late or changing priority data.** Arrival scans may be delayed by up to two minutes, but that bound does not tell us when a priority flag became visible. A late flag could explain B05 at 09:48. It would make that episode less informative about the scheduler’s ranking rule; the recorded two-minute scan bound alone does not explain it.

5. **Estimate distrust or private re-estimation.** The published estimates equal realized processing times in this sample, but dispatch may have had other information or distrusted estimates. That could weaken the SPT diagnosis in QC. There is no supplied evidence that such re-estimation occurred.

6. **Minute-level timestamps and scan noise.** Close arrival pairs may tie or change order, especially in QC and QD. This can affect FIFO tie-breaks, but does not explain the distinctive SPT choices in QC or the long D-F idle period.

7. **Unmeasured resumption and setup costs.** B01’s observed segment durations sum to its estimate, with no visible extra resumption time. Hidden setup costs could still affect choices, but the supplied data offer little support for that explanation. If present, they would make resume-first decisions more understandable.

# 7. Final synthesis

The best-supported reading is **FIFO with appointment insertion in QA**, **FIFO with priority-1 preemption and resume protection in QB**, **SPT with apparent urgency and age overrides in QC**, and **skill-based eligibility in QD, with ordering within eligible sets largely unidentified**. These are queue-specific diagnoses; the evidence does not support one common rule across the repair center.

QC provides the clearest repeated evidence for a baseline rule: SPT uniquely predicts several successive choices. QD is least identifiable because skills constrain most assignments and the unexplained idle interval could reflect hidden readiness. The most dangerous single inference would be to call QB uniformly preemptive priority: that would mispredict both resume-first choices and B04 beating B06, and would obscure the unresolved B05 episode.
