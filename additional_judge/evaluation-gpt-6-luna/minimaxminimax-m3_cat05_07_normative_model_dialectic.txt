4/10 — The response attempts a layered revision of the model and data, but its visible proposal has material semantic and evidence-traceability problems.

It does well to separate control flow from policy predicates, recognize the limited effective windows of PD-3 and FD-7, and distinguish potential control violations from logging gaps, scope ambiguity, and desirable practice. Its treatment of SUP-703 and SUP-708 as serious deviations, and SUP-704 as potentially justified under the emergency directive, is directionally sound.

However, the proposed model is internally inconsistent: it first builds `M1` with a top-level partial order containing terminal paths, then replaces it with a different construction, while claiming that post-loop XOR choices express exactly one terminal outcome. The separate pre-screen flow does not actually hand accepted leads into M1 in POWL; the response acknowledges this relies on an external case-id rule. Reactivation is presented as a proposed normative amendment, although the source only says CS-9 lacks a procedure. That is a recommendation, not a current conformance rule.

The policy and data rules also overstate what the evidence supports. The response treats notification mirroring as mandatory, although N4 says only that about 70% are mirrored and CRM evidence may be linked; it also classifies SUP-706 as failing pending reconciliation despite the record being uncertain. It adds requirements such as a permanent-style `coi_disclosure_present` check and a `reporting_line_conflict` prohibition that are not stated in the supplied standard. Its emergency activation check mentions retrospective approval before notification, which FD-7 does not require. Several schema/check expressions appear malformed or incomplete, weakening their auditability.

A stronger evaluation would explicitly classify each case as supported, disputed, or unproved under current authority, then distinguish that judgment from proposed policy changes. It should preserve uncertainty for SUP-706 and SUP-709, treat SUP-710 as unresolved scope/policy, and avoid turning stakeholder preferences into normative obligations.
