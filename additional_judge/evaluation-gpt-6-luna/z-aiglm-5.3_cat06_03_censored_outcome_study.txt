5.0 — The answer identifies the central problem correctly: activation selects which applications have an observed default outcome, and rejected, withdrawn, or open cases must not be labeled non-default. It also gives useful leakage controls, temporal validation ideas, and process-aware features.

The main weakness is that it does not settle on a coherent target and cohort. It calls the primary target conditional on eventual activation, yet predicts at an earlier landmark and proposes applying the score to applications expected to activate. That conditions on a future event and leaves the deployment population and estimand unclear. Its proposed cohort flow also does not reconcile the 3,600 activated accounts without mature outcomes: the code labels them “censored” or “feed missing” using a fixed 35-day delay, although the snapshot does not provide the activation dates or label-delay distribution needed to establish those counts.

The implementation has substantial correctness and availability problems. It takes the first Credit Review timestamp as the prediction time but includes every event tied at that timestamp without defining a tie-breaking rule. More seriously, it uses `case:channel` and other case attributes from the full event table, takes the first non-null `default_90d` and minimum label-availability timestamp without verifying they refer to the same outcome record, and assigns labels without requiring the horizon to have matured. It proposes a 72-hour elapsed-time prefix but does not implement the required as-of cutoff for that version. The code also assumes specific renamed activities without evidence, drops duplicates using a potentially overbroad rule, and does not demonstrate the stated transfer-linkage or missing-feed sensitivity analyses.

The validation and metric discussion is broad, but it overstates what can be evaluated in the post-migration cohort while labels are largely immature; IPCW does not by itself solve permanent, potentially non-random feed missingness. Overall, this is a thoughtful design outline, but the target ambiguity and flawed cohort/label handling undermine the core statistical claims and the requested implementation.
