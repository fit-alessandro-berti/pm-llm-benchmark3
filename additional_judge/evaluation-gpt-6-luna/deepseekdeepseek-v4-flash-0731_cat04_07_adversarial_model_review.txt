# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

The models should be judged against their intended use:

- **Exploration:** broad coverage of plausible behavior is useful; lower precision can be acceptable if the model is clearly labeled as non-authoritative.
- **Conformance enforcement:** mandatory policy must be enforced. A model that accepts forbidden traces is unsafe even if its fitness is excellent.
- **Simulation:** the model should be sound, generate plausible behavior, and avoid both excessive restrictions and implausible paths.
- **Stakeholder communication:** readable structure matters, but simplicity cannot substitute for correct policy semantics.

These uses call for different trade-offs. Exploration may favor coverage; enforcement needs policy checks and a curated negative suite. Neither reported fitness nor precision is a substitute for testing the relevant use case. I treat “accepts” below as exact trace generation by the model; an alignment can still assign a nonzero-cost fit to a trace the model cannot generate.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for S:** It is compact, fast in the reported runs, and easy to explain at a high level. Its broad behavior may help exploratory analysis when logs omit events or contain combinations not seen during discovery. Its training and holdout fitness are high.

**Strongest case against S:** Its structure does not merely tolerate noisy observations. The parallel block contains an exclusive choice between each activity and `tau`, so Credit Check and Technical Review can each be skipped. Parallel execution also permits Expedite Review before either mandatory check. These are semantic flaws for enforcement, not noise handling.

Examples it generates:

- `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips both mandatory checks.
- `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips Technical Review.
- `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips Credit Check.
- `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — B4; Expedite precedes the mandatory checks.
- `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips both mandatory checks while performing optional reviews.

S does enforce registration first, release last, and exactly one approval in its generated traces. But it has no Correct Dossier activity, so it cannot represent correction cases at all. Its high fitness may partly reflect the ability to skip events that are absent from the log. That is different from tolerating extra logging noise around a mandatory process backbone.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case for C:** Its enumerated branches include both orders of the two mandatory checks in several cases, place Expedite after the checks when it appears, and select exactly one approval before release. It rejects B3, B4, and B8. Its high reported training precision is consistent with a model that closely follows common observed variants.

**Strongest case against C:** It is an exclusive choice among eight fixed sequences. A valid combination absent from those branches is rejected, even when every component is familiar and policy permits the combination. B2, B5, and B6 illustrate that problem. Supplier Review is represented in selected branches, not as a condition on external sourcing. The Auto Approval branch has no Supplier Review or alternate check order.

Correction handling is equally narrow: one branch contains one Correct Dossier followed by both repeated checks. It does not represent repeated corrections or a correction that affects only Credit Check or only Technical Review. The reported holdout drop from 0.973 to 0.744 is consistent with branch enumeration overfitting frequent variants, though the unreported split and missing-event bias prevent a firm attribution.

The 0.941 training precision does not show that C captures policy. It may reflect that C memorizes observed positive traces and excludes novel combinations, including valid ones. The reported duplicate-label count, low simplicity score, runtime, and comprehension score all reinforce maintainability concerns, but their causes need verification. “Audit-ready” is not established by matching frequent variants.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by likely decision impact:

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior.** S can fit traces missing Credit Check or Technical Review because each activity has a silent skip. Its fitness is not evidence that mandatory checks occurred.
2. **Missing-event bias and positive-log-only precision.** A missing logged activity is indistinguishable from a skipped activity unless the model or data includes evidence about event capture. The reported precision uses positive traces only; it does not test curated forbidden behavior.
3. **C’s branch enumeration and holdout sensitivity.** C’s high training precision may come from memorizing sequences, while its low holdout fitness reflects valid unseen combinations, missing events, or split sensitivity.
4. **Train/holdout split uncertainty.** Seeds, preprocessing, exact cases, and split unit are absent. Duplicate or related cases across partitions could inflate results; variant-level splitting could instead make the holdout intentionally novel.
5. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness.** S’s high token-based score may result from allowing many combinations, including policy violations. Generalization is not a safety measure.
6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Unreported log-move and model-move costs can change fitness and diagnostics. This is especially consequential when one model has silent skips and the other has many same-label branches.
7. **Duplicate-label effects.** C’s count is undefined. Same-label transitions may complicate alignments and attribution, and branch conversion may create structural duplication that the count conflates with legitimate repeated activities.
8. **Silent-transition effects.** S’s `tau` choices make required checks optional. Silent transitions can improve fit to missing-event traces while hiding the policy decision in the model structure.
9. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** The informal score may reflect notation complexity, participants, or question design as much as comprehension. It should inform communication choices, not decide compliance.
10. **Runtime confounding by model size.** C’s larger enumerated net may explain much of its runtime. Hardware, trace length, and implementation settings were not controlled in the reported summary.

# 7. Third Alternative

Use a mandatory procedural backbone with a correction loop and a separate policy gate for attributes absent from the log. Let `R`, `C`, `T`, `S`, `E`, `Cor`, `M`, `A`, and `Rel` denote the activities in the prompt. `seq` means sequence, `par` means parallel interleaving with every branch completed, `choice` means exclusive choice, and `τ` means silent completion.

```text
seq(
  R,
  par(C, T),
  par(
    choice(S, τ),
    choice(E, τ),
    choice(
      τ,
      loop(
        seq(Cor, par(choice(C, τ), choice(T, τ))),
        τ
      )
    )
  ),
  choice(M, A),
  Rel
)
```

The initial `par(C,T)` requires both checks but permits either order. The correction loop permits zero or more correction episodes; each episode can repeat Credit Check, Technical Review, or both. Supplier Review and Expedite Review are optional in the control-flow model and can occur in either order. Expedite is after the initial mandatory checks and before approval. Exactly one approval occurs before release.

The model cannot determine whether Supplier Review is required without the external/internal attribute. Nor can it verify whether the repeated checks match the affected-check set without that attribute. Those cases must produce `unknown` or a manual-review result, not an unqualified compliance pass.

    if source_is_external is None:

def repeated_check_gate(names):
    last_correct = -1
    last_seen = {}

# 9. Validation Experiments

Publish the log version, case identifiers, preprocessing code, PM4Py version, random seeds, split method, alignment variant and costs, and exact held-out cases. Split by case or dossier identity so related records do not cross partitions. Use a seeded case split, then separate variant-held-out and attribute-stratified tests. Since source type and affected checks are missing, stratify on available proxies such as Supplier Review, approval type, and correction presence, and label these as proxies rather than substitutes.

A reproducible variant-stratified split skeleton:

```python
import random
from collections import defaultdict
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import EventLog

def variant_split(log, seed=2026, ratio=0.8):
    buckets = defaultdict(list)
    for trace in log:
        variant = tuple(e["concept:name"] for e in trace)
        buckets[variant].append(trace)

    rng = random.Random(seed)
    train, test = [], []
    for variant in sorted(buckets, key=repr):
        cases = list(buckets[variant])
        rng.shuffle(cases)
        if len(cases) < 2:
            train.extend(cases)
            continue
        n = max(1, min(len(cases) - 1, round(ratio * len(cases))))
        train.extend(cases[:n])
        test.extend(cases[n:])

    return EventLog(train), EventLog(test)
```

This split deliberately places common variants in both partitions. Add leave-one-variant-out and leave-one-combination-out experiments; otherwise the split does not test novel combinations. Where attributes become available, stratify or hold out by source type and affected-check set.

Run a curated negative suite including B3, B4, B8, repeated mandatory checks without correction, two approvals, release before approval, and correction paths that violate the recorded affected-check set. Include valid probes B1, B2, B5, B6, and B7. For B6, record `unknown` if its affected-check attribute is unavailable.

Perturb positive traces by removing each event type and by duplicating Credit Check or Technical Review with and without an intervening correction. Report exact acceptance separately from alignment fitness. Missing mandatory events must not silently become compliant; missing Supplier Review on a case with unknown source type should be reported as unresolved.

Convert each model and report soundness, fitness, precision, generalization, and simplicity separately:

```python
def metrics(model, log):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    sound = pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
    return {
        "soundness": sound,
        "fitness": pm4py.fitness_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        "precision": pm4py.precision_alignments(log, net, im, fm),
        "generalization": pm4py.generalization_tbr(log, net, im, fm),
        "simplicity": pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(
            net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree"
        ),
    }
```

For the negative suite, use alignment diagnostics and classify zero-cost alignments as exact acceptance. Repeat alignments over a pre-registered range of log-move and model-move costs; pin the PM4Py version and confirm parameter names for the chosen alignment variant.

Test unseen combinations such as alternate check order plus Supplier Review plus Auto Approval, with and without Expedite. Check reachability and soundness on converted nets; these do not establish business-policy correctness.

Measure comprehension with a documented, counterbalanced study: recruit compliance, operations, and IT participants; ask each to judge the same valid and forbidden traces and explain the model’s behavior; record accuracy, confidence, and completion time. Report participant counts and the scoring rubric.

**Decision rule:** reject a compliance candidate if soundness fails, any curated negative is accepted, a valid known probe is rejected without a documented data-related `unknown`, or an `unknown` is presented as a pass. Pre-register a holdout fitness threshold and subgroup criteria; a proposed starting gate is 0.90, with documented review for low scores caused by confirmed missing-event bias. No aggregate score overrides a failed negative probe.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

- **Model S:** Do not use for compliance. It may be retained in a clearly labeled exploratory sandbox, with its mandatory-check and Expedite-order limitations visible to users.
- **Model C:** Do not use as the compliance model. Archive it as a record of enumerated frequent variants, not as a general policy model. Its holdout performance, correction limitations, and maintainability concerns require resolution.
- **Third alternative:** Use for exploration and simulation after soundness review. Consider it for compliance only after the validation program passes and the policy gates are operational.

Before compliance deployment, require zero acceptance of curated negatives, acceptance of valid probes or explicit `unknown` handling, reproducible results, a sound net, tested attribute gates, acceptable runtime, and documented stakeholder comprehension. Cases with missing source or affected-check data must be routed for review until those attributes are captured.

# 11. Dissenting View

The third alternative may still make the same basic mistake as Model S: it permits Supplier Review and post-correction rechecks in the control-flow model, then relies on external code to decide whether they were required. If that code is skipped, stale, or fed incomplete attributes, a trace can appear structurally conformant without satisfying policy. C is at least explicit about what it accepts, and a compliance-first organization may prefer rejecting unseen behavior to granting a misleading pass. The third model also uses repeated labels and a loop, which could complicate the very ownership and auditability problems raised about C. Until the board knows which attributes are available at decision time, C’s fail-closed behavior may be safer operationally, even though its coverage is poor. High holdout fitness is not a reason to trust the third model if the policy checks are outside the audited execution path.

I would change this recommendation if source type and affected-check data were captured reliably, the policy gates were mandatory in the compliance pipeline, and a reproducible attribute-stratified holdout showed that the third model accepts valid combinations while rejecting the curated negative suite.
