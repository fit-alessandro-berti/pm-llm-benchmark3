## 1. Interaction-graph description

The object graph links Jobs to Components, ComponentLots, Batches, Machines, Tools, and Shipments. Its observed relations include `requires` (Job→Component), `contains` (ComponentLot→Component), `batches` (Batch→Job), and `consolidates` (Shipment→Job). Event records add links such as Batch Cure→B1/OVEN-1/RACK-7/J1/J2/J3 and Final Test→Job/TEST-1/CAL-3. The event links are observations; edges such as “J4 waits for FIX-A” or “J1 waits for J4’s shipment readiness” are interpretations.

Compact edge list:

```text
J1 ->requires C1; ->batches B1; ->consolidated_by S1
J2 ->requires C2; ->batches B1; ->consolidated_by S2
J3 ->requires C3; ->batches B1; ->consolidated_by S2
J4 ->requires C4; ->batches B2; ->consolidated_by S1
J5 ->requires C5; ->batches B2; ->consolidated_by S3
J6 ->requires C6; ->consolidated_by S3
L7 ->contains C2,C5; Quarantine 10:20; Release 13:50
L8 ->contains C1,C3
B1 ->batches J1,J2,J3; Batch Cure 12:05 on OVEN-1 with RACK-7
B2 ->batches J4,J5; Batch Cure 14:50 on OVEN-1 with RACK-7
J3 ->acquires/releases FIX-A 09:00–11:40
J4 ->reaches CNC-2 09:50; Machine Process 11:45 with FIX-A
J5,J6 ->Final Test on TEST-1 with CAL-3
S1 ->consolidates J1,J4; dispatch 19:25
S2 ->consolidates J2,J3; S3 ->consolidates J5,J6
```

Three higher-order motifs show how dependencies cross job boundaries:

- **Batch and supply motif:** J1/J2/J3 require C1/C2/C3; those components come from L8 or L7; the jobs share B1, which uses OVEN-1 and RACK-7. A single cure event therefore connects several jobs, components, a batch, a machine, and a tool. The inferred edge “J1 waits for the other B1 members” is not directly recorded.
- **Tool and machine motif:** J3 holds FIX-A while J4 is at CNC-2; J4’s later Machine Process is related to FIX-A. The hold and process are observed, but the claim that FIX-A caused J4’s wait is inferred because there is no `Wait for Tool` event.
- **Shipment motif:** J1 and J4 are consolidated into S1. Their packing times differ by 5 h 40 min, while S1 dispatches shortly after J4 is packed. J1’s partner wait is inferred from those timestamps.
- **Lot and batch motif:** L7 connects C2/J2 and C5/J5, which enter different batches and shipments. The lot events relate to both jobs in the supplied evidence, but the Job flattening omits them; J2’s supervisor override further complicates the inferred blocking relationship.

## 2. Quantitative delay decomposition

These are counterfactual estimates, not causal proofs. Each assumes the stated readiness times are accurate and that the proposed alternative would not introduce another constraint.

1. **J1’s B1 synchronization wait:** 12:05 − 08:10 = **3 h 55 min**. If J1 could start a suitable cure when ready, or enter an earlier/smaller batch, this interval might be avoided. That assumes OVEN-1, RACK-7, and all required components were available.
2. **J2’s B1 synchronization wait:** 12:05 − 08:25 = **3 h 40 min** under the same counterfactual. This estimate is especially uncertain: C2 belongs to L7, quarantined from 10:20 to 13:50, yet J2 entered B1 before release under a supervisor override. The 08:25 readiness may not represent valid component availability.
3. **Combined B1 waiting:** 3 h 55 min + 3 h 40 min + 10 min for J3 = **7 h 45 min of job waiting**. If the jobs could be cured independently without extra setup or quality costs, that much job-time could be removed. It is not equivalent to 7 h 45 min of machine time saved.
4. **J4 and FIX-A:** J4 reaches CNC-2 at 09:50; CNC-2 is idle from 10:05 to 10:47; FIX-A is released at 11:40; J4 starts at 11:45. If CNC-2 could have processed J4 at 10:47 and FIX-A was the only remaining constraint, the plausible tool-associated delay is 11:45 − 10:47 = **58 min**. J4’s full arrival-to-process interval is 1 h 55 min, but the evidence does not justify attributing all of it to FIX-A.
5. **J4’s B2 wait:** 14:50 − 13:10 = **1 h 40 min** from batch readiness to start. If J4 could have started a separate cure at readiness, that interval might be avoided. RACK-7 is recorded unavailable until 14:35, however, so under that constraint only the final **15 min** is beyond the earliest rack-available time.
6. **J1’s S1 wait:** 19:25 − 13:30 = **5 h 55 min** from packing to dispatch. If J1 could ship independently 15 minutes after packing—the observed interval from J4’s 19:10 packing to S1’s dispatch—the estimated consolidation-related excess is **5 h 40 min**. This assumes comparable loading readiness and a permissible standalone dispatch.
7. **J6 and CAL-3:** J6 is ready at 16:05 and starts at 17:25, a total wait of **1 h 20 min**. If CAL-3 were available immediately after J5’s test at 16:35, with no additional setup, the recalibration interval would account for **50 min** of J6’s delay. A second available CAL-3 could theoretically remove more, but that is a stronger counterfactual. TEST-1’s idle interval supports a tool constraint over a machine-capacity explanation.

## 4. What flattening misses

- **Job viewpoint:** Makes each job’s activity sequence and local transitions visible. It hides cross-job batch synchronization, shared tool holds, lot quarantine, and waits for shipment partners. A batch cure related to three jobs may be repeated in three job logs, obscuring that it was one event. A normal Pack→Dispatch median or the absence of an extreme total job duration can therefore suggest smooth flow even while multiple jobs wait at the same barrier.
- **Machine viewpoint:** Shows machine events, utilization, and gaps. It does not explain whether an idle machine is waiting for a batch member, a tool, a lot release, or a scheduled availability window. It may also obscure which jobs share one event. OVEN-1’s 44% utilization does not rule out long waits: B2 waits while RACK-7 is unavailable, and a machine can be idle because the required tool or batch policy makes processing infeasible.
- **Batch viewpoint:** Shows batch membership and cure events, including B1’s single 12:05 event. It can hide the spread in member readiness: J1 and J2 wait hours while J3 waits 10 minutes. A batch-level duration can look ordinary while its earliest-ready members incur substantial waiting.
- **Tool viewpoint:** Can reveal FIX-A holds and CAL-3 recalibration. Without explicit job wait events or reservations, it cannot reliably show which ready job was blocked or whether another tool was feasible. A tool log might show an ordinary recalibration interval while TEST-1 sits idle and J6 waits.
- **ComponentLot viewpoint:** Shows L7’s quarantine and release but may not establish which job was actually blocked. The supplied extraction omits these events from the Job log, while an alternative pipeline would link them to J2 and J5. Treating a lot release as a job wait without resolving the override and readiness semantics would overstate causality.
- **Shipment viewpoint:** Shows shipment creation, loading, and dispatch, but the supplied DFG excludes how long an already packed job waits for its shipment partners. S1’s short final interval after J4 is packed can coexist with J1 waiting 5 h 55 min after its own packing.

Thus low machine utilization and long job waiting can coexist: the binding constraint may be a tool, a lot hold, a batch policy, or a shipment partner rather than the machine.

## 8. Falsification and additional data

Observations that would weaken batch synchronization as the leading operational explanation include:

1. J1/J2 readiness timestamps precede actual staging, inspection, or component availability.
2. OVEN-1 or RACK-7 was unavailable before B1’s 12:05 start for a reason independent of batch membership.
3. A component or quality hold—not the arrival of J3—determined B1’s start.
4. Earlier or smaller cures fail to reduce job completion or dispatch times because downstream constraints dominate.
5. Batch policy did not require waiting for all listed members, or B1 membership was changed by an override.
6. Comparable batches with early-ready members do not show similar waits.
7. Smaller batches increase setup and rack-cleaning time enough to increase total job waiting.

Useful additional fields or event types include:

1. Explicit wait events with reason codes for batches, tools, lots, and shipments.
2. Component staging-complete and inspection events linked to Jobs and ComponentLots.
3. Machine and tool availability calendars, including RACK-7 cleaning and CAL-3 calibration.
4. Batch policy fields: minimum/maximum membership, latest start, cure constraints, and override reason.
5. L7 quality decisions linked to C2, C5, J2, and J5, including authorization and timestamps.
6. FIX-A reservations, setup requirements, and tool handoff events.
7. Shipment cutoffs, delivery windows, partner-ready times, and standalone-shipment eligibility.
8. Job-level packed-to-dispatch timestamps and shipment assignment history.
