## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw/mapped fitness gap is primarily evidence about labels and event provenance, not a real conformance collapse. Raw fitness falls from about 0.92 to 0.55 in March while mapped fitness recovers to about 0.90. This indicates that the old normative model does not recognize the migrated label and technical-event vocabulary. It does not establish that activities disappeared or that cases became non-compliant.

Mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicated `Open Case` events can recover conformance because alignments no longer treat renamed activities as missing or technical duplicates as deviations. But good fitness under that mapping only shows that the mapped abstraction fits the old model. The business process could still change through rework, queueing, prioritization, or service.

Duplicated technical events mainly distort trace length, variant frequency, process discovery, and conformance. Missing resource identifiers chiefly undermine organizational and resource-performance analyses; they do not necessarily change control-flow fitness. The post-May patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone: mapped fitness also declines modestly while `Assess -> Request Info`, rework share, cycle times, Supplier Z share, and high-complexity share change. Logging explains March’s large raw-fitness shift and adds later measurement noise, but does not by itself explain the May rework increase, the later cycle-time tail, or the October complexity spike.

**Candidate change points and scope.**

- **Logging drift:** March 1 is the strong candidate for the label/schema and duplicate-event shift. March–June is a second candidate window for worsening resource completeness and persistent technical-event duplication.
- **Behavioral drift:** May 5 is a candidate for supplier-driven rework. July 1 is a candidate for priority-induced queue redistribution; the July–October DFG changes may also reflect case mix and inconsistent Senior Assessment recording.
- **Performance drift:** May is the first clear rise in median and P90 cycle time. Mid-June through August is a candidate acceleration window after the staffing loss and priority change. October is a further seasonal peak.

The evidence supports multiple mechanism-specific change points, not one global change point: March’s raw fitness shift is largely semantic, May’s rework increase follows the supplier disruption, queueing grows around the staffing and priority changes, and October’s population shifts with renewal season. December’s improvement is also mixed.

## 6. Segmentation plan

| segment level | variables | purpose | minimum support rule | comparisons enabled | risk of conditioning bias |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time window | Month and, where volume permits, week; event date | Detect timing, seasonality, and short-lived shifts | At least 50 cases per weekly cell; otherwise pool to month | Same-season comparisons and change-window analyses | Seasonal load can be mistaken for drift. |
| Migration era | Pre/post March 1; raw vs normalized activity label; technical-duplicate flag | Separate schema and provenance shifts from process changes | At least 100 cases per period and relevant stratum | Raw/mapped conformance and DFG comparisons | Migration may coincide with unobserved operational changes. |
| Supplier and disruption phase | Supplier Z vs others; pre-May, May–September, post-September; region | Isolate supplier disruption and recovery | At least 30 cases per cell; pool regions or months when lower | Supplier contrasts within phase, complexity, and season | Supplier assignment is non-random and associated with complexity and region. |
| Complexity and priority | High/standard complexity; recorded priority class; Senior Assessment indicator | Separate population mix from queue redistribution | At least 30 cases per cell; combine adjacent periods for sparse cells | Within-class process and waiting comparisons | Priority and Senior Assessment can be mediators or inconsistently recorded. |
| Resource/team | Team, assessor where support permits, reassignment period, resource-missing flag | Diagnose capacity and resource attribution | At least 20 cases per assessor-quarter; otherwise aggregate to team | Departed vs continuing teams; assigned workload and wait | Missing resource data can select which cases enter the comparison. |
| Process behavior and outcome | Rework yes/no, variant family, decision outcome | Monitor loops, conformance, and outcomes | Collapse rare variants; avoid interpreting cells below 10 cases | Rework vs non-rework and outcome-stratified trends | Conditioning on rework or outcome can introduce selection bias. |

The primary monitoring population should be the full case population with labels normalized and known technical duplicates handled, reported overall and stratified by Supplier Z and complexity. For a relatively stable reference stream, use non-Z, standard-complexity cases compared within season. Keep Supplier Z and high-complexity cases in their own monitored strata rather than excluding them from operational reporting.

Use season-matched or seasonally adjusted references where historical data permit, and compare both observed and case-mix-standardized metrics. Set alert limits by metric and stratum using control limits, robust dispersion, or quantile targets rather than one threshold for all monitors. Manage multiple testing by grouping related monitors, applying a procedure such as Benjamini–Hochberg within each family, and requiring sustained or corroborated signals before treating a statistical alert as an operational change.

# Validate row order, required columns, ranges, and completeness.
assert monthly["month"].is_monotonic_increasing
assert monthly["month"].is_unique
assert dfg["month"].is_monotonic_increasing
assert dfg["month"].is_unique

pct_cols = [
    "high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
    "rework_variant_pct", "missing_resource_pct", "duplicate_open_case_pct"
]
fitness_cols = ["raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness"]
assert monthly[pct_cols].notna().all().all()
assert monthly[fitness_cols].notna().all().all()
assert monthly[pct_cols].apply(lambda s: s.between(0, 100).all()).all()
assert monthly[fitness_cols].apply(lambda s: s.between(0, 1).all()).all()
assert (monthly["cases"] > 0).all()
assert monthly["distinct_activity_labels"].notna().all()

edge_cols = [
    "Register_Eligibility", "Eligibility_CollectDocs", "CollectDocs_Assess",
    "Assess_RequestInfo", "RequestInfo_CollectDocs",
    "Assess_SeniorAssessment", "SeniorAssessment_to_Decision"
]
assert dfg[edge_cols].notna().all().all()
assert dfg[edge_cols].apply(lambda s: s.ge(0).all()).all()
assert set(dfg["month"]).issubset(set(monthly["month"]))

# Baseline-relative absolute and relative changes for monthly metrics.
baseline = monthly.loc[monthly["month"] == pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01")].iloc[0]
metrics = [
    "cases", "high_complexity_pct", "supplier_z_pct", "fast_variant_pct",
    "rework_variant_pct", "median_cycle_h", "p90_cycle_h",
    "raw_model_fitness", "mapped_model_fitness", "missing_resource_pct",
    "duplicate_open_case_pct"
]
change_rows = []
for _, row in monthly.iterrows():
    out = {"month": row["month"].strftime("%Y-%m")}
    for metric in metrics:
        delta = row[metric] - baseline[metric]
        out[metric + "_abs_vs_Jan"] = delta
        out[metric + "_pct_vs_Jan"] = (
            100 * delta / baseline[metric] if baseline[metric] != 0 else np.nan
        )
    change_rows.append(out)
baseline_changes = pd.DataFrame(change_rows)

# Fitness recovery: absolute mapped-minus-raw gap and fraction of raw non-fitness
# removed by the mapping. This is a diagnostic, not proof of business equivalence.
monthly["raw_to_mapped_recovery_abs"] = (
    monthly["mapped_model_fitness"] - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
)
monthly["raw_fitness_gap"] = 1 - monthly["raw_model_fitness"]
monthly["fraction_raw_gap_recovered"] = np.where(
    monthly["raw_fitness_gap"] > 0,
    monthly["raw_to_mapped_recovery_abs"] / monthly["raw_fitness_gap"],
    np.nan
)

# Transparent distance on the supplied seven-edge fragment only.
jan_vector = dfg.loc[dfg["month"] == pd.Timestamp("2026-01-01"), edge_cols].iloc[0].to_numpy(float)

def fragment_l1(vector, reference):
    return float(np.abs(vector - reference).sum())

def fragment_mad(vector, reference):
    return float(np.abs(vector - reference).mean())

distance_rows = []
for _, row in dfg.iterrows():
    vector = row[edge_cols].to_numpy(float)
    distance_rows.append({
        "month": row["month"].strftime("%Y-%m"),
        "fragment_L1_vs_Jan": fragment_l1(vector, jan_vector),
        "fragment_MAD_vs_Jan": fragment_mad(vector, jan_vector),
        "max_edge_change_vs_Jan": float(np.abs(vector - jan_vector).max())
    })
distances = pd.DataFrame(distance_rows)

# Explainable candidate flags based on month-to-month changes.
# These are prompts for investigation, not formal change-point tests.
distance_by_month = dict(zip(distances["month"], distances["fragment_MAD_vs_Jan"]))
candidate_rows = []
for i in range(1, len(monthly)):
    prev = monthly.iloc[i - 1]
    cur = monthly.iloc[i]
    reasons = []

    if (
        cur["raw_model_fitness"] - prev["raw_model_fitness"] <= -0.15
        or cur["distinct_activity_labels"] != prev["distinct_activity_labels"]
    ):
        reasons.append("logging/schema")

    if (
        cur["supplier_z_pct"] - prev["supplier_z_pct"] >= 15
        or cur["rework_variant_pct"] - prev["rework_variant_pct"] >= 8
    ):
        reasons.append("supplier/rework")

    if (
        cur["median_cycle_h"] - prev["median_cycle_h"] >= 5
        or cur["p90_cycle_h"] - prev["p90_cycle_h"] >= 10
    ):
        reasons.append("performance")

    current_month = cur["month"].strftime("%Y-%m")
    previous_month = prev["month"].strftime("%Y-%m")
    if (
        current_month in distance_by_month
        and previous_month in distance_by_month
        and distance_by_month[current_month] - distance_by_month[previous_month] >= 3

import pm4py

def format_event_log(df, case_id="case_id", activity="activity", timestamp="timestamp"):
    """Return a formatted copy; do not overwrite the caller's dataframe."""
    work = df.copy()
    work[timestamp] = pd.to_datetime(work[timestamp], errors="coerce")
    work = work.dropna(subset=[case_id, activity, timestamp]).copy()
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work,
        case_id=case_id,
        activity_key=activity,
        timestamp_key=timestamp
    )

def slice_time_range(formatted_df, start, end):
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(
        formatted_df,
        start,
        end,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )

def monthly_dfg(formatted_df, freq="M"):
    work = formatted_df.copy()
    work["__period"] = work["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period(freq)
    result = {}
    for period, subset in work.groupby("__period"):
        result[str(period)] = pm4py.discover_dfg(
            subset,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    return result

def monthly_variants(formatted_df, freq="M"):
    work = formatted_df.copy()
    work["__period"] = work["time:timestamp"].dt.to_period(freq)
    result = {}
    for period, subset in work.groupby("__period"):
        result[str(period)] = pm4py.get_variants(
            subset,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    return result

def temporal_profile(formatted_df, start=None, end=None):
    subset = formatted_df
    if start is not None and end is not None:
        subset = slice_time_range(formatted_df, start, end)
    return pm4py.discover_temporal_profile(
        subset,
        case_id_key="case:concept:name",
        activity_key="concept:name",
        timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
    )

def discover_optional_log_skeleton(formatted_df):
    discover = getattr(pm4py, "discover_log_skeleton", None)
    if discover is None:
        raise NotImplementedError("This PM4Py version has no discover_log_skeleton function.")
    try:
        return discover(
            formatted_df,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    except TypeError:
        return discover(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted_df))

def discover_optional_declare(formatted_df):
    discover = getattr(pm4py, "discover_declare", None)
    if discover is None:
        raise NotImplementedError("This PM4Py version has no discover_declare function.")
    try:
        return discover(
            formatted_df,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    except TypeError:
        return discover(pm4py.convert_to_event_log(formatted_df))

def drop_exact_duplicate_activity_events(formatted_df, activity_name="Open Case"):
    """Drop duplicate rows for the specified activity by case, activity, and timestamp."""
    work = formatted_df.copy()
    mask = work["concept:name"].eq(activity_name)
    if not mask.any():
        return work
    key = ["case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"]
    kept = pd.concat([
        work.loc[~mask],
        work.loc[mask].drop_duplicates(subset=key, keep="first")
    ]).sort_index()
    return kept

def compare_raw_vs_normalized_label_mappings(
    df,
    mapping,
    case_id="case_id",
    activity="activity",
    timestamp="timestamp",
    deduplicate_open_case=True
):
    """
    Compare raw and mapped views while preserving the input dataframe and raw log.
    Example mapping: {"Eligibility Screen": "Validate Eligibility"}.
    """
    raw = format_event_log(
        df, case_id=case_id, activity=activity, timestamp=timestamp
    )
    normalized = raw.copy()
    normalized["concept:name"] = normalized["concept:name"].replace(mapping)

    if deduplicate_open_case:
        normalized = drop_exact_duplicate_activity_events(
            normalized, activity_name="Open Case"
        )

    raw_results = {
        "dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(
            raw,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        ),
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(
            raw,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    }
    normalized_results = {
        "dfg": pm4py.discover_dfg(
            normalized,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        ),
        "variants": pm4py.get_variants(
            normalized,
            case_id_key="case:concept:name",
            activity_key="concept:name",
            timestamp_key="time:timestamp"
        )
    }
    return {"raw": raw_results, "normalized": normalized_results}

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest attribution is March’s raw-fitness collapse: fitness falls from 0.92 to 0.55, while mapped fitness recovers to about 0.90 alongside 11 activity labels, duplicated `Open Case`, and higher resource missingness. This is high-confidence semantic and provenance drift, not evidence of a comparable loss of business-process conformance. The mapped DFG fragment changes little in March.

There is moderate-to-high confidence that the Supplier Z disruption increased incomplete-bundle rework: Supplier Z share rises from 18% in April to 43–46%, rework rises from 13% to 24–27%, and Request Info relations increase. September partly reverses these patterns, though residual backlog or persistent adaptation could explain the incomplete recovery.

Staffing loss plausibly accounts for much of the later queueing and P90 tail. Assess service time changed only modestly while reconstructed waiting before Assess increased more. The July priority rule likely redistributed delay between high-complexity and standard cases, but aggregate data cannot establish which group benefited. Renewal season plausibly explains the October–November volume and high-complexity peak, amplified by reduced capacity and remaining supplier backlog. The Senior Assessment increase is consistent with this shift but is confounded by historically inconsistent recording.

December’s improvement is mixed: lower seasonal load, Supplier Z recovery, backlog reduction, overtime, and the document-quality campaign coincide. It is not clean evidence of permanent recovery. Supplier Z cases are non-random and more complex; staffing, priority, and seasonality overlap. The data support multiple mechanism-specific change points rather than one global change. I would revise this account if event-level analyses showed that rework persists after supplier recovery and complexity adjustment; if queue increases concentrate in teams affected by assessor departures; if high-complexity waits fall as standard-case waits rise after July 1; or if mapped, deduplicated logs show no substantive process or performance change.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. IF raw fitness drops sharply while mapped fitness recovers and new labels or duplicated `Open Case` events appear, THEN audit label mapping and technical-event provenance before treating conformance as deteriorated, BECAUSE this signature points to semantic or logging drift.

2. IF Supplier Z share rises together with `Assess -> Request Info` and rework variants, THEN stratify cycle time and DFG by supplier before adjusting capacity, BECAUSE incomplete bundles can generate rework and queueing while case-mix differences remain a confounder.

3. IF Assess service time is stable but queue waiting and P90 cycle time increase after assessor departures, THEN treat queueing and capacity as primary diagnostic targets, BECAUSE stable service duration suggests delay is accumulating outside the assessment activity.

4. IF high-complexity waits fall while standard-case waits rise after the priority rule, THEN report queue metrics separately by priority class and review the rule against service levels, BECAUSE aggregate medians can hide priority-induced redistribution.

5. IF October–November volume and high-complexity share rise in a recurring annual pattern, THEN compare against same-season baselines and adjust monitoring references, BECAUSE seasonal population drift can resemble process deterioration.

6. IF mapped DFG, variant, or rework measures remain elevated after supplier recovery and complexity adjustment, THEN investigate persistent behavioral adaptation and backlog-clearing practices, BECAUSE supplier recovery alone should reduce the disruption-related signature.

7. IF resource missingness clusters in migrated activities and biases team comparisons, THEN run resource analyses by provenance stratum with missingness sensitivity checks, BECAUSE measurement gaps can create or conceal apparent organizational drift.
