1. **Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof**

M0 requires registration, both checks before the decision, and—on the approved branch—manual approval before activation. Its checks are concurrent; the model imposes no order between Sanctions_Check and Quality_Review. The decision is an exclusive choice between approval followed by activation and rejection. Notification follows the decision, and closure follows notification.

The evidence establishes that CS-9 is formally approved and requires sanctions and quality checks before activation, manual approval before activation, and different humans as requester and manual approver. It also establishes that PD-3 and FD-7 have stated effective windows, that FD-7 expired automatically, and that CRM migration made the onboarding log incomplete. The record does not establish whether PD-3 had authority to waive CS-9, whether rejected intake and reactivation are in M0’s scope, or whether SUP-706’s CRM message belongs to that case. SUP-712’s aliases resolve the requester and approver to the same person. SUP-715 raises a disclosure concern, but no supplied rule bars spouses from those roles.

For a noncompliance finding, require reliable evidence that the case was in scope, the governing rule applied at the time, and the required act was absent or out of order. Treat weak linkage or unresolved scope as indeterminate or a model/scope defect, not as proof of compliance. For an exception, require evidence of applicable authority, eligibility, effective dates, and satisfaction of the exception’s conditions. Operational practice, desirable outcomes, or a belief that a policy change was imminent do not meet that burden.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

CS-9 is the formally approved baseline. PD-3 was a time-limited steering decision, not an amendment to CS-9; the Compliance observer explicitly disclaimed approval to weaken the standard. Even if PD-3 authorized a monitored exception, it applied only to eligible low-risk DE suppliers in an approved corporate group, from June 1 through July 31. FD-7 was narrower still: it applied to critical flood-relief suppliers during a three-day interval and imposed monitoring, a spend cap, and retrospective obligations. Neither instrument supports permanent practice.

SUP-703 is a clear breach of the baseline and not a valid pilot exception: the supplier was high-risk, new, foreign, and purchased EUR 180,000. Choosing the “pilot” option did not confer eligibility. SUP-708 activated before sanctions clearance with no applicable exception. The fact that no purchase occurred before clearance limits realized harm but does not cure the prohibited sequence. SUP-705 followed the FD-7 pattern after its automatic expiry; the continuing factual emergency did not extend the written authority. SUP-714 likewise used Auto_Approve after PD-3 expired.

SUP-712 fits M0’s control flow but violates CS-9’s separate-person rule. SUP-715 presents a credible control concern: the relationship required disclosure and none was recorded, although the supplied facts do not establish that the relationship itself prohibited approval. SUP-704 appears to satisfy FD-7’s stated timing, spend, monitoring, and next-business-day review conditions, but its justification depends on evidence of the directive’s authority and the supplier’s critical-relief status.

The aggregate evidence suggests exception drift: 312 Auto_Approve cases include 14 clearly ineligible cases and six post-pilot uses in early August. The nine cases with missing eligibility attributes cannot be presumed eligible. Forty-seven information-request cases demonstrate that a recurring practice has developed outside M0. Their desirability does not retroactively amend policy. The prosecution must nevertheless concede that M0 omits resource rules, loops, and overlays, and that replay against it can overstate deviations when scope or logging is defective.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is a narrow control-flow abstraction, not a complete compliance specification. Its partial order permits sanctions and quality checks in either order, while CS-9’s “before activation” language allows either check order as long as both precede activation. A simple M0 replay cannot encode identity, eligibility, authority, deadlines, spend caps, or disclosure duties. Treating every replay mismatch as misconduct confuses model fit with policy compliance.

The model omits the information-request cycle used in 47 cases. Those requests obtained missing certificates, and none of the cases activated before receiving the evidence. This is a genuine model defect with a desirable control effect. It should be modeled explicitly and governed, rather than counted as an unexplained anomaly.

The pilot and emergency overlays change permitted routes and timing. SUP-702 matches PD-3’s stated eligibility and review timing, with a stable CRM notification ID. SUP-704 falls within FD-7’s effective window and, on the supplied facts, meets its spend, monitoring, and review conditions. These cases should not be called ordinary baseline failures merely because M0 does not encode the overlays. But the defense cannot infer that PD-3 possessed waiver authority simply from the pilot decision or operational adoption.

Notification evidence is incomplete after migration. SUP-706 has a CRM message, but its mutable link entered two days later does not securely prove case attribution. That is a data-quality limitation, not evidence that notification definitely occurred or definitely did not. SUP-713’s QA_Clearance is supported by equivalent audit content; the mapping’s late approval is a governance issue, not substantive proof that quality review was skipped. SUP-711’s duplicate registration rows share a source transaction ID, supporting a migration duplication explanation.

SUP-709 may be an intake lead outside M0 if the model owner’s scope is correct; the scope document is ambiguous, and CS-9 allows abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads. SUP-710 is a reactivation, a case type with no explicit CS-9 procedure, and has prior quality certification. Neither should be assessed as an ordinary new onboarding without a clear scope rule. Still, factual emergency need, normalized pilot behavior, or anticipated permanent approval cannot substitute for valid authority. The defense also cannot dismiss the clear high-risk violations in SUP-703 and SUP-708.

**System-level judgment:** The operation is materially noncompliant in specific, consequential respects, especially SUP-703, SUP-705, SUP-708, SUP-712, and SUP-714. That does not validate a blanket conclusion from M0 replay rates. M0 is a useful standard-route model, but it is incomplete for information requests, resource checks, exceptions, intake scope, reactivation, and migrated event evidence. The defensible conclusion is neither “mostly compliant because the model is obsolete” nor “every deviation is misconduct”: some violations are established, some exceptions appear justified subject to authority, and several cases require better scope or data controls.

I reject the prosecution’s broadest claim that M0 deviation proves policy breach in cases with valid overlays, alternate case types, or unreliable event linkage. I reject the defense’s broadest claim that operational normalization, continued emergency need, desirable requests, or expected policy change excuses conduct without applicable authority. The system needs both enforcement of existing rules and a governed revision of the model and evidence.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Use three coordinated layers:

1. **Control-flow model:** Keep a standard onboarding route with concurrent sanctions and quality checks, both before standard activation; manual approval before activation; and notification before closure. Add an optional repeatable Request_More_Info stage before the route decision. Model pilot and emergency routes separately so their permitted timing is explicit. Define separate processes for rejected intake, reactivation, pre-activation withdrawal, and post-activation termination.

2. **Policy and data rules:** Gate routes on case type, effective dates, authority, eligibility, provisional status, deadlines, spend, and required monitoring. Require different canonical humans for requester and manual approver. Check conflict disclosure and mitigation under the applicable policy. A route label cannot itself establish exception authority.

3. **Evidence and organizational controls:** Use canonical activity IDs, effective-dated label mappings, append-only event records, source-backed deduplication, immutable CRM notification links, stable person IDs, and preserved correction history. Publish versioned rules with named authority and automatic expiry controls. Reconcile CRM and onboarding events by period and source; report data confidence separately from process conformance.

Use route-conditioned ordering checks: sanctions before every activation; manual approval before standard activation; quality before standard activation; notification before closure; no activation after rejection; and one canonical registration after verified deduplication. Do not impose quality-before-all-activation because PD-3 and FD-7 allow delayed review. Use data checks for business-day deadlines, spend and currency, eligibility, identity, conflicts, duplicates, and notification coverage.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

Treat POWL as a control-flow envelope. Use external, versioned data rules to gate pilot and emergency routes and validate eligibility, authority, deadlines, provisional status, spend, and monitoring. The repeatable information stage can be represented as an XOR between silent skip and a loop whose body is Request_More_Info, redo is silent, and exit is silent. Use distinct transition objects for repeated activity occurrences.

The supplied implementation defines standard, pilot, emergency, rejected-intake, reactivation, and withdrawal routes; places standard checks concurrently; and orders route-specific events as stated above. It is a proposal, not evidence that a new policy has been approved. Before adoption, verify PM4Py loop semantics and ensure route guards are enforced outside the POWL model.

The normalized policy overlay should retain immutable rule version, scope, effective interval, authority status, eligibility conditions, and obligations. In particular, record the authority basis for PD-3, enforce FD-7’s automatic expiry, and do not infer a permanent auto-approval rule from practice.

Event records should retain case and event IDs, source transaction ID, canonical activity ID, displayed label, mapping version, event time, recorded time, source system, and correction history. Store case type, route, rule version, exception decision, authorizer and delegation evidence, eligibility snapshot, actor identities and aliases, conflict disclosures, notification ID and delivery status, and—where applicable—spend, monitoring, retrospective approval, and review deadline. Resolve case type and policy version first, validate event identity and order next, then apply route-specific policy and data rules.
