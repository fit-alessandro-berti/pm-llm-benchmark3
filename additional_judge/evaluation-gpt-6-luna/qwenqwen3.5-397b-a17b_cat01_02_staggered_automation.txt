The answer is cautious about whether a single causal percentage is defensible and identifies many central issues: nonrandom rollout, rising use after availability, concurrent demand and staffing changes, fraud desk spillovers, mixed median and p90 behavior, and survey response limitations. It also gives a concrete embedded-evaluation recommendation and includes the requested Python analysis.

However, several problems materially weaken it. The estimands table calls multiple effects identifiable despite major confounding and only five branches, and it does not adequately clarify interference or treatment-version differences. The proposed primary DiD is not robustly specified for staggered treatment effects; its adjustment set includes post-treatment variables such as staffing, arrivals, and complexity, which may themselves be affected by treatment or concurrent policy changes. The quantitative section contains a major inconsistency: it labels the Alpha-versus-Delta median difference-in-differences as an 18% benefit, even though it subtracts percentage changes with different baselines and the resulting contrast is not a defensible relative causal effect. Several table claims also overreach the aggregates: for example, escalation rises in untreated branches too, and “rework rates remained stable relative to complexity” is not established.

The final claim ladder includes a conditional 10–15% relative improvement without supporting calculations or a credible identification argument, undercutting the otherwise cautious conclusion. The recommendation’s within-Delta randomization is also described as a step-wedge even though randomizing cases within one branch is not a cluster step-wedge, and its proposed thresholds are arbitrary. Finally, the supplied code omits validation, constructs relative time with an inaccurate 30-day approximation, suppresses warnings, and estimates models that do not address month effects or staggered-treatment heterogeneity. These are substantial analytical and execution flaws despite broad coverage of the required sections.
