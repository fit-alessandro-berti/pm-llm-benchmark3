1. **Review Scope and Assumptions**

I distinguish four uses:

- **Exploration:** discover possible behavior. High fitness and tolerance for incomplete or noisy records can be useful, if the model is clearly labeled as descriptive.
- **Conformance enforcement:** decide whether work may proceed or whether a case violates policy. Mandatory checks and approval constraints must be explicit; uncertainty should prompt review, not authorization.
- **Simulation:** estimate paths, workload, and delays. The model needs credible choice probabilities and routing data, which these control-flow expressions and metrics do not provide.
- **Stakeholder communication:** explain what is allowed and why. Readability, stable activity meaning, and explicit uncertainty matter alongside coverage.

Fitness and precision trade-offs depend on the use. Exploration can tolerate a permissive model that admits extra behavior. Enforcement cannot safely use high fitness to excuse forbidden paths, or high precision to reject plausible but unobserved combinations. Simulation requires evidence about frequencies and durations that neither model supplies.

I treat P1–P7 as the policy basis, subject to the unresolved detail in P3 and P6. I assume the expressions use conventional process-tree meanings: sequence `->`, exclusive choice `X`, optionality `?`, loop `*`, and silent `tau`. Exact behavior can still depend on parser and conversion semantics. The missing external/internal attribute and affected-check attribute are material evidence gaps.

**3. Adversarial Review of Model S**

**Strongest case for S.** It is compact, fast in the reported evaluation, easy to explain at a high level, and appears to cover the observed and holdout logs well. Its permissiveness could be useful for discovery when events are missing or behavior is still being learned. As F7 says, if it is only an exploratory model, low precision may be a tolerable cost.

**Strongest case against S.** Its structure makes nearly all activities optional, including the mandatory checks. It also allows Expedite Review before those checks. That is a semantic flaw for enforcement, not merely noise tolerance. A model can be robust to missing events only by accepting traces that might actually violate policy; it cannot tell the difference between a missing record and omitted work.

Concrete undesirable or policy-ambiguous behaviors it admits include:

1. `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips both mandatory checks (forbidden by B3).
2. `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — Expedite precedes both checks (forbidden by B4).
3. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips Technical Review.
4. `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips Credit Check.
5. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Auto Approval, Release Order` — takes both approval branches because `X('Manager Approval', 'Auto Approval')` permits both in sequence.
6. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Release Order` — skips approval entirely.
7. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Correct Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` — allows correction without any affected check repeating.
8. `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Technical Review, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` — repeated check without correction is admitted, despite P7.

The optional parallel block permits additional interleavings of Supplier and Expedite with the checks. Some may be legitimate, but the model cannot enforce P2–P4. Supplier’s optionality may accommodate internal orders or missing events, but without the sourcing attribute it also accepts omission for external cases. The same ambiguity applies in retrospective analysis: an accepted trace is not evidence that the required work was performed.

**4. Adversarial Review of Model C**

**Strongest case for C.** C explicitly lists some meaningful paths, including both orders of the mandatory checks, optional supplier and expedite cases, one correction-and-repeat pattern, and Auto Approval in one branch. It may serve as a catalog of observed variants, a regression fixture, or a conservative “represented by this catalog” check. Its high training precision suggests it restricts behavior around observed traces.

**Strongest case against C.** It enumerates complete sequences rather than expressing policy constraints. The eight branches do not cover the stakeholder policy: they omit many combinations and do not encode the conditions that would explain why a sequence is allowed.

- **Branch enumeration:** every new combination of check order, sourcing type, expedite presence, correction effect, and approval type requires more branches. F2’s complaint is supported by the expression.
- **Duplicate labels:** repeated activities across branches are a natural consequence of sequence enumeration; the reported count is undocumented, so it is a diagnostic clue, not a measured defect count. Still, duplicated paths make ownership, change review, and impact analysis harder.
- **Unseen combinations:** B2 is a valid alternate check order with Auto Approval, yet the listed Auto Approval branch has Credit Check before Technical Review. F4’s holdout combinations are therefore plausible sources of low holdout fitness.
- **Correction handling:** C permits one enumerated correction followed by both checks. It cannot choose whether only Credit Check, only Technical Review, or both must repeat, and does not represent repeated corrections generally. It can reject a valid correction affecting only one check and admit a repeat pattern that is wrong for the actual correction.
- **Holdout degradation:** 0.744 versus 0.973 training fitness is consistent with overfitting to listed variants. However, unknown split and alignment details prevent treating the score as a definitive generalization estimate.
- **Maintainability:** the branch list is difficult to inspect and update. A policy change can require many variants and risks inconsistent edits.
- **Precision and memorization:** high training precision may be an artifact of a narrow catalog evaluated against observed positive traces. F5 notes there was no curated negative suite. It does not show that C distinguishes compliant from noncompliant unseen cases.
- **Policy gaps:** P5 requires exactly one approval before release, but C includes branches with Manager Approval and no Auto Approval, and one with Auto Approval and no Manager Approval; that fits an exclusive approval interpretation. However, C does not combine Auto Approval with Supplier Review or alternate check order, and its single repeated-check correction pattern is not a general P6 mechanism.

F8’s proposed default—treat unseen behavior as noncompliant—is defensible only for a clearly defined allowlist control with an approval path for novelty. It is not equivalent to proving a policy violation. For audit use, “not represented in this catalog” should remain distinct from “forbidden.”

**6. Model S Semantic Audit**

Given its expression, S’s intended backbone is Register first, then any interleaving of four optional activities, then one of three approval outcomes (Manager, Auto, or silent), then Release. The result is broader than “optional supplier and expedite.” It permits either mandatory check to be absent, any of the four activities to repeat or run in parallel under `+`, and activities to occur in orders that violate P2 and P4. The silent alternative in the approval choice makes approval optional.

| Policy | S behavior | Finding |
|---|---|---|
| P1: Register first; Release last | S enforces this sequence for modeled visible events. | Supported |
| P2: Both checks before approval, either order | S makes both checks optional and does not constrain their order relative to approval. | Violated |
| P3: Supplier only for external orders | S makes Supplier optional for every case; no condition guards it. | Not representable without an attribute or explicit case context |
| P4: Expedite optional, after both checks, before approval | S makes Expedite optional but can place it before either or both checks. | Violated |
| P5: Exactly one approval before release | S permits a silent approval, Manager only, or Auto only. The inclusive concurrent block can also execute both approval labels before Release. | Violated |
| P6: Correction repeats affected checks | S omits Correct Dossier entirely and has no dependency between correction and repeats. | Not represented |
| P7: Repeated mandatory check without correction is suspicious | S’s inclusive loop permits repeated checks without correction. | Violated |

The `+` operator’s exact repetition and concurrency semantics should be confirmed in the declared process-tree dialect. Under standard inclusive-concurrent semantics, it generates repetitions and interleavings. If a tool interprets it differently, the team still needs to document the mapping, because the model’s apparent control guarantees depend on it.

**7. Proposed Third Alternative**

Call the third alternative **Third: policy-guarded token net with case attributes and review states**. It combines a structured workflow net with an explicit policy checker. The net provides the main control-flow skeleton and simple activity labels; the checker handles data-dependent rules the observed event labels cannot establish.

**Shared activity flow:** Register Dossier starts the case. Credit Check and Technical Review are mandatory, can complete in either order, and must both be current before approval. Supplier Review is required when the dossier is externally sourced. Expedite Review is optional and may occur only after both checks are current; it may precede or follow Supplier Review. Exactly one of Manager Approval and Auto Approval occurs, and it follows the required activities. Release Order occurs last.

**Correction state:** Correct Dossier advances the dossier version and marks the checks whose results are invalidated as stale. Approval is blocked until the required checks for the current version are current. This is governed by an explicit correction-scope attribute, such as `{credit, technical, both}`, recorded with the correction. Repeated corrections create a new version and new invalidation decision each time. A repeated check without a preceding correction is flagged. If correction scope, source type, or event capture is unknown, the case goes to review rather than being silently accepted or declared a policy violation.

**Conditional sourcing:** record an external/internal field with provenance and effective time. External cases require Supplier Review. Internal cases do not. If the field is missing, third should return “indeterminate—review,” not infer the case is internal.

**Illustrative policy pseudocode:**

```text
state:
  registered
  checks_current = {credit: false, technical: false}
  source_type ∈ {external, internal, unknown}
  correction_scope ∈ {credit, technical, both, unknown}
  expedite_done = false
  approval ∈ {none, manager, auto}
  released = false

on Credit Check:
  require registered and not released
  require credit is not already current
       or a prior correction invalidated credit
  set credit current

on Technical Review:
  require registered and not released
  require technical is not already current
       or a prior correction invalidated technical
  set technical current

on Supplier Review:
  require registered and not released
  require source_type = external
  require no duplicate supplier event unless policy explicitly permits it
  set supplier_done = true

on Expedite Review:
  require both checks current
  require approval = none and not released
  set expedite_done = true

on Correct Dossier(scope):
  require registered and approval = none and not released
  require scope is known
  mark the checks in scope stale
  record new dossier version and correction provenance

on Manager Approval or Auto Approval:
  require all mandatory checks current
  require Supplier Review if source_type = external
  require approval = none and not released
  set approval to the selected type

on Release Order:
  require approval ∈ {manager, auto}
  require not released
  set released = true

for every visible event:
  require it is consistent with the registered case version and event-capture rules
  otherwise return indeterminate or route to review, according to evidence quality
```

This is a conceptual specification, not a directly executable PM4Py process tree. A practical implementation could be a small Petri net for the main control flow plus data-aware transition guards, with a separate monitor for event completeness and correction invalidation. The policy checker should return three states: **compliant**, **noncompliant**, and **indeterminate/review**. It must record a reason code and the policy version for each result.

**B1–B8 expected behavior under Third:** accept B1–B2; reject B3–B4; accept B5 if source type is external and its required data are present; accept B6 only with evidence that Technical Review alone was invalidated; accept B7 if external; flag B8 as suspicious/noncompliant absent a prior correction. Missing source type or correction scope produces indeterminate/review.

**8. Validation and Implementation Plan**

Before using any model as a control, the board should require a pinned and reproducible evaluation:

1. **Freeze definitions and artifacts.** Version the event-label mapping, process-tree or net conversion, PM4Py version, preprocessing, cost functions, metric variants, timeouts, and case split. Publish seeds and a case-level split manifest. Protect sensitive case data while making the selection reproducible for authorized reviewers.
2. **Separate known and unseen cases.** Use at least one fixed, stratified holdout by case and a time-based holdout. Preserve combinations such as check order, sourcing type, approval kind, Expedite presence, correction scope, and event-missingness. Test known combinations on blind cases, and separately test deliberately unseen but policy-consistent combinations.
3. **Curate positive and negative suites.** Include B1–B8, boundary cases, malformed traces, missing-event scenarios, forbidden ordering, duplicate checks, double or absent approval, release before approval, and all correction scopes. Have compliance and process owners adjudicate independently; resolve disagreements before scoring.
4. **Evaluate attributes and event capture.** Verify source type, correction scope, dossier version, and event completeness against source systems. Include unknown, delayed, contradictory, and missing values. Measure the “indeterminate/review” rate and the cost of obtaining evidence.
5. **Report metric details separately.** For each model, publish alignment fitness and precision variants, token generalization, structural simplicity, runtime distributions and timeouts, exact membership results for curated probes, and trace-level diagnostics. Do not combine them into an aggregate without explicit board-approved normalization and weights.
6. **Test model quality directly.** Check workflow soundness, dead transitions, reachable completions, labeling, and whether every accepted terminal trace obeys policy invariants. Check termination behavior and boundedness assumptions. A sound net can still encode the wrong policy, so include policy tests independently.
7. **Measure human and maintenance performance.** Run structured comprehension tasks with compliance, operations, IT, and audit participants. Ask them to identify mandatory activities, explain a rejection, handle a correction update, and find the owner of a rule. Time routine changes, count inconsistent updates, and assess impact analysis.
8. **Use shadow deployment first.** Compare model outcomes with adjudicated cases without authorizing or blocking work. Track disagreement categories, missing attributes, case mix, runtime, and review burden. Define escalation and override reasons.
9. **Set decision thresholds before seeing results.** Establish acceptable false-accept and false-reject rates, uncertainty routing, maximum review burden, availability, and runtime targets by use case. Require documented sign-off for policy changes and regression reruns.

A “holdout” must be case-disjoint, and where relevant time-disjoint, to avoid leakage from variants or repeated cases. The board should know whether alignment fitness charges missing and extra events symmetrically, how silent transitions are treated, and whether timeouts or failed alignments were excluded.

**Illustrative PM4Py validation sketch**

The following is an API-oriented outline, not executable evidence for the submitted models. Pin the PM4Py version, confirm the documented variant and parameter names against that installation, and record all outputs. Convert process trees to Petri nets for alignment and soundness checks. Run each trace with fixed costs and retain move-level alignments, not just aggregate fitness.

**Explicit alignment-cost experiment**

```python
from pm4py.objects.log.obj import Event, Trace
from pm4py.algo.conformance.alignments.petri_net import (
    algorithm as align_alg,
)

def align_with_costs(labels, net, im, fm,
                     log_costs=None, model_costs=None, tau_cost=1):
    log_costs = log_costs or {}
    model_costs = model_costs or {}
    trace = Trace([Event({"concept:name": x}) for x in labels])
    P = align_alg.Parameters

    parameters = {
        P.PARAM_TRACE_COST_FUNCTION: [
            log_costs.get(x, 10000) for x in labels
        ],
        P.PARAM_MODEL_COST_FUNCTION: {
            t: tau_cost if t.label is None
            else model_costs.get(t.label, 10000)
            for t in net.transitions
        },
        P.PARAM_SYNC_COST_FUNCTION: {
            t: 0 for t in net.transitions
        },
        P.PARAM_MAX_ALIGN_TIME_TRACE: 60,
    }
    return align_alg.apply_trace(
        trace, net, im, fm,
        parameters=parameters,
        variant=align_alg.Variants.VERSION_DIJKSTRA_NO_HEURISTICS,
    )
```

The separate cost mappings are supported by the alignment interface. A timeout or missing result must remain a failed/unknown evaluation, not be converted into a favorable score.

Exact probe membership should be checked independently of normalized alignment fitness. The supplied implementation includes visible-transition replay with silent closure, so it does not assume that `fitness == 1` or raw alignment cost zero is a universal membership test.

### What was actually verified here

I executed an independent, ordinary-Python enumeration of the third model’s transition rules. It found **19 reachable markings**; every marking was 1-safe and could reach the final marking; every transition was reachable; and no final marking retained other tokens. The probe and adversarial outcomes shown above were checked against those rules.

The construction also has a direct invariant argument: each active mandatory-check pair contains exactly one token, approval consumes all active control tokens, and release produces only the final token. Repeated corrections can produce arbitrarily long traces without producing unbounded token counts. Soundness here means completion remains possible, not that every infinite correction sequence must terminate.

**PM4Py was unavailable in the execution environment, so its construction, conversion, soundness, alignment, and metric calls were not executed here.** Those checks remain part of the pinned-environment validation program. No new training/holdout metric values are claimed.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

**Model S may remain an exploratory baseline, but not an enforcement model.** Its interface should display the mandatory-check and Expedite-order limitations prominently. It should not be used to conclude that omitted events represent permitted work, and its inability to represent correction must remain visible.

**Model C may remain a historical variant catalog and regression baseline.** It can identify “not represented by this catalog,” but that finding must not be labeled “policy violation.” Its audit-ready claim should be withdrawn unless the branches have independently approved policy meaning and the missing data conditions are enforced elsewhere. The existing rejection of B2 already prevents treating this particular catalog as a complete expression of the supplied policy.

**Retire both originals as stand-alone normative authorities, not as retained analytical artifacts.**

Advance Third to shadow-mode evaluation, conditional on four deployment requirements:

1. The process owner ratifies sourcing semantics, current-check validity, correction scope, treatment of successive corrections, and whether Supplier or Expedite Review can be invalidated.
2. Source type, correction scope, dossier version, and event-capture provenance are recorded reliably enough for the intended decisions.
3. The net and policy checker are deployed and versioned together, with no path that authorizes release on control-flow fitness alone.
4. Blind behavioral, empirical, soundness, comprehension, maintenance, and operational tests meet predeclared acceptance criteria.

For autonomous authorization, an unresolved required fact should lead to **evidence collection or authorized review**, not silent approval. For retrospective audit, the same uncertainty should not automatically become an allegation that the underlying work was omitted.

Third’s main advantage is not a predicted improvement in every metric. It is the explicit separation of **what is mandatory, what may vary, what a correction invalidates, and what cannot be established from the available evidence**.

# 11. Dissenting View

**Rebuttal.**

The recommendation may underestimate the operational risk of replacing a familiar allowlist with a custom validity-state model and a separate policy interpreter. The third alternative is compact in source code, but its correction variants, hidden routing, and uncertainty states could be harder for auditors to inspect than eight explicit sequences. Its data guard is also outside ordinary label-based alignment, creating a new failure mode: one component can be upgraded or bypassed while the other continues reporting apparently satisfactory conformance.

The proposed correction semantics deserve particular skepticism. Treating one later check as satisfying several outstanding corrections, allowing correction before both initial checks finish, and retaining an earlier Expedite Review after correction are reasonable interpretations, not agreed requirements. Introducing them during a model-quality review risks turning the reviewer into the policy author.

A safer interim strategy could be to repair C only with expressly ratified variants, retain S for discovery, and route novel combinations to a documented approval process. That may impose additional review work, but an organization with low volumes or high consequences could rationally prefer visible conservatism to inferred flexibility. Until reliable attributes exist and the complete monitoring system has demonstrated lower total error and review burden, the third alternative should not receive a presumption of superiority.

**Evidence that would change my decision.** I would favor a corrected, guarded allowlist if the owner explicitly adopted that policy, measured novelty and review volumes were manageable, and it outperformed Third on documented comprehension and operational tests without misclassifying approved behavior. I would revise Third if correction records showed that Supplier or Expedite Review must also be invalidated, or that separate checks are required for successive revisions. Conversely, reliable attribute capture, blind unseen-combination success, integrated guard testing, and lower measured review burden would strengthen the case for Third.
