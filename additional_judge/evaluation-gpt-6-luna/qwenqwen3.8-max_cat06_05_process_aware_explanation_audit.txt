## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Enforce point-in-time availability.** Reconstruct each case prefix through the first `Coverage Check Completed` event. For every feature, retain its source event time, effective time, ingestion time, and computation window. Assert that all inputs were available by `t0`; use as-of joins for snapshots and changing attributes. If a same-timestamp event lacks a reliable sequence index, mark its ordering ambiguous.

2. **Check activity-order feasibility against the normative process.** Use a domain-approved process model with precedence constraints such as registration before coverage check, damage assessment before a settlement decision, appeal only after Partial or Deny, and closure after a final decision or withdrawal. Test prefixes and full traces with alignments or declarative constraints. Flag `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` as a violation or ambiguous ordering, not a valid risk pattern.

3. **Classify explained activities from the prefix state.** Immediately after registration and coverage check, `Damage Assessment Completed` and `Documents Requested` are possible next steps; a fraud investigation may also be possible if its trigger is met. A settlement decision is not yet enabled. Appeal requires a later Partial or Deny, and closure requires a later final decision or withdrawal. The explanation linter should distinguish activities that are enabled now, merely future-possible, and inconsistent with the process.

4. **Handle same-timestamp events explicitly.** Detect equal timestamps, require a trustworthy sequence index or source transaction identifier, and suppress any order-dependent pattern when neither exists. For Case Z, test alternative event orders if useful, but do not show the pattern to users unless its order is resolved.

5. **Detect full-trace feature construction.** Review feature lineage and code for end-of-case fields, full-trace variants, totals without a `time <= t0` filter, and “last” or “final” values. Compare features computed from the full case with features recomputed from its `t0` prefix; any difference identifies a candidate leakage path.

6. **Validate repeated decision events semantically.** Permit a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` only when a prior decision was Partial or Deny and an Appeal occurred before reconsideration. Treat duplicates without that sequence, or with unresolved same-timestamp order, as data-quality issues. A valid appeal/reconsideration sequence is still future information for the `t0` model.

DFGs and footprints can help find unusual transitions, while alignments can measure conformance to a supplied model. A frequent DFG edge is evidence of observed behavior, not a normative rule; data errors can make it frequent. Declarative checks are useful only when their constraints reflect the actual process, so domain review remains necessary.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Predictive validity requires a reliable feature available at `t0` and evidence that it helps predict the target. Causal interpretation requires evidence about what would happen under an intervention. Operational actionability additionally requires a feasible intervention shown to improve outcomes. Fairness review asks whether use of an otherwise predictive feature creates unjustified or disparate effects.

| Class | Examples | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signals | `initial_damage_complexity`, `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; repaired prior-claim history or reserve if verified as of `t0` | These can contribute statistical risk information available at the prediction point. | Their importance alone does not prove they cause delay or identify an effective intervention. |
| Process-state or congestion signals | `queue_length_at_t0`; prefix-safe workload, evidence state, or fraud-open status if timestamped by `t0` | They describe the operational state associated with predicted risk and may support triage. | They do not prove that changing queue length, staffing, or document handling will reduce breaches. |
| Proxy requiring fairness and robustness review | `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel` | A governed model may retain them if their predictive value and segment effects are validated. | They should not be framed as inherent claimant causes or used for differential treatment without appropriate review. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week`, holiday and policy periods | They may capture period-specific operating conditions when validated and monitored. | A week number is not itself a causal explanation, and historical importance does not guarantee future stability. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, current `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, and future or ambiguous sequence patterns | They may be investigated for data quality or replaced by timestamped prefix equivalents. | They must not support a `t0` prediction or explanation in their current form. |
