The answer understands the central problem: default is observed only after activation, so rejected, withdrawn, and open applications must not be relabeled as non-default. It also proposes useful alternatives, flags selection and missing feeds, and addresses temporal validation and feature leakage.

Several major flaws keep it from scoring higher:

- The primary target is inconsistently defined. Prediction is described as occurring at an early, pre-decision prefix, while the population is defined by eventual activation. That can be a valid retrospective estimand, but the answer does not make clear that eventual activation is future information unavailable at prediction time or explain the resulting selection in evaluation.
- The cohort flow calls all 3,600 activated but omitted accounts “right-censored / delayed / missing-feed.” Those are distinct states, and the provided counts do not say how many are immature, delayed, or permanently missing. Likewise, the flow does not cleanly distinguish the approximately 1,740 linked transfers from the unlinked cases in the primary accounting.
- The proposed observation weighting is under-specified and potentially invalid. It does not define the observation indicator or correctly separate administrative immaturity from permanent missingness; weighting cannot repair MNAR feeds without additional assumptions. A completed 90-day window with a delayed feed is not ordinary right censoring of the outcome.
- The code’s case-level terminal-state logic gives rejection, withdrawal, or transfer precedence over activation regardless of event order. Its maturity rule also treats a window plus the maximum feed delay as sufficient to establish label maturity, despite the stated possibility of permanently missing feeds.
- Prefix extraction does not actually construct both requested prefix types. The fixed-length PM4Py call is demonstrated, but elapsed-time prefixes are only described. More seriously, stopping the entire trace at the first decision event leaves activated cases with no pre-decision events after that point, while the code never ensures the selected prefix is the intended prediction point or that a case’s label is aligned to that prefix.
- The code’s static-feature extraction uses the first value in the prefix, which may be unsafe for fields that change or are backfilled. The `insert_case_service_waiting_time` call uses the event timestamp itself as the start timestamp, so the resulting service-time interpretation is not justified. The PM4Py dataframe/log API usage and waiting-time column assumptions are not sufficiently verified for a concrete implementation skeleton.
- Validation gives an Oct–Dec 2025 drift check but no credible matured default evaluation for that cohort, and the suggested top-risk alert metric does not clearly match the pre-decision prediction setup.

The response is broad and often statistically cautious, but its cohort accounting and implementation details contain material ambiguities and errors.
