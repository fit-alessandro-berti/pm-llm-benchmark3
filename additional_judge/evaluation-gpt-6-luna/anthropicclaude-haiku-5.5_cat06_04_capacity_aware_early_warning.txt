## 1. Decision rule

The response follows the requested six-section structure and recognizes stage eligibility, repeated-alert suppression, calibration uncertainty, and queue spillovers. It correctly excludes I-03 from swarm eligibility and distinguishes I-05’s stale repeated alert from I-07’s new evidence.

However, key elements of the policy are not reliably deployable. The response contains malformed comparisons such as `p_adj_central  0.5`, omits the promised specific calibration offsets, and does not clearly define a usable prioritization score or how calibration adjustments change the decision. It also treats some proposed thresholds and quotas as settled choices without showing how they fit together.

## 2. Capacity allocation for the current cases

It provides a row for each case and chooses a portfolio while reserving one slot. The table gives rationales and identifies important uncertainties, including I-06’s imputed feature and I-09’s sparse segment data.

The allocation is weakened by inconsistent reasoning: it appears to rely on benefit estimates and spillover costs that are not fully derived or consistently applied. The reserve rationale is reasonable, but the response does not clearly show that its selected portfolio remains preferable across the stated uncertainty ranges.

## 3. Expected-impact table

The response attempts optimistic, base, and adverse scenarios and acknowledges that effects are uncertain.

The central requirement—auditable arithmetic for avoided breaches, direct economic benefit, intervention cost, and spillover harm—is not met robustly. It uses point-like case estimates without making the assumptions behind them sufficiently clear, and does not consistently distinguish risk reduction from breach probability under intervention. The adverse scenario also needs a clearer account of which selected case may receive little or no benefit and how that changes the portfolio’s net impact.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

The I-05 versus I-07 comparison is one of the stronger sections. It suppresses I-05 absent new evidence and reopens I-07 because of the uploaded log, while sensibly requiring targeted review before considering another swarm.

The temporal rules remain incomplete: the response does not give a clear cooldown duration or a fully operational minimum-evidence rule, and relies on malformed threshold comparisons.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

The response addresses all required fairness and feedback-loop topics and proposes concrete safeguards, including segment audits, intervention logging, holdouts, and spillover tracking.

Some safeguards are arbitrary or potentially counterproductive without further justification. A quota, segment spillover budget, and randomized deferral policy need clearer definitions and safeguards for contractual obligations and small samples. The treatment of Non-Native-Language Support uncertainty is thoughtful but does not establish how uncertainty changes the actual allocation decision.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

The plan covers operational, predictive, causal-effect, capacity, and fairness monitoring. It separates risk prediction from intervention-effect estimation and proposes randomized tie-breakers plus complementary observational methods.

Several triggers and thresholds are presented without a clear link to the decision rule, and the plan does not resolve how the team should act while estimates remain too uncertain. Overall, the response shows good awareness of the decision problem but falls short on numerical coherence, reproducibility, and operational precision.
