The answer covers all six required sections and handles several key constraints well: it excludes I-03 because the swarm is ineffective after Diagnosis Complete, suppresses I-05’s unchanged repeat alert, treats I-07’s new log as meaningful, reserves a slot, and explicitly discusses calibration, fairness, feedback loops, and spillover harms.

The largest weakness is the impact analysis. It treats spillover as a fixed €20,000 average value per affected case without support, and the scenario rows’ direct benefits and breaches avoided do not consistently follow from the selected cases’ effect ranges. The optimistic spillover figure also lacks a clear calculation. The adverse scenario does not clearly show how its “little or no benefit” case is selected or quantify the net impact.

The allocation is not fully auditable either: it labels cases by tiers without computing comparable scores, and I-07’s stated €1,300 midpoint benefit is outweighed by its €450 cost and estimated spillover, before false-alarm burden. The fixed calibration shifts are presented as precise adjustments despite being rough backtest ranges, and subtracting a negative value is ambiguous. Some proposed monitoring triggers, such as reducing a spillover estimate when queue delays rise, point in the wrong direction.

Overall, it is a thoughtful policy draft, but the inconsistent arithmetic and a few operationally weak rules prevent it from being a reliable deployable decision policy.
