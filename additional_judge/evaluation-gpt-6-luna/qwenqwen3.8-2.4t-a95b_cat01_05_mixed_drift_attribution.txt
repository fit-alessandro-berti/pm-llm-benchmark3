## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The March raw/mapped fitness gap is large: raw fitness falls from 0.93 in February to 0.55 in March, while mapped fitness is 0.90. The new `Eligibility Screen` label, duplicated technical `Open Case` events, and migration timing make a logging or semantic explanation for most of the raw decline plausible. This gap does not prove that the business process was unchanged. Mapping assumes `Eligibility Screen` is equivalent to `Validate Eligibility`; that equivalence needs validation against event context, outcomes, and case-level traces.

Mapping can restore conformance to an old model when the activity’s recorded name changes but its meaning and position remain stable. It cannot establish that eligibility decisions, routing, or timing stayed the same. The mapped fitness series itself also trends down after May, reaching 0.86 in July, which warrants operational investigation.

Duplicated technical events can create spurious DFG edges, inflate event counts, distort variants, and create false conformance deviations. Removing them is reasonable for process analysis but should be auditable and limited to verified duplicates. Missing resource identifiers primarily weaken workload, handoff, and team attribution; if missingness varies by activity or team, resource comparisons can be biased.

Post-May DFG and performance patterns cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone. Rework variants and normalized `Assess -> Request Info` rates rise sharply, cycle time and p90 worsen, and reconstructed queue waiting increases much more than recorded assessment service time. These signals have plausible operational causes, though supplier, complexity, staffing, and priority effects remain confounded.

### Normalized DFG distance

Use the supplied seven-edge relation-rate vector, in occurrences per 100 cases. Define the distance between months as the L1 distance, the sum of absolute edge-rate differences, divided by two. This is a transparent movement measure, not a statistical test or a complete process distance. It assumes the listed relations have comparable definitions across months and gives each edge equal weight. Because the fragment omits edges, loops, starts, and ends, it cannot describe all process change.

### Cycle-time deterioration decomposition

From January to August, median cycle time increases 13.5h, or 43%; p90 increases 41h, or 59%. From January to November, median increases 17.5h and p90 52h.

- **Case mix:** High-complexity share rises from 21% to 29% in August and 45% in November. This plausibly contributes to the increase, but no stratum-specific cycle times are supplied, so its hours contribution cannot be identified.
- **Control-flow/rework:** Rework variants rise from 12% to 27–29% in May–August. Normalized `Assess -> Request Info` rises from 12 to 29 per 100 cases by July, then falls to 20 in September. Additional document cycles plausibly add elapsed time.
- **Service:** Recorded `Assess` service time rises only from about 4.8h to 5.4h by July–August, about 0.6h. This is small relative to the 13.5h median increase, although other service tasks are not measured here.
- **Queueing:** Reconstructed waiting before `Assess` increased much more than assessment service time. The especially large p90 increase suggests growing tail delays and supports queueing as a major contributor, particularly after assessor departures and with higher volume. The supplied data do not permit exact component shares.

These mechanisms overlap: rework adds future queue demand, while priority can move waiting between case classes. A numeric additive decomposition would be unjustified.

### Candidate change points and global versus mechanism-specific timing

- **Logging:** March 1 migration is a strong candidate onset; March–April persistence is a confirmation window. A secondary logging or recording change around October is possible for `Senior Assessment`, but not established.
- **Behavioral:** May 5 supplier disruption is a candidate onset for rework; July 1 priority rule is a separate candidate for routing and queue redistribution. The October increase in senior assessments is another candidate requiring validation.
- **Performance:** June 15 assessor departures are a candidate for queueing deterioration; October 1 renewal season is a separate demand-related candidate. December 5 interventions are a candidate for partial improvement.

A single global change point is not supported. The evidence points to multiple mechanism-specific events and gradual responses, with seasonal recurrence and intervention effects layered on top.

## 9. Provisional attribution

The strongest conclusion is that the March raw-fitness collapse is primarily logging or semantic drift from migration. The raw/mapped gap, new label, technical duplicates, and stable mapped fitness support this strongly. This does not establish that the underlying eligibility behavior was identical.

The May–September rework increase is plausibly supplier-driven, with moderate confidence. Supplier Z share rises sharply; request-info rates and rework variants increase and then partially recede as the supplier recovers. Supplier Z’s prior complexity and regional concentration prevent clean attribution.

Staffing loss likely contributed substantially to queueing delays from June onward. Queue waiting increased more than assessment service time, and p90 cycle times rose sharply. Supplier rework and the July priority rule are concurrent contributors.

The priority rule likely redistributed delay between high-complexity and standard cases, but the aggregates cannot demonstrate that. October–November deterioration is consistent with seasonal increases in volume and complexity against constrained capacity. December improvement is consistent with both interventions and lower seasonal load, so it cannot be credited to either intervention from these data alone.

Supplier-stratified rework, priority-class queue waits, complexity-adjusted mapped conformance, and team-level missingness would most change these attributions.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **If** raw fitness drops sharply while mapped fitness and normalized DFGs remain stable, and new labels or duplicates appear, **then** route the alert to logging triage and validate the mapping, **because** the pattern is consistent with semantic drift but does not prove business-process equivalence.

2. **If** mapped conformance declines while request-info loops rise, especially within Supplier Z and complexity strata, **then** inspect bundle completeness and rejection reasons, **because** incomplete documents are a plausible rework mechanism.

3. **If** queue waiting before Assess rises while assessment service time changes little, **then** review assessor capacity, work in progress, and team queues, **because** the pattern is consistent with capacity-driven delay.

4. **If** post-July high-complexity and standard-case waits diverge, **then** review priority thresholds and queue protection for standard cases, **because** priority can redistribute rather than remove waiting.
