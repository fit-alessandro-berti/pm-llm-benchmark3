## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March:** Metrics and data-quality measures are stable, with fully mature labels. These months provide the cleanest baseline. V3 already has higher breach rates than V1 and V2, so the aggregate rate depends on variant mix.

**April:** This is primarily a pipeline incident. The renamed diagnosis event caused invalid or missing prediction prefixes; duplicate handling and incomplete cached-feature backfill add further uncertainty. The high mean predicted risk and poor discrimination/calibration should not be read as ordinary concept drift. April is useful as a pipeline regression case, not as a routine calibration or training cohort until features are rebuilt.

**May:** The mapping patch and backfill coincide with substantial recovery in metrics and data quality, but incomplete derived-feature recomputation means April is not necessarily repaired. May is closer to baseline, subject to lineage checks.

**June:** The remote-routing policy sharply increases V2 share. Aggregate breach rate falls, while ECE rises. Remote-fix time stays stable and V2’s matured breach rate falls to 0.09. This looks more like a mix/base-rate and calibration change than broad process deterioration. Check within-variant performance before retraining.

**July:** Supplier delays begin to show in the parts edge (65 h), and V3 matured breach rate rises to 0.46. AUROC falls despite near-baseline data-quality indicators. This is an early signal of operational risk change, with labels almost mature but not fully so.

**August–September:** The supplier issue becomes severe: parts-wait reaches 130–148 h and V3 matured breach rises to 0.64 in August and 0.61 in September. This is process-linked risk deterioration. September also introduces Partner Z and a large V3 share, creating population and mix changes. Its observed aggregate breach rate is additionally vulnerable to slower label maturity. Thus August’s deterioration predates Z; September adds a separate population-shift question.

**October:** The reported breach rate of 0.16 is not evidence that the supplier issue resolved. Only 55% of labels are mature, the API backlog favors longer paths, and the parts edge remains at 155 h. Treat October’s outcome metrics as censored; continue relying on process evidence while waiting for mature labels.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment:** Keep October out of outcome-based performance conclusions and training. Maintain operational escalation for V3/parts-dependent cases based on the rising parts-wait edge, with human review of any threshold-based decisions. Gate predictions when diagnosis-prefix integrity or required features fail.

**Short-term recalibration:** Consider recalibration only after labels reach a comparable maturity and pipeline checks pass. June may support a calibration review because the remote-routing policy changed the variant mix and aggregate base rate, but first check calibration within variants. Do not fit a calibrator on corrupted April records or immature September–October outcomes.

**Feature repair:** Rebuild April features from normalized, deduplicated events, including the cached pre-diagnosis count. Audit rural distance, device age, queue features, and parts-wait signals for availability at prediction time and reliable lineage. A parts-delay feature could make the model responsive to the observed operational change, but validate its timing and leakage risk.

**Full retraining:** Use clean, mature January–March data as baseline training evidence, and include later months only after fixed follow-up and pipeline validation. May–June can be considered after lineage checks; July–September require explicit review for supplier and population changes. April is unsafe until rebuilt; October is unsafe until label maturity is adequate. Retraining on immature October labels risks learning from an outcome-incomplete cohort.

A variant-specific model is not yet justified. First test whether performance problems persist within variants and whether each segment has enough mature data. Variant-specific calibration or a carefully validated segment model may be justified if stable within-segment miscalibration remains. Partner Z-specific calibration or modeling should wait for equal-maturity comparisons and adjustment for variant, rurality, device age, and supplier conditions.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

- **Predictive:** AUROC, Brier score, score distributions, and threshold workload by month, variant, and source, computed on fixed-maturity vintages.
- **Calibration:** Reliability curves, calibration intercept/slope, and ECE by segment; only trigger action when data quality and maturity gates pass.
- **Label maturity:** Maturity curves, median delay, pending-label share, and delay by variant/source. This alarm pauses outcome-based decisions; it does not directly trigger retraining.
- **Data quality:** Diagnosis-prefix coverage, unknown activity share, duplicates, queue-feature missingness, feature recomputation lineage, and cache-versus-recompute discrepancies.
- **Process variants:** Variant mix and unmapped share, plus case-start and event-calendar counts.
- **Performance-DFG:** Edge times and distributions for parts ordering/receipt and diagnosis-to-remote-fix; alert on operational deterioration independently of outcome-label maturity.
- **Population:** Source/partner shares and changes in rural distance, device age, and variant composition; track both raw and reweighted comparisons.
- **Intervention/deployment:** Threshold actions, overrides, workload, and post-intervention outcomes. Record model/version and data vintage for each decision.

Alert dependencies should separate evidence sources: data-quality failures block calibration and training; immature labels defer outcome-based performance actions; process-edge deterioration can trigger operational review without triggering retraining; retraining requires mature labels, clean lineage, and evidence of conditional performance degradation.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE, ACT, TS = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
DIAGNOSIS = "Initial Diagnosis Completed"

def load_and_clean(path):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)
    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        raw, case_id=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
    )
    df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS], utc=True)
    df[ACT] = df[ACT].replace({
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": DIAGNOSIS,
    })

    # Remove exact duplicate event rows after normalization; retain the first
    # stable occurrence and return an audit count.
    before = len(df)
    df = df.drop_duplicates([CASE, ACT, TS], keep="first")
    removed_count = before - len(df)
    return df.sort_values([CASE, TS]).copy(), removed_count

def monthly_process_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    df, removed_count = load_and_clean(path)
    case_start = df.groupby(CASE)[TS].min().rename("case_start")
    df = df.join(case_start, on=CASE)
    rows = []

    for month in pd.period_range("2026-01", "2026-10", freq="M"):
        start = month.to_timestamp(how="start")
        end = (month + 1).to_timestamp(how="start")

        # Case-start cohort: cases begun this month, retaining full traces.
        started = df[(df["case_start"] >= start) & (df["case_start"] < end)]
        # Event-calendar cohort: events occurring this month; useful for
        # workload, but cross-month process edges may be truncated.
        event_month = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, start, end, mode="events", timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE
        )
        pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, start, end, mode="traces_starting",
            timestamp_key=TS, case_id_key=CASE,
        )  # cross-check for the case-start cohort

        for cohort, log in (("case_start", started), ("event_calendar", event_month)):
            dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
            perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
            variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
            temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
                log, case_id_key=CASE, activity_key=ACT, timestamp_key=TS
            )
            rows.append({
                "month": str(month), "cohort": cohort,
                "cases": int(log[CASE].nunique()), "events": len(log),
                "variants": len(variants), "dfg_edges": len(dfg),
                "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
                "temporal_feature_rows": len(temporal),
                "deduplicated_rows_for_input": removed_count,
            })

    return pd.DataFrame(rows)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

Retraining on immature October labels could teach the model that recent long-path cases rarely breach, because those cases’ outcomes are disproportionately pending. That would distort future thresholds and hide the ongoing supplier delay. Use fixed-vintage labels and maturity gates.

Calibrating on April’s broken prefixes could mask a pipeline defect rather than repair it. Rebuild and validate features first; retain April as a regression case for the pipeline.

Treating August as another April-style data incident could miss the operational deterioration: parts-wait rose sharply and matured V3 breach rates increased while data-quality indicators remained near baseline. Keep process monitoring and operational review active even when outcome metrics are delayed.

Attributing September’s entire gap to Partner Z could confuse partner effects with V3 mix, rurality, device age, supplier conditions, and slower label maturation. Compare equal-maturity, like-for-like cohorts and adjust for these factors before partner-specific intervention.
