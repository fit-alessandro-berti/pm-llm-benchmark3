The response follows the required six-section structure, covers every case, selects a portfolio within capacity, and addresses repeated alerts, calibration uncertainty, spillovers, fairness, and monitoring. Its core decision logic is coherent: it excludes I-03 as past the effective stage, suppresses I-05 without new evidence, and treats I-06’s imputed feature as a reason to wait.

The main weakness is that the arithmetic and prioritization are not fully consistent. The stated expected-benefit formula omits multiplication symbols, and the displayed per-case base benefits do not match the listed midpoint effects: I-04’s midpoint reduction is 0.19, I-09’s is 0.24, and I-02’s is 0.15, but the combined direct-breach estimate and economic ranges are not transparently reconciled across scenarios. The spillover estimate applies an assumed €20,000 average value to all affected cases without grounding that value in the portfolio, and the response does not clearly distinguish expected breach changes from monetary harm. Some allocation choices also conflict with its own rule: I-04’s stated lower-bound benefit before cost and false-alarm burden is negative, yet it is called robust; I-02’s calibration adjustment is not reflected in its calculation. The proposed randomized holdout and fairness safeguards are useful, but they need operational limits and clearer protections for urgent cases.

Overall, this is a thoughtful, operationally aware policy with useful safeguards, but the expected-impact table and tier assignments need more auditable, internally consistent calculations.
