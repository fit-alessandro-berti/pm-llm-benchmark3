The response identifies the central estimand problem and sensibly chooses default risk conditional on eventual activation. It also recognizes that rejected, withdrawn, and open cases do not have observed default labels, distinguishes several sources of missingness, proposes temporal validation, and covers most of the required leakage fields.

The main weakness is that the cohort construction and implementation do not support the claims. The cohort-flow table is missing, despite the prompt requiring all 48,000 applications to be accounted for by status. The code does not correctly establish case-level terminal outcomes or label maturity: it takes a maximum `label_available_at` across events, which can be missing or unrelated to the target label, and its activation-time aggregation is invalid when a case has no activation. It also constructs prefixes from whole traces of eventually activated, mature cases without truncating each trace at a defined prediction time. That risks using future events and selects on later activation and label maturity. The code’s `get_prefixes_from_log` call and subsequent filtering do not demonstrate the promised as-of prefix extraction.

The analysis also needs a clearer primary censoring and missing-feed strategy. It mentions landmark survival analysis and sensitivity analysis, but does not specify enough to make the approach operational, and the validation plan excludes delayed labels without addressing the resulting selection bias. The answer is therefore directionally thoughtful, but falls short on cohort accounting and executable, leakage-safe implementation.
