7.5/10 — The answer follows the required structure and gives a strong, grounded simulation design. It addresses non-stationary arrivals, calendars, rework, cross-case effects, missing objection data, intervention comparisons, uncertainty, validation, and the separation between PM4Py analytics and simulation.

The main weaknesses are material. The arrival model’s architect factor multiplies a rate based on an `architect_id` that the arrival generator does not yet generate, so it does not specify a coherent burst process. The proposed 90-day warm-up and “long-run equilibrium” initialization are poorly suited to this finite-horizon, seasonal permit system. The PM4Py skeleton also risks incompatibility: it passes an event log to `discover_dfg` and `discover_performance_dfg`, whose common conventions expect a DataFrame, and the process-tree fallback silently substitutes a discovered tree for the supplied model. Finally, the validation criteria rely on questionable tests and thresholds, including a KS-test pass-rate target and utilization estimates from event gaps despite missing starts. These issues weaken implementation credibility and calibration rigor, though the broader design is thoughtful.
