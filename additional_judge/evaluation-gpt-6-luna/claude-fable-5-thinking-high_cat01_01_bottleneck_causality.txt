## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: mainly confounded. Confidence: 82/100.** Orion’s crude mean cycle time is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, a 6.72-hour gap. But Orion receives a markedly different mix: 42.9% high-complexity cases versus 12.5%, and 92.9% night cases versus 25%. Within each observed complexity stratum, Orion’s mean is lower: by 3.43 hours for high, 2.14 for medium, and 2.14 for low complexity. That reversal is strong evidence that the overall gap reflects composition and operating conditions. It is not causal identification: cells are small, shift and group scarcely overlap, and the three-level complexity label may hide severity.

The data do not rule out group effects through queueing, service, or rework. Orion’s staffing, night coverage, and assignment to difficult work form a bundle; the observed group label is partly a proxy for routing and operating conditions. The proposal to move 50% of Orion cases may reduce delays if Nova has spare capacity and can handle those cases. It could fail or backfire if the transferred cases exceed Nova’s expertise or increase its queues, especially for high-complexity work. The safer course is a limited, monitored pilot with eligibility rules, capacity checks, and speed and quality measures, while creating overlap between groups and shifts.

The aggregate gap reverses within each observed complexity stratum, a Simpson-like pattern consistent with case-mix confounding. The standardized estimate still favors Nova by about 2.09 hours, but it uses small cells and does not adjust for shift, load, residual severity, or selection. Within complexity, Orion’s mean wait is lower by roughly 1.6–3.3 hours, while service means are close; this does not establish a group effect because case composition and operating conditions remain entangled.

Trace variants are similar: both groups include compliance checks and Request Info rework. Orion has more cases with those paths largely because its mix is more complex. Without event timestamps, traces cannot show which step consumes time.

## 5. Causal estimands and identification

The **total effect** of assigning an eligible future case to Orion rather than Nova would compare cycle time under each assignment, allowing queueing, service, compliance execution, and rework to take their natural values under that assignment. The supplied data do not identify it: routing is selective, case severity is incompletely measured, shift support is poor, and only completed cases are observed.

A **controlled direct effect** would compare group assignments while fixing mediators such as queue wait or review service. That answers a narrower mechanism question, not whether reassignment improves overall cycle time. Adjusting for observed wait and service in a regression would not automatically identify it: both are post-assignment, measured imperfectly, and affected by workload and case risk.

A **mediated effect** through queueing or service asks how much of any assignment effect operates through those pathways. The current aggregate wait combines queueing and compliance-related waiting; service time aggregates repeat reviews. Thus the mediator measurements are insufficient to separate mechanisms reliably.

The proposed policy estimand is the effect of routing half of future Orion-eligible work to Nova under the resulting workload distribution. It is not the same as an individual-level Orion-versus-Nova contrast: reassignment changes both groups’ queues and may change which cases each can handle.

## 6. Process and operational interpretation

Longer observed cycle time can arise from queueing after classification, waits around compliance, review service, and repeated review after Request Info. These mechanisms imply different actions. More Orion night work and high arrival load could raise queue waits; higher certification could improve handling of difficult cases. The within-complexity descriptive pattern—lower Orion wait and cycle time, with similar service time—does not suggest that Orion is slower in these data, but the cells cannot isolate a group effect.

Compliance is primarily associated with high complexity, so its greater frequency in Orion may add process time without reflecting inefficient execution. Rework is somewhat more common overall in Orion, but within strata the rates are similar or lower; neither comparison establishes a quality mechanism. Appeals occur once per group, and the 30-day measure may be affected by factors after closure. Faster completion should therefore be monitored alongside a meaningful quality measure.

Selection into the completed-case sample matters: group-specific long-running or abandoned cases are absent. If group, case risk, or duration affects completion, observed means can be biased in either direction. Management should not infer that moving cases will reproduce the observed Nova mean, because Nova’s future queue and case mix would change.

## 7. Recommended analysis and intervention

1. **Run a bounded pilot on eligible, comparable cases.** Randomize or rotate a modest share between groups within complexity, shift, and load strata where both groups can plausibly handle the work. Keep high-risk or certification-required cases out unless Nova has the relevant capability. Monitor total time, queue time, review service, rework, appeal or overturn, and open-case age. Use a prespecified stop rule for quality deterioration or worsening queues.

2. **Create overlap before estimating group effects.** Arrange limited cross-shift coverage or a Nova night pod, with comparable eligibility in both groups. Current shift-by-group support is too sparse to distinguish shift from group.

3. **Record pre-routing risk and eligibility.** Add granular case severity fields and the routing rule’s inputs, eligible groups, assignment, and any override. Analyze assignment with a target trial design or, if randomized assignment is infeasible, adjust only for pre-assignment variables with demonstrated overlap.

4. **Capture event timestamps and all cases.** Record queue entry/exit, compliance start/completion, each review, Request Info, and closure. Include open and abandoned cases in time-to-event analysis, treating completion as an event and other dispositions explicitly. This separates queue, compliance, and service mechanisms and avoids completed-case-only comparisons.

5. **Report uncertainty and support.** Show cell sizes, overlap, confidence intervals, and sensitivity to residual severity and selection. Do not present within-stratum differences as causal estimates.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Within-label case severity.** The routing engine selects difficult cases, and the three-level complexity label may hide residual severity. Most valuable addition: a granular pre-routing risk score, including claim amount, policy type, and fraud flags.

2. **Shift–group collinearity.** With only one Orion day case and four Nova night cases, shift and group effects are difficult to separate. Most valuable change: limited cross-shift staffing to create genuine overlap.

3. **Completed-case survivorship.** Long-running open or abandoned cases are invisible and may differ by group. Most valuable addition: a snapshot of open and abandoned cases with age, group, complexity, and current activity.

4. **Composition of wait.** Recorded wait mixes queueing with time around compliance. Most valuable addition: timestamps for queue entry and exit and compliance start and completion.

5. **Weak quality signal.** Two appeals and a 30-day horizon contaminated by post-process factors cannot establish a speed-quality trade-off. Most valuable addition: longer-horizon appeal or overturn outcomes and payment-accuracy audit results.
