The answer recognizes the central problem—default is observed only for activated accounts—and correctly avoids labeling rejected or withdrawn applications as non-default. It also proposes useful alternatives, discusses policy-driven selection and deployment feedback, and gives a substantial implementation sketch. But several statistical and implementation errors undermine the proposed primary analysis.

- **The primary target is not fully coherent.** The answer calls this a target for “every application,” but its population is applications that reach the first Credit Review landmark. It also combines process disposition within 60 days with default among eventual activations, without clearly defining how activations after that window or applications with competing outcomes before activation enter the joint estimand. The product formula is only valid when both probabilities refer to compatible event definitions and horizons; those conditions are not established.

- **The cohort table overstates what the snapshot tells us.** It classifies all 3,600 activated accounts without mature labels as a single bucket, then implies that those with activation age over 125 days have permanently missing feeds. The snapshot gives no breakdown of the 3,600 by maturity, feed delay, or permanent missingness. The assumed 125-day rule does not establish that a feed is permanently missing, especially when the stated delay is common rather than a guaranteed maximum. Transfer cases likewise cannot simply be treated as competing outcomes if the study’s outcome follow-up may continue on another platform.

- **The censoring plan conflates distinct mechanisms.** IPCW for “right-censoring and missing outcome feeds” is not justified merely by naming observed predictors. Accounts without a label because the feed is delayed are not necessarily censored at the same time as accounts whose feed is permanently missing, and the latter may be MNAR. The answer acknowledges this uncertainty but still presents IPCW as the primary correction without sufficiently specifying censoring times, weights, or assumptions. It also treats ordinary pre-activation censoring and post-activation outcome censoring as if they fit neatly into the same analysis.

- **The temporal validation plan contains inconsistent cutoffs.** It proposes a training cutoff at 2025-06-30, yet labels for cases in the stated training window may not have matured by then. Later it proposes a validation window through 2025-08-15 “evaluated as of” 2025-12-31, then an expanded training window that includes early post-policy cases. The final test spans the migration, but does not clearly define when model fitting, tuning, recalibration, and label observation occur for each split. The asserted 140-day start-to-label buffer also depends on activation time and cannot be treated as a fixed maturity guarantee.

- **The code does not implement several claims safely.** It takes the first repeated label fields without ensuring they are available as of the cutoff, and `label_available_at` is not masked by the cutoff before grouping. It drops the label columns from the feature table, but merges the full case table back into the dataset, so the later feature-only assertion does not prove the training matrix is leakage-free. The fixed-length prefix extraction is not explicitly constrained to events at or before the prediction cutoff or to cases active at that point, and the validity filter can fail to exclude short traces as intended depending on PM4Py’s prefix output. Dynamic resource experience is calculated over the full dataset before splitting, allowing future events and test cases to affect it. The deduplication rule can also remove legitimate repeated activities. These are material defects in a skeleton whose purpose is to enforce as-of and leakage controls.

Overall, the answer has sound instincts and broad coverage, but the mismatched estimands, unsupported missing-feed classifications, unclear censoring assumptions, validation inconsistencies, and leakage vulnerabilities prevent it from being a defensible study design as written.
