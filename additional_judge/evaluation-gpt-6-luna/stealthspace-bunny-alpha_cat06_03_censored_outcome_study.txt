The answer is thoughtful about selection through activation, target semantics, leakage, and temporal validation, but several errors undermine its proposed analysis and implementation.

- **The primary target and prediction point are misaligned.** It frames the model as an early warning model, then scores at activation plus 24 hours. That may be defensible as an onboarding model, but it is not prediction from the first few application events. The answer should make that change of business question explicit and provide a primary design that supports the requested early application prefix.
- **The cohort flow is not fully reconciled with the supplied counts.** It labels 2,900 transfers as having no observed subsequent activation, though the prompt does not establish that. More seriously, it says activated rows total 25,200, “not 27,300”; 27,300 is not the sum of the provided activated counts. The answer also treats 21,600 labeled accounts as “activated, 90-day label reported” without establishing that all are mature, feed-complete outcomes.
- **The censoring strategy is not sufficiently coherent.** It proposes inverse-probability weighting and complete-follow-up criteria, but does not specify the observation model, time origin, risk set, or how weights address delayed labels and partner feeds that may be permanently missing. It also conflates an event-history endpoint of 31 December 2025 with outcome-feed availability at 15 February 2026. A recorded default may have occurred by the horizon but arrived later; the proposed rules do not clearly reconcile that case.
- **The code has multiple execution and validity problems.** `g["activation"]` is accessed on `train`, which comes from `df` and has no such column. `pm4py.get_case_prefix` is used without justification and may not be an appropriate PM4Py API for the stated version. The prefix loop’s unpacking and mutation pattern is fragile, and `train` is assigned from the raw event table even though later logic expects case-level fields. The hard `assert len(df) == 48000` is unjustified after event filtering and conflicts with the fact that the CSV is one row per event, not one row per application. The row-level label extraction also does not establish feed completeness, while labels may be duplicated across events.
- **The fixed and elapsed prefixes do not answer the same prediction question.** The elapsed prefix is cut at activation plus 24 hours, which necessarily uses activation to set the boundary; that is acceptable for the stated landmark model, but it does not demonstrate an application-stage elapsed-time prefix. The proposed fixed prefix is also built only for eligible training cases, limiting its usefulness as a coherent cohort feature pipeline.
- **The validation description overstates what the proposed test can establish.** The Q3 2025 test crosses the policy change, while the shadow period is not mature. The answer acknowledges this, but does not define a clean post-policy, mature-outcome evaluation or a clear backtest that respects when labels would actually have been available.

The response earns credit for refusing to code rejected or unresolved applications as non-default, distinguishing activation-conditioned risk from counterfactual risk, and auditing many key leakage fields. The substantial target mismatch, flawed cohort assumptions, and broken implementation skeleton prevent a higher grade.
