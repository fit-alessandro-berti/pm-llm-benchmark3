**8.0/10.** The answer is unusually careful about selective outcome observation, competing outcomes, censoring, leakage, temporal validation, and limits on counterfactual claims. It follows the nine-section structure, accounts arithmetically for all 48,000 cases, and provides a detailed PM4Py-oriented skeleton. Its main weakness is that several parts of the proposed primary estimand and implementation do not fully align, which matters for an evaluation that asks for statistical precision.

The largest issue is the six-outcome target: `O` is defined as no disposition by day 180, but the proposed factorization assigns `p_O` as the residual after subtracting activation and terminal-event incidences. That residual also includes cases whose first disposition occurs after day 180, unless the competing-risk model explicitly treats post-horizon dispositions as outside the cumulative incidence—which the answer does not clearly specify. More significantly, activation is called a first disposition, while the described process places acceptance and contract signing before activation; the target rules out other listed outcomes before activation but does not fully specify how a rejection, withdrawal, or transfer after acceptance but before activation is ordered. The answer acknowledges reopened and multiple-activation exceptions only later.

There are also implementation concerns. The skeleton’s `case_table` uses `drop_duplicates(C)` on disposition events rather than selecting the earliest event, so competing-event assignment can depend on input row order. It marks repeated timestamps across any events as ambiguous, which is conservative but may quarantine many legitimate simultaneous events. Its `observed90` logic checks that a label is available after the horizon and by the cutoff, but does not explicitly check `label_available_at <= cutoff`; the preceding filter does, so this is covered in the present code. More substantively, the skeleton does not actually build the stated competing-risk/observation-weighted estimators or a complete six-class target from censored histories; those remain comments, and some `snapshot_group` labels are not horizon-aware. The validation schedule is thoughtful, but its proposed test period cannot support mature evaluation for most post-change cases, a limitation the answer correctly admits.

The answer is also much longer than needed, and its code is near or beyond the requested 80–160-line range depending on how comments and blank lines are counted. These shortcomings keep it below a near-flawless score, despite its strong statistical judgment and unusually explicit treatment of uncertainty.
