The response has a sound central boundary: it rejects treating rejected, withdrawn, and open applications as non-defaults, and limits its main claim to default among activated accounts. It also identifies important leakage risks, proposes as-of feature rules, and recognizes policy-driven selection and deployment feedback.

However, several required pieces are incomplete or internally inconsistent:

- The cohort-flow table is cut off and does not clearly account for all 48,000 cases. The omitted categories sum to 26,400, but the response does not reconcile the 3,600 immature activated accounts with the other groups or state unambiguously how transfers are counted.
- It says to model activation selection with weights or a joint model, yet gives no defined estimand or credible way to identify activation probabilities for rejected applicants. Weighting cannot recover their counterfactual default outcomes without stronger assumptions and adequate overlap.
- The primary strategy is unclear: it names both cause-specific Cox and Fine–Gray models, while the target is a fixed 90-day binary outcome among activated accounts. It does not adequately specify how delayed and permanently missing feeds enter that analysis.
- The validation maturity rule is not coherent with the stated 90-day horizon plus 7–35 days for feed arrival. The proposed activation cutoff of 15 November 2025 does not ensure the slowest feeds have matured by the 15 February 2026 extraction date.
- The code does not meet the requested implementation standard. It uses questionable or unsupported PM4Py APIs, has flawed label-maturity and activation aggregation logic, and does not implement the promised as-of prefix feature guard. It also does not show a complete, reliable cohort construction or usable fixed-count and elapsed-time prefix extraction pipeline.

These gaps undermine the statistical design and the requested PM4Py-oriented skeleton, despite good leakage awareness and a defensible high-level target choice.
