## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

CS-9 is controlled, formally approved, and effective throughout the cases. It requires sanctions and quality checks before activation, manual approval before every activation, and different humans as requester and manual approver. M0 expresses much of the control flow, but not the identity rule, conflict-of-interest disclosure, or the later exceptions.

PD-3 records a time-limited pilot exception from June 1 through July 31, 2026. It permits qualifying low-risk German suppliers in approved corporate groups to use Auto_Approve, with sanctions and automated master-data validation before activation and quality review within two business days after provisional activation. The record supports the existence and terms of the pilot; the Compliance observer’s statement does not establish that she had authority to amend CS-9. The evidence does not settle whether the steering committee had authority to grant this exception under the organization’s governance rules.

FD-7 is an emergency directive with a defined scope and automatic expiry at the end of July 12. It permits provisional activation before quality review and manual approval, subject to sanctions clearance, a EUR 20,000 spend cap pending retrospective approval, daily monitoring, and quality review within two business days.

M0 treats sanctions and quality as concurrent checks that must both precede the decision. It does not require one check to precede the other. It also has no route for pilot or emergency processing, no Request_More_Info loop, and no explicit procedures for rejected leads or reactivations. The CRM migration weakens the onboarding log as evidence of notification: the stated 70% mirroring rate makes an absent log event insufficient, by itself, to prove that no notification occurred.

For a noncompliance finding, require an applicable rule in force at the time, a case within its scope, reliable evidence of the relevant event and conditions, and no established authorized exception. Use a higher evidentiary bar for severe findings. For an exception, the operator invoking it should establish the authority, effective period, eligibility, and required safeguards. Missing records may leave a proposition unproved; they should not automatically be treated as proof that an event did not happen.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

CS-9 remains the controlling baseline. Operational convenience, a desirable outcome, or a belief that a permanent amendment is imminent does not change a formally approved standard. PD-3 is limited in scope and duration; its documented terms do not support applying the pilot to high-risk foreign suppliers or continuing it after July 31. FD-7 is similarly narrow and expired automatically. Continuing the factual flood response does not extend its authority.

The strongest evidence of material failures is not simply that traces differ from M0:

- **SUP-703** used Auto_Approve for a high-risk new foreign supplier, although the pilot was limited to low-risk German suppliers in an approved group. Its EUR 180,000 first purchase reinforces the seriousness of the eligibility failure.
- **SUP-708** activated a high-risk supplier before sanctions clearance and before manual approval, with no pilot or emergency exception. The absence of transactions before sanctions clearance may reduce realized harm but does not cure the control breach.
- **SUP-705** used the emergency sequence after FD-7 had expired, with no written extension.
- **SUP-712** violates CS-9’s separation rule if the stated alias resolution to one human is reliable, despite the trace fitting M0.
- **SUP-714** used Auto_Approve after PD-3 expired. Operations’ expectation of a permanent amendment is not authority.
- **SUP-715** fits M0’s event order but lacks the disclosure required by the conflict-of-interest policy.

Aggregate evidence makes these more than isolated anomalies. Of 312 June and July Auto_Approve cases, 14 are clearly ineligible and nine lack eligibility attributes. Six more used Auto_Approve in the first week of August, after the pilot ended. The 47 Request_More_Info cases, none of which activated before the requested evidence arrived, do not establish misconduct, but they do show that actual practice exceeds M0.

The prosecution should not claim that every M0 deviation is a policy violation. It must account for the explicit pilot and emergency windows, the CRM evidence, and the known duplicate event. Those defects narrow the case for enforcement; they do not authorize ineligible or expired exceptions.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is an incomplete representation of the process, and a raw trace comparison overstates the number of meaningful violations. It omits acknowledged Request_More_Info cycles, lacks policy-overlay routes, has no resource constraints, and does not define whether rejected leads and reactivations belong to the modeled population.

The strongest defense cases are:

- **SUP-702** meets the stated PD-3 eligibility conditions, has sanctions before activation, and had quality review the next business day. A stable CRM notification ID supplies evidence that notification occurred despite its absence from the trace.
- **SUP-704** fits FD-7’s scope and date window. Sanctions preceded activation, spend was below the cap, monitoring existed, and quality review and approval followed the next business day.
- **SUP-707** includes extra information requests that obtained missing certificates before approval. This is an unmodeled, desirable process step, not evidence of control evasion.
- **SUP-711** contains two register rows with the same source transaction ID, supporting a migration duplicate rather than a second real registration.
- **SUP-713** uses QA_Clearance, an approved renamed procedure with equivalent audit content. The label mismatch should not be mistaken for failure to perform quality review.
- **SUP-709** may be an intake pre-screen outside M0’s scope; the scope document is ambiguous.
- **SUP-710** concerns reactivation, for which CS-9 supplies no explicit procedure, and prior certification remained valid.
- **SUP-706** has CRM notification evidence, but its mutable, late-entered link does not reliably establish which case received the message.

The defense is right that trace deviation is not synonymous with business misconduct. Yet it cannot turn a time-limited pilot into permanent policy, expand eligibility by operator choice, or carry FD-7 past its expiry. The evidence for SUP-703, SUP-708, and post-pilot auto-approval remains serious even after recognizing the model and data defects.

At system level, the operation is **materially noncompliant in specific, consequential routes**, but the evidence does not support treating every deviation from M0 as misconduct. SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-705, SUP-712, and SUP-714 establish distinct failures; the 14 clearly ineligible pilot cases and six post-pilot cases reinforce the conclusion. The nine cases with missing eligibility attributes remain unproved, not automatically noncompliant.

M0 is still a useful baseline for standard control flow, but it is not a valid complete representation of current practice or scope. Its concurrency is appropriate; its omissions include the information-request loop, explicit exception routes, resource controls, label mapping, reactivation and intake boundaries, and the changed notification evidence path. The increase in missing notifications from 2% to 29% after CRM migration materially weakens log-only conformance claims.

I reject the prosecution’s broadest possible claim that deviations alone prove widespread misconduct: several are justified exceptions, scope questions, or known data/model defects. I reject the defense’s broadest claim that obsolescence or normalized practice excuses clear control failures: neither can make an ineligible pilot case eligible, extend FD-7, or authorize permanent Auto_Approve.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

Use a layered model with a formally controlled baseline and explicitly governed overlays.

- **POWL control flow:** Model the standard route as registration, concurrent sanctions and quality checks, then an exclusive decision between manual approval followed by activation and rejection. Add separately identifiable pilot and emergency routes, a repeatable information-request mechanism before the decision, and a common notification-before-closure tail.
- **DECLARE or log-skeleton constraints:** Enforce sanctions-before-activation on every route; manual-approval-before-activation on the standard route; response to requested information before activation; and notification-before-closure. Express time-bounded obligations such as quality review within two business days where route and policy conditions select that requirement.
- **Data checks:** Evaluate risk, country, group membership, master-data validation, policy effective dates, spend caps, elapsed business time, notification IDs, event timestamps, actor aliases, and delegation evidence from immutable snapshots.
- **Resource and conflict controls:** Enforce distinct requester and manual approver identities. Require disclosure and review of conflicts, including the reviewer-approver relationship. Do not infer that a relationship is prohibited unless the governing policy says so.
- **Policy-overlay governance:** Give every pilot or emergency exception an authority, version, scope, start and end time, eligibility conditions, monitoring obligations, retrospective-approval requirements, and immutable effective and record timestamps. Expired overlays must fail closed. Permanent low-risk auto-approval requires a formal CS-9 amendment.
- **Scope rules:** Put rejected-lead pre-screening in a separate intake model if it is outside onboarding; define when a lead enters the CS-9 process. Give reactivation its own route, including registration of reactivation, sanctions, material-change and risk checks, certification validity, and required approval. Define withdrawals as a no-activation path ending in notification and closure.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def t(label):
    return Transition(label=label)


# Shared case start and closeout.
register = t("Register_Supplier")
notify = t("Notify_Requester")
close = t("Close_Case")

# Approximate a repeatable information-request cycle. In this representation,
# the loop is a subprocess that can repeat before the route decision. Exact
# interleaving of requests with concurrent checks and evidence-response rules
# should be enforced with log-skeleton/DECLARE constraints.
rmi = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[t("Request_More_Info"), SilentTransition()],
)

# Standard route: sanctions and quality are concurrent and both precede
# the decision; the decision is manual approval then activation, or rejection.
std_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[t("Sanctions_Check"), t("Quality_Review"), rmi])
std_manual_activate = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[t("Manual_Procurement_Approval"), t("Activate_Supplier")]
)
std_manual_activate.order.add_edge(
    std_manual_activate.nodes[0], std_manual_activate.nodes[1]
)
std_decision = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[std_manual_activate, t("Reject_Supplier")],
)
std_route = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_checks, std_decision])
std_route.order.add_edge(std_checks, std_decision)

# Pilot route: data eligibility and time conditions are checked outside POWL.
pilot = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        t("Sanctions_Check"),
        t("Automated_Master_Data_Validation"),
        t("Auto_Approve"),
        t("Activate_Supplier"),
        t("Quality_Review"),
    ]
)
pilot.order.add_edge(pilot.nodes[0], pilot.nodes[2])
pilot.order.add_edge(pilot.nodes[1], pilot.nodes[2])
pilot.order.add_edge(pilot.nodes[2], pilot.nodes[3])
pilot.order.add_edge(pilot.nodes[3], pilot.nodes[4])

# Emergency route: authority, scope, amount cap, monitoring, and time window
# are data/organizational checks. Quality and retrospective manual approval
# may follow provisional activation.
emergency_post = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[t("Quality_Review"), t("Manual_Procurement_Approval")]
)
emergency = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[t("Sanctions_Check"), t("Activate_Supplier"), emergency_post]
)
emergency.order.add_edge(emergency.nodes[0], emergency.nodes[1])
emergency.order.add_edge(emergency.nodes[1], emergency.nodes[2])

routes = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[std_route, pilot, emergency, t("Reject_Supplier")],
)
model = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[register, routes, notify, close])
model.order.add_edge(register, routes)
model.order.add_edge(routes, notify)
model.order.add_edge(notify, close)

print(model)
```

The POWL is a control-flow approximation. It does not enforce eligibility, identity, policy authority, amount caps, business-day deadlines, or whether an event is provisional. The code also uses a common rejection option in the route choice for compactness; a production model should separate intake pre-screen rejection from standard onboarding rejection so that quality-check obligations are unambiguous. The RMI loop is likewise an approximation; enforce “requested evidence received before activation” with data-aware constraints.

| Overlay | Authority and version | Scope and effective period | Control-flow effect | Data conditions | Monitoring and retrospective approval |

| CS-9 baseline | Compliance, CS-9 | All defined onboarding; effective 2025-01-01 onward | Standard checks and manual route | Sanctions and quality before activation; requester differs from approver | Standard audit trail |
| PD-3 pilot | Steering committee, PD-3 | Low-risk DE suppliers in approved groups; 2026-06-01 through 2026-07-31 | Auto_Approve instead of manual; quality may follow provisional activation | Eligibility snapshot; master-data validation and sanctions before activation; quality within two business days | Monitored exception; retain authority and eligibility evidence |
| FD-7 emergency | Emergency authority, FD-7 | Critical relief suppliers; 2026-07-10 00:00 through 2026-07-12 23:59 | Provisional activation may precede quality and manual approval | Sanctions first; spend cap EUR 20,000 until retrospective approval; quality within two business days | Daily monitoring and retrospective approval; automatic expiry |
| Permanent auto | Requires formal CS-9 amendment | Not currently approved | No permanent auto route until amendment | Formally enacted eligibility and safeguards | Defined by amendment |

Collect `case_id`, activity and event time, separate record time, source system, immutable source record ID and event hash; `rule_version_id`, `policy_overlay_id`, authority ID and immutable effective/record timestamps; stable `notification_id`, channel and status; an eligibility snapshot ID with risk, country, group, approval status and master-data validation; amount, currency, cap and retrospective-approval ID; and actor ID, actor alias, role, delegation ID and delegation evidence. Retain conflict-disclosure IDs and reviewer-approver relationships. Resolve aliases through an authoritative identity source and preserve the resolution evidence used at decision time.
