## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 protects against one human both initiating and authorizing a payment. Its control objective includes preventing unilateral error or fraud, maintaining independent review, catching defects before funds become irrevocable, and preserving a path to recover funds. The compensating controls cover different parts of that objective:

| Objective or threat | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Prevent an individual from initiating and authorizing an improper payment | Can block specified anomalies, but does not ensure another human reviews the payment | Can provide independent review if qualified, conflict free, and timely | Detects after release; may support recovery if timely | Permits same-operator handling only when all listed conditions hold; does not remove the underlying risk |
| Detect duplicate, sanctions, account, limit, or basic PO mismatch | Prevents or warns on these covered conditions; warnings may be overridden | May detect if evidence is actually reviewed | Can detect later | Requires a full pass without override |
| Detect missing receipt or commercially unreasonable payment | Does not address these | Can detect when supporting receipt and invoice are reviewed | May detect later | Requires MG-4 review, but does not itself validate receipt |
| Resist collusion or compromised credentials | Does not establish who controlled a compromised account or detect collusion | Reduced if approver is conflicted or review is rubber-stamped | May reveal patterns, but is retrospective | Requires an independent manager; does not guarantee collusion resistance |
| Stop settlement or recover funds after a defect | Blocks covered conditions before release | Can prevent settlement if approval precedes settlement | Can support recall if audit is timely; recovery becomes uncertain after 24 hours | Requires approval before settlement and timely audit |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Assess a package against the threat it is meant to control, using these dimensions:

- **Timing:** A preventive barrier must act before the relevant commitment. Approval after release but before settlement may prevent settlement, even though release has already occurred.
- **Independence:** A reviewer must be a separate human with no unresolved conflict. A supervisor who controls a preparer’s performance rating may not be independent.
- **Coverage:** The controls must address the plausible defect. AV-2 covers specified data checks, not receipt, commercial reasonableness, or collusion.
- **Overrideability:** A warning that the operator can override is weaker than a block. A clean pass is evidence only for checks actually performed.
- **Recoverability:** Retrospective review is partly corrective only if it enables a stop, recall, or recovery. Audit after the usual 24-hour recall window offers much less corrective capacity.
- **Evidence quality:** A logged approval is not enough if it was rubber-stamped or lacked supporting evidence. Missing logs or unclear sequencing reduce confidence.
- **Collusion resistance:** Separate users do not ensure independent judgment if they routinely work as a pair or share a workstation.

A package is **fully equivalent** when it provides timely, independent protection across the relevant threats, with reliable evidence and adequate recovery for residual defects. It is **partially equivalent** when it provides meaningful coverage but leaves material gaps. It is **merely mitigating** when it lowers expected loss without providing adequate prevention or recovery. It is **ineffective** when its gaps, conflicts, overrides, or timing leave the relevant threat substantially uncontrolled.

Same expected loss alone is insufficient: a control can reduce financial loss while failing to preserve independent authorization, and the estimate may omit integrity, fraud, or governance consequences.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control or timing | Primary role | What it can establish | Main limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| AV-2 full pass before release | Preventive for covered checks | Blocks or clears duplicate, sanctions, bank, limit, and basic PO checks | Does not establish receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion resistance, or account control |
| AV-2 warning with override | Warning and possible mitigation | Surfaces a covered anomaly | Override can defeat the barrier; reviewer independence matters |
| AV-2 block followed by cancellation | Preventive | Prevents the payment from completing | Does not establish that other attempted payments are safe |
| MG-4 before release | Preventive | Independent review can stop an improper release | Depends on reviewer independence and evidence quality |
| MG-4 after release but before settlement | Hybrid | Detects a problem in an already released payment and can prevent settlement | Does not prevent the release event; protection depends on settlement being held |
| MG-4 after settlement | Detective | May identify a defect | Usually cannot prevent the transfer; recovery is uncertain |
| RA-7 within one business day | Detective, potentially corrective | Finds defects and can support recall within the usual window | Audit timing alone does not prove recall or recovery |
| RA-7 after the recall window | Detective | Identifies defects for follow-up and remediation | Little immediate corrective capacity for the settled payment |
| ED-1 | Conditional permission | Defines conditions under which same-operator handling may be used | Does not make the four-eyes objective disappear; failed conditions leave the exception unjustified |

Here, “preventive” is measured against the outcome of an irrevocable settlement. A manager can therefore act as a final preventive barrier to settlement after release, while also detecting an issue in the release decision.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

Collect timestamped, attributable records for preparation, release, validation, override, manager review, settlement, audit completion, and recall attempts. Preserve evidence reviewed by the manager, including invoices, bank details, and receipt or service records. Record whether a payment was held pending approval.

Monitor:

- **Same-user rate:** Track completed payments with identical preparer and releaser, separating authorized ED-1 cases from other cases.
- **Override rate:** Track warnings, overrides, the credential used, and any independent approval.
- **Late manager approval:** Flag approval after release and separately flag approval after settlement.
- **Audit before recall window:** Compare audit start and completion with release and settlement, and record whether recall was attempted or successful.
- **Repeated resource pairs:** Identify unusually frequent preparer/releaser pairs and investigate patterns such as u12/u13.
- **Shared workstations:** Correlate workstation use with repeated pairs and unusual approvals.
- **Bank-change proximity:** Flag changes within seven days and verify bank ownership independently before release.
- **Control outages:** Record AV-2 availability, fallback checks, responsible reviewers, and any payments released during an outage.
- **Emergency-token sequencing:** Verify the token was active before preparation and retain system timestamps to distinguish late creation from logging delay.

These indicators support investigation; an alert, repeated pairing, or shared workstation is not proof of misconduct.
