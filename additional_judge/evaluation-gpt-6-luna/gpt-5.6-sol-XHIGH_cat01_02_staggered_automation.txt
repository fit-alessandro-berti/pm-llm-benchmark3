The answer is broad and attentive to key identification issues: endogenous rollout, changing treatment intensity, shared-queue spillovers, small-cluster uncertainty, survey nonresponse, and the distinction between median and tail performance. It also proposes a useful embedded randomized rollout and supplies self-contained code that validates the panel, prints trends, computes contrasts, and labels its regressions as descriptive.

The main weakness is substantial quantitative sign and reporting errors in Section 5. Improvements in cycle time and reductions in rework are repeatedly shown with positive signs even though the stated DiD definition is treated change minus control change. The Alpha/Beta CSAT DiD is also reported as +0.087, though the listed changes imply about −0.082. Several intensity-table entries are malformed or omit signs, and the sensitivity ranges inherit ambiguous signs. These errors undermine the answer’s central effect assessment and final claim ladder.

The primary strategy is described but not actually estimated; the supplied event-time regression pools cohorts and uses already-treated branches in later comparisons, despite the warning that this is not a substitute for cohort-specific group-time estimates. Overall, the causal reasoning and policy design are strong, but the arithmetic and presentation errors are too material for a higher grade.
