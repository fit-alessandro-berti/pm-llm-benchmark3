4.0 — The answer follows the requested six-section structure and correctly excludes I-03 because the swarm is no longer effective after diagnosis. It also distinguishes I-05’s unchanged repeated alert from I-07’s new log, and it recognizes calibration differences, spillovers, fairness risks, and selective labels.

The core allocation arithmetic is unreliable. The stated score formula is ambiguous and inconsistently calculated: it multiplies adjusted breach probability by the effect estimate, then subtracts costs, but several displayed scores do not match that formula. More seriously, its “direct economic benefit” table mostly sums case values as if each selected case’s breach were avoided. The supplied effect ranges are probabilities of risk reduction, so benefit should be based on avoided-breach probability times case value. The arithmetic note briefly recognizes this, then contradicts it by “scaling up for simplicity.” The “direct breaches plausibly avoided” counts are similarly unsupported, and the spillover estimate assumes an average case value without establishing that it represents the six affected cases or accounting for overlap across slots.

Other material problems weaken the policy. It rejects I-09 despite listing a score of €30,000, without a coherent uncertainty adjustment or a comparison against selected cases. It uses arbitrary probability corrections as if they were calibrated case-level estimates, and the Strategic Enterprise adjustment makes I-04/I-08 appear less risky without discussing the uncertainty of applying a segment-wide correction to individual cases. It claims the regulatory-incident risk is low despite being given no probability, and overrides management’s reserve preference without a defensible contingency rule. Its proposed random withholding of 10% of eligible interventions does not address ethical or operational constraints, and fairness safeguards such as manual approval for Non-Native-Language cases and a lower-bound effect cutoff could themselves create unequal access. Finally, several thresholds and monitoring triggers are arbitrary, and the plan does not clearly separate causal-effect estimation from predictive calibration or define how spillovers will be attributed.

These flaws undermine the answer’s decision-theoretic coherence, especially the portfolio choice and scenario analysis. It earns credit for broad coverage and some sound process-stage and repeat-alert judgments, but the impact calculations and allocation logic fall well short of a reliable, auditable policy.
