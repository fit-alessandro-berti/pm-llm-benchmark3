7/10 — The response applies a risk-based, case-by-case approach and recognizes that a logged control is not necessarily sufficient. It discusses control timing, audit delays, the AV-2 override and outage, late emergency-token creation, and the repeated actor pair; it also distinguishes risk indicators from proof of misconduct.

The main weakness is that its case verdicts and ED-1 analysis are not fully reliable. In particular, manager ratification before settlement is treated as potentially compensating even though ED-1 requires approval before settlement and MG-4 is strongest before release. The response also gives too much weight to automated validation and manager review where the facts leave independence or control quality unresolved. The included checking code has misleading shortcuts, including treating event rows as case summaries and deriving bank-change status from a broad vendor-risk label.
