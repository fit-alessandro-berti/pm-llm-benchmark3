## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: not identifiable from the supplied evidence. Confidence: 84/100** in that evidentiary judgment, not in a claim that Orion has no effect.

The strongest evidence against interpreting Orion’s higher overall mean as harm is the reversal within every observed complexity stratum: Orion’s mean cycle time is lower than Nova’s for low, medium, and high complexity. The overall means are 21.96 hours for Orion and 15.24 for Nova, while Orion has far more high-complexity cases (42.9% vs 12.5%), night work (92.9% vs 25.0%), and required compliance checks (64.3% vs 25.0%). This is a pronounced aggregate-versus-stratified reversal, consistent with case mix and operating conditions explaining much of the crude gap.

That pattern does not identify a causal effect. Within-stratum cells are small, especially Nova high complexity and Orion low complexity, and complexity alone does not control residual risk, load, shift, routing policy, or selection into completed cases. The observed lower Orion wait and cycle time within complexity strata is evidence against a simple “Orion is slower” story, but does not establish that reassignment would improve outcomes. Assignment follows classification and is deliberately directed toward difficult cases; group also bundles reviewer skill, shift, and operating context.

A 50% reassignment could help if Nova has spare capacity and can handle the transferred cases efficiently. It could fail or backfire if it overloads Nova, moves night or compliance work into less suitable coverage, or changes review quality and rework. The committee should first test a limited, randomized routing change among cases eligible for either group, with capacity and quality safeguards, and measure queue, service, cycle time, rework, and completion.

The aggregate and within-complexity comparisons point in opposite directions, so the crude group mean is a poor guide to the reassignment decision. The standardized contrast also uses sparse strata and adjusts only for coded complexity; it is not a causal estimate.

A decomposition of cycle time into recorded wait and review service shows that both components are lower for Orion within each complexity stratum. The wait field is not pure queue time: it includes waiting around compliance, and the supplied data do not separate those contributions. Service time includes repeated review, so it can reflect both reviewer speed and rework.

In the full case records, Orion has lower rework prevalence overall than Nova (28.6% vs 18.8%?); this crude comparison actually points the other way. Within complexity strata, rework prevalence is similar or lower for Orion, but cell sizes are small. Appeals occur in one Orion high-complexity case and one Nova low-complexity case. These outcomes do not establish a group quality effect.

## 5. Process-mining interpretation

The traces show the same broad process structure in both groups: Register → Classify → Review → Close, with optional Compliance Check and optional Request Info followed by another Review. This is useful for identifying pathway variation, but the traces contain no event timestamps, so they cannot reveal where elapsed time accrued.

Orion’s cases include more compliance checks and more high-complexity cases, while both groups show repeated review paths. A higher frequency of a path may reflect policy-driven case mix rather than a group-created process problem. Process comparison should therefore measure transition rates conditional on pre-routing risk and eligibility, and attach durations to each activity and waiting state.

The provided `wait_before_review_h` mixes queueing after classification with waiting around compliance, while `review_service_h` combines review segments. These fields cannot isolate compliance duration, pure queue time, or first-pass review speed. Event-level timestamps for Classify completion, compliance start/end, queue entry, each review start/end, Request Info, and Close are needed for a performance decomposition.

## 6. Causal estimands and adjustment strategy

A useful primary estimand is the intention-to-treat total effect of routing an eligible case to Orion versus Nova on elapsed time to close, for a defined population of cases eligible for either group under a stated staffing policy. Include time-to-close for all routed cases, treating open cases as censored rather than excluding them. Specify whether the policy includes the staffing and load changes induced by moving cases.

A total effect includes pathways through queueing, review service, compliance handling, rework, and completion. A controlled direct effect would ask about group assignment while holding selected mediators—such as queue wait or service time—fixed; that is a different, more restrictive question and may not be operationally meaningful. A mediated effect through queueing or rework requires stronger assumptions and better timing data than supplied here. “Direct” should not be interpreted as a reviewer-quality effect unless staffing, routing, and process mechanisms are separately defined.

For observational adjustment, use pre-assignment variables only: measured case difficulty/risk, arrival-time load, shift, and policy eligibility for compliance, plus calendar and staffing conditions if collected. Do not adjust for post-assignment wait, service time, rework, or completion when estimating the total effect; those may be mediators or selection variables. Check overlap and report supported populations; the current cross-tabs show limited support, particularly for Orion low complexity, Nova high complexity, and day/night comparisons within groups.

Prefer a randomized routing trial among cases that the committee deems safely assignable to either group, with blocked randomization by pre-routing risk, shift, and load where feasible. Record assignment, actual reviewer, timestamps, capacity, and deviations. Analyze the assignment effect (intention to treat), while monitoring completion, rework, and quality as safeguards. If randomization is infeasible, emulate a target trial from a complete case census using the engine’s pre-routing variables and sensitivity analyses for unmeasured risk; do not treat a complexity-adjusted regression on these 30 completers as decisive.

## 7. Proposed decision and discriminating analyses

Do not reassign 50% of future Orion work solely on the basis of the crude overall mean. First define which cases are eligible for both groups and verify Nova’s capacity and appropriate certification across shifts. Then run a staged, randomized routing pilot on that eligible population, with conservative allocation and prespecified stop criteria for worsening queue time, completion, rework, or quality. Expand only if the results support it and capacity remains adequate.

Collect all routed cases, including still-open and abandoned cases, with assignment time, routing-rule inputs, case-risk features, shift, arrival load, staffing, reviewer identity and certification, compliance start/end, queue entry, each review segment, Request Info, close, and follow-up quality events. Report intention-to-treat effects on time to close and completion, with survival methods for censoring, plus component outcomes for queue, service, rework, and quality.

For observational discrimination, compare groups only in regions of covariate overlap and standardize on pre-routing covariates. Examine within-complexity and within-shift/load contrasts, but show cell counts and uncertainty. Model routing as the treatment and group as the assigned resource when policy is the intervention; evaluate a group label separately only if staffing and operational mechanisms can be defined. Use stratified or blocked randomization to determine whether observed time differences persist under comparable assignment, and test whether any benefit comes through reduced queueing, faster service, fewer repeat reviews, or changed case composition.
