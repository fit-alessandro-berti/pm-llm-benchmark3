## 1. Executive causal assessment

**Provisional verdict: not identifiable from the supplied evidence. Confidence: 82/100.** Orion’s crude mean cycle time is 21.96 hours versus Nova’s 15.24, but Orion receives a much larger share of high-complexity cases (42.9% versus 12.5%), night work (92.9% versus 25%), and cases requiring compliance (64.3% versus 25%). Within each observed complexity stratum, Orion’s mean is lower: 7.50 versus 9.64 hours for low, 17.17 versus 19.38 for medium, and 31.57 versus 35.00 for high. This reversal is consistent with case-mix confounding, but the strata are small, especially Orion low (n=2) and Nova high (n=2), and do not balance shift, load, compliance, reviewer skill, or queue state. It does not identify a causal effect.

A total effect of changing assignment policy could operate through queueing, service time, rework, or quality. A direct effect holding those mechanisms fixed is a different estimand, and these data cannot isolate either. The proposed 50% reassignment may reduce delays if Orion’s cases benefit from Nova’s faster observed queues or if Nova has spare capacity. It could fail or backfire if Nova lacks capacity or expertise for complex night cases, increasing waits, rework, or adverse outcomes. Recommend a limited randomized routing pilot within supported case and shift strata, with capacity and quality safeguards, rather than a blanket transfer.

## 5. Causal analysis and competing explanations

The total effect relevant to management is the change in completed-case cycle time if the assignment policy changes, including induced changes in queueing, service, rework, and potentially quality. A direct group effect holding those pathways fixed asks a narrower question and is not the effect of moving half the work in practice. A mediated interpretation asks whether assignment changes waiting, review effort, or rework, and how much those pathways contribute. The data provide descriptions of these variables, not the temporal or assignment information needed to identify mediation.

The case traces show process variants with optional compliance and optional Request Info followed by repeated review. These paths are not randomly distributed. Compliance is policy-required for some cases, mainly complex ones, so its presence is a marker of both process burden and case mix. Wait before review includes queueing and compliance-related waiting, while review service includes repeat review; neither can be treated as a clean, isolated mechanism from the available fields.

Plausible explanations include:

- **Case-mix and operating-condition confounding:** Orion gets more difficult cases, night work, and compliance cases. Its higher aggregate mean may largely reflect these assignments.
- **A beneficial group mechanism:** Orion’s lower means within complexity strata are consistent with faster handling, perhaps from greater certification or expertise. Small samples and remaining imbalance prevent attributing that pattern to the group.
- **Queueing or capacity effects:** Orion’s observed mean wait is higher, but wait combines multiple sources and differs with load and shift. Redirecting cases could either relieve Orion’s queue or congest Nova’s.
- **Different rework or quality tradeoffs:** Orion has a higher observed rework share, but this may reflect case risk. Appeal counts are sparse, coarse, and potentially affected by factors after closure; speed-only evaluation could miss harm.
- **Completed-case selection:** Open or abandoned cases are absent. If the chance or timing of completion depends on group, risk, or workload, comparisons among completed cases can be biased.

The group label is therefore a treatment only under a defined intervention. In this setting it also proxies reviewer skill and bundles shift coverage, selection rules, and capacity. To discriminate among explanations, log timestamps for every process activity and queue state; reviewer IDs, certification, and workload; richer case-risk features; and all assigned cases including open and abandoned cases. Then compare randomized assignment within feasible complexity-by-shift-by-load blocks, or use a defensible quasi-experiment such as an exogenous routing threshold. Estimate intention-to-treat policy effects first; report overlap and uncertainty. Analyze queue wait, service, rework, and quality as secondary outcomes, with mediation claims only under explicit assumptions.

## 8. Residual uncertainty

1. **Reviewer skill and workload:** Group-level certification information does not reveal which reviewer handled each case or their concurrent workload. Reviewer IDs and time-varying assignment/load data would help.
2. **Queue and compliance timing:** The wait measure combines queueing and compliance-related waiting. Activity start/end timestamps and queue snapshots would separate these sources.
3. **Case risk:** Complexity is coarse and does not capture all factors that affect routing, rework, and completion. More detailed pre-assignment risk features are needed.
4. **Selection of completed cases:** Open and abandoned cases are missing. Follow every assigned case to closure, abandonment, or censoring to assess completion-time effects without conditioning only on completers.
5. **Quality:** The 30-day appeal outcome is sparse and may reflect post-process events. Track richer quality outcomes for all cases over a defined follow-up period.
