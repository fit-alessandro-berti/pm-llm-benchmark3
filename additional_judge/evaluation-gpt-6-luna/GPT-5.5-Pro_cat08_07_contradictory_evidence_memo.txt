The main weakness is overconfidence in the proposed controls and causal explanations. The memo treats billing before activation as a control failure and survey skipping as a likely cause of defects, although the evidence establishes frequent deviations and segment correlations, not causality or the applicable exception rules. It also recommends scaling coordinator ownership and the merged validate/schedule step without isolating their effects from the other redesign changes. The threshold for broader rollout is directionally sound but vague: “agreed tolerances” are not specified, and requiring no worsening in every listed metric could be impractical without a stated trade-off or statistical standard.

The value-of-information ranking is plausible, but M1 and M6 overlap, while M5’s coordinator-absence question is more directly relevant to the resilience risk than its lower rank suggests. These limitations keep the answer below an excellent score, though it addresses nearly all requested evidence and alternatives.
