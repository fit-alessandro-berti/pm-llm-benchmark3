## 1. Conformance semantics

- **Forbidden behavior:** An in-scope relation explicitly prohibited by the norm. The norm is closed-world for forbidden relations.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** An in-scope required relation is absent. The norm is closed-world for mandatory columns within their stated scope, but observed graph counts alone may not establish per-event coverage.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** A relation the norm marks unspecified, or otherwise does not govern. Under the open-world assumption, its presence is not itself a violation.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** A typical relation is missing. That is a diagnostic signal, not a violation; legitimate exceptions may explain it.
- **Likely data-quality error:** Evidence points to an incorrect or incomplete recording, identity match, or object binding. This describes the likely explanation, not the underlying business behavior.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** A graph relation is absent or present because the model’s abstraction—such as direct succession—does not match the norm’s business meaning.
- **Insufficient evidence:** The summary does not establish scope, identity, timing, exception status, or per-event coverage well enough to classify the behavior.

Open-world treatment means unspecified relations are not violations merely because they are unexpected. Closed-world treatment applies only to explicit prohibitions and in-scope mandatory requirements. Typical relations remain nonbinding.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG summarizes directly-following event relations along object lifecycles. Counts can reveal where an observed relation is frequent or absent in the summarized log, but they do not by themselves prove event-level compliance, object identity, complete histories, or eventual outcomes.

N1 requires eventual validation, while the observed summary reports direct succession. The two missing direct edges may reflect an intervening review event, so the graph cannot establish that validation never occurred. This is the danger of converting an “eventually expected” rule into a directly-follows rule: a valid path with intermediate events can be mislabeled as noncompliant.

N3 is a forbidden direct relation, and its two observations deserve urgent investigation. Confirm that each dispatch followed cancellation for the same Order and that the records are correctly ordered. N4 is mandatory only for externally shipped Packages; 198 of 200 observed edges leaves two cases to check, but graph counts alone do not identify whether they are true omissions.

N2 and N5 are typical, so missing direct succession is not noncompliance. Validate -> Close can be interrupted by modeled or unmodeled events. Invoice paths without observed Record Payment successors may reflect legitimate open credit or write-offs, or simply an incomplete observation window. Reopen -> Close is unspecified under N6. The three Create -> Manual Compliance Review cases do not violate any stated rule.

### OTG

An OTG represents object-to-object relations. It can show that relation types and weights occur, but aggregate edges do not establish that each qualifying interaction has its required relation, or that object identities are correct.

The Order–Item edge is present with weight 1,450, as required for Add Item interactions under T1. The Customer and RiskCase edges are unspecified, so their presence is unregulated here. The Invoice–Payment relation is typical under T3; its 92 edges do not make the remaining invoices noncompliant.

The single Order–Payment co-birth edge is forbidden by T6 if it represents the same actual business birth event. Verify event lineage before treating it as a business violation. The seven Package–Delivery codeath edges are unregulated. A relation can therefore be present and conforming at this abstraction while a different model family exposes a suspicious event-to-object binding.

### ET–OT

ET–OT records which object types relate to events. It can flag aggregate mismatches against required event types, but a weight difference does not establish which events lack bindings or why. Per-event bindings, scope, and identity need checking.

Create Order has 100 Order edges, matching E1’s event count. Add Item has 1,450 Item edges and 1,448 Order edges against 1,450 events; E3 appears covered in aggregate, while E2 has a two-edge difference that needs event-level reconciliation. Pack Package has 200 Package edges, and Dispatch Package has 200 Package edges. Dispatch has 198 Delivery edges against 200 events, indicating two apparent E6 gaps.

Record Payment has 93 Payment edges and 92 Invoice edges. E7 appears covered in aggregate. E8 requires an Invoice unless the event is explicitly typed as unapplied cash; the lone event without an Invoice is not so typed in this snapshot. Finance’s statement that unapplied cash can occur does not, on its own, satisfy the stated labeling exception.

Manual Compliance Review–RiskCase is unspecified under E9; Review–Customer is also unregulated. The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge conflicts with E10 if genuine, but the suspected reused-ID join makes data quality the leading explanation. An edge can be valid as an observed graph artifact while suspicious as an event binding; event-level verification is needed.

def classify(norm_id, *, in_scope, verified, relation_observed,
             explicitly_typed_exception=False, suspected_data_error=False):
    status, meaning = STATUS[norm_id]
    if in_scope is None or not verified:
        result = "insufficient evidence"
    elif not in_scope:
        result = "outside stated scope"
    elif status == "unspecified":
        result = "unexpected but unregulated"

    elif status == "mandatory" and not relation_observed:
        result = "missing mandatory behavior"
    elif status == "typical" and not relation_observed:
        result = "typical-pattern absence"
    else:
        result = "no deviation established"
    return {"norm": norm_id, "meaning": meaning, "classification": result}
