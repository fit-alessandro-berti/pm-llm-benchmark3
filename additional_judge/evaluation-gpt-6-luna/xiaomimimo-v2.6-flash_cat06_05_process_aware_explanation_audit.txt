**## 2. Process-model consistency checks**

Run a validation suite against the event log, feature store and explanation output:

1. **Prefix-time availability:** For each training and scoring row, reconstruct the state immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Require each feature’s `available_at` to be no later than `t0`, using a documented tie rule. Compare joined values with values recomputed from the prefix. Event time alone is insufficient if a record arrived later.
2. **Complete-trace use:** Record feature lineage, query version and whether a feature reads suffix or closure data. Reject full-trace fields and any mutable field without a historical snapshot. Test suffix independence: append arbitrary post-`t0` events to a case and verify that its `t0` features and explanation do not change.
3. **Activity-order feasibility:** Use normative constraints and alignments to flag traces such as closure before damage assessment. Use footprints or DFGs to find unusual directly-follows relations, then inspect violations against explicit process rules. A frequent DFG edge is observational evidence, not a normative rule; a rare edge is not automatically impossible.
4. **Same-timestamp ambiguity:** Detect events tied at `t0` or tied in patterns. Require a reliable sequence index or transaction ordering for order-dependent features. Otherwise mark the order ambiguous and exclude the affected pattern.
5. **Explanation enabledness:** At `t0`, derive the enabled, impossible and future-possible activities from the normative state. For a prefix of registration and coverage check, evidence requests, damage assessment and a triggered investigation are future-possible, not yet observed. Appeal is not yet enabled because no Partial or Deny decision exists; closure is not enabled before a final decision or withdrawal. Do not present future-possible activity as a current state. When a purported pattern contradicts process order, investigate log quality and lineage before labeling it impossible.
6. **Repeated decisions:** Validate event instances, not just coarse labels. Permit a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` after an Appeal, while checking that the appeal follows Partial or Deny and that the reconsidered decision follows the appeal. A repeated decision without an eligible preceding appeal needs review.

Alignment can identify deviations from a model but does not prove whether the model or the trace is wrong. Prefix checks establish admissibility only if timestamps and ingestion metadata are trustworthy. Declarative checks help express ordering and conditional rules, but must encode the actual business semantics.

**## 3. Alternative feature interpretations**

- **Defensible predictive signals:** Frozen `initial_damage_complexity` and correctly computed `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0` are available prefix features. They can support predictive associations; they do not establish causal effects.
- **Process-state or congestion signals:** `queue_length_at_t0` is a timestamped operational state if the join falls within tolerance. A prefix-derived fraud-investigation-open flag or document state could be useful at later prediction points when already observed. These may inform monitoring or operational investigation, but predictive contribution alone does not show that changing the state changes breach risk.
- **Proxy requiring fairness/robustness review:** `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, `initial_reserve`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, and a repaired risk score may encode legitimate risk information and also reflect unequal access or service conditions. Check proxy strength, subgroup calibration, attribution differences and downstream impact. Do not automatically delete or present them as claimant-caused delay.
- **Drift/calendar proxy:** `calendar_week` is known at prediction time but entangles holidays and policy regimes. Separate regime and staffing context where possible, test across time boundaries, and monitor target comparability. It is not evidence that a particular week intrinsically causes delay.
- **Unusable until lineage is repaired:** `final_handler_team`, employee experience, the prior-claim count under its current SQL, same-day document snapshot, mutable current risk score, and initial reserve if its `t0` availability cannot be established. Full-trace duration, appeal indicator, total request count, complete-trace variant, final decision and last resource role are inadmissible for this `t0` prediction. Repairable fields require historical availability and prefix recomputation before reconsideration.

Predictive validity asks whether a feature improves reliable out-of-time predictions. Causal interpretation needs a causal design or other credible identification, not SHAP importance. Operational actionability asks whether a decision-maker can change a factor and whether that intervention helps; congestion may be a candidate, but requires evaluation. Fairness review asks whether predictions or their use create unjustified disparities, including through proxies. These are distinct standards.

**## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation**

1. **Block inadmissible features at ingestion.** Require feature definitions to include `available_at`, event time, as-of rule, source lineage, and full-trace usage. Fail model builds when metadata is missing or a feature reads beyond `t0`.
2. **Build historical feature snapshots.** Store assignment, employee experience, risk score, document state and other mutable values as of each prediction time. Fix prior-claim SQL to use a strict pre-`t0` cutoff.
3. **Recompute using prefixes.** Replay each case to the first coverage check, compare every training and scoring feature with its prefix-derived value, and test that appending future events cannot change a `t0` row.
4. **Add process-conformance tests.** Enforce normative ordering and appeal eligibility, detect tied timestamps without sequence provenance, validate activity enabledness at each prediction point, and test repeated-decision semantics after appeal.
5. **Recalibrate after feature removal.** Refit on admissible features, assess calibration and discrimination out of time, and report subgroup calibration after removal or repair.
6. **Check explanation stability.** Use prefix-only explanation backgrounds; monitor feature ranks and per-case contribution sign stability across samples, time periods and reasonable model variations.
7. **Review proxies and fairness.** Examine region, channel, reserve, history and repaired risk-score components for proxy effects, subgroup errors and downstream impact. Consider operational use separately from predictive validity.
8. **Monitor schema and process drift.** Track feature lineage and availability changes, missing sequence indices, new or shifting process patterns, policy regimes, holidays, target-definition changes and explanation drift. Revalidate after material changes.
