The largest issue is treating the CFO email as an effective waiver of GPIP-4’s pre-commitment budget-owner and Finance Controller approvals. The facts establish the CFO’s authority to interpret Finance controls, not to waive them; A2 requires both approvals, and the email’s scope and authority to displace that rule are uncertain. The answer also overstates evidence. For P-505, a call starting at 17:57 and an acknowledgment arriving at 17:59:31 do not establish exactly when the supplier commitment became binding. The ERP clock is said to *may* have been nine minutes fast, not proven to have been. The answer calls the drift documented and relies on email headers, carrier logs, and an ERP offset that the scenario does not provide. For P-507, the signed PDF is evidence of a timely record if its creation time is reliable, but the answer calls it cryptographically verified and treats the outage as established for that document system without supporting facts.

P-503’s authorizer was not necessarily the “final business approver” barred by A1, though the missing Compliance clearance above EUR 100,000 remains a strong breach. The answer shows strong process awareness and useful treatment of scope and timing, but its confident assertions, unsupported evidentiary details, and consequential classification errors prevent a higher grade.
