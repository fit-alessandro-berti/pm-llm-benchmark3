The response shows strong awareness of the central target problem: it defines a primary estimand among eventually activated accounts with mature, observed 90-day outcomes, rejects treating declined applications as non-defaults, and discusses censoring, delayed labels, transfers, missing feeds, leakage, and temporal validation. Its PM4Py-oriented code gives a useful starting point for prefix extraction and as-of feature controls.

The main weakness is that the required nine-section answer and cohort accounting are incomplete in the supplied response. It does not provide a cohort-flow table that accounts for all 48,000 applications, and the code classifies every activated case without a mature observed label into one bucket, conflating not-yet-mature outcomes with permanently missing feeds. Its maturity rule also requires waiting through the maximum 35-day delay even when a label may already be available, while the label logic does not explicitly check that the recorded outcome corresponds to the full 90-day horizon.

Other gaps limit the implementation’s reliability: the prefix example filters a hard-coded set of post-prefix activities, which cannot safely represent a decision-time boundary for every prediction point; event deduplication and migration-era activity normalization are not implemented; and the shown event aggregation uses `last` without clearly sorting the selected rows by event time. The response acknowledges selection into activation but does not make a concrete primary missing-label adjustment or quantify sensitivity bounds.

Overall, this is a thoughtful design direction with useful leakage controls, but it falls short of the required accounting and leaves important cohort, maturity, and prefix details underspecified.
