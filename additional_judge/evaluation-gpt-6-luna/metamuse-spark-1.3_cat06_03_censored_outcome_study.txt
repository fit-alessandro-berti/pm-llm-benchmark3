The response captures the central target problem: it rejects treating rejected or withdrawn applications as non-default, distinguishes observed outcomes from counterfactual default risk, and proposes a defensible joint activation-and-default estimand alongside alternatives. It also addresses censoring, transfers, missing feeds, temporal validation, leakage risks, and deployment feedback.

However, the required cohort-flow table is missing from the available response, and the proposed cohort accounting and maturity logic do not clearly reconcile all 48,000 applications. The implementation has material correctness issues: it treats Account Activated as a terminal event, uses `label_available_at.max()` for case maturity, and derives maturity for nonactivated cases from an assumed activation lag. It also excludes short cases from the fixed-prefix cohort without showing how that affects the stated estimand, and its temporal split leaves the 2025–2026 train/validation/test chronology and maturity handling underspecified. These gaps weaken the promised auditability and could bias the analysis.
