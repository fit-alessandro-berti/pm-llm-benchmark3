## 3. Causal diagnosis

The June–July collapse in measured conformance is most plausibly driven by a combination of changed labels, added technical events, and unreliable event ordering. The alignment improvements are large and correspond to dated system changes. The 40-case audit backs the label mapping, while the screenshot evidence supports timestamp misordering for some reversed traces. These findings explain much of the measured decline, but they do not establish that every normalization is correct: the gateway event’s business meaning and the reliability of source sequence still need validation.

August–September adds a separate model-governance problem. The policy now requires sign-off for claims above EUR 50,000, while the normative tree has no such step or data condition. Allowing optional sign-off improves fitness, but that permissive model cannot establish compliance. The relevant test is whether each high-value case received sign-off, and whether low-value cases are being overconstrained.

The residual enhanced-review traces without a recorded compliance check are the clearest unresolved compliance concern. They could represent bypasses or missing events. The available evidence does not justify labeling employees noncompliant. Stable approval and appeal rates do little to settle this because incident detection is incomplete and those outcomes do not directly measure whether a required check occurred.

## 5. Highest-value diagnostic plan

1. **Rebuild event order and provenance.** For June–September, retrieve source timestamps, source sequence numbers, ingestion times, activity codes, and case identifiers. Recompute ordering from the source sequence, validate sequence integrity, and quantify how many `Notify -> Decision` traces remain. Preserve the raw log and report each transformation’s effect on variants and alignment.

2. **Validate event meaning at the source.** Compare UI action, source event code, gateway record, and extracted label in cases sampled before and after each release. Establish whether `Risk Screen` is functionally equivalent to `Classify` and whether `Queue Routed` represents a business step or infrastructure routing. Do not remove or merge either event until the source mapping is confirmed.

3. **Audit all missing-check cases.** For the 55 enhanced-review traces without a recorded check, inspect independent compliance records, reviewer notes, and source-system history. Classify each as performed and logged, performed but missing from extraction, or not performed. Record case amount, reviewer, system path, and period to identify patterns.

4. **Test the sign-off rule with the omitted attribute.** Join claim amount and policy applicability to the event log. Verify whether every case above EUR 50,000 has sign-off after August 1. Report high- and low-value fitness, precision, and rule violations separately. Compare the current model with a conditional model, not a globally optional sign-off model alone.

5. **Investigate `Manual Override` and resource missingness.** Review override event definitions and sampled source cases to determine whether it is a routing correction, a newly logged action, or both. Trace the increase in missing resources to the ETL and system changes before attempting reviewer-level comparisons.

Keep preprocessed and raw results side by side so a measurement repair is not mistaken for a process improvement.

## 6. Technical reproducibility appendix

The supplied aggregates support directional checks of the listed variants and alignments. They are insufficient to reproduce event-level fitness, precision, cycle-time, resource, or timestamp analyses. The underlying case-level event log, timestamp fields, source sequence numbers, case attributes, and preprocessing code are not supplied here.

Any reproduction should retain raw event labels and timestamps, then record each normalization as a separate, reversible transformation. In particular, label mapping assumes functional equivalence; removing `Queue Routed` assumes it is not a required business activity; and source-sequence ordering assumes sequence numbers are complete and authoritative. Alignment fitness should be reported alongside precision because an optional-sign-off model can improve fitness by allowing more traces.

The June–July numbers suggest most of the measured collapse is attributable to data representation and ordering, but the exact share cannot be established from aggregate tables alone. The remaining missing-check traces require source audit before any conclusion about employee compliance.

## 7. Provisional conclusion

**Primary explanation for the measured collapse — confidence 90/100.** The conformance deterioration is predominantly a measurement-pipeline failure, not a process failure. Between April–May (93% fitting) and June–July (32%), roughly 57 of the ~61 lost fitness points are recovered by three data-side repairs that map cleanly onto dated technical changes: remapping `Risk Screen` to `Classify` (+26, after the 31 May UI release), removing the gateway's `Queue Routed` (+23, after 3 June), and ordering by source sequence rather than warehouse-ingestion time (+8, after 15 June). Each repair is monotone, isolated and reversible, none touches business outcomes, and a 40-case audit independently confirms the rename. The August–September deterioration is a separate and legitimate mechanism: a 1 August sign-off requirement that a control-flow-only tree never absorbed costs ~23 points (66% under preprocessing identical to Jun–Jul's 89%) and is recovered to 86% by a candidate model.

**A real compliance concern may remain — confidence 45/100.** After full preprocessing and model extension, about 7 points of residual deviation persist, concentrated in enhanced reviews with no recorded `Compliance Check` (25 then 30 traces) and `Manual Override` (20 then 35). Because April–May recorded the check in 180 of 180 cases, the regression is genuine *in the log*; but no audit yet separates a bypassed step from an unlogged one, and resource-attribution loss rising 1.2% to 6.4% blocks reviewer-level analysis. Flat appeal and approval rates neither convict nor exonerate, given compliance's own admission of incomplete detection.

**Immediate actions.** *IT:* version the label and gateway event contract; dual-write source sequence alongside ingestion time; demote `Queue Routed` to a technical lifecycle; repair resource capture. *Process owners:* release a versioned tree containing conditional sign-off and register the EUR 50,000 attribute as a mandatory stratifier. *Compliance:* commission the 55-case audit, and once resources are complete, a reviewer-level deviation study.

**What must not be claimed yet:** that reviewers are noncompliant; that the extended model is correct (precision is unmeasured); that stable appeal rates demonstrate conformance; or that post-repair fitness equals regulatory compliance.

## 8. Model-governance recommendation

1. **Model versioning.** Every normative process tree carries a version identifier, effective date, named owner and change log. A policy or regulation that changes the process without a corresponding model release is a blocking defect tracked like a schema migration; conformance jobs declare the model version they used, and results from two versions are never placed in the same trend line.

2. **Event-semantic contracts.** Each activity label in the model is bound to a documented, versioned contract naming the source system, field, transformation and business function it represents. A UI relabel is permitted only if ingestion emits a declared, tested, reversible alias (or the model is released in the same change window); undeclared label changes fail the pipeline rather than silently entering conformance.

3. **Technical-event policy.** Gateway and infrastructure events are published in a separate lifecycle/technical namespace and are excluded from application-activity analysis by an explicit whitelist. Emitting any new technical event requires a conformance-impact assessment; partial coverage of a technical event must be explained by sampling rules, never discovered as a fitness drop.

4. **Timestamp provenance.** Every event stores source-system time and source-system sequence number and warehouse-ingestion time, with the conformance job ordering by source sequence by default. Ingestion-only ordering is flagged as a data-quality incident, and any conformance report without provenance columns is void.

5. **Data-aware constraints.** Control-flow models are paired with declarative or conditional constraints (here: `Supervisor Sign-off` required iff amount > EUR 50,000); key case attributes are mandatory inputs to conformance reporting and every fitness figure is published alongside precision and attribute-stratified breakdowns, so that a more permissive model can never be mistaken for a more conformant one.

6. **Post-release validation.** Every UI, gateway or ETL release triggers an automated pre/post conformance diff—weekly variant and frequency-DFG series, fitness and precision deltas, label and technical-event counts—with a threshold that blocks promotion until the movement is attributed to process, model, or measurement. No release closes without that attribution.
