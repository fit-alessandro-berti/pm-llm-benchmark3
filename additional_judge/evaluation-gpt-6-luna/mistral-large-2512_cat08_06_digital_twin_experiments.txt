**3/10** — The response identifies several important modeling needs—nonstationary arrivals, resource calendars, rework, applicant response delays, and cross-case dependencies—and proposes a useful intervention set. But the implementation sketch has major conceptual and coding gaps that make its results unreliable.

The most serious issue is that the simulator does not faithfully implement the stated process. Its routing and event logic appear simplified, and the excerpt does not establish correct handling of parallel reviews, conditional steps, appeals, or dependencies between related cases. It also treats public notice as a fixed 14-day service despite the stated objection-related tail, and uses generic gamma draws that do not fit the reported medians and p90s.

Resource and time handling are weak. Availability checks use only the hour and weekday, so service cannot span shifts or account for holidays and meeting weeks correctly. Applicant response times are generated during time advancement rather than scheduled once per request, which can change the response event unpredictably. The utilization calculation uses elapsed time since midnight as its denominator, so it does not measure utilization over the simulation period. Several resources and capacity rules are also incomplete or conflated.

The response mentions parameter estimation, validation, and experiment comparisons, but does not give a sufficiently rigorous plan for calibrating uncertain and missing data, validating against held-out periods or operational metrics, using paired random seeds, measuring confidence intervals, or checking stability across replications. It also needs clearer definitions of outcome measures and guardrails, such as backlog, p90 cycle time, rework, workload, and rejection rates.

Overall, this is a reasonable outline of topics and candidate interventions, but the code and experimental design are too incomplete and internally inconsistent to support credible intervention comparisons without substantial redesign.
