## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity reflects the business consequence of a deviation, not its alignment cost. I use this scale:

- **1 — Negligible:** harmless telemetry, retries, or a fitting silent move.
- **2 — Low:** explainable evidence or model-scope gap with no indicated control failure.
- **3 — Moderate:** unresolved control or evidence concern that could affect assurance, but no confirmed material breach.
- **4 — High:** a policy breach or substantial control weakness needing prompt review.
- **5 — Critical:** confirmed payment or approval control failure with significant exposure, or a high-risk case where required controls cannot be evidenced.

The assessment considers the control objective, amount and potential exposure, reversibility, timing, customer or regulatory harm, recurrence, evidence reliability, and whether the case falls within the model’s intended scope. A large amount raises potential exposure but does not by itself prove a failure. Evidence quality affects confidence: a confirmed event sequence can support high confidence in a breach, while missing or mutable records lower confidence. Severity and confidence are separate; a potentially critical case can have only medium confidence when key evidence is missing.

## 4. Prioritized Remediation

1. **Contain and investigate confirmed or likely payment-control failures.** Review CL-604 immediately for recovery and affected cases. Investigate CL-610’s recent bank change and Auto Approve path, and CL-609’s out-of-limit Auto Approve use. For the four synchronous policy failures, reconcile approval and payment records against the policy conditions.
2. **Resolve CL-602’s missing fraud evidence.** Determine whether screening occurred, whether it was valid, and what exposure remains. A verbal assertion without retained evidence is insufficient to establish the required control.
3. **Repair cross-system evidence linkage.** Establish durable case identifiers and timestamped links for the nine external-adjuster cases and portal notifications; prioritize the two adjuster cases without stable identifiers and CL-608’s mutable link.
4. **Fix connector and event-data quality.** Correlate the 74 duplicate notifications by message ID and connector retry records. Normalize CL-611’s activity label using effective-dated mappings, and separate UI telemetry such as CL-601’s events.
5. **Resolve process scope and update the model.** Decide whether denied and withdrawn cases belong in the modeled process. Add applicable terminal paths so CL-607 and CL-612 do not accrue misleading costs.
6. **Encode and monitor the policy overlay.** Make amount, risk, and recent bank changes explicit routing and payment-gate conditions. Report policy breaches separately from alignment conformance, since zero-cost, fully synchronous paths can still violate policy.

Raw alignment costs are technical edit-distance measures under uniform visible-move weights. They help locate mismatches, but their business meaning depends on the event, control, evidence, and process scope.
