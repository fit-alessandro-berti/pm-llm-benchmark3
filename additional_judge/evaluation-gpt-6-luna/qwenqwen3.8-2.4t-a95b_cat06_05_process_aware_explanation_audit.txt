## 2. Process-model consistency checks

1. **Reconstruct the prediction prefix.** Identify the first `Coverage Check Completed` using a reliable sequence index, then freeze the prefix immediately after that event. If timestamps tie and no sequence index or transaction ordering resolves them, mark the order ambiguous rather than guessing.

2. **Enforce point-in-time feature availability.** Store each feature’s valid time and transaction or extraction time, then require an as-of-`t0` join. Assert that no event-derived value uses events after the prefix. For snapshots such as queue length, verify the snapshot time is at or before `t0`; test that nightly document counts exclude uploads after `t0`. For prior claims, require claim creation strictly before `t0` and exclude the current claim.

3. **Check activity order against the normative process.** Use declarative constraints for required precedence and conditions: coverage check follows registration; evidence cycles follow coverage check; appeals follow Partial or Deny; closure follows a final decision or withdrawal. Classify activities relative to the `t0` state:
   - **Enabled:** `Damage Assessment Completed` on the fast track, or `Documents Requested` on the evidence path; a fraud investigation may be possible if its trigger is already present.
   - **Future-possible:** later evidence events, damage assessment on the evidence path, settlement decisions, appeals after an eligible decision, and closure.
   - **Impossible at `t0`:** an appeal without a prior Partial or Deny decision, or closure before a final decision/withdrawal. A feature that refers to a future-possible activity is still inadmissible for this early prediction.

4. **Handle ties explicitly.** Compare timestamps and sequence indices, and flag any directed pattern whose order depends on an unresolved tie. The `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` pattern should be blocked unless reliable ordering evidence resolves it. If the tie remains ambiguous, do not use the order-dependent pattern.

5. **Detect complete-trace features.** Maintain lineage metadata and tests for features built from the full trace, including names such as `total`, `final`, `anywhere`, `last`, and `variant`. For each prediction row, compare the stored feature with a version computed from the reconstructed prefix. A mismatch or missing temporal anchor should block the feature from the explanation.

6. **Validate repeated settlement decisions.** Permit a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` only when the first decision is Partial or Deny and an Appeal occurs before reconsideration. Flag repeated decisions without that sequence, or appeals after Approve or Withdrawn, for review. The coarse activity label can legitimately repeat, but neither decision nor appeal is available at this `t0`.

7. **Use process-mining methods with appropriate limits.** Prefix checks and alignments can identify deviations from the normative process; footprints can expose ordering conflicts; declarative constraints can encode required and conditional behavior. DFGs are useful for describing observed transitions, but a frequent edge is not a normative rule. Conformance results can flag inconsistency, but they do not establish why a breach occurred or prove a feature’s causal effect.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

| Class | Examples | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | `initial_damage_complexity`; `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; `prior_claim_count_before_t0` after repair; `initial_reserve` if verified as-of `t0` | These may be described as statistically associated with predicted risk when their point-in-time lineage is verified. | Their importance does not prove they cause delay, guarantee an outcome, or justify an adverse action by themselves. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `queue_length_at_t0`; other timestamped workload or staffing measures | A high queue describes congestion at prediction time and may support capacity monitoring or triage review. | It does not prove congestion caused a particular breach or that a specific intervention will prevent one. |
| Proxy requiring fairness and robustness review | `claimant_region`, `claimant_channel`, and related network, language, or socioeconomic proxies | Their predictive association can be documented and tested; direct operational variables may be preferable when they capture the relevant mechanism. | They should not be presented as intrinsic delay drivers or used to disadvantage claimants without governance and fairness review. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week`, holidays, policy periods | Period-specific risk can be monitored and modeled if the underlying operational context is understood. | A week number is not itself a causal explanation, and its association may not persist after policy or calendar changes. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `final_handler_team`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, future sequence patterns, `final_decision`, `risk_score_current`, `last_resource_role`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, defective prior-claim count, and extraction-time employee experience | These may support appropriately separated post-hoc analyses, or be replaced with prefix-derived, historically timestamped versions. | They must not support an early `t0` explanation in their current form. |

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

**P0 — Anchor features and explanations to `t0`.** Build a point-in-time feature store with event time, valid-from/valid-to, transaction time, and extraction time. Reconstruct `t0` from the first `Coverage Check Completed` using a reliable sequence index. Block features and explanation items without a verifiable temporal anchor.

**P0 — Remove leakage and recompute.** Disable the complete-trace and post-outcome features and patterns from early explanations. Re-score Cases X, Y, and Z using validated prefix features, then recompute global importance and local explanations. Treat the current probabilities as unverified until that work is complete.

**P1 — Repair mutable feature history.** Version reserves, risk scores, document counts, team and resource assignments, employee experience, queue snapshots, and fraud indicators. Support as-of-`t0` retrieval for both training and serving.

**P1 — Add process-conformance tests.** Test required activity order, appeal eligibility, repeated settlement decisions, same-timestamp ambiguity, and activity-state classification at `t0`. Use declarative constraints and alignments for conformance; do not treat DFG frequency as a normative rule.

**P1 — Retrain and calibrate after feature removal.** Rebuild with prefix-only features and report discrimination, calibration, Brier score, reliability curves, and subgroup calibration. Document any performance change after leakage removal.

**P1 — Govern proxy features.** Evaluate region, channel, and related proxies for disparate impact, robustness, and omitted-variable behavior. Document business justification and human oversight; consider direct operational measures where appropriate.

**P2 — Test explanation stability.** Perturb ambiguous event ties and snapshot tolerances, and remove correlated proxies in sensitivity analyses. Flag explanations that change materially.

**P2 — Monitor drift and schema changes.** Track feature names, schema updates, timestamp granularity, sequence-index coverage, process variants, queue behavior, holidays, and policy changes. Trigger revalidation when these change.
