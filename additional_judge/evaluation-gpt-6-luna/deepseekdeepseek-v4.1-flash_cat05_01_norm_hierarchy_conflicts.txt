8/10. The assessment is thorough, consistently applies the main timing and approval rules across all eight cases, and handles the ERP outage as an evidentiary issue rather than an exception. It distinguishes the directive’s local workflow effects from GPIP-4 controls, discusses credible alternative readings, and offers concrete remediation and policy clarification.

The main weakness is that some conclusions are firmer than the record supports. P-504 is outside the Aachen SOP’s stated maintenance and production-support scope, so its B1 and B3 deviations need qualification; “unapproved supplier” also does not by itself establish that the supplier is new under GPIP-4 A3. The P-503 segregation concern should distinguish an Incident Command authorization from the “final business approver” specified in A1. More generally, the hierarchy and rule catalogue occasionally treat contested interpretations as settled, and several “missing evidence” entries are not clearly tied to a required control. These are meaningful but limited issues in an otherwise strong, process-aware evaluation.
