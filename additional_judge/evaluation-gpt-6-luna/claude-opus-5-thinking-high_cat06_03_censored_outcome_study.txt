The answer shows strong awareness of selective label observation, leakage risks, temporal validation, and the limits of counterfactual reject inference. However, major statistical and implementation errors undermine its central claim that the primary estimand is identified and the proposed pipeline estimates it.

The primary target is not coherently specified. It describes a 270-day composite event—activation within 180 days and default within 90 days after activation—but then treats “activated, no default” as a competing event at day 270. That outcome is only known after the activation and performance windows, so its event time is not simply the end of follow-up in the way the code represents it. The stated ratio for conditional default risk is also generally invalid: dividing the default CIF by the sum of default and activated-no-default CIFs does not recover risk conditional on activation when activation times and competing events differ.

More seriously, the claim that the full-population composite is “identified” and well-defined for all applications is overstated. It is a policy-dependent outcome that includes approval and transfer decisions, and cases already terminal before the 72-hour landmark are excluded. That does not answer the request for predictions from the first few events of every application. The answer acknowledges early terminals in the cohort flow, but its primary population definition excludes them, while the operational rationale claims coverage of all live applications.

The cohort accounting also conflicts with the stated target. The 48,000-case table places the 21,600 matured activated accounts into default/no-default categories and the 3,600 immature activated accounts into censoring, but the 72-hour landmark cohort excludes cases terminal by that time. The table never reconciles the reported totals with that landmark restriction, nor does it clarify which transfers or terminal outcomes occur before versus after the landmark. Its 5.6%–60.6% counterfactual bounds likewise use all 48,000 applications as a denominator despite the landmark target’s restricted risk set, and do not clearly define the counterfactual outcome horizon.

The censoring analysis is overconfident. Administrative censoring at a common extraction date is not automatically independent of outcomes in the proposed multi-stage process, especially with policy and calendar effects. A fixed 35-day cutoff handles known maximum delay only if the feed delay really is bounded and all sources share that bound; the prompt says “commonly” 7–35 days and separately flags permanently missing partner feeds. The proposed IPCW approach cannot by itself solve MNAR outcome loss, and its stated model does not establish identification.

The code has serious defects beyond those estimand issues:

- `case_timeline` computes `terminal_state` using `idxmin()` across timestamp columns, but then strips the prefix without resolving missing values; cases with no terminal event can produce unusable state values. More importantly, it omits `Accepted` and `Contract Signed` as intermediate states, while the definition of terminality and activation is not consistently enforced.
- `event_time` is built with `np.where` over mixed timestamp/NaT values, then passed to `.clip(upper=cutoff)`. This is fragile and can fail or produce incorrect timestamps; it also assigns censored cases the extraction cutoff even when their 270-day horizon ends sooner.
- For immature activated cases, censoring should occur at the last time their outcome was observable, with delayed-label logic represented explicitly. The code instead assigns generic censoring and does not construct the per-case risk intervals promised by the analysis.
- `label_available_at` is aggregated with `max`, which can make a case appear immature when an earlier valid label was available; the code also does not reconcile repeated or conflicting label values.
