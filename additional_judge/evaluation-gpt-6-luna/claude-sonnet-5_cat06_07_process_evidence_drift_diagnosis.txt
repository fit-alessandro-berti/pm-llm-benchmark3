## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**Jan–Mar:** This is the clean reference period: labels are mature, data-quality issues are low, and AUROC, Brier, ECE, and variant mix are stable. The elevated V3 breach rate is already present, so V3’s higher baseline risk is not itself a new failure.

**Apr:** Treat the severe metric deterioration primarily as a pipeline incident. The diagnosis rename disrupted prediction prefixes; unknown activities and missing queue features spiked. Although labels were mature, the model was often evaluated on incorrectly constructed inputs. The 0.28 mean predicted risk against a 0.19 observed rate is consistent with a feature or prefix artifact. Do not use April as ordinary evidence of model drift until the pipeline is reconstructed.

**May:** The mapping patch and backfill align with a substantial recovery, but the cached “events before diagnosis” feature was not fully recomputed and duplicate handling differed within April. May is improved, not automatically clean; verify lineage and feature recomputation before including either month in training.

**Jun:** Remote routing raises V2 share to 52%, while matured breach rates by variant remain broadly stable or improve slightly. The aggregate breach rate falls to 0.15, which may largely reflect the mix shift toward lower-risk V2. The higher ECE warrants investigation, but this is not evidence by itself of a broad concept change.

**Jul:** Supplier delays begin to show in the `Order Part -> Part Received` edge, rising to 65 hours. Matured V3 breach rate jumps to 0.46. This is early evidence of process change with an outcome impact concentrated in V3, while overall labels are nearly mature.

**Aug–Sep:** Supplier delay becomes severe, and V3’s matured breach rate reaches 0.64 in August. September’s V3 rate remains high at 0.61, while V3’s share also increases. These months combine process and likely outcome-relationship change with population shift: Partner Z enters with more rural cases, older devices, and a higher V3 share. September’s raw breach gap by partner is suggestive but not conclusive because labels mature more slowly for Partner Z. The worsening aggregate metrics cannot be attributed to one cause.

**Oct:** Do not interpret the observed 0.16 breach rate as improvement. Only 55% of labels are mature, the median delay is 52 days, and longer paths are disproportionately affected. The 0.64 AUROC and poor calibration are provisional snapshots; maturity and pipeline gates should precede any retraining decision.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

| Test | Under one hypothesis | Under a competing hypothesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting:** Track the fraction matured by days since case start, stratified by month, variant, and source. Recompute metrics at common label-age vintages. | If Oct’s apparent breach drop is censoring, later fixed-vintage snapshots should rise as labels mature, especially for long paths. | If it is a real improvement, mature-vintage breach rates should remain lower without a path-specific maturity gap explaining the difference. |
| **Recompute after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning:** Normalize both diagnosis labels; audit duplicate decisions; recompute April prefixes and cached features, then backtest. | If April’s collapse is a pipeline failure, prefix coverage and metrics should improve materially after repair. | If April represents genuine drift, repaired features will leave poor performance on properly constructed cases. |
| **Calibration and discrimination by variant, source, and month:** Report AUROC, Brier, reliability curves, and maturity alongside each slice. | If aggregate decline is mostly mix shift, within-variant/source metrics should be relatively stable while the aggregate changes. | If model performance has deteriorated within segments, within-segment calibration or discrimination will also worsen. |
| **Compare process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges:** Measure variant frequencies and key edge durations using event-calendar and case-start cohorts. | A process-change explanation predicts longer `Order Part -> Part Received` durations and rising V3 share alongside the breach increase. | A pure population-mix explanation without process deterioration predicts no comparable within-V3 edge-duration increase. |
| **Same-path conditional outcome test:** Within V3 and comparable paths, model breach against supplier, order-to-receipt duration, calendar period, and relevant case covariates; test period interactions. | If the relationship changed, period or supplier-delay terms remain associated with breach after path and population controls. | If only the mix changed, period effects should shrink after conditioning on path, covariates, and process duration. |
| **Partner Z reweighting or transport check:** Compare mature cases within variant, then reweight non-Z cases to Partner Z’s rural-distance, device-age, and path distribution. | If observed gap is explained by population composition, reweighting should substantially reduce it. | A persistent mature, within-path residual suggests a partner-specific difference or missing partner-related feature. |
| **Feature missingness and lineage audit:** Trace feature availability, source schema, recomputation version, and duplicates by month and source. | A pipeline explanation predicts April errors aligned with the rename, missingness spike, and incomplete backfill. | A genuine process or concept shift should persist in records with verified, consistent feature lineage. |

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment:** Keep the current model from silently driving high-impact decisions on V3 or Partner Z cases during the investigation. Surface uncertainty and route those cases for operational review. Do not treat October’s observed breach rate as a performance gain.

**Short-term recalibration:** Consider recalibration only after labels are mature enough for the selected cohort and pipeline health is verified. If within-segment ranking is stable but calibration has shifted, recalibrate by variant or use a shared recalibrator with variant indicators. June deserves stratified review before any recalibration because the mix change may explain much of the aggregate shift.

**Feature repair:** Repair diagnosis normalization, duplicate handling, and incomplete April recomputation before using those records. Add and validate process signals such as supplier lead time, order-to-receipt duration, rural distance, and device age, subject to availability at prediction time.

**Full retraining:** Retrain after the pipeline is stable, labels have matured or censoring is handled, and the relationship tests establish a durable change. Use fixed-vintage data and time-based validation; keep supplier-distressed periods identifiable rather than blending them without checks.

**Training-month guidance:** Jan–Mar are the safest baseline months. April is unsafe until reconstructed; May is conditional on verifying the residual cached feature and lineage. June–August can support analysis, but should not be naively pooled because routing and supplier conditions changed. September is unsafe for ordinary training until Partner Z labels mature and population effects are evaluated. October is unsafe for ordinary supervised training at 55% maturity.

A V3-specific model is not yet established as necessary: first test whether a shared model with process and population features resolves the within-V3 deterioration. Variant-specific recalibration may be justified sooner if mature, stratified tests show a calibration-only problem. A Partner Z-specific model is premature; consider it only if a mature residual gap persists after reweighting and feature repair.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

- **Predictive:** AUROC and Brier on matured, fixed-vintage cohorts, plus clearly labeled current-label snapshots.
- **Calibration:** Reliability curves and ECE by month, variant, and source.
- **Label maturity:** Maturity curves and delay percentiles by case-start cohort, variant, and partner.
- **Data quality:** Unknown activities, missing features, duplicate rates, schema versions, prefix coverage, and recomputation lineage.
- **Process variants:** Variant proportions by case-start month and source, annotated with routing and onboarding changes.
- **Performance-DFG:** Duration distributions for `Order Part -> Part Received`, diagnosis to `Remote Fix`, and dispatch to repair completion.
- **Population:** Partner/source shares and covariate distributions, including rural distance, device age, and path mix.
- **Intervention and deployment:** Model and feature-pipeline versions, overrides, recalibration or retraining dates, and before/after results.

**Alert dependencies:** Maturity and data-quality alarms should qualify predictive and calibration alerts. Low maturity marks results provisional; it does not trigger retraining. Elevated pipeline errors block affected cohorts from training until repaired. A mix or population alarm without within-segment performance loss routes to reweighting or recalibration review. A performance-DFG shift paired with a same-path conditional outcome change routes to feature repair and retraining assessment.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

RENAME = {"Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"}

def load_events(path):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"])
    raw["raw_activity"] = raw["activity"]
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace(RENAME)

    # Flag exact duplicate event rows for audit, then retain one copy.
    raw["duplicate_flag"] = raw.duplicated(
        subset=["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="first"
    )
    clean = raw.loc[~raw["duplicate_flag"]].copy()
    events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        clean, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    return events, raw

def monthly_evidence(events, raw, start, end):
    # Event-calendar cohort answers which events occurred during the month.
    event_calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        events, start, end, mode="events"
    )
    # Case-start cohort answers what happened to cases that began in the month.
    case_start = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        events, start, end, mode="traces_contained"
    )

    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_calendar)
    performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(event_calendar)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(case_start)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(case_start)

    in_month = raw["timestamp"].ge(pd.Timestamp(start)) & raw["timestamp"].lt(
        pd.Timestamp(end)
    )
    audit = {
        "event_case_count": event_calendar["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "case_start_count": case_start["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "variants": {str(k): len(v) for k, v in variants.items()},
        "dfg": dfg,
        "performance_dfg": performance_dfg,
        "unknown_activity_share": raw.loc[in_month, "raw_activity"]
            .eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
        "duplicate_count": int(raw.loc[in_month, "duplicate_flag"].sum()),
        "temporal_features": temporal.to_dict(orient="records"),
    }
    return audit

events, raw = load_events("field_service_events.csv")
months = pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-11-01", freq="MS")
auditable_months = [
    monthly_evidence(events, raw, start, end)
    for start, end in zip(months[:-1], months[1:])
]
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

1. **Retraining on immature September or October labels** could teach the model that breaches are falling when long-path labels are merely missing. Gate training on maturity or use an appropriate censoring method.
2. **Calibrating to April’s pipeline artifact** could encode a temporary schema failure into future predictions. Repair and recompute the affected features before using April for calibration.
3. **Treating supplier-driven V3 deterioration as only a mix shift** could leave the model blind to a major operational risk. Test within-path outcomes and incorporate valid process features.
4. **Treating Partner Z’s raw breach gap as proof of a partner effect** ignores slower maturation, path mix, rurality, and device age. Compare mature, stratified cohorts and reweight before partner-specific action.
5. **Treating June’s aggregate breach-rate drop as broad improvement** could prompt needless retraining when V2’s larger share may explain it. Check within-variant metrics before escalating.
