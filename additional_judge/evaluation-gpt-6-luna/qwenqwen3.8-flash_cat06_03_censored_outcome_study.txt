The response covers the required nine sections and correctly identifies that default is only observed after activation. Its primary target—90-day default among activated accounts—is operationally meaningful, and it distinguishes that association from a policy-specific counterfactual. It also addresses selective activation, delayed and missing feeds, transfers, future information leakage, temporal validation, policy and migration changes, and appropriate limitations.

The cohort flow is not fully reconciled. It lists the supplied categories, but does not resolve the apparent overlap between the 21,600 labeled accounts and the 3,600 activated accounts whose outcomes have not matured, nor explain whether the listed counts are mutually exclusive as of the extraction date. The proposed maturity logic also assumes a 35-day feed delay and treats an absent label as a negative once that date passes; this can misclassify permanently missing outcomes. The `last_activity` terminal classification can mistake an activated case’s later event for its application disposition, and default event timing alone does not establish complete follow-up.

The implementation is a useful sketch, but its fixed-prefix features can include events after an application has been rejected, transferred, or activated, making the prediction point invalid for those cases. The elapsed-time prefix does not enforce an explicit prediction time for cases with no events in the window. It also does not actually build a cohort-flow table, deduplicate migration events with a justified rule, or implement the proposed weighting and sensitivity analyses. The model’s reported evaluation therefore remains vulnerable to selection and label-observation bias.
