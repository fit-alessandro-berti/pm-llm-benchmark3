1. Purpose: Assess whether the supplied explanations for predicting a 10-day claim-settlement breach are temporally valid, consistent with the process, and appropriately framed. Provide process-aware replacement explanations for Cases X, Y, and Z using only information available immediately after `Coverage Check Completed`.

2. Audience: Model risk reviewers, process owners, data engineers, and operational leaders responsible for approving or using predictive process monitoring explanations.

3. Scope and assumptions:
   - Prediction time is immediately after the first `Coverage Check Completed` event.
   - Only information available at or before that point is admissible.
   - The supplied process description is normative but simplified; observed deviations may be data errors or genuine exceptions and should be investigated.
   - SHAP-like contributions describe model attribution, not causal effects.
   - No exact accepted feature list is assumed. A feature may be useful predictively, yet unsuitable for a causal claim or operational action.

4. Key findings:
   - The reported global explanation is not fit for use at the stated prediction time. `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, and `full_trace_variant_id` use complete-trace or post-closure information. `final_handler_team` is populated at closure and lacks historical availability evidence. `num_document_requests_total` also depends on later events. These features should be excluded from the coverage-time model or recomputed strictly from the prefix.
   - `final_decision` in Case X is also future information. An appeal can occur only after a Partial or Deny decision, so appeal-related patterns and features are impossible at `t0`; they cannot explain the initial coverage-time prediction.
   - The Case Z pattern `Claim Closed -> Damage Assessment Completed` is future-dependent and has ambiguous ordering because the two events share a timestamp and lack a reliable sequence index. `risk_score_current` has no as-of timestamp, and `last_resource_role` is defined over the complete trace. The current explanation is invalid.
   - Some features may become admissible after lineage repair: queue length with a verified snapshot within the stated tolerance; prior claims using a strict cutoff before `t0`; same-day document counts from a timestamped snapshot that excludes post-`t0` uploads; and a historical handler team only if its value at `t0` can be established.
   - Region, channel, calendar week, reserve, prior claims, and fraud-related signals may have predictive value. Their predictive use requires separate fairness and proxy review; their importance does not show that they cause delays or are appropriate operational levers.
   - Legitimate prefix signals include frozen initial complexity, elapsed time since registration, and valid queue conditions at `t0`. At this point, the normative process has observed registration and coverage completion; later assessment, evidence cycles, investigation, decision, appeal, and closure are future possibilities.

5. Recommended actions:
   - Build an as-of feature view for the precise prediction time and recompute features from trace prefixes. Record feature timestamps and enforce `feature_as_of_time <= t0` in both training and inference.
   - Remove or repair all future-derived features and regenerate global and local explanations. In particular, exclude post-closure duration, complete-trace appeal and request counts, full-trace variants, closure-populated fields without historical timestamps, future decisions, and complete-trace roles.
   - Validate event ordering with reliable sequence indices where timestamps tie. Check prefix availability, process feasibility, complete-trace dependencies, and whether explained activities are observed, enabled, or merely future-possible. Treat repeated `Settlement Decision Recorded` events as legitimate only when the later decision follows an eligible Partial or Deny decision and an appeal.
   - Rebuild Case X’s explanation from valid prefix features such as verified queue length, frozen initial complexity, and elapsed time. Do not cite the future appeal or Partial decision as drivers.
   - Rebuild Case Y’s explanation after replacing the full-trace variant with a prefix-only representation and correcting the prior-claims cutoff. Reserve and region can be described as associations if validated, not as causes.
   - Rebuild Case Z only after resolving event-order ambiguity and obtaining a risk-score snapshot and resource-role value valid at `t0`; otherwise suppress its current explanation.
   - Evaluate predictive performance and calibration after repairs. Separately assess proxy effects, group-level errors, fairness, actionability, and stability over time. Frame explanations around observable process conditions and service configuration, not unsupported causal stories about claimants or regions.
