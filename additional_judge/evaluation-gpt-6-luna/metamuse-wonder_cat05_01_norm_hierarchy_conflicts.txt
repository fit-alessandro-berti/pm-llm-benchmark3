**8.0/10**

The answer is thorough, well organized, and unusually careful about authority, scope, timing, and evidence. It follows the required headings, assesses all eight cases, distinguishes late system entry from late action, and offers credible alternatives and remediation.

The main weakness is the P-501 analysis. The answer treats Plant and Finance approvals as pre-commitment requirements under the local SOP, but B2 does not expressly set that timing. More importantly, B4 permits commitment before the PO in an emergency; its absence-and-delegation condition applies to substituting a Shift Lead for the Plant Manager, not necessarily to the PO sequencing exception. P-501 may still have a segregation-of-duties issue, but the answer’s stated basis for noncompliance is too categorical.

There are also some internal tensions. P-505 is classified as a justified exception despite acknowledging that the record does not establish an independent budget owner or Finance Controller approval; the CFO email’s authority to interpret Finance controls does not clearly establish that joint Incident Command authorization satisfies GPIP-4’s separate budget-owner requirement. P-507 relies on a PDF created within four hours, but could more clearly distinguish the PDF’s creation from proof that the authorization was actually recorded in the durable record required by C3. The proposed clause also says integrity rules may be excepted under paragraph 4 while later prohibiting one form of segregation-of-duties exception, which would benefit from clearer drafting.

These are meaningful analytical and drafting flaws, but the answer identifies most of the uncertainties and explains what evidence or premise could change its conclusions.
