## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January–March are the cleanest baseline: metrics, data quality, and variant mix are relatively stable. V3 already has a higher breach rate than V1 or V2, so aggregate metrics depend on the variant mix.

April is primarily a pipeline incident. The diagnosis rename, duplicate events, and stale derived features make both prediction prefixes and measured features unreliable. Its poor metrics should not be read as evidence of a genuine change in the outcome relationship.

May is a partial recovery after the mapping patch and backfill, but the cached feature was not fully recomputed. Treat it as contaminated until reprocessing confirms otherwise.

June brings a routing-policy shift: V2 rises to 52% and V1 falls to 30%. V2’s breach rate improves and its process time remains stable. This changes the population of predictions and may affect aggregate metrics, but does not by itself show concept drift.

July is an early warning in V3: supplier edge time rises to 65 hours and its matured breach rate to 0.46. Labels are slightly less mature, but the available evidence points to a real process problem beginning alongside label delay.

August shows severe supplier delays and a V3 breach rate of 0.64. V3 now makes up 35% of cases. Worsening AUROC and calibration are consistent with a process shock exposing missing predictors or a changed conditional relationship; aggregate evidence alone cannot distinguish them.

September adds Partner Z, a more rural population with more V3 routing. V3 remains high-risk, and current Partner Z breach labels mature more slowly. The observed source gap therefore mixes population shift, process mix, and label delay. September’s 82% maturity makes it more informative than October, but not a final comparison.

October is not directly comparable on observed outcomes: only 55% of labels are mature, median delay is 52 days, and longer resolution paths are disproportionately affected. Its metrics warrant investigation, but should not trigger a conclusion about true breach rates or retraining.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

- **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting:** Plot cumulative breach rates by time since case start, stratified by month, variant, and source; compare cohorts at the same label age. Under censoring, October estimates should move as labels mature, especially for long paths. Under genuine process deterioration, the mature August–September V3 gap should persist at comparable vintages.

- **Activity normalization and duplicate cleaning:** Normalize both diagnosis labels, apply a documented case/event deduplication rule, and recompute April–May prefixes and derived features from source events. If pipeline failure explains April, its unknown activity, missingness, and metrics should improve after recomputation. If performance remains poor on corrected data, other explanations remain.

- **Stratified calibration and discrimination:** Calculate AUROC, Brier score, and calibration by variant, source, and month on mature, comparable cohorts. A mix-shift explanation predicts more stable within-segment performance than aggregate performance. A changed relationship predicts deterioration within a fixed variant or source.

- **Variant and DFG/performance-DFG comparison:** Compare variant frequencies, directly-follows edges, and edge durations, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`. Supplier-driven process drift predicts longer V3 parts delays and worse V3 outcomes, with more stable V1/V2 paths. A broad model or data issue would also affect paths without parts.

- **Conditional outcome relationship:** Within V3 and comparable source/time cohorts, test whether breach risk conditional on path, part delay, and available case features changed. A changed conditional relationship supports concept drift. Stable conditional relationships with a changed distribution of delays or cases supports covariate or process shift instead.

- **Partner Z transport check:** Reweight or transport-test September cases to comparable rural distance, device age, and variant distributions. If population composition explains much of the gap, adjusted outcomes and metrics should narrow. A persistent within-stratum gap suggests residual source or process differences; maturity must be aligned first.

- **Missingness and lineage audit:** Trace diagnosis prefixes, queue features, duplicate removal, and backfill/recomputation by source and month. A concentrated April–May lineage break supports pipeline failure. Similar lineage and missingness across months would weaken that explanation for later deterioration.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

1. **Immediate containment:** Flag April–May predictions and exclude those records from training or recalibration until features are recomputed and audited. Treat October outcome metrics as provisional; compare only fixed label-age vintages for now.
2. **Short-term recalibration:** Once labels are sufficiently mature and pipeline lineage is sound, assess calibration on recent data by variant and source. Recalibrate only if discrimination and the conditional risk relationship remain useful and the calibration error is stable within segments.
3. **Feature repair:** Add or repair causal-time features available at prediction time, including variant/path indicators, queue state, part-delay context, and relevant rural/device attributes where operationally appropriate. Verify no post-prediction information leaks into features.
4. **Full retraining:** Consider retraining if repaired, mature, within-segment evaluation shows persistent discrimination loss or changed conditional relationships. Compare against the existing model with time-based validation.

January–March are the strongest clean training baseline. June–July may be usable after checking label maturity and the routing-policy shift. April is unsafe until recomputed; May is unsafe until cached features are repaired. August–September are useful for evaluation and eventual training only after maturity and segment checks. October is unsafe for outcome training until labels mature.

V3-specific evaluation is justified by its distinct path and sharp process deterioration. A separate V3 model is not yet justified: first test whether added process features and recalibration address the gap. Partner Z-specific modeling is premature; evaluate transportability and fairness across comparable populations before segmenting models.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

- **Predictive:** Track AUROC and Brier by month, variant, and source on mature, fixed-vintage cohorts.
- **Calibration:** Track ECE and reliability plots by the same strata; distinguish current provisional values from mature values.
- **Label maturity:** Monitor maturity curves, delay distributions, and backlog by variant/source. A delay alert should mark metrics provisional and trigger a maturity review, not retraining by itself.
- **Data quality:** Alert on unknown activities, missing features, duplicates, and lineage/recomputation failures. Block affected data from training until corrected.
- **Process variant:** Track variant and activity-path frequencies, including unmapped paths.
- **Performance-DFG:** Track median and tail edge times for key transitions, especially parts ordering and receipt, by variant and source.
- **Population:** Track source mix and feature distributions such as rural distance and device age; evaluate shifts with reweighting checks.
- **Intervention/deployment:** Route label-delay alerts to delayed evaluation, pipeline alerts to repair/reprocessing, and process/population alerts to stratified investigation. Retraining decisions require mature labels and confirmed within-segment performance or relationship change.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")
raw["event_time"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["event_time"], utc=True)
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
})

# Keep the earliest copy for each case, timestamp, and activity; retain an audit count.
raw = raw.sort_values(["case_id", "event_time", "activity"])
raw["duplicate"] = raw.duplicated(["case_id", "event_time", "activity"], keep="first")
duplicate_audit = raw.groupby("case_id")["duplicate"].sum()
clean = raw.loc[~raw["duplicate"]].drop(columns="duplicate")

events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    clean, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="event_time"
)

# Event-calendar cohort: events occurring in the month; useful for operational workload,
# but cases may span months and are not an outcome cohort.
# Case-start cohort: all events for cases whose first event occurs in the month.
events["event_month"] = events["event_time"].dt.to_period("M")
starts = events.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
events["case_start_month"] = events["case:concept:name"].map(starts.dt.to_period("M"))

reports = []
for month in sorted(events["event_month"].dropna().unique()):
    event_cohort = events[events["event_month"] == month]
    case_ids = events.loc[events["case_start_month"] == month, "case:concept:name"].unique()
    start_cohort = events[events["case:concept:name"].isin(case_ids)]

    # Restrict event-calendar evidence to the month; case-start evidence follows full cases.
    event_cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        event_cohort, str(month.start_time), str(month.end_time), mode="events"
    )
    dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(start_cohort)
    perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(start_cohort)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(start_cohort)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(start_cohort)

    reports.append({
        "month": str(month),
        "event_cases": event_cohort["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
        "case_start_cases": len(case_ids),
        "dfg": dfg,
        "performance_dfg": perf_dfg,
        "variant_counts": len(variants),
        "temporal_features": temporal,
        "duplicate_count": int(duplicate_audit.sum()),
    })

monthly_process_evidence = pd.DataFrame(reports)
```

## 6. Risks of Acting on the Wrong Diagnosis

Retraining on immature October labels could teach the model from censored outcomes. Calibrating on April’s broken prefixes could encode pipeline artifacts into production predictions. Treating June’s variant mix change as concept drift could lead to unnecessary retraining.

Conversely, attributing August–September deterioration only to label delay or population composition could leave the supplier-driven V3 risk unaddressed. Partner Z’s higher observed breach rate should not be treated as a mature or adjusted source effect without aligned label vintages and like-for-like comparisons.
