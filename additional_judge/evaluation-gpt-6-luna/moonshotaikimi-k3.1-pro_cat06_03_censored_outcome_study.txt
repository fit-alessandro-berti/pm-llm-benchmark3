The response gives a statistically careful, operationally defensible primary target: 90-day default among accounts activated under the observed policy, with a clearly defined prediction landmark and explicit limits on generalizing to every application. It distinguishes that estimand from all-application competing-outcome and policy-specific counterfactual targets, and it does not mislabel rejected, withdrawn, or open applications as non-default.

It also addresses the key risks well: cohort accounting, maturity and delayed feeds, right censoring, activation selection, transfers, prefix leakage, policy and migration changes, and post-outcome fields. The PM4Py-oriented skeleton is unusually concrete and includes temporal splits, leakage checks, and process features.

The main weakness is that parts of the code appear more complete than the stated schema supports. In particular, it relies on a stop-event set and fields such as activation and terminal times without showing robust status reconciliation for renamed activities or incomplete event histories. The cohort flow also risks presenting the supplied counts as fully reconciled without demonstrating how overlapping categories are resolved. These are material implementation and accounting caveats, but the response generally identifies the assumptions and limitations rather than hiding them.
