The answer recognizes the central issue: default is observed only after activation, so rejected, withdrawn, and open applications cannot be labeled non-default. It also identifies important leakage risks and proposes temporal validation. But several errors undermine the design and implementation.

- The primary estimand is internally inconsistent. It defines risk conditional on eventual activation for all cases reaching the prediction point, then excludes cases that do not activate. That yields a selected, post-outcome cohort and does not directly support prediction at the stated early decision time. It needs to specify how predictions for not-yet-activated cases are evaluated and how conditioning on future activation affects interpretation.
- The cohort-flow table assigns all 3,600 activated but immature accounts to right censoring without distinguishing whether the 90-day horizon has elapsed but the delayed feed has not arrived. Delayed labels and event-time censoring are not interchangeable. The proposed “censor until `label_available_at` or extraction date” also does not give a coherent event-time survival rule.
- Transfers are simultaneously described as competing outcomes and as censoring, without a clear primary treatment. The IPAW proposal is not justified: weighting for activation does not recover default risk for rejected applicants without strong, untestable assumptions and a defined counterfactual target.
- The prediction-time rule is ambiguous for cases with fewer than three events, and the treatment of early terminal cases leaks their terminal outcome into the prefix features. In particular, calling out Reject/Withdraw in a “terminal prefix” makes the early-warning target depend on a later process outcome.
- The validation dates do not ensure maturity for the full test period. Cases starting as late as 1 November 2025 may not have a 90-day outcome plus the maximum 35-day feed delay by the 15 February 2026 extraction date. The test also mixes pre- and post-migration data without a clear migration-specific evaluation.
- The code’s maturity flag uses start time rather than activation time and does not account for outcome-feed delay. Its leakage assertion fails on any raw post-decision values instead of filtering them by the per-case prediction cutoff. It calls `get_prefixes_from_log` on the full log without enforcing either the elapsed-time rule or the as-of feature rule; the train log and temporal features likewise include future events. It also does not produce the requested 48,000-case flow accounting.

These are material problems in the target semantics, censoring, validation, and the core as-of implementation. The answer has useful safeguards, but does not provide a coherent, executable study design as requested.
