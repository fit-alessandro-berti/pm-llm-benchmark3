## 2. Raw-versus-normalized interpretation

The raw-to-mapped fitness gap in March is large: raw fitness falls from 0.93 in February to 0.55, while mapped fitness is 0.90. This strongly supports a migration-related semantic contribution: mapping `Eligibility Screen` to `Validate Eligibility` and removing duplicate `Open Case` events restores much of the prior conformance score. It does not show that the business process was unchanged. Fitness is relative to the chosen normative model, and an alias mapping can conceal a genuine change if the new label represents a different activity or behavior.

The duplicate technical event and missing resource identifiers affect different analyses. Duplicate `Open Case` events can distort traces, directly-follow relations, variants, and conformance unless deduplicated. Missing resources undermine resource and team comparisons, workload attribution, and staffing analyses; because missingness is concentrated in migrated activities, treating it as random could bias those comparisons.

Post-May evidence cannot be dismissed as logging drift alone. After label and duplicate-event normalization, mapped fitness remains roughly 0.86–0.90, but the normalized `Assess→Request Info` rate rises from 12 per 100 cases in March to 24 in May and 29 in July. Rework variants also rise, while cycle times and reconstructed queue waits increase. These signals are based on operational tables or normalized relations and align with the supplier disruption and assessor departures. They support real process and performance changes, though aggregates do not identify each cause’s independent effect.

# Part B: reusable event-level PM4Py analysis functions
import pm4py

def format_log(df):
    """Format a copy of an event dataframe for PM4Py."""
    required = {"case_id", "activity", "timestamp"}
    missing = required - set(df.columns)
    if missing:
        raise ValueError(f"Missing required columns: {sorted(missing)}")
    work = df.copy()
    work["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(work["timestamp"], utc=True)
    return pm4py.format_dataframe(
        work,
        case_id="case_id",
        activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )

def slice_time_range(log, start, end):
    start, end = pd.Timestamp(start, tz="UTC"), pd.Timestamp(end, tz="UTC")
    return pm4py.filter_time_range(log, start, end, mode="events")

def discover_monthly_dfg(log, start, end):
    sliced = slice_time_range(log, start, end)
    return pm4py.discover_dfg(sliced)

    sliced = slice_time_range(log, start, end)

def raw_vs_normalized_mapping(log):
    """Compare raw and mapped variants on copies; leave the original untouched."""

## 9. Provisional attribution

The evidence supports several overlapping drift mechanisms with distinct onsets. March’s raw fitness collapse is primarily a logging and semantic effect: the migration introduced an eligibility-label alias, duplicate technical events, and more missing resource identifiers. Mapping the alias and removing duplicates recovers much of fitness, but that does not prove business behavior stayed constant.

From May, the evidence supports real control-flow change: Supplier Z’s share rises sharply alongside rework variants and `Assess→Request Info` rates. Supplier mix is non-random and Supplier Z cases were already somewhat more complex, so the supplier disruption’s independent effect is uncertain. After the June assessor departures, increasing queue waits and cycle times, with only modest growth in assessment service time, support a capacity-related performance effect. The July priority rule plausibly redistributed waits across priority classes, but aggregate monthly values cannot show who benefited.

Renewal season adds higher volume and complexity in October and November. December improvement coincides with lower load, overtime, and the quality campaign, so their contributions cannot be disentangled here. These observations argue for multiple mechanism-specific change points rather than a single global break. Case-level, stratified process and queue analysis is needed to quantify the mechanisms and their interactions.

## 10. Monitoring decision rules

1. **IF** unexpected activity labels appear or mapped label shares depart from their validated range, **THEN** inspect source schema and mapping, **BECAUSE** the March migration showed that label changes can sharply lower raw fitness without an equivalent mapped decline.

2. **IF** duplicate-event rates exceed event-specific control limits, **THEN** audit event ingestion and idempotency, **BECAUSE** duplicate `Open Case` events distort traces and conformance.

3. **IF** normalized `Assess→Request Info` or repeat-request rates rise persistently within supplier and complexity strata, **THEN** inspect document quality and loop duration, **BECAUSE** supplier disruption is associated with the post-May rise in rework.

4. **IF** queue waits or p90 cycle times worsen while assessment service times remain stable within comparable strata, **THEN** review staffing, arrivals, and queue allocation, **BECAUSE** this pattern is consistent with capacity-related delay.

5. **IF** waits diverge between priority classes after routing changes, **THEN** assess both classes’ wait distributions and outcomes, **BECAUSE** the priority rule may transfer delay across groups while aggregate means conceal it.

6. **IF** case mix changes materially, **THEN** report both observed and seasonally standardized process metrics, **BECAUSE** renewal season changes volume and high-complexity share.

7. **IF** missing-resource rates rise by activity or team, **THEN** audit identity propagation and quantify sensitivity of workload estimates to missingness, **BECAUSE** migration-related missingness is concentrated and non-random.

8. **IF** mapped fitness or alignment deviations change after mapping is validated, **THEN** review traces and process constraints by segment, **BECAUSE** normalized conformance can identify residual behavioral change.

9. **IF** outcome or SLA rates shift after adjusting for case mix and calendar season, **THEN** review decision quality and downstream results, **BECAUSE** flow and timing changes matter only in relation to service outcomes.

10. **IF** December-like improvements coincide with both load reduction and interventions, **THEN** compare against same-season history or a suitable control group, **BECAUSE** concurrent changes prevent clean attribution to one intervention.
