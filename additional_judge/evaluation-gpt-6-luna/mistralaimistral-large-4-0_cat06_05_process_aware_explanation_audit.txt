## 2. Process-model consistency checks

Run a point-in-time audit for every feature and explanation contribution. Record `t0`, feature as-of time, source event time, and any extraction or refresh time. Reject a feature when its lineage shows a value became available after `t0`; treat missing historical timestamps as unverified rather than assuming the value was available.

Check observed prefixes against the normative process with prefix conformance and alignment checks. At X, Y, and Z, the only observed activities are Claim Registered and Coverage Check Completed. Later activities—including evidence cycles, damage assessment, fraud investigation, settlement decision, appeal, and closure—are future-possible where the process permits them, but they have not occurred and cannot explain a prediction made at `t0`. A future-possible activity is not an observed predictor.

Use process alignments and declarative constraints to flag impossible ordering, including closure before damage assessment. Use DFGs and footprints to identify unusual edges and common variants, but treat observed frequency as a data-quality signal rather than proof of normative validity. Validate paths against explicit process rules, including that Appeal follows Partial or Deny and that Claim Closed follows a final decision or withdrawal.

For identical timestamps, require a reliable sequence index before asserting order. If one is missing, mark the order ambiguous and suppress order-dependent patterns. Validate whether a coarse `Settlement Decision Recorded` label occurs twice only when an appeal leads to reconsideration; distinguish the initial decision from the post-appeal decision using event context or a richer event label.

For every pattern contribution, report whether its activities are already observed in the prefix, enabled as a next step, impossible under the process, or merely possible later. This prevents a valid future transition from being misrepresented as evidence available at `t0`.

## 3. Corrected interpretation of global importance

The reported ranking is not a ranking of trustworthy early predictors: several leading features use complete-trace or post-closure information. Remove `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, and `full_trace_variant_id` from the `t0` model. The lineage of `final_handler_team` is also insufficient for use until replaced with a historically verified team snapshot at `t0`.

After leakage repair and model recomputation, plausible early signals include timestamp-validated queue length, frozen initial damage complexity, elapsed time since registration, and calendar week subject to policy and holiday analysis. Region and channel are available but may act as proxies requiring fairness and robustness review. Prior claim count and same-day document count require point-in-time repair; they are not valid merely because their feature names suggest past or same-day data.

The remaining features may have predictive value without supporting the team’s causal stories. Importance does not show that appeals cause breaches, that a final team determines delay, that region drives delay, or that congestion itself causes breaches. Operational actionability requires evidence that an intervention is feasible and changes the outcome; a predictive association alone is insufficient.

1. **P0: Suspend current explanations and remove confirmed leakage.** Remove post-closure duration, complete-trace appeal and request counts, full-trace variant, future decision, and future sequence patterns from the `t0` model. Suppress the ambiguous closure-to-assessment pattern.

2. **P1: Repair point-in-time lineage.** Store feature as-of and source event timestamps. Rebuild handler team, prior claims, document counts, risk score, resource role, and employee experience using values available at `t0`. Verify initial reserve lineage before use.

3. **P1: Recompute from prefixes.** Rebuild features from events at or before `t0`, then regenerate predictions and explanations. Decompose base-value aggregates into named contributions.

4. **P2: Add process-conformance validation.** Check feature availability, feasible event order, same-timestamp ambiguity, complete-trace dependencies, activity status at `t0`, and legitimate repeated decisions after appeal. Use alignments, footprints, DFGs, and declarative checks against explicit process rules.

5. **P2: Retrain or recalibrate after repair.** Measure predictive performance and calibration after removing or repairing features. Predictive validity should be assessed on held-out, time-appropriate data.

6. **P3: Review proxies and fairness.** Evaluate region, channel, and repaired prior-claim features for proxy effects, robustness, and disparate impact. Availability can support predictive use but does not settle fairness or causal interpretation.

7. **P3: Validate explanation stability and actionability.** Check stability across folds and perturbations. Treat importance as association; require separate intervention evidence before claiming an operational action will reduce breaches.

8. **P4: Monitor drift and data quality.** Track feature distributions, policy and holiday effects, event-order conformance, timestamp ambiguity, and point-in-time lineage failures.
