# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

1. **Tail latency:** Faster routine cases can hold the average steady while p90/p99 waits and cycle times worsen.
2. **High-complexity cases:** These are deliberately routed to the shared technical queue and may accumulate queue time or additional handovers that are hidden in overall results.
3. **Local knowledge loss:** Averages can miss more referrals, repeated customer explanations, and rework caused by losing regional conventions or early informal advice.
4. **Fairness gaps:** An overall improvement can conceal longer waits or worse outcomes for East, North/South, particular locations, language-sensitive customers, or specialty groups.
5. **Learning effects:** If local staff stop handling difficult cases, their technical skills may erode gradually. This may not affect early cycle-time results but can reduce future capacity and resilience.
6. **Accountability diffusion:** Shared approval may produce consistent decisions while making it harder to identify who owns a stalled case or communicates a decision to the customer.
7. **Exception handling:** Standard routes may work for common cases but fail for unusual combinations of specialty, complexity, or regional need. Exception variants and repeated transfers can reveal this.
8. **Resilience and hidden work:** A central pool can cover individual absences, yet also become a single point of failure. Unlogged local advice may disappear without appearing as a formal handover or delay.
9. **Quality checks:** Faster completion can mask delayed or weakened quality review if difficult checks are added to an already busy shared queue.

# 5. Pilot design

**Scope:** Pilot the Technical Shared Service first for high-complexity cases in East, where E_Tech is already highly utilized. Keep North and South on the current technical model initially. Treat shared approval and the centralization of difficult Quality Check as later phases, so the pilot can distinguish the effect of technical pooling. Track Legal/Risk demand and waits during the pilot because technical triage may change referrals to those specialists.

**Comparison and observation windows:** Use a staggered rollout if feasible: East enters the pilot first, while North and South provide comparison data. Collect at least 8–12 weeks of pre-pilot data and 8–12 weeks after launch; extend the post period if volumes are too small for reliable segment comparisons. Compare similar calendar periods and control for case volume, priority, and case mix. Report both absolute changes and differences relative to the comparison teams.

**Segmentation:** Report results by team, location, complexity, required specialty, priority, and customer segment. Separate high-complexity and legal/risk/security cases from routine work, and identify cases requiring language or regional accommodation.

**Guardrails:** Monitor p50/p90/p99 queue waits and cycle times; high-complexity SLA misses; rework after specialist advice; repeat handovers and reassignments; queue WIP and utilization; customer satisfaction and outcomes; fairness gaps across teams and segments; local technical participation; and explicit case ownership.

**Staff feedback:** Collect weekly short surveys from caseworkers, specialists, approvers, and implementation staff. Interview staff at weeks 2, 6, and 12. Review cases with repeated handovers, high reassignment counts, or customer repeat contacts to find issues that the log cannot explain.

**Adjustment or rollback triggers:** Agree thresholds before launch. For example, investigate if high-complexity p90 cycle time or SLA misses worsen materially against the comparison group for two consecutive weeks; if rework rises by more than a pre-agreed margin; if shared-queue WIP grows faster than arrivals; if utilization remains near saturation while waits rise; or if fairness gaps or role-clarity reports deteriorate. Restore local liaison capacity, narrow routing, add triage, or pause the rollout if problems persist.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first, with modifications to the proposal.** The pooled model may help East, improve cover, and make decisions more consistent. The estimated increases in technical referrals, approval referrals, and Legal/Risk referrals, together with longer expected waits, make a full rollout premature—especially given that Legal/Risk is already near capacity.

Before the pilot, management should define queue ownership and approver accountability, preserve a local technical escalation path, ensure queue and handover events are logged consistently, and set capacity and fairness guardrails. Expand only if high-complexity tail performance, rework, SLA outcomes, fairness gaps, and role clarity remain acceptable—not because utilization rises or the overall median improves.

Management should not infer success from early averages. Routine cases can improve while complex cases wait longer; downstream rework, customer effects, skill loss, and accountability problems may take longer to emerge.

# 7. PM4Py analysis sketch

The sketch assumes the log includes a case-level pilot/control and period label, and explicit queue-entry and service-start events or equivalent timestamps. If queue events are not logged, handover-to-next-event time is only a proxy for queue waiting, since it may include other work. Case attributes should be consistent across a case before filtering.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

CASE = "case:concept:name"
ACT = "concept:name"
TS = "time:timestamp"
RES = "org:resource"

df = pd.read_csv("event_log.csv")
df[TS] = pd.to_datetime(df[TS])

# Add period and pilot/control labels from the rollout plan.
# Preserve team, case_complexity, required_specialty,
# customer_segment, priority, outcome, rework_flag,
# handover_reason, reassignment_count, and local_expertise_flag.
df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    df,
    case_id=CASE,
    activity_key=ACT,
    timestamp_key=TS,
    resource_key=RES,
)
df = df.sort_values([CASE, TS])

def make_log(frame):
    return pm4py.convert_to_event_log(frame)

# Split by case-level labels; avoid splitting a case's events across periods.
case_labels = df.groupby(CASE)[["period", "pilot_group"]].first()
before_ids = case_labels.index[case_labels["period"] == "before"]
after_ids = case_labels.index[case_labels["period"] == "after"]
before_log = make_log(df[df[CASE].isin(before_ids)])
after_log = make_log(df[df[CASE].isin(after_ids)])

# Repeat these analyses for pilot/control and for each segment.
def org_outputs(log):
    return {
        "handover": pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(log),
        "working_together": pm4py.discover_working_together_network(log),
        "subcontracting": pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(log),
        "resource_similarity":
            pm4py.discover_activity_based_resource_similarity(log),
        "roles": pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(log),
    }

before_org = org_outputs(before_log)
after_org = org_outputs(after_log)

def process_outputs(log):
    dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    performance_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    with_service_wait = pm4py.insert_case_service_waiting_time(log)
    service_time = pm4py.get_service_time(log)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(with_service_wait)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    rework = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(log)
    return {
        "dfg": dfg,
        "performance_dfg": performance_dfg,
        "service_wait_log": with_service_wait,
        "service_time": service_time,
        "temporal_features": temporal,
        "variants": variants,
        "rework": rework,
    }

before_process = process_outputs(before_log)
after_process = process_outputs(after_log)

# Filter to compare teams, complexity, specialty, and customer segment.
def filter_cases(log, attribute, values):
    return pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values(
        log, attribute, values, level="case"
    )

east_high = filter_cases(after_log, "team", ["East"])
east_high = filter_cases(east_high, "case_complexity", ["high"])

legal_risk_security = filter_cases(
    after_log, "required_specialty", ["legal", "risk", "security"]
)
segment_cases = filter_cases(after_log, "customer_segment", ["segment_value"])

# Compare variants, including paths with Rework or repeated specialist activity.
before_variants = pm4py.get_variants(before_log)
after_variants = pm4py.get_variants(after_log)
rework_variants = [
    variant for variant in after_variants if "Rework" in str(variant)
]
if rework_variants:
    after_rework_paths = pm4py.filter_variants(after_log, rework_variants)

# Example case-level summary for cycle time, SLA, and fairness.
events = pm4py.convert_to_dataframe(after_log).sort_values([CASE, TS])
case_summary = events.groupby(CASE).agg(
    start=(TS, "min"),
    end=(TS, "max"),
    team=("team", "first"),
    complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
    specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
    segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
    priority=("priority", "first"),
    reassignments=("reassignment_count", "max"),
    rework=("rework_flag", "max"),
    outcome=("outcome", "last"),
).reset_index()

case_summary["cycle_hours"] = (
    case_summary["end"] - case_summary["start"]
).dt.total_seconds() / 3600

# Use the actual SLA rule or logged SLA outcome; do not invent a threshold.
# Report median and tail cycle time and SLA/rework by each segment.
summary = case_summary.groupby(
    ["team", "complexity", "specialty", "segment"]
).agg(
    cases=(CASE, "count"),
    median_hours=("cycle_hours", "median"),
    p90_hours=("cycle_hours", lambda x: x.quantile(0.90)),
    p99_hours=("cycle_hours", lambda x: x.quantile(0.99)),
    rework_rate=("rework", "mean"),
    mean_reassignments=("reassignments", "mean"),
).reset_index()

# Where timestamps and queue events are available, calculate queue-entry
# to service-start waits directly. Otherwise, treat next-event gaps as
# approximate handover waits and label them accordingly.
```
