6.0/10 — The answer is comprehensive and follows the requested structure, with strong attention to rollout selection, changing use, process mechanisms, uncertainty, and the limits of branch-month aggregates. It also offers a sensible embedded evaluation and a detailed reproducible-analysis script.

The main weakness is quantitative discipline: several reported ranges and summaries are not clearly derived from the stated contrasts, and the “raw DiD” comparisons rely on only two never-treated branches despite substantial differences and divergent trends. The proposed panel regressions adjust for post-rollout variables that may be mediators, and the event-time indicators are constructed but not used in the model. The rollout recommendation also proposes case-level randomization without fully addressing how that design would estimate branch availability effects or manage shared-queue interference. These issues weaken the causal conclusions, especially the suggestion of a median-cycle improvement.
