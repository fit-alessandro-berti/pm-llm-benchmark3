## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity reflects the business consequence of the observed or plausible deviation, not its edit distance. I use this scale:

- **5 — Critical:** A major approval or payment control appears bypassed, with substantial exposure or an irreversible transaction.
- **4 — High:** A material control or evidence gap creates significant exposure or regulatory concern, though the underlying activity may have occurred.
- **3 — Moderate:** A meaningful process or evidence issue warrants timely investigation, but harm or a control breach is uncertain or limited.
- **2 — Low:** A minor operational or recordkeeping issue with limited likely harm.
- **1 — Negligible:** Harmless telemetry, a fitting silent move, or a mismatch explained by scope or logging, with no identified control consequence.

I consider control purpose, payment amount and exposure, reversibility, timing, potential customer or regulatory harm, recurrence, evidence reliability, and whether the case belongs in the model’s scope. Confidence describes how well the evidence supports the interpretation; it is separate from severity. A severe potential failure can have low confidence when the log is incomplete.

## 6. Why Alignment Cost Is Not Business Risk

Raw alignment cost sums visible log-only and model-only moves under a uniform technical cost. It does not encode the importance of the control, the claim amount, policy attributes, timing, reversibility, customer harm, or evidence quality.

- **CL-609 and CL-610 both cost 0**, because the tree permits Auto Approve and every event is synchronous. Yet the amount in CL-609 exceeds the policy limit, and CL-610 has a recent bank change. Those data conditions are not represented by control-flow alignment.
- **CL-604 costs 3**, but its main concern is that the bank accepted payment before recorded approval. The business risk depends on sequencing, control purpose, and potential recovery, not the number of inserted or skipped moves.
- **CL-602 costs 1**, although a required fraud-screen event is missing for a EUR 480,000 high-risk claim. The small edit distance says nothing about the exposure or whether an informal check met policy.
- **CL-601 costs 3**, even though its extra moves are UI telemetry that does not represent claim-processing activity. Uniform event weights make such noise look like a larger deviation.
- **CL-605 costs 0** because the hidden `tau` move is silent and fitting. A hidden move describes model behavior; it does not establish that every associated control was performed.
- **CL-607 and CL-612 cost 4** because the payable-claim model expects steps after a denial or withdrawal. If those terminal outcomes are valid and out of scope, the high cost measures a scope mismatch rather than a business failure.
- **CL-608 costs 1** because the core log lacks notification, even though a linked portal message exists at +3 hours. The evidence is plausible but weakened by its mutable link; neither the cost nor the missing core event resolves whether notification was valid.
- **CL-611 costs 2** because event labels do not match. If the underlying activity is the same, the cost reflects a mapping problem rather than a missed assessment.

Alignment cost is therefore useful as a description of model-to-log mismatch under a specified cost function. It is not a calibrated measure of compliance risk, financial exposure, customer harm, or confidence. Business judgment must account for data conditions and evidence, activity granularity, duplicate transport events, process scope, and the irreversibility of actions such as payment.

## 7. PM4Py Reproduction and Aggregation Code

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

PROCESS_TREE = """-> ( 'Register Claim', 'Validate Coverage', 'Assess Loss',
    X ( -> ( 'Fraud Screen', 'Manual Approve' ), 'Auto Approve' ),
    'Pay Claim', 'Notify Claimant' )"""

# Parse the supplied tree and convert it to an accepting Petri net.
tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(PROCESS_TREE)
net, initial_marking, final_marking = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(tree)

# Construct a small event log from the supplied business traces.
# This example preserves the supplied visible business-event order for selected cases.
trace_events = {
    "CL-601": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-602": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Manual Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-604": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Pay Claim", "Manual Approve", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-609": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
    "CL-610": ["Register Claim", "Validate Coverage", "Assess Loss",
               "Auto Approve", "Pay Claim", "Notify Claimant"],
}
events = [
    {"case:concept:name": case_id, "concept:name": activity, "time:timestamp": i}
    for case_id, activities in trace_events.items()
    for i, activity in enumerate(activities)
]
event_df = pd.DataFrame(events)
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    event_df,
    case_id="case:concept:name",
    activity_key="concept:name",
    timestamp_key="time:timestamp",
)
alignments = pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
    log, net, initial_marking, final_marking
)
print("Computed alignments:", len(alignments))

# Supplied move summaries and case attributes. These are diagnostic inputs;
# they are not recomputed from the simplified event log above.
rows = [
    ("CL-601", 3, 1200, "LOW",  "UI telemetry", False),
    ("CL-602", 1, 480000, "HIGH", "missing fraud evidence", True),
    ("CL-603", 1, 3200, "LOW",  "notification retry duplicate", False),
    ("CL-604", 3, 90000, "MEDIUM", "payment before approval", True),
    ("CL-605", 0, 800, "LOW", "silent tau move", False),
    ("CL-606", 1, 75000, "HIGH", "external assessment not in core log", False),
    ("CL-607", 4, 0, "LOW", "denial scope mismatch", False),
    ("CL-608", 1, 250000, "HIGH", "external notification evidence", False),
    ("CL-609", 0, 18000, "MEDIUM", "auto approval over amount limit", True),
    ("CL-610", 0, 4500, "LOW", "auto approval after recent bank change", True),
    ("CL-611", 2, 62000, "HIGH", "Assess Damage / Assess Loss label mismatch", False),
    ("CL-612", 4, 2000, "LOW", "withdrawal scope mismatch", False),
]
summary = pd.DataFrame(rows, columns=[
    "case", "raw_cost", "amount_eur", "risk_level", "move_pattern",
    "policy_overlay_flag"
])

# Aggregate move patterns, costs, amounts, risk levels, and policy flags.
pattern_summary = (
    summary.groupby("move_pattern", dropna=False)
    .agg(
        cases=("case", "count"),
        total_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
        total_amount_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"),
        policy_flagged_cases=("policy_overlay_flag", "sum"),
        risk_levels=("risk_level", lambda s: ",".join(sorted(set(s))),
    )
    .reset_index()
)
cost_by_risk = (
    summary.groupby("risk_level")
    .agg(cases=("case", "count"), total_raw_cost=("raw_cost", "sum"),
         total_amount_eur=("amount_eur", "sum"))
    .reset_index()
)

# Keep judgmental priority separate from technical alignment cost.
severity = {
    "CL-601": 1, "CL-602": 5, "CL-603": 1, "CL-604": 5,
    "CL-605": 1, "CL-606": 3, "CL-607": 1, "CL-608": 2,
    "CL-609": 4, "CL-610": 5, "CL-611": 2, "CL-612": 1,
}
summary["business_severity"] = summary["case"].map(severity)
priority_evidence = summary[
    ["case", "raw_cost", "amount_eur", "risk_level",
     "policy_overlay_flag", "business_severity", "move_pattern"]
].sort_values(["business_severity", "amount_eur"], ascending=[False, False])

print(pattern_summary.to_string(index=False))
print(cost_by_risk.to_string(index=False))
print(priority_evidence.to_string(index=False))

# Alternative optimal alignments or PM4Py formatting may differ from the
# supplied authoritative alignment notation. Keep computed technical results
# separate from the supplied moves and the narrative severity assessment.
```
