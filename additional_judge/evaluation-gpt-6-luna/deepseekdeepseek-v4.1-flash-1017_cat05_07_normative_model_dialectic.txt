## 1. Stipulated Facts, Disputed Propositions, and Burden of Proof

**Established by the record:** CS-9 is a controlled, formally approved standard. It requires sanctions and quality checks before activation, manual approval before activation, and different humans for the requester and manual approver. M0 represents some of those control-flow requirements, but not the identity rule. Its sanctions and quality checks are unordered relative to each other, so either may occur first; both must precede the decision. The approved branch orders manual approval before activation. Notification precedes closure.

PD-3 has a documented steering-committee decision, a fixed June–July effective window, and stated eligibility conditions. It conflicts with CS-9’s manual-approval requirement and permits quality review after provisional activation. Whether it was authorized to create an exception to CS-9 is disputed: the compliance observer called it a monitored exception, then said she had not approved weakening CS-9. FD-7 expressly allowed provisional activation before quality review and manual approval, but only for critical flood-relief suppliers during its exact effective window and subject to sanctions, spend, monitoring, and retrospective-review conditions.

The CRM migration caused a substantial rise in missing notifications in the onboarding log. An absent log event therefore does not establish that no notification occurred. Conversely, a mutable CRM link does not reliably establish that a particular message belongs to a particular case. “Same activity” labels, duplicate events, requester identity, and case scope also require evidence beyond the raw trace.

**Disputed or unproved:** Whether rejected leads fall within M0; whether reactivation is onboarding under CS-9; whether PD-3 had authority to override CS-9; and whether post-expiry flood recovery justified action under any other authorized exception. Operations’ belief that a permanent amendment was imminent does not establish one. The facts do not establish that the spouse relationship in SUP-715 was prohibited, only that a conflict-of-interest disclosure was required and none was recorded.

**Burden of proof:** Declare a violation only when the applicable rule, its scope, the relevant event order or resource fact, and the absence of a valid exception are supported by reliable evidence. The party invoking an exception should establish its authority, effective dates, case eligibility, and required safeguards. An exception should not be inferred from custom, operational need, or an anticipated amendment. Missing or mutable data should result in an indeterminate finding where it prevents a reliable conclusion, rather than being treated as proof of misconduct.

## 2. Prosecution Brief

CS-9 remains the controlling formal standard on this record. A pilot decision not incorporated into CS-9 may authorize monitored experimentation if the steering committee had delegated authority, but that authority is not established here. The compliance observer’s “monitored exception” language supports the defense; her later qualification weakens it. At most, this makes eligible pilot cases disputable. It cannot justify clearly ineligible cases or continued use after expiry.

SUP-703 is not a close pilot-eligibility question. The supplier was high-risk, foreign, and outside the approved corporate group. The operator’s pilot selection could not make it eligible. Auto approval replaced manual approval, and activation occurred without that approval. A EUR 180,000 first purchase makes the deviation especially consequential.

SUP-708 activated a high-risk supplier before sanctions clearance, with no applicable pilot or emergency exception. The fact that sanctions clearance followed 40 minutes later and no transactions occurred in the interval reduces demonstrated harm, but not the breach: CS-9 requires clearance before activation.

SUP-705 used the emergency sequence after FD-7 expired. The factual continuation of the flood emergency does not extend an automatically expiring directive. Without a written extension or another authorized basis, the manual approval and quality review occurred too late. SUP-714 likewise used Auto_Approve after PD-3 expired, despite the absence of a permanent amendment. “Permanent approval was imminent” is not an effective policy instrument.

The aggregate figures make informal normalization a material control concern: 14 of 312 pilot-period Auto_Approve cases were clearly ineligible, nine lack eligibility attributes, and six more cases used Auto_Approve after the pilot. This is not simply a case of an outdated model producing noisy deviations. The ineligible cases and post-expiry cases indicate that operational practice sometimes exceeded even the stated pilot boundary.

The prosecution should nevertheless concede that raw M0 deviation counts overstate misconduct. M0 cannot express the recognized Request_More_Info practice, has a defective or at least unclear rejection scope, omits reactivation rules and resource constraints, and is applied to incomplete notification data. Those limitations weaken broad statistical claims based solely on trace fitness; they do not excuse the specific activation-order violations.

## 3. Defense Brief

M0 is not a complete representation of the governing operating environment. Its unordered sanctions and quality checks correctly permit either check to occur first, but its placement of both before the XOR decision also forces quality review before rejection. That is a poor fit for a failed sanctions pre-screen. CS-9 permits abbreviated pre-screening for rejected leads and does not resolve whether those leads are onboarding cases governed by M0.

The model also lacks the repeated Request_More_Info behavior used in 47 cases. None of those cases activated before the requested evidence arrived, and Compliance considers the behavior desirable. SUP-707 therefore shows a model gap, not an unsafe shortcut. M0 also omits the expressly required requester/approver identity constraint, which means a trace can fit M0 while violating CS-9, as SUP-712 illustrates.

SUP-702 has a stronger exception case than the prosecution acknowledges: the decision was made by a steering committee, recorded in minutes and follow-up email, included an effective window and eligibility conditions, and was described by the observer as a monitored exception. This supports treating a fully eligible case within the window as an authorized pilot exception, although the later qualification leaves the authority question unresolved. SUP-704 is stronger still: FD-7 expressly authorized the observed order and the evidence supports its time, scope, spend cap, monitoring, and retrospective checks.

The CRM migration makes missing notification events unreliable evidence of a process omission. In SUP-706, a CRM message exists, but its mutable link is weak case-association evidence; this is an evidential gap, not proof either way. SUP-713’s renamed QA_Clearance is supported by audit evidence as substantively equivalent to Quality_Review. SUP-711’s duplicate register event shares a source transaction ID and is a migration defect, not a second registration action.

Reactivation in SUP-710 is not explicitly covered by CS-9, and the prior quality certification was still valid. Treating every reactivation as a new onboarding without a rule about certification validity would overclaim. Likewise, SUP-709’s scope cannot be settled from the current ambiguous document. These are reasons to define scope and event semantics, not to declare the whole operation compliant.

The defense must still concede that operational practice does not amend policy by repetition. SUP-703 is ineligible; SUP-705 is after FD-7’s expiry; SUP-708 violates the sanctions-before-activation rule; and SUP-714 is after PD-3’s expiry. Business need and favorable outcomes do not supply missing authority.

**System-level judgment:** The operation is materially noncompliant in specific, important respects, especially SUP-703 and SUP-708, with additional unauthorized post-expiry use in SUP-705 and SUP-714 and a segregation-of-duties violation in SUP-712. The full operation should not be characterized as compliant merely because many cases fit an obsolete trace model. At the same time, raw M0 deviations are not a sound measure of overall misconduct: pilot and emergency overlays, information-request cycles, label changes, duplicate events, unclear scope, and the CRM migration each affect interpretation.

M0 is a useful baseline for the standard route but is not a valid complete model of the operation or its exception governance. I reject the prosecution’s broad reliance on literal trace deviation where scope or collection is defective. I reject the defense’s reliance on normalized practice, continuing operational need, and likely future policy changes where formal authority had expired or eligibility was absent.

## 6. Revised Normative Architecture

1. **Control-flow model:** Define separate standard, approved pilot, emergency provisional, rejection, reactivation, and withdrawal paths. Make sanctions and quality checks concurrent in the standard path, with both required before manual approval and activation. Put manual approval before standard activation. Model notification before closure. Add an explicit repeated information-request mechanism.

2. **Data-aware route conditions:** Outside the control-flow model, check risk, country, approved-group membership, critical-relief status, effective date, amount cap, provisional status, and required monitoring against a versioned policy overlay. Match overlays using event time and eligibility snapshots. Missing eligibility data should not silently qualify a case for an exception.

3. **Resource and conflict controls:** Check requester/approver human identity across aliases, including delegation evidence. Check required COI disclosures and recusals. Do not encode these as ordinary activity order alone; use data constraints and organizational controls with auditable identity resolution.

4. **Overlay governance:** Each pilot or emergency overlay needs a named authority, the document granting authority, scope, conditions, approver, effective and expiry times, monitoring obligations, and closure/review requirements. Expiry must disable the route automatically. A steering decision should not be treated as a CS-9 amendment unless its authority to do so is established.

5. **Scope rules:** Define whether rejected leads are intake records outside onboarding, whether reactivation requires fresh checks or can rely on a still-valid certification, and how withdrawals and abandoned applications close. Record scope transitions explicitly so absence of an activity is not mistaken for an omitted control.

6. **Tool allocation:** Use POWL for route structure, ordering, concurrency, and choice. Use DECLARE or log-skeleton-style constraints for requirements such as “sanctions precedes activation,” “manual approval precedes standard activation,” “notification precedes closure,” and bounded follow-up obligations. Use pandas or an equivalent data-validation layer for eligibility, dates, amounts, identity, deduplication, and reconciliation. Use organizational governance for authority, policy approval, exceptions, and COI handling.

## 7. PM4Py Model and Data Proposal

The following sketch uses a repeated loop around the information-request activity as an explicit approximation. It places that loop before route selection, so it does not represent every possible interleaving of requests with checks. Route eligibility and exception authority remain data and governance decisions; the POWL structure alone must not authorize them.

```python
from pm4py.objects.powl.obj import (
    StrictPartialOrder,
    OperatorPOWL,
    Transition,
    SilentTransition,
)
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import Operator


def seq(*nodes):
    """Build an ordered POWL partial order."""
    result = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=list(nodes))
    for left, right in zip(nodes, nodes[1:]):
        result.order.add_edge(left, right)
    return result


# Approximation: zero or more information requests before route selection.
# LOOP is intended to repeat the request activity; validate the accepted
# loop arity against the installed PM4Py version.
request_more_info = Transition(label="Request_More_Info")
no_request = SilentTransition()
request_cycle = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.LOOP,
    children=[no_request, request_more_info],
)

# Standard route: sanctions and quality checks are concurrent.
std_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
std_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
std_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval")
std_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier")
standard_checks = StrictPartialOrder(nodes=[std_sanctions, std_quality])
standard_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[standard_checks, std_manual, std_activate]
)
standard_route.order.add_edge(standard_checks, std_manual)
standard_route.order.add_edge(std_manual, std_activate)

# Pilot route: the overlay must separately establish eligibility and authority.
pilot_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
master_data = Transition(label="Automated_Master_Data_Validation")
auto_approve = Transition(label="Auto_Approve")
pilot_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier_Provisional")
pilot_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
pilot_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[
        pilot_sanctions, master_data, auto_approve,
        pilot_activate, pilot_quality
    ]
)
pilot_route.order.add_edge(pilot_sanctions, auto_approve)
pilot_route.order.add_edge(master_data, auto_approve)
pilot_route.order.add_edge(auto_approve, pilot_activate)
pilot_route.order.add_edge(pilot_activate, pilot_quality)

# Emergency route: sanctions precede provisional activation;
# quality and retrospective manual approval follow activation.
em_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
em_activate = Transition(label="Activate_Supplier_Provisional")
em_quality = Transition(label="Quality_Review")
em_manual = Transition(label="Manual_Procurement_Approval_Retrospective")
emergency_route = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[em_sanctions, em_activate, em_quality, em_manual]
)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_sanctions, em_activate)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_activate, em_quality)
emergency_route.order.add_edge(em_activate, em_manual)

# Rejection route: abbreviated pre-screen without forcing quality review.
rej_sanctions = Transition(label="Sanctions_Check")
reject = Transition(label="Reject_Supplier")
rejection_route = seq(rej_sanctions, reject)

route_choice = OperatorPOWL(
    operator=Operator.XOR,
    children=[
        standard_route,
        pilot_route,
        emergency_route,
        rejection_route,
    ],
)

register = Transition(label="Register_Supplier")
notify = Transition(label="Notify_Requester")
close = Transition(label="Close_Case")

model = StrictPartialOrder(
    nodes=[register, request_cycle, route_choice, notify, close]
)
model.order.add_edge(register, request_cycle)
model.order.add_edge(request_cycle, route_choice)
model.order.add_edge(route_choice, notify)
model.order.add_edge(notify, close)
```
