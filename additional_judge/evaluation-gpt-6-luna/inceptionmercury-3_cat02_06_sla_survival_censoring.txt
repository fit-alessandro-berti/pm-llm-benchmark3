7.0 — The answer is strong in its estimand definitions, competing-risk discussion, process-feature timing, and coverage of the requested methods. It follows the nine-heading format and uses case IDs to illustrate key distinctions.

The main flaw is in the code’s Aalen–Johansen implementation. It processes open-case censoring times as if they were terminal-event times: at a censoring time, the risk-set survival factor is left unchanged, but the code still carries the reduced risk set forward. This incorrectly removes censored cases from survival without applying the censoring update consistently, biasing the reported cumulative incidence and active-case survival. Consequently, its printed primary and stratified estimates are not reliable. The code also counts cases with `dur >= 72` as the 72-hour risk set, which includes cases whose event occurs exactly at 72, and its claimed extreme bounds are described but not implemented.

There are smaller conceptual imprecisions: a transfer at 68 hours is not an ordinary “already breached” internal-resolution event, though it does mean internal Resolve was not observed by 72; and the code’s “known unresolved beyond 72” count includes terminal events after 72, not just cases still open then. These issues materially weaken the computational result, despite otherwise careful treatment of uncertainty and leakage.
