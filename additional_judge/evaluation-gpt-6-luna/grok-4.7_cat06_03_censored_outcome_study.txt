5/10 — Strong overall study design, but the cohort accounting and implementation have material errors.

The answer correctly rejects treating rejected, withdrawn, or open applications as non-defaults; defines an activation-conditioned 90-day target; distinguishes competing outcomes, censoring, delayed and missing feeds; and addresses temporal validation, policy and migration shifts, leakage, and deployment effects. Its limitations and decision-use boundary are also appropriately cautious.

The main weakness is that the cohort-flow table does not account for all 48,000 applications. It omits 3,600 activated accounts whose outcomes have not matured, despite that group being explicitly listed in the snapshot. The table’s categories therefore cannot be reconciled to the stated total.

The code also risks incorrect case-level outcomes: `default_90d` is aggregated with `max`, and terminal status is selected using the first matching event without explicitly sorting those terminal events. These choices can misclassify repeated or inconsistently populated records. The fixed-prefix example is extracted from a milestone-truncated log, but it does not clearly establish that every case has reached that milestone, and the feature function can carry forward a latest `risk_score` without proving it was available at the prediction time. Finally, the proposed fold boundaries do not cleanly define a fully matured post-migration test cohort as of the stated extraction date and feed delay.

These issues undermine the promised accounting and reproducibility, while the conceptual treatment of target semantics and selection bias remains sound.
