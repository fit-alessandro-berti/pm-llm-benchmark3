8.2/10 — The response is thorough, well structured, and consistently distinguishes authority, scope, timing, and evidence. It addresses all eight cases, offers credible alternatives, and gives useful remediation and draft policy language.

The main weakness is that several conclusions are more definite than the record supports. P-501 is classified as a justified exception even though the response itself acknowledges that B2’s timing is ambiguous and that B5 preserves Finance approval; the later Finance event may not satisfy B2 if approval was meant to precede commitment. For P-502, P-505, P-507, and P-508, the response treats the lack of evidence that required actors held budget-owner or Finance Controller roles as noncompliance, although its own method says unresolved dispositive facts may warrant INDETERMINATE. P-506 similarly relies on a missing budget-owner approval without clearly establishing whether A2’s independent approval could have occurred but simply was not recorded.

There are also smaller inconsistencies: the rule catalogue appears to add a EUR 150,000 Legal threshold from the CFO email without fully resolving whether that email can create a binding requirement; the proposed policy clause adds a Chief Compliance Officer signature and a Legal requirement above EUR 150,000 that are recommendations rather than existing controls; and the evidential summary sometimes groups unproven controls with established breaches. These issues prevent a near-flawless score, but the answer remains analytically strong and unusually complete.
