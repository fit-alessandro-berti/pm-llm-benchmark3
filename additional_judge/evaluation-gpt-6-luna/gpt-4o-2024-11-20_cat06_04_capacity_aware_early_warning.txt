The response follows the required six-section format, rejects I-03 for stage eligibility, and correctly distinguishes I-05’s unchanged alert from I-07’s new evidence. It also recognizes calibration differences, spillovers, fairness risks, and selective labels. Those are meaningful strengths.

The portfolio choice is not well supported by the stated decision rule. I-02 and I-04 are Strategic Enterprise cases, whose predictions are known to be overestimated; the answer then says to adjust their probabilities upward, worsening that bias. It adjusts SME probabilities downward despite known underprediction. The rule also sets a `p_now > 0.50` gate while allowing exceptions without defining them, and its “lower bound” robustness rule is not reflected in the case rankings or scenarios.

The impact table has major arithmetic and modeling problems. For the selected I-02, I-04, and I-07, midpoint risk reductions imply about 0.40–0.82 expected breaches avoided, not 0.9–1.5. The listed economic benefits also do not follow transparently from those cases’ supplied values and effect ranges. Spillover harm is given in euros without specifying the affected cases’ breach values or how the ranges were calculated. Calling the adverse result “marginal or negative” is therefore unsupported.

The capacity rationale also overlooks the explicit preference to retain one slot: it selects only three cases but does not explain why those three dominate I-01, I-06, I-08, I-09, or I-10 under a consistent score. I-07’s new log supports reassessment, but does not by itself establish that it should outrank alternatives. The repeated-alert policy’s “two SLA thresholds pass” re-entry condition is unclear and could delay action; the 10-point escalation threshold is arbitrary and conflicts with treating new evidence as material.

Finally, the monitoring plan is too vague for a high score. A random holdout alone does not address selection bias, spillovers, or ethical constraints in estimating intervention effects. It proposes a weekly segment slot quota without explaining how to set it or prevent wasteful interventions. Overall, the answer covers many requested topics but contains reversed calibration adjustments, weak allocation logic, and impact estimates that are not auditable.
