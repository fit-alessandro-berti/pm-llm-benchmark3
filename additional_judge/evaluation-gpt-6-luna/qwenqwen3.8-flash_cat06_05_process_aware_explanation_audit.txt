## 2. Process-model consistency checks

Build an auditable prefix and lineage check for every prediction and every feature contribution:

- **Check point-in-time availability.** Store source event IDs, event time, valid time, extraction time, aggregation window, and feature version. Assert that each source value and aggregation window ends at or before `t0`. For mutable fields, require a version valid at `t0`. Check the queue snapshot’s actual timestamp against its ±5-minute tolerance.
- **Replay the prefix against the normative process.** For each claim, replay only events through the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Confirm that any activity cited as having occurred is in that prefix. The Case X/Y/Z prefixes contain no documents, damage assessment, decision, appeal, closure, or fraud investigation.
- **Check order and feasibility.** Encode the stated ordering rules: registration precedes coverage; evidence requests precede receipts and validations; assessment precedes the first settlement decision; appeal follows Partial or Deny; closure follows a final decision or withdrawal. Flag an edge that violates these rules, but allow the evidence loop and permitted parallel fraud work.
- **Handle ties as partial order.** If timestamps tie and no reliable sequence index exists, do not infer a directed edge from ingestion order. Check whether any ordering consistent with the available evidence satisfies the process. If order cannot be resolved, label the relation ambiguous.
- **Detect complete-trace features.** Use lineage metadata and source-event tracing to flag full-case aggregates and fields populated at closure, including duration, appeal presence, total requests, full-trace variant, final team, final decision, and last role. Apply the same check to hidden features such as employee experience.
- **Classify activity references.** For each activity named in an explanation, label it as occurred in the prefix, enabled next, future-possible, impossible under the model, or ambiguous. “Future-possible” means a valid continuation could contain the activity; it does not make that activity available evidence at `t0`.
- **Validate repeated settlement decisions.** Permit a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` only when the first decision was Partial or Deny and a subsequent Appeal led to reconsideration. Flag a second decision without that sequence, and flag ties where the ordering cannot be established.

Prefix replay and alignments can test whether a trace fits a process model; footprints and DFGs can reveal observed ordering patterns; declarative constraints can encode rules such as “appeal only after Partial or Deny.” Each method has limits: alignments depend on the model, DFGs can hide concurrency and loops, and a frequent DFG edge is not automatically a normative rule. These methods also do not establish that a feature was available at `t0`; lineage checks remain necessary.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

| Class | Examples | What may be claimed | What may not be claimed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensible predictive signal | `elapsed_since_registration_at_t0`; frozen `initial_damage_complexity`; `prior_claim_count_before_t0` after point-in-time repair; `initial_reserve` only if verified at `t0` | These values were available at `t0` and may help rank breach risk if validation confirms predictive performance. | Their importance does not prove they cause a breach or that changing them will change the outcome. |
| Process-state or congestion signal | `queue_length_at_t0`; elapsed SLA time; `team_at_t0` if reconstructed; a fraud or document status only if it had occurred by `t0` | The model may describe current workload, assignment, or remaining slack. Queue routing or staffing may be operational levers worth evaluating. | SHAP attribution alone does not show that changing staffing or routing would reduce breaches; intervention evidence is needed. |
| Proxy requiring fairness and robustness review | `claimant_region`; `claimant_channel` if confirmed at registration; prior-claim history; possibly complexity measures | A feature can be predictive while serving as a proxy for access, service availability, or other characteristics. It may be retained subject to review. | Do not present the proxy as a causal claimant characteristic or rely on it without testing group error rates, disparate impact, and recourse. |
| Drift/calendar proxy | `calendar_week`, holiday periods, policy changes | Week-level association can describe the conditions represented in the training data and signal drift. | It does not establish that a week causes delay or that the association will persist after policy or staffing changes. |
| Unusable until lineage is repaired | `final_handler_team`, `prior_claim_count_before_t0`, `same_day_document_count_snapshot`, `risk_score_current`, `initial_reserve`, employee experience, `last_resource_role` | Nothing about the current stored value should be presented as a valid `t0` driver until its historical value is reconstructed and verified. | Do not treat extraction-time or complete-case values as if they were available at prediction time. |
| Unusable at this prediction point; replace or drop | `total_case_duration_hours`, `appeal_seen_anywhere`, `num_document_requests_total`, `full_trace_variant_id`, `final_decision`, and post-`t0` sequence patterns | These may describe completed cases in retrospective analysis or a later prediction point. | They cannot support a prediction explanation at `t0`; there is no lineage repair that makes a future event available earlier. |

For predictive validity, a feature must be available at `t0` and validated on appropriate data. Causal interpretation requires evidence beyond predictive attribution. Operational actionability also depends on whether an intervention can change the relevant process state. Fairness review is a separate requirement for features that may encode unequal access or treatment.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **P0 — Quarantine current explanations.** Label them as retrospective until point-in-time lineage is established. Remove future and complete-trace features from `t0` explanations.
2. **P0 — Build point-in-time feature lineage.** Store event time, valid time, extraction time, source event IDs, aggregation window, and feature version. Require as-of joins at `t0`, and emit each contribution’s feature timestamp and admissibility status.
3. **P0 — Recompute from prefixes.** Generate features only from events at or before the first `Coverage Check Completed`. Replace complete-trace features with verified `team_at_t0`, document counts before `t0`, `risk_score_at_t0`, role at `t0`, and event-time document snapshots where available.
4. **P1 — Add process-conformance gates.** Replay prefixes against the normative model, handle tied timestamps as partial orders unless a reliable sequence index resolves them, and block explanations that cite future or impossible activities. Validate repeated settlement decisions only after an eligible appeal.
5. **P1 — Retrain and recalibrate.** Evaluate discrimination, calibration, and breach-rate estimates after removing leakage. Compare old and rebuilt predictions to estimate how much apparent performance came from future information; do not restore it with outcome proxies.
6. **P1 — Check explanation stability.** Test attribution stability across seeds, time windows, and correlated feature groups. Report grouped importance where individual attributions shift among correlated proxies.
7. **P2 — Review proxies and fairness.** Assess region, channel, prior-claim history, and related proxies for disparate errors and impact. Decompose operational mechanisms, provide human review and recourse for consequential use, and evaluate any operational interventions separately.
8. **P2 — Monitor drift and data quality.** Alert on missing sequence indices, tied timestamps, new activities or loops, schema changes, snapshot timing violations, policy and calendar shifts, and complete-trace features entering production.
