It uses many of the supplied metrics and recognizes key conflicts such as urgent-first starvation, M2 repeat risk, and the danger of pulling technical validators into accession correction.

Some figures also conflict with the supplied DFG or queue data. The answer treats observed peak performance as if it were a direct forecast for specific scenarios.

Claims such as “4,100 cases” or a particular share of cases requiring medical validation need careful qualification.

For example, sending “100% of Urgent” work to M2 during M1 outage assumes spare M2 capacity despite the stated repeat risk; autovalidation and diversion of “routine Complex-panel” cases need stronger clinical and operational conditions. Overall, this is a thoughtful and well-structured response, but unsupported quantitative certainty, a few internal inconsistencies, and insufficiently grounded policy assumptions prevent a high grade.
