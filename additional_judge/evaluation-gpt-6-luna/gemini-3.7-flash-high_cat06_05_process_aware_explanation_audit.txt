## 2. Process-model consistency checks

Build validation around the prediction snapshot and the normative process model:

- **Prefix-time availability:** For each prediction, materialize the trace and feature values using only records available at or before `t0`. Require event-time or valid-time lineage for mutable fields and joins. Flag equality-boundary cases for a defined sequence rule.
- **Activity-order feasibility:** Check prefixes against the process model using alignments or state-based Petri-net replay. Enforce registration before coverage; evidence cycles in the specified order; damage assessment before settlement; appeals only after Partial or Deny; and closure after a final decision or withdrawal.
- **Same-timestamp ambiguity:** Where timestamps tie, use a reliable monotonic sequence index. If absent, do not infer the ordering; mark the affected order-dependent feature invalid and quarantine or resolve the trace from authoritative records.
- **Complete-trace detection:** Tag feature lineage as prefix-derived, event-time snapshot, or complete-trace/closure-derived. Reject complete-trace fields at `t0`, including aggregates, final values, last-role fields, and variants built from the full trace.
- **Enabled, impossible, or future-possible explanations:** At `t0`, compare each explained activity with the state reached by the prefix. An enabled activity is possible next; a future-possible activity requires intervening steps; an impossible activity violates the model. None of these categories makes an unobserved activity an admissible observed feature. Report observed prefix events separately from future possibilities.
- **Repeated decision semantics:** Preserve decision instances and decision types. Validate a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` only when a preceding Partial or Deny enables an appeal and the appeal leads to reconsideration. Do not collapse the two instances into one or treat every repetition as an error.

Use DFG or footprint checks as useful diagnostics, but let the normative model and event semantics determine whether a pattern is feasible. A frequent transition does not prove temporal availability, and a rare transition is not automatically impossible.
