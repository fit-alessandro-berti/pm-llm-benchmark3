## 1. Feasibility model

```text
Register < Triage
Triage < Identity Check
Triage < Document Check                         [no originals]
Triage < Originals Receipt < Document Check    [originals required]
Identity Check < Assessment
Document Check < Assessment
Assessment(final) < Compliance Review(final) < Decision < Notify
Assessment(k) < Request More Info
Request More Info < external response < Document Check(k+1) < Assessment(k+1)
Assessment(latest) < Compliance Review(new or reaffirmed) < Decision
Escalation Advice may overlap with verification and assessment work.
Preliminary Compliance Screen may occur early; it does not replace the final review.
```

```text
DECLARATIVE
- Register is first, followed by Triage.
- Identity Check and Document Check must both complete before Assessment.
- Decision precedes Notify.
- A resource cannot perform overlapping service intervals.
- Queue estimates are forecasts, not guaranteed start times.

CONDITIONAL
- If originals are required, Originals Receipt precedes Document Check; Identity Check may proceed earlier.
- If a case is high-risk or high-value, final Compliance Review follows the latest relevant Assessment and precedes Decision.
- After Request More Info, a new Document Check and Assessment are required before Decision.
- After an information request in a high-risk/high-value case, Compliance Review must be new or explicitly reaffirmed.
- The person performing final Assessment must differ from the person performing final Compliance Review.
- K-337's intervention prohibits a second Request More Info; an unresolved material discrepancy must lead to rejection rather than silent acceptance.
```

K-104 changes timing and allocates scarce compliance capacity through early preparation and a provisional slot. K-219 changes routing from D1 to D2. K-337 changes the allowed decision policy by prohibiting a second information request. K-411 changes task creation and timing to permit concurrent checks, without changing decision rights.

## 3. Counterfactual derivations

### K-104

Factual mechanism: Triage noticed the ambiguity but did not escalate it. The complex D1 check finished Tuesday morning; A1 found the same issue Tuesday evening, after which C1 did not review the case until Wednesday and had to read most evidence from scratch.

Minimal intervention: Send the packet to S1 at Triage and have C1 do preliminary screening while verification proceeds. Hold a provisional final-review slot after the expected Assessment. The screen and reservation do not replace final review.

Temporal propagation: The verification completion times need not change. The benefit comes from S1 preparing advice and C1 becoming familiar with the issue before A1 finishes. If the held slot is honored, final review can start Tuesday evening instead of waiting for Wednesday’s C1 availability; M1 may then decide at its next Wednesday window. If Assessment runs late, the slot can be wasted or displace other work.

Process-feasibility check: Identity and Document Check both finish before Assessment. The final C1 review follows A1’s final Assessment, and A1 and C1 are different people. Preliminary screening may overlap the checks but is not the final review.

Best/base/worst case: Best is roughly 39–43 h end-to-end if C1 reviews promptly and M1 can decide Tuesday night; base is about 49 h, around 9.5 h faster than factual; worst is little or no gain if the slot slips. Approval with conditions is plausible in each, but early review could change the conditions or support rejection.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: C1 reservation and utilization logs showing frequent idle holds or urgent cases delayed; evidence that preliminary packets seldom reduce rereading; or assessments that routinely finish after the reserved window would weaken the case for this intervention.

### K-219

Factual mechanism: D1 was selected for handwriting accuracy, but the standard document check did not require rework. The case then waited for A2 and for M1’s Wednesday decision window.

Minimal intervention: Route the document check to D2 when originals arrive, while treating “as soon as” as eligible for service rather than a guaranteed immediate start. The base path assumes D2 begins around Mon 22:30 after its queue.

Temporal propagation: A five-hour D2 check finishes Tuesday around 02:30, earlier than factual D1’s 13:30 completion. Assessment can therefore enter the queue earlier. If A2 and M1 both have Tuesday capacity, notification advances substantially; if M1 remains unavailable until Wednesday, earlier readiness does not change the endpoint. If Assessment issues a clarification request, the external response and repeat document and assessment work can consume the saved time.

Process-feasibility check: Originals Receipt precedes D2’s check; Identity Check may proceed earlier; both checks complete before Assessment. Any formal information request is followed by a new Document Check and Assessment before Decision. Compliance is not required for this low-risk, low-value case.

Best/base/worst case: Best is a clear handwriting read and a Tuesday M1 slot, for roughly 26–32 h elapsed. Base is around 32 h if M1 acts Tuesday, or 72 h if the original manager window still governs. A clarification branch could take about 50–120 h, depending on supplier response and the next assessment and manager slots.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: D2’s actual service-time distribution for handwritten files, its clarification and error rates, and the displacement of other D2 work would reverse the assessment if D2 routinely adds more delay or rework than the D1 wait it avoids.

### K-337

Factual mechanism: Assessment 2 was followed by a weekend supplier wait, Document Check 3, and Assessment 3. Only then did C1 review the evidence and M1 approve. The additional loop resolved the discrepancy but delayed notification.

Minimal intervention: After Assessment 2, do not issue Request More Info 2. Send the available evidence to C1 for final review. M1 then approves with explicit compensating conditions only if C1 concurs; otherwise M1 rejects.

Temporal propagation: The path removes the second supplier response, the third document check, and the third assessment. In the illustrated path this makes the case ready for C1 Friday rather than Tuesday. The gain depends on C1 and M1 capacity, not just the avoided weekend. A slow C1 queue or delayed manager decision can consume much of the saved time.

Process-feasibility check: Request More Info 1 is followed by Document Check 2 and Assessment 2. C1’s final review follows Assessment 2 and precedes Decision; A1 and C1 satisfy four-eyes. No second information request is issued. The remaining discrepancy must be explicitly addressed in the decision.

Best/base/worst case: Best is a Friday C1 review and M1 decision, saving about 111 h against the factual cycle. Base is a substantial gain if both are available Friday. Worst is near zero gain if the same review or decision cadence binds; a material discrepancy leads to rejection rather than unsupported approval. Quality and appeal risk are higher than in the factual resolved-evidence path.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Follow-up showing frequent losses, reversals, failed conditions, or compliance findings for similar unresolved discrepancies would argue against conditional approval. If C1 nearly always rejects such cases, the intervention may still save process time but provide little value to applicants.

### K-411

Factual mechanism: The case lock allowed only an identity task to be created at Triage. Document work therefore started after Identity Check, and both completions entered a congested assessment queue.

Minimal intervention: Create both tasks at Triage and enable the controlled workaround for I2 and D2. Record task creation, resource acquisition, service start, and service end as they occur.

Temporal propagation: Document work can overlap Identity Check, bringing assessment readiness forward by the amount of serial waiting avoided. The checks do not create assessment capacity: if A2 is still occupied, the case waits there and the end-to-end gain can be zero. At scale, earlier arrivals can lengthen the assessment queue for both pilot and non-pilot cases.

Process-feasibility check: This case needs no originals, so Identity Check and Document Check may run concurrently. Both finish before Assessment; I2 and D2 use separate resources. Decision still precedes Notify, and no compliance review is required.

Best/base/worst case: Best is an earlier Tuesday assessment slot, saving up to about 19 h; base is about 29.5 h end-to-end if the existing Wednesday M1 decision remains the constraint. A longer assessment wait could erase the gain or make the case slightly slower. With validated timestamps and complete checks, approval quality should be similar.

Evidence that would reverse the conclusion: Resource-level event logs showing that the original lock was not the binding delay, or that earlier arrivals materially increase assessment waits for target and non-target cases, would weaken the intervention. Audit evidence of false overlap or missing check steps would make it infeasible.

## 4. Capacity and interference analysis

| intervention | local resource demand change | queue likely to shrink | queue likely to grow | affected non-target cases | sign of system-wide effect | key nonlinearity |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K-104 | Adds S1 advice and C1 preliminary work; reserves C1 time; may shorten final rereading. | Target’s wait for final C1 review if the slot is honored. | C1 queue if reservations hold capacity or preliminary screens add work. | Urgent high-risk cases waiting for C1; cases using S1. | Ambiguous; local improvement can displace higher-priority cases. | A provisional slot has an opportunity cost even if the target’s Assessment is late. |
| K-219 | Shifts standard work from D1 to D2; D2 may need clarification and repeat checks. | D1 queue. | D2 queue and, after rework, assessment queue. | Other D2 cases, especially complex or handwritten files; cases receiving freed D1 capacity. | Ambiguous; depends on D2 backlog and rework. | Small increases in handwriting clarifications can add external wait and another assessment, not just service hours. |
| K-337 | Removes a supplier response, document check, and assessment; retains final C1 and M1 work. | D1, A1, and supplier-response demand. | Monitoring, disputes, appeals, or risk-review work if a condition fails or a decision is challenged. | Similar applicants may gain freed capacity; risk and appeal teams may inherit work. | Potentially positive for throughput, uncertain for quality-adjusted outcomes. | Residual discrepancy creates asymmetric loss risk; one bad conditional approval can outweigh many saved cycles. |
| K-411 | Releases identity and document tasks together; total service demand is similar but earlier. | Target’s serial wait before Document Check and readiness for Assessment. | Early Identity/Document demand and potentially the Assessment queue. | Non-pilot cases competing for I2, D2, or A2. | Local benefit may be offset by queue displacement or downstream bottlenecks. | Earlier readiness can cluster arrivals at a fixed-capacity assessment pool. |

Treating 100 cases as independent would be misleading: each policy changes release times or priorities, and queues turn local gains into displacements. In K-104, early packets and provisional C1 reservations consume the only certified reviewer’s capacity. Even when preliminary work saves 1–3 hours at final review, held slots occupy capacity urgent cases cannot use. If assessments slip, reservations can sit idle and later create a C1 or manager decision burst. Selective, cancellable reservations are more defensible than universal holds.

For K-219, moving standard files from D1 to D2 frees D1 but loads D2, whose standard service is typically slower. As D2’s queue and handwriting clarification rate grow, repeat document checks, supplier responses, and assessments consume the initially freed capacity. Weekend responses can make rework especially costly. D1 should absorb useful work released by the routing change rather than be treated as idle capacity.

For K-337, suppressing the second request removes an external wait that crosses a weekend, a document check, and an assessment, freeing D1 and A1 capacity. This could improve throughput, but conditional approvals can transfer work to monitoring, disputes, appeals, and loss investigation; rejections can shift it to appeal handling. C1 review and M1 decisions remain required bottlenecks.

For K-411, simultaneous task creation raises early identity and document arrivals without adding service capacity. Earlier readiness may simply move waiting into the assessment queue, where a cluster of 100 cases can delay non-pilot work as well. Real-time task, resource-acquisition, service-start, and completion events are essential: backdated timestamps can create apparent concurrency without actual overlap, and can undermine both auditability and the process path. Queue forecasts should be checked against arrival and departure counts and waiting reasons, not treated as guaranteed slots.

## 7. Final synthesis

Among the four, K-411 appears most robust because it changes task creation and scheduling while leaving decision rights and compliance requirements intact. It is feasible for digital, no-original cases, uses distinct resources for genuine concurrency, and is highly reversible. Its benefit is conditional, however: earlier readiness does not create assessment capacity, so a pilot should measure queue delay rather than infer speed from check overlap.

K-337 is the most dangerous despite its potentially largest saving. Prohibiting the second information request is a change in decision policy. A conditional approval can leave the discrepancy unresolved; a rejection can be safe but invite appeal. Compliance defensibility depends on C1’s explicit concurrence, four-eyes separation, and effective compensating controls. Ending a long loop is valuable only if residual risk is consciously accepted or refused.

The K-104 reservation counterfactual is least transportable to a system-wide policy. C1 is a single scarce reviewer, and a reservation that helps one case is an opportunity cost for another. K-219 is less attractive because D2’s handwriting clarification risk may convert document delay into assessment rework.

The most valuable additional data are event-level task creation, resource acquisition, service start, service end, and waiting-reason timestamps; queue arrivals and departures by resource and priority; reservation and no-show histories; assessment-to-compliance handoffs; and rework triggers with external-response times. For K-337, decision rationales, imposed conditions, subsequent disputes, reversals, losses, and compliance findings are essential. A 100-case pilot should distinguish true concurrency from backdated completion and measure non-target queue displacement before scaling.

## 8. Assumption audit

1. structural — The stated prerequisites, final review requirements, four-eyes rule, and Decision-before-Notify order remain binding.
2. temporal — Queue snapshots are uncertain forecasts; the illustrative service windows are plausible rather than guaranteed.
3. resource — C1 is the only fully certified reviewer in the window, and M1’s decision availability can constrain endpoints.
4. temporal — External supplier responses may take 8–36 hours and can cross weekends.
5. behavioral — D2’s handwriting clarification risk is real, and suppliers do not respond faster merely because routing changes.
6. quality — A K-337 discrepancy may be controlled in some cases, but a material unresolved discrepancy requires rejection.
7. interference — Single-case paths hold other system behavior approximately fixed; 100-case rollout effects include queue displacement.
8. resource — Earlier task release changes arrival timing but does not add service capacity; concurrent work uses separate resources.
