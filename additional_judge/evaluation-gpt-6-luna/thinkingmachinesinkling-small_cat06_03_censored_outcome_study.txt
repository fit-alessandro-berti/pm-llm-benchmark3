The response addresses the central target problem: it chooses default within 90 days after activation among activated accounts, defines a prediction time before activation, and warns that this estimates risk in the selected activated population rather than default risk for every application. It also gives a competing-outcome alternative and rejects coding rejected, withdrawn, or open cases as non-default.

Its cohort and bias discussion is thoughtful, including delayed labels, activation selection, transfers, missing feeds, policy change, migration, and limitations on counterfactual claims. It covers the requested leakage fields and proposes temporal validation with calibration and subgroup checks.

The main weaknesses are implementation and accounting. The cohort-flow table does not reconcile cleanly: the listed categories sum to 48,000, but the 3,600 immature activated accounts are described separately from the 21,600 labeled accounts without a clear, mutually exclusive breakdown of activated accounts by maturity and outcome. The code’s maturity rule also adds a fixed 35-day delay to `label_available_at`, although that field appears to represent availability time itself; it then treats label presence as sufficient maturity without requiring an observed, valid label. The prefix example filters to eventual activated cases before extracting prefixes and does not actually enforce a strict pre-activation prediction cutoff. Those choices weaken the claimed as-of safeguards and could bias the analysis.
