## 1. Conformance semantics

- **Forbidden behavior:** An observed relation explicitly prohibited by the norm within its scope. This is a violation, subject to checking that the observation is correctly attributed.
- **Missing mandatory behavior:** A required relation is absent where the norm says it must apply. Scope and the model’s abstraction matter: a missing directly-follows edge does not by itself prove a missing eventual behavior.
- **Unexpected but unregulated behavior:** An observed relation whose status is unspecified. Its absence from the norm is not evidence of noncompliance.
- **Typical-pattern absence:** A typical relation is missing or less frequent than expected. This is a signal for investigation, not automatically a violation; no frequency thresholds are specified.
- **Likely data-quality error:** An observation with evidence of mislinking or incomplete capture. It should be checked before treating it as business behavior.
- **Abstraction mismatch:** The model records a relation at a different level than the norm describes, such as directly-follows standing in for eventual succession.
- **Insufficient evidence:** The summary lacks the event-level context needed to distinguish a violation, legitimate exception, or recording gap.

The norm is closed-world only for explicitly forbidden relations and mandatory columns within their stated scope. Elsewhere it is open-world: unlisted or unspecified relations are not violations merely because they are absent from the specification.

## 3. Detailed reasoning by model family

### OC-DFG

An OC-DFG summary can show event-to-event relations within an object’s lifecycle and their counts. It can flag the observed Cancel -> Dispatch pairs against N3, and compare the Create -> Validate and Pack -> Dispatch counts to N1 and N4. It also shows typical patterns such as Validate -> Close and Issue Invoice -> Record Payment, plus the reported invoice write-offs and missing successors.

The graph cannot establish from a directly-follows edge alone that an “eventually expected” relation did or did not occur. N1 says an Order should eventually enter validation, while the summary approximates this with Create -> Validate directly-follows. An intermediate review can break that edge even if validation later occurs. The two exceptions therefore need full traces before they can be classified as missing mandatory behavior.

N4 is scoped to packages intended for external shipment. Its 198 of 200 count is strong evidence of a high rate in that scope, but the two gaps still need review. N2 and N5 are typical, so their absences warrant context rather than a violation label. N6 is unspecified; the observed Reopen -> Close relation is not noncompliant under this norm.

### OTG

The OTG shows object-type relations and their weights. It can compare the Order-Item and Package-Delivery interactions with mandatory T1 and T2, identify the observed Order-Payment cobirth against forbidden T6, and report the Invoice-Payment interaction under typical T3.

An OTG does not establish which event created a relation, whether a particular event had its required object, or whether an interaction was legitimate in context. The weight of 200 for Package-Delivery establishes that the edge is present at that frequency, but does not prove every Dispatch Package event has a Delivery relation; the ET–OT counts show two dispatch events lack one. Likewise, the Invoice-Payment edge does not prove that every invoice should have a payment, because T3 is typical and unapplied cash can exist.

Customer and RiskCase interactions are unspecified under T4 and T5. Their presence is not noncompliance. The Package-Delivery codeath edges are also outside the norm. The OTG’s forbidden cobirth observation is concerning, but event-level provenance is needed to establish whether it represents a real business event or a modeling/linking error.

### ET–OT

ET–OT shows which object types participate in each event type. It can check the mandatory event-object columns E1–E8 and the forbidden Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice relation E10. The counts reveal two Add Item events without an Order relation, two Dispatch Package events without a Delivery relation, and one Record Payment event without an Invoice relation.

This model does not establish temporal order, eventual succession, or business intent. For E8, the exception applies only to events explicitly typed as unapplied cash; none are so typed here. The missing Invoice relation is therefore a mismatch with the mandatory column as recorded, while the possibility of unapplied cash remains an evidence question. The carrier reference on two Dispatch Package events may explain how the shipment was recorded, but it does not itself satisfy the required Delivery object relation.

The three Manual Compliance Review events’ RiskCase and Customer links are unspecified by E9, T5, and T4. They should not be labeled violations. The Sensor Heartbeat–Invoice edge is explicitly forbidden, but the known reused-identifier join makes data quality a credible explanation to investigate before interpreting it as a genuine business link.
