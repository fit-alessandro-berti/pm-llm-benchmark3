## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January–March provide a stable reference: metrics, data quality, and variant mix are broadly steady.

April is primarily a pipeline incident. The diagnosis-label rename disrupted prediction prefixes and features; unknown activities and missing queue features also spike. The AUROC and calibration decline should not be read as evidence of concept drift until records are rebuilt and rescored.

May shows substantial recovery after the mapping patch, but cached features were not fully backfilled. It is improved, not automatically clean for training.

June brings a genuine mix change from remote routing: V2 increases to 52%. Overall breach rate falls, while remote-fix time remains stable. This is consistent with population or covariate change, but does not by itself indicate deteriorating conditional predictions.

July is an early process warning: parts wait rises to 65 hours and V3 breach rate to 0.46. Overall AUROC softens, though label maturity is still 98%. Supplier-related process deterioration is plausible; conditional concept drift remains to be tested.

August has strong evidence of parts-process deterioration: parts wait reaches 130 hours and matured V3 breach rate reaches 0.64. The increased V3 share contributes to the overall breach-rate rise, but cannot alone explain V3’s within-variant increase. This raises a concept-drift hypothesis as well as process drift.

September combines further V3 process pressure, Partner Z population shift, and only 82% label maturity. Partner Z’s observed breach rate is not directly comparable without accounting for slower label maturation. The aggregate metrics blend these effects.

October is dominated by immature, selectively delayed labels: only 55% maturity, with longer paths disproportionately affected. The observed 0.16 breach rate is not a stable estimate of eventual risk. Its poor metrics should not be treated as evidence of a new model failure until labels mature.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

- **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting:** Follow each monthly cohort’s label resolution over time and recompute metrics at a common age. Under censoring, October’s breach rate and metrics move materially as labels arrive; under genuine performance decline, poor metrics persist in mature fixed-vintage comparisons.
- **April normalization and duplicate-cleaning recomputation:** Rebuild April prefixes and cached features after normalizing both diagnosis labels and applying consistent duplicate handling. If metrics recover, pipeline failure is supported; if not, additional causes remain.
- **Calibration and discrimination by variant, source, and month:** Compare AUROC, Brier, and calibration within mature strata. Under mix shift, within-stratum metrics remain relatively stable; under conditional drift, they worsen within a stratum such as V3.
- **Variant and DFG/performance-DFG comparison:** Compare path frequencies and edge durations by month and variant. Supplier process drift predicts a growing `Order Part -> Part Received` duration concentrated in V3; a broader process change predicts changes on other edges too.
- **Conditional outcome relationship test:** Within the same path, compare breach rates by month, conditioning on available case features and part-wait duration. Stable conditional relationships support covariate or process mix explanations; changes after conditioning support concept drift.
- **Partner Z transport check:** Reweight September cases to the pre-September partner, variant, and feature distribution, then compare performance and calibration. Recovery after reweighting supports population shift; persistent within-stratum decline suggests other causes.
- **Feature missingness and lineage audit:** Trace diagnosis-prefix and queue-feature values from source through cache and prediction. April-specific defects support pipeline failure; similar defects in later months indicate a more persistent issue.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment:** Treat April metrics as invalid for model-quality conclusions. Do not use October’s observed breach rate for retraining or recalibration. Preserve predictions while displaying maturity and data-quality status alongside performance metrics.

**Short-term recalibration:** Consider recalibration only on mature, pipeline-verified data and only if discrimination remains acceptable but calibration is persistently off. Validate by variant; do not assume one global calibration layer will handle the changing mix.

**Feature repair:** Normalize diagnosis labels at ingestion, consistently handle duplicates, recompute April’s cached features, and add prediction-time measures of part delay, rural distance, device age, and partner where available and appropriate.

**Full retraining:** First evaluate a clean, mature training window. January–March are the safest baseline. May may be usable after lineage verification and feature recomputation. April is unsafe until rebuilt; September and October are unsafe until labels mature. July–August contain valuable process change but need mature labels and careful treatment of the changing case mix before inclusion.

A variant-aware model or calibration layer is worth evaluating because path-specific breach rates differ substantially and V3 risk changed. A Partner Z-specific model is premature: partner, variant, and rurality are confounded, and the new segment has limited history.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Monitor predictive metrics on mature cohorts; calibration and discrimination by month, variant, and source; label maturity and delay by cohort and path; data quality including schema validity, unknown activity, duplicates, missingness, and feature lineage; variant mix; performance-DFG edge times by variant; population composition including partner and geography; and intervention rates and outcomes.

Alert dependencies should gate interpretation: a label-maturity alarm marks outcome metrics provisional and blocks automatic retraining triggers, while data-quality alarms trigger pipeline investigation. Process and population alarms trigger diagnosis and stratified evaluation, not automatic retraining. Reassess model actions only when labels are sufficiently mature and the scoring pipeline passes validation.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def monthly_evidence(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    df = pd.read_csv(path, parse_dates=["timestamp"])
    df["activity"] = df["activity"].replace({
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    })
    # Deduplicate identical case/activity/time events; audit counts before and after.
    before = len(df)
    df = df.drop_duplicates(["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"])
    removed = before - len(df)

    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        df, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    df["month"] = df["timestamp"].dt.to_period("M")
    starts = df.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min().rename("case_start")
    df = df.join(starts, on="case_id")

    rows = []
    for month, events in df.groupby("month"):
        # Event-calendar cohort: events occurring in this month.
        # Case-start cohort: cases whose first event occurred in this month.
        started = events[events["case_start"].dt.to_period("M") == month]
        event_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            events, str(month.start_time), str(month.end_time), timestamp_key="timestamp"
        )
        dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(event_log)
        perf_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(event_log)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(event_log)
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(started)

        rows.append({
            "month": str(month),
            "event_cases": event_log["case_id"].nunique(),
            "started_cases": started["case_id"].nunique(),
            "duplicates_removed": removed,
            "dfg_edges": len(dfg),
            "performance_edges": len(perf_dfg),
            "variants": len(variants),
            "temporal_rows": len(temporal),
        })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

- Retraining on immature September–October labels can teach the model that long-path cases are low risk because their breaches have not yet been recorded.
- Calibrating on April’s broken prefixes can encode a pipeline artifact into subsequent predictions.
- Treating the V3 deterioration as only a mix shift can miss a real within-path increase in risk associated with supplier delays.
- Treating Partner Z’s raw breach rate as a partner effect can confuse slower label maturation, V3 mix, rurality, and device age.
- Treating October’s low observed breach rate as improvement can conceal delayed outcomes.
