## 1. Current-process diagnosis

The flow is concentrated in the fast route: 3,600 of 5,000 intakes go to Auto eligibility screen, 2,550 proceed from there to Fast approval, and 2,300 go from Fast approval to Dispatch replacement. That route is attractive on speed: the median is 0.5 hours from intake to auto screen, 0.4 hours from auto screen to fast approval, and 1.8 hours from fast approval to dispatch.

The speed and initial cost advantage do not tell the whole story. Claims using Fast approval have a 14.1% complaint/reopen rate, compared with 3.3% for the Repair quote → Customer approval → Repair device path. Fast approval cases are 31% cheaper before rework, but only 9% cheaper after complaints, reshipments, and service credits. The long tail after dispatch is also material: Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen has a mean of 96 hours and a p90 of 180 hours. For high-value devices, the fast route has an 18–22% complaint rate, compared with 5–6% on reviewed or repair paths.

Compliance evidence points to the same weak points. R1 requires Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval for claims above EUR 400; R2 requires evidence completeness before replacement dispatch, with a narrow Premium low-risk accessory exception. Alignment diagnostics report Fast approval before Evidence completeness and Dispatch replacement without risk review for high-value devices. Replay fitness is 0.91, but only 68% of traces fit the normative model, and the discovered model has low precision. This indicates meaningful policy deviations and a noisy set of observed variants, rather than a single clean process.

Evidence handling is a separate source of delay. Request missing evidence appears in 1,430 directly-follows edges from eligibility activities, and 890 cases then return to Manual eligibility review. In 26% of cases that enter evidence collection, the request occurs at least twice. The Request missing evidence → Manual eligibility review edge has a mean of 34 hours and a p90 of 88 hours. The unclear defect/missing evidence segment is 22% of cases, has a median cycle time of 140 hours, and has the highest abandonment rate.

The reviewed paths are not uniformly better. They reduce complaints for high-value devices, but Manual eligibility review → Repair quote has a p90 of 60 hours and is often too slow for Premium cases. Premium low-risk accessories have an 8-hour fast-path median versus 46 hours on the manual path, with high service-credit exposure above 24 hours. By contrast, Premium high-value devices have a 20-hour fast-path median but a 22% complaint rate, versus 96 hours and 5% on the reviewed/repair path. Routing should therefore depend on segment and risk.

Capacity constrains any broad shift to review. Manual review queues grow sharply above 82% utilization; updating the auto-screen rules may increase manual inflow by 15–35%. Fraud/risk review has two specialists and peak bottlenecks, while repair quotes wait for part-price confirmation. Quality checks are underused except after repair campaigns, and the escalation board’s daily 45-minute slot does not scale easily.

The central trade-off is that the most frequent route is fast and cheap before rework, but it carries more complaints and compliance exceptions. Slower reviewed and repair routes produce better outcomes for high-value devices, yet applying them indiscriminately would delay low-risk claims and threaten Premium service expectations. The redesigns below preserve fast handling where risk is low and intervene where the evidence shows costly failures.

## 2. Redesign A: Segment- and risk-aware triage with compliance checkpoints

**Redesigned process flow**

At intake, update the auto-screen rules to use claim value, product type, defect type, customer segment, evidence status, and reopen history.

- Premium low-risk accessory claims with complete evidence can use the fast route under the R2 exception.
- Standard low-risk accessory claims can remain on the fast route when evidence is complete and no risk flags are present.
- Claims above EUR 400, high-value devices, unclear defects, and reopened claims must receive Manual eligibility review or Fraud/risk review before Fast approval, as required by R1 and R3.
- Evidence completeness is checked before replacement dispatch, subject to the stated Premium low-risk accessory exception.

**Targets**

This redesign changes the front-end routing through Auto eligibility screen, Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, and Fast approval. It targets high-value claims, claims above EUR 400, unclear defects, and reopened claims while preserving the fast route for eligible low-risk accessories.

**Mechanism of improvement**

Segment-aware rules address the mismatch between the current auto screen and the different outcomes by claim type. The high-value segments have 18–22% complaint rates on the fast path versus 5–6% on reviewed or repair paths, and a wrong high-value replacement has an expected cost of EUR 410. Requiring the appropriate review and evidence checks should reduce R1/R2 deviations and avoid some downstream complaints, while leaving the low-risk accessory route fast.

**Deliberate sacrifice and unresolved issues**

High-value and ambiguous claims will take longer and incur more review labor. The 15–35% possible increase in manual inflow could push reviewers past the 82% utilization threshold and worsen waits for other cases; the two-person fraud/risk team is also a likely constraint. Broad or inaccurate routing rules could send Premium low-risk accessories to manual review and increase service-credit exposure. This redesign does not remove repeated evidence requests or prevent wrong part selection at fulfillment.

## 3. Redesign B: Evidence-first completion with a human-assisted portal

**Redesigned process flow**

At intake or the first evidence request, identify the documents, photos, serial numbers, or defect information required for that specific product and claim. Use a portal to collect the evidence in one structured step, validate that required fields and files are present, and show the customer the claim’s status and next action. Only move the claim to Fast approval, review, or dispatch once the evidence check is complete.

Offer phone or agent-assisted collection for customers who need help, including customers with low digital literacy and Premium customers whose claims are at risk of missing service expectations. Preserve the R2 exception for Premium low-risk accessories.

**Targets**

This redesign targets the Request missing evidence loop, the unclear defect/missing evidence segment, abandonment, and evidence-related R2 deviations. It aims to reduce repeated requests and the long wait from evidence submission back to Manual eligibility review.

**Mechanism of improvement**

The proposed portal could reduce repeated evidence requests by 30–45%. That directly addresses the 26% repeat-request rate among cases entering evidence collection and the 34-hour mean from Request missing evidence to Manual eligibility review. Structured collection and status visibility may also reduce abandonment in the segment with the 140-hour median cycle time. A completeness check before dispatch would help prevent replacement shipments that violate R2.

**Deliberate sacrifice and unresolved issues**

The portal requires investment and may increase abandonment for customers who cannot use it comfortably; assisted collection must be available to avoid that result. More complete submissions may move cases onward and increase pressure on manual review, fraud/risk, or repair quoting. The portal does not resolve high-value replacement errors, R1 routing failures, or the part-price delay in repair quotes.

## 4. Redesign C: Right-first-time fulfillment with a targeted quality gate

**Redesigned process flow**

Before Dispatch replacement, add a lightweight quality gate for high-value devices and other cases with elevated replacement risk. Check the selected part and model against the claim, confirm evidence completeness and risk review status, and verify the fulfillment details. Use the available quality-check capacity where possible, with a risk-based or sampled gate for low-risk accessories to protect their service times. The gate is expected to add 6–18 hours depending on its design.

For every Customer complaint/reopen, route through Manual eligibility review before final close or denial, as R3 requires. Use the escalation board selectively for complex or high-impact cases, and feed gate catches, complaints, and repair rework into regular updates to replacement-selection rules.

**Targets**

This redesign targets Fast approval → Dispatch replacement, Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen, Repair device → Quality check → Rework repair, and the complaint/reopen loop. Its primary fit is replacement-heavy high-value claims, where wrong replacements are costly and the post-dispatch complaint tail is long.

**Mechanism of improvement**

The gate is aimed at the long-tail pattern linked to incorrect replacement part selection: Dispatch replacement → Customer complaint/reopen has a mean of 96 hours and a p90 of 180 hours. Catching selection errors before shipment could reduce complaints and reshipments; the feedback loop can also address the 12.5% rework-repair rate for high-value devices. Enforcing manual review on reopened claims improves R3 conformance.

**Deliberate sacrifice and unresolved issues**

The gate adds 6–18 hours to dispatch and may threaten the 24-hour Premium low-risk service threshold if applied too broadly. It does not fix evidence-collection loops or the front-end manual and fraud/risk queues. If the gate checks the wrong attributes or is routinely overridden, it may add delay without preventing the underlying errors.

## 6. Failure modes and displaced bottlenecks

**Redesign A**

- The updated rules may route too many cases to manual review. A 15–35% rise in inflow could exceed the 82% utilization threshold and increase queues, including for Premium customers.
- Overly broad rules could put Premium low-risk accessories on the manual path, increasing the risk of missing the 24-hour service expectation.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** Work shifts from the auto/fast route to Manual eligibility review and Fraud/risk review, especially during Monday and post-campaign peaks.

**Redesign B**

- Customers with low digital literacy may abandon the process if portal use is required or assisted collection is not available.
- The portal may collect files without collecting the specific evidence needed to make a technical decision, leaving repeated requests and R2 deviations in place.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** Fewer evidence loops may send more complete claims into Manual eligibility review, Fraud/risk review, or Repair quote, shifting the queue downstream.

**Redesign C**

- The gate may add enough time to breach Premium service expectations, particularly if it is applied to low-risk accessories or if resource waits push cases beyond the expected 6–18 hours.
- A gate with incomplete part-selection rules may miss the underlying error; agents may also override it under dispatch pressure.
- **Likely bottleneck migration:** Work shifts from post-dispatch complaints and rework to the pre-dispatch quality gate and the two quality inspectors, who are currently underused except after repair campaigns.

## 7. Recommended experiment sequence

**First pilot**

Pilot Redesign A on high-value devices and claims above EUR 400 in one product family or region. This is the clearest initial test because high-value fast-path complaint rates are 18–22%, reviewed/repair rates are 5–6%, and a wrong high-value replacement has an expected cost of EUR 410. Keep low-risk accessory routing out of the pilot’s manual-review rules unless the R1/R2 conditions require it.

**Metrics to monitor**

Track R1 and R2 deviations; complaint/reopen rates by value, segment, and route; median and p90 cycle time by segment; total cost including rework, reshipments, and service credits; manual-review utilization and wait; fraud/risk queue and wait; abandonment; and Premium service-credit exposure. Compare pilot cases with a contemporaneous control group.

**Stop/go thresholds**

- **Go** if high-value R1/R2 deviations fall substantially, high-value complaints move toward the reviewed-path range, and manual-review utilization remains below or close to 82% without a sustained queue increase.
- **Revise** if high-value complaints improve but Premium waits, service-credit exposure, or fraud/risk queues rise materially.
- **Stop or narrow the routing rules** if manual utilization remains above roughly 82–85%, queues grow sharply, or low-risk Premium cases are being delayed without a corresponding quality or compliance benefit.

**Sequencing logic**

After the routing pilot stabilizes, test Redesign B on the unclear defect/missing evidence segment. Monitor repeat evidence requests, abandonment, evidence completeness, and the segment’s cycle-time distribution; provide assisted collection from the start. Then test Redesign C on replacement-heavy high-value claims where complaints persist, measuring both errors caught and added gate time before expanding it.

**Learning whether improvement is real**

Use a control group and follow cases for 30–60 days after first close. Compare rework-inclusive cycle time and total cost, not just time to first decision or first dispatch. Track post-close complaints, reshipments, repair rework, audit exceptions, abandonment, and queues at manual review, fraud/risk, repair quote, quality gate, and escalation. This will show whether a redesign removed delay and errors or moved them to a later step.
