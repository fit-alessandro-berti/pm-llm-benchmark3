The answer is thoughtful and unusually careful about selection, censoring, feature leakage, temporal validation, and the limits of claims about default among all applicants. It correctly distinguishes an activated-account outcome from a counterfactual default outcome for rejected applicants, and its cohort-flow table accounts for all 48,000 cases without inventing subgroup counts.

The main weakness is that the proposed primary estimand and analysis are not fully aligned. The target conditions on eventual activation, which is unknown at prediction time and can be affected by the score or deployment policy. The answer acknowledges this limitation, but the operational interpretation remains ambiguous: it does not explain how a model used at the third event can support decisions for cases whose eventual activation status is unknown. Its weighting proposal also needs sharper definition. Weighting for label observation among activated cases cannot address selection into activation, and the answer should state more plainly that the primary estimand is descriptive of the historically activated population, not a risk estimate for all eligible applicants.

The code has material implementation flaws. `usable_label` does not require `label_available_at <= EXTRACT`, despite that being specified in the prose; `horizon_complete` is based on the extraction date rather than the event-observation boundary or reliable outcome-follow-up cutoff; and `lag_eligible` is computed but not used to define the analysis cohort. Label filtering checks availability time but does not enforce that the label corresponds to the 90-day horizon or a reliable feed. Also, the PM4Py prefix call is largely redundant because the event data is already truncated to exactly three events per case, and its assertions assume behavior that may depend on PM4Py’s conversion and prefix semantics. The answer flags missing ingestion history and other assumptions, but the skeleton does not implement several of its stated safeguards.

These issues prevent a high score despite strong coverage of the requested design topics.
