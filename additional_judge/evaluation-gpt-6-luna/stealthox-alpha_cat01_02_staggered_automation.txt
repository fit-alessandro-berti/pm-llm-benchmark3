6.0/10 — The answer is thorough and appropriately cautious about endogenous rollout, changing treatment intensity, shared-queue spillovers, and the difference between median and tail outcomes. Its recommendation for a randomized phase-in with better process and queue data is well matched to the decision.

Several quantitative claims are inaccurate or insufficiently supported. For example, the stated 13–29% rise in adopter arrivals per FTE does not match all branches under the stated comparisons, and the “3–7h” median improvement and “1–2.6pp” rework improvement are presented without transparent calculations in the supplied text. The causal ranges and confidence scores therefore convey more precision than this five-branch, eight-month panel can justify. The proposed event-study is also underidentified in practice: there are only three pre-rollout months for the April adopters, and the regression code’s pooled event-time indicators do not implement the named group-time ATT or Sun–Abraham estimator. Its event-time reference and control coding also make the coefficients difficult to interpret as clean staggered-adoption effects.

The answer meets most format requirements and has useful code, but the questionable arithmetic and overly confident effect ranges weaken its central assessment.
