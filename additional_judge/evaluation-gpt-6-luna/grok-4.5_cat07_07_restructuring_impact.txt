# 4. Risks invisible in average cycle time

- **Tail latency:** A stable mean can hide a much worse p90 or p95, leaving a subset of cases waiting far longer.
- **High-complexity cases:** Faster simple cases can mask slower complex cases, particularly if all high-complexity work enters a shared queue.
- **Local knowledge loss:** Average time may hold while regional conventions are missed, increasing rework or reducing customer satisfaction.
- **Fairness:** Overall averages can hide longer waits or worse outcomes for East, particular customer segments, or cases needing language or regional accommodation.
- **Learning:** Local staff may gradually lose technical skills and become more dependent on the shared service, a trend unlikely to appear in early averages.
- **Accountability:** Standardized approvals can improve consistency while leaving case ownership or responsibility for follow-up unclear.
- **Exception handling:** Unusual cases may require extra referrals, repeated queue visits, or workarounds, even if the ordinary path gets faster.
- **Resilience:** Higher utilization can look efficient in normal periods while making the service more vulnerable to absence or demand spikes.

# 5. Pilot design

- **Pilot scope:** Start with one bounded technical shared-service queue and a defined set of high-complexity cases. Keep legal/risk and difficult Quality Check centralization out of the first phase, or introduce them as separately measurable phases.
- **Comparison group:** Use a team or case class that remains on the current model as a concurrent comparison. Where feasible, phase the change across teams so East’s experience and North/South’s potential loss of embedded expertise can be assessed separately.
- **Observation window:** Collect a pre-change baseline long enough to cover normal weekly variation, then observe the pilot through rollout and a period sufficient for outcome and rework events to accumulate. Keep dates and any staffing or policy changes recorded.
- **Segmentation:** Report results by team, case complexity, required specialty, and customer segment. Also distinguish cases with local expertise and known absence periods where the data supports it.
- **Guardrails:** Set in advance acceptable limits for p90 wait and cycle time, rework, SLA misses, reassignment, and gaps between teams or customer segments. Include queue-specific thresholds for legal/risk and approval work.
- **Staff feedback:** Collect regular feedback from local caseworkers, specialists, supervisors, and intake staff about ownership, advice quality, workload, and exceptions. Record concrete cases and connect them to event-log traces where possible.
- **Adjustment or rollback triggers:** Pause expansion if queue p90 waits exceed agreed limits, high-complexity tails or rework worsen materially, team/segment gaps widen, or absences cause severe backlogs. Adjust routing, capacity, local advice access, or ownership rules before resuming.

# 6. Recommendation

**Pilot first**, with a bounded technical shared-service phase and explicit queue, quality, fairness, and accountability guardrails. The expected reduction in local loops and relief for East are plausible, but the proposed centralization combines several scarce functions and could move delay into shared queues. Expand only if the pilot shows sustainable queue tails, acceptable rework and SLA performance, clear ownership, and no material worsening in team or customer-segment gaps.

Management should not infer from early average cycle times that the change has improved the process overall. Early averages can be driven by simple cases, miss delayed outcomes and rework, and conceal deterioration in high-complexity cases, fairness, resilience, or local capability.

import pandas as pd

df_post = pm4py.format_dataframe(

    activity_key="concept:name", timestamp_key="time:timestamp"

def filter_segment(df, team=None, complexity=None, specialty=None, segment=None):
    if team is not None:
        df = df[df["team"].isin(team)]
    if complexity is not None:
        df = df[df["case_complexity"].isin(complexity)]
    if specialty is not None:
        df = df[df["required_specialty"].isin(specialty)]
    if segment is not None:
        df = df[df["customer_segment"].isin(segment)]
    return df

def case_metrics(df):
    cases = df.groupby("case:concept:name").agg(
        start=("time:timestamp", "min"),
        end=("time:timestamp", "max"),
        team=("team", "first"),
        complexity=("case_complexity", "first"),
        segment=("customer_segment", "first"),
        specialty=("required_specialty", "first"),
        outcome=("outcome", "first"),
        rework=("rework_flag", "max"),
        reassignments=("reassignment_count", "max"),
        resources=("org:resource", "nunique"),
    )
    cases["cycle_days"] = (cases["end"] - cases["start"]).dt.total_seconds() / 86400
    return cases.reset_index()

def handover_waits(df):
    df = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"])
    rows = []
    for case, g in df.groupby("case:concept:name", sort=False):
        g = g.sort_values("time:timestamp")
        resources = g["org:resource"].to_numpy()
        times = g["time:timestamp"].to_numpy()
        for i in range(1, len(g)):
            if resources[i] != resources[i - 1]:
                rows.append({
                    "case": case,
                    "from_resource": resources[i - 1],
                    "to_resource": resources[i],
                    "wait_hours": (
                        pd.Timestamp(times[i]) - pd.Timestamp(times[i - 1])
                    ).total_seconds() / 3600,
                    "team": g["team"].iloc[i],
                    "complexity": g["case_complexity"].iloc[0],
                    "segment": g["customer_segment"].iloc[0],
                    "specialty": g["required_specialty"].iloc[0],
                })
    return pd.DataFrame(rows)

# Compare networks, flow, waits, variants, roles, rework, and outcomes by segment.
for label, df in [("pre", df_pre), ("post", df_post)]:
    handovers = pm4py.discover_handover_of_work_network(df)
    collaboration = pm4py.discover_working_together_network(df)
    subcontracting = pm4py.discover_subcontracting_network(df)
    roles = pm4py.discover_organizational_roles(df)
    dfg, starts, ends = pm4py.discover_dfg(df)
    performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(df)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(df)
    rework = pm4py.get_rework_cases_per_activity(df)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(df)
    # Use pm4py.filter_event_attribute_values and pm4py.filter_variants
    # for focused comparisons; report results by team, complexity,
    # required specialty, and customer segment.

    waits = handover_waits(df)
    wait_summary = waits.groupby(
        ["from_resource", "to_resource", "team", "complexity", "specialty", "segment"]
    )["wait_hours"].agg(p50="median", p90=lambda s: s.quantile(.9), n="count")

    metrics = case_metrics(df)
    cycle_summary = metrics.groupby(
        ["team", "complexity", "specialty", "segment"]
    )["cycle_days"].agg(
        median="median", p90=lambda s: s.quantile(.9), mean="mean"
    )
