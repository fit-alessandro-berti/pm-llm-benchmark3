## 2. Process-model consistency checks

Build validation around each prediction instance and its t0 prefix.

- **Prefix-time availability:** Require a definition, source timestamp, and point-in-time value for every feature. Check that the value could have been known by t0, including joins and refresh schedules. Flag missing timestamps and values computed at extraction or closure.
- **Complete-trace dependence:** Trace feature lineage to event sets and source tables. Recompute trace aggregates from the prefix ending at t0; test that adding post-t0 events cannot change the t0 feature value.
- **Activity-order feasibility:** Align each prefix and reported sequence pattern to the normative process model. Use prefix and declarative constraints for required predecessors, appeal eligibility, closure, and evidence cycles. Treat violations as data-quality findings unless a documented process variant explains them.
- **Same-timestamp ambiguity:** Where events share a timestamp, use a reliable sequence index or other validated ordering metadata. Without it, mark order-dependent features and patterns ambiguous; do not infer ordering from the coarse activity labels alone.
- **Explanation-state checks:** For every cited activity, classify it relative to t0 as observed in the prefix, enabled next, impossible in the current state, or merely future-possible. A future-possible event may be plausible in the case but cannot explain a t0 prediction if it has not occurred.
- **Repeated settlement decisions:** Permit a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` only when an earlier Partial or Deny is followed by an eligible appeal and the later decision is linked to reconsideration. Distinguish first decision, reconsidered decision, and final decision in event semantics and explanation checks.

These checks validate process consistency and timing; they do not establish that a feature causes delay.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

Predictive validity, causal interpretation, actionability, and fairness are separate standards. A feature may improve held-out prediction while being a proxy, unavailable at the prediction point, or unsuitable as an operational reason. A correlation does not establish that changing the feature changes settlement time. Actionability requires a plausible intervention and evidence that it improves outcomes. Fairness review asks who is affected and whether errors or decisions differ across groups; it is not a substitute for temporal validation.

- **Potentially valid after lineage repair:** Prefix document-request count; prior-claim count with a strict t0 cutoff; employee experience as of t0; same-day document count from an as-of-t0 snapshot; and a risk score frozen at t0. Final handler team requires historical assignment lineage before it can be considered.
- **Legitimate prefix-derived signals:** Initial damage complexity, elapsed time at t0, and a properly timestamped queue snapshot. They can support predictive descriptions, not unqualified causal or blame claims.
- **Temporally available but requiring review:** Calendar week, claimant region, and claimant channel. They may carry predictive information while reflecting policy, holiday, service-access, or other group differences. Validate stability, fairness, and intended use.
- **Not valid for this prediction point:** Total case duration, appeal seen anywhere, full-trace variant, final decision, last resource role, and future sequence patterns. Recompute only from information available at the relevant prediction point.
- **Local features with unknown lineage:** Initial reserve needs a point-in-time availability check before it can be described as an admissible signal.
