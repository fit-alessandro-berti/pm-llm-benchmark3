5.5/10 — The answer shows a strong grasp of selective outcome observation, distinguishes activation-conditioned risk from counterfactual risk, and addresses leakage, temporal validation, policy change, migration, and process-aware features. But several central design and implementation flaws make it fall short of the requested rigorous study.

- **The primary target is not operationally well-defined.** It calls for a vector of eventual states while also describing the estimand as a probability of activation followed by default. Those require an explicit time origin and prediction horizon for application-pathway states, plus rules for cases that can transfer and later reactivate. “Still open at extraction” is an as-of status, not a stable competing outcome.
- **The cohort flow overstates what the snapshot establishes.** The supplied counts are labels and omissions from the current export, but the answer treats them as mutually exclusive final categories without resolving overlap, transfer reappearance, or which of the 3,600 activated cases are immature versus permanently missing. The table also does not identify these ambiguities.
- **Censoring and weighting are under-specified.** IPCW requires a defined censoring process, time scale, risk sets, and positivity assumptions. The answer proposes weights for administrative censoring and label delay but does not show how to handle informative permanent missingness or distinguish it from ordinary delayed feeds. Transfer linkage covering only 60% does not support treating transfer as a fully observed competing outcome.
- **The code has material correctness issues.** It filters events to the observation window before building case outcomes, which can hide later activation or terminal events needed for labels and follow-up. The fixed-prefix logic uses `get_prefixes_from_log` and then groups by case, but does not establish that this function returns only the intended prefix or one row per case; the subsequent `actual_prefix_len >= PREFIX_LEN` check cannot reliably enforce a full three-event prefix. The prediction-time audit is tautological because `prediction_time` is derived from the same prefix events. The code also labels every activated account without an observed label but past the 125-day maturity rule as a “feed missing candidate,” although `default_90d` may simply be unavailable for other reasons.
- **Some requested details are asserted rather than implemented.** Duplicate events are removed using a broad key without demonstrating that it distinguishes legitimate repeated events. Activity renaming is represented by a small illustrative map, but unreconciled names are not actually quarantined. The temporal split places cases starting after the migration into a “holdout” despite the stated extraction date and does not define how label maturity will be enforced in each split or how calibration will be assessed.
- **The answer’s prose is stronger than its code.** It correctly says rejected and withdrawn cases do not have observed default labels, but the proposed multi-state estimator and reported probability vector are not concretely tied to a specified model or estimand.

The answer is thoughtful and covers most required topics, but the ambiguous primary endpoint and flawed cohort/prefix implementation prevent a higher grade.
