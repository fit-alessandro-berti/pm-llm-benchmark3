6.8/10. The answer is well structured and engages seriously with scope, timing, competing authority, and evidence. It assesses every case, distinguishes breaches from evidentiary defects, gives plausible alternatives, and correctly treats later approvals as non-curative.

The main weakness is inconsistency around Finance approval. GPIP-4 A2 requires both an independent budget owner and a Finance Controller before commitment. The answer’s own hierarchy says the CFO email cannot override board-level GPIP controls without clear authority, yet it classifies P-505 and P-507 as justified based on joint Incident Command authorization without evidence that those authorizers held either required role. Independence from the requester would not, by itself, establish those roles. Under the answer’s stated burden of proof, these cases should be noncompliant or indeterminate unless it explains why D1 validly satisfies A2.

The policy recommendation also conflicts with the existing local SOP: it allows Plant *or* Finance approval for purchases from EUR 25,000 to EUR 49,999, while B2 requires both, and its authority clause does not clearly permit an incident directive to change those approval requirements. A few case conclusions are also stated more firmly than the evidence supports: P-505’s supplier acknowledgment supports a pre-expiry commitment but does not conclusively establish when commitment occurred, and treating an Incident Command authorizer as a “final business approver” under A1 is an assumption rather than an explicit rule.

These are material issues in an otherwise thoughtful assessment, especially because they affect classifications and the proposed replacement clause.
