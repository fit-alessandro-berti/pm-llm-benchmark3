5.0 — The answer follows the required structure and identifies several important cases correctly, but it has material semantic, evidentiary, and implementation flaws.

The strongest parts are its treatment of SUP-703, SUP-708, SUP-714, and SUP-712 as serious concerns; its recognition that PD-3 and FD-7 have limited effective windows; and its acknowledgement that missing notification evidence, reactivation, rejected-lead scope, and the missing information-request loop complicate conformance assessment.

Major problems:

- It misstates M0 as serializing sanctions and quality checks. The prompt explicitly defines them as concurrent. This error weakens both briefs and the revised architecture.
- It calls SUP-715 a CS-9 violation. CS-9 requires requester and manual approver to be different humans; it does not prohibit a reviewer and approver from being spouses. The stated conflict-of-interest policy requires disclosure, but the case does not establish that this pairing violates that policy or that the relevant people failed to disclose.
- It says the defense “correctly” identifies resource rules as a data enrichment problem, although SUP-712 has evidence resolving the aliases to the same human. That is evidence of a substantive CS-9 breach, not merely missing data.
- Its SUP-706 conclusion is too strong: the CRM message exists, but the mutable link makes attribution uncertain. The appropriate issue is whether the event can be reliably linked to this case, not simply a logging artifact.
- It treats SUP-711 as data-quality indeterminate despite the duplicate rows sharing a source transaction ID. That supports a duplicated logging event, not an unresolved process violation.
- The prosecution’s claim that SUP-707 creates a “black box” violating a decision-state requirement is unsupported. The evidence says the requests obtained missing certificates before approval; the defect is that M0 cannot express the practice.
- The revised POWL proposal is not executable as written: it references `approved_branch` before defining it, omits Auto_Approve and the emergency route from the actual model, and leaves reactivation disconnected. It also claims to represent concurrency while adding a sanctions-before-quality edge, which makes them serial.
- The validation pseudocode is incomplete and brittle: it assumes required fields and trace events exist, checks only a limited set of rules, and does not handle emergency eligibility, spend caps, or quality deadlines. The overlay table also presents a conditional reactivation rule without any cited authority.

The final classifications are therefore not fully reliable, especially SUP-715, SUP-706, and SUP-711. A stronger answer would distinguish trace conformance from policy conformance, avoid treating M0 as the sole authority, and provide a runnable model that actually represents the routes it describes.
