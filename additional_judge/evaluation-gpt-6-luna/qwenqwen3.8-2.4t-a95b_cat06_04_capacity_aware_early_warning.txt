The answer covers all six required sections, gives a case-by-case allocation, and handles the clearest policy requirements well: it excludes I-03 on stage grounds, suppresses I-05’s unchanged alert, treats I-07’s new log as meaningful evidence, and addresses segment calibration, spillovers, fairness, and intervention bias in future training data. Its proposed portfolio of I-04, I-02, and I-09 with one slot held in reserve is plausible.

The main weakness is the expected-impact arithmetic. The adverse row’s stated “direct economic benefit” range is inconsistent with its own lower-bound calculation: if I-09 gets no benefit, the benefit after false-alarm burden is €4,600, while the all-lower-bound case gives €8,200. The row reverses those endpoints. The adverse net-impact range also does not clearly follow from the stated costs and spillover figures. In addition, the spillover estimate uses an assumed €20,000 average exposure without showing support for that figure, and the optimistic spillover range is asserted rather than derived from the stated per-case model.

The decision rule is thoughtfully structured, but it uses the risk adjustments mainly to describe alert credibility; it does not clearly show how they change the ranking or address the uncertainty in the intervention effects for Strategic Enterprise and Non-Native-Language Support in the portfolio calculations. Several proposed thresholds and monitoring triggers are reasonable, but appear arbitrary and are not tied to evidence or a specified decision procedure.

Overall, this is a strong and operationally aware answer, but the arithmetic and uncertainty treatment fall short of the requested decision-theoretic rigor.
