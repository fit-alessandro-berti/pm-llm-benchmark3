1. Control-Objective Decomposition

FEP-1 aims to prevent a single person from initiating and authorizing an erroneous or fraudulent transfer. Its objectives include:

- **Separate initiation from authorization.** Distinct human preparers and releasers directly support this objective. AV-2, MG-4, and RA-7 do not by themselves make a same-user payment conform to FEP-1. ED-1 permits a bounded exception only when all its conditions are met.
- **Prevent invalid or misdirected payments before release.** AV-2 can block duplicates, sanctions matches, recent bank changes, limit breaches, and basic purchase-order mismatches. It does not confirm receipt, commercial reasonableness, or freedom from collusion. MG-4 can prevent release when independent, informed, and timely. RA-7 is generally too late to prevent release.
- **Ensure independent scrutiny of the transaction.** MG-4 can provide this if the approver is independent, has no unresolved conflict, and reviews the invoice, bank details, and receipt evidence. A shared or conflicted credential, late ratification, or rubber stamp weakens it. AV-2 provides automated checks, not independent human judgment.
- **Detect errors and fraud after release.** RA-7 can detect defects if review is timely and sufficiently broad. AV-2 detects only its defined conditions. MG-4 can detect issues only if its review is substantive.
- **Stop, recall, or recover a defective transfer.** A pre-settlement MG-4 approval can still prevent settlement. RA-7 may support correction while recall is available, usually within 24 hours; later recovery is uncertain. AV-2 can block before release but cannot recover settled funds.
- **Resist collusion and compromised credentials.** FEP-1’s independent human actors reduce the risk of unilateral action. AV-2 does not detect collusion or determine who controlled a compromised account. MG-4 helps only if genuinely independent. RA-7 may find patterns but is not inherently collusion-resistant. ED-1 does not remove these risks.

2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

Assess the package against the threat it is meant to address, not by counting control events.

- **Fully equivalent:** The package achieves the relevant FEP-1 objectives with comparable prevention and independent authorization. Approval occurs before release or irrevocable settlement; the reviewer is independent and informed; controls cover the material risks; overrides are blocked or independently authorized; evidence is reliable; and collusion or credential compromise is meaningfully constrained. For a same-user payment, ED-1’s conditions must all hold for the exception to be permitted.
- **Partially equivalent:** The package meaningfully substitutes for some objectives but leaves material gaps, such as an effective independent review before settlement with a late release-stage approval, or strong automated checks that do not cover receipt or commercial validity.
- **Merely mitigating:** The package reduces exposure but does not provide equivalent authorization or dependable prevention. Examples include prompt post-release audit with a possible recall window, or AV-2 checks that address only specific transaction risks.
- **Ineffective:** Controls are absent, overridden without independent scrutiny, conflicted, too late to prevent or recover the loss, or unsupported by reliable evidence.

For each package, consider:

- **Timing:** Did control activity occur before release, before settlement, or only after recovery became uncertain?
- **Independence:** Were reviewer and operator genuinely separate, free of conflicts, and using distinct credentials?
- **Coverage:** Did controls address the actual risk, including receipt and commercial validity, as well as AV-2’s narrower checks?
- **Overrideability:** Could an operator bypass a warning, and was the override independently reviewed?
- **Recoverability:** Was there a realistic opportunity to stop settlement or recall funds?
- **Evidence quality:** Are approvals and checks documented with evidence of substantive review?
- **Collusion resistance:** Could repeated pairings, shared workstations, or shared credentials undermine nominal separation?

Equivalent control need not mean identical steps or exactly the same expected loss. It must also satisfy the control objective, provide credible independent challenge, and leave risk within the organization’s acceptable tolerance. A low expected loss can still conceal a failure of authorization or independence; conversely, a bounded and evidenced exception may meet the objective despite nominal same-user handling.

4. Cross-Case Conclusions and Remediation

- FEP-1 is a clear role-separation rule, while the broader control objective concerns independent authorization and prevention of erroneous or fraudulent transfers. A case may violate the rule without proving a control-objective failure, and distinct users may satisfy the rule while independence or transaction validity fails.
- AV-2’s full pass supports only the checks it performs. It cannot substitute for receipt confirmation, commercial review, or independent human authorization. Overrides require particular scrutiny.
- MG-4 is strongest before release. Approval after release but before settlement may still prevent settlement; approval after settlement is retrospective ratification and cannot be treated as equivalent preventive approval.
- RA-7 is detective. Its value as a corrective control depends on prompt review and a real chance to stop or recall the transfer. Audits outside the recall window cannot be assumed to restore the lost funds.
- ED-1 must be evaluated against every stated condition. A plausible emergency or clean outcome does not waive the pre-existing token, amount, vendor-tenure, bank-change, AV-2, approval, and audit requirements.
- Record ED-1 token creation and effective times so a late token can be distinguished from delayed logging. Require documented, independent review of invoice, bank details, and receipt evidence; restrict and audit overrides; and route emergency payments for prompt audit while recovery remains possible.
- Investigate the u12/u13 pairing and shared workstation across AP-204 and AP-209. Confirm whether the payment cell has safeguards that provide independent challenge, while preserving the distinction between a suspicious pattern and proven collusion.
- Track exceptions and defects by risk and control gap, not by amount or activity count alone. AP-201’s low exposure does not make its procedure conforming; AP-204’s distinct users do not establish control effectiveness; and AP-205’s prevented payment should not be counted as a completed-payment breach.
