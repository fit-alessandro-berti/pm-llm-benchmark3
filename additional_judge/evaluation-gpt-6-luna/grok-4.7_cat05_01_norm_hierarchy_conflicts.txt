The main flaw is an inconsistency in P-507. The answer says an exception proponent must prove the scope and identity predicates, and acknowledges that the asset identification and authorizer identities are missing. Yet it classifies the case as JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION, then suggests those gaps could reverse the label. Under its own burden-of-proof method, those missing predicates should prevent an unconditional justified-exception classification. It also treats A1 as barring any requester or committer from participating in joint authorization, although A1 bars them from being the *final business approver*; that broader rule is defensible only if the joint authorization is established as that approval.

These are material defects in the case classification and rule formalization, but the remaining analysis is largely consistent and carefully supported.
