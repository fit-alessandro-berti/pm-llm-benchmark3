The response presents a clear, defensible choice—segmented hybrid deployment—and correctly recognizes the central conflict: faster median cycle time and fewer handovers came alongside worse tail duration, rework, complaints, and activation quality. It also proposes relevant guardrails and follow-up measurement.

However, it overstates what the evidence supports. It treats the simple-house segment’s observed results as causal proof that national scaling will save €23 per case, and proposes precise rollout thresholds and a 3-person pod design without a stated basis. It also recommends retaining survey skipping for low-risk houses even though the evidence does not isolate the effect of skipping from the other redesign changes. The measurement plan favors operational monitoring and broad cost tracking over a clearly prioritized value-of-information argument; it does not sufficiently foreground the randomized survey-skip experiment or the need to improve incomplete segment and risk measurement. The requested evidence table and other required sections are also absent or not clearly delivered in the visible answer.
