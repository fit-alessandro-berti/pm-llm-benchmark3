## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

**January–March:** Baseline is stable: AUROC is 0.81–0.82, Brier 0.130–0.132, ECE 0.022–0.025, and label maturity is complete. Variant breach rates are also broadly stable. These months are the strongest reference period, subject to ordinary representativeness limits.

**April:** This is primarily a pipeline incident. The diagnosis rename disrupted prediction prefixes, duplicates and unknown activities increased, and queue features were missing. AUROC fell to 0.67 and calibration degraded sharply while the observed breach rate remained near baseline. The large score-to-outcome gap is consistent with corrupted inputs, not sufficient evidence of a changed outcome mechanism.

**May:** The mapping patch and backfill coincided with substantial metric recovery. However, the cached “number of events before diagnosis” feature was not fully backfilled, and duplicate removal was incomplete before April 20. May looks closer to baseline but needs lineage checks; April should not be treated as a clean training cohort.

**June:** Remote routing changed the population of paths: V2 rose to 52%. Aggregate breach rate fell to 0.15, but that could reflect more low-breach remote cases rather than a model improvement. V2’s matured breach rate fell to 0.09 while V1 stayed at 0.16 and V3 rose to 0.35. Remote edge time remained stable. This points to a mix shift and possibly a policy-related change in which cases enter each path, not uniform process improvement.

**July:** Supplier delays begin to show in the V3 path: part receipt time rose to 65 hours and V3 breach rate to 0.46. Aggregate metrics worsened, but labels are 98% mature, so censoring is unlikely to be the main explanation for July. This is an early operational warning.

**August–September:** The strongest evidence is localized to V3 and parts performance. Part receipt time reached 130–148 hours; matured V3 breach rates rose to 0.64 and 0.61. V3’s share also grew, so the aggregate metrics combine within-path deterioration with a changing path mix. September adds Partner Z, whose population is more rural, older-device, and more V3-heavy. The raw Partner Z breach rate is not a fair comparison because its labels mature more slowly. These months may involve process drift, covariate and population shift, and possibly conditional concept drift; the available evidence does not isolate their contributions.

**October:** AUROC, Brier, and ECE look poor, but only 55% of labels are mature, median delay is 52 days, and the backlog disproportionately affects long resolution paths. The observed breach rate of 0.16 is therefore not comparable to mature months and may be biased downward. Treat October predictive and outcome metrics as provisional; its process evidence still supports ongoing V3 delays.

These episodes should not be combined into one failure: April has a clear input-construction defect, June has a routing and mix change, July–September show real parts-process deterioration alongside population changes, and October has severe outcome-maturity limitations.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

| Test | Expected under one explanation | Expected under competing explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Plot label-maturity curves by start month, variant, source, and breach status; backtest at fixed vintages such as 30, 60, and 90 days | Under censoring, observed breach rates and metrics shift materially as labels mature, especially for long paths and Partner Z. | Under true model/process deterioration, the same fixed-vintage metrics remain degraded after comparable maturity is reached. |
| Recompute April–May features and scores after diagnosis-label normalization and duplicate cleaning; compare with stored features and predictions | Under pipeline failure, April prefixes, missingness, and metrics materially improve after clean reconstruction; discrepancies align with affected source dates. | Under genuine concept or process change, corrected inputs leave within-path degradation largely intact. |
| Stratify calibration, AUROC, and Brier by variant, source, and month on maturity-matched cohorts | Under mix shift, aggregate performance changes but within-variant/source metrics are relatively stable; standardizing mix reduces the gap. | Under within-segment deterioration, metrics remain worse inside V3 or a source after composition is held constant. |
| Compare process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges by both event-calendar month and case-start cohort | Under process drift, path frequency or edge duration changes, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, and associates with V3 outcomes. | Under a purely predictive or label issue, process edge performance is stable after cohort definitions are aligned. |
| Test whether outcome relationships conditional on the same path and available decision-time features changed, using mature cohorts and month interactions | Under concept drift, month or supplier-period effects persist after controlling for path, features, and maturity. | Under covariate/process drift explained by measured inputs, conditional relationships are stable once those inputs and path are included. |
| Reweight or transport September Partner Z cases to non-Z distributions of variant, rural distance, device age, and other decision-time covariates | Under population shift alone, the raw gap shrinks after reweighting and maturity adjustment. | Under a residual source-specific relationship or missing operational feature, a gap persists after reweighting. |
| Audit feature missingness, source lineage, event order, duplicate rates, and cached-feature recomputation by source and date | Under pipeline failure, defects cluster around the rename, patch, or duplicate-cleaning boundary and reconstructed values differ from production values. | Under real drift, lineage is consistent while feature distributions or conditional outcomes change. |

For each test, retain cohort definitions, extraction versions, label vintage, and source-level counts so apparent improvement cannot come from changing the evaluated population.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment:** Do not use October’s available labels as ground truth for training or broad performance claims. Preserve the deployed model for shadow comparison while adding operational safeguards for the deteriorating V3 parts path, such as supplier escalation and targeted case review. Keep April out of supervised training until its features and predictions are reconstructed and reconciled.

**Short-term recalibration:** Consider recalibration only after maturity-matched, pipeline-clean evaluation. If discrimination remains acceptable within a segment and the main residual is a stable calibration offset, recalibrate on a recent, mature cohort. Validate separately for V1, V2, and V3 and by source; avoid a global adjustment that could distort stable V1/V2 performance.

**Feature repair:** Normalize diagnosis activities before prefix generation; rebuild April’s cached count and other derived features from raw events; apply a documented duplicate policy consistently across sources; audit queue-feature lineage. Add decision-time rural travel distance, device age, and supplier/parts status if they are available at prediction time and are operationally valid.

**Full retraining:** Retrain only after clean feature reconstruction, mature labels, and evidence that conditional outcome relationships have changed or that repaired/expanded features materially improve stable out-of-time evaluation. Use temporal validation and evaluate each variant and source. Until then, January–March are the cleanest training reference; May–June may be usable only after lineage checks and with explicit period effects; April is unsafe pending reconstruction; July–September are useful for process diagnosis but should enter supervised training only after maturity checks and drift analysis; October is unsafe until label maturity stabilizes.

A variant-specific or segment-specific model is not yet justified by the aggregate tables alone. First test whether V3 has enough mature data and persistent conditional residuals after adding supplier and population features. A targeted V3 calibration or model may be reasonable if that evidence holds and sample sizes support stable estimation. Partner Z needs a separate adjusted evaluation, not an automatic standalone model.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Maintain linked monitors with explicit cohort and vintage definitions:

- **Predictive:** AUROC, Brier, precision/recall at operational thresholds, and score distributions by case-start month, variant, and source; publish counts and confidence intervals.
- **Calibration:** calibration-in-the-large, slope, reliability bins, and ECE on fixed-vintage labels, stratified by variant and source.
- **Label maturity:** maturity curves and delay distributions by variant, source, outcome, and path length. Mark immature estimates as provisional.
- **Data quality:** unknown activity share, duplicate rates, event ordering, missing queue features, diagnosis-anchor success, prefix integrity, and feature lineage/recomputation checks.
- **Process variant:** V1/V2/V3 and unmapped-path frequencies, transition deviations, and variant discovery, reported separately for case-start and event-calendar cohorts.
- **Performance-DFG:** edge frequency and elapsed-time distributions, especially `Order Part -> Part Received` and diagnosis-to-`Remote Fix`; alert on sustained changes by source and variant.
- **Population:** covariate and source mix, Partner Z share, rural distance, device age, and support/overlap checks for transport estimates.
- **Intervention/deployment:** score and recommendation rates, overrides, action uptake, and outcomes after intervention. Track policy/model versions so an intervention-induced process change is not mistaken for passive drift.

Alert dependencies should gate model action. A label-delay alarm should mark outcome metrics provisional and schedule fixed-vintage refreshes; it should not trigger retraining by itself. A data-quality alarm should block affected cohorts from training and calibration until reconstruction. A process alert can trigger operational review immediately, while predictive retraining requires mature labels and evidence of residual conditional deterioration. Population-mix alerts should prompt stratification or reweighting before model changes.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")
raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)

# Normalize before feature extraction and variant discovery.
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
})

# Example policy: exact case/activity/timestamp duplicates keep the first source row.
# Prefer a stable source event ID when available; log policy and counts.
raw["source_row"] = range(len(raw))
dedup = raw.sort_values(
    ["case_id", "timestamp", "source_row"]
).drop_duplicates(
    ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"], keep="first"
).copy()

events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    dedup, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
    timestamp_key="timestamp"
)

temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(events)

# Cohort A: events occurring in each calendar month, for operational flow evidence.
# Cohort B: all events belonging to cases started in each month, for case outcomes.
rows = []
for start in pd.date_range("2026-01-01", "2026-10-01", freq="MS"):
    end = start + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)
    event_cohort = pm4py.filter_time_range(
        events, start, end, mode="events"
    )

    starts = events.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"].min()
    case_ids = starts[(starts >= start) & (starts < end)].index
    start_cohort = events[
        events["case:concept:name"].isin(case_ids)
    ].copy()

    for cohort_name, cohort in [
        ("event_calendar", event_cohort),
        ("case_start", start_cohort),
    ]:
        if cohort.empty:
            continue
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(cohort)
        perf_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(cohort)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(cohort)
        rows.append({
            "month": start.strftime("%Y-%m"),
            "cohort": cohort_name,
            "cases": cohort["case:concept:name"].nunique(),
            "events": len(cohort),
            "variants": len(variants),
            "part_edge_count": dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"), 0),
            "part_edge_duration": perf_dfg.get(("Order Part", "Part Received")),
            "remote_edge_duration": perf_dfg.get(
                ("Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix")
            ),
            "unknown_share": cohort["concept:name"].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
        })

monthly_evidence = pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["month", "cohort"])
print(monthly_evidence)

# Archive monthly_evidence, temporal features, input counts, duplicate counts,
# mapping version, cohort definition, and PM4Py version for an auditable record.
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Why it is harmful | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retraining on immature October labels | The backlog hides outcomes disproportionately for longer paths, likely understating risk in those paths. | Exclude October from supervised training until fixed-vintage maturity criteria are met; compare later vintages. |
| Calibrating around the April pipeline defect | A calibration fit could encode broken prefixes, missing queue features, and duplicate events as if they were real risk changes. | Rebuild affected features from normalized, deduplicated events and reconcile score lineage before using April. |
| Ignoring supplier deterioration because recent labels are immature | Label uncertainty does not erase the observed rise in part receipt time and mature V3 breaches. | Act on supplier and V3 operations now; keep model conclusions separate until labels mature. |
| Treating June’s aggregate breach reduction as model improvement | The rate may fall because more cases went through lower-breach V2, while within-path performance did not improve. | Report variant-stratified and mix-standardized outcomes. |
| Treating Partner Z’s raw gap as model failure | The comparison mixes slower label maturity, greater V3 use, rural distance, and older devices. | Compare maturity-matched, reweighted cases and assess residual errors within comparable paths and covariates. |
| Dismissing Partner Z’s gap as only mix | Missing travel, device, or operating constraints may leave real residual calibration error. | Test conditional residuals and add valid decision-time features; consider segment calibration only with adequate stable data. |
| Calling all August–September degradation concept drift | Process changes and omitted supplier or population features may explain the relationship shift. | Run same-path conditional tests and feature-availability audits before retraining. |
| Calling all August–September degradation covariate drift | A genuine conditional change could persist after mix adjustment and require model adaptation. | Compare within-V3 discrimination and calibration on mature matched cohorts; retrain if residual change remains. |
| Treating event-calendar DFG evidence as case-start outcome evidence | Event cohorts include cases started earlier and can misattribute outcome changes to the wrong month. | Label both cohort views and use case-start cohorts for prediction/outcome evaluation. |
| Replacing the global model for a localized V3 issue | Broad retraining can damage stable V1/V2 performance and obscure the supplier problem. | Evaluate targeted V3 calibration or modeling while maintaining a stable baseline for other variants.
