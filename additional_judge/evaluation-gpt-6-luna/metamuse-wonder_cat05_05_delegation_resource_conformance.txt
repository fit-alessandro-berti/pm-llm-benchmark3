It also identifies several important issues, including CHG-508’s alias-based self-approval, CHG-510’s prohibited subdelegation, and CHG-506’s account-use uncertainty.

However, several material errors undermine the analysis:

- **CHG-505’s retrospective review is not late.** Two business days after the Tuesday, August 11 approval is Thursday, August 13; the review occurred Friday, August 14. The response repeatedly calls it late, apparently miscalculating the deadline.
- **The code’s RR4 deadline calculation is wrong.** It adds two business days and then sets the deadline to 23:59 on that date, which would make CHG-505 late despite the stated event being within two business days. The response also does not clearly define whether the rule measures elapsed time or permits the full second business day.
- **CHG-505’s retrospective reviewer authority needs more careful treatment.** Ben’s D-QM-01 covers production-equipment changes, not cooling-system changes, so the review is out of scope. That is the relevant defect, regardless of lateness.
- **CHG-503’s and CHG-509’s service-account cases are not consistently carried through.** CHG-503 should remain unattributed for RR2 absent reliable session mapping. CHG-509 has strong evidence identifying Eva, so it should not be described as still indeterminate on identity; its approval authority remains problematic because Asha’s absence on August 15 is not established.
- **The code’s PM4Py four-eyes interpretation is fragile.** It treats every case missing from the filtered log as a same-human violation, which can conflate missing activity pairs with actual same-human pairs. A robust check should separately verify that both activities exist before interpreting the filter result.
- **Some conclusions overstate what is established.** The executive summary labels CHG-506 among identity/data-quality problems while calling its account use unidentified, and calls CHG-504 “authorised subject to identity confidence” despite the principal’s VPN session creating a real absence-condition ambiguity. Those distinctions should be sharper.

The response is strong in breadth and control recommendations, but the business-day error and corresponding code defect affect a central case finding and prevent a higher grade.
