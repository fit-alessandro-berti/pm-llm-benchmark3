The response uses the required headings and tables and notices the key reversal: Orion’s crude mean is higher, while its mean is lower than Nova’s in each complexity stratum. It also recommends a cautious routing pilot and recognizes that completed-case selection limits inference.

However, several central claims and calculations are wrong or internally inconsistent. The executive section says Orion’s mean exceeds Nova’s within every stratum, then cites High complexity as 31.6h versus 35h, where Orion is lower. It labels complexity and shift as mediators of a group effect without establishing that they occur after assignment; complexity precedes routing, and shift may be a confounder, policy component, or effect modifier. Its DAG omits important links, including `case_completed` as a selection node, and the explanatory text describes arrows that are not actually drawn. It also does not discuss the required static-DAG caveat accurately.

The decomposition contains serious numerical and semantic errors: rework counts are confused with rework cases, appeal counts are incorrect, and queue/service summaries are unsupported or mislabeled. The hypotheses table repeats those errors and offers weak tests: comparing Orion cases with Nova-night cases of radically different complexity does not isolate a shift effect, and the compliance comparison is malformed.

Most decisively, Section 7 fails the requested diagnostic specification. The code does not compute a standardized contrast, does not produce valid overlap diagnostics, does not compare group-specific variants or directly-follows relations, and does not use PM4Py as requested. Section 8 has five items, but several proposed fields are less valuable than recording all open and completed cases with timestamps. These failures substantially undermine the answer’s auditability and causal discipline.
