4/10 — The response recognizes the core portfolio trade-off and makes a clear process-level case for addressing delays and rework rather than choosing registration on frequency alone. It covers all twelve candidates, offers three portfolio alternatives, discusses dependencies and controls, and considers resource demand and rollout constraints.

However, several recommendations rely on unsupported or contradictory claims. The bottleneck portfolio uses A4, A6, A7, and A5, but the rollout plan names A4 and A6 for integration and later adds A3 and A9, exceeding the four-module first-year limit if A5 is included. It also treats A4 as able to reduce a 31-hour wait substantially without evidence that an assistant can resolve the underlying entity issues, and claims A7 can cut an external wait despite that dependency being outside the bank’s control. The response invents precise cycle-time, error-rate, and labor improvements from qualitative data, and misstates resource conditions, such as describing back office as overloaded despite utilization falling below 65% after 15:00.

Control reasoning is incomplete: the selected portfolio does not strengthen controls as required for the first wave, and the answer understates the need to enforce KYC-before-signing and the four-eyes rule for high-exposure cases. Its candidate matrix and dependency discussion are useful, but the final recommendation and phased plan do not consistently honor the budget, integration, and control constraints.
