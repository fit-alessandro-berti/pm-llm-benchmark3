8/10 — The response gives a clear, evidence-based distinction between resilience and hidden risk, uses the incident phases and workflow counts to explain the trade-offs, and offers concrete controls for degraded operations, recovery, and backlog fairness. It correctly treats hospital prioritization, manual checks, temporary release controls, deferrals, and reconciliation as potentially resilient when governed, while identifying skipped authorization and dispatch without custody capture as unsafe. It also recognizes that service recovery does not equal full recovery while audit work remains open.

The main limitation is that the response appears to overstate some conclusions from aggregate data: the phase-level summaries do not establish which specific orders or product classes experienced each exception, and the DFG counts alone cannot prove that a control caused an outcome. It could also distinguish more explicitly between missing evidence and a confirmed compliance breach, and quantify proposed recovery thresholds.
