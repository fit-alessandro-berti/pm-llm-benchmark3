4/5 — The response’s strongest feature is its case-by-case distinction between genuine violations, justified exceptions, scope ambiguity, and logging uncertainty. It handles the dated PD-3 and FD-7 authority carefully, correctly treating SUP-703 as ineligible and SUP-705 as outside FD-7’s effective window despite the continuing emergency. It also recognizes that M0 cannot express the desirable information-request loop and that incomplete CRM mirroring makes some notification findings uncertain.

The proposed overlays, eligibility snapshots, immutable event times, actor relationships, and notification IDs provide a useful, traceable revision. The response also makes a serious effort to argue competing interpretations rather than treating every deviation as equivalent.

The main weakness is that M1 does not faithfully encode the policies it describes. Its emergency route places a `provisional_activate` before Quality_Review and manual approval, then also requires a separate `final_activate`; the source directive describes provisional activation before those activities, but does not establish that a second activation event is required. The model also reuses `sanctions` and `quality` transition objects across routes, which may make the intended route semantics ambiguous, and the “with RMI” branch uses an XOR between a single RMI and the checks, rather than clearly allowing the checks plus repeated RMI cycles. The response acknowledges some of these approximations, but the resulting model is still materially underspecified.

Overall, this is a well-evidenced and balanced evaluation with a strong data and authority analysis, but its proposed POWL revision needs clearer route semantics and closer alignment between the stated policy and the modeled behavior.
