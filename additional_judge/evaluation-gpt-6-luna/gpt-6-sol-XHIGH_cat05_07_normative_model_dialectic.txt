The answer is unusually thorough and balanced. It follows the required structure, covers all 15 cases, separates trace conformance from policy and evidence issues, and gives both sides credible arguments. It handles authority, expiry, scope ambiguity, identity, notification logging, and model limitations carefully. The revised architecture and policy table are also strong.

The main deductions are for the proposed PM4Py model:

- `standard_checks()` puts `Request_More_Info` alongside sanctions and quality with no ordering edges. That allows requests after the checks or even after the decision, rather than constraining them to an information-gathering phase.
- The standard rejection route requires both checks before rejection. The answer discusses abbreviated pre-screen rejection as a separate route, but its code’s `early_prescreen_rejection` still includes `Request_More_Info` without establishing whether that activity belongs before rejection.
- The model permits notification before quality review on the pilot route and closure waits for quality, but does not explicitly encode the pilot’s two-business-day deadline or distinguish provisional activation from final activation. Those constraints are described externally, yet the activity model could make these distinctions clearer.
- Several classifications are stated more firmly than the evidence fully supports. SUP-711 is labeled COMPLIANT based on a likely duplicate, though the answer itself says the duplicate should be verified. SUP-704 is labeled JUSTIFIED_EXCEPTION while issuer authority and cumulative spend remain unverified; that tension is acknowledged but could instead be reflected in a more conditional primary classification.
- The code is presented as executable, but it is not demonstrated or verified. Its PM4Py operator semantics and use of repeated labels across XOR branches therefore remain uncertain.

These are meaningful issues, especially in the required model proposal, but the answer’s careful evidence treatment and explicit limits keep them from outweighing its substantial strengths.
