7.0/10 — The answer is thorough, well structured, and mostly consistent with the supplied data. It separates the outage, recovery, case mix, staffing intervention, and weekday/weekend patterns; its rate and WIP calculations are generally sound; and it appropriately cautions that most Little’s Law ratios are descriptive rather than defensible flow-time estimates.

The main weaknesses are:

- It calls the average of daily medians and p90s “pooled” service quantiles. Those averages are not the corresponding case-level pooled quantiles.
- It labels the contrast between outage and recovery completion rates a “Simpson-like reversal.” That is heterogeneity hidden by averaging, not a Simpson’s paradox.
- The code prints regime summaries but does not explicitly calculate and print the pooled and weekday/weekend summaries presented in the prose.
- Some capacity interpretations are asserted without applying the stated WIP-per-FTE proxy. In particular, calling intervention weekends “low pressure” is not clearly supported by their WIP and complex-case levels.
- The intervention’s apparent effect is observational and confounded by the changing backlog and the unlogged start time; the answer acknowledges uncertainty but still gives the causal interpretation some weight.

These issues keep it below a high score, though the analysis is substantial and the central conclusions are mostly defensible.
