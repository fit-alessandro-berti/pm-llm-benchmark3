6.5/10 — The answer is broad and generally careful about confounding, treatment intensity, small-cluster uncertainty, and the distinction between median and tail performance. Its rollout recommendation and process-aware discussion are useful.

The main weakness is quantitative discipline. It contains arithmetic and labeling errors: the final ladder says Gamma’s use rose to “2532%,” and it describes roughly 1 pp escalation steps at go-live even though the data show larger jumps for Alpha and Beta (1.0 and 0.8 pp) but no increase for Gamma from June to July relative to the controls. More seriously, its matched-control comparisons and effect ranges are asserted without enough visible calculation or consistent windows to verify them. Some interpretations, such as calling rework changes “control-parallel,” go beyond what the displayed evidence establishes.

There are also design problems. The proposed two-branch rollout offers very weak randomization-based inference, yet its decision rule uses a p-value threshold without specifying a valid procedure for such a tiny number of clusters. The suggested six-month “pre-period” conflicts with the stated rollout dates, and its guardrails and treatment threshold are presented with little justification. The code provides useful descriptive analyses, but its event-study specification is not a robust modern staggered-adoption estimator, and it includes covariate adjustments that may be post-treatment. These issues prevent the answer’s conditional causal claims from being as well supported as its caveats imply.
