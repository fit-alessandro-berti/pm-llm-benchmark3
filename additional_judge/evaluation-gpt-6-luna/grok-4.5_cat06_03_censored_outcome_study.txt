**6.5/10**

The answer is thoughtful and strong on the central issue: it defines default only for activated accounts, treats the estimate as selection-conditional, rejects labeling non-activated applications as non-default, and gives a useful leakage audit and temporal validation plan. It addresses most required topics and the code is substantial.

Several problems materially weaken it:

- **The cohort flow does not fully account for the 48,000 cases.** Its categories are presented as if they partition the cohort, but “transferred (no activation on this ID)” can overlap with later activation, and “still open” may overlap with eventual activation after the cutoff. The stated counts imply 25,200 activated, but the answer does not reconcile transferred cases, relinked identities, and partner-originated activations into mutually exclusive, as-of categories.
- **The maturity rule can misclassify missing feeds.** Substituting `activation + 90 days + 35 days` for a missing `label_available_at` treats an assumed maximum delay as proof of maturity. The prompt says the feed commonly arrives 7–35 days later and partner feeds can be permanently missing. A case with no observed label after that date cannot safely be called matured without a documented completeness rule.
- **The primary estimand and prediction point are not fully aligned.** It proposes prefixes strictly before the decision, but describes prediction as an early warning for accounts that eventually activate. Requiring eventual activation selects on a future event relative to prediction. That can be a defensible retrospective conditional estimand, but the answer should distinguish it more carefully from the risk available for operational use among applications still in process at scoring time.
- **The missingness strategy is under-specified and partly misleading.** Sample weighting or “doubly weighting” partner channels does not by itself correct outcome-dependent permanent feed loss. Also, treating transfers as competing outcomes in one analysis and censoring them in another requires explicit definitions of the estimand and assumptions; 60% linkage does not justify assuming the unlinked cases are non-default.
- **The survival sensitivity is not specified correctly enough.** Censoring at the earlier of extraction and 90 days gives no event-time analysis beyond the horizon and does not explain how delayed feeds affect event observation. It also does not clarify that the binary 90-day endpoint is fixed-horizon while survival methods would model time to default.
- **The code has consequential implementation gaps.** It uses `max()` to collapse repeated `default_90d` values, which can hide conflicting labels; takes the first observed label-availability timestamp without checking consistency; and its prefix routine truncates to the first \(k\) events but does not enforce a prediction timestamp or ensure those events precede a terminal/decision event. It also builds case timelines from the supplied event names without applying the activity map to all relevant terminal and activation names, and the temporal splits do not enforce fold-specific label availability/maturity. The cohort report’s counts are nested totals, not a mutually exclusive flow accounting for all cases.

The answer’s conceptual framing is better than its operational details. The unresolved cohort accounting, maturity assumptions, and code behavior make the proposed study less reliable than its polished presentation suggests.
