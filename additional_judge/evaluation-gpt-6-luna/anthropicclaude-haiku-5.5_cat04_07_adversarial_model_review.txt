# 1. Review Scope and Assumptions

The intended use matters. For **exploration**, a permissive model can help reveal patterns, provided it is clearly labeled as non-normative. For **conformance enforcement**, mandatory checks, approval, and release order must be enforced; false acceptance of forbidden traces is a critical failure. For **simulation**, the model also needs plausible frequencies and choices, which neither the reported metrics nor these expressions establish. For **stakeholder communication**, the model must be understandable and faithfully express policy.

A high fitness score can be useful for exploration, but it does not compensate for accepting forbidden behavior in enforcement. Precision matters too, but precision on observed positive traces does not establish that the model rejects policy violations. The current policy is incomplete about external sourcing and which checks a correction affects. Those decisions need explicit attributes or human review, not silent assumptions.

# 3. Adversarial Review of Model S

**Strongest case for S.** It is compact and reportedly easy to communicate. Its high holdout fitness and generalization may be useful for exploratory alignment when event logging is incomplete. Its optional Supplier Review avoids rejecting internal orders, although the model has no attribute that distinguishes internal from external orders.

**Strongest case against S.** The expression makes every listed activity optional and unordered within the parallel block. Each `X(activity, tau)` allows either that activity or silence; `+` permits the chosen activities to interleave. Thus it accidentally weakens mandatory checks and ordering rules. This is a semantic flaw, not a principled way to tolerate logging noise. A missing event should be flagged as uncertain or as a logging defect, not converted into policy permission.

Examples S admits:

- `Register Dossier, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips both mandatory checks.
- `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Credit Check, Technical Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — expedite precedes the checks.
- `Register Dossier, Credit Check, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips Technical Review.
- `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` — skips Credit Check.
- `Register Dossier, Supplier Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — skips both checks.
- `Register Dossier, Expedite Review, Auto Approval, Release Order` — skips both checks and expedites before approval.
- `Register Dossier, Technical Review, Credit Check, Supplier Review, Expedite Review, Manager Approval, Release Order` — valid ordering if external, but S cannot tell whether Supplier Review was required.

S also has no Correct Dossier activity and cannot distinguish a suspicious repeated check from an appropriate recheck after correction. Repeated activities are not in its ordinary one-occurrence expression, though alignment diagnostics may still assign costs to extra log events.

# 4. Adversarial Review of Model C

**Strongest case for C.** Each branch states an explicit finite trace, so its admitted variants are easy to enumerate. Most branches include both mandatory checks before approval, and it does not admit B3 or B4. High training precision is consistent with a narrow language that closely matches observed variants.

**Strongest case against C.** It enumerates variants rather than expressing the reusable policy that generates them. It includes only Manager Approval in most branches, permits Auto Approval only in one specific check order without Supplier Review, and includes a correction branch that repeats both checks. It omits valid combinations such as B2 and B5. This creates brittle false rejections as combinations change.

The eight branches do not capture Supplier Review’s conditional requirement because the source attribute is absent. They also cannot represent correction-dependent rechecking of only Credit Check, only Technical Review, or both. The holdout fall from 0.973 to 0.744 is consistent with memorization, split sensitivity, or a shifted holdout mix; it does not establish which explanation is correct. The 0.941 training precision may be an artifact of enumerating observed positive variants, particularly since there was no curated negative suite.

The duplicate-label count of 33, if accurate under a useful definition, suggests repeated activity labels across branch-specific transitions. Such duplication can make ownership, change impact, and diagnostics harder. The reported simplicity and runtime also raise maintainability and operational concerns. Conversely, C’s narrowness may be appropriate for a tightly controlled, explicitly approved workflow—but only if the business actually intends to reject valid unseen combinations pending review. F8 is a governance choice, not a conclusion established by low fitness.

# 6. Likely Metric Pathologies

Ranked by decision impact:

1. **Fitness rewarded by optionalizing mandatory behavior.** S can skip both required checks and still fit many incomplete traces. This makes its high fitness especially weak evidence for enforcement.
2. **Positive-log-only precision.** A model can be precise relative to observed positives while accepting forbidden traces. Without F5’s negative suite, neither precision value measures policy discrimination.
3. **Missing-event bias.** If mandatory activities or Supplier Review are absent from logs, observed traces can make omission look normal. F4 makes this a live concern. A model should surface missingness rather than infer that the omitted activity is optional.
4. **Train/holdout leakage or split sensitivity.** No seed, held-out cases, or preprocessing are available. Case-level splits may also place near-duplicate variants in both sets. C’s degradation could reflect a changed mix of combinations rather than a general property of the model.
5. **Generalization rewarded by excessive permissiveness.** S’s 0.907 may reward broad acceptance. Generalization is not a safety metric.
6. **Alignment-cost sensitivity.** Costs determine how alignment fitness penalizes skipped model activities versus extra log events. Without settings, a permissive model may appear particularly fit under costs that make model moves expensive.
7. **Duplicated-label effects.** Duplicate transitions can change net size, precision, simplicity, and alignment behavior, depending on conversion and metric implementation. The undocumented count prevents interpretation.
8. **Silent-transition effects.** `tau` branches in S can change the converted net and alignment search. Silent transitions can also affect reachability and simplicity calculations. Their treatment must be reported.
9. **Runtime confounding by model size.** C’s 0.440 s versus S’s 0.018 s may reflect net size, case length, search settings, or hardware, not just the modeling approach.
10. **Stakeholder-score methodology.** An informal 1–5 score lacks a defined task or sample. Ease of reading is not the same as correctly identifying forbidden behavior.

# 7. Third Alternative

Use a small procedural backbone for policy-invariant control flow, plus explicit, auditable checks for attributes and correction decisions. Do not treat missing attributes as permission to omit required steps.

**Compact formal description:** Register first; perform Credit Check and Technical Review in either order; optionally perform Supplier Review only when the order is external; optionally perform Expedite Review after both checks and before approval; perform exactly one approval; release last. A correction starts a new decision episode in which only the checks marked affected must be repeated before approval. If external/internal or affected-check data is missing, the trace is **indeterminate and routed for review**, not accepted as compliant.

The PM4Py process tree below captures the attribute-independent backbone. Supplier Review and correction are deliberately checked outside this tree because the required attributes are missing. The tree expresses the mandatory control flow and exactly-one approval.

```python
from pm4py.objects.process_tree.obj import ProcessTree, Operator
import pm4py

def leaf(label):
    return ProcessTree(label=label)

def seq(*children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.SEQUENCE)
    node.children = list(children)
    return node

def xor(*children):
    node = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.XOR)
    node.children = list(children)
    return node

def optional(label):
    return xor(leaf(label), ProcessTree())

# Parallel checks allow either order; their internal concurrency is a
# modeling convenience. Confirm both orders and no omission on the probe suite.
checks = ProcessTree(operator=Operator.PARALLEL)
checks.children = [leaf("Credit Check"), leaf("Technical Review")]

approval = xor(leaf("Manager Approval"), leaf("Auto Approval"))

model_third = seq(
    leaf("Register Dossier"),
    checks,
    optional("Expedite Review"),
    approval,
    leaf("Release Order"),
)

net_third, im_third, fm_third = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model_third)
```

External policy checks should operate on case attributes and event sequence, independent of whether the net can align the trace:

```python
def policy_findings(events, attrs):
    """Return findings; do not silently classify missing policy data as compliant."""
    labels = [e["concept:name"] for e in events]
    findings = []

    if not labels or labels[0] != "Register Dossier":
        findings.append("invalid_or_missing_start")
    if not labels or labels[-1] != "Release Order":
        findings.append("invalid_or_missing_end")

    approval_labels = {"Manager Approval", "Auto Approval"}
    approvals = [x for x in labels if x in approval_labels]
    if len(approvals) != 1:
        findings.append("exactly_one_approval_required")

    positions = {name: [i for i, x in enumerate(labels) if x == name]
                 for name in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review",
                              "Supplier Review", "Expedite Review",
                              "Correct Dossier")}

    checks = positions["Credit Check"] + positions["Technical Review"]
    if not positions["Credit Check"] or not positions["Technical Review"]:
        findings.append("mandatory_check_missing")

    approval_pos = min((i for i, x in enumerate(labels) if x in approval_labels),
                       default=None)
    if approval_pos is not None and checks and any(i > approval_pos for i in checks):
        findings.append("check_after_approval")

    expedite_pos = positions["Expedite Review"]
    if expedite_pos and (
        not positions["Credit Check"] or not positions["Technical Review"]
        or max(positions["Credit Check"] + positions["Technical Review"])
           > expedite_pos[0]
        or (approval_pos is not None and expedite_pos[0] > approval_pos)
    ):
        findings.append("expedite_order_violation")

    external = attrs.get("external")
    if external is None:
        findings.append("indeterminate_external_attribute")
    elif external and not positions["Supplier Review"]:
        findings.append("supplier_review_missing")
    elif not external and positions["Supplier Review"]:
        findings.append("supplier_review_unexpected_or_review")

    affected = attrs.get("affected_checks_by_correction")
    corrections = positions["Correct Dossier"]
    if corrections and affected is None:
        findings.append("indeterminate_correction_scope")
    elif corrections:
        # The production implementation must define correction episodes and
        # map each correction to its affected checks and required rechecks.
        findings.extend(validate_correction_episodes(labels, affected))

    for check in ("Credit Check", "Technical Review"):
        if len(positions[check]) > 1 and not corrections:
            findings.append(f"suspicious_repeat_without_correction:{check}")

    return findings
```

`validate_correction_episodes` is an application-specific rule requiring a defined mapping from each correction to its affected checks. It must ensure affected checks repeat after the correction and avoid requiring unaffected checks. That mapping cannot be inferred from this control-flow log.

Limitations: a parallel block can represent concurrency, not necessarily the business meaning of sequential completion in either order; verify the converted language against B1/B2 and additional probes. The tree does not model Supplier Review or correction episodes. Those require reliable attributes and a validated policy layer. Missing attributes yield indeterminate outcomes.

# 9. Validation Experiments

Run the comparison on versioned source data and code. Publish case identifiers or a privacy-safe manifest, preprocessing, PM4Py version, model definitions, parameters, and seeds. Split by case, not event, and test alternate seeds. Stratify holdouts by external/internal status, approval type, check order, correction, and Supplier Review presence when those attributes exist.

Create owner-labeled positive and negative suites before evaluating models. Include B1, B2, B5, B7 and valid Supplier Review/Expedite/Auto Approval combinations. Negatives must include missing mandatory checks, early expedite, repeated checks without correction, no or two approvals, correction without required recheck, and expedite after approval. Report acceptance by class and scenario.

Generate drop, duplicate, and adjacent-swap perturbations on both positives and negatives. Separately drop Supplier Review to simulate F4’s missing events. Report false acceptance and false rejection by perturbation type. Test valid unseen combinations such as alternate check order plus Supplier Review, Expedite Review, and Auto Approval. Do not call them valid solely because the model accepts them; use process-owner labels.

Test unit, policy-weighted, and asymmetric alignment costs. Report fitness and negative-suite alignment cost separately. Check soundness, transition reachability using state-space analysis, silent transitions, duplicate labels, and probe-language behavior after conversion. Report fitness, precision, generalization, simplicity, runtime, and negative acceptance separately. Runtime tests should control hardware, case lengths, repetitions, and warm-up.

Example skeletons (API details should be checked against the pinned PM4Py version):

```python
import random
import pm4py

def evaluate(model, train_log, test_log, negative_log):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    return {
        "fitness_train": pm4py.fitness_alignments(train_log, net, im, fm),
        "fitness_test": pm4py.fitness_alignments(test_log, net, im, fm),
        "precision_test": pm4py.precision_alignments(test_log, net, im, fm),
        "generalization_train": pm4py.generalization_tbr(train_log, net, im, fm),
        "simplicity": pm4py.simplicity_petri_net(net, im, fm, variant="arc_degree"),
        "negative_diagnostics": pm4py.conformance_diagnostics_alignments(
            negative_log, net, im, fm
        ),
    }

def split_cases(cases, seed):
    # Stratify by policy-relevant attributes before sampling in production.
    rng = random.Random(seed)
    ids = list(cases)
    rng.shuffle(ids)
    cut = int(0.8 * len(ids))
    return ids[:cut], ids[cut:]

def negative_acceptance(diagnostics):
    accepted = [d.get("fitness", 0) >= 1.0 - 1e-9 for d in diagnostics]
    return sum(accepted) / max(1, len(accepted))

def soundness_report(model):
    net, im, fm = pm4py.convert_to_petri_net(model)
    return pm4py.check_soundness(net, im, fm, print_diagnostics=True)
```

Do not treat a sampled play-out as proof that every transition is reachable; use state-space exploration. For stakeholder comprehension, counterbalance model presentation and recruit at least three participants from each of process ownership, compliance, operations, and analysis. Use a pre-registered set of 20 policy questions; measure accuracy, response time, confidence, and “cannot answer” rate, with error types and confidence intervals.

Reject a candidate regardless of aggregate metrics if it accepts any agreed critical negative trace, exceeds the agreed false-rejection threshold on labeled positives, fails soundness, has unstable results across seeds, or falls below the pre-registered comprehension threshold for enforcement users. A proposed starting threshold is zero acceptance for critical negatives and at most 5% false rejection on positives, subject to governance approval.

# 10. Governance Recommendation

Keep Model S only as a clearly labeled exploratory artifact, if it remains useful for investigating broad event sequences. Do not present it as a policy description or use it for compliance enforcement: it accepts traces that skip mandatory checks and violate expedite ordering.

Retire Model C as a deployable policy model. Its narrow branch enumeration rejects valid combinations, does not encode conditional Supplier Review, and does not transparently handle correction scope. It may remain as a historical discovery artifact with provenance.

Advance the third alternative to validation, not deployment. Before deployment, require reliable external/internal and affected-check attributes; approved positive and negative suites; reproducible, stratified evaluation; soundness and probe checks; acceptable false-accept and false-reject rates; and documented stakeholder comprehension. Until the missing attributes are available, affected cases must be flagged as indeterminate for review.

# 11. Dissenting View

The recommendation may be too cautious about Model C. In a regulated operation, conservative rejection can be safer than accepting a trace that policy owners have not explicitly approved. C’s high training precision and explicit finite branches could be read as an auditable whitelist: only variants already observed and reviewed are accepted. The low holdout score may therefore expose a genuine control boundary rather than overfitting. Likewise, Model S’s strong fitness and comprehension scores may make it a practical exploratory tool while a formal policy model is being built. The proposed third alternative adds a second policy layer and relies on attributes that are currently absent; this creates integration risk and can produce many indeterminate cases. A simpler, strictly enumerated model might be easier to govern in the short term if every new variant must pass review before use. The board could retain C provisionally for narrowly scoped workflows, with manual escalation for rejected traces, while collecting evidence about the operational cost of false rejection and the prevalence of unmodeled combinations. This position would change if C is shown to reject policy-valid cases at material rates or if its branch ownership and change process prove unreliable.

I would change the recommendation if a reproducible, attribute-stratified study showed that C’s rejected cases are truly noncompliant, that its branches cover all approved behavior, and that its complexity is maintainable under documented change control. I would also reconsider S for limited exploration if users reliably understand that its permissiveness is not policy approval. For the third alternative, reliable attributes, validated correction rules, low false acceptance on curated negatives, and a controlled comprehension study would support deployment.
