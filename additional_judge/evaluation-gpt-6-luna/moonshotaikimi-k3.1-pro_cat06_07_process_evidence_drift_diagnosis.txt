## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January–March:** Stable reference period. Metrics, data-quality indicators, and variant shares are broadly consistent. These months are the cleanest available baseline, subject to confirming consistent feature lineage and evaluation protocol.
- **April:** A distinct pipeline failure. The diagnosis rename disrupted prediction prefixes; 18% of variants are unmapped, unknown activity is 17%, and queue-feature missingness is 14%. Poorer AUROC and calibration are therefore not reliable evidence of underlying concept drift. Labels are mature, so replay can directly test the pipeline explanation.
- **May:** Partial recovery after the mapping patch. Remaining metric and data-quality gaps may reflect incomplete April backfill, the cached event-count feature, and inconsistent duplicate removal. Recompute before treating May as a clean post-migration month.
- **June:** A process and covariate shift begins with remote routing: V2 rises to 52%. Aggregate breach rate falls to 0.15, while remote-fix time remains stable. This is not evidence of global model failure; compare performance within variants and assess whether the changed mix explains aggregate metrics.
- **July:** Supplier delays begin to show in the part edge, and matured V3 breaches rise to 0.46. Labels are nearly mature, making this stronger evidence of real process-related deterioration than October’s aggregate metrics. Investigate V3 conditional risk and whether the model has prediction-time signals for supplier exposure.
- **August:** Supplier disruption becomes severe. Part-wait median reaches 130 hours and matured V3 breach rate reaches 0.64. AUROC and calibration worsen while the aggregate mean predicted risk stays near 0.19 against an observed rate of 0.28. This supports material underprediction in affected cases, though path mix and conditional change still need separation.
- **September:** Several effects overlap: Partner Z enters, V3 rises to 42%, and supplier delays persist. Partner Z is more rural and more V3-heavy, and its labels mature more slowly. The observed 0.31 aggregate breach rate and 0.45 for Partner Z are provisional. Separate source, variant, and maturity effects before attributing the entire change to the model.
- **October:** Do not interpret the observed 0.16 breach rate as recovery. Only 55% of labels are mature, delays are longest, and the API backlog disproportionately hides outcomes on long paths. AUROC and calibration computed on the available-label subset may be selection-biased. Use process evidence and data-quality monitoring now; defer outcome conclusions until fixed-vintage cohorts mature.

April, June, August–September, and October therefore represent different failure mechanisms: pipeline breakage, routing and mix change, real supplier-related process deterioration with possible localized conditional drift, and substantial label censoring.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

1. **Immediate containment:** Keep the current model in shadow or assisted use only where diagnosis-prefix and feature-quality checks pass. For April, quarantine records whose prefixes or cached features cannot be reconstructed. Do not use October’s available-label metrics to make a model-performance decision. Escalate the supplier delay as an operational issue independent of model retraining.
2. **Short-term recalibration:** Consider only after pipeline repair and maturity checks. If within-variant discrimination is stable but mature predicted risks are systematically too low or high, recalibrate on a recent, representative, mature cohort and validate forward in time. Do not calibrate on unrepaired April data or immature September–October labels.
3. **Feature repair:** Recompute April–May features; standardize activity normalization and duplicate handling; verify prediction-time availability and lineage. Assess whether rural distance, device age, supplier exposure, and queue information are available at prediction time and reliable across sources.
4. **Full retraining:** Proceed only if repaired, mature data show persistent within-path conditional or discrimination changes that recalibration cannot fix. Include the supplier-disruption regime if the model must operate through it, and validate after process recovery to avoid overfitting a temporary outage.

January–March are the cleanest training candidates. April is unsafe until replayed and audited. May is provisional until cached features and duplicates are repaired. June is usable only with variant-aware evaluation because routing changed. July and August have valuable mature or near-mature evidence, but must be treated as a distinct supplier regime and checked for label maturity. September is unsafe for ordinary supervised training until labels mature by source and path. October is unsafe for outcome-based training now.

A single global model remains the default until stratified results justify otherwise. Variant-specific calibration may be justified if persistent calibration differences remain after maturity, data repair, and mix adjustment. A separate model needs enough mature examples and stable operational ownership; Partner Z alone does not yet justify one. Reweighting or adding validated prediction-time features may be preferable if the issue is covariate shift.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

- **Predictive:** AUROC, precision/recall at operational thresholds, and score distributions on mature fixed-vintage cohorts; show confidence intervals and counts.
- **Calibration:** Brier score, reliability curves, and ECE overall and by variant, source, and month.
- **Label maturity:** Maturity curves, delay distributions, and matured-case proportions by source and path. Mark immature metrics provisional.
- **Data quality:** Activity vocabulary, unknown labels, duplicate rates by source, missingness, event ordering, prefix construction, and feature lineage.
- **Process variants:** Variant frequencies, unmapped paths, and transitions by event-calendar and case-start cohort.
- **Performance-DFG:** Edge duration distributions, especially `Order Part -> Part Received`, plus diagnosis-to-remote-fix. Alert on sustained shifts with volume and uncertainty.
- **Population:** Source, rural distance, device age, variant, and covariate distributions; monitor overlap and reweighting stability.
- **Intervention and deployment:** Model version, thresholds, routing, overrides, action rates, and intervention outcomes. Changes in policy or capacity should be recorded alongside score and outcome trends.

Alert dependencies:

1. A label-delay alarm marks predictive metrics provisional and blocks performance-triggered retraining; it does not suppress process, data-quality, or score-distribution monitoring.
2. A data-quality alarm triggers pipeline investigation and replay before recalibration or retraining.
3. A process-performance alarm can prompt supplier or operations action without waiting for labels, but does not itself prove concept drift.
4. A mix alarm with stable within-stratum performance leads to routing, resource, or threshold review.
5. Persistent mature within-stratum calibration error supports recalibration review; persistent conditional or discrimination change supports retraining review.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

def monthly_process_audit(path="field_service_events.csv"):
    raw = pd.read_csv(path)
    raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
        "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed",
    })
    raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)

    # Remove exact duplicate rows only. Prefer a verified source event ID in production;
    # quarantine conflicting IDs and do not deduplicate on case/activity alone.
    duplicate = raw.duplicated(keep="first")
    clean = raw.loc[~duplicate].copy()

    df = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        clean, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity",
        timestamp_key="timestamp"
    )
    C, A, T = "case:concept:name", "concept:name", "time:timestamp"
    df = df.sort_values([C, T])
    case_start = df.groupby(C)[T].min()  # Use Case Created time if available.
    rows = []

    def summarize(month, cohort, frame):
        if frame.empty:
            return {"month": month, "cohort": cohort, "cases": 0, "events": 0}
        log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(frame)
        dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
        perf, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
        variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
        temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(log)
        part_edge = perf.get(("Order Part", "Part Received"))

        return {
            "month": month, "cohort": cohort,
            "cases": frame[C].nunique(), "events": len(frame),
            "unknown_activity_share": frame[A].eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean(),
            "dfg_edges": len(dfg), "part_edge_performance": part_edge,
            "variant_counts": {
                str(v): (n if isinstance(n, int) else len(n))
                for v, n in variants.items()
            },
            "temporal_feature_rows": len(temporal),
        }

    for month in sorted(df[T].dt.strftime("%Y-%m").unique()):
        lo = pd.Timestamp(month + "-01", tz="UTC")
        hi = lo + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)

        # Calendar cohort: events occurring in this month; traces may be truncated.
        calendar = pm4py.filter_time_range(
            df, lo.isoformat(), hi.isoformat(), mode="events"
        )
        rows.append(summarize(month, "event_calendar", calendar))

        # Start cohort: all observed events for cases starting this month.
        # Recent traces are right-truncated at the extraction cutoff.
        ids = case_start.index[(case_start >= lo) & (case_start < hi)]
        started = df[df[C].isin(ids)]
        rows.append(summarize(month, "case_start_to_cutoff", started))

    result = pd.DataFrame(rows)
    result.attrs["raw_rows"] = len(raw)
    result.attrs["exact_duplicate_rows_removed"] = int(duplicate.sum())
    result.attrs["diagnosis_label_mapping"] = "Diagnostic Assessment Completed -> Initial Diagnosis Completed"
    return result
```

The event-calendar cohort describes activity during the month and may include partial traces. The case-start cohort groups cases by their start month and includes observed later events; recent cases are right-truncated. Neither substitutes for mature, fixed-vintage outcome evaluation.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Training on immature September–October labels | Missing adverse outcomes can make long V3 or Partner Z cases appear safe and teach the model to underpredict them. October’s low observed rate could be mistaken for recovery. | Gate training on fixed-vintage, source- and path-specific maturity. Preserve outcome timestamps and validate censoring sensitivity methods before use. |
| Calibrating around April’s pipeline bug | Calibration could compensate for invalid prefixes and become harmful after repair, while hiding recurring schema failures. | Replay and repair first; compare repaired and original scores; require data-quality gates and rollback. |
| Treating June’s routing mix shift as concept drift | Global retraining could overfit a remote-heavy month and worsen other variants without fixing a conditional relationship. | Standardize by variant; use within-stratum metrics; adjust resource planning or thresholds if relationships remain stable. |
| Dismissing August–September as only mix shift or label delay | Matured V3 deterioration and long part waits would be overlooked, prolonging supplier-related customer harm and underprediction. | Escalate supplier remediation immediately; evaluate mature V3 outcomes and conditional calibration after data validation. |
| Attributing Partner Z’s observed rate to model bias—or dismissing a real transport problem as mix alone | Either conclusion may be wrong while labels mature slowly; rural customers could be underserved or unjustifiably segregated by source-specific thresholds. | Compare mature outcomes within source-by-variant strata; reweight on observed covariates, check overlap, and review intervention access and outcomes. |
| Retraining on a temporary supplier outage or using future process information | The model may fail after supplier recovery, or appear strong in backtests that leak post-prediction information. | Use only prediction-time features, validate forward across normal and disrupted regimes, and reassess after process interventions. |
