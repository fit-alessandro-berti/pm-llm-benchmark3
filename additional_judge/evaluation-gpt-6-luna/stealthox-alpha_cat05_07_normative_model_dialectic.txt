4/10 — The response shows a strong grasp of the cases and proposes useful controls, but the model and overlay are not fully faithful to the supplied rules.

It handles key distinctions well: SUP-703 is an ineligible pilot use, SUP-704 is covered by FD-7, SUP-705 is outside the directive’s effective window, SUP-706 is uncertain because the CRM link is mutable, and SUP-707 reflects desirable practice that M0 cannot express. It also recognizes that scope is disputed for rejected leads and reactivation, and that M0 omits the requester/approver separation rule.

The proposed M1 has material semantic problems. It allows the standard route’s manual approval to occur after both checks, whereas CS-9 requires checks before activation but does not establish that ordering. More seriously, its `LOOP` with a silent do-part and `Request_More_Info` as redo does not clearly model zero-or-more requests at the claimed insertion point; the response itself acknowledges approximation but still presents it as a repeatable pre-routing cycle. It also puts rejection in the same route structure as onboarding without resolving the disputed scope.

The overlay overreaches its authority. It treats a proposed amendment as a computable rule, assumes “PD-3 successor” and “FD-class” regimes without approved sources, and adds controls such as conflict-of-interest disclosure and signed notification waivers that were not supplied. The tabular notation also contains missing comparison operators in several rules, making some statements ambiguous. The event-data proposal is thoughtful, especially immutable timestamps, identifiers, and deduplication, but it cannot make unsupported policy judgments reproducible merely by logging them.

A stronger evaluation would separate current binding rules, time-limited exceptions, proposed policy changes, and unresolved scope decisions; state the cases’ conformance conclusions under each; and describe a revision that preserves M0’s supported requirements while leaving unauthorized or disputed changes outside the normative model.
