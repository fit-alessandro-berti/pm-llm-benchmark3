The response follows the requested structure and makes a defensible recommendation: hold national rollout, run a controlled experiment, and prioritize measurement. It recognizes the conflict between faster median times and worse tail duration, rework, complaints, and activation outcomes, and it discusses the operational trade-off in coordinator ownership.

The evidence table is incomplete and sometimes inaccurate. It presents only a few facts, omits several important figures and caveats, and treats some interpretations as established facts—for example, claiming model precision is higher for the coordinator segment when pilot precision is reported as low. The metric-conflict discussion also misstates or muddles some evidence, including handover and cost findings.

The value-of-information section ranks measurements but gives limited justification for why that order would change the decision. It also confuses M2 and M3 in one entry and overlooks the importance of measuring downstream cost despite the known cost-data gap. The final recommendation’s thresholds, such as coordinator utilization below 85%, are invented without a rationale. Overall, it is directionally sound but falls short on evidence discipline and decision-specific measurement priorities.
