**5.5/10**

The answer follows the six-section format and shows sound instincts on stage eligibility, stale versus evidence-changing alerts, reserve capacity, and spillovers. It is substantially weakened by inconsistent arithmetic and a prioritization method that does not reliably support its chosen cases.

- **Arithmetic errors undermine the impact table.** For I-04, I-08, and I-02, the midpoint effects are 0.19, 0.10, and 0.15, respectively. That gives about **0.44 avoided breaches and €25.15k in direct benefit**, before costs. The table’s optimistic effects of 0.24, 0.125, and 0.19 are not the upper halves of the supplied ranges; the stated direct-benefit sum is also about €31.3k, not €31k exactly. More seriously, the adverse case states €8k gross benefit against €8.95k intervention costs and €9k spillover harm, which is a **€9.95k loss**, not €9.9k, and it omits the additional 15–30 minute queue delay as a separate operational consequence. The adverse scenario also assumes only I-08 is a dud without explaining why that satisfies the scenario’s stated risk of at least one mistaken alert.
- **The spillover charge is too blunt.** It applies the same €1,800 expected harm to each candidate, but the spillover affects untreated diagnosis-stage cases, and its cost depends on which cases are untreated, their exposure, and congestion. The answer does not reconcile that per-slot charge with the selected portfolio’s scenario-specific spillover harms.
- **The allocation is not auditable from the rule.** The listed tiers conflict: I-02 and I-04 are selected, while I-07 is tier 3 and I-09 tier 4, yet both are passed over without a calculated score showing why. I-07’s “low € value” rationale also ignores its substantial estimated treatment effect and the new log. The answer applies a blanket 0.7 discount for uncertainty without showing how it affects the actual ranking or distinguishing uncertainty in the estimate from lower expected effect.
- **Calibration adjustments are oversimplified.** Fixed segment offsets using the midpoint of the stated error ranges imply precision the evidence does not support. They can also produce invalid probabilities near 0 or 1. The answer does not clearly explain how adjusted probabilities interact with the supplied intervention-effect ranges, which are uncertain and not necessarily corrected by probability calibration.
- **The temporal policy contains arbitrary or inconsistent rules.** “Any feature changed by 10% relative weight” is not operationally defined, and the two-hour cooldown, escalation after two suppressed alerts, and re-entry after eight hours do not fit together clearly. The answer says I-07’s new log passes the filter, but its table still says “Wait” without a clear reason tied to today’s capacity decision.
- **Fairness safeguards are underdeveloped.** A 20%–40% capacity budget is proposed without a basis, and the exception for negative expected value could nullify it. Capping a segment’s “spill-over risk score” is vague and does not explain how to protect untreated cases while allocating shared queue capacity. Randomized holdouts are a useful idea, but the answer does not address ethical or operational limits for critical cases, or how to account for intervention assignment and spillovers in effect estimates.

Overall, this is a coherent draft policy with several relevant safeguards, but the scoring, arithmetic, and allocation logic are not consistent enough to support the specific portfolio with confidence.
