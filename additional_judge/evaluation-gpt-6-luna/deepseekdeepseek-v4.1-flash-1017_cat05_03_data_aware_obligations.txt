The response follows the requested seven-section structure and gives a substantial event-time analysis, rule formalization, case table, executable-looking pandas/PM4Py code, and practical discussion of why control-flow-only conformance is insufficient. It correctly handles several central traps, including TX-301’s post-review increase, TX-302’s late database insertion backed by a qualified signature, TX-309’s second increase after enhanced review, and TX-311’s event-index ordering.

The main weakness is inconsistency between the written findings and the code. The case analysis also leaves some required uncertainty distinctions underdeveloped. TX-305’s settlement amount is correctly not copied backward, but the response should make clearer whether the case is indeterminate because the amount at release is unknown, and how an immutable release payload would change that finding. TX-310’s contradictory evidence merits a more explicit explanation of why the permitted classification is indeterminate versus noncompliant under the stated evidence standard. Overall, this is a strong and useful answer, but the code and a few case classifications do not reliably match the formal rules.
