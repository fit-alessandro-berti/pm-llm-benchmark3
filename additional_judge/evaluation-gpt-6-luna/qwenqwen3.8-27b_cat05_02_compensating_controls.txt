## 1. Control-Objective Decomposition

| Objective or threat scenario | AV-2 | MG-4 | RA-7 | ED-1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stop one operator from issuing an erroneous or fraudulent payment | Prevents covered defects before release; cannot establish receipt or commercial reasonableness | Prevents settlement when an independent manager reviews the supporting evidence in time | Detects after release; may stop or recover a payment only while action remains possible | Permits a single operator only with all stated safeguards; does not remove the objective |
| Detect duplicate invoices, sanctions matches, bank changes, ownership mismatches, limit breaches, or basic PO mismatches | Preventive for a block; a warning is weaker if it can be overridden | Can provide a separate review, especially of bank details and invoice support | May detect missed or overridden defects later | Requires a full, unoverridden AV-2 pass |
| Establish that goods or services were received and the payment is commercially justified | Does not establish either | Can assess supporting receipt evidence if actually reviewed | Can find defects retrospectively, subject to audit scope and timing | Depends on manager review and audit; does not independently establish receipt |
| Preserve independence and resist collusion or credential sharing | Does not address human collusion or who controlled a compromised account | Helps only if the manager is independent, conflict-free, and reviews evidence | Can identify suspicious patterns or defects, but cannot itself establish collusion | Requires an independent manager; does not make a single operator independent |
| Limit loss after a bad release | Can block before release, but cannot recall a settled payment | Can stop settlement if approval occurs before the irrevocable point and the manager can hold the payment | Can support a stop, recall, or recovery while those remain possible | Requires a timely audit; it is not a substitute for timely prevention |
| Respond safely to emergencies | Does not justify a same-user release by itself | Provides a separate decision point | Requires 100% audit within one business day for emergency same-user payments | Sets the conditions under which the single-operator exception is permitted |

## 2. Equivalence and Sufficiency Test

A compensating package is **fully equivalent** when it preserves the relevant control objective for the case: it blocks or independently challenges material defects before the payment becomes irrevocable, covers the case’s actual risks, relies on independent actors and reliable evidence, cannot be bypassed without effective review, and leaves a realistic recovery path for defects that escape prevention. It should also resist the relevant collusion or credential-sharing scenario.

A package is **partially equivalent** when it materially reduces risk but leaves a meaningful gap—for example, an independent review occurs before settlement but after release, or automated checks cover bank data but not receipt evidence.

It is **merely mitigating** when it lowers potential loss or helps identify a problem but does not reliably stop the payment: examples include a late audit after recall is uncertain, a warning that the operator overrides, or a manager ratification after settlement.

It is **ineffective** for the relevant threat when coverage is missing, independence is compromised, the control can be bypassed without review, or detection arrives after practical recovery has ended.

Apply the test in this order:

1. **Timing:** Did the control act before release, before settlement, or only afterward? Define the point at which the payment becomes irrevocable.
2. **Independence:** Was the reviewer separate from the preparer and releaser, sufficiently senior, and free of unresolved conflicts? Are the actors actually independent in practice?
3. **Coverage:** Did the control address the specific threat? A clean AV-2 pass does not establish receipt, commercial reasonableness, or absence of collusion.
4. **Overrideability:** Was a warning overridden, and by whom? Was the override independently reviewed before settlement?
5. **Recoverability:** Could the payment still be held, recalled, or recovered when the defect was found?
6. **Evidence quality:** Are approvals, credentials, bank ownership, receipts, and timing supported by reliable records?
7. **Collusion resistance:** Could one person or a cooperating pair defeat the control through shared credentials, repeated pairing, or coordinated fabrication?

“Same expected loss” is not enough to establish equivalence. The estimate may be unreliable, and equal average loss can conceal very different tail exposure, detection delay, recoverability, and accountability. The control objective also concerns independent authorization and credible evidence, not only a modeled financial outcome.

## 4. Preventive–Detective–Corrective Comparison

| Control | Preventive contribution | Detective contribution | Corrective contribution | Key limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FEP-1, distinct preparer and releaser | Prevents a single person from both preparing and releasing, assuming genuine independence | None by itself | None by itself | Distinct resource IDs do not prove actual independence or rule out collusion |
| AV-2 | Blocks covered defects before release; a warning is weaker if overridden | A warning can reveal a covered risk to an authorized reviewer | A block prevents completion; it does not recover a completed payment | Does not verify receipt, commercial reasonableness, collusion, or control of compromised accounts |
| MG-4 before release | Strong preventive challenge before release | Review may also detect a defect in the materials | Can stop the payment if the manager has authority to do so | Depends on independence, authority, evidence, and substantive review |
| MG-4 after release but before settlement | Prevents settlement if the manager can hold the payment and approves only after review | Detects problems in the already-released instruction | Can stop settlement; that is partly corrective relative to release | Hybrid: detective relative to Release_Payment, preventive or corrective relative to irrevocable settlement |
| MG-4 after settlement | None for that transfer | May identify a defect | Recovery only if exceptional recovery remains possible | Ratification after settlement cannot be treated as timely prevention |
| RA-7 | None before release | Detects defects through retrospective audit | Partly corrective only while a stop, recall, or recovery remains realistic | Audit completion alone does not restore lost funds |
| ED-1 | Allows emergency same-user processing only with its specified safeguards | Requires a timely audit | Depends on manager authority before settlement and timely audit | Permission is conditional; it does not erase the FEP-1 violation |

For this assessment, **settlement** is the internal-control point of practical irreversibility. A manager’s review after release but before settlement is therefore a hybrid: it detects issues in an instruction already released, and can still prevent the transfer from becoming irrevocable if the manager can hold it. It is weaker than approval before release because the same-user release has already occurred. AP-207 fits ED-1’s stated timing condition; AP-201’s review also preceded settlement, but the case lacked an emergency token and therefore was not an ED-1 use.

## 6. Evidence and Monitoring Recommendations

- **Same-user rate:** Track same-user prepare/release cases by operator, business unit, emergency status, amount band, and time. Treat unusual rates as alerts for review, not proof of misconduct.
- **Override rate:** Record each AV-2 warning, override credential, operator, approver, reason, and supporting evidence. Flag overrides involving recent bank changes, limits, or new vendors.
- **Late manager approval:** Measure approval timing relative to release and settlement. Distinguish approval before release, after release but before settlement, and after settlement. Preserve evidence that the manager could actually hold the payment.
- **Audit before recall window:** For emergency same-user payments, monitor completion of 100% audit within one business day and separately track whether audit occurs while the transfer is still normally recallable. An audit completed on time by a business-day measure may still be too late for recovery.
- **Repeated resource pairs:** Track ordered preparer/releaser pairs and repeated pair frequency, including whether a pair is formally designated. Compare against organizational design and investigate unexplained concentration.
- **Shared workstations:** Correlate workstation, session, badge, and identity-provider logs with payment events. Shared workstation use is a risk signal, not proof that users colluded or shared credentials.
- **Bank-change proximity:** Alert when a bank change falls within seven days of payment, with stronger escalation for changes shortly before payment or where AV-2 is unavailable or overridden. Retain independent bank-ownership evidence from the release time.
- **Control outages:** Alert on validation-service outages, manual workarounds, unsigned checklists, and payments released during degraded operation. Require evidence of any approved alternate control and its reviewer.
- **Emergency-token timing:** Retain tamper-evident token creation and activation times; flag tokens created after preparation. Reconcile system timestamps to incident and outage records.
- **Evidence retention:** Preserve invoice versions, receipts, bank verification, approval records, override logs, workstation/session records, settlement times, audit workpapers, and recall requests.
- **Investigation standard:** Use alerts to prioritize evidence gathering. A repeated pair, shared workstation, family-linked vendor, or late token is not by itself proof of misconduct; conclusions require corroborating records and interviews.

## 7. PM4Py and pandas Checking Code

The code below reconstructs the supplied event log and joins case facts. PM4Py versions may differ in the exact behavior or return type of `filter_four_eyes_principle`; the code reports its case IDs and cross-checks them against a direct resource comparison for completed payments. The direct check is retained as a robustness check, not as the final compliance judgment. The output is explicitly preliminary evidence.
