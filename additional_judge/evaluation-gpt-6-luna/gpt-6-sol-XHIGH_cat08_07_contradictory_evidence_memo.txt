1. Executive memo

**Decision: Adopt a targeted hybrid now.** Keep the merged validation and scheduling path for verified simple, low-risk houses; pause survey skipping for medium-risk and permit-sensitive addresses, and require successful activation before billing. Add controls and backup coverage for coordinator ownership, then use a controlled, risk-stratified experiment before widening the redesign.

The pilot shows a real speed and handover benefit for simple houses, but the overall median hides worsening outcomes for apartments, older buildings, and other exception-heavy cases. Pilot p90 and p95 duration, second visits, complaints, manual activation, and SLA breaches all worsened. Billing before successful activation and missing permit checks are concrete sequencing concerns. These signals make national rollout premature, while a full rollback would discard the strongest demonstrated benefit.

Treat the cost result cautiously: visible cost fell, but the incomplete downstream estimate rose. Likewise, the pilot model’s high fitness does not establish a disciplined or stable process; its low precision and generalization, together with observed deviations, call for case-level monitoring. Case mix differences, incomplete risk data, and sparse start timestamps limit causal conclusions.

Start billing and permit compliance monitoring immediately. In parallel, validate the risk rule and randomize survey skipping only within eligible strata; capture complete 60-day costs and structured exception reasons. Test coordinator backup and capacity. Expand only into segments that meet predefined quality, tail-duration, cost, and control gates against a contemporaneous comparison.

3. Causal and metric-conflict analysis

- **Better median, worse tail:** Faster simple-house cases can lower the median while slower apartment and old-building exceptions raise p90 and p95. The pilot’s segment results fit this pattern, but noncomparable case mix and incomplete risk data prevent firm causal attribution.
- **Lower visible cost, higher estimated cost:** Fewer upfront steps can reduce measured cost while repeat visits and complaints add later expense. The full downstream estimate is incomplete, so it is a warning rather than a conclusive cost comparison.
- **Fewer handovers, more concentration:** Coordinator ownership reduced handovers, but high utilization and absence-related stalls suggest continuity may depend too heavily on particular coordinators. The simpler handover network does not establish resilience.
- **Higher fitness, lower precision:** The model fits observed pilot traces, many of which follow the simplified path. Low precision and generalization mean it allows many additional combinations, including skipped surveys, manual activation, repeat visits, and early billing. Fitness alone does not demonstrate process discipline.
- **Speed to appointment, service quality after installation:** Scheduling improved for simple houses, while apartment installation-to-activation performance worsened and complaints shifted toward nonworking service and billing before activation. These are different customer outcomes and should be monitored separately.

4. Decision alternatives

| Alternative | Advantage | Principal risk | Reasonable if… |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Scale all of R nationally** | Captures median-speed and handover gains quickly. | Extends worsening tail, rework, complaints, and control deviations. | Risk-stratified evidence shows acceptable quality, cost, and compliance, with adequate capacity. |
| **B. Roll back to baseline** | Restores familiar checks and process. | Loses demonstrated simple-house gains and ownership benefits. | Targeted controls fail or harm cannot be contained during a trial. |
| **C. Scale ownership only; suspend survey skipping and post-billing activation** | Preserves ownership while removing the most concerning shortcuts. | Ownership may still create bottlenecks; it does not test the simple-house scheduling benefit. | Backup and capacity evidence supports ownership independently. |
| **D. Controlled regional experiment** | Improves causal evidence by segment and risk. | Delays broader gains and requires follow-up. | Used with immediate safety controls and clear eligibility rules. |
| **E. Targeted hybrid plus measurement** | Retains simple-house benefit while restoring checks for higher-risk cases and learning before expansion. | Requires reliable risk classification, routing, and monitoring. | **Preferred now**, with enforceable controls and prospective gates. |

6. Final recommendation

**Proceed with E: a limited, targeted hybrid with C’s safeguards and D’s experimental discipline.**

- Keep the merged validation/scheduling path for verified simple, low-risk houses in a phased rollout.
- Pause survey skipping for medium-risk and permit-sensitive cases, and require successful activation before billing.
- Add coordinator backup and capacity controls; limit intake when teams lack room to handle exceptions.
- Begin M7 monitoring immediately; run M1 and M6 in eligible strata, and collect M3 and M4 data with sufficient follow-up. Test ownership resilience with M5.
- Expand only into segments that show sustained speed gains without material worsening in p90/p95, first-time-right, complaints, SLA breaches, or full downstream cost. Require compliance with activation and permit controls, adequate follow-up, and reliable backup coverage.
