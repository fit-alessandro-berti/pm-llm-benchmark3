The answer covers all six required sections and shows strong operational awareness: it gates on stage, suppresses I-05 while treating I-07’s new log as a re-entry signal, flags I-06’s imputed feature, and proposes concrete fairness and monitoring safeguards. Its central weakness is the impact accounting, which is not reliable enough to support the allocation.

I-02’s conservative direct value is roughly `0.08 × €25,000 − €600 − €450 = €950` before spillover, while I-04’s is about `0.10 × €60,000 − €2,000 − €450 = €3,550`; the answer does not show why I-09 outranks I-02 under its spillover-adjusted rule.

The base scenario says each used slot raises risk by 0.01–0.03 for six untreated diagnosis-stage cases, but the answer uses an unexplained €15k–€20k average case value, then reports harm ranges that do not transparently follow from that calculation. The adverse scenario’s wider harm range is not derived from the stated 15–30 minutes of delay either.
- “Direct economic benefit” is ambiguous: the case arithmetic subtracts false-alarm burden but not swarm cost, while the table lists swarm cost separately. The scenarios also do not reconcile the reported direct breaches avoided with their economic ranges, and the adverse lower-bound effects for I-04 and I-09 sum to about 0.28 breaches avoided before allowing one case to receive little or no benefit.
- The reserve policy is unclear: two slots are selected, one is reserved, and a fourth is left uncommitted, without explaining whether the reserve is one of the four or how long the other slot remains available.

The policy design and monitoring are thoughtful, but these quantitative and allocation inconsistencies materially weaken the decision-theoretic case.
