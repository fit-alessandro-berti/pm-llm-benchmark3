The most significant problem is P-505: it is classified COMPLIANT even though the answer says Finance approval after commitment does not cure A2’s missing pre-commitment approval. Its alternative interpretation identifies this timing uncertainty only for ECD-2 expiry, not for A2. It makes IC joint authorization a Finance proxy under D1 even though D1’s “all operational approvals” scope and D4’s integrity caveat are ambiguous; its own case analysis then treats that proxy inconsistently across P-507 and P-508. It also suggests emergency necessity under B4 can substitute for Plant approval, though B4 permits a delegated Shift Lead to substitute for an absent Plant Manager and does not itself waive Plant approval.

The remediation advice to “reverse or re-procure” P-504 is not well supported after the supplier commitment and receipt-related process, and the answer does not assess the listed Receive_Goods activity. The proposed clause is useful, but it preserves the ambiguous Finance proxy while leaving unclear whether late CRO exceptions can validate acts already taken.

These are material inconsistencies in the core case assessments and policy interpretation, despite strong structure, explicit uncertainty, and generally careful treatment of evidence.
