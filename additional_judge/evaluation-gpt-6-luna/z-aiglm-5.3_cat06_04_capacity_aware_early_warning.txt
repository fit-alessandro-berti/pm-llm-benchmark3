## 1. Decision rule

Assess cases at meaningful process events and at scheduled refreshes, but do not create a new intervention decision for every unchanged 30-minute refresh.

A case is eligible for a swarm only while it is before `Diagnosis Complete` and has enough time for the swarm and subsequent diagnosis to plausibly affect the SLA outcome. Require a verified alert driver, a feasible intervention window, and positive expected marginal value after swarm cost, false-alarm burden, and estimated queue harm. Treat risk reduction as an uncertain causal range, not a known effect.

Correct probabilities using recent, segment-specific calibration. For SME cases, apply an upward correction consistent with observed underprediction; for Strategic Enterprise cases, discount risk for the post-redesign overprediction. Use broad intervals and shrinkage for moderate or sparse samples, especially Non-Native-Language Support. Flag missing or imputed features for verification before a costly decision. Show raw and adjusted risks separately.

Rank eligible cases by a robust net-benefit tier: expected avoided-breach value minus €450, false-alarm burden, and the estimated spillover cost of consuming a slot. Use conservative effect and calibration assumptions where estimates are weak. Include non-monetary obligations, including regulatory commitments, as explicit overrides or constraints. When capacity binds, use a documented equity safeguard alongside value-based ranking.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

Set a 60-minute alert cool-down after a decision. Suppress refresh alerts within the cool-down unless a materially new event, verified feature change, stage transition, or meaningful risk change occurs. Require a verified driver and a change large enough to alter eligibility, expected net benefit, or urgency before escalating.

After a swarm, do not swarm the same case again unless a materially new event changes the diagnosis path or a senior reviewer documents why another swarm can help. Re-enter suppressed cases at the next meaningful event, on a new verified risk driver, or when deadline feasibility materially changes.

**I-05** has repeated alerts without a new event and only a small probability change, so suppress the refresh alerts and do not escalate on alert count. **I-07** has a new customer log, so review it immediately as new evidence; however, the short remaining window makes a swarm unlikely to be feasible, so use non-swarm escalation and customer communication.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based prioritization:** Ranking only by monetary value can repeatedly deprive lower-value cases of capacity. Track allocation and breach outcomes by value band and segment. Use capped value weighting and a documented minimum allocation or tie-break preference for eligible lower-value cases, subject to feasibility and obligations.
- **Non-Native-Language Support:** Sparse calibration and effect data can produce either harmful interventions or permanent under-service. Preserve uncertainty intervals, ensure language-capable review, and set a small, monitored learning allocation when the conservative net-benefit case is acceptable.
- **Differential calibration:** SME underprediction risks missed interventions; Strategic Enterprise overprediction risks wasted slots. Publish adjusted and raw calibration by segment, recalibrate after the workflow redesign, and trigger review when sustained bias exceeds a pre-set tolerance.
- **Selective intervention and training labels:** Treated outcomes are not untreated counterfactuals; a successful swarm can make a correct alert look false. Log decisions, treatment, outcomes, and policy version. Mark treated cases in training data and estimate effects with appropriate causal methods rather than treating outcomes as unconfounded labels.
- **Spillover distribution:** Queue delays may fall on untreated groups that already receive fewer slots. Measure delay and breach outcomes for exposed untreated cases by segment and value band. Stagger swarms, cap concurrent swarms during congestion, and account for the burden in future allocation and equity reviews.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

Monitor daily slot use, decision times, stage eligibility, swarm duration, specialist interruption, queue delay, repeat alerts, and reserve use. Review weekly whether delays and operational burden match assumptions.

Track rolling calibration, bias, and error by segment, stage, and language-support context. Verify imputed features before intervention and monitor missingness and feature drift. Recalibrate SME and Strategic Enterprise separately, including a post-redesign review, and retain wide uncertainty intervals where samples are small.

Estimate intervention effects using logged eligibility, decisions, timing, outcomes, and relevant case features. Where operationally and ethically acceptable, use a small randomized comparison among marginal cases; otherwise use a pre-specified quasi-experimental design and clearly state its limitations. Track diagnosis time and discovered or corrected information as early mechanism measures, alongside breach outcomes.

Measure spillover by comparing queue delays and outcomes for untreated diagnosis-stage cases across swarm exposure and congestion levels. Report who bears those delays. Re-estimate capacity and spillover assumptions as evidence accumulates, and simulate policy changes before deployment.

Review policy thresholds and allocation safeguards on a fixed schedule and when pre-specified calibration, effect, congestion, or fairness triggers are crossed. Keep versioned decision logs so changes can be audited and their portfolio effects compared.
