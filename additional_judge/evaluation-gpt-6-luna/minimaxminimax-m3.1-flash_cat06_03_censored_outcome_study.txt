The answer identifies the core problem: default is observed only for activated accounts, and treating rejected, withdrawn, or open applications as non-default would be misleading. It gives a reasonable conditional-on-activation target, distinguishes delayed labels from right censoring, addresses transfer and missing feeds, and proposes temporal validation across the policy and migration changes. It also covers the requested leakage fields and offers process-aware features.

The cohort accounting and implementation have material flaws:

- The cohort-flow table does not reconcile the supplied counts to all 48,000 applications. In particular, the 21,600 labeled accounts plus 3,600 immature activated accounts imply 25,200 activated cases, while the listed omitted categories sum to 26,400; the answer does not flag or resolve this 1,200-case discrepancy.
- The code infers label maturity mainly from activation time and the extraction cutoff, but does not establish that each label is specifically the 90-day outcome or check whether the feed is complete. It also treats absent labels as delayed or permanently missing through a fixed timing rule.
- The implementation’s prefix handling does not reliably preserve the prefix’s original event order, and it filters prefixes based on the case’s eventual review time. That uses future case information to determine eligibility and can introduce selection bias. Its prediction point is also effectively the end of the third event subject to that filter, rather than a clearly implemented “first few events” rule.
- The primary validation split is not a full backtest of label availability and maturity at each historical training cutoff. The final test periods also overlap key policy and migration transitions without a clear separate evaluation of those effects.
- The answer proposes inverse-probability weighting and related sensitivity analyses without enough detail on estimating selection or censoring probabilities, positivity, or how conclusions would change under untestable assumptions.

These gaps weaken the cohort, as-of, and leakage claims central to the task. The answer is thoughtful and broad, but the inconsistencies and code-level errors keep it well below a strong score.
