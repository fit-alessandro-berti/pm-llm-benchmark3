## 2. Process-model consistency checks

Build validation around a reconstructed event log with event timestamps, reliable sequence indexes, case identifiers, and a documented prediction timestamp for each case.

- **Prefix-time availability:** For every training and scoring example, reconstruct feature values using only records available by `t0`. Compare stored feature values with an independent as-of recomputation. A feature timestamp is necessary but not sufficient: its source data must also have been available then.
- **Complete-trace use:** Maintain lineage from each feature to its source events and aggregation window. Flag fields derived from later events, closure data, or complete-trace aggregates. Test that prefix-derived replacements are unchanged when events after `t0` are hidden.
- **Activity-order feasibility:** Use alignments or declarative constraints to test required ordering and conditions, such as registration before coverage check, assessment before decision, and appeal only after Partial or Deny. Alignments identify deviations against a chosen model; they do not prove the model captures every valid operational exception.
- **Same-timestamp ambiguity:** When timestamps tie, order events only with a reliable source sequence index. Otherwise mark the ordering unresolved and suppress order-dependent features and explanations. Timestamp sorting alone cannot establish event order.
- **Explanation event status at `t0`:** Check each referenced activity against the observed prefix and the process state. Distinguish activities already observed, enabled next steps, future-possible steps, and impossible transitions. An activity that is merely future-possible is not an observed driver at `t0`.
- **Sequence and DFG checks:** Use prefix constraints to test whether explained sequences are possible at the current point, and DFG or footprint checks to find unexpected directly-follows relations. A frequent DFG edge is descriptive evidence, not a normative rule; process owners must validate constraints and exceptions.
- **Repeated settlement decisions:** Preserve the possibility of a second `Settlement Decision Recorded` after an Appeal, which is valid after Partial or Deny. Check event context and decision sequence so that a repeated coarse label is not automatically treated as a duplicate or invalid transition.

## 3. Alternative feature interpretations

**Defensible predictive signal.** Frozen `initial_damage_complexity`, valid prior history after the strict `< t0` repair, and elapsed time since registration can be evaluated as predictors. They support claims about association with predicted risk, not that complexity or prior claims cause a breach. Elapsed time can be described as less remaining slack, while avoiding the unsupported claim that it independently causes delay.

**Process-state or congestion signal.** A valid `queue_length_at_t0` snapshot can support the description “the observed queue is high and the model associates that state with greater breach risk.” This is an operational state that may be useful for triage, but feature importance alone does not establish that reducing the queue would prevent a particular breach.

**Proxy requiring fairness and robustness review.** `claimant_region` and possibly `claimant_channel` may be predictive while representing differences in network coverage, language, channel, or socioeconomic conditions. Assess subgroup performance, calibration, stability, and consequences of use. Their predictive utility does not justify claims that a group characteristic causes delay or is an appropriate intervention target.

**Drift/calendar proxy.** `calendar_week` is available at `t0`, but its association may encode policy changes or holidays. Evaluate temporal holdouts and performance across policy periods; describe it as a period marker unless supported by a more specific analysis.

**Unusable until lineage is repaired.** Complete-trace duration, appeal status, request totals, full-trace variant, final handler, nightly document snapshot, extraction-date employee experience, unverified mutable risk score, and last resource role cannot support a `t0` explanation. Some may become usable after point-in-time reconstruction or at a later prediction point. Case-specific final decision is also impossible at this `t0`.

These are different review standards: predictive validity asks whether a feature improves out-of-sample prediction; causal interpretation asks whether changing it would change the outcome; operational actionability asks whether a responsible intervention exists; fairness review asks whether performance and impacts differ across groups or proxies. Passing one standard does not imply passing the others.

## 5. Recommendations for explanation validation

1. **Feature-store design:** Capture point-in-time values and source lineage for every feature, with explicit valid-time and availability-time semantics.
2. **Historical timestamps:** Record event-time assignments and versions for mutable scores, roles, employee experience, snapshots, and other changing attributes.
3. **Prefix-only recomputation:** Rebuild training and scoring features from each example’s `t0` prefix. Test invariance when post-`t0` events are withheld.
4. **Process-conformance tests:** Automate ordering, enablement, decision/appeal conditions, repeated-decision handling, and tie-order checks; keep process-owner-approved exceptions explicit.
5. **Calibration after removal:** Refit or recalibrate after removing leaked features, then measure discrimination, calibration, and performance on time-based holdouts.
6. **Explanation stability:** Compare explanations across model versions, resamples, and small valid feature changes; investigate unstable rankings and contributions.
7. **Proxy and fairness analysis:** Review region and channel with subgroup metrics, calibration, robustness, and operational impact; do not infer causality from attribution.
8. **Drift monitoring:** Track feature and process distributions, policy/calendar shifts, schema and lineage changes, and explanation behavior; alert when point-in-time assumptions or conformance patterns change.
