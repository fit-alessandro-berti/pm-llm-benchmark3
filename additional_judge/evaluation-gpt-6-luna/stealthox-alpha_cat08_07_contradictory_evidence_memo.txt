1. Executive memo

**Decision: adopt a modified hybrid (Option E).** Continue coordinator ownership and merged validation/scheduling for simple, low-risk houses, where the pilot shows faster cycle times and better first-time-right results. Pause survey skipping for apartments and other medium-risk addresses, restore permit checks in older buildings, and require activation to pass before billing starts. Do not scale Redesign R nationally or roll back its useful parts.

The pilot’s gains are real but uneven: median cycle time fell by 3.4 days, and cases with three or more handovers fell from 34% to 12%. Yet p95 duration rose by 17 days, first-time-right fell by 6 percentage points, complaints rose by 3.7 points, and estimated cost including revisits and complaints increased. Segment evidence points in different directions: simple houses benefited, while apartments and permit-sensitive older buildings saw worse tails, quality, and complaints. The pilot regions’ older-building mix and incomplete early segment data limit causal confidence, but they do not justify exposing those segments to known process risks while measurement improves.

The model findings reinforce caution. High pilot fitness shows the discovered model can replay observed cases; its low precision means it permits many paths beyond those actually observed. Frequent billing before successful activation and missing permit checks are operational warning signals, not proof that the redesign alone caused the outcome.

Pair the limited rollout with immediate conformance monitoring, full 60-day downstream costing, building-risk rule validation, and better event timestamps and reason codes. Set coordinator capacity and absence coverage before expanding further. Revisit broader rollout after measured quality, cost, and tail-duration thresholds are met.

3. What the process model does and does not tell us

The pilot model’s high fitness means it can reproduce the observed pilot traces relatively well. Its low precision means it allows many possible combinations of skipped surveys, manual activation, repeat visits, and billing start. Together with low generalization, this is weak support for treating the model as a reliable representation of a stable process. It describes the observed behavior better than it establishes which paths are appropriate or predicts how the process will perform at scale.

The alignment and temporal findings locate potential control and delay problems: billing before successful activation, absent permit checks, longer installation-to-activation timing in apartments, and longer failed-visit-to-second-visit delays where coordinator utilization is high. They do not establish that Redesign R caused each deviation. Process discovery is diagnostic evidence about traces; it is not causal proof.

4. Decision alternatives

| Alternative | Main benefit | Main risk | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Scale R nationally | Extends routine-case speed and handover gains | Scales poor tail, quality, and complaint outcomes | Not supported |
| B. Roll back | Restores the baseline process broadly | Loses demonstrated gains for simple houses | Too broad |
| C. Keep coordinator ownership, suspend risky changes | Preserves accountability while restoring checks | Does not target rollout by segment or resolve capacity and evidence gaps | Plausible interim option |
| D. Controlled regional experiment | Improves causal evidence | Delays action while harms may continue | Useful for unresolved components, with guardrails |
| E. Hybrid rollout | Keeps gains in the proven favorable segment and limits exposure elsewhere | Requires reliable routing and capacity management | Best fit to the evidence |

6. Final recommendation

Adopt **Option E**: continue the coordinator-led, merged validation/scheduling path for simple, low-risk houses. Keep apartments, older permit-sensitive buildings, and business customers on the baseline process pending stronger evidence. Suspend medium-risk survey skipping and billing before successful activation; restore permit checks.

Monitor conformance and capacity, including coordinator absence coverage. Run M6 and M4 now; add timestamps and reason codes, and use M5 before expanding coordinator ownership. Run M1 if risk-rule validation suggests survey skipping may be viable. Reassess wider rollout when segment-level quality, tail duration, and fully loaded costs are available.
