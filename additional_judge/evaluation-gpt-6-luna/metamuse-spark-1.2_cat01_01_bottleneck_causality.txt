The answer is organized and makes a serious effort to discuss confounding, mediation, overlap, selection, and a safer pilot. It correctly notices the crude-versus-stratified reversal and treats the completed-case data and bundled nature of group assignment as limitations. However, several numerical and methodological errors undermine its conclusions.

- The stated complexity standardization is wrong. Using the case data’s overall weights (12/30 Low, 10/30 Medium, 8/30 High), the standardized means are about **17.15h for Orion and 18.76h for Nova**, a difference of **−1.61h**, not −2.50h or 19.65h for Nova. The recommendation also overstates the reversal as a “textbook confounding artifact”: stratification on three coarse categories does not establish that explanation.

Overall mean waits are about **12.53h Orion vs 8.47h Nova**, and mean review service is **7.29h vs 5.03h**; the answer reports different values. “Compliance Yes” averages about **25.42h**, not 26.78h. It also calls the compliance pathway “front-door” mediation without establishing the assumptions needed for that identification.

It also treats cycle time as a cause of 30-day quality without adequate support, and describes a queue/rework feedback loop that the stated process sequence does not establish.

The standardized calculation also fails to check for missing group-by-complexity cells before indexing, and overlap reporting omits zero-count combinations.

The pilot’s proposed sample size and rollback thresholds are arbitrary, and the recommendation’s claim that reassignment will “likely” raise Nova’s mean is stronger than the evidence supports.

These are material issues in the core calculations and required reproducible diagnostics, despite the answer’s strong structure and breadth.
