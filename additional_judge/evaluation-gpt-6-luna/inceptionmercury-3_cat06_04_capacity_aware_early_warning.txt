The main weakness is that the allocation and impact arithmetic are not reliable enough for a portfolio decision. The score formula is malformed (`_mid`, missing operators), and the table’s scores do not clearly follow it. More seriously, the selected portfolio omits I-02 while its own table calls it the best reserve-release candidate; reserving a slot until 13:30 also conflicts with I-02’s three-hour deadline unless the policy explains how much delay is acceptable. I-07 is rejected partly on a short-horizon concern, but the stated eligibility rule permits it with 90 minutes remaining, and no calculation reconciles that choice.

The impact table mixes direct intervention costs with false-alarm burden, yet labels the sum “intervention cost”; it also uses unadjusted probabilities for expected false-alarm burden despite prescribing segment calibration. The scenario arithmetic is not auditable from the supplied effect ranges: for example, the chosen reductions and “direct breaches plausibly avoided” do not consistently map to the selected cases’ ranges. Spillover harm assumes a €20,000 average breach value for untreated cases without support from the prompt, and counts six untreated diagnosis-stage cases even though their identities, values, and number are unspecified. These assumptions should be explicit and presented as rough sensitivity estimates.

Finally, the calibration offsets are applied as fixed point corrections despite substantial uncertainty, and the robust lower-bound test is not actually shown for the selected cases. Those gaps make the stated positive net impacts and priority ordering more confident than the evidence warrants.
