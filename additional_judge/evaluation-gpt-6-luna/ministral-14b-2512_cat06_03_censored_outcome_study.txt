The response recognizes the central selection problem and correctly limits its main target to default risk among activated accounts. It also distinguishes right censoring, delayed labels, transfer, and permanently missing feeds, and proposes temporal validation around the policy change and migration.

Major shortcomings keep it from being a sound study design:

- The cohort flow does not account for all 48,000 cases. It omits 3,600 activated but immature accounts, combines unlike groups, and gives no coherent as-of accounting for the stated totals.
- The proposed primary target is underspecified: it does not clearly define the prediction time or estimand. “At activation” conflicts with the request to predict from the first few events, and activation-time risk stratification is not an early warning for every application.
- It does not give a credible, operational censoring and missing-label analysis. In particular, it suggests excluding or weighting cases based on a censoring threshold without specifying a defensible method or addressing how activation selection affects inference.
- The prefix extraction example is not a valid leakage-safe implementation. It includes events based on activation in a way that can expose future process information, does not construct snapshots at a defined prediction point, and the claimed PM4Py conversion call may not match the API.
- The leakage table is incomplete: it omits several required elements, including updated `risk_score`, resource experience, future events, and label availability.
- The code does not implement the requested case-level maturity, censoring, split, or prefix safeguards; its split is event-level and its cohort logic misclassifies cases.

It earns credit for the target caveat, risk distinctions, and recognition that random splits can conceal temporal shifts, but does not meet the required specificity or implementation standard.
