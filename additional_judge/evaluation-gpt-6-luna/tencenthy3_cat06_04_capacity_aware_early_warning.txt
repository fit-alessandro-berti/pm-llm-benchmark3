The response follows the required six-section structure, covers all ten cases, selects a portfolio, and addresses repeated alerts, fairness, spillovers, and monitoring. It also recognizes stage eligibility and calibration uncertainty.

The main weakness is that the allocation and impact arithmetic are not fully coherent. It labels I-02 and I-09 as Tier A but does not compare their estimated direct value against untreated-case spillover using the affected cases’ actual values or identify which cases would bear the harm. The scenario table also reports inconsistent ranges: its base direct-benefit figure is a midpoint estimate, while the “direct breaches avoided” range combines effects from different scenarios. It then treats expected extra breaches as monetary harm using an unsupported €20,000 average. The capacity rationale is unclear too: two slots are selected, one is held for a possible regulatory incident, and another is described as a buffer, despite only four slots and management’s preference to reserve one.

The policy has useful safeguards, but some rules are arbitrary or internally mismatched: the €0.80 fairness floor does not ensure a slot when capacity is full, and the suggested calibration offsets risk treating observational prediction errors as simple probability shifts. These issues weaken the decision-theoretic coherence and operational realism.
