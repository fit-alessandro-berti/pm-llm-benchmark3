This is a strong, well-grounded design. It addresses non-stationary arrivals, resource calendars, parallel reviews, rework, applicant delays, missing data, cross-case effects, intervention comparisons, uncertainty, validation, and decision metrics. Its separation of PM4Py diagnostics from the simulation engine is appropriate, and the code avoids the nonexistent `pm4py.read_csv` function.

The main weakness is that the Python skeleton is extensive but not executable as a simulation: several core event handlers raise `NotImplementedError`, and key components are placeholders. That is allowed by the request, but it limits how much the code demonstrates that the proposed routing, synchronization, calendars, and policy effects work together. Some assumptions and policy parameterizations also need more justification—for example, the specific D10 effects and batching parameters—so results would depend heavily on calibration that the answer does not yet provide. The design recognizes these limits and avoids claiming optimality, which keeps them from being more serious flaws.
