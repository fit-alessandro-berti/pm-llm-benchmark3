## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

- **January–March:** This is the clean baseline. Label maturity is complete, unknown activity and missing queue features are low, and AUROC, Brier score, and ECE are stable. The modest V3 risk level is consistent across these months.
- **April:** Treat this primarily as a pipeline incident. The diagnosis rename disrupted the scoring prefix, while duplicates and unknown activities damaged trace construction. The sharp increase in missing features, unknown activity, and unmapped variants coincides with much worse discrimination and calibration. April’s observed breach rate is not itself extreme, and label maturity is complete, so label delay is not the central explanation. The May patch does not make April safe: a cached feature was not fully recomputed.
- **May:** Data-quality metrics and predictive scores recover substantially after the mapping patch. Remaining degradation from baseline could reflect incomplete April backfill, residual migration effects, or other changes; the aggregate metrics cannot distinguish these. Validate feature lineage and recompute before using May as training data.
- **June:** The rise in V2 from 35% in March to 52% in June is a real routing and case-mix change. V2’s matured breach rate improved from 0.11 to 0.09, and remote-fix time stayed stable. Aggregate breach rate fell to 0.15. This does not by itself indicate concept drift or model failure; a changed mix of reliably resolved cases can change aggregate metrics. Calibration worsened somewhat, so assess within-variant calibration before deciding whether recalibration is needed.
- **July:** The supplier problem begins to appear in process evidence: the parts edge reaches 65 h. V3’s matured breach rate rises to 0.46 from 0.35 in June, while V1 and V2 remain near their earlier rates. This pattern supports an emerging V3-specific operational risk change. With 98% label maturity, it is less plausibly just a censoring artifact, although the 2% immature fraction warrants checking.
- **August:** The supplier delay becomes severe: the parts edge reaches 130 h and V3’s matured breach rate rises to 0.64. V3 also grows to 35% of cases, so both within-variant deterioration and mix change can affect aggregate outcomes. The large drop in AUROC and worsening calibration are consistent with a genuine risk change, but should be confirmed on mature, stratified cohorts. Label maturity is 93%, so censoring is relevant but unlikely to explain the full V3 signal.
- **September:** Several effects overlap: V3 reaches 42% of cases, Partner Z enters and supplies 24% of volume, and the parts edge remains very slow. V3’s matured breach rate is 0.61; V1 and V2 remain at 0.19 and 0.12. This supports a parts-path problem, while Partner Z adds population shift and may change the mix within V3. The observed Partner Z breach rate of 0.45 is not a fair mature comparison because its labels arrive more slowly. The overall deterioration cannot be assigned to a single cause from these aggregates.
- **October:** Do not read the observed breach rate of 0.16 as an improvement. Only 55% of labels are mature, the median delay is 52 days, and the backlog disproportionately withholds outcomes from long paths. Its mean predicted risk of 0.23 and available-label metrics are therefore subject to selection bias. Partner Z and V3 remain substantial, but October’s stable variant-specific breach rates are unavailable. Wait for fixed-vintage maturity evidence before drawing model-performance conclusions.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

1. **Immediate containment:** Do not use April as ordinary training or calibration data. Preserve the production model while validating scoring prefixes and queue-feature lineage. For operations, use the measured parts-delay signal as a process alert; it is not an admissible diagnosis-time feature unless it was known when the prediction was made.
2. **Short-term recalibration:** After fixing data and selecting mature, fixed-vintage labels, assess calibration within variant and source. If ranking remains stable but V3 risks are systematically underpredicted, apply a monitored recalibration or a provisional V3 adjustment. Do not calibrate to October’s currently observed outcomes.
3. **Feature repair:** Recompute April-derived features from raw events, normalize the renamed activity, and handle duplicates consistently across sources. Add only legitimate, available-at-scoring predictors. Rurality, device age, or supplier information may improve transport or risk estimates if their use is justified and their timing is verified.
4. **Full retraining:** Consider retraining only after pipeline repair, label maturity, and within-segment tests show persistent deterioration that calibration cannot fix or that new predictors are needed. Compare a challenger against the incumbent on a chronological, mature holdout and shadow it before deployment.

**Training-data disposition:** January–March are the cleanest baseline. May–June are potentially usable after lineage checks; May needs particular scrutiny for residual backfill issues, while June reflects a changed but potentially learnable routing mix. July–August are useful for evaluating the supplier regime once labels are mature and process features are verified, but should not be mixed blindly with the baseline. September is provisional because of Partner Z’s new population and slower label maturity. October is unsafe for outcome training or calibration at this cutoff. April is unsafe until raw-event replay and feature repair are complete. Exclude immature cases or use a censoring-aware method with explicit assumptions.

A variant-specific or segment-specific model is not yet justified solely by aggregate performance. First test whether a shared model remains useful and can be calibrated within each variant. A V3-specific calibration or operational policy is plausible given its sharp risk increase; a separate V3 model requires sufficient mature data and demonstrated conditional or ranking differences. Treat Partner Z as an audit and transport stratum, not automatically as a model segment or decision feature.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

- **Predictive performance:** Track AUROC and precision/recall at operational thresholds by month, variant, and source, using both raw results and fixed-mix results. Attach cohort vintage and maturity status to every metric.
- **Calibration:** Monitor Brier score, calibration slope/intercept, reliability plots, and ECE by variant and source on mature outcomes. Keep calibration alarms separate from ranking alarms.
- **Label maturity:** Track time-to-label survival curves, maturity fractions at fixed ages, and delay by path, variant, and partner. Mark current-cohort performance provisional when maturity is low or selective.
- **Data quality:** Alert on unknown/unmapped activities, duplicate rates, missing queue features, diagnosis-prefix availability, event ordering, schema changes, and feature-lineage/version mismatches.
- **Process variants:** Monitor variant shares, unmapped cases, and transition frequencies against a baseline; distinguish case-start cohorts from events occurring in a calendar month.
- **Performance DFG:** Track edge counts and elapsed-time distributions, especially `Order Part -> Part Received` and diagnosis to `Remote Fix`, with volume and censoring context.
- **Population:** Monitor source, geography, device age, and other feature distributions, overlap, and support. Treat changes as investigation triggers rather than automatic evidence of model harm.
- **Intervention and deployment:** Track alert volume, intervention uptake, outcomes, workload, and benefits by risk band, variant, and source. Record model and feature versions; use shadow evaluation, staged rollout, and rollback criteria.

**Alert dependencies:** A label-delay alarm should suppress or qualify outcome-based performance alarms; it should not trigger retraining. A data-quality alarm should quarantine affected cohorts and trigger replay/lineage checks. Process and population alarms should trigger stratified diagnosis. Recalibration requires mature labels and trustworthy features. Retraining requires those gates plus evidence of persistent conditional deterioration and a validated challenger. Monitor intervention effects separately because interventions can change the outcomes used to assess the model.

The code below writes an auditable monthly summary with both event-calendar and case-start views. Calendar cohorts describe activity occurring during a month and may contain incomplete traces; case-start cohorts group all recorded events for cases created that month and are retrospective when full histories are available. Temporal features are computed from each case’s diagnosis prefix to avoid including post-diagnosis events.

```python
from collections import Counter
import json
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")
raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)

# Adapt these column names to the source schema.
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
    "Diagnostic Assessment Completed": "Initial Diagnosis Completed"
})
keys = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
before = len(raw)
events = raw.drop_duplicates(subset=keys, keep="first").copy()
events["duplicates_removed"] = False  # Counts are recorded separately below.
duplicate_count = before - len(events)

# Retain source columns if present; use the standard PM4Py keys.
event_cols = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
events = events.sort_values(["case_id", "timestamp"])
log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events[event_cols].copy(),
    case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp",
)

# Case-start month is based on each case's earliest recorded event.
case_start = events.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min()
months = pd.date_range(
    events["timestamp"].min().to_period("M").start_time,
    events["timestamp"].max().to_period("M").start_time,
    freq="MS", tz="UTC",
)

def edge_median_hours(perf, source, target):
    # PM4Py performance DFG values are typically lists of durations in seconds.
    values = perf.get((source, target), [])
    return float(np.median(values) / 3600) if len(values) else np.nan

def summarize(month, cohort, frame, pm_log, interpretation):
    dfg = pm4py.discover_dfg(pm_log)
    perf_result = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(pm_log)
    performance = perf_result[1] if isinstance(perf_result, tuple) else perf_result
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(pm_log)
    counts = Counter(variants)
    return {
        "month": month.strftime("%Y-%m"),
        "cohort": cohort,
        "cases": int(frame["case_id"].nunique()),
        "events": int(len(frame)),
        "unknown_activity_share": float(
            frame["activity"].str.upper().eq("UNKNOWN_ACTIVITY").mean()
        ) if len(frame) else np.nan,
        "variant_counts": json.dumps(
            {" > ".join(k): int(v) for k, v in counts.items()}, sort_keys=True
        ),
        "dfg_edges": int(dfg.number_of_edges()),
        "performance_dfg_edges": int(len(performance)),
        "order_to_part_received_median_h": edge_median_hours(
            performance, "Order Part", "Part Received"
        ),
        "diagnosis_to_remote_fix_median_h": edge_median_hours(
            performance, "Initial Diagnosis Completed", "Remote Fix"
        ),
        "interpretation": interpretation,
    }

rows = []
for lo in months:
    hi = lo + pd.offsets.MonthBegin(1)

    # Event-calendar cohort: events in the month; paths can be incomplete.
    calendar_frame = events.loc[
        events["timestamp"].ge(lo) & events["timestamp"].lt(hi)
    ].copy()
    calendar_log = pm4py.filter_time_range(log, str(lo), str(hi), mode="intersect")
    rows.append(summarize(
        lo, "event_calendar", calendar_frame, calendar_log,
        "Events during month; open paths may be right-censored.",
    ))

    # Case-start cohort: all recorded events for cases first seen in the month.
    ids = case_start.index[(case_start >= lo) & (case_start < hi)]
    start_frame = events.loc[events["case_id"].isin(ids)].copy()
    start_log = pm4py.format_dataframe(
        start_frame[event_cols].copy(),
        case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp",
    )

    # Diagnosis-prefix temporal features: restrict each case to events through diagnosis.
    diagnosis_ts = (
        start_frame.loc[start_frame["activity"].eq("Initial Diagnosis Completed")]
        .groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min()
    )
    prefix = start_frame.join(diagnosis_ts.rename("_diagnosis_ts"), on="case_id")
    prefix = prefix.loc[prefix["_diagnosis_ts"].notna()
                        & prefix["timestamp"].le(prefix["_diagnosis_ts"])].copy()
    prefix["variant"] = prefix.groupby("case_id")["activity"].transform(
        lambda s: tuple(s)
    )
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(
        pm4py.format_dataframe(prefix[event_cols + ["variant"]].copy()),
        variant="variant",
    ) if not prefix.empty else pd.DataFrame()

    rows.append(summarize(
        lo, "case_start", start_frame, start_log,
        "Cases started this month with recorded follow-up; open cases remain censored.",
    ))

summary = pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values(["month", "cohort"])
summary["duplicates_removed_total"] = duplicate_count
summary.to_csv("monthly_process_audit.csv", index=False)
```

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

| Failure mode | Likely consequence | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Retraining or calibrating on immature labels | October’s available labels overrepresent faster or shorter paths, potentially teaching the model that long, high-risk paths are safe. Later label arrivals can reverse the apparent result. | Gate outcome decisions on maturity; use fixed-vintage cohorts and, where appropriate, censoring-aware analysis. |
| Calibrating around April’s pipeline bug | Calibration may encode wrong prediction prefixes, missing features, and duplicate-driven artifacts as genuine risk levels. | Quarantine April; replay raw events, repair lineage, and evaluate corrected predictions before using the month. |
| Ignoring supplier-driven deterioration | The model may continue assigning V3 its historical risk despite matured breach rates and parts delays rising sharply. | Escalate the parts process operationally and validate V3-specific calibration; add supplier regime only if available at scoring time. |
| Attributing Partner Z’s observed gap directly to unfair model behavior | Slower labels, higher V3 share, rurality, and older devices may confound the raw gap; a source-based intervention could be poorly targeted. | Compare within variant, account for maturity, assess feature overlap, and reweight where support permits before interpreting residual gaps. |
| Treating June’s routing shift as general model failure | Unnecessary retraining could disrupt a model while remote-fix timing and V2 outcomes remain stable. | Standardize to a fixed variant mix and inspect within-variant performance before changing the model. |
| Using future process durations as diagnosis-time predictors | Post-score information can leak outcome progress and make offline performance misleading. | Enforce feature-availability timestamps; use later edge durations for process diagnosis, not as predictors at the diagnosis score. |
