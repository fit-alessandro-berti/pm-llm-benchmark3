7.2/10 — The answer is thorough and well structured, with strong attention to authority, scope, timing, the ERP outage, and alternative interpretations. It correctly treats late approval as non-curative and identifies meaningful remediation and policy safeguards.

The main weakness is inconsistency in applying its own hierarchy. It treats the CFO email as sufficient to replace Finance and other operational approvals in some cases, yet classifies P-508 as noncompliant above the email’s EUR 150,000 cap even though the email says Legal clearance is required “as well.” It also calls P-507 justified despite uncertainty about whether Incident Command authorization satisfies GPIP-4’s independent budget-owner requirement, while other cases treat that gap as disqualifying. Several case assessments appear to rely on unverified assumptions about Plant approval, scope, and timing without consistently reflecting them in the primary classifications.

The response also contains unsupported aggregate figures and some overreaching remediation proposals, and its draft policy language is not fully aligned internally: it says the CRO alone may issue exceptions to A1–A3, then says a CFO determination may substitute for an A2 approval. These issues materially weaken the consistency and evidential discipline of an otherwise detailed assessment.
