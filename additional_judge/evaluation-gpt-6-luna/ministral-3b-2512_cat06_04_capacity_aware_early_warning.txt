The response follows the requested six-section structure and covers capacity, repeated alerts, spillovers, fairness, and monitoring. Its portfolio choice is plausible, but several errors undermine the decision analysis.

- The eligibility and calibration rules are too blunt. A fixed €5,000 value cutoff and fixed segment adjustments do not account for the supplied ranges of calibration error or explicitly distinguish observed risk from risk under intervention. I-01 is incorrectly labeled “low value” at €9,000, and I-04 is called a repeated alert though its history says first alert.
- The impact table has material arithmetic and modeling errors. For example, the base direct benefit for the stated effects is €26,750, not €13,500. It prices I-07’s avoided breaches at €5,000 rather than its €5,000 value? I-07 is indeed €5,000, but the table’s stated base effects and multiplication still do not yield its total. More fundamentally, it counts four slots in costs despite selecting four cases while saying only three slots are used, and it mixes spillover breaches with money without valuing or explaining them.
- The adverse scenario’s claim that spillover “hurts more” is unsupported: no monetary value for spillover harm is calculated, and its direct benefit arithmetic is also inconsistent. The confidence labels are overconfident given uncertain, noncausal effect ranges and calibration issues.
- Temporal rules use arbitrary percentage thresholds and a 24-hour cooldown that may not fit a case’s remaining SLA time or the intervention’s diagnosis-stage eligibility. I-07 should be evaluated on the new log, not made contingent on a specified probability increase.
- Monitoring proposes before/after comparisons and direct intervened-versus-untreated comparisons, which are confounded by selective intervention. It lacks a credible causal design, such as randomized or staged allocation where feasible, and concrete safeguards for equitable allocation of scarce capacity.

These flaws make the impact estimates unauditable and weaken the policy’s operational coherence despite broad coverage of the requested topics.
