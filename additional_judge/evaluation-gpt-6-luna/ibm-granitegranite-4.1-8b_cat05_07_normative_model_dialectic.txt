3/5 — The proposal shows a good grasp of the competing authorities and operational evidence, but its model and data revision is too inconsistent to serve as a reliable conformance design.

It correctly distinguishes CS-9’s approved requirements from PD-3’s time-limited pilot and FD-7’s expired emergency exception. It also identifies SUP-703 and SUP-705 as unauthorized deviations, treats SUP-706 as uncertain because the notification link is mutable, and recognizes that SUP-707 reflects desirable practice the current model cannot express. The discussion of SUP-709’s scope ambiguity and SUP-710’s missing reactivation procedure is appropriately cautious.

The main weakness is that the proposed POWL does not implement the rules it describes. The base checks are not correctly composed: it adds an edge from Sanctions_Check to Quality_Review, making them sequential, while the stated base model requires concurrency. It also connects decision logic inconsistently, places the pilot condition after the decision, and does not integrate the pilot or emergency overlays into the final `model`, which is just `base`. The proposed loop is malformed and does not provide a sound repeated Request_More_Info cycle. Some snippets also refer to undefined or mismatched objects and labels, so the code is not a usable executable proposal.

The data layer has similar gaps. The notification requirement in the PD-3 eligibility table is unsupported by the directive. The conflict rule is malformed and introduces a reviewer relationship not established in CS-9. The monitoring rule permits activation whenever an eligibility condition is true, without requiring the corresponding approval, sanctions check, timing, spend cap, retrospective approval, or post-activation quality review. The emergency rule should be restricted to FD-7’s scope and dates, while post-expiry flood conditions alone do not authorize continued exception use.

A stronger revision would preserve M0’s concurrent checks and explicit approval-before-activation as the normative baseline, then represent pilot and emergency exceptions as scoped, versioned overlays with complete guards and required follow-up controls. It should add Request_More_Info as an explicit repeatable subprocess, distinguish process scope from logging defects and data-quality uncertainty, and retain evidence provenance and stable event identifiers. The response’s analytical judgments are often sound, but the proposed implementation does not reliably encode them.
