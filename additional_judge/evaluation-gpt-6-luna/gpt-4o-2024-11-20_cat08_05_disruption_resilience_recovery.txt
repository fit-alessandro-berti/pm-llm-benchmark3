3/5 — The response gives a clear, actionable framework and correctly distinguishes useful workarounds from shortcuts that undermine authorization and traceability. It covers prioritization, manual controls, reconciliation, audit remediation, and future recovery design.

It does not fully ground its judgments in the event-log evidence: it rarely quantifies changes against baseline or compares T2, T3, and T4 trends, and it overstates some claims (for example, that audit remediation eventually recovered T2 compliance). Its proposed controls also leave gaps: handwritten proof of delivery alone does not ensure chain of custody, and “minimum manual form completion” for lower-priority orders is too vague to protect cold-chain, controlled-substance, or recall-sensitive products. The fairness metric is also poorly specified as a cycle-time ratio without adjustment for differing service expectations.
