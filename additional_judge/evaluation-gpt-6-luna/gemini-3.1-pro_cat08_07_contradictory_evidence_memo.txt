4.5/10 — **Mixed: partly succeeds, but contains major alignment and evidence-quality problems.** It makes a defensible hybrid recommendation and identifies the key tradeoff between faster median times and worse tail outcomes, rework, and complaints. It also recognizes the value of measuring downstream costs and monitoring billing and permit conformance.

However, it overstates the evidence: it calls simple-house gains “proven” and “massive” despite the nonrandomized pilot and different regional mix. It also proposes nationally scaling coordinator ownership and merged scheduling for simple houses even though coordinator utilization was already 93% in two regions and the available segment data may not support reliable routing. The suggested “>90% accuracy” threshold is arbitrary and does not specify how accuracy or acceptable risk would be defined. The memo also treats several interpretations as settled facts, such as claiming the skip rule caused failures and delays.

A stronger answer would recommend a bounded, measured rollout for clearly identifiable low-risk cases, immediately stop billing before successful activation, restore required permit and survey checks where risk warrants them, and set explicit guardrails and decision criteria for expansion.
