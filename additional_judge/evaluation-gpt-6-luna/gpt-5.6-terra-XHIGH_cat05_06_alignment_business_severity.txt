## 1. Materiality Framework

Severity reflects the business consequence of a deviation: the control objective at stake, payment amount and recoverability, whether payment or notification can still be corrected, timing, potential customer or regulatory harm, recurrence, and the reliability of the evidence. Model scope also matters: an alignment deviation on a denied or withdrawn claim may indicate a model gap rather than an operational failure.

Severity 1 means harmless noise or a fitting path; 2 means a limited process or evidence gap with little indication of control failure; 3 means a meaningful unresolved control or evidence concern; 4 means a confirmed policy breach with material exposure; and 5 means a severe payment-control failure, such as paying before required approval. These are case-level judgments, not calculated scores.

Confidence describes how well the evidence supports the interpretation, not how serious the potential outcome is. A high-severity concern can have low confidence if evidence is incomplete; a low-severity explanation can be highly certain. The uniform raw costs count visible log/model moves, not business impact.

## 4. Prioritized Remediation

1. **Investigate payment before approval (CL-604).** Confirm the payment and approval records, assess recovery and customer impact, verify whether Fraud Screen occurred, and identify why release was not gated on approval.
2. **Investigate the bank-change bypass (CL-610).** Establish the change history and payment destination, assess recoverability, and ensure recent beneficiary changes trigger the required screening and manual approval before release.
3. **Resolve missing Fraud Screen evidence for CL-602.** Seek independent evidence rather than treating a verbal check as proof; review whether the high-risk, high-value payment met the control.
4. **Enforce policy conditions independently of process-tree fit.** Apply amount, risk, and recent bank-change attributes to approval routing and payment release. Review all four synchronous Auto Approve policy violations.
5. **Strengthen notification evidence and connector handling.** Preserve immutable case-to-message links for CL-608 and similar cases; classify retry duplicates by message ID and connector so operational monitoring distinguishes retries from separate sends.
6. **Improve external assessment evidence linkage.** Establish stable document-to-case identifiers and ingest or reconcile external adjuster events. Resolve the two cases without stable identifiers first.
7. **Correct mapping and model scope.** Validate `Assess Damage` equivalence and preserve mapping history. Decide whether denied and withdrawn claims are in scope, then represent their valid terminal paths and define closure-notice evidence for withdrawals.
8. **Separate technical diagnostics from business prioritization.** Keep raw alignment cost as an explanation of model/log edits. Use policy checks, evidence quality, and business impact for control prioritization; retain silent moves as fitting and UI/transport events as non-business events unless they reveal a control issue.
