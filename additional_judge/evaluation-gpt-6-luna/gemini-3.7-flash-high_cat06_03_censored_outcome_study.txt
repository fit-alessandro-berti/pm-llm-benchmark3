**4.0/10**

The answer shows strong awareness of selection through activation, the danger of labeling rejected cases as non-default, temporal leakage, policy shift, and the need for time-based validation. The leakage table and discussion of deployment feedback are useful. However, several core design choices and implementation details are inconsistent or statistically unsound.

The primary target is not clearly defined. It combines a multi-state outcome over all applications with a cause-specific default hazard conditional on activation, then describes a decomposition of default risk that assumes a meaningful activation probability model. Those are distinct estimands with different time origins and risk sets. The proposed horizon is 90 days after activation, but prediction occurs before credit review is complete; the answer does not clearly define how transitions before activation enter the risk process or how to estimate the stated joint probability.

The cohort flow accounts arithmetically for all 48,000 applications, but labels all 3,600 remaining activated accounts immature based on a single activation-date cutoff. The snapshot says their outcome or delayed feed has not matured; actual label availability, activation time, and permanent feed missingness must distinguish these cases. The maturity cutoff is also applied to activation while validation splits by application start, so applications starting in the same window can have very different label maturity. The table’s classification of linked and unlinked transfers is not supported by observed outcomes, and it does not resolve the elevated permanent feed missingness among partner-originated activated accounts.

The censoring section proposes Aalen–Johansen, cause-specific Cox, and IPCW without specifying a coherent time scale, event definitions, censoring time, or how activation and post-activation default are represented in one model. Rejection and withdrawal are competing outcomes for the application process, but they are not competing events for a default-after-activation outcome unless the estimand is explicitly defined as a multi-state process. IPW for activation is asserted without a defensible propensity model or a clear estimand, and cannot address structural lack of positivity. Some claims also conflate ordinary censoring, delayed label arrival, and missing-not-at-random feeds.

The implementation has serious correctness problems. It treats any mature activated account with a missing `default_90d` value as a non-default, directly creating the false labels the answer warns against. It uses the maximum `data_extract_time` from the file rather than a separately configured as-of cutoff, does not use `label_available_at` to establish maturity, and derives terminal states from activity presence without enforcing event order or handling duplicate/multiple terminal events. Its migration deduplication rule can collapse legitimate repeated activities with the same timestamp. Prefix extraction may not return a DataFrame in the assumed shape, and the fallback silently changes the requested PM4Py behavior. The fixed event-count prefix is not anchored to a reliable prediction milestone, short traces are not handled explicitly, and the code does not demonstrate elapsed-time prefixes. It also does not construct censoring duration or competing-risk labels, and the reported test cohort can include applications whose activation-based outcomes are not mature.

The answer therefore contains many of the right cautions but does not carry them through into a coherent target, valid cohort labels, or reliable code. It also does not provide the requested detail on process-aware features in the visible response’s section 5 beyond a partial list.
