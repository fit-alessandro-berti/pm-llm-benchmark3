7/10 — The response offers a clear, defensible recommendation: use a segmented rollout, retain the apparent benefits for simple low-risk houses, restore the controls linked to poorer outcomes, and gate broader expansion on measured results. It separates evidence from interpretation, explains the median-versus-tail conflict, accounts for regional mix and incomplete cost data, and ranks measurements by how they could change the decision.

The main weakness is overconfidence in some operational prescriptions and thresholds. The evidence supports concern about coordinator overload, but does not establish that an 85% utilization cap or the proposed segment-level rollout will work. Some claims about causal mechanisms also go beyond the observational pilot. The memo would be stronger if it framed these as testable safeguards and made a controlled comparison central before scaling coordinator ownership nationally.
