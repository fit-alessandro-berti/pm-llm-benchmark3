5.1

The answer follows the required headings and covers all nine cases, the main control dimensions, counterfactuals, monitoring recommendations, and a substantial code block. But several significant errors undermine its case judgments and code.

- **AP-207 is misclassified.** ED-1 requires manager approval before settlement, which the case meets. It does not require approval before release. The answer’s own rationale says the conditions are met, yet labels the case only partially met. Given the facts, OBJECTIVE_MET under ED-1 is better supported.
- **AP-201’s audit timing is wrong.** Audit began 25 hours after release, so it missed the stated one-business-day requirement for emergency same-user payments—though this case was not an emergency. The answer describes this as a mitigating control without clearly accounting for its limited corrective value.
- **AP-204 overstates the evidence.** A fabricated receipt and repeated pairing create serious concern, but the prompt explicitly leaves collusion unresolved and says the pair may reflect a dedicated payment cell. Calling the objective failed with high confidence goes beyond the evidence; an indeterminate judgment is more defensible.
- **The control decomposition overstates AV-2.** It says AV-2 can prevent a wrong vendor/account by checking vendor history, but AV-2’s specified checks do not establish vendor legitimacy or receipt of goods. The answer also treats ED-1’s amount limit as if it enforces the limit, when ED-1 is a conditional permission and AV-2 performs the operator-limit check.
- **The sufficiency test is too categorical.** It says controls acting after settlement are ineffective, although they can still detect defects, and recovery may sometimes be possible. It also labels pre-settlement manager action broadly preventive without consistently distinguishing prevention of release from prevention of irrevocable loss.
- **The code’s audit flag is not reliable.** It uses elapsed whole days (`time_diff.days < 2`) as a proxy for one business day, which can misclassify elapsed times and ignores weekends and business-day rules. Its “Emergency_Eligible” flag is only the token field, not eligibility under all ED-1 conditions. The code also uses `all_completed_ids - compliant_ids` even though cases omitted or dropped by PM4Py may be treated as violators; the stated version caveat does not fully resolve that risk.

These are material shortcomings in the requested timing analysis, evidence discipline, and executable checking logic.
