## 1. Decision rule

A case is eligible for a swarm only while it is before `Diagnosis Complete`, with enough time remaining to complete the 45-minute intervention and act on what it finds. Eligibility also requires a plausible, documented path by which a swarm could reduce breach risk. A high predicted risk alone is insufficient.

For prioritization, estimate a range of direct value:

`adjusted breach-risk reduction × value of avoiding breach − €450 swarm cost − false-alarm burden − estimated spillover harm`

Use conservative estimates when effects or calibration are uncertain. Treat this as a decision aid, not as a precise causal estimate. Apply the supplied segment corrections as provisional adjustments: add 0.04–0.09 to SME risk, subtract 0.05–0.12 from Strategic Enterprise risk, and use a wider uncertainty interval for Non-Native-Language Support. Public Sector estimates remain broadly as supplied, with moderate uncertainty. For I-06, do not rely on the imputed queue feature without a human check.

Prioritize cases with positive conservative value, near enough to the deadline to benefit, and with stronger evidence that the alert reflects a treatable problem. Include contractual or regulatory obligations as overrides, with the reason documented. Consider a case only once per material evidence change; retain one of four slots as a reserve when feasible.

## 4. Repeated alerts and temporal policy

After an alert that does not lead to intervention, suppress repeat paging for 60 minutes unless material evidence changes. Record routine model refreshes silently. At each stage transition or material update, reassess eligibility, treatability, and priority.

Suppress repeat alerts when the only change is a routine refresh, the case has no new evidence, and the decision would not change. Escalate to a human review when a verified risk increase, a new artifact, a stage transition, or an approaching deadline changes the potential value of intervention. Re-entry requires a short record of what changed and why the swarm could now help. A repeat swarm on the same case remains prohibited absent a materially new event and senior approval.

## 5. Fairness and feedback-loop risks

- **Value-based prioritization can starve lower-value cases.** Track selection and outcomes by value band and segment. Set aside a share of capacity for cases with strong conservative benefit outside the highest-value band, subject to contractual obligations and queue conditions.
- **Non-Native-Language Support estimates are uncertain.** Do not automatically include or exclude cases based on the support flag. Require human review with appropriate language support, record uncertainty, and report calibration and outcomes separately for this group.
- **Calibration differs across segments.** Display adjusted estimates with uncertainty intervals; review calibration by segment and stage. Prevent Strategic overprediction from dominating allocation and account for SME underprediction in watchlists and prioritization.
- **Intervention changes future training labels.** Record predictions, intervention decisions, reasons, and outcomes. Do not treat post-swarm non-breaches as untreated examples of low risk; use causal adjustment or a suitable comparison group in model updates.
- **Spillover may fall disproportionately on untreated groups.** Log queue membership and measure delays and breach outcomes for untreated cases after each swarm, broken down by segment and value band. Include expected spillover in slot decisions and tighten allocation when congestion or subgroup harm rises.

## 6. Monitoring and recalibration plan

Monitor operationally: slot use, reserve availability, intervention timing and duration, specialist interruption, alert volume, suppressed repeats, and reasons for overrides. Track predictive calibration and error by segment, stage, and data-quality status, including imputed versus complete records. Review intervention outcomes separately from untreated outcomes.

Estimate causal effects through logged treatment assignment and decision reasons, matched comparisons with adjustment for selection, and a prospective randomized tie-break among similarly ranked cases where operationally and contractually acceptable. Measure queue delays and untreated-case outcomes before and after swarms to estimate spillover. Widen uncertainty intervals when samples are sparse or workflow changes.

Review fairness metrics alongside capacity use: selection, intervention, breach, false-alarm burden, and spillover rates by segment, support need, and value band. Investigate persistent disparities before changing allocation. Recalibrate segment adjustments when observed calibration shifts; revisit the policy when effects, spillover, congestion, or fairness outcomes materially diverge from assumptions.
