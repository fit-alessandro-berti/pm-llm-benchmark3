1/5 — The draft is detailed and organized, but it does not reliably apply the supplied facts and rules. Several conclusions appear to be based on invented or misread provisions, which undermines the case assessments and recommendations.

The strongest issue is the treatment of the CFO email. D1 covers operational approvals only up to EUR 150,000; it does not establish a waiver for every control or settle whether “operational approvals” includes Finance approval. The draft repeatedly treats Finance approval as waived, including for P-502 and P-505, without adequately addressing D3’s limit on the CFO’s authority or D4’s integrity-control caveat. It also misstates D1 as covering up to EUR 155,000 in its alternative interpretation of P-503. P-508’s EUR 155,000 amount is above D1’s stated cap, and D2 calls for Legal clearance above EUR 150,000.

The draft also misreads ECD-2. C2 waives the approved-supplier-register rule; C5’s silence about Compliance clearance does not waive that control. C4 gives precedence over ordinary purchasing timing and supplier-onboarding requirements, which supports the PO timing and supplier-registration exceptions, but does not clearly displace GPIP-4’s Compliance requirement, segregation rule, or Finance approvals. The draft’s claim that ECD-2 waives Compliance clearance in P-508 is therefore unsupported.

Several case outcomes conflict with the facts:

- P-501’s commitment at 08:50 preceded ECD-2’s 09:00 start. The emergency timing exception may apply under B4, but the reachable Plant Manager and lack of delegation weigh against substituting SHIFT-1 for Plant approval. Its later approvals do not automatically cure any missing pre-commitment controls.
- P-503 has a requester/committer and authorization-role overlap, no separate Finance or Compliance clearance, and an ambiguous fit with ECD-2’s “critical sterilization-line lots” scope. Backup-line components may qualify under Operations’ interpretation, but stock expected to last three days weighs against Audit’s narrower view. The draft should not label this compliant on an assumed waiver.
- P-504 is outside ECD-2’s stated sterilization-event scope, and the emergency form’s “site continuity” label does not establish an emergency under B4. The supplier was unapproved, and no Incident Command authorization is recorded.
- P-505’s supplier acknowledgment at 17:59:31 is evidence consistent with a pre-expiry commitment; the 18:06 ERP timestamp alone is not decisive given the documented clock issue. But the draft’s broad Finance-waiver conclusion remains unproven.
- P-506 occurred after ECD-2 expired. A buyer’s report of a verbal overnight extension, without a written extension or joint authorization record, does not establish that the directive remained effective.
- P-507’s supplied evidence appears incomplete in the excerpt, so a firm classification is not defensible from what is visible. The draft’s assertion that an ERP outage made Compliance clearance late needs support from the actual timeline and signed-PDF evidence.

The draft does recognize uncertainty and offers remediation, but its proposed replacement clause invents new thresholds and waiver mechanisms, including a six-hour harm test, a EUR 150,000–250,000 band, and a new role for the CFO and Chief Risk Officer. Those may be policy options, but they should be clearly presented as proposals rather than as implications of the existing sources. A stronger evaluation would separate established breaches, plausible interpretations, and unresolved evidence; then classify each case conditionally where necessary.
