7/10 — The recommendation is clear and defensible: keep coordinator ownership for simple, low-risk houses in the pilot regions, pause survey skipping and billing before successful activation, and defer geographic expansion pending segment-level evidence. It connects conflicting metrics to the risks of tail delays, rework, complaints, and overloaded coordinators, while distinguishing observed facts from interpretations and setting measurable rollout gates.

The memo’s main weakness is its value-of-information analysis. It prioritizes several measurements and experiments, but the excerpt gives limited comparative reasoning about their cost, timing, or expected decision impact; it also describes a randomized survey-skip test without specifying sample size or success criteria beyond the later gates. The detailed alternatives and operating plan are useful, though the volume of analysis makes the executive decision less concise than it could be.
