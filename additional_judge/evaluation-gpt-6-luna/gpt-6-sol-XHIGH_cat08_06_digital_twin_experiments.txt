## 1. Model scope and state variables

The digital twin covers submitted building-permit applications from portal submission to permit issuance or rejection, including applicant responses, third-party and public-notice delays, rework, appeals, and resource scheduling. Pre-submission effort should also be tracked when evaluating D1, so its costs are not hidden outside the modeled process.

Model individual cases with type, district, architect, value, risk and routing attributes; current state, enabled tasks, completed results, queue age, rework count, notice and objection status, inspection readiness, appeal status, and submission cohort. Represent shared architects, neighborhood objection groups, and inspection routes as cross-case links.

Use separate queues and calendars for clerks, planners, fire reviewers, environmental reviewers, inspectors, and legal staff. Include working hours, part-time availability, non-permit duties, holidays, council meeting weeks, district travel times, and system tasks. Planning, fire, and environmental reviews may run in parallel; decision drafting waits for all applicable reviews, notice, and inspection results. Applicant and third-party response times are separate from municipal service.

A discrete-event simulation is appropriate: arrivals, task enablement and completion, responses, notice expiry or extension, inspection batching, and case closure are events. Track both case-level outcomes and daily resource use, queue lengths, backlog age, and travel.

## 2. Parameter-estimation plan

Use the full event log, not the two-case excerpt, for calibration. With PM4Py, format data using `pm4py.format_dataframe`, then use variants, DFGs, performance DFGs, arrival summaries, and process discovery/conformance diagnostics to inspect observed paths and timing. The event log and process tree are evidence for paths and ordering, not a complete executable specification: implement routing and joins explicitly, and verify `parse_process_tree` syntax against the installed PM4Py version.

Estimate arrivals by week or day, month, weekday, case type, district, and architect. Preserve the March–May and September–November peaks and Monday burst pattern. Model large-architect clusters using submission histories and linked case attributes.

Estimate service times from activity start and completion records where available, stratified by type, district, resource, and calendar period. Where starts are missing, infer service and waiting separately only with defensible task-enable or roster data; otherwise calibrate aggregate elapsed time and mark the split uncertain. Do not treat gaps between completion timestamps as service times. Estimate waits from task enablement to start, applicant response from request to receipt, and notice durations using a mixture or conditional model for no-objection and objection cases.

Estimate branching probabilities and rework conditional on case attributes and preceding path. Resolve rework using text coding where feasible; include uncertainty from unclassified reasons. Estimate resource availability from HR rosters and calendars, then reconcile with observed work patterns. Infer shared-architect, neighborhood, and inspection-batch dependencies from linked cases and dates; test a range of linkage rates because the flattened log may miss links.

## 3. Assumptions

**Arrivals:** Use a seasonal weekday rate model or resampled historical arrival blocks, with type and district composition allowed to vary. Represent architect bursts, not just independent arrivals.

**Service times:** Use positive, right-skewed distributions or empirical resampling by activity and relevant strata. Keep municipal service separate from external waits. Preserve observed tails; do not winsorize them simply to stabilize means.

**Routing:** Apply explicit case flags and verified policy rules. Missing fire-area and environmental-sensitivity data require probabilistic routing or scenario bounds. Represent conditional public notice, approvals, rejection, and appeals.

**Resources/calendars:** Apply the supplied staffing and calendars, HR-derived availability, holidays, council weeks, part-time fire availability, and environmental non-permit allocation. Treat reconstructed calendars as uncertain.

**Rework:** Model completeness loops, inspection and planning rework, legal rework, and appeals as separate transitions with probabilities and delays conditional on case type and outcomes. Do not assume every loop is captured or coded reliably.

**Applicant/third-party response:** Model response times separately with heavy tails. Include no-response or withdrawal outcomes only if supported by data or policy evidence.

**Cross-case dependencies:** Use shared architect and district links, objection groups where identifiable, and inspection batches. Vary linkage and batching assumptions because dependencies are under-recorded.

**Non-stationary demand:** Preserve seasonal peaks and weekday patterns. Include demand growth as a stress scenario rather than assuming the observed 18-month pattern remains fixed.

**Missing data:** Treat missing starts, objections, risk flags, rework reasons, emergency decisions, and case links as uncertainty. Bound or impute with documented assumptions and include sensitivity analysis.

## 5. Warm-up, replications, and uncertainty treatment

Use a warm-up period long enough for queues and backlog to reach a representative state, determined by inspecting time-series of backlog, queue age, and utilization. For a steady-state comparison, start with at least 12 months warm-up and measure 24 months to cover two seasonal cycles. Also run a finite-horizon version initialized from the observed backlog if the operational decision concerns a specific upcoming period.

Run enough independent replications to stabilize confidence intervals for the primary metrics; begin with roughly 100 and increase based on interval width. Use fixed, recorded seeds and paired seeds across interventions. Report replication-based 95% confidence intervals for differences, stratified by case type and district where practical. For uncertain parameters, draw parameter sets from bootstrap or posterior distributions and distinguish parameter uncertainty from run-to-run variation.

Handle heavy tails using empirically supported survival or mixture distributions, compare alternative tail models, and report p90/p95 and threshold exceedance alongside means. Avoid truncating long cases for convenience. Test sensitivity to hidden objections, linkage, calendar availability, D2 eligibility and safety, travel savings, and demand growth.

## 6. Validation strategy

- **Face validation:** Review representative paths with clerks, planners, inspectors, legal staff, and applicants. Check parallel reviews, notices, rework, appeals, council weeks, travel, and policy eligibility.
- **Event-log replay:** Compare simulated variants and conditional activity counts with PM4Py results and investigate deviations. Do not force incomplete or rare traces into invalid routes.
- **Distributional validation:** On held-out periods, compare p50/p90/p95 total time, stage gaps, response times, notice length, rework, rejections, and arrival counts by type, district, and month.
- **Resource validation:** Compare roster-adjusted utilization, weekday queues, travel, fire availability, environmental reserved time, and legal downtime. Check that tasks do not complete while required resources are unavailable.
- **Bottleneck validation:** Confirm Monday completeness spikes, environmental/legal queues, and D3/D5 travel delays. Perturb capacity and check expected queue responses.
- **Out-of-sample validation:** Fit earlier months and predict later months, including peak periods and holiday effects. Pilot and instrument policies whose effects are not observable in historical data.

## 7. Decision criteria

Agree targets with the municipality before choosing a policy. Illustrative provisional SLAs are 30 days for Simple, 90 for New residential, 120 for Commercial, and 240 for Complex variance. A candidate target could be at least a 10% reduction in overall p90 duration and a 5 percentage-point increase in peak-month SLA compliance; these are planning thresholds, not asserted legal deadlines.

Report submission-to-closure median, p90 and p95, SLA compliance by type, district and architect group, effort shifted to applicants, resource utilization, cost, travel, rework, appeals, backlog size and age, and unresolved cases past SLA. Require no unexplained case-type or district p90 deterioration above 5% or 5 days, no district or small-architect SLA gap widening over 3 percentage points, and no more than 2 days of median effort or waiting shifted to applicants without explicit consideration.

Target sustainable utilization around 65–85%, investigate prolonged utilization above 90% and growing backlog, and compare staffing and applicant costs per permit. For D2, require independently supported safety and legal non-inferiority evidence before adoption. Select policies that meet safeguards and improve outcomes across plausible parameter draws; report the probability of meeting targets and worst-case tail/backlog. Present Pareto trade-offs where speed, fairness, safety, and cost conflict.

def load_excerpt() -> pd.DataFrame:

def inspect_log(df: pd.DataFrame) -> dict[str, Any]:
    log = pm4py.convert_to_event_log(df)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(log)
    dfg, start_activities, end_activities = pm4py.discover_dfg(log)
    performance_dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(log)
    arrival_average = pm4py.get_case_arrival_average(log)
    try:
        tree = pm4py.parse_process_tree(TREE)

        tree = None
        print(f"Illustrative tree needs review: {exc}")

    timed = df.sort_values(["case:concept:name", "time:timestamp"]).copy()
    timed["observed_gap_minutes"] = (
        timed.groupby("case:concept:name")["time:timestamp"]
        .diff().dt.total_seconds() / 60
    )
    for col in ("start:timestamp", "enabled_at"):
        if col in timed:
            timed[col] = pd.to_datetime(timed[col], utc=True, errors="coerce")
        else:
            timed[col] = pd.to_datetime([pd.NaT] * len(timed), utc=True)
    timed["service_minutes"] = (
        timed["time:timestamp"] - timed["start:timestamp"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    timed["waiting_minutes"] = (
        timed["start:timestamp"] - timed["enabled_at"]
    ).dt.total_seconds() / 60
    return dict(variants=variants, dfg=dfg, starts=start_activities,
                ends=end_activities, performance_dfg=performance_dfg,
                case_arrival_average=arrival_average, tree=tree, timed=timed)
