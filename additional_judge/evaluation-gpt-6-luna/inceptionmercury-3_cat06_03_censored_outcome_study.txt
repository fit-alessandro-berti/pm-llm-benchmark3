The response covers the core design concerns: it distinguishes activation-conditional default risk from outcomes across all applications, avoids calling rejected or withdrawn cases non-default, discusses censoring and transfer, flags key leakage risks, and proposes temporal validation and a PM4Py pipeline.

The grade is limited by material inconsistencies between the stated target and implementation. The target is default within 90 days after activation, but the code’s `default` and `outcome` fields take any repeated `default_90d` value without checking label maturity, the 90-day horizon, or feed delay. Its `permanent_missing` rule treats a nonmissing `label_available_at` as evidence of a permanently missing outcome, which does not establish that. The cohort-flow table also appears to count the 3,600 immature activated cases separately from 5,100 still-open applications without making clear whether those groups overlap, despite the requirement to account for all applications.

There are further implementation gaps: the split assigns all dates before July 2025 to training, including potentially immature labels; prediction time is not consistently defined in the code; and filtering events relative to the first decision timestamp does not implement an auditable common prediction landmark. These weaken the otherwise thoughtful discussion of leakage and temporal evaluation.

Overall, this is a strong conceptual answer with a useful skeleton, but its cohort accounting and label-handling code do not reliably implement the proposed estimand.
