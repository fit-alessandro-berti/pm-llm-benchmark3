6.5 — The answer has strong target semantics, selection-bias discussion, leakage audit, and temporal-validation intent, but several central cohort and implementation flaws prevent a higher grade.

The largest issue is that the cohort-flow table does not actually account for all 48,000 cases: it mixes overlapping and nested categories, leaves the 3,600 immature activated accounts split into unspecified “parts” and “subsets,” and gives no reconciled row counts for the activated population. Some timing arithmetic is also inconsistent: the stated feed-complete date for a 125-day lag is approximately 2025-10-13, but that is a practical completeness assumption, not a rule that identifies permanently missing feeds. A missing feed after 125 days cannot by itself establish permanent missingness.

The primary estimand is reasonable, but its deployment interpretation is blurred. It is defined among cases that are eventually activated while predictions are made before the decision, and then described as the score needed at Credit Review. That conditional population is selected using a future decision. The answer recognizes the selection issue, yet its suggestion that selection is “largely recorded” does not establish exchangeability or support for cases the policy rarely activates. The target should be more explicit about whether it is a prediction conditional on eventual activation or a policy-specific risk score for currently reviewed cases, and how the former is evaluated for cases whose activation is not yet known.

The competing-risk discussion also overstates what the stated data support. It proposes following linked transfers into partner activation, but the prompt says linkage exists for only about 60% and partner feeds are more often permanently missing. Treating unlinked transfers as competing outcomes may be suitable for an operational process-state target, but it does not resolve their missing outcome follow-up. The answer should distinguish the observed transfer state from a competing event in a defined estimand and quantify uncertainty from selective linkage and feed loss.

The code skeleton is concrete, but has consequential correctness issues. It infers activation and label status from event rows without clearly resolving repeated or conflicting labels; `feed_default == 1` can label a case positive without verifying that the reported default occurred within 90 days. Its `np.select` status conditions make every activated case with a nonmissing `y90` labelled before considering censoring logic, while the survival representation treats a default event as observed only if `days_default <= fu` and assumes near-real-time event recording without implementing the stated delayed-feed uncertainty. `elapsed_prefixes` uses a transform over full case histories before filtering, and its decision check can drop all rows of a case rather than establish a valid as-of prefix at a specified prediction time. The fixed-prefix extraction also depends on PM4Py API behavior and does not robustly ensure that the third event is the first Credit Review or that the case was undecided at the prediction timestamp. Finally, the “temporal folds” use `ct_now` and `X_now` built at extraction time for test cases, so the test feature table is not reconstructed as of each fold’s prediction point; the advertised as-of backtest is therefore not implemented.

The answer nevertheless handles many requirements well: it rejects convenient zero-labeling, separates activation selection from ordinary censoring, discusses delayed labels and policy/migration shifts, identifies key leakage risks, and proposes sensible survival and calibration metrics. The implementation and cohort accounting, however, are central required deliverables, and their defects materially weaken the study’s claimed validity.
