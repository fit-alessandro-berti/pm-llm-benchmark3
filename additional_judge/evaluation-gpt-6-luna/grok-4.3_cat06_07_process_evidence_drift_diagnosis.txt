## 2. Competing explanations and timeline

January–March provide a stable baseline: labels are mature, variant shares change little, and predictive metrics are consistent.

April is chiefly a data-pipeline incident. The renamed diagnosis activity broke prediction prefixes, and unknown activities and missing features rose sharply. The resulting metric collapse should not be treated as evidence of underlying concept drift until April is recomputed.

May shows recovery after the mapping patch, but the cached “events before diagnosis” feature was not fully backfilled for April. May’s residual feature gaps warrant a lineage check.

June brings process-mix change from the remote-routing policy: V2 rises to 52%. Yet matured breach rates within V1, V2, and V3 do not show a corresponding increase. This points to process and covariate shift, not demonstrated concept drift.

July–August bring supplier-related process deterioration. The parts-receipt edge slows sharply, and V3’s matured breach rate rises from 0.46 in July to 0.64 in August while V1 and V2 remain relatively stable. This supports a V3-specific performance problem. Whether the outcome relationship changed conditional on the same path remains to be tested.

September combines continued parts delays with Partner Z onboarding. Partner Z contributes a more rural, V3-heavy population, and its labels mature more slowly. The observed source-level breach gap is suggestive, but not conclusive until maturity and case mix are controlled.

October should be interpreted primarily as a label-maturity and censoring problem. Only 55% of labels are mature, and the backlog disproportionately affects longer paths. The observed 0.16 breach rate cannot be compared directly with mature cohorts; it does not establish performance recovery.

## 3. Diagnostic tests

- **Label-maturity curves and fixed-vintage backtesting:** Plot label availability by case-start month and path. Recompute metrics at a common elapsed time and on fully matured cohorts. Under censoring, October’s breach rate should rise as labels mature; under genuine improvement, it should remain lower on mature, comparable cases.

- **Recomputation after activity-label normalization and duplicate cleaning:** Normalize both diagnosis labels, remove duplicate events with an auditable rule, and rebuild all derived features and prefixes for April. Under pipeline failure, April metrics should improve materially; under genuine model deterioration, they will remain poor after repair.

- **Calibration and discrimination by variant, source, and month:** Report AUROC, Brier score, and calibration by V1/V2/V3 and Partner Z versus other sources, using mature labels. Under V3-specific deterioration, the worsening should concentrate in V3; under broad concept drift, it should persist across variants and sources.

- **Process variants and DFG/performance-DFG edges:** Compare monthly variant frequencies and edge durations, particularly `Order Part -> Part Received`. Under process drift, V3 share and parts delay should rise with V3 breaches; under a model or label issue, breaches may rise without corresponding process changes.

- **Outcome relationship conditional on the same path:** Within variants, compare breach risk conditional on path, elapsed edge times, and relevant case attributes across months. Under concept drift, conditional relationships should change; under process drift alone, the path or duration distribution changes while conditional risk remains similar.

- **Partner Z reweighting or transport check:** Compare mature Partner Z and non-Partner-Z cases within variant and covariate strata, then reweight to a common population. Under population shift, much of the aggregate gap should shrink after adjustment; under a source-specific outcome change, a gap should persist among comparable cases.

- **Feature missingness and lineage audit:** Trace source fields through label normalization, event deduplication, prefix generation, cached features, and model inputs. Under pipeline failure, April defects should be reproducible in lineage and corrected by recomputation; under other drift, lineage should be intact while distributions or outcome relationships change.

## 4. Recalibration-versus-retraining decision

**Immediate containment:** Mark April metrics invalid pending reconstruction. Mark October outcome metrics provisional and avoid using them for retraining or deployment decisions. Continue monitoring with mature cohorts and show label maturity alongside all outcome metrics.

**Short-term recalibration:** Consider recalibration only after maturity and pipeline gates pass, using recent mature data and evaluating each variant separately. Do not calibrate to October’s incomplete outcomes. A variant-specific calibration layer is justified for investigation, especially for V3; a separate V3 model is premature without evidence that it improves out-of-time performance.

**Feature repair:** Recompute April after label normalization, duplicate handling, and full derived-feature backfill. Assess adding supplier-delay, rural-distance, and device-age features, with lineage and availability-at-prediction-time checks.

**Full retraining:** Wait for mature labels and repaired features, then compare candidate models on temporally held-out, mature cohorts and by variant and source. January–March are safe as a stable baseline but may not represent current operations. May–June may be usable after feature-lineage checks. April is unsafe until repaired; July–September require maturity-controlled analysis and explicit handling of supplier and population changes; October is unsafe while labels remain immature.

## 5. Monitoring architecture

Use linked monitors with cohort definitions and maturity status attached to every report:

- **Predictive:** AUROC and Brier score by case-start month, variant, and source, only on comparable matured cohorts.
- **Calibration:** ECE and reliability plots by variant and source; gate interpretation on sufficient mature labels.
- **Label maturity:** Maturity curves, median delay, and backlog by case-start month and path length.
- **Data quality:** Unknown-activity share, missingness, duplicates, event order, and feature lineage, with deployment and backfill records.
- **Process variants:** Monthly variant frequencies, unmapped paths, and path changes.
- **Performance DFG:** Edge-duration distributions, especially parts receipt and diagnosis-to-remote-fix.
- **Population:** Source shares and covariate distributions, including rural distance and device age.
- **Intervention/deployment:** Track policy, supplier, partner, mapping, and model changes against metric timelines.

Alert dependencies should prevent a label-delay alarm from triggering retraining. A maturity alarm should mark outcome metrics provisional and defer outcome-based drift conclusions; a data-quality alarm should trigger pipeline investigation and block training on affected cohorts. Recalibration or retraining alerts should require mature labels, valid lineage, and evidence of persistent deterioration within comparable segments.

```python
import pandas as pd
import pm4py

raw = pd.read_csv("field_service_events.csv")
raw["timestamp"] = pd.to_datetime(raw["timestamp"], utc=True)
raw["activity"] = raw["activity"].replace({
    "Initial Diagnosis Completed": "Diagnostic Assessment Completed"
})

# Keep an audit count, then collapse exact duplicate events deterministically.
duplicate_key = ["case_id", "activity", "timestamp"]
duplicate_counts = raw.groupby(duplicate_key, dropna=False).size()
events = raw.sort_values(duplicate_key).drop_duplicates(duplicate_key, keep="first")
events = pm4py.format_dataframe(
    events, case_id="case_id", activity_key="activity", timestamp_key="timestamp"
)

# Event-calendar cohorts describe activity occurring in a month.
event_month = pm4py.filter_time_range(
    events, "2026-01-01", "2026-11-01", mode="events"
)
event_month["event_month"] = event_month["timestamp"].dt.to_period("M")

# Case-start cohorts assign each case to its first-event month.
starts = events.groupby("case_id")["timestamp"].min().rename("case_start")
case_start = events.merge(starts, on="case_id")
case_start["case_start_month"] = case_start["case_start"].dt.to_period("M")

# Extract monthly process evidence; retain cohort keys for auditability.
rows = []
for month, group in case_start.groupby("case_start_month"):
    dfg, _, _ = pm4py.discover_dfg(group)
    performance_dfg = pm4py.discover_performance_dfg(group)
    variants = pm4py.get_variants(group)
    temporal = pm4py.extract_temporal_features_dataframe(group)
    rows.append({
        "case_start_month": str(month),
        "cases": group["case_id"].nunique(),
        "events": len(group),
        "variants": variants,
        "dfg": dfg,
        "performance_dfg": performance_dfg,
        "temporal_features": temporal,
        "duplicate_rows_collapsed": int(duplicate_counts.sum()),
    })

monthly_evidence = pd.DataFrame(rows)
```

Event-calendar cohorts count events occurring during the month, including cases that started earlier. Case-start cohorts group complete case histories by when cases began; they are preferable for linking predictors to outcomes but require explicit handling of cases still in progress. Preserve cohort month, maturity, source, and transformation counts so each metric can be reproduced.

## 6. Risks of acting on the wrong diagnosis

Training on immature October labels could encode unobserved breaches as non-breaches. Require maturity-adjusted evaluation and exclude incomplete labels from training.

Treating April’s broken prefixes as real model deterioration could drive needless recalibration or retraining. Require normalized-label recomputation and lineage review before using April.

Treating all V3 deterioration as model drift could obscure the supplier delay that changed process performance. Monitor edge durations and compare conditional outcome relationships before selecting a model response.

Treating Partner Z’s observed breach gap as a settled source effect could misattribute differences caused by V3 mix, covariates, or slower label maturity. Compare mature, like-for-like cases and report adjusted results.
